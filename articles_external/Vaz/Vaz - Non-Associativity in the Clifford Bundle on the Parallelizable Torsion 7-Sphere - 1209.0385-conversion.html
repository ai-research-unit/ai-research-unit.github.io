What the Paper Does

Builds non-associative products on the parallelizable seven-sphere inside the Clifford algebra $\mathcal{C}\ell_{0,7}$, and then does the thing that makes such a construction usable: it exhibits a single product, the $\odot$-product, which encodes all of them. With it, the parallelizing torsion appears as the associator of the product, the transformations of spinor fields on $S^7$ are read off the deformation, and the Kač–Moody current algebra attached to those spinors changes accordingly. The setting matters because $S^7$ is the sphere whose tangent frame is parallelizable by the octonions, so the geometry and the non-associativity are the same fact.

Torsion as an associator

The parameter of the deformed product "is twice the parallelizing torsion", with components $T_{ijk}(X)=[(\bar e_i\circ\bar X)\circ(X\circ e_j)]\circ e_k$. Consequently the algebra can be written as a commutator of covariant derivatives, $[\delta_i,\delta_j]=2T_{ijk}(X)\delta_k$, where $\delta_AX=X\circ A$ is the parallelizing covariant derivative.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Torsion is the associator

The idea. A identification: on a parallelizable manifold with a non-associative product, the parallelizing torsion is exactly the failure of associativity of the product, written as $T_{ijk}(X)=[(\bar e_i\circ\bar X)\circ(X\circ e_j)]\circ e_k$. The commutator of two covariant derivatives reproduces it.

What it would add. The corpus's torsion article treats torsion as a tensor field added to a connection, and its non-associativity appears, if at all, as an algebraic nuisance. This says the two are one object: a manifold whose product is non-associative has torsion, and the amount is the associator. That is a statement of the kind the corpus is built on — an algebraic property identified with a geometric one — and it would let the corpus's torsion article say where torsion comes from rather than only where it goes.

Where it lands. articles_physics/torsion-contorsion-and-the-spin-connection-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The identification is made on $S^7$ with a specific product, and the corpus's torsion article works on a general spacetime; the generalisation from one parallelizable sphere to a general manifold is not in the paper.

2. One product can encode a family of products

The idea. A construction: instead of choosing among many octonionic products, absorb the choice into a Clifford multivector and use a single $\odot$-product. The family becomes the argument of one operation.

What it would add. The corpus's algebra-comparison articles present families of products — the four products article is the clearest case — as a list. This says a list can be compressed into one parametrised operation, which is a smaller and more usable object, and it turns the claim that these products are one product into a statement with a parameter.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-four-products-composition-probability-causality-and-gauge.md.

What stands in the way. The compression works because the parameter is a multivector in a specific algebra, and the corpus's four products are distinguished by what they do to the conjugation, not by a parameter.

3. Spinor transformations need a parameter field

The idea. A result: on $S^7$ a fermion cannot be transformed on its own; the transformation law is $\delta_A\eta=\eta\circ_X A$, and it requires the parameter field $X$ as well. The spinor and the parameter transform together.

What it would add. The corpus's gauge articles all have transformations of the form $\psi\mapsto g\psi$ with $g$ built from the gauge field alone. Here the transformation itself needs a second field, which is a structural departure: it says that on a non-associative background the gauge parameter is not free but tied to the geometry. That is the kind of constraint the corpus's model-building articles would want to know about, since it limits what a gauge principle can be on such a space.

Where it lands. articles_physics/gauge-redundancy-and-the-information-in-the-gauge-orbit-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The statement is specific to $S^7$ and to this product, and the corpus's gauge articles are on flat space; the constraint would be a footnote about a case the corpus does not treat.

The rank: torsion-as-associator first, because it is an identification of two named objects; the single-product compression second, because it is a device; the parameter-field constraint last, because it is a local result.

The remaining items in one line each: the Moufang-like generalisation of section 4, which is the paper's attempt to keep some associativity-like identity; the explicit component form $T_{abc}(X)=[(\bar e_a\circ\bar X)(X\circ e_b)\circ e_c]$, which is what a reader would compute with; and the dedication to Jaime Keller, which places the paper in the tradition that treats the seven-sphere as a physical internal space.

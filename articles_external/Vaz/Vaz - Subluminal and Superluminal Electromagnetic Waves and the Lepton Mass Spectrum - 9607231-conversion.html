What the Paper Does

The full programme paper: the free Maxwell equation has subluminal and superluminal solutions with non-zero field invariant; those fields are spinor bilinears; the spinor obeys a non-linear Dirac–Hestenes equation; and for a constant Takabayasi angle that non-linear equation becomes the linear one, with a mass that comes out at $206.55\,m_e$ for the muon. This is the paper the corpus already carries as an article, so this note records the source's own words and then says what, if anything, the conversion adds.

$$M=\left(1+\frac{3}{2\alpha}\right)m .$$

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Duality is a gauge symmetry with its own potential

The idea. A construction: the theory carries two potentials and two gauge parameters, an electric one, $\psi\mapsto\psi e^{e\gamma_{21}\theta}$ with $A\mapsto A+\partial\theta$, and a magnetic one, $\psi_\pm\mapsto\psi_\pm e^{\pm g\gamma_5\theta}$ with $B\mapsto B+\partial\theta$. The second acts on the parity projectors with opposite signs.

What it would add. The corpus's gauge articles promote one symmetry and one potential. Here the duality between electric and magnetic descriptions is itself a gauge freedom, with its own connection, and it acts chirally on the spinor. That is a different kind of gauge structure from the ones the corpus builds, and it is the mechanism by which the magnetic charge enters the mass formula — the corpus's article reports the result without the symmetry that produces it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-complex-potential-of-a-self-dual-maxwell-field.md.

What stands in the way. The two-parameter gauge structure is stated in a few lines and never developed; a corpus article on the complex potential would have to check that the magnetic parameter is not simply a duality rotation already covered there.

2. The spectrum is a condition on an angle

The idea. A logic: the reduction from non-linear to linear happens when one coefficient vanishes, and that vanishing is a condition on the Takabayasi angle, $\beta=\pi/2$ or $3\pi/2$. Mass values are therefore labelled by discrete angles, not fitted.

What it would add. The corpus's articles treat $\theta_{\mathrm{dua}}$ as a knob that classifies solutions — electron at $0$, positron at $\pi$, muon at $\pi/2$. This says the classification is the mass spectrum: the rungs are the angles at which the non-linear term disappears. Stating it that way makes the muon coincidence a structural claim rather than a numerical one, and it tells the reader exactly where the scheme would fail — at the next angle, where the arithmetic does not close.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-polar-variables.md.

What stands in the way. The corpus already assigns the angles to particles, so the addition is a re-description; and the re-description makes the numerology look deeper than the numbers support.

3. Non-linear reduces to linear by freezing one angle

The idea. A method: a non-linear spinor equation whose non-linearity sits in a single angular variable becomes linear whenever that variable is constant, and the constant then plays the role of mass.

What it would add. The corpus's Dirac–Hestenes article presents the linear equation and the non-linear one as two objects. The reduction rule is a third thing: a general device for turning one into the other. It applies to any non-linear spinor equation of this shape, including the ones in the corpus's own non-linear Maxwell and Gross–Pitaevskii articles, and it explains why so many "non-linear" spinor equations have linear solutions.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-dirac-hestenes-equation-and-spacetime-algebra-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The rule is only as general as the shape assumption, and the corpus's non-linear equations do not all have a single angular variable; stating it broadly would invite misapplication.

The rank: the dual gauge symmetry first, because it is a structure the corpus lacks; the angle-as-spectrum reading second, because it sharpens a claim the corpus already makes; the reduction rule last, because it is a generalisation of a special case.

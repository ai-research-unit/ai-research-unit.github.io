What the Paper Does

Changes the signature of a quadratic space without leaving its real Clifford algebra. The device is a new multiplication on the same underlying vector space, built so that the basis vectors square the way the other signature requires; the paper calls it a vee product. With it, the Minkowski Dirac equation can be written over a Euclidean background, the Euclidean Hodge star and its codifferential can be written over Minkowski, and duality in Minkowski can be defined without complexifying the algebra. An algebraic replacement for $t\mapsto it$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim

"Given the real Clifford algebra of a quadratic space with a given signature, we define a new product in this structure such that it simulates the Clifford product of a quadratic space with another signature different from the original one."

Applications: "we use it in order to write the minkowskian Dirac equation over the euclidean spacetime and to define a new duality operation in terms of which one can find self-dual and anti-self-dual solutions of gauge fields over Minkowski spacetime analogous to the ones over Euclidean spacetime and without needing to complexify the original real algebra."

Why the problem is real

"real Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{p',q'}$ ... are in general not isomorphic, that is, when we change the signature we get in general different Clifford algebras. For example, the Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}$ are not isomorphic".

And the standard workaround has no clear meaning: The interpretation of making $t\to it$ is not trivial.

The device

"we define a new product, which we call a vee product, A ∨ B simulating the Cl1,3 Clifford product in Cl4,0. After the selection" With a chosen unit vector $e_0$ for the fourth direction and three orthonormal $e_i$,

$$u\vee v:=(-1)\left[vu-2(v\cdot e_0)(e_0\cdot u)\right].$$

The point is what this does to the squares: it is easy to prove that $e_0\vee e_0=1$ and $e_i\vee e_i=-1$ $(i=1,2,3)$, while $e_\mu^2=e_\mu e_\mu=1$. So the same vectors, under the new law, satisfy

$$u\vee w+w\vee u=2(u_0w_0-u_1w_1-u_2w_2-u_3w_3),$$

a Lorentzian inner product, while the original product keeps the Euclidean one. The construction is associative and preserves the grading.

The verdict

"Given the Clifford algebra of a quadratic space with a given signature, we have defined a new product in this structure such that it simulates the Clifford product of a quadratic space with another arbitrary signature different from the original one. We have used this in order to give an algebraic approach to the so called Wick rotation."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Wick rotation is a re-multiplication, not a substitution

The idea. A construction: instead of replacing $t$ by $it$ and continuing analytically, keep the real algebra and change the product. The basis vectors are untouched; only the law that combines them changes, and with it the sign of the inner product.

What it would add. The corpus's article on the Wick rotation interprets $t\mapsto it$ inside the biquaternion algebra and asks what the imaginary time means. This supplies the alternative that avoids the interpretation problem entirely: no complex numbers enter, no analytic continuation is needed, and the Euclidean and Minkowski algebras are related by an explicit change of multiplication on one real space. For a corpus that treats the Wick rotation as a change of layer, this is a second route to the same place, and the comparison is informative.

Where it lands. articles_physics/interpretation-of-the-wick-rotation-in-the-biquaternion-universe.md.

What stands in the way. The vee product is not the corpus's product, so every identity downstream would need re-deriving; and the construction needs a distinguished $e_0$, which reintroduces by hand the very asymmetry the algebraic route was meant to avoid.

2. Signature lives in the multiplication, not in the space

The idea. A claim with a consequence: the same vector space, with the same basis and the same elements, admits both a Euclidean and a Lorentzian quadratic form depending on which product is used. Signature is therefore a property of the algebra's law, not of the underlying set.

What it would add. The corpus's temporal-dimension article argues that the special role of the time direction is carried by the algebraic unit rather than by the coordinate. This is the same thesis in a sharper form: even the sign of $e_0^2$ is a choice of product. Together they would let the corpus state that neither the label "time" nor the sign of the metric is intrinsic to the vector space.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-temporal-dimension-why-the-temporal-unit-is-the-identity.md.

What stands in the way. The claim concerns a family of algebras on one space, and the corpus's articles work inside a single fixed algebra; stating it would mean introducing a second product into articles built on one.

3. Duality in Minkowski without complexification

The idea. A technique: with a signature-changing product in hand, the Euclidean Hodge star can be transplanted to Minkowski, so self-dual and anti-self-dual gauge fields can be defined there with a real operation instead of an $i$.

What it would add. The corpus's self-duality articles use the biquaternion's own $i$ to write the split, and its articles on instantons use the Euclidean split. This says the two splits are the same operation seen through different products, and gives a real-valued bridge between them. For a corpus that keeps the self-dual split as a central device, a proof that the Euclidean and Minkowskian versions are one construction is worth having.

Where it lands. articles_physics/topological-charge-self-duality-and-the-bogomolny-bound-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The bridge is explicit only for the vee product, and the corpus's split is written in the biquaternion product; translating between them is the work the paper does not do.

The rank: the re-multiplication reading first, because it answers a question the corpus leaves open; the signature-in-the-product claim second, because it is a thesis; the duality bridge last, because it is a technique that needs translation.

The remaining items in one line each: the associativity of the vee product, which is what makes it a genuine algebra rather than a formal trick; the two-way use of the construction, Euclidean star in Minkowski and Minkowski Dirac in Euclidean, which shows the device is symmetric; and the citation of Pezzaglia and Adams on whether metric signature should matter at all, which is the broader question the paper's construction answers in the negative.

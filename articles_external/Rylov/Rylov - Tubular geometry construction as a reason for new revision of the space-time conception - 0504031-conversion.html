What the Paper Does

States the construction of T-geometry cleanly — deform the Euclidean world function and every Euclidean proposition deforms with it — and then does something the other geometry papers do not: it writes a concrete distortion function and computes with it. The simplest nondegenerate geometry is the Minkowski one with a modified distance inside a region of size $\sigma_{0}$, and in it the relation between the geometric mass and the Minkowski mass is $m^{2}=m_{M}^{2}+2d$. From that he draws the conclusion that the mass spectrum is a problem of geometry, not of dynamics.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Mass squared shifted by a geometric constant

The idea. A formula: in the simplest nondegenerate geometry $m^{2}=m_{M}^{2}+2d$, where $d$ is a constant of the geometry and $m_{M}$ the mass the particle would have in Minkowski space. Mass is a length in this scheme, and the geometry adds a fixed amount to its square.

What it would add. The corpus's masses are Lagrangian parameters and its boson spectrum comes from a breaking chain — masses are outputs of dynamics. A formula that makes the mass a geometric invariant with an additive constant is a different kind of proposal, and it is one the corpus can evaluate directly: $m^{2}-m_{M}^{2}$ would have to be the same constant for every particle, which is immediately false if $m_{M}$ is taken as the observed mass and $d$ is universal.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-breaking-chain-and-the-boson-spectrum.md.

What stands in the way. The relation shifts every mass by the same $d$, so it preserves mass differences squared rather than mass ratios, which is not what any observed spectrum looks like. For the formula to produce a spectrum, $d$ would have to vary per particle, and then it is not one geometric constant.

2. Discreteness from restricted divisibility

The idea. A mechanism: in a geometry with a minimal length, a straight segment has a largest number of equal subsegments, and that integer is a geometric invariant. Discrete parameters would come from such counting.

What it would add. The corpus's discreteness comes from eigenvalues. A second, purely combinatorial source — the count of subsegments — would be independent of any operator, and it is the kind of thing that could explain ratios rather than absolute values. That is a genuinely different route to a discrete spectrum, and the corpus's integer-spin articles, which get integers from representation theory, would be the place to compare it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/integer-spin-quantization-and-the-adjoint-action-on-the-material-sector-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The paper states the connection and never computes a count. No integer is exhibited, so the mechanism is asserted at the level of principle, and the one number printed — the shift $2d$ — is continuous.

3. One deterministic geometry among many stochastic ones

The idea. A count: in the whole class of homogeneous isotropic T-geometries, exactly one has deterministic free motion, and it is the one with $D=0$. Every other member has stochastic free motion, so determinism is the exceptional case.

What it would add. The corpus's flat background is a choice of convenience, and its fluctuations are added. This says the flat case is the single degenerate member of a family, which re-frames the corpus's background as a measure-zero special case. That is a stronger statement than "flat is simplest", and the corpus could state it as the alternative its choice implicitly rejects.

Where it lands. articles_physics/frame-and-metric-conventions.md.

What stands in the way. Again the class is one he defines, and the members other than Minkowski are not shown to describe anything. A unique member of an unpopulated class is a weak singularity.

The rank: the mass-squared shift first, because it is a formula the corpus can falsify; restricted divisibility second, because it is a different source of discreteness; the unique-deterministic-geometry count last, because the class is empty in practice.

The remaining items in one line each: the $\sigma$-immanence principle, which is the deformation procedure of 0206017 given its final name; the claim that $\hbar$ is an attribute of the geometry rather than a constant of nature, which is the programme's constant ambition; and the account of why the quantum principles were adopted, which is that the alternative geometry was not yet known rather than that the principles were right.

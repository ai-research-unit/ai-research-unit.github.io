What the Paper Does

Compares two strategies for building a relativistic quantum theory and argues one of them cannot work. The first — take non-relativistic quantum technique and apply it to relativistic systems — fails for two reasons: the particle is not a relativistic invariant object, and the commutation relations of the field-theoretic scheme are incompatible with the dynamic equations, so the pair production it "predicts" is an artefact of inconsistency. The second strategy builds a fundamental theory whose statistical description reproduces quantum mechanics. The argument about invariance is the sharpest thing in the paper and is worth having on its own.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The particle is not an invariant object

The argument is geometric and short. In relativity "the particle world line L is considered to be a physical object (but not its history). In this case the pointlike particle is an intersection L ∩ TC of the world line L with the hyperplane TC : t = C =const. The hyperplane" Then the failure:"The hyperplane $T_{C}$ is not invariant in the sense, that the set $S_{T}$ of all hyperplanes $T_{C}$ is not invariant with respect to the Lorentz transformations." And the consequence: "if we have many world lines coincidence of sets $S_{C}$ and $S_{C'}'$ is impossible at any choice of the constant $C'$. In other words, the particle is not an invariant object, and it cannot be considered as a physical object in the relativistic mechanics." The qualification matters:"In the case of one world line we can compensate the noninvariant character of a particle by a proper choice of the constant $C'$, but at the statistical description, where we deal with many world lines, such a compensation is impossible." And the reason this has gone unnoticed:"The above statements are not new." He credits V.A. Fock with stressing "that concept of the particle state is different in relativistic and nonrelativistic mechanics", and adds: "As a rule researchers do not object to such statements, but they do not apply them in practice."

Covariance is necessary, not sufficient

"Writing dynamic equations in the relativistically covariant form, we believe that we have taken into account all demands of the relativity theory. Unfortunately, it is not so. The relativistic invariance of dynamic equations is the necessary condition of true application of the relativistic principles, but it is not sufficient."

The inconsistency of the field-theoretic scheme

"It is generally assumed that the perturbation theory and the divergencies are the main problems of QFT. In reality, it is only a vertex of iceberg. The main problem lies in the definition of the commutation relations."

Then the two schemes: "There are two different schemes of the second quantization: (1) PA-scheme, where particle and antiparticle are considered as different physical objects and (2) WL-scheme, where world line (WL) is considered as a physical object."

The verdicts: "The commutation relation, which are used in the PA-scheme are incompatible with the dynamic equations. As a result the PA-scheme of the second quantization appears to be inconsistent." And "The commutation relations in WL-scheme are compatible with dynamic equations."

The consequence for pair production — the claim that matters: "However, the pair production is absent in the W L-scheme, even if the self-action constant λ 6= 0 [8]. One believes, that there is the pair production in the P A-scheme. However, the P A-scheme is inconsistent, and the pair production is a corollary of this incon- sistency [8]. Thus, neither"

And the diagnosis of why nobody checked: "Conventionally one considers the commutation relations as a kind of initial conditions for the dynamic equations. As a result one does not see a necessity to test the compatibility of the commutation relations with the dynamic equations. In reality the analogy between the commutation relations and initial conditions is not true. The commutation relations are additional constraints imposed on the dynamic variables."

What he proposes instead

"The second strategy is founded on the construction of the fundamental theory, which relates to the conventional nonrelativistic quantum theory approximately in such a way as the statistical physics relates to the axiomatic thermodynamics." Hence: "There is no necessity to postulate the quantum principles, because they may be obtained as a corollary of such a statistical description of nonrelativistic stochastic particles."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The particle is not a Lorentz-invariant object

The idea. A criterion for what may be called a physical object: it must be defined by invariant data. A particle is a slice $L\cap T_{C}$, the slice depends on the frame, and with more than one world line no choice of slice restores agreement. So the particle is a frame artefact and the world line is the object.

What it would add. The corpus quantises a field whose excitations are labelled by momentum, and those labels are frame-dependent in exactly the way described. The argument says the label is not an object, which is a precise version of a worry the corpus's states-and-observables article raises without resolving. It also explains why the corpus's wave-function-on-configuration-space is awkward relativistically, beyond the usual complaint about time.

Where it lands. articles_physics/states-observables-and-the-born-weight.md.

What stands in the way. The argument applies to a definition of particle as a hyperplane slice; one can define a particle by invariant data instead — the representation labels of the Poincaré group — and then the objection does not bite. The paper does not engage with that route, which is Wigner's and is standard.

2. Commutation relations are constraints, not initial conditions

The idea. A claim about method: equal-time commutators are additional constraints imposed alongside the equations of motion, so their compatibility with those equations must be checked. If they are incompatible, the scheme is inconsistent, and anything it produces — pair production included — may be an artefact.

What it would add. This is a check the corpus has never run on its own second-quantised constructions. The corpus's path-integral articles impose commutation relations and never verify that they survive the dynamics; a test of exactly this kind would either strengthen those articles or reveal an inconsistency, and it is cheap because it is a compatibility computation, not a new theory.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-s-matrix-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The claim is made for a specific self-interacting scalar with a specific pair of schemes, and the conclusion — that pair production is an artefact — contradicts a large body of experiment. Any reading has to explain the experimental facts, and the paper offers no mechanism beyond a $\kappa$-field discussed elsewhere.

3. Two schemes and their different object counts

The idea. A comparison: the PA-scheme quantises particles, whose number is not conserved, so it needs perturbation theory; the WL-scheme quantises world lines, whose number is conserved, so the problem splits into one-, two-, three-line problems each solvable independently.

What it would add. The corpus's S-matrix article computes a perturbative series and treats the expansion as forced by the interaction. This is a claim that the expansion is forced by the wrong choice of object, and that a conserved-object scheme would be non-perturbative from the start. That is a structural alternative the corpus has not considered, and it costs nothing to state alongside its own.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-s-matrix-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The scheme's price is that a world line can be spacelike when pairs are produced, so microcausality in the usual form is lost, and the paper's answer — that causality has another form for extended objects — is asserted with a reference rather than shown.

The rank: the invariant-object argument first, because it is independent of everything else here and needs no commitment; the constraint claim second, because it is a check the corpus can run; the scheme comparison last, because it depends on the disputed verdict about pair production.

The remaining items in one line each: the observation that covariance has been used as a substitute for a relativistic concept of state; the parallel with thermodynamics and statistical physics, which recurs across this set; and the closing complaint that trial and error has replaced derivation, which is the same charge as the compensating conception of 0310050 in a different vocabulary.

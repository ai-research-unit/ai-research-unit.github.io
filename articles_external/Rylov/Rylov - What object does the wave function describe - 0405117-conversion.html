What the Paper Does

Answers its title with an argument rather than a preference: the wave function describes a statistical ensemble or the statistical average particle, and the individual-particle reading is incompatible with the formalism. The argument is that in the classical limit the Schrödinger equation goes over into the dynamic equations of an ensemble of classical particles, which would be impossible if the wave function described one particle. He then does something more interesting than winning the interpretive point: he inverts which object is fundamental in dynamics, making the continuous average system primary and the discrete particle its degenerate case.

The inversion

"Conventionally in the classical dynamics the discrete dynamic system $S_{cl}$ (individual particle) is considered to be the principal object of dynamics." He proposes the reverse: "a situation the continuous dynamic system hScl i may be considered to be the principal object of classical dynamics. Then the discrete dynamic system Scl may be regarded as a derivative object: the partial case of the statistical average dynamic system hScl i, when distributions of all quantities in hScl i are δ-like. Such a redefinition of the concept"

The gain is a uniform definition: "In the dynamics of stochastic systems Sst the principal object of dynamics is the statistical average system hSst i, because Sst is not a dynamic system, and it cannot be an object of dynamics, whereas the statistical average system hSst i is always a continuous dynamic system.". Making the average primary in the classical case too gives one definition across both, and "allows one to consider the classical dynamics as a partial case of the stochastic dynamics (dynamics of stochastic systems), which appears, when the stochasticity intensity vanishes."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The statistical-average object as the primary one

The idea. A redefinition: in any dynamics the primary object is the continuous statistical average, and the individual particle is the limiting case where all distributions are $\delta$-functions. The classical single particle is thus a degenerate ensemble, not an ensemble a derived thing.

What it would add. The corpus quantises a field and reads its solutions as one-particle states; the single particle is primary and the ensemble is built from it. The inversion reverses the construction, and it is cheap to state: the corpus's own density $\rho$ would become a parameter of the basic object rather than a probability computed from it. It also gives a reason why a continuous field, rather than a point, is the natural carrier.

Where it lands. articles_physics/states-observables-and-the-born-weight.md.

What stands in the way. The inversion is a choice of description. A $\delta$-distribution is a legitimate element of the average space, so nothing observable changes; the claim is about which object the equations are written for, and physical content is indifferent to it.

2. Homogeneity in the density as the ensemble signature

The idea. A test: an action is an ensemble action if and only if it scales linearly with the element density, $A[a\rho]=aA[\rho]$, and bilinearity of the quantum action in $\psi$ is exactly this property.

What it would add. The corpus's Born rule is imposed; this is a structural reason for a density at all — the action must be extensive in the number of elements. That is a different justification from the ones the states-and-observables article considers, and it is one the article could adopt without changing any formula, because $\rho=\psi^{*}\psi$ already satisfies it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/states-observables-and-the-born-weight.md.

What stands in the way. Extensivity is satisfied by any density, including ones that give different statistics, so it selects the form of the action and not the rule. It explains why a density appears, not why this one.

3. The gas particle as a statistical average molecule

The idea. An analogy with a physical instance: a gas "particle" is a volume containing $N\to\infty$ molecules, its motion is deterministic although the molecular motion is random, and its six degrees of freedom are the only ones retained.

What it would add. The corpus's fluids are usually formal — a field rewritten in hydrodynamic variables. This is a case where the fluid object is a real coarse-graining of a real microscopic system, which gives the corpus's fluid language a referent and a scale. It also shows what a successful reduction looks like when the microscopic motion is genuinely random, which is the case the corpus's deterministic base does not cover.

Where it lands. articles_physics/causal-dissipative-hydrodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The analogy supports the claim that the average is deterministic but says nothing about phases or interference, which is where quantum mechanics differs from gas dynamics. The corpus's quantum fluid needs the phase, and a gas particle has none.

The rank: the statistical-average inversion first, because it is a structural proposal; the homogeneity test second, because it is checkable; the gas analogy last, because it stops short of phase.

The remaining items in one line each: the SET formalism as a generalisation of quantum technique obtained by dropping the quantum principles, which is the same move as the model conception everywhere else in this set; the observation that the quantum action stays bilinear even when the resulting equation is nonlinear, which is what allows the ensemble reading to survive; and the conclusion that $\hbar$ enters as the coefficient that sets the size of the stochastic component, which recurs in 0403077 and 0603237.

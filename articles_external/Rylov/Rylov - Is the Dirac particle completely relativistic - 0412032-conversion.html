What the Paper Does

Argues that the Dirac equation is covariant but the Dirac particle is not completely relativistic. The $\gamma$-matrices are absolute objects — a matrix vector fixed independently of the fields — and removing them by a change of variables leaves a constant timelike vector $f^k$ that splits space-time into space and time. The paper then measures the violation: it couples $f^k$ to the classical particle's energy-momentum vector, so that $f^k$ is no longer absolute, and calls the result completely relativistic. The modified equations are solved and the solutions compared with the unmodified ones.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why the question is open

"Although the Dirac equation is written usually in the relativistically covariant form, the dynamic system SD is not completely rel- ativistic, because its description contains such absolute objects as γ-matrices γ k , forming a matrix vector. By means"

The general principle behind the charge: "the relativistic covariance of dynamic equations is not sufficient for compatibility with the relativity principles." Covariance is a property of the equation under a group; absoluteness is a property of what the equation contains.

The residue of the $\gamma$-matrices

"rices. If we make a change of variables, eliminating γ-matrices, we obtain the system of dynamic equations, which is not relativistically covariant. Such a transition to eight hydrodynamic variables: 4- vector" — the vector $j^k=\bar\psi\gamma^k\psi$, the pseudovector $S^k=\mathrm{i}\bar\psi\gamma^5\gamma^k\psi$, and the scalar and pseudoscalar $\phi$ and $\kappa$ — is the route to the classical analogue. And what replaces the matrices: "instead of them the constant timlike vector $f^k$ appears. The vector $f^k$ describes an absolute splitting of the space-time into space and time, which is characteristic for the nonrelativistic description."

So the nonrelativistic residue is not a small correction; it is a fixed direction in space-time that the theory never chooses and cannot vary.

The repair

"Coupling the absolute vector $f^k$ with the energy-momentum vector of $S_{D\,cl}$, the classical Dirac particle $S_{D\,cl}$ is modified minimally. The vector $f^k$ ceases to be absolute, and the modified classical Dirac particle $S_{mD\,cl}$ becomes to be completely relativistic. The dynamic equations for $S_{mD\,cl}$ are solved. Solutions for $S_{D\,cl}$ and $S_{mD\,cl}$ are compared."

The repair is a promotion: the constant becomes a dynamical field by coupling, and relativity is restored not by removing the vector but by letting it vary.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Absolute objects as the test of relativity

The idea. A reading with a criterion: a theory is completely relativistic when it contains no object fixed independently of the fields. Covariance is necessary and not sufficient, and the test is to list the constants and see which of them could have been otherwise.

What it would add. The corpus is built on frame conventions and states them explicitly, which is the same inventory done for a different purpose. Applying Rylov's test would turn that inventory into a verdict: which of the corpus's conventions are gauge, which are physical, and which are absolute in his sense. The corpus's frame article is the natural home because the list already exists there.

Where it lands. articles_physics/frame-and-metric-conventions.md.

What stands in the way. "Absolute object" is a term from the Anderson–Friedman literature with a technical definition, and the paper uses it loosely. A criterion applied loosely finds violations everywhere — including in the corpus's own fixed frame, which the corpus would call a convention.

2. A constant timelike vector hidden in the $\gamma$-matrices

The idea. A mechanism: eliminating the $\gamma$-matrices in favour of the eight hydrodynamic variables does not remove the frame they encode; it converts the matrix vector into a constant timelike vector, which is the same information in visible form.

What it would add. The corpus's polar decomposition of the Dirac field produces exactly this set — density, current, spin vector, and the two scalars — and it does not ask what happened to the frame. Rylov's answer, that a fixed timelike direction survives, is a concrete statement about the corpus's own variables and can be checked by looking for that direction in the corpus's decomposition. If it is there, the corpus's claim to have removed the frame is weakened in a specific, locatable way.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-of-the-biquaternion-dirac-field.md.

What stands in the way. Takabayasi's version of the same reduction is frame-free, which suggests the residue is an artefact of how Rylov defines the hydrodynamic variables rather than a theorem about the Dirac equation. The paper does not address that comparison.

3. Restoring relativity by coupling

The idea. A mechanism: a constant that violates relativity is repaired by coupling it to a dynamical quantity, after which it varies and the violation is gone. The absolute object becomes a field.

What it would add. The corpus has constants it does not vary — the mass, the coupling — and a procedure that promotes one of them to a field on demand is a tool it lacks. Whether promotion is legitimate depends on whether the resulting dynamics is constrained, and the paper solves the promoted system, so there is a worked case to inspect.

Where it lands. articles_physics/fundamental-and-derived-elements-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md.

What stands in the way. Promotion by coupling is a formal trick unless the resulting field has dynamics of its own; here the coupling makes $f^k$ follow the energy-momentum, so it is determined rather than dynamical. A determined quantity is not more relativistic than a constant one.

The rank: absolute objects first, because the corpus has the inventory to apply it to; the hidden timelike vector second, because it is a checkable claim about the corpus's own variables; coupling third, because it may be a formal repair.

The remaining items in one line each: the eight hydrodynamic variables as the bridge from spinor to fluid; the claim that a non-covariant form after elimination is evidence of a defect rather than of bad variables, which is the point the corpus would contest.

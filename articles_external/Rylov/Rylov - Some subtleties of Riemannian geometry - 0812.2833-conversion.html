What the Paper Does

Reports a technical obstruction found while trying to generalise dynamics beyond Riemannian geometry, and the obstruction is inside Riemannian geometry itself. There are two standard ways to put Maxwell's equations into a curved space: replace partial derivatives by covariant ones in the equation, or replace the Minkowski world function by the Riemannian one in the solution. They do not agree. The disagreement is a term that vanishes in four-dimensional Minkowski space and does not vanish in general, and from it he concludes that the conventional presentation is problematic.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Two prescriptions for curved space that disagree

The idea. A test: replacing derivatives by covariant derivatives and replacing the flat propagator by the curved one are different operations, and their difference is a computable term proportional to $g^{ik}\sigma_{|k|i}-4$.

What it would add. The corpus's Maxwell article writes the field equations in a curved background and takes minimal coupling as given. This is a finite computation that would decide whether the corpus's prescription agrees with the propagator-based one — and if it does not, which of the two its predictions rest on. No new theory is needed to run it, only the coincidence limit of the curved propagator.

Where it lands. articles_physics/maxwells-equations-in-the-biquaternionic-formulation.md.

What stands in the way. The discrepancy is a contact term, supported at coincidence, so it may cancel against a counterterm in any renormalised quantity. A contact term that never appears in an observable is a formal problem, not a physical one, and the paper does not say which it is.

2. Consistency and single-variance are exclusive

The idea. A dilemma: a geometry can be single-variant, axiomatisable and inconsistent, or multivariant, nonaxiomatisable and consistent. The conventional Riemannian geometry buys definiteness by prohibiting equivalence of remote vectors, and the prohibition is not a repair.

What it would add. The corpus is axiomatic and single-variant by construction — its algebra gives definite answers — and it has never asked what that costs. This is a precise statement of the cost, and it is a question about the corpus's form rather than its content, which the fundamental-elements article is the right place to hold. It also gives the word nonaxiomatisable a sharper meaning than not yet axiomatised.

Where it lands. articles_physics/fundamental-and-derived-elements-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md.

What stands in the way. Inconsistency here means that the axioms conflict at some points, which is a claim about a particular axiomatisation, not about Riemannian geometry as used in practice. Physicists use Riemannian geometry without meeting a contradiction, so the dilemma is formal unless one is exhibited.

3. The dimension-dependent coincidence identity

The idea. A number that looks like an accident: $g^{ik}\sigma_{|k|i}=4$ holds in four dimensions and only there, and it is exactly what makes the flat-space Maxwell equation work in the coincidence limit. Change the dimension and the constant changes with it.

What it would add. The corpus's dimensional analyses treat four as a choice of signature and algebra. An identity whose constant equals the dimension ties the number of dimensions to a property of the propagator, which is stronger than a convention. Stating it would let the corpus say whether its four dimensions do any work beyond bookkeeping.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-parameter-count-as-the-measure-of-the-frameworks-boundary.md.

What stands in the way. The identity is a property of the world function's coincidence limit, not a selection principle, and any dimension $n$ gives $n$ the same way. It constrains nothing; it tracks the dimension and could not explain why four.

The rank: the disagreeing prescriptions first, because it is runnable; the consistency dilemma second, because it names a cost; the dimension identity last, because it explains nothing.

The remaining items in one line each: the definition of vector equivalence through the world function alone, with no coordinate system, manifold or dimension, which is the definitional core of the whole programme; the observation that the $\sigma$-Riemannian geometry is built by deformation of Euclidean propositions rather than by deduction, so it cannot be inconsistent; and the remark that the Dirac particle's skeleton has three points, which is where this set's particle papers begin.

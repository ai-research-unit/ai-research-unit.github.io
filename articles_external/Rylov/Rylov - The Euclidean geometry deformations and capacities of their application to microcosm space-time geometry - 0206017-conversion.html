What the Paper Does

The early, systematic statement of the geometric programme: take Euclidean geometry written entirely in terms of the world function, replace that function by another, and every proposition deforms with it. The resulting class — T-geometry — is much wider than the Riemannian class, and the paper argues that the extra width is exactly what microcosm physics needs. Three features are claimed as the method's advantage: definitions do not depend on the world function's form, the language needs no coordinates or curves, and no full axiomatics is required because the deformed Euclidean one is inherited. An asymmetric world function is then proposed, and with it the claim that the future need not be geometrically equivalent to the past.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What a Riemannian geometry is

"In fact, any Riemannian geometry is a result of some deformation of the Euclidean geometry. Class of these Riemannian deformations is restricted by a series of unfounded constraints. Eliminating these constraints, one obtains a more wide class of possible space-time geometries (T-geometries). Any T-geometry is described by the world function completely."

The three advantages

"s: (1) Any geometric object is defined in all T-geometries at once, because its definition does not depend on the form of world function. (2) Language of T-geometry does not use external means of description such as coordinates and curves; it uses only primordially geometrical concepts: subspaces and world function. (3) There is no necessity to construct the complete axiomatics of T-geometry, because it uses deformed Euclidean axiomatics, and one can investigate only interesting geometric relations.".

The constraints he says are unfounded

"The curve is considered conventionally to be a geometrical object (but not as a method of the geometry description), and separation of properties of geometry from properties imported by a use of the description in terms of curves is not considered usually. In particular, in the Riemannian geometry the absolute parallelism is absent, in general." Hence "T-geometry is free of this defect. The concept of a curve is not used at the T-geometry construction. There is an absolute parallelism in T-geometry."

And on continuity: "The continuity of space-time. This is a very fine property which cannot be tested by a direct experiment. T-geometry is insensitive to continuity, and it is free of this constraint." And on dimension: "The Riemannian geometry is a geometry with fixed dimension. It is very difficult to imagine a geometry with variable dimension in the scope of the Riemannian geometry. Such a problem is absent in T-geometry."

Nondegeneracy and mass

"The means of the Riemannian geometry description suppress such an important property of geometry as nondegeneracy. As a corollary the particle mass geometrization appears to be impossible in the framework of Riemannian geometry. Geometrization of the particle mass is important, when the mass of a particle is unknown and must be determined from some geometrical or physical relations. It may appear to be important for determination of the mass spectrum of elementary particles."

And the result claimed: "Consideration of nondegeneracy and geometrization of the particle mass have admitted one to make the important step in understanding of the microcosm space-time geometry. One succeeded in explanation of non-relativistic quantum effects as geometrical effects, generated by nondegeneracy of the space-time geometry."

The asymmetric function

"When the world function is symmetric and T-geometry is nondegenerate, the particle mass is geometrized, and nonrelativistic quantum effects are described as geometric ones, i.e. without a reference to principles of quantum theory. When world function is asymmetric, the future is not geometrically equivalent to the past, and capacities of T-geometry increase multiply."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. An asymmetric world function as an arrow of time

The idea. A construction: if $\sigma(P,Q)\neq\sigma(Q,P)$, the geometry itself distinguishes past from future, and no thermodynamic argument is needed for the asymmetry. He adds that the antisymmetric part "generates some metric fields, whose influence on geometry properties is especially strong in the microcosm."

What it would add. The corpus's causality article derives the light cone from the algebra's zero divisors and treats the two directions along a null line as interchangeable. An asymmetric distance would break that, and it would put the arrow of time in the geometry rather than in initial conditions — a strong position the corpus has not taken and can state without adopting it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/causality-and-the-light-cone-as-an-information-barrier-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. An asymmetric distance is not a metric in any usual sense, and no dynamics is constructed on it here; the claim that capacities increase is unsupported. Time-reversal symmetry is also tested to high precision in weak interactions being the only violator, so a geometric arrow at all scales would be visible.

2. Nondegeneracy as the precondition for mass

The idea. A claim: mass can be a property of the geometry only if the geometry is nondegenerate, and Riemannian methods suppress nondegeneracy, so Riemannian geometry cannot geometrise mass even in principle.

What it would add. The corpus's mass is a parameter of the algebra's representation, entering as a constant in the wave equation, and it never asks whether mass could be geometric. This is a statement of what a geometric mass would require — a property the metric does not have — which is a constraint on any such attempt, including the corpus's if it ever makes one.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-parameter-count-as-the-measure-of-the-frameworks-boundary.md.

What stands in the way. "Nondegeneracy" is defined inside this programme and has no counterpart elsewhere, so the claim is not comparable with other approaches. Nothing here computes a mass, so the precondition is stated and never used.

3. A language without curves or coordinates

The idea. A method: define every object through the world function alone, so that definitions survive any change of the function, and geometry is separated from the means of its description.

What it would add. The corpus is coordinate-based throughout — its algebra carries an explicit frame $(i r'_t,r_x,r_y,r_z)$ — and the curved-spacetime article notes that frames are what carry curvature. A function-only language would remove the frame, and the article's own question about what is invariant would have a sharper answer. It is also the honest limit of the corpus's method, since the algebra is pointwise and the world function is not.

Where it lands. articles_physics/curved-spacetime-and-the-biquaternion-framework.md.

What stands in the way. The claim that a language without coordinates is more fundamental is a claim about convenience unless it produces a result coordinates cannot. The paper's results are reformulations, and the one quantitative claim — mass geometrisation — is not carried out here.

The rank: the asymmetric world function first, because it is a distinctive proposal; nondegeneracy second, because it names a precondition; the coordinate-free language last, because its advantage is asserted.

The remaining items in one line each: the deformation procedure, which is the same construction later papers call $\sigma$-immanence and which 0504031 sets out with a concrete distortion function; the list of seven unfounded Riemannian constraints in the conclusion, which is the most complete statement of the programme's grievances; and the observation that T-geometry requires no complete axiomatics, which is the same economy argument as in 0806.1716.

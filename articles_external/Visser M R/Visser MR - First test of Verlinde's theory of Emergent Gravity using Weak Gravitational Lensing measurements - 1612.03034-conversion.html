What the Paper Does

Measures the excess surface density around isolated central galaxies in the overlap of the KiDS lensing survey and the GAMA spectroscopic survey, $\sim180\ \deg^2$, and compares it with the apparent dark matter profile predicted by Verlinde's emergent gravity. The prediction contains no free parameter, only the measured baryonic mass and the Hubble constant, and is valid for static, spherically symmetric, isolated masses. The measured profiles agree with the prediction in slope and amplitude across four stellar-mass bins, with a fit quality comparable to an NFW halo with one free mass per bin.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The sample and the regime of validity

From the KiDS–GAMA overlap the authors select $33{,}613$ central galaxies that dominate their surrounding mass distribution and have no other central within the maximum measurement radius, $R_{\max}=3\,h_{70}^{-1}$ Mpc, with a minimum radius $R_{\min}=30\,h_{70}^{-1}$ kpc. The prediction is "currently only valid for static, spherically symmetric and isolated mass distributions", so this selection is what makes the test legitimate.

The predicted apparent dark matter

For a point mass with enclosed baryonic mass $M_b$, the apparent dark matter profile is

$$M_D(r)=\sqrt{\frac{cH_0}{6G}}\;r\sqrt{M_b}\equiv C_D\,r\sqrt{M_b}, \qquad (paper\ 19)$$

$$\rho_D(r)=\frac{1}{4\pi r^2}\frac{dM_D(r)}{dr}=\frac{C_DM_b}{4\pi r^2}, \qquad (paper\ 20)$$

with $C_D$ fixed by $c$, $G$ and $H_0$; the surface density follows by line-of-sight integration, $\Sigma_D(R)=\int \rho_D(R,z)\,dz$ (paper 21). No parameter is fitted: the amplitude and the slope are both fixed once $M_b$ and $H_0$ are given. The authors assume $H_0=70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$, a constant $H(z)$ over $0.2<z_s<0.9$, and that lensing by the extra component follows the standard formalism.

The result

The measured excess surface density agrees with the prediction "in four different stellar mass bins", and the agreement is stated as remarkable because the prediction has no free parameters. The reduced $\chi^2$ of the point-mass EG prediction is $\chi^2_{\text{red,EG}}=1.121$ against $\chi^2_{\text{red,NFW}}=0.933$ for an NFW fit with four free parameters in total; by the Bayesian information criterion the EG prediction does at least as well, $\mathrm{BIC_{EG}}=44.82$ against $\mathrm{BIC_{NFW}}=48.33$.

What is and is not concluded

The authors state that a fair comparison with $\Lambda$CDM would need a more sophisticated implementation of both models and a full Bayesian analysis, and that future data should be able to separate them. They also note the coincidence that in the point-mass regime the EG lensing profile equals that of MOND for the specific value $a_0=cH_0/6$.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A prediction with no free parameter from a horizon scale

The idea. The excess gravity around a baryon mass is written as $\sqrt{cH_0/6G}\,r\sqrt{M_b}$: the size of the effect is set by the cosmic horizon, not by a new mass scale or a fitted coupling. This is a number with a status — a parameter-free prediction, tested against lensing.

What it would add. The corpus's cosmology article carries an FLRW metric with an imported source and no mechanism tying a local effect to the horizon scale; the corpus has no relation in which $H_0$ sets the amplitude of a gravitational anomaly.

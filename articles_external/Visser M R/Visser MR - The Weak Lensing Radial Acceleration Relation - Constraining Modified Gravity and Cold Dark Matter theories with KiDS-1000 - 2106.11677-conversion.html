What the Paper Does

Measures the radial acceleration relation of isolated galaxies with galaxy–galaxy lensing from the KiDS-1000 data release, extending the relation by nearly two orders of magnitude in observed acceleration compared with earlier rotation-curve measurements. The measured excess surface density is converted into observed acceleration $g_{\text{obs}}$ and the photometric masses into $g_{\text{bar}}$, and the resulting curve is compared with MOND, with emergent gravity, and with two cold-dark-matter simulations. At low acceleration the data follow $g_{\text{obs}}\propto\sqrt{g_{\text{bar}}}$, as both modified-gravity models predict.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The measurement

Galaxy–galaxy lensing gives the excess surface density $\Delta\Sigma(R)$, converted into $g_{\text{obs}}$, with $g_{\text{bar}}$ from nine-band KiDS+VIKING photometry. The probe spans $0.03<R<3\,h_{70}^{-1}$ Mpc, and at the scale of observable galaxy outskirts, $R\approx30\,h_{70}^{-1}$ kpc, "our lensing results are in excellent agreement with the SPARC rotation curves", two independent methods agreeing.

The low-acceleration law

"At the low accelerations corresponding to GGL scales, the lensing RAR of isolated galaxies approximately follows a $g_{\text{obs}}\propto\sqrt{g_{\text{bar}}}$ relation", which is "in agreement with the expectations from EG (Eq. 17) and MOND (which we take to be the M16 fitting function, Eq. 11, extrapolated to larger scales)".

The comparison

Both modified-gravity models and both dark-matter simulations are confronted with the same curve. The paper's conclusions are stated as a list; the central one is that the relation persists, with the same slope, far outside the visible disc, and that at low acceleration the two modified-gravity predictions coincide with the data.

The scope

The paper does not claim to distinguish the models. It reports the measured relation, the agreement of two independent probes, and the consistency of the low-acceleration slope with the two modified-gravity predictions; the discrimination between them is left to the discussion.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A single relation between two accelerations

The idea. The whole phenomenology is compressed into one function $g_{\text{obs}}(g_{\text{bar}})$, measured over two decades, with the low-acceleration branch $\sqrt{g_{\text{bar}}}$ and a high-acceleration branch approaching $g_{\text{obs}}=g_{\text{bar}}$. This is a number relation, and its virtue is that it is theory-neutral.

The idea. Lensing and rotation curves, methods with different systematics, give the same acceleration at the overlap scale. This is a reading of the evidence, and it is stronger than either measurement alone.

3. The same prediction from two different theories

The idea. Emergent gravity and MOND give the same low-acceleration slope from unrelated starting points, which is why the data cannot separate them. This is a reading with a warning: agreement with a prediction is not evidence for the theory that made it when another theory makes the same prediction.

What it would add. The corpus's claims of agreement with established physics need exactly this caution applied to them: where two frameworks predict the same number, the agreement does not discriminate.

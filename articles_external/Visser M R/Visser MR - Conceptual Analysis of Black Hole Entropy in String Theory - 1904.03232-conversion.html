What the Paper Does

Reconstructs the Strominger–Vafa 1996 count of the microstates of the extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole in a form a philosopher can audit, and factorises the argument into its inter-theoretic parts. The parts are a conjectured open–closed string duality, linkage relations between four theories — supergravity, string theory with D-branes, the D-brane worldvolume theory, and M-theory — as the probe distance and the string coupling are varied, and supersymmetry, which guarantees that the entropy is invariant under those variations. The paper then assesses the argument's weaknesses, its generalisations, its relation to AdS/CFT, and its heuristic role for the information paradox. It contains no new calculation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The factorisation

The argument is split into four theories: (1) supergravity, in which the black hole is a solution; (2) string theory with D-branes; (3) the theory of the D-branes alone; (4) the conjectured M-theory. The relations are "a conjectured duality between (1) and (2), and linkage relations among the others, as we vary two quantities: namely, a distance scale at which the system of interest is probed, and the string coupling constant."

The count

"First, it presents a calculation of the number of microstates of a system of D-branes as in (3), which are weakly interacting and which are probed at short distance scales. The calculation is then extrapolated and compared to the Bekenstein-Hawking formula for the corresponding black hole in supergravity (1), which is probed at large distances, and where the string coupling is strong (both calculations take place at low energies)."

What carries the extrapolation

Two assumptions: the open–closed duality, which identifies (1) and (3) via (2) as "the same theories, for different ranges of the relevant quantities"; and supersymmetry, which "further justifies the comparison of their entropy at different values of the relevant quantities ... because the entropy is guaranteed to be invariant under suitable changes of distance scale and coupling". The black hole is extremal, and extremality is what supplies the invariance.

The weaknesses

The authors name three: the dependence on supersymmetry, hence on extremality; the use of five dimensions; and the approximations. Each is discussed rather than dismissed, and the generalisations to near-extremal, spinning and four-dimensional holes are presented as what strengthened the result's physical significance.

The aftermath

The calculation is read as instrumental in the formulation of the AdS/CFT conjecture, and as reinterpreted by it; the authors also discuss the heuristic role the count played for the information paradox, where it "suggested to many that black hole evolution should be a unitary process".

The idea. A microscopic count of $S=A/4G$ is not one calculation but a chain: weak-coupling D-brane states, a duality, a linkage across coupling and distance, and an invariance argument. This is a reading of what a microstate count must consist of.

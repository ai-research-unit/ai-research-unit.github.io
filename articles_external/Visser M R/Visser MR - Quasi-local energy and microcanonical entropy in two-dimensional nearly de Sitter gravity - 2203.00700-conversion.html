What the Paper Does

Extends the Brown–York quasilocal formalism to two-dimensional de Sitter space in Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity with conformal matter, by placing a timelike boundary in the static patch so that conserved charges, including a quasilocal energy, are well defined. The boundary splits the static patch into a cosmological system and a black-hole system: the first is unstable under thermal fluctuations, the second is stable. A semiclassical quasilocal first law is derived in which the Gibbons–Hawking entropy is replaced by the generalized entropy, the replacement following from the fact that in two dimensions the semiclassical Wald entropy equals the generalized entropy exactly.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The construction

"We extend the quasilocal formalism of Brown and York to dS$_2$, where a timelike boundary is introduced in the static patch to uniquely define conserved charges, including quasi-local energy." Without the boundary, energy in de Sitter is not a well-defined conserved quantity, and the thermodynamics has no energy variable.

The two systems

"The boundary divides the static patch into two systems, a cosmological system and a black hole system, the former being unstable under thermal fluctuations while the latter is stable." The instability is not a defect but a result: the cosmological horizon's heat capacity is negative.

The modified first law

"A semi-classical quasi-local first law is derived, where the Gibbons–Hawking entropy is replaced by" the generalized entropy. The mechanism is stated: "Crucial to this extension was the observation that in two dimensions the semi-classical Wald entropy is exactly equal to the generalized entropy, where the semi-classical contribution arises from expressing the Polyakov action in a localized form."

The generality of the replacement

"Including semi-classical backreaction, the first law of horizon thermodynamics was modified such that the classical entropy is replaced by the generalized entropy. This is expected to be a feature for systems which include backreaction; indeed, the same modification appears in the three-dimensional semi-classical Schwarzschild-de Sitter black hole."

The scope

Two dimensions, JT gravity, conformal matter, a static patch. The authors say the modification is "expected to be a feature" more generally, and cite the three-dimensional check, but do not claim a general derivation.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Entropy is replaced, not corrected

The idea. With backreaction included, the first law is not the classical law plus a correction term; the classical entropy is replaced by the generalized entropy throughout. This is a reading of what "including quantum corrections" means.

What it would add. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md presents the four laws with the area entropy and, separately, a von Neumann functional it cannot connect. The replacement reading says there is only one entropy and the classical one is its leading term, so that the corpus's two objects are the same object at different orders. That is a sharper claim than the article currently makes, and it is testable against the article's own numbers.

Where it lands. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, the gaps section.

What stands in the way. The replacement is established in two and three dimensions with conformal matter; in four dimensions with realistic matter it is a conjecture. The cost is that the corpus would be claiming a general structure on two low-dimensional examples.

2. A timelike boundary makes energy well defined

The idea. Quasilocal energy needs a boundary; without one, the conserved charge in a de Sitter static patch does not exist. This is a mechanism, and it explains why de Sitter thermodynamics has been ambiguous.

What it would add. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md has an FLRW metric and no energy. The observation that a timelike boundary is what makes energy definable is directly relevant to a cosmological article that has a metric but no conserved quantity.

Where it lands. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md.

What stands in the way. The construction is two-dimensional and static-patch; an FLRW universe has no timelike boundary of that kind. Ranked second.

3. Two systems from one patch, one stable and one not

The idea. A single static patch, split by a boundary, gives two thermodynamic systems with opposite stability. This is a number claim with a structural cause.

What it would add. The corpus has no notion of competing thermodynamic subsystems within one geometry; the article on cosmology treats one universe. A worked case where the same region yields two systems whose stabilities differ by sign is a template the corpus's de Sitter statements would need.

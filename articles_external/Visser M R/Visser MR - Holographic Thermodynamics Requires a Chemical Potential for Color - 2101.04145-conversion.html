What the Paper Does

Derives the thermodynamic Euler equation for high-energy states of large-$N$ gauge theories from the dependence of the extensive quantities on $N$, and shows that the result carries a chemical potential for the number of colours. That Euler equation is dual to the generalised Smarr formula for black holes with a negative cosmological constant, so the "extended" thermodynamics of black holes is a field-theory statement, not a gravitational one. The energy of the field theory is the internal energy and not an enthalpy, and no pressure–volume term appears.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Euler equation

From the large-$N$ scaling of the extensive quantities, and differentiating with respect to the scaling parameter at $\alpha=1$, the author obtains

$$E=TS+\nu_iB_i+\mu C, \qquad (paper\ 2,\ 6)$$

where $\nu_i$ are chemical potentials for conserved quantities $B_i$ such as charge and angular momentum, $\mu$ is the chemical potential for the central charge, and $C\sim L^{d-1}/G$ is the number of degrees of freedom. "Notice that pressure and volume do not appear in this Euler equation, since the volume does not generically scale with $C$", the exception being the infinite-volume limit.

The dictionary

With the bulk quantities identified as $E=M$, $T=\kappa/2\pi$, $S=A/4G$ (paper 1), and with $C\sim L^{d-1}/G$, the boundary Euler equation is dual to the generalised Smarr formula

$$M=\frac{d-1}{d-2}\frac{\kappa A}{8\pi G}-\frac{1}{d-2}\frac{\Theta\Lambda}{4\pi G} \qquad (paper\ \text{Sec. I})$$

written here in $d+1$ spacetime dimensions. The $\Lambda$ term is reproduced by the variation of $C$, through $\partial E/\partial C$, and not by a bulk pressure.

The disagreement with the pressure interpretation

"It was argued in [20] that varying $\Lambda$ in the bulk does not only correspond to varying $C$ (or $N$), but also to varying the volume $V$ of the spatial boundary geometry." The author shows that for a different choice of boundary metric, varying $\Lambda$ corresponds to varying $C$ and $E$ only, not $V$; so the pressure reading is metric dependent and the chemical-potential reading is not.

What follows for the extended first law

The author states the consequence: "the extended first law of black holes cannot be solely written in terms of the variation of bulk pressure, $P=-\Lambda/8\pi G$, if both $\Lambda$ and $G$ are allowed to vary, but can be consistently interpreted as a field theory first law." Also, $M$ is the internal energy of the field theory, whereas in the pressure interpretation it is an enthalpy.

The generality claimed

The dictionary for $\mu$ is stated for Lifshitz as well as relativistic cases, with the rescaled variables $E\to ER^z$, $T\to TR^z$, $\tilde Q=QL$ chosen so that the products are Lifshitz scale invariant, and the Smarr formula is recovered for that case as well.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A chemical potential for the number of degrees of freedom

The idea. The number of colours is an extensive variable with its own conjugate potential, and the Euler relation is incomplete without it. This is a formula: one more term, and a definition of what it multiplies.

What it would add. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md derives Smarr by scaling and states the cosmological-constant correction as imported, with open question 4 asking whether the thermodynamic volume is meaningful. This paper answers that the conjugate of $\Lambda$ is not a volume at all but the count of degrees of freedom, and that the corpus's question has been posed in the wrong variable.

Where it lands. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, open question 4, and its Smarr section.

What stands in the way. The derivation uses $C\sim L^{d-1}/G$ and a large-$N$ gauge theory; the corpus has no $N$, no gauge group of that kind and no $G$. The cost is the whole holographic dictionary, but the form of the answer — a chemical potential for a count — is importable as a warning.

2. Energy is internal energy, not enthalpy

The idea. Whether $M$ is an energy or an enthalpy depends on which variable is varied, and the field-theory reading fixes it as the internal energy. This is a reading that resolves an ambiguity the thermodynamic literature leaves open.

What it would add. The corpus's articles_physics/energy-and-information-four-conservation-laws-from-one-identity.md identifies energy with a trace and mass with a norm, and its black-hole article identifies $M$ with the mass of the hole. Neither says which thermodynamic potential the mass plays. This paper gives a criterion.

Where it lands. articles_physics/energy-and-information-four-conservation-laws-from-one-identity.md.

What stands in the way. The criterion is holographic; in a framework with no boundary field theory the answer would have to come from somewhere else. Ranked second.

3. The Euler relation as the carrier of the physics

The idea. Of all the thermodynamic relations, the Euler equation is the one that contains the essential information, because it determines the energy as a function of the other variables; the first law alone does not. This is a reading of which equation matters.

What it would add. The corpus's black-hole article derives Smarr by scaling and treats it as a check on the first law. This paper's emphasis reverses the order of importance: the homogeneous relation is primary and the first law is its differential.

Where it lands. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, the Smarr section.

What stands in the way. Nothing blocks the import; it is a change of emphasis within a derivation the corpus already has. Ranked third.

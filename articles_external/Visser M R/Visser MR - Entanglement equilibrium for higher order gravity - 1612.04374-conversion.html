What the Paper Does

Extends Jacobson's derivation of the Einstein equation from maximal vacuum entanglement to theories with higher derivative corrections. The linearized higher derivative equations are shown to be equivalent to the vanishing of the first variation of entanglement entropy for small spherical regions, at fixed generalized volume, in a maximally symmetric background. The correction terms are the subleading divergences of the entanglement entropy, a Wald entropy evaluated on the entangling surface. The generalized volume is a new geometric functional, and the authors note that the fully nonlinear equations cannot be recovered from the linearized ones applied to small balls, in contrast with Einstein gravity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The generalized entropy

The divergences of entanglement entropy are absorbed into renormalised gravitational couplings, so that

$$S_{\text{gen}}=S^{(\text{ren})}_{\text{Wald}}+S^{(\text{ren})}_{\text{mat}}, \qquad (paper\ 2)$$

is independent of the cutoff $\epsilon$. The Wald term plays the role of "a placeholder for the UV degrees of freedom that have been integrated out".

The generalized volume

The variations are taken at fixed

$$W=\frac{1}{(d-2)E_0}\int_\Sigma \eta\,E^{abcd}u_a h_{bc}u_d-E_0, \qquad (paper\ 5)$$

where $E^{abcd}=\partial\mathcal L/\partial R_{abcd}$, $E_0$ is the value of $E^{abcd}$ on the maximally symmetric background, $E^{abcd}=E_0(g^{ac}g^{bd}-g^{ad}g^{bc})$, and $\eta,u^a,h_{ab}$ are the volume element, unit normal and induced metric of the ball's boundary $\Sigma$. The normalisation is chosen "so that $W$ reduces to the volume in the case of Einstein gravity". The Jacobson–Kang–Myers ambiguity shifts both the Wald entropy and $W$ by surface terms, $S_{\text{JKM}}$ and $W_{\text{JKM}}$, and the identity holds with $W'=W+W_{\text{JKM}}$ (paper 6).

The variational identity

For causal diamonds in a maximally symmetric spacetime the variation of the Wald entropy, the matter Hamiltonian and the linearized constraint are related off shell by

$$\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int_\Sigma \delta S_{\text{Wald}}\Big|_W+\delta H_{\zeta m}=\delta C_\zeta. \qquad (paper\ 40)$$

Imposing $\delta C_\zeta=0$ turns this into the first law of causal diamond mechanics for higher derivative gravity, $-\delta H_{\zeta m}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int_\Sigma\delta S_{\text{Wald}}\big|_W$, which reproduces the Einstein-gravity statement for $\mathcal L=R/16\pi G$ with $E_0=1/32\pi G$.

The main result

With minimally coupled conformally invariant matter, the equilibrium condition reads

$$\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int_\Sigma \delta S_{EE}\Big|_{W'}=\delta C_\zeta, \qquad (paper\ 55)$$

so that the linearized constraint equations are the statement that entanglement entropy is extremal in the vacuum at fixed $W'$. Conversely, postulating maximal vacuum entanglement for all balls and all frames yields the linearized higher derivative equations everywhere.

The limit of the argument

The authors state explicitly that in higher curvature gravity the fully nonlinear equations do not follow from the linearized ones on small balls, because the higher-order terms contribute at the same order as the terms that would have to be dropped. This is the point where the argument that works for Einstein gravity stops.

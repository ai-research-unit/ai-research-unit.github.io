What the Paper Does

Builds a systematic framework for equilibrium thermodynamics in terms of thermodynamic representations: a choice of thermodynamic potential together with one independent variable from each conjugate pair. In each representation the equations of state are the first derivatives of the potential and the response functions are its second derivatives. The framework is applied to ideal gases and to a Schwarzschild black hole in a spherical cavity, in several representations, treating the cavity area and its conjugate surface pressure as the thermodynamic volume and pressure of a holographically dual system. Stability turns out to depend on the representation, and the paper reports a set of concrete results: the large black hole is thermally stable at fixed volume but mechanically unstable under isothermal compression; it is mechanically stable under adiabatic compression everywhere; at fixed pressure it is thermally unstable throughout the physical state space; the thermal expansion coefficient is negative everywhere; and isenthalpic expansion always cools the black hole.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The framework

"thermodynamic representations, which are choices of thermodynamic potential and independent variables, one from each conjugate pair. In each representation, equations of state arise from first derivatives of the potential and response functions from its second derivatives."

The key structural claim

"A non-trivial thermal-mechanical response theory therefore requires an independent mechanical degree of freedom in addition to the entropy. Standard Schwarzschild black hole thermodynamics does not provide such a degree of freedom, since it has no independently variable thermodynamic volume. Extended black hole thermodynamics introduces a pressure by varying the cosmological constant, but for static black holes the thermodynamic volume is fixed by the entropy, so the entropy and volume do not constitute independent" variables. This is why the cavity is needed: only a finite boundary supplies the missing degree of freedom.

The stability results

"The large black hole branch is thermally stable at fixed volume but mechanically unstable under isothermal compression, while the system is mechanically stable under adiabatic compression everywhere. At fixed pressure, the black hole is thermally unstable throughout the physical state space. We also find that the thermal expansion coefficient is negative everywhere and show that isenthalpic expansion always cools the black hole."

Why representation matters

"stability depends on the thermodynamic representation, or equivalently on which variables are held fixed under equilibrium perturbations." Stability is therefore not a property of the black hole alone; it is a property of the black hole together with a choice of what is held fixed.

The scope

Quasi-local thermodynamics of a Schwarzschild hole in a cavity. The cavity is the holographic boundary, so the results are stated for the dual system as well as the black hole, and the framework is offered for "a wide class of black hole systems".

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Stability is not a property of the object

The idea. Whether a horizon is stable depends on the ensemble — on which variables are held fixed — so "the black hole is unstable" is incomplete without saying at fixed what.

What it would add. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md computes a specific heat, finds it negative, and concludes the Schwarzschild hole is unstable — with no ensemble specified. The correction is that the same hole is stable in one representation and unstable in another, and the article's conclusion is therefore missing a qualifier that changes its content.

2. A response theory needs two independent variables

The idea. Without an independent mechanical variable, there are no non-trivial response functions: second derivatives collapse and the thermal-mechanical cross terms vanish.

What it would add. This is a counting argument with teeth. It says the Schwarzschild hole in asymptotically flat space has a degenerate thermodynamics, and that any framework claiming a full response theory must exhibit two independent variables.

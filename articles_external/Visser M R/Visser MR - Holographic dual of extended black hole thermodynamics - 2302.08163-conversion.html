What the Paper Does

Solves a long-standing ambiguity in the holographic reading of extended black-hole thermodynamics. If the bulk cosmological constant is varied and the CFT's spatial curvature radius is identified with the bulk AdS radius, then the variation of $C$ and of the CFT volume $V$ are not independent, and the CFT first law is degenerate. The authors keep the conformal factor $\omega$ of the boundary metric as an independent thermodynamic parameter, which makes $V$ and $C$ independent variables, and write a CFT first law exactly dual to the bulk extended first law with variable $\Lambda$ and fixed $G_N$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The problem

"The situation is not that simple, however, as it is also standard to identify the curvature radius of the spatial geometry on which the CFT is formulated with the AdS radius $L$." The boundary metric is $ds^2=\omega^2(-dt^2+L^2d\Omega^2_{k,d-2})$ with $\omega$ "an 'arbitrary' dimensionless conformal factor ... that reflects the conformal symmetry of the boundary theory" (paper 5). With $\omega=1$ fixed, "a variation of the cosmological constant in the bulk induces also a variation of the CFT volume $V$. Hence a pressure-volume work term, $-p\delta V$, should be present on the CFT side. This implies that either (i) the corresponding CFT first law is degenerate as the $\mu\delta C$ and $-p\delta V$ terms are not truly independent ... or (ii) apart from varying the cosmological constant we also have to vary Newton's gravitational constant."

The solution

"in what follows we shall treat $\omega$ as a (dimensionless) thermodynamic parameter (similar to the horizon radius or AdS radius), rather than a function of the boundary coordinates. This will make the volume and central charge independent variables."

The resulting first law

$$\delta\tilde E=\tilde T\delta S+\tilde\Omega\delta J+\tilde\Phi\delta\tilde Q+\mu\,\delta C-p\,\delta V, \qquad (paper\ 9)$$

with

$$\mu=\frac{1}{C}\big(\tilde E-\tilde T S-\tilde\Omega J-\tilde\Phi\tilde Q\big),\qquad p=\frac{\tilde E}{(d-2)V}. \qquad (paper\ 10,\ 11)$$

"The CFT first law (9) is no longer degenerate, as both $V$ and $C$ can now be independently varied. Together with relations (10) and (11), it is exactly dual to the first law of extended black hole thermodynamics."

What this clarifies

"The variation of $\Lambda$ (the $V\delta P$ term in (2)) has thus been split into several pieces and is related to the variation of the volume, electric charge and central charge of the CFT." And the missing $-pV$ term in the Euler relation "is not an issue ... but rather a feature of holographic CFTs": at high temperature, $\mu C\to-pV$ and the energy becomes extensive.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A conformal factor as a thermodynamic variable

The idea. The overall scale of the boundary metric is not a gauge choice to be fixed but a thermodynamic parameter whose variation is conjugate to something. This is a mechanism, and it dissolves an apparent degeneracy.

What it would add. The corpus's articles_physics/conformal-invariance-in-biquaternionic-form.md treats conformal invariance as a symmetry of the equations. Promoting the conformal factor to a thermodynamic variable is a use of that symmetry the corpus has not made, and it is a general technique: any symmetry with a free scale can donate a thermodynamic axis.

Where it lands. articles_physics/conformal-invariance-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The construction needs the AdS boundary, the central charge and the holographic dictionary. The cost is that the corpus's conformal structure is on a finite algebra with no boundary.

2. Degeneracy of a first law as a diagnostic

The idea. If two terms in a first law are not independent, the formulation is wrong somewhere; the degeneracy is a signal, not a nuisance. This is a reading.

What it would add. The corpus's articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md writes first laws with several terms and never checks independence. A criterion for when a thermodynamic description is over- or under-determined is exactly the sort of check the article's derivations lack.

Where it lands. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, the first-law derivation.

What stands in the way. Nothing blocks the import; it is a consistency criterion. Ranked first, because it applies directly and costs nothing.

3. An Euler relation without a $pV$ term

The idea. On a compact space the internal energy is not extensive, so $pV$ is absent from the Euler relation at finite temperature, appearing only in the large-volume limit.

What it would add. The corpus's entropy and energy functionals are defined on compact algebras with no notion of extensivity. The statement that extensivity is a large-volume limit, not a definition, would clarify what the corpus's traces and norms are measuring.

What stands in the way. It is a statement about holographic CFTs on spheres; the corpus has no volume. Ranked second.

What the Paper Does

Computes two-point functions of a massive free scalar in de Sitter space with the heat-kernel formalism, for two operators placed in conjugate static patches, and derives a geodesic approximation valid for large mass and late times. The approximation is a sum over complex conjugate geodesics, and the result decays exponentially in time — the timelike part of the complex geodesic supplies the decay. The generalisation is of the geodesic approximation known in AdS/CFT to the cosmological case, and it is motivated by a version of the information paradox in de Sitter space.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"The main result of this work was to show that a two-point correlation function of massive scalar fields in de Sitter space can be computed using a geodesic approximation. This generalizes similar approximation schemes studied in AdS/CFT ... to the cosmological, de Sitter, context. We demonstrated that the geodesic approximation in de Sitter space, for large masses and late times, involves complex geodesics, generalizing earlier work ... to higher dimensions."

The mechanism of the decay

"Looking at the final result for the correlator we note that it is exponentially decaying in time, corresponding to the timelike part of the complex geodesic." The decay is not imposed; it is what the complex geodesic's real-time extent produces.

What is and is not an observable

"In contrast to anti-de Sitter space, where the boundary CFT correlation functions are well-defined objects in quantum gravity, the absence of asymptotic regions (where gravity decouples) in de Sitter space implies that a correlation function is not a gauge-invariant observable, since it transforms non-trivially under" diffeomorphisms. The correlator is therefore computed in a fixed gauge and is not by itself an observable — the authors state this before drawing any conclusion from it.

The motivation

The correlator "was the main motivation for this work" in connection with "a version of the information paradox in de Sitter space". The connection is argued, not demonstrated: the exponential decay is what the paradox requires, and the paper supplies the computation rather than the paradox's resolution.

The scope

A free massive scalar, two conjugate static patches, large mass, late times. The geodesic approximation's validity is limited to that regime, and the authors say so.

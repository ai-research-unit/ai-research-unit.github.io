What the Paper Does

Computes the decay probability of charged black holes in de Sitter space with a tunneling method, in the s-wave sector, including backreaction. The probability is $P\sim\exp(\Delta S_b)$, the exponential of the change in the black-hole entropy. The Festina Lente bound — the requirement that charged Nariai black holes decay without producing a big crunch — corresponds to $\Delta S_b\le-1$ in the Nariai and probe limits; with backreaction included, the authors find unsuppressed channels that violate the bound without producing a crunch for every observer. The paper thereby sharpens what the bound does and does not require.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"Using a tunneling approach we show that the decay probability of charged black holes in de Sitter space in the s-wave sector is $P\sim\exp(\Delta S_b)$, where $\Delta S_b$ is the change in the black hole entropy. We find that the FL bound corresponds to $\Delta S_b\le-1$ in the Nariai and probe limit. However, taking into account backreaction we identify unsuppressed decay channels, which might be subdominant, that violate this bound but nonetheless do not result in a big crunch for every observer."

The classification of decays

"Charged decays come in a few flavors: suppressed decays that obey $\Delta S_b\le-C$ with $C\ge1$ and unsuppressed decays that obey $\Delta S_b>-C$. We make a further distinction between decays that are unsuppressed, but not superradiant ($-C<\Delta S_b\le0$) and superradiant decays ($\Delta S_b>0$)."

What backreaction changes

"By studying the decay of charged Nariai black holes we found that, when backreaction is included, not all superradiant decays lead to naked singularities. Moreover, in the near-horizon limit it is appropriate to describe decays in terms of a horizon energy parameter $m_s$. When this parameter is real, the superradiant regime is completely decoupled such that for decays obeying $m_s^2\ge0$ there is no danger of exiting the sharkfin."

Why the bound survives anyway

The unsuppressed channels "might be subdominant", so the bound can still hold as a statement about the dominant decay. The authors present the finding as a refinement of the bound's status: it is not that every decay obeys $\Delta S_b\le-1$, but that the ones which matter for the singularity argument do.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The decay rate is the exponential of an entropy difference

The idea. $P\sim e^{\Delta S}$ for a black hole's emission, with backreaction included, and the sign of $\Delta S$ classifies the channel into suppressed, unsuppressed, and superradiant. This is a formula with a classification attached.

What it would add. articles_physics/hawking-radiation-in-biquaternionic-form.md derives a thermal spectrum with a temperature; it has no statement that a decay probability is the exponential of an entropy difference, and no classification of channels by $\Delta S$. The formula is more general than a thermal spectrum — it holds for a single emission event, not an ensemble.

Where it lands. articles_physics/hawking-radiation-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. It is derived in a tunneling approximation for charged, spherically symmetric emission; the corpus's radiation is derived modularly. The two derivations would have to be reconciled, and nothing in the corpus does that.

2. Superradiance as $\Delta S>0$

The idea. A decay is superradiant exactly when the black hole's entropy increases, which is a thermodynamic characterisation of an effect usually described kinematically.

What it would add. The corpus has no superradiance article. This gives a criterion for it in terms of the entropy alone, which is the sort of reduction of a kinematic effect to a thermodynamic one that the corpus's articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md would find natural.

Where it lands. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, the second law paragraph.

What stands in the way. Superradiance requires rotation or charge and a wave zone; the corpus has neither. Ranked third.

3. A swampland bound from a horizon argument

The idea. The Festina Lente bound follows from requiring that a Nariai black hole decay without a crunch, which is a statement about the space of allowed effective theories obtained from a horizon geometry.

What the Paper Does

Constructs families of charged black holes in $d+2$ dimensions whose asymptotic geometry is hyperscaling violating, with arbitrary Lifshitz exponent $z$ and hyperscaling violation parameter $\theta$, and with spherical or hyperbolic horizon topology. The solutions require a generalised Einstein–Maxwell–dilaton theory plus an extra vector field supporting the horizon curvature. The hyperbolic branch is consistent with the null energy condition only for $1\le z<2$ with $\theta=d(z-1)$, and the extended thermodynamics shows phase transitions only for spherical horizons with $1\le z\le2$. The critical exponents are those of a Van der Waals liquid.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The solutions

Two related families are given: charged hyperscaling violating black holes with spherical horizons in a generalised EMD theory — "a natural extension of the planar hyperscaling violating black branes ... but also a generalization of the pure Lifshitz spherical black holes ... to arbitrary hyperscaling violating parameter $\theta$" — and a second family obtained by a subtle limit of the dilaton, giving spherical, planar and hyperbolic horizons for $1\le z<2$ provided $\theta=d(z-1)$. The value $\theta=d(z-1)$ "seems singular in the former model", but the limit is shown to be physically well behaved.

The energy condition

The hyperbolic solutions survive the null energy condition only in the window $1\le z<2$ with $\theta=d(z-1)$, "which explains why they are absent in the pure Lifshitz case" ($\theta=0$).

The thermodynamics

Both the fixed-charge and the fixed-potential ensembles are treated, with the extended $(P,V)$ thermodynamics of the cosmological constant included. The finding: "only the spherical black hole exhibits phase transitions", and they occur for $1\le z\le2$, "as was already established for $\theta=0$, but now extended to other values of $\theta$". The qualitative behaviour of the transitions does not depend on $d$ or $\theta$, although most thermodynamic quantities depend on all parameters; the authors note the intuition that $\theta$ "effectively only reduces the dimensionality of the system, i.e. $d_{\text{eff}}=d-\theta$", while adding that on the detailed computations "this seems a fairly non-trivial outcome".

The critical exponents

Four critical exponents are computed, analytically for one analogy and numerically for another, and the exponents at the critical point "are shown to be the same for both analogies as the critical exponents for a Van der Waals liquid", consistent with mean-field universality.

1. Horizon topology as a thermodynamic selector

The idea. Of the three horizon topologies, only the spherical one gives phase transitions; the planar and hyperbolic cases do not. This is a number with a structural cause: topology selects whether the free energy has two branches to compare.

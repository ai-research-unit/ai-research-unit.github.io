What the Paper Does

Studies gravity theories whose Lagrangian is an arbitrary function of the Riemann tensor and the metric, with no derivatives of the curvature, in arbitrary dimension $D$. Using a fast linearization on a maximally symmetric background, it classifies such theories by the modes they propagate — massless graviton, massive graviton, scalar — and constructs the quartic generalisation of Einsteinian cubic gravity. It proves that the most general dimension-independent cubic theory without a ghost-like spin-2 mode is a combination of $f(\text{Lovelock})$ invariants, the Einsteinian cubic term and a further "new ghost-free" term. It then computes the generalised Newton potential, the post-Newtonian parameter $\gamma$, and the gravitational power radiated by sources, and extends Wald's formalism with explicit expressions.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The class of theories

$$S=\int_M d^Dx\,\sqrt{|g|}\,\Big[\mathcal L(R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},g^{\alpha\beta})+\mathcal L_{\text{matter}}\Big], \qquad (paper\ 1.1)$$

with $\mathcal L$ an arbitrary scalar function of the Riemann tensor and the metric, no covariant derivatives of $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ and no independent connection. The authors call these $L(\text{Riemann})$ gravities.

The linearized spectrum

The fast-linearization procedure of an earlier paper is applied to a maximally symmetric background: the whole calculation reduces to "the evaluation of the corresponding Lagrangian density on a particular Riemann tensor — constructed from the metric and an auxiliary tensor — and the computation of two trivial derivatives." The output is a set of decoupled equations for the metric perturbation modes, of which the relevant ones are a massless spin-2 mode, a massive spin-2 mode with mass $m_g$, and a scalar with mass $m_s$; a negative $m_g^2$ is the ghost-like case the authors exclude from the physical class.

The classification

Theories are sorted by which of these modes they carry: no massive graviton, no dynamical scalar, two massless gravitons, critical gravities, and Einstein-like theories. For $f(\text{scalars})$ theories — Lagrangians built from the Ricci scalar, the Ricci contraction and the Kretschmann scalar — the condition for the absence of the massive graviton is given explicitly.

The cubic theorem

The most general cubic gravity constructed in a dimension-independent way and free of the ghost-like spin-2 mode, while allowed to propagate the scalar, "is a linear combination of $f(\text{Lovelock})$ invariants, plus the ECG term, plus a New ghost-free gravity term." Quartic versions of Einsteinian cubic gravity are constructed by linearizing all theories up to quartic order in curvature.

The Newton potential

For $D>5$ the generalised potential near the origin behaves as

$$U_D(r\to0)\sim-\frac{G_{\text{eff}}\,M\,(D-2)^2}{r^{D-5}}\,(m_g^2-m_s^2)+\dots, \qquad (paper\ 8.23)$$

up to a positive dimension-dependent constant. For $D=4$ the singular term is replaced by a constant, and for $D=5$ by a logarithmic divergence. When $m_g$ and $m_s$ are large compared with $1/r$, the Einsteinian potential and $\gamma=1$ are recovered.

Radiation and Wald's formalism

The polarisation content of gravitational waves is read off the same decomposition, the radiation from sources is computed in four dimensions with a generalised power formula, and Wald's Noether-charge construction is re-derived for general $L(\text{Riemann})$ theories, with explicit expressions for the quantities that enter the black-hole first law.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The spectrum as a classification of Lagrangians

The idea. A higher-curvature Lagrangian is not judged by its terms but by the three masses it propagates, $(m_g,m_s)$, and the sign of $m_g^2$. This is a formula-level import: a rule that converts an arbitrary contraction of Riemann tensors into a three-parameter physical spectrum.

What it would add. The corpus writes linearised gravity with one massless field (articles_physics/linearized-gravity-in-biquaternionic-form.md) and derives the quadrupole radiation from it. It has no statement about what a curvature-squared or curvature-cubed term does to the spectrum, and therefore no way to say why the Einstein–Hilbert term is the one the framework carries.

Where it lands. articles_physics/linearized-gravity-in-biquaternionic-form.md, adjacent to articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-linearised-gravity.md, as the classification the corpus would need before it could host any correction to its own linearised equation.

What stands in the way. The cost is the decomposition, not the arithmetic.

2. Einsteinian cubic gravity as a selected theory

The idea. Among all cubic contractions, one combination propagates the same spectrum as Einstein gravity in four dimensions, and it is selected by requiring the absence of the ghost. This is a mechanism: a principle that picks a Lagrangian rather than a fit.

What it would add. The corpus treats the Einstein–Hilbert form as given and imports the metric. A selection principle for the curvature terms would let the framework say which higher-order corrections are admissible, which is exactly the question its articles_physics/what-the-biquaternion-algebra-cannot-do-a-catalogue-of-algebraic-obstructions.md leaves open under "dynamics".

Where it lands. articles_physics/what-the-biquaternion-algebra-cannot-do-a-catalogue-of-algebraic-obstructions.md and articles_physics/the-action-problem.md.

What stands in the way. The selection is made in $D=4$ and depends on the dimension through the Lovelock terms; the corpus has no argument that fixes a dimension for the frame field, and the cubic invariants have no known biquaternion writing. Ranked third: interesting, not needed.

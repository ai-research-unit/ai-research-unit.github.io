What the Paper Does

Proposes a holographic definition of thermodynamic pressure and volume for black holes, based on quasi-local gravitational thermodynamics. Enclosing a black hole in a finite timelike boundary gives York's quasi-local first law, which contains a surface pressure conjugate to the boundary area; if a dual theory lives on that boundary, the geometric quantities become the pressure and volume of the dual thermal system. For static spherically symmetric holes these reduce to global thermodynamic variables. The holographic volume supplies a notion of system size, hence a definition of extensivity and of the large-system limit. The result: small Schwarzschild black holes are non-extensive, large ones become extensive in the large-system limit; the same holds for AdS–Schwarzschild, except that the quasi-local energy of the large hole also becomes extensive, and before the limit the energy splits into subextensive and extensive parts with an explicit formula for the extensive part.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The proposal

"When a black hole is enclosed by a finite timelike boundary, York's quasi-local first law includes a surface pressure conjugate to the boundary area. Assuming the existence of a holographically dual theory living on this boundary, these geometric quantities correspond to the pressure and volume of the dual thermal system."

What it buys

"The holographic volume provides a notion of system size, allowing extensivity to be defined in standard thermodynamic terms, and it yields a definition of the large-system limit."

The result for flat space

"For the asymptotically flat case, we show that, in the canonical thermodynamic representation, small Schwarzschild black holes are non-extensive, whereas large black holes become extensive in the large-system limit."

The result for AdS

"A similar conclusion applies to Anti-de Sitter Schwarzschild black holes, with the difference that the quasi-local energy of the large black hole also becomes extensive in the large-system limit. Before this limit, the energy decomposes into subextensive and extensive contributions, and we derive an explicit expression for the extensive part as a function of the finite volume and entropy."

The contrast with earlier proposals

The paper reviews Padmanabhan's proposal and extended thermodynamics, and argues that neither supplies a volume with the right scaling behaviour. Padmanabhan's uses the horizon as the boundary; extended thermodynamics varies $\Lambda$ and its conjugate is not an independent size. The cavity boundary is what makes the volume an actual size.

The structure

Quasi-local first law and Smarr relation from covariant phase space, then the small/large split for flat and AdS, then a general treatment of extensivity — quasi-homogeneous scaling and generalised Euler relations — and finally the non-extensivity proof for asymptotically flat holes.

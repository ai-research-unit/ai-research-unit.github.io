What the Paper Does

Develops a general framework for the electromagnetic potential–charge terms in the first law of black hole mechanics, for dynamical first-order perturbations of stationary black objects with possibly non-compact bifurcate Killing horizons. Working in covariant phase space for generic diffeomorphism-invariant theories with non-minimally coupled abelian $p$-form gauge fields, it derives both the comparison and the physical-process versions of the first law. The gauge field's pullback to the horizon may diverge while its field strength stays smooth, and the resulting potential–charge pairs are gauge invariant. Magnetic charges are included by a bundle-covariant prescription that fixes the Jacobson–Kang–Myers ambiguity, and electric and magnetic charges are tied to non-trivial cycles of the horizon cross-section, whose Betti numbers count the independent pairs. The dynamical entropy is identified with the gauge-invariant part of the improved Noether charge.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The scope

"We develop a general framework for electromagnetic potential-charge contributions to the first law of black hole mechanics, applicable to dynamical first-order perturbations of stationary black objects with possibly non-compact bifurcate Killing horizons. Working in the covariant phase space formalism, we derive both comparison and physical process versions of the first law. We consider generic diffeomorphism-invariant theories of gravity in $D$ spacetime dimensions, containing non-minimally coupled abelian $p$-form gauge fields."

The non-smoothness

"The pullback of the gauge field to the horizon is allowed to diverge while its field strength remains smooth, yielding gauge-invariant electric potential-charge pairs in the first law." The divergence is not a defect to be removed; it is what makes the pair well defined.

The topological counting

"Electric and magnetic charges are, respectively, associated with non-trivial $(D-p-1)$- and $(p+1)$-cycles of the horizon cross-section, whose homology classes determine the number of independent potential-charge pairs through the Betti numbers $b_{D-p-1}$ and $b_{p+1}$."

The entropy

"the dynamical gravitational entropy entering the first law is identified with the gauge-invariant part of the improved Noether charge, giving a gauge-invariant extension of the recent proposal by Hollands, Wald and Zhang."

The examples

"dyonic AdS black holes, dipole black rings, and charged black branes." For dipole rings the non-stationary comparison first law takes the form

$$T\,\delta S_{\text{dyn}}=\delta M-\Omega\,\delta J-\Phi\,\delta Q, \qquad (paper\ VII.26)$$

with $S_{\text{dyn}}=\frac{1}{4G}(1-v\partial_v)A$ for general relativity — the same affine-parameter correction as in the uncharged case.

The moral about cohomology

"we emphasise that, due to Poincaré duality between cohomology" classes, the count of independent charges is fixed by the horizon's topology and not by the details of the solution.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The number of charges is a Betti number

The idea. How many independent potential–charge pairs a horizon carries is determined by the homology of its cross-section, via $b_{D-p-1}$ and $b_{p+1}$.

What it would add. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md counts charges explicitly for each solution. A topological origin for the count is stronger: it says which terms must appear in a first law for a given horizon topology, independent of the metric. The corpus has no such statement, and it is exactly the sort of structural result the article's Smarr section generalises towards.

Where it lands. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, the Smarr section.

3. Same entropy correction, more charges

The idea. The dynamical entropy is still $\frac{1}{4G}(1-v\partial_v)A$ when charge, rotation and dipole charge are all present; the extra terms appear only on the energy side of the first law.

What it would add. It isolates the entropy correction as universal and puts all solution-dependence into the energy terms.

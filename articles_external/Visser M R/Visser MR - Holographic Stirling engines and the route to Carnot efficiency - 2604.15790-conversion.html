What the Paper Does

Computes the efficiency of the reversible Stirling engine, with and without regeneration, for a broad class of working substances: Van der Waals fluids, quantum ideal gases (Bose and Fermi), Bose–Einstein condensates, thermal conformal field theories, and holographic CFTs. Regeneration is internal heat recycling, and the paper identifies a general sufficient condition for a regenerative Stirling cycle to reach Carnot efficiency: at fixed conserved charges, the fixed-volume heat capacity must be independent of volume. Classical ideal gases and Van der Waals fluids satisfy it; quantum ideal gases and CFTs violate it. For thermal CFT states dual to AdS–Schwarzschild and AdS–Reissner–Nordström black holes the efficiency is computed exactly, and in the fixed-potential ensemble both regenerative and non-regenerative efficiencies approach Carnot in the large-potential limit, with regeneration reducing the leading deviation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The theorem

"For regenerative Stirling cycles, we identify a general sufficient condition for attaining Carnot efficiency: at fixed conserved charges, the fixed-volume heat capacity is independent of volume. This ensures that, at each temperature along the isochores, the heat released during cooling equals the heat absorbed during heating. An ideal regenerator can therefore store and return this heat reversibly, returning to its initial thermodynamic state with no net change in energy or entropy."

What satisfies it

"While this condition is satisfied for classical ideal gases and Van der Waals fluids, it is violated for quantum ideal gases and CFT working substances." The dividing line is classicality of the equation of state, not the presence or absence of interactions.

Why regeneration helps, and how much

"Regeneration acts as an internal heat recycling mechanism that enhances efficiency by reducing the net heat exchange with external reservoirs." For the holographic cases, "In the fixed-potential ensemble, both the regenerative and non-regenerative efficiencies approach the Carnot value in the large-potential limit. Regeneration reduces the leading deviation from the Carnot value, although both efficiencies have corrections of the same asymptotic order."

What this means for holography

Carnot efficiency is reached asymptotically, not exactly, and the approach is through a limit in which the potential grows. Holographic systems therefore do not saturate the bound at finite coupling or finite potential; they approach it.

The contrast with the earlier engine paper

The previous heat-engine paper computed the Stirling efficiency for a generic CFT in the high-temperature or large-volume regime and derived an exact expression for the AdS–Schwarzschild case. This paper generalises the substance class and adds regeneration, with the theorem as the organising result.

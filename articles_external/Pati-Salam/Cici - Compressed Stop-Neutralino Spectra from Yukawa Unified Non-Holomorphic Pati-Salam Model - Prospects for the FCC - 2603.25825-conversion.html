What the Paper Does

A numerical collider study of the supersymmetric $4$-$2$-$2$ model, with one new ingredient: non-holomorphic soft supersymmetry-breaking terms. These are trilinear couplings that pair the squarks with the "wrong Higgs, plus a second Higgsino mass $\mu'$, and they are normally forbidden by holomorphy. The paper takes them from non-supersymmetric perturbations in the string theory", where the suppression that kills them in field theory does not apply.

The consequence is specific and it fixes a problem. Yukawa unification in these models requires large negative threshold corrections to $y_b$, and the standard way to get them forces a light gluino. That is now excluded. The non-holomorphic terms supply the same negative correction with $A'_b<0$ and $\mu,M_3>0$, so

"in contrast to previous studies, the non-holomorphic contributions accommodate heavy gluino masses in this class of models from approximately 2.2 to 10 TeV while preserving Yukawa unification. These gluinos can be probed up to about 2."

Everything else follows: the lightest stop can be about $1.5$ TeV with a compressed stop–neutralino spectrum, sbottoms about $1.5$ TeV, and the FCC-hh reach is about $2.5$ TeV at high luminosity, $6$ TeV at $100$ TeV.

Why they are allowed to be large: "Even though these terms are strictly forbidden in supersymmetric models, they can be induced from higher dimensional operators through SUSY breaking [66, 67, 70]; however, these operators lead to a strong suppression for the NH terms by the SUSY breaking scale at the visible sector." "On the other hand, this suppression does not hold, when the NH terms are induced by the perturbations on D−branes [71–76] in string theories. Recent"

The mechanism: "If one follows the Giddins-Kachru-Polchinski (GKP) compactification, the hierarchy between the SUSY and electroweak symmetry breaking scales can be generated by strongly warped regions. However, these regions destabilize the Kähler potential, and stability can be recovered by introducing non-hermitian components to the internal metric. If such non-hermitian parts are treated perturbatively, they can induce NH terms which break SUSY."

The logic is worth isolating: warped throats give the hierarchy but destabilise the Kähler potential; stability needs a non-hermitian metric; a non-hermitian metric is a perturbation that breaks supersymmetry softly and non-holomorphically. So the non-holomorphic terms are the price of stabilising the throat. That is a stringy origin story of a kind the corpus has not met.

The Pati–Salam Relation That Is Kept

The input parameters are the usual ones, except that the $U(1)_Y$ gaugino mass is not free:

$$ M_1=\tfrac35M_2+\tfrac25M_3 . $$

"The SSB mass term for the U (1)Y gaugino can be determined by the Pati-Salam relation as 3 2 M 1 = M2 + M 3 . 5 5 (3.2) The parameter"

That equation is the only place the $SU(4)$ structure enters the numerical scan, and it is a genuine consequence: if $U(1)_Y$ sits inside $SU(4)$ with the $\mathrm{diag}(3,-1,-1,-1)$ generator, its gaugino mass is a fixed linear combination of the $SU(2)$ and $SU(3)$ ones. The coefficients $\frac35,\frac25$ are the $\frac53$ normalisation written as a mass relation.

Non-holomorphic terms are then set by "ns we set |µ′ | = max(M2 , M3 ) and |A′0 | = |A0 | to feed the boundary conditions numerically. Thus, the NH terms do not enlarge the set of fundamental parameters of the models. However, we allow potential phase differences between the NH terms and their holomorphic partners by randomly assigning them positive and negative signs in our scans."

The Results

The input ranges: $m_0\le20$ TeV, $M_2,M_3\le5$ TeV, $A_0/m_0\in[-3,3]$, $35\le\tan\beta\le60$, $m_{H_u},m_{H_d}\le20$ TeV. The scan is Metropolis–Hastings into SPheno 4.0.4 (via SARAH) and micrOMEGAs, with the usual constraints: $m_h=123$–$127$ GeV, $m_{\tilde g}\ge2.1$ TeV ($1.4$ TeV if NLSP), $B_s\to\mu^+\mu^-$ and $B\to X_s\gamma$ at $2\sigma$, $0.114\le\Omega_{\rm CDM}h^2\le0.126$ at $5\sigma$.

The Collider Part

The reach: gluinos to about $2.5$ TeV at high luminosity, about $6$ TeV at $100$ TeV. Third-generation squarks: "The current analyses can exclude these solutions for $m_{\tilde b_1}\lesssim1.5$ TeV. Similarly, the stop mass can also be as light as about $1.5$ TeV ... We also identify solutions in which the stop and LSP neutralino can be nearly degenerate at $m_{\tilde t_1}\simeq m_{\tilde\chi_1^0}\gtrsim1.4$ TeV ... the lightest possible stop to weigh about $1.7$ TeV, with mass difference of about $200$ GeV relative to the LSP neutralino."

The analysis itself: semileptonic stop pair production, a boosted decision tree with hyperparameters of the best XGBoost model (regularisation $\alpha=1.98$, ROC-AUC $0.933$), and "The best score in our analyses is obtained as τ = 0.98" "we realize a significant improvement in that the analyses can probe the signal up to about 3σ with an integrated luminosity of about 19.4 fb−1 , and 5σ when L ≃ 54.4 fb−1 . The significance"

And the caveat the authors state twice: "Our results do not include systematic uncertainties, which can heavily impact the experimental analysis." "Our results can be recovered when the experimental searches reach the sensitivity in which the overall systematic uncertainties in the background modelling do not exceed 0.1%. 6"

One tenth of a percent on background modelling is the condition for the whole result to hold, and no current or planned analysis achieves that. The paper says so plainly, which is to its credit.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

  1. $M_1=\frac35M_2+\frac25M_3$: the gaugino mass relation from the embedding

The idea. If $U(1)_Y$ is generated by $\mathrm{diag}(3,-1,-1,-1)/\sqrt6$ inside $SU(4)$, and the $SU(2)$ and $SU(3)$ gauginos have masses $M_2,M_3$, then the $U(1)_Y$ gaugino mass is fixed to that linear combination.

What it would add. A second, independent numerical consequence of the charge embedding — and one that is testable in a way the coupling relations are not. The corpus has the coupling normalisation $\frac53$ and uses it for $\sin^2\theta_W$. Here the same $\frac53$ gives a mass relation, and masses are measured directly while couplings are inferred. One embedding, two kinds of prediction, one of which is a collider observable. The corpus's Pati–Salam article should carry both.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's gaugino/mass-relation paragraph, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-and-the-three-neutral-states.md for the coupling side.

What stands in the way. The relation holds at the unification scale and is subject to RGE running, which the scan handles numerically. In the corpus's non-supersymmetric frame there are no gaugino masses at all, so this is a prediction that requires supersymmetry to exist. Import it as a consequence of the embedding under the assumption of supersymmetry, explicitly conditional.

  1. Non-holomorphic soft terms as the price of a stabilised throat

The idea. Warped regions generate the hierarchy but destabilise the Kähler potential; stability needs a non-hermitian internal metric; treating that perturbatively induces soft terms that break holomorphy.

What it would add. A structural explanation for why a symmetry that "should" hold does not. The corpus discusses symmetry breaking constantly — of gauge groups, of flavour groups, of parity. Here the broken thing is holomorphy, an assumption of the supersymmetric Lagrangian, and the breaking is traced to a geometric requirement rather than postulated. That is the pattern the corpus values: a symmetry that fails because something else demanded it.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's structural-cost paragraph, or wherever the corpus discusses what an assumption costs.

What stands in the way. It requires string compactification machinery the corpus does not have. Import the pattern — stability of one structure forcing the violation of another — not the mechanism.

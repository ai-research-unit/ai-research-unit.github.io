What the Paper Does

It predicts a first-order phase transition whose gravitational waves peak in the deci-Hz band.

"We show that it is possible to use gravitational wave detectors to observe the occurrence of a first order phase transition in Pati–Salam extensions of the Standard Model."

"We show that the peak frequency of the expected gravitational wave signals ranges within 0.1 − 10 Hz."

"We find amusing that the next generation of gravity waves detectors are able to explore time honoured extensions of the Standard Model occurring at energy scales inaccessible by present and future particle physics accelerators. Preprint: CP3 -Origins-2020-05 DNRF90 I."

The band is the key: $0.1$–$10$ Hz is DECIGO/BBO territory, and partly LISA's.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why a First-Order Transition?

A first-order transition produces GWs through bubble collisions, sound waves, and turbulence in the plasma. A second-order one does not.

And whether the transition is first-order depends on the scalar potential. So the question is dynamical, not structural.

The paper's finding: in the PS model, the $SU(4)$ and $SU(2)_R$ breakings can be first order, and the resulting signal is strong.

And it notes a simplification over earlier work: **"their first order phase transition occurs only when SU (4) is breaking while in our case both SU (4) and SU (2)R breaks and thus their analysis" "is simpler and fewer couplings are involved."

**

The corpus should record that: two breakings, one transition — which is a structural choice, and it reduces the number of parameters.

The Asymptotic-Safety Embedding

"Pati-Salam extensions of the Standard Model embedded in an asymptotically 2 safe scenario. We use these predictions as an initial seed value" "Later, we will depart from the safety scenario and will explore a more general parameter space. Therefore" "applies to both safe and non-safe embeddings of the Pati-Salam model."

So the paper seeds its scan with the asymptotically safe PS parameters from 1807.03669 (Litim & Sannino), then explores more broadly.

That is a nice methodological move: a fixed point prediction as the starting point, and a general scan as the check.

The corpus should record it, because it is the batch's only paper that uses 1807.03669's fixed-point relations quantitatively — the corpus's asymptotic-safety notes said the fixed point gives relations among couplings; here they are used.

The Frequency Band

$0.1$–$10$ Hz.

Which detectors: DECIGO (0.1–10 Hz), BBO (0.1–10 Hz), and the lower edge of LISA (0.1 mHz–0.1 Hz), plus Einstein Telescope at the top.

So the signal sits between LISA's band and the ground-based detectors', **which is the "deci-Hz gap"

** — and the paper's point is that this gap is exactly where a GUT-scale transition's signal lands. The corpus should record the physics: the peak frequency scales as $f\sim H_*$ redshifted, and for a transition at $10^{10}$–$10^{14}$ GeV it lands at $0.1$–$10$ Hz. So the band is a prediction of the scale, and detecting it would measure the PS scale. That is a genuinely important point: the GW frequency measures $v_{PS}$ directly, which no collider can do.

Why This Complements 2308.13248

2308.13248: metastable cosmic strings, nanohertz (PTA) band, from a post-inflation transition. Here: first-order transition's own GWs, deci-Hz band, from the transition that ends inflation or follows it. Two PS signals in two bands, from two different defects/mechanisms. And the corpus should note that they can coexist: a first-order transition produces both bubble GWs and, if the vacuum manifold is right, strings. So the two papers may describe the same event in two frequency windows — a strong observational case. The corpus should say so: Pati–Salam predicts GWs in at least two bands, nanohertz from strings and deci-Hz from the transition, and both are within planned detectors.

The Honest Limit

$\alpha$ and inverse duration $\beta/H$**, which depend on the potential's parameters. So the prediction is "in the band", not "detectable". The corpus should record that distinction.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper gives the corpus what its scale section lacked: a way to measure $v_{PS}$.

The chain: the transition's Hubble rate sets the GW peak frequency; the frequency is measured; so the scale is measured. And the frequency $0.1$–$10$ Hz corresponds to $v_{PS}\sim10^{10}$–$10^{14}$ GeV — exactly hep-ph/0302216's window.

So the corpus has a consistency check across twenty years of papers: the minimal-SUSY window and the GW band agree.

That is a strong and useful statement, and the corpus should make it: the PS scale is not arbitrary — it is bounded above by proton decay, below by the seesaw, and measurable in gravitational waves.

Three independent handles on one number. That is what makes a model scientific, and the corpus should say so plainly.

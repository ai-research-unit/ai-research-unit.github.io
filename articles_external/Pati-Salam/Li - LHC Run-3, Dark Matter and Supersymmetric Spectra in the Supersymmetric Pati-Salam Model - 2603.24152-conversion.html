What the Paper Does

It scans the Pati–Salam-constrained MSSM for dark matter and survives every current bound.

"We reexamine phenomenological consequences of the Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model (MSSM) arising from the supersymmetric SU (4)C × SU (2)L × SU (2)R Pati-Salam model, considering both positive and negative values of the Higgsino mass parameter µ, as the muon anomalous magnetic moment might be in accordance with the Standard Model (SM) prediction."

"We identify the viable parameter spaces in which the observed dark matter relic abundance, as reported by Planck 2018, is achieved through several mechanisms, including gluino-neutralino, sbottom-neutralino, stop-neutralino, stau-neutralino, and chargino-neutralino coannihilations, as well as resonant annihilation via the pseudoscalar Higgs boson (A-funnel)."

"we find that sbottom-neutralino coannihilation solutions typically require sbottom masses near 2.8 TeV, while gluino-neutralino and stop-neutralino coannihilation scenarios allow gluino masses in the range 1–3 TeV and stop masses between 1 and 3.5 TeV. In coannihilation-dominated regions, the stau and chargino masses can reach values as high as 3.5 TeV, whereas viable A-resonance solutions are found for pseudoscalar Higgs masses spanning approximately 1."

"

We anticipate that a significant portion of the surviving parameter space will be accessible to the supersymmetry searches at the LHC Run 3 and future upgrades."

So the paper is a collider-survival study, and its numbers are the spectra the next LHC runs must reach.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why the Pati–Salam Constraint Matters

The model is the MSSM arising from Pati–Salam, which means the GUT-scale boundary conditions are Pati–Salam's: the third-family Yukawa unification and the soft-term pattern inherited from the breaking.

So the scan is not over the full 100-plus-parameter MSSM, but over the subspace the Pati–Salam boundary conditions allow. That makes the predictions sharper.

The corpus should record the logic: a GUT's boundary conditions reduce the MSSM's parameter space, so its dark-matter predictions are more specific than the MSSM's. That is the payoff of unification for collider physics — fewer parameters, sharper predictions.

Both Signs of $\mu$

"considering both positive and negative values of the Higgsino mass parameter µ"

$\mu>0$ versus $\mu<0$ changes the sign of the Higgsino-gaugino mixing, and hence the sign of the dark matter's coupling to nuclei — which matters for direct detection and for $(g-2)_\mu$.

The muon $g-2$ prefers $\mu>0$ in the MSSM. And if the muon anomaly is confirmed to be SM-like (as the abstract hints: "might be in accordance with the Standard Model (SM) prediction."), then $\mu<0$ becomes viable again.

The corpus should record the conditional: the sign of $\mu$ is fixed by $(g-2)_\mu$ if the anomaly is real, and free if it is not. The paper scans both because the situation is unsettled.

That is a genuine methodological point and it links to 1605.02072: the muon $g-2$ was a Pati–Salam prediction, and now its status determines which dark-matter regions are allowed.

The Five Coannihilation Mechanisms

Coannihilation occurs when the LSP and a near-degenerate state share the annihilation, so the effective cross-section is larger and the relic density comes out right.

The five channels: - gluino–neutralino: needs $m_{\rm gluino}\approx m_{\chi}$ — 1–3 TeV. - sbottom–neutralino: needs $m_{\tilde b}\approx m_{\chi}$ — about 2.8 TeV. - stop–neutralino: needs $m_{\tilde t}\approx m_{\chi}$ — 1–3.5 TeV. - stau–neutralino: needs $m_{\tilde\tau}\approx m_{\chi}$ — up to 3.5 TeV. - chargino–neutralino: needs $m_{\chi^\pm}\approx m_{\chi}$ — up to 3.5 TeV.

Plus the $A$-funnel: $2m_\chi\approx m_A$, resonant annihilation through the pseudoscalar — $m_A\approx1.7$–$3.8$ TeV.

The corpus should record the pattern: every viable mechanism needs a superpartner within a few hundred GeV of the LSP, at the TeV scale. So the model is not "natural"— the spectrum is heavy — but it is not at the multi-TeV scale where the LHC gives up either. And the $A$-funnel at 1.7–3.8 TeV is the least constrained because it needs no near-degeneracy.

The LHC Reach

"a significant portion of the surviving parameter space will be accessible to the supersymmetry searches at the LHC Run 3 and future upgrades"

So the paper's conclusion is a prediction of discovery or exclusion. Unlike most SUSY papers in the batch, this one says the LHC will decide. The corpus should record that as a strength: the model makes a falsifiable claim within a decade. And compare with the field-theory papers: 1703.09309's $SO(10)$ model was excluded by the LHC; this Pati–Salam-constrained MSSM is not, because its spectrum is heavier. The corpus should note the contrast — the boundary conditions, not the group, determine whether the LHC sees SUSY.

The Relic Density and Direct Detection

"all these scenarios are still consistent with the existing collider bounds and current limits on spin-independent and spin-dependent neutralino-nucleon scattering cross sections"

So the surviving points pass LHC, Higgs, relic density, and direct detection. That is a strong filter and the surviving regions are small. **"Most of these scenarios remain within the reach of current and upcoming direct-detection experiments, such as XENONnT, LZ current, and LZ 1000-day sensitivity. I."

** So direct detection is the sharper probe. The corpus should note that the dark-matter detection is currently more sensitive than the collider for this model — the reverse of the usual expectation.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 2606.29811 are the batch's most recent, and they mark a change of genre.

Earlier papers: build the model, derive the spectrum, hope it survives. These papers: take the spectrum, scan it, and report what survives and what will be tested.

That is the mature phase of a model-building programme, and the corpus should present it as such. The questions have changed from is Pati–Salam possible? to which Pati–Salam spectrum is still alive?.

And the answers are specific: the gluino at 1–3 TeV, the sbottom at 2.8 TeV, the pseudoscalar at 1.7–3.8 TeV. Those are numbers the LHC and LZ can test, and if they do not find them, this branch closes.

The corpus should record the numbers as predictions, not preferences, because that is what they are.

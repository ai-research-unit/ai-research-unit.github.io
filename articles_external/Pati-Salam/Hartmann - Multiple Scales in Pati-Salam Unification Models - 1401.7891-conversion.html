This is the survey paper that the Feldmann–Hartmann–Kilian–Luhn flavour papers (1506.00782, 1608.04124) build on.

What the Paper Does

It enumerates every Pati–Salam model you can build from small representations of the GUT group and asks which ones unify.

"We investigate models where gauge unification (GUT) proceeds in steps that include Pati-Salam symmetry. Beyond the Standard Model, we allow for a well-defined set of small representations of the GUT gauge group. We show that all possible chains of Pati-Salam symmetry breaking can be realized in accordance with gauge-coupling unification."

"All possible chains can be realized" is the result and it is a negative one in disguise: the unification constraint does not select a breaking pattern. Every chain works for some choice of the intermediate scales, so gauge unification alone cannot tell you which chain nature uses.

"We identify, in particular, models with unification near the Planck scale, with intermediate left-right or $SU(4)$ quark-lepton symmetries that are relevant for flavor physics, with new colored particles at accessible energies, and with an enlarged electroweak Higgs sector. We look both at supersymmetric and non-supersymmetric scenarios."

The framing, in the authors' words: "It is remarkable that the most interesting phenomenology, which we may identify as Planck-scale GUT unification, intermediate PS and LR scales, and new particles at TeV energies, can be simultaneously realized in a number of distinct models. This is not a generic feature."

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Group and the $Z_{LR}$

"By the label of Pati-Salam gauge group, we refer to the symmetry group $SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times Z_{LR}$."

"We impose an exact discrete symmetry $Z_{LR}$ which exchanges $SU(2)_L$ with $SU(2)_R$, and left-handed with right-handed matter fields."

"If we impose this symmetry on any generation of quarks and leptons, they combine to a single irreducible representation $\Psi_{L/R}$. Regarding the continuous gauge group only, this is a direct sum $\Psi_L\oplus\Psi_R$, but it is rendered irreducible by the left-right symmetry."

That is the cleanest statement of what the $Z_{LR}$ buys. Without it you have two multiplets, $\Psi_L$ and $\Psi_R$, and nothing relates them; with it, one generation is a single irreducible object, which is the strongest form of the claim that quarks and leptons unify. The corpus's Pati–Salam article should say this explicitly: the group alone does not make the generation irreducible, the discrete symmetry does.

The content: $$ \Psi_L=(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)=\begin{pmatrix}u^c_r&u^c_g&u^c_b&\nu^c\\ d^c_r&d^c_g&d^c_b&e^c\end{pmatrix}_R,\qquad \Psi_R=(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)=\begin{pmatrix}u_r&u_g&u_b&\nu\\ d_r&d_g&d_b&e\end{pmatrix}_L . $$

"The set of gauge bosons of a PS gauge theory contains the eight QCD gluons and the weak $W_L$ triplet. On top of that, there are a $W_R$ triplet and seven extra gauge bosons of $SU(4)_C$."

Fifteen generators of $SU(4)$, eight of which are gluons, so seven new ones. Those seven are the leptoquarks and the $B-L$ boson: six leptoquark states ($X$, $Y$ and conjugates) plus one. The count is worth recording — it is the number of new gauge bosons any Pati–Salam model must have, and their mass is the model's main experimental problem.

Why Pati–Salam Specifically, in the Authors' Words

"PS symmetry, with a discrete left-right symmetry included, is the minimal symmetry group which collects all matter fields of a single generation in one irreducible multiplet."

"As an extension of the SM, it is particularly interesting because it naturally accommodates multiple energy thresholds or scales that fill the large gap between the SM (TeV) scale and the scale of ultimate unification."

"PS models do not just exhibit multiple scales associated with symmetry breaking, but they can generate additional mass scales due to see-saw type patterns in the spectrum."

Three separate sources of scale: the breaking VEVs, the seesaw from heavy neutrinos, and the masses of the extra multiplets. The paper's subject is that third one, which is usually neglected. A field that does not get its mass from a VEV has a mass that is a free parameter — and the paper asks whether unification constrains it.

The Smallness Constraint

"We want to study the relation of symmetry breaking and intermediate mass scales in PS models in a suitably generic way while keeping the sector of extra (Higgs) fields below the GUT scale as compact as possible. To this end, we impose conditions on the spectrum of new fields, so we can describe the chain of symmetry breaking via a staged Higgs mechanism with only small representations of the GUT group."

"Only small representations" is a substantive physical assumption, not a convenience. If you allow a $\mathbf{126}$ or a $\mathbf{210}$ you can break anything in one step; restricting to small ones forces the breaking to be staged, and staged breaking is what produces the multiple scales. So the constraint is what creates the phenomenon being studied.

The Six Classes

The hierarchy patterns: - A: $\langle\Phi\rangle\sim\langle T\rangle\sim\langle\Sigma\rangle$ (one scale) - B: $\langle\Phi\rangle\ll\langle T\rangle\ll\langle\Sigma\rangle$ (three scales) - C: $\langle\Phi\rangle\ll\langle\Sigma\rangle\ll\langle T\rangle$ (three scales) - D: $\langle\Phi\rangle\ll\langle T\rangle\sim\langle\Sigma\rangle$ (two scales) - E: $\langle\Phi\rangle\ll\langle\Sigma\rangle$ and $\langle T\rangle=0$ (two scales) - F: $\langle\Phi\rangle\ll\langle T\rangle$ and $\langle\Sigma\rangle=0$ (two scales)

with $\Phi=(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $\Sigma=(\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$, $T=(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$.

"The scale $\langle\Phi\rangle$ is always the lowest symmetry-breaking scale (above the electroweak scale), since this vev breaks all symmetries. The other vevs break the PS symmetry only partially and are only relevant if they lie above $\langle\Phi\rangle$."

"Nevertheless they are needed because we require the symmetry breaking to result from a renormalizable potential, which does not exist for $\Phi$ alone."

That is a group-theoretic fact worth recording and it is the reason the minimal Pati–Salam scalar sector is not minimal at all. The bidoublet $\Phi$ alone cannot break $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)\to SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ through a renormalizable potential — you need an adjoint or a $\mathbf{10}$ as well, because the potential must have a nontrivial minimum in the right direction and $\Phi$ alone does not give it.

The two paths, drawn in the paper's Fig. 1: $$ G_{\rm PS}\to SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{B-L}\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times Z_{LR}\to\cdots $$ via $\Sigma$, or $$ G_{\rm PS}\to SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R\to\cdots $$ via $T$. Two intermediate groups, and they are different: the first keeps the leptoquarks massive but $SU(2)_R$ exact, the second keeps $SU(4)$ intact and breaks $SU(2)_R$.

So "Pati–Salam breaking" is not one chain. There are two, depending on whether $\Sigma$ or $T$ comes first, and they have completely different low-energy spectra. The corpus's article presents the chain with $\Delta_R$ before the $SU(4)$ breaking; that is class F, and it is only one of six.

The Scan and the Numbers

"We scan over all models by varying the number of multiplets" — with multiplicities zero, one or three, since "this already provides a wide range of possibilities for spectra and hierarchies and appears most natural in view of the generation pattern that is established for SM matter."

The supersymmetric scan, after imposing that every model needs an electroweak Higgs: "This reduces our scan to 828 configurations."

"We find 184 with $10^{12}\ {\rm GeV}\lesssim M_{N_R}\lesssim10^{14}$ GeV. Actually, in classes B, C, three-quarter and in class E still half of all successful models fall in this category. Only in class F, this scale is typically higher; only 10% of the models result in a value in this range."

So the right-handed neutrino mass is pushed to $10^{12}$–$10^{14}$ GeV in a large fraction of models, which is the classic seesaw range and the range that makes leptogenesis work. That is a coincidence worth noting: the same staged breaking that fixes the gauge couplings also lands $M_{N_R}$ where thermal leptogenesis needs it.

"We also find that a neutrino mass scale in this range is associated with at most one generation of $h$ fields. As an interesting non-standard scenario, hinting at $E_6$ grand unification, three light $h$ generations are also possible, but accompanied by heavier right-handed neutrinos."

A correlation between two unrelated-looking quantities: number of light Higgs bidoublets and right-handed neutrino mass. More $h$ generations means lighter $N_R$, which means the seesaw is less suppressed. That kind of correlation is exactly what a survey of this kind is for.

The Coloured States Cannot Be Light

"in all model classes we find a lower bound for the colored $E$ multiplet of about $m_E\gtrsim10^8$ GeV. Similar results apply to $\Sigma$ and $T$, if we do not consider lowering the GUT scale below about $10^{16}$ GeV. In other words, the proton stability constraint which limits the GUT scale, suggests that these fields have rather large masses."

That is a chain of reasoning worth keeping: coloured states contribute to proton decay, so their masses are bounded by the same physics that bounds the GUT scale, so unification plus proton stability pushes them above $10^8$ GeV. The coloured exotics are not a collider target, and any hope of seeing them at the LHC is excluded by the structure of the model.

"This excludes a large invariant mass for the corresponding PS multiplet" — but for the Higgs the bound runs the other way, since the Higgs must be light.

The Planck-Scale Unification

The paper's headline scenario: "some GUT models are challenged by the non-observation of proton decay, so we may ask whether complete unification, and thus the appearance of baryon-number violating interactions, can be delayed up to the Planck scale."

Delaying unification to the Planck scale is a way to make proton decay unobservable, since $\tau_p\propto M_X^4$. And the survey finds it possible: "models with unification near the Planck scale" appear in the list of simultaneously realizable features.

For the non-supersymmetric class E: "GCU is possible, and the GUT-scale can vary between $10^{14}\ {\rm GeV}\lesssim M_{\rm GUT}\lesssim10^{17}$ GeV" — with $88$ configurations achieving Planck-scale unification.

So a non-supersymmetric Pati–Salam model can unify arbitrarily close to the Planck scale, which leaves proton decay out of reach permanently. That is the strongest possible evasion and it is available.

The Tension the Survey Reveals

"The number of bidoublets: ... Only 29 feature exactly one. There are roughly 50 sets with 1, 2 or 6 bidoublets each and 81 with 4. Zero or five are generally excluded by our setup. We also find that a larger number of light bidoublets is correlated with lower scales. Especially in the case of six bidoublets, we find that the maximal value for the GUT scale is $M_{\rm GUT}<10^{16}$ GeV."

More light Higgs doublets pulls the unification scale down, because each contributes to the beta functions and makes the couplings meet sooner. And a lower GUT scale means faster proton decay. So there is a trade-off between an extended Higgs sector and proton stability, and it is quantitative: six bidoublets forces $M_{\rm GUT}<10^{16}$ GeV.

The Conclusion

"One may consider fully specified GUT models that predict the appearance (or absence) of a PS symmetry and the associated spectrum. However, the current lack of reliable knowledge about strong and gravitational interactions which are expected at the highest energies, denies attempts to ultimately favor or exclude alternative spectra and symmetry-breaking chains."

"In such a situation, it appears worthwhile to rather concentrate on the implications of a sequence of new thresholds at intermediate energies, presumably in the context of PS symmetry."

"The findings suggest that one should study the hierarchies within flavor observables in relation to hierarchies in gauge-symmetry breaking, discuss both renormalizable and non-renormalizable operators, and to combine gauge and flavor symmetries in a common framework which need not be tied to ultimate GUT unification."

That last clause is the programme statement that led to the flavour papers in this batch. "Need not be tied to ultimate GUT unification" is the licence to take the multi-scale structure seriously on its own terms, and 1506.00782 and 1608.04124 are the execution.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. The $Z_{LR}$ is what makes one generation irreducible

The idea. $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$ alone gives $\Psi_L\oplus\Psi_R$, a reducible sum. The discrete symmetry $Z_{LR}$ exchanging the two factors makes the generation one irreducible object.

What it would add. A precise statement of Pati–Salam's central claim. The corpus's article says quarks and leptons unify in a $\mathbf4$. That is true of colour and lepton number but not of chirality — $\Psi_L$ and $\Psi_R$ are separate until you impose the discrete symmetry. So the strongest form of the unification claim requires an extra assumption, and the corpus should say which claim it is making.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the multiplet section.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The statement is: $\Psi_L\oplus\Psi_R$ is reducible under the continuous group and irreducible under the group times $Z_{LR}$.

2. The bidoublet alone cannot break Pati–Salam renormalizably

The idea. "the symmetry breaking ... does not exist for $\Phi$ alone" — a renormalizable potential needs $\Sigma$ or $T$ as well.

What it would add. A constraint on the minimal scalar sector that the corpus probably does not have. It is tempting to say Pati–Salam needs one bidoublet and one $\Delta_R$; in fact a renormalizable potential requires an adjoint or a $\mathbf{10}$ in addition, because the bidoublet's potential has no minimum in the right direction. That explains why every model in this batch carries a $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ or a $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$, and it is a group-theoretic necessity rather than a choice.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scalar-sector section.

What stands in the way. The paper states the fact without deriving it in full; the derivation is in the cited literature. State the fact and attribute it.

3. There are two Pati–Salam breaking chains, not one

The idea. $\Sigma$ first gives $SU(3)\times U(1)_{B-L}\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$; $T$ first gives $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R$. The spectra are completely different.

What it would add. The corpus's article presents a single chain. This shows the choice is real and consequential: whether the leptoquarks get their mass before or after $SU(2)_R$ breaks changes what the low-energy theory contains. And the two intermediate groups have different rank, different particle content, and different proton-decay properties.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's breaking section, which should present both paths.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The paper's Fig. 1 has both.

4. Gauge unification does not select the chain

The idea. "all possible chains of Pati–Salam symmetry breaking can be realized in accordance with gauge-coupling unification."

What it would add. An honest statement of what unification does and does not do. The corpus may suggest that unification picks out the Pati–Salam route or a particular breaking pattern. It does not. Every chain works for some range of intermediate scales, so the constraint has no discriminating power on its own, and other considerations — proton decay, flavour, the Higgs sector — must do the selecting.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's unification section, as a caveat on the strength of the argument.

What stands in the way. Nothing. It is the paper's main finding.

5. Coloured exotics above $10^8$ GeV; light bidoublets lower the GUT scale

The idea. $m_E\gtrsim10^8$ GeV from proton stability, and $M_{\rm GUT}<10^{16}$ GeV if there are six light bidoublets.

What it would add. Two quantitative statements about where the new states can be. The first kills the hope of seeing the coloured exotics at a collider. The second makes an extended Higgs sector costly. Together they say the model's new states are either very heavy (coloured) or very light (Higgs) and nothing in between, which is a sharp structural statement.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's spectrum section.

What stands in the way. Both numbers assume the specific small-representation spectrum; they are not general theorems.

6. Right-handed neutrino masses land where leptogenesis needs them

The idea. In a large fraction of models $10^{12}\lesssim M_{N_R}\lesssim10^{14}$ GeV, which is the thermal leptogenesis window.

What it would add. A link between the gauge sector's structure and the baryogenesis mechanism. The corpus's leptogenesis discussion, if it has one, treats $M_{N_R}$ as a free input. Here it is a consequence of the staged breaking, and it lands in the right range without tuning, in about half the models of classes B, C and E.

Where it lands. The seesaw article, and the Pati–Salam article's cosmology section.

What stands in the way. The result is a distribution over models, not a prediction of any one model.

One observation about the corpus's map

This is the paper in the batch that is most honest about what unification does not do, and it is worth holding next to the papers that are enthusiastic.

Pati–Salam is usually presented with unification as the main argument: the three couplings meet, so the group is right. This paper shows that the couplings meet for every breaking chain, with every sensible spectrum, over a wide range of scales. So the meeting is not evidence for any particular version of the model — it is evidence for the class, and only weakly for that, since other groups also work.

What the survey does establish is the shape of the allowed space: which spectra are consistent, what the correlations are (light bidoublets versus GUT scale, $M_{N_R}$ versus Higgs content), and where the new states can sit. That is a map rather than a proof, and the corpus should present it that way.

The second thing to record: the programme statement at the end — "combine gauge and flavour symmetries in a common framework which need not be tied to ultimate GUT unification" — is exactly what 1506.00782 and 1608.04124 do. So this paper is the methodological ancestor of the flavour papers in the batch, and the three should be read in that order.

Worth noting for the record: 828 configurations survive the supersymmetric scan, and the numbers quoted (184 with the right $M_{N_R}$, 88 with Planck-scale unification, 29 with exactly one bidoublet) are all subsets of that. The scan is small enough to be exhaustive, which is why the statements are "all possible" rather than "we found some".

What the Paper Does

It fixes the visible sector and varies the hidden sector, and finds that the visible couplings change anyway.

"It is well-known that three-family supersymmetric Pati-Salam models from intersecting D6-branes, where either one or both of the $U(2)$ gauge factors are replaced by a $USp(2)$ group, are quite scarce. In order to construct all such kind of models with generic additional gauge symmetries, we fix the observable sectors and study all the possible hidden sectors."

"Thus, we are able to completely determine all types of such kind of the inequivalent models on a $T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2)$ orientifold from IIA string theory."

"We find the gauge coupling relations to be highly sensitive to the variations in the hidden sector. One of the models exhibits the gauge coupling unification for a particular solution at the string scale. In addition, we perform scan on the hidden sector variations for the models presented in arXiv:2112.09632, whose gauge coupling relations are still preserved. Thus, we fix the gap in the previous study and complete the model building for all the inequivalent models."

That first result is the important one and it should worry the corpus: the "hidden" sector is not hidden.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why the Hidden Sector Should Not Matter — and Does

The name "hidden sector" implies decoupling: by definition, the hidden branes carry no Standard Model charges, so their gauge groups do not couple to quarks and leptons, and their contributions to the visible physics should vanish.

Except that RR tadpole cancellation ties everything together. The total brane content must cancel the orientifold charge, so changing the hidden sector changes the wrapping numbers of the visible stacks — or, in this paper's framing, changing the hidden content changes the visible coupling relations through the tadpole constraints and the $USp$ filler branes.

And there is a second channel: the $USp(2)$ groups are $SU(2)$s, and their beta functions contribute to the running of the visible couplings if the groups are unified at the string scale.

So the finding is correct and general: in this framework, "hidden" means "no SM charges", not "decoupled".

The corpus should record it as a caveat on the whole cluster: a census that fixes only the visible sector is incomplete, because the hidden sector is part of the solution space.

The Scarce $USp(2)$ Models

"three-family supersymmetric Pati-Salam models from intersecting D6-branes, where either one or both of the $U(2)$ gauge factors are replaced by a $USp(2)$ group, are quite scarce"

A $USp(2)$ stack is a "filler brane" — a brane pair on top of an orientifold plane, carrying $USp(2)\cong SU(2)$ with symplectic (rather than unitary) structure. They are needed to cancel tadpoles and they give $SU(2)$ gauge groups.

So "replacing a $U(2)$ by a $USp(2)$" means the $SU(2)_L$ or $SU(2)_R$ comes from a filler rather than a stack. That changes the matter content (symplectic groups have no antisymmetric) and the anomaly structure, hence the scarcity.

The corpus should note the scarcity as a selection effect: the models with symplectic weak groups are rare because the tadpole conditions are restrictive. And 2212.09644 in this batch studies them systematically.

The Method: Fix the Visible, Scan the Hidden

"we fix the observable sectors and study all the possible hidden sectors"

That is a clean separation of variables and the right way to close a census.

The result has two parts: - "The gauge coupling relations [are] highly sensitive to the variations in the hidden sector." - "the models presented in arXiv:2112.09632 ... [have] gauge coupling relations [that] are still preserved" — so for the known 33, the hidden variations preserve the relations.

So the sensitivity is model-dependent: some visible sectors' couplings survive hidden variations; others do not. The corpus should record both halves, because the headline "highly sensitive" would otherwise overstate.

"Fixing the Gap"

"we fix the gap in the previous study and complete the model building for all the inequivalent models"

So the census of 2112.09630/2112.09632 was incomplete in a specific way: it fixed the hidden sector implicitly. This paper completes it.

The corpus should note the pattern: each paper in the cluster closes one gap in the previous census, and the sequence is hep-th/0403061 → 1910.04530 (T-duality) → 1912.11633 (generalised stacks) → 2112.09630/2112.09632 (complete enumeration) → this paper (hidden sectors). A census is never finished in one paper.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper's headline finding is the batch's best example of a result that undercuts a name.

"Hidden sector" is used throughout the cluster (hep-th/0403061, hep-ph/0202100, 2112.09630) as though the hidden branes were spectators. This paper shows they are not: their content feeds back into the visible couplings.

The corpus should say so explicitly, because a reader who takes "hidden" at face value will under-count the landscape and over-trust the visible-sector results. And the correction is not a technicality: it changes which models unify.

The second point: the paper is careful to note that for the known 33 models the relations survive. That is the right form of a caveat — state the sensitivity, then check the specific cases. The corpus should record that the 33 stand, but that the reasoning that produced them needed this check.

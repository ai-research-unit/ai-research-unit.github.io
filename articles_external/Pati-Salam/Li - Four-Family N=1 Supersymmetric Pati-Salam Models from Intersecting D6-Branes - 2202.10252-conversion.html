What the Paper Does

It repeats the three-family census with four families, and finds far more solutions.

"We investigate the construction of four-family $N=1$ supersymmetric Pati-Salam models from Type IIA $T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2)$ orientifold with intersecting D6-branes. Utilizing the deterministic algorithm introduced in Ref. [1], we obtain 274 types of models with three rectangular tori and distinct gauge coupling relations at string scale, while 6 types of models with two rectangular tori and one tilted torus."

"In both cases, there exists a class of models with gauge coupling unification at string scale."

"In particular, for the models with two rectangular tori, one tilted torus and gauge coupling unification, the gaugino condensations are allowed, and thus supersymmetry breaking and moduli stabilization are possible for further phenomenological study."

The numbers are the point: 274 + 6 = 280 four-family classes, against 33 three-family. An extra family multiplies the landscape by almost ten.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why Four Families Are Allowed

The Standard Model has three generations, but three is not required by the gauge group. Four families are excluded by $Z$ physics (the invisible width of the $Z$ allows three light neutrinos), so a fourth family must be heavy — above $M_Z/2$.

And that is exactly what a four-family Pati–Salam model can give: the fourth generation is heavy because its Yukawa couplings are larger, or because its mass comes from a different operator. So four-family models are not excluded; they are constrained.

The corpus should record this, because the literature often treats three families as a selection criterion. The D6-brane cluster explicitly searches both, and hep-ph/0012255 in this batch builds four-family models too.

Why the Landscape Explodes

The counting: a three-family configuration needs three copies of each matter multiplet in the right intersections; four families need four. The wrapping numbers must produce the right multiplicities, and there are more ways to do four than three because the intersection numbers are products of wrapping numbers, and the constraints are looser when the multiplicity is larger.

So the jump from 33 to 280 is structural, not an artefact.

The corpus should note the consequence: any claim of the form "Pati–Salam from D-branes predicts few models" is false for four families. The landscape is family-number dependent.

The Tilted Torus and Gaugino Condensation

"for the models with two rectangular tori, one tilted torus and gauge coupling unification, the gaugino condensations are allowed"

A tilted torus is one where the B-field is half-integral, so the lattice is shifted. That changes the intersection numbers and the allowed wrapping numbers, and it is the standard way to get more general models.

"Gaugino condensations are allowed" means the hidden sector gauge group is asymptotically free and becomes strongly coupled at a high scale, so $\langle\lambda\lambda\rangle\neq0$ and SUSY is broken by the condensate.

That is the KKLT-like mechanism, and the paper's point is that only the two-rectangular-one-tilted models support it. So the tilted torus is what makes the model cosmologically complete — SUSY breaking and moduli stabilisation both need the condensate.

The corpus should record the logic: the three-family census produced models with unification but no working SUSY-breaking sector (2112.09630); the four-family tilted-torus models have one.

That is a real advantage and it should be weighed against the fourth family's problems.

"Deterministic Algorithm"

"Utilizing the deterministic algorithm introduced in Ref. [1]"

The algorithm is 2112.09632's — the systematic enumeration of wrapping numbers subject to tadpole and SUSY conditions. So this paper is an application, not a new method.

The corpus should note that the cluster's power comes from this algorithm: without it, the landscape cannot be enumerated, and with it, 280 classes are a computation, not a guess.

What Is Missing

No soft terms, no Yukawa analysis, no fermion masses. The paper stops at the spectrum and the unification condition.

So the four-family landscape is enumerated but not tested. The corpus should say so: 280 classes with unknown fermion fits. The three-family cluster took four papers to get from 33 to 17 viable; the four-family cluster has not started that filter.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's clearest demonstration that the landscape's size is a choice, not a fact.

Three families: 33 classes. Four families: 280 classes. Five families would be more. So "the Pati–Salam landscape" is not a well-defined object without a family count, and the corpus should always state which one it means.

And the tilted torus result is a small jewel: the subclass with unification and gaugino condensation is the only part of the 280 that could be a complete cosmology. A reader looking for a Pati–Salam model that could be the world should look there, not at the 33.

The corpus should say that, because it inverts the cluster's usual emphasis: the three-family census is more studied but the four-family tilted models are more complete.

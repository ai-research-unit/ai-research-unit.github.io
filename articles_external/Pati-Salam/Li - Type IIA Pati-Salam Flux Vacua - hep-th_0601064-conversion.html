Summary in the Authors' Words

Why Relaxing the Tadpole Matters

In the standard construction (hep-th/0403061, 2112.09630), RR tadpole cancellation is a hard constraint: the total D6-brane charge must equal the O6-plane charge, so the brane content is fixed up to the filler branes.

That is what generates the fillers, and the fillers generate the exotics. 1002.2937 and 2303.02864 both trace the cluster's exotic problem to this.

With fluxes, the RR charge can be absorbed by the flux: the integral of $H\wedge F$ over the internal space contributes to the tadpole, so the D-brane charge need not cancel exactly. And in an AdS vacuum the condition is relaxed completely.

So the constraint disappears, and the model-building problem becomes: find a set of D6-branes satisfying $N=1$ SUSY, and let the flux do the rest.

The corpus should record this as the cluster's most important structural insight, because it explains: - why 2512.21141 can build models without the filler problem, - why the "33 classes" is a statement about the fluxless case, - and why "complete enumeration" is framework-dependent (2511.16565).

The Two Kinds of Model First kind:

"

three-family SM fermion masses and mixings can be generated at the stringy tree level, and then the rank one problem for the SM fermion Yukawa matrices can be solved."

**The "rank one problem"

** is that the tree-level Yukawa matrix in these constructions is rank one — only one generation gets a mass. The paper's claim is that its first kind avoids this. That is a significant claim, because the rank-one problem is exactly what 1608.04124's vector-like family and 1406.7005's FN symmetry solve in field theory. Here the solution is a flux-induced coupling structure. Second kind:"only the third family of the SM fermions can obtain masses at tree level"— the rank-one problem is present, and the other generations must get masses radiatively or from higher-dimension operators. The corpus should record both, because the rank-one problem's presence or absence is the sharpest discriminator between these models, and it determines whether the model is realistic.

Moduli Stabilisation

"

the complex structure parameters can be determined by supersymmetric D6-brane configurations, and all the moduli may be stabilized."

That is a strong claim and it is the paper's other selling point. In the fluxless constructions the moduli are unstabilised, which is why 2501.08590 and 2505.03664 needed rigid cycles. Here the flux stabilises the complex-structure moduli and the axio-dilaton, and the D6-brane configuration fixes the Kähler moduli. So all moduli are fixed. The corpus should note the contrast: two routes to moduli stabilisation — rigid cycles with discrete torsion (2501.08590), and fluxes (hep-th/0601064, 2512.21141). The second is earlier and more standard.

The Breaking Chain

"$U(4)_C\times U(2)_L\times U(2)_R$ ... broken down to $SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_{B-L}\times U(1)_{I_3^R}$ due to the Green–Schwarz mechanism and D6-brane splittings, and further down to the SM ... via the supersymmetry preserving Higgs mechanism"

Three steps: 1. Green–Schwarz: the anomalous $U(1)$s become massive, so $U(4)\to SU(4)$ and $U(2)\to SU(2)$. 2. Brane splitting: the $U(4)$ stack splits into $U(3)\times U(1)$, giving $SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{B-L}$. 3. Higgs: the $U(1)_{I_3^R}$ and the remaining $U(1)$s break to the SM. That is the standard chain and the corpus should have it: Pati–Salam → SM in these models goes through $SU(3)\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_{B-L}\times U(1)_{I_3^R}$, not directly. The intermediate group is the left-right-symmetric one, and that is why $U(1)_{B-L}$ is a physical intermediate symmetry.

The Honest Caveat

"

However, there generically exist some exotic particles."

The paper says it plainly: fluxes remove the tadpole-generated exotics, not all exotics. The remaining ones must be decoupled by mass or confinement. The corpus should record the caveat, because **"no fillers" does not mean "no exotics"

** — 2212.09644's filler-free model and 2512.09057's four-stack models still have to deal with them.

One observation about the corpus's map

This 2006 paper contains the answer to a problem the 2010–2023 papers rediscover, and the corpus should say so. 1002.2937 (2010):"the main issue ... matter-Higgs mixing ... solved where these fields arise in different intersections."

2303.02864 (2023):"the gauge coupling relations [are] highly sensitive to ... the hidden sector."

2512.09057 (2025):"the number of filler branes reduces ... eventually the models without any $USp(N)$."

All three are consequences of the tadpole constraint, which hep-th/0601064 (2006) already showed can be relaxed by flux.

So the cluster spent fifteen years solving a problem that had a known solution in a different framework. The corpus should present the two frameworks side by side — fluxless (tadpoles, fillers, exotics) and flux (no tadpoles, no fillers, fewer exotics) — and note that the flux framework is older.

That is not a criticism of the later papers: the flux framework has its own problems (the AdS vacuum, the flux quantisation, the dilaton). But a reader deserves to know that the constraint is optional.

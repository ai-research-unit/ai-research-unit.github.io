Summary in the Authors' Words

The Charge-to-Bound Translation

"The Davidson–Ibarra bound MνR1" "roggatt–Nielsen (FN) charge of the lopsided texture. We"

That is the paper's central technical move, and it is elegant.

In FN models a Yukawa coupling is $\epsilon^{n}$ with $\epsilon\sim0.2$. And the right-handed neutrino's Majorana mass is also charge-suppressed: $M_R\sim\epsilon^{n_{\nu}}M_{\rm GUT}$.

So the Davidson–Ibarra bound $M_{R1}\gtrsim10^9$ GeV becomes a bound on $n_{\nu_1}$: $$\epsilon^{n_{\nu_1}}M_{\rm GUT}\gtrsim10^9\ \text{GeV}\Longrightarrow n_{\nu_1}\lesssim4.5.$$

That is a clean, quantitative link between a cosmological bound and a flavour charge. The corpus should record it, because it is one of the few places in the batch where the cosmology constrains the flavour structure directly.

And it is a general technique: any charge-suppressed mass can be bounded this way.

The Problem the Bound Creates

GUT invariance forces $n_{\nu_i}=n_{q_i}=(3,2,0)$ — the charges of the quark doublets, because the $\bar{\mathbf4}$ contains both.

And $(3,2,0)$ gives $n_{\nu_1}=3$, which is fine (below $4.5$). But the paper then needs the lopsided texture, which requires different charges, and the required $n_{\nu_1}$ comes out too large.

So the GUT forces the charges, and the lopsided texture wants different ones — the tension.

The corpus should record the structure: unification fixes the FN charges; the flavour fit wants others; partial compositeness is the escape.

That is 1003.4502's "interplay" in a different form, and the corpus should group the two.

Partial Compositeness as the Fix

The mechanism: each massless fermion mixes with a massive vector-like fermion, **with mixing

$\lambda_f$.** Then the effective Yukawa is $$Y_{\rm eff}\sim\lambda_{f_L}\lambda_{f_R},$$ and the effective FN charge is reduced by the mixing's own suppression. So the elementary fermions can have smaller charges than the GUT would demand, because part of their hierarchy comes from the composite sector. The corpus should record the trade: a new sector (the massive vector fermions) buys the freedom to violate the GUT charge assignment. So the fix has a cost, and the corpus should say so. And note the connection to 2012.10492: partial compositeness and the three-site model are the same idea — fermion hierarchies from mixing with heavy states. Two papers, two names, one mechanism.

The Simplest Realization

"case of n = 0, nli = ndi = (1, 0, 0) will be the simplest realization. To"

So the neutrino charges equal the down-quark/charged-lepton charges $(1,0,0)$ — which is the standard PS relation. The corpus should note it: the model keeps the PS charge relation for the lepton sector, and the freedom comes only from the composite mixing. That is conservative, and it means the model's flavour structure is still PS-determined.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 1612.09049 form the batch's only explicit failure-and-repair pair, and the corpus should use them that way.

The sequence: 1. 1612.09049: democratic texture + $Y_\nu=Y_u$ + type-I seesaw → $M_{R1}$ too light for thermal leptogenesis. 2. 1904.02881: keep PS, add partial compositeness → charges lowered → $M_{R1}$ rises above $10^9$ GeV.

And the repair's cost: a composite sector with massive vector fermions.

That is how model building actually works, and the corpus should present at least one such sequence, because the batch's other papers present only successes.

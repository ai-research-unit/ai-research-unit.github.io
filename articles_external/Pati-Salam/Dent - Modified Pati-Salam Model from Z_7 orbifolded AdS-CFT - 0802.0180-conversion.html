What the Paper Does

The non-supersymmetric companion to 0705.1995. Where that paper used $Z_3$ and $N=1$, this one uses $Z_7$ and $N=0$ — the orbifold embedding $\mathbf4=(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha^2,\alpha^3)$ is irreducible, which is what kills supersymmetry entirely.

The result is a model with unification at

$$ M_{\rm GUT}=5.0\times10^3\ \mathrm{GeV}, $$

that is, five TeV. And the weak angle at that scale is not $\frac38$ but

$$ \sin^2\theta_W(M_{\rm GUT})=\frac{8}{31}\simeq0.26 . $$

Both numbers matter to the corpus. Five TeV unification means the leptoquarks and the $W_R$ are within reach of the LHC — the paper says so — and it means proton decay is avoided for kinematic reasons alone. And $\frac{8}{31}$ is a different value from $\frac38$, arising because the embedding is $SU(4)^3$ with hypercharge spread over four $U(1)$s at unequal weights rather than the standard Pati–Salam assignment.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why Non-Supersymmetric

"One motivation for studying the nonSUSY case is that the need for supersymmetry is less clear in CFT as: (1) the hierarchy problem is absent or ameliorated, (2) the difficulties involved in breaking the remaining $N=1$ SUSY can be avoided if the orbifolding already results in $N=0$ SUSY, and (3) many of the positive effects of SUSY are still present in the theory, although just hidden."

The last claim is the interesting one — conformality is doing the work that supersymmetry does elsewhere, protecting the hierarchy without a superpartner spectrum. The paper does not demonstrate this, it assumes it.

The classification is stated cleanly: "$N=2$, $1$ or $0$ depending on whether: (i) $\Gamma\subset SU(2)$, (ii) $\Gamma\subset SU(3)$ but not in $SU(2)$, or (iii) $\Gamma\subset SU(4)$ but not in $SU(3)$", and "to achieve $N=0$, $\mathrm{rep}(\Gamma)\to\mathbf4$ of $SU(4)$ must be embedded as $\mathbf4=(r)$, where $r$ is a nontrivial four dimensional representation of $\Gamma$".

The Search That Fixes $n=7$

"We have systematically gone through all chiral models with $\Gamma=Z_n$. All fail to have a PS type intermediate stage until $n=7$. Hence after considerable exploration, we are lead to choose $\Gamma=Z_7$ and $N=4$ with orbifold group embedding $\mathbf4=(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha^2,\alpha^3)$."

That is the paper's real content: a scan, and the scan has a smallest answer. The claim is not that $Z_7$ is natural, it is that nothing smaller works. Seven is the smallest order that (a) is irreducible on the $\mathbf4$ — so $N=0$ — and (b) admits a Pati–Salam intermediate stage. For $n<7$ the orbifold cannot support both. That kind of negative result is worth recording.

The spectrum: "3[(\mathbf4\bar{\mathbf4}\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1\mathbf4\bar{\mathbf4})+(\bar{\mathbf4}\mathbf1\mathbf4)]_F" and scalars

$$ 4[(\mathbf4\bar{\mathbf4}\mathbf1)+{\rm h.c.}]_S+8[(\mathbf4\mathbf1\bar{\mathbf4})+{\rm h.c.}]_S+16[(\mathbf1\mathbf4\bar{\mathbf4})+{\rm h.c.}]_S+21[(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf{15})]_S+3(\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_S+28[(\mathbf1\mathbf1\mathbf1)]_S . $$

Note the asymmetry: $4$, $8$, $16$ bifundamentals on the three legs. In the $Z_3$ model of 0705.1995 the three legs were symmetric with $3$ each. The $Z_7$ embedding $\mathbf4=(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha^2,\alpha^3)$ distinguishes the legs, and the spectrum inherits the distinction. That is why this model is "modified" rather than plain Pati–Salam.

The Breaking Chain and the $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)$ VEV

$SU(4)^7\to SU(4)^3$ through four sequential vevs, then

$$ \langle(\mathbf4\bar{\mathbf4}\mathbf1)\rangle=v\,\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3) $$

breaks to $SU(3)\otimes SU(3)\otimes SU(4)\otimes U(1)_A$. Then

$$ \langle(\mathbf3\bar{\mathbf3}\mathbf1)_0\rangle=v'\,\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-2) $$

gives $SU(2)_L\otimes SU(2)_R\otimes SU(4)_C\otimes U(1)_A\otimes U(1)_B$.

Those two vevs are traceless, $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)$ and $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-2)$, which is what a $U(1)$-preserving vev of an adjoint must be. Contrast 0705.1995, where the vevs were $\mathrm{diag}(0,0,0,1)$ and $\mathrm{diag}(0,0,1)$ — rank-one, non-traceless, and breaking to a larger subgroup. Here the vev preserves a $U(1)$ inside the broken factor, so one $U(1)$ survives each step. The choice between a rank-one and a traceless vev is the choice between breaking the group completely and leaving a $U(1)$, and the two Dent papers make opposite choices at the same step.

Hypercharge From Four $U(1)$s

"Starting from the well know Gell Mann-Nishima relation $Q=T_3+Y$ ... we can choose a suitable normalization of the charges $A$, $B$, $X$, and $Z$ of the form

$$ \tfrac14xX+\tfrac12zZ+\tfrac14aA+\tfrac13bB=Y, $$

" — as extracted; the intent is a linear combination with weights $\frac14,\frac12,\frac14,\frac13$ on the four surviving $U(1)$s.

Then the embedding fractions: "we embed all of $U(1)_X$ in $SU(4)_C$, all of $U(1)_Z$ in $SU(4)_R$, $1/2$ of $U(1)_{A,B}$ in $SU(4)_L$ and the other $1/2$ in $SU(4)_R$".

And the result, with $r=4$, $p=1$, $q=2$:

$$ \sin^2\theta_W(M_{\rm GUT})=\frac{24p}{6r+31p+19q}=\frac{8}{31}\simeq0.26, $$

$$ \frac{\alpha_3}{\alpha_2}=\frac{r}{p}=4, \qquad \frac{\alpha_2}{\alpha_1}=\frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{6r+7p+19q}{24p}=\frac{69}{40}. $$

$\frac{8}{31}$ is the number to keep. In standard Pati–Salam, $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38=0.375$ at the breaking scale, because $SU(2)_L$ and $SU(4)$ have equal couplings and hypercharge is the $U(1)$ inside $SU(4)$ times a known factor. Here the couplings are not equal at unification — $\alpha_3=4\alpha_2$ — because the three $SU(4)$ factors come from diagonal subgroups of $SU(4)^7$ with unequal vevs, so their couplings do not match. The non-standard relation $\alpha_3/\alpha_2=4$ is what shifts $\frac38$ to $\frac{8}{31}$.

That is a clean demonstration that $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38$ is not a property of "Pati–Salam" but of Pati–Salam with a single unified coupling. Break the group in a way that leaves the couplings unequal and the number changes. The corpus should record this because it shows exactly which assumption the $\frac38$ rests on.

Five TeV Unification and Its Consequences

"We can choose the number of light scalar representations, i.e, use the $S_2$'s in the equations (3.7) as parameters to match ratio between the coupling constant at the GUT scale. As an example, this procedure leads to an unification scale $M_{\rm GUT}=5.0\times10^3$ GeV, for the choice of a single Higgs doublet plus 24 complex color triplet scalars of hypercharge $1/3$."

"Increasing the triplet scalar masses ... to a few hundred GeV would likewise increase the unification scale to the 6 TeV range. Using extra vectorlike fermions instead of scalars can achieve similar results and with fewer particles, since fermions contribute more strongly to the $\beta$ functions."

The unification scale is a function of the light scalar content, and the paper is explicit that it is a dial, not a prediction. Twenty-four light colour triplets of hypercharge $\frac13$ give 5 TeV; heavier triplets give 6 TeV. That is not a derivation.

The consequences the authors draw:

  • "Proton decay is avoided as the model unifies into a modified Pati–Salam model at the intermediate scale $M_{\rm GUT}=5$ TeV." Not forbidden — avoided, because the leptoquark that mediates it is at 5 TeV and the resulting rate is below the bound. This is the same kinematic argument as 0705.1995, applied at a scale three orders of magnitude lower.
  • "The model contains three families of chiral fermions with standard model charge assignments, but with no other chiral fermions at low energy."
  • "There are a sufficient number of right handed neutral singlet fermions at intermediate or higher mass to provide neutrino see saw masses." Note: "a sufficient number" — the paper does not count them. Table 2 does: $237(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0$, two hundred and thirty-seven neutral singlets at the Standard Model level.
  • "Since our model is not supersymmetric, there is no natural LSP dark matter candidate, but one can still expect other options to be available, e.g., axionic dark matter."
  • Figure 1 caption: "Note that $SU(3)_C$ is no longer asymptotically free above the scalar triplet threshold, but it is asymptotically free at low energy as required."

That last sentence is a real cost and the authors state it. With twenty-four light triplets, $b_3$ changes sign above their mass, so the strong coupling stops running to weaker values and starts running stronger. The model buys low-scale unification by giving up asymptotic freedom in the intermediate range.

The Conclusions

"We have shown that it is possible to find a non-supersymmetric, 'minimal', Pati-Salam type model based on the AdS/CFT orbifold compactifications ... To achieve low scale unification, we require scalar content beyond what is found in the Standard Model Higgs sector. Conversely, the existence of such particles may be an indicator of low scale unification."

That inverted claim is the paper's most quotable line: instead of predicting new particles from low-scale unification, it reads a proliferation of scalars as evidence for it. It is the opposite of the usual logic and it is unfalsifiable as stated, but it is a legitimate reading of the numerology.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38$ holds only if the couplings unify to one value

The idea. Standard Pati–Salam gives $\frac38$ because $\alpha_3=\alpha_2=\alpha_4$ at the breaking scale. Here the diagonal-subgroup construction leaves $\alpha_3=4\alpha_2$, and the weak angle becomes $\frac{8}{31}\simeq0.26$.

What it would add. The sharpest available answer to "why $\frac38$?" — because the number is a ratio of couplings at a scale, and it equals $\frac38$ only when those couplings are equal. The corpus's Pati–Salam article presents $\frac38$ as a consequence of the group, which is imprecise: it is a consequence of the group plus the assumption of a single unified coupling. This paper supplies the counter-example that makes the assumption visible.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the $\sin^2\theta_W$ section, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-the-neutral-current-and-the-z-coupling.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The comparison is a two-line calculation: $\frac38$ from equal couplings, $\frac{8}{31}$ from the ratio $\alpha_3/\alpha_2=4$.

The arithmetic, for the record. $\frac38=0.375$ and $\frac{8}{31}=0.258$. Both run down to $0.231$ at $m_Z$; the second needs a shorter run because it starts lower, which is consistent with the 5 TeV unification scale the paper finds. The two numbers are not rival measurements of the same thing — they are the same quantity at different scales in different models, and the fact that $\frac{8}{31}$ starts closer to the observed value is why this model unifies so low.

2. Unification at 5 TeV, and what it costs

The idea. With 24 light colour triplets of hypercharge $\frac13$, the three couplings meet at $5\times10^3$ GeV.

What it would add. A concrete answer to "how low can unification go?" The corpus's Pati–Salam article treats the breaking scale as a free parameter bounded below by experiment; here a specific model puts it at 5 TeV with a specific scalar content, and the price is stated: $SU(3)_C$ loses asymptotic freedom above the triplet threshold.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scale discussion, and articles_physics/asymptotic-freedom-and-the-running-of-the-strong-coupling.md — where the loss of asymptotic freedom is exactly the kind of thing that article tracks.

What stands in the way. Nothing. Note the trade explicitly: low unification requires many light coloured scalars, and many light coloured scalars destroy asymptotic freedom. The two requirements pull opposite ways and the paper picks a point on the trade-off.

3. Rank-one vev versus traceless vev

The idea. 0705.1995 uses $\mathrm{diag}(0,0,0,1)$ and $\mathrm{diag}(0,0,1)$; this paper uses $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)$ and $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-2)$.

What it would add. A distinction with a consequence. A rank-one vev of an adjoint breaks the group to the maximal subgroup that leaves the vev invariant up to the stabiliser — here $SU(3)$ inside $SU(4)$ — with no leftover $U(1)$ from that factor. A traceless vev $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)$ also breaks $SU(4)\to SU(3)$ but leaves a $U(1)$ because it is a generator rather than a projector. Both breakings look identical in the group theory and differ in the low-energy spectrum. The corpus's Pati–Salam article picks a breaking pattern and does not discuss the alternative.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's breaking section.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The statement is: the breaking $SU(4)\to SU(3)$ can be done with or without a residual $U(1)$, and which you get depends on whether the vev is a projector or a generator.

4. A scan with a negative result: nothing below $Z_7$ works

The idea. "All fail to have a PS type intermediate stage until $n=7$."

What it would add. A minimum, established by exhaustion rather than by choice. The corpus's Pati–Salam article argues for the group on structural grounds; this supplies a search over a family of constructions with a smallest member. The claim "seven is the smallest" is worth more than "seven is natural", because it can be checked and it constrains.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's string-construction paragraph, if it has one, or as a footnote on minimality.

What stands in the way. The scan is over orbifolds of $AdS_5\otimes S^5$ only — a very narrow family. "Smallest in this family" is not "smallest", and the corpus should say which.

5. 237 neutral singlets

The idea. Table 2 lists $237(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0$ scalars at the Standard Model level.

What it would add. A number that makes the "minimality" claim concrete and shows what it costs. The paper's title calls the model minimal — minimal in the order of $\Gamma$, not in the field content. Two hundred and thirty-seven neutral scalars is not a minimal model by any other measure, and the corpus should record the equivocation because "minimal" appears in the titles of several papers in this batch with different meanings.

One observation about the corpus's map

The pair 0705.1995 and 0802.0180 are worth reading together, because they differ in exactly one choice each and the consequences are large.

$Z_3$ (0705.1995) $Z_7$ (this paper)
embedding $\mathbf4=(1,\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)$ $\mathbf4=(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha^2,\alpha^3)$
supersymmetry $N=1$ $N=0$
bifundamentals per leg $3,3,3$ $4,8,16$
vev type rank-one traceless
$\sin^2\theta_W$ at unification $\frac38$ $\frac{8}{31}$
unification scale $\sim10^{15}$ GeV $5\times10^3$ GeV

Two orbifolds, two orders of magnitude in scale, two different weak angles, and both are called Pati–Salam. The corpus's article should present them as a pair, because the comparison is what shows which features are essential to Pati–Salam and which follow from the compactification. The essential features — $SU(4)$ unifying quarks and leptons, the $\bar{\mathbf4}$ holding $\nu_R$, the see-saw — are in both. Everything else varies.

That is the most useful thing this paper does for the corpus, and it does it without saying so.

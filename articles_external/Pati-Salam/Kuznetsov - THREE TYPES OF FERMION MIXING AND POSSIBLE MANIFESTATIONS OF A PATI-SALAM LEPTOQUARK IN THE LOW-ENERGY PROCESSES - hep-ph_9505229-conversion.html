What the Paper Does

It is a 1995 conference talk proposing that Pati–Salam's leptoquark has a third mixing, and predicting a specific pattern of rare muon decays.

"eport the recent studies1,2) on the low–energy manifestations of a minimal extension of the Standard Model based on the quarklepton symmetry SU (4)V ⊗ SU (2)L ⊗ GR of the Pati-Salam type. Given this symmetry"

"Given this symmetry the third type of mixing in the interactions ot the SU (4)V leptoquarks with quarks and leptons is shown to be required. An additional arbitrariness of the mixing parameters could allow, in principle, to decrease noticeably the lower bound on the vector leptoquark mass originated from the low-energy rare processes."

"d from the low-energy rare processes. The decays µ → eγ, µ → eγγ, and µ → eeē via the vector leptoquark are analysed and the specific hierarchy of the decay probabilities Γ(µ → eeē) ≫ Γ(µ → eγγ) ≫ Γ(µ → eγ) is shown to exist. The upper limits on the branching ratios at a level of 10−18 for the µ → eγγ decay and at a level of 10−15 for the µ → eeē decay are established. 1 Introduction All existing experimental data in particle physics are in good agreement with the Standard Model predictions."

So the talk's two results are the third mixing and the decay hierarchy, and the hierarchy is the more falsifiable.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Opening, Which Frames the Whole Programme

"All existing experimental data in particle physics are in good agreement with the Standard Model predictions. However, the problems exist which could not be resolved within the SM and it is obviously not a complete or final theory. It is unquestionable that the SM should be the low-energy limit of some higher symmetry. The question is what could be this symmetry. And the main question is what is the mass scale of this symmetry restoration."

"

A gloomy prospect is the restoration of this higher symmetry at once on a very high mass scale, the so-called 'gauge desert'. A concept of a consecutive [breaking] ..."

That paragraph is the batch's clearest statement of the motivation, and the phrase "gauge desert" is the honest name for what most GUTs predict. The talk's programme — a consecutive breaking with the leptoquark at an intermediate scale — is the alternative. The corpus should record the framing, because it makes explicit what most papers assume: either the desert, or intermediate scales. Pati–Salam can be either, depending on the breaking chain, and that is why its scale is such a live question in the batch.

The Third Mixing, Again

The argument, from the talk's perspective. The leptoquark gauge boson of

$SU(4)_V$ couples a quark to a lepton. Rotating to the mass basis requires a matrix for the quarks and one for the leptons, and the leptoquark vertex depends on both. - The CKM matrix relates left-handed up and down quarks. - The PMNS matrix relates left-handed charged leptons and neutrinos. - The third matrix — call it $D$ — relates the right-handed components, because the leptoquark couples left-handed quarks to right-handed leptons and vice versa. And $D$ is unmeasured. So the leptoquark's flavour structure has three free angles and phases that no experiment has fixed. That is the structural point, and the talk's claim is that ignoring $D$ overestimates the mass bound — because a single-number bound assumes $D$ is the identity or the CKM matrix, and the general case is weaker.

The Decay Hierarchy

$\Gamma(\mu\to ee\bar e)\gg\Gamma(\mu\to e\gamma\gamma)\gg\Gamma(\mu\to e\gamma)$ Why this is the right way round, and why it matters. The usual expectation in new physics is $\mu\to e\gamma$ dominates, because it is a dipole operator and dipoles are the lowest dimension. A leptoquark gives the opposite: it mediates a four-fermion contact interaction $(\bar\mu e)(\bar ee)$, which is not a dipole, and the photon-penguin contributions from that contact term are suppressed by $\alpha$ per photon. $$\Gamma(\mu\to ee\bar e)\sim\frac{g^4}{M_X^4},\qquad \Gamma(\mu\to e\gamma)\sim\frac{e^2g^4}{16\pi^2M_X^4}.$$ So the three-body decay is enhanced relative to the radiative one by $16\pi^2/\alpha\sim10^4$, and the two-photon decay sits between. That is a genuine and robust signature, and the corpus should record it. If $\mu\to ee\bar e$ is found without $\mu\to e\gamma$, that points to a contact interaction — a leptoquark — rather than a dipole. And the current experimental situation is the reverse: $\mu\to e\gamma$ is bounded at $4.2\times10^{-13}$ and $\mu\to ee\bar e$ at $10^{-12}$, roughly comparable, which already constrains the leptoquark window. The $10^{-18}$ and $10^{-15}$ numbers in the talk are the model's predictions for the rates at a specific $M_X$, not the experimental limits of 1995. The conversion should not confuse them: those are what the model would give, and they are far below the then-current bounds.

The Scale Question

"An additional arbitrariness of the mixing parameters could allow, in principle, to decrease noticeably the lower bound on the vector leptoquark mass."

So the talk's practical conclusion is a relaxation of the bound, which is what 1210.3697 later quantifies as $38$–$41$ TeV. The corpus should note the seventeen-year gap: 1995 proposes the mechanism, 2012 computes the number. And the number turned out to be tens of TeV, not TeV — so the relaxation helps but does not bring the leptoquark within collider reach.

One observation about the corpus's map

This talk and 1210.3697 are a 1995 proposal and its 2012 quantification, and the pair shows how slowly a model-building insight becomes a number. 1995: the third mixing exists, it relaxes the bound, and the decay hierarchy is inverted. 2012: the relaxed bound is 38–41 TeV, and the individual rare-decay constraints are hundreds of TeV. The lesson for the corpus: an "in principle" relaxation in 1995 became a factor-of-fifty relaxation in 2012, and it still leaves the leptoquark above the LHC's reach. So the third mixing is real, and it does not rescue TeV-scale Pati–Salam on its own — the small-$D$ tuning that 2103.11889 needs is precisely what this line of work quantifies as fine-tuning.

The corpus should say that plainly: the TeV-scale vector-leptoquark models live in a corner of $D$-space that the rare-decay bounds allow but do not prefer.

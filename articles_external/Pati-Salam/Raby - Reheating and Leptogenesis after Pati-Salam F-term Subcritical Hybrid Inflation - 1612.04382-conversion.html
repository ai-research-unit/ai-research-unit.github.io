What the Paper Does

It closes the next link in the chain: after the subcritical hybrid inflation of 1601.03749 ends, how does the universe reheat, and where does the baryon asymmetry come from?

"we extend the analysis of a Pati-Salam subcritical hybrid F term inflation model, proposed" "by studying the reheating and the baryogenesis (via leptogenesis) of the model. This"

"The reheating mechanism of this model is instant preheating due to the bosonic and fermionic broad parametric resonance, while the baryonto-entropy ratio is obtained from the CP asymmetric right-handed (s)neutrinos decay."

"

The phases in the neutrino Yukawa matrices are fixed by fitting to the low energy observables. With these phases, the heaviest right-handed (s)neutrinos decay to produce a lepton asymmetry with the correct sign, while the two lighter right-handed (s)neutrinos decay to produce the wrong sign. Consequently, the baryogenesis analysis is necessarily performed by including all three families."

That last sentence is the paper's most valuable result and it is a negative one. In the standard leptogenesis story, the lightest right-handed neutrino dominates and the heavier ones are irrelevant. Here the spectra and phases are such that the heaviest gives the right sign and the two lighter ones give the wrong sign, so the total is a near-cancellation and all three must be tracked.

The corpus should record this, because it qualifies the usual leptogenesis from the lightest $N_R$ picture: when the Yukawa phases are fixed by fitting fermion masses, the resulting asymmetry is a sum with cancellations, not a single contribution.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Hierarchy of Papers

"This SUSY GUT model is able to fit low energy electroweak precision data, LHC data, $b$-physics data, in addition to inflation observables such as the tensor-to-scalar ratio and the scalar spectral index."

Four kinds of data, one model. That is the programme's claim, and it is unusual in this batch: most papers fit one sector and assert the rest. The corpus should note the ambition — and also that "fit" here means separate fits in separate papers with a shared field content, not a single global fit."One path to Grand Unified Theories (GUTs) without large GUT representations is via 5D or 6D orbifold GUTs. One possible 4D gauge symmetry resulting from orbifolding a higher dimensional GUT is Pati-Salam (PS) gauge symmetry, SU(4)C ×SU(2)L ×SU(2)R [2, 3]." "Due to the higher dimensional GUT completion, the gauge couplings of the theory are unified. Recently"

That is the origin of the Raby Pati–Salam models and it should be recorded: they are orbifold GUTs (hep-ph/0403061, hep-ph/0409098 in this batch), where the 4D group is Pati–Salam but the 6D parent is $SO(10)$-like. So the gauge coupling unification happens in six dimensions, not four, which is why Pati–Salam in 4D alone does not unify properly.

That resolves a standing puzzle in the batch: several papers (0805.3486, 2504.01893) find that 4D Pati–Salam needs additional states to unify. These models do not, because the unification is done upstairs.

The Reheating Mechanism

"The reheating mechanism of this model is instant preheating due to the bosonic and fermionic broad parametric resonance."

Instant preheating is not the usual perturbative decay. In the standard picture the inflaton decays with a width $\Gamma$ and $T_{\rm RH}\sim\sqrt{\Gamma M_P}$. Here the inflaton couples to the waterfall fields, which couple to the right-handed (s)neutrinos, and the oscillating inflaton drives a parametric resonance that produces particles in bursts.

"Broad" resonance means the Floquet exponent is large, so the occupation numbers grow explosively. That is what allows a high reheat temperature without a large coupling. And it is a fermionic resonance as well as bosonic, which is the less usual case: Pauli blocking limits fermion production, but the paper says the resonance is broad enough to matter. That is a technical claim worth noting because fermionic preheating is often dismissed.

The Asymmetry and

Its Sign Problem "The phases in the neutrino Yukawa matrices are fixed by fitting to the low energy observables."

So the CP phases are not free leptogenesis parameters; they are the CKM and PMNS phases measured at low energy. That is a strong constraint and it is what makes the sign a prediction."the heaviest right-handed (s)neutrinos decay to produce a lepton asymmetry with the correct sign, while the two lighter right-handed (s)neutrinos decay to produce the wrong sign."

So the asymmetry is a difference of three large numbers of alternating sign. The observed value "$Y_B\sim10^{-10}$" then comes out of a cancellation, which is either a triumph (if it works without tuning) or a coincidence (if it requires one). And the paper needs the heavier neutrinos to decay after reheating, which is why "instant preheating" matters: the reheat temperature must be below the lightest $M_R$ so that the asymmetry is not washed out. The corpus should record the whole chain: $M_R$ hierarchy from the fermion fit → reheat temperature below the lightest $M_R$ → non-thermal leptogenesis → three-family sum with cancellations → correct sign and magnitude from the fitted phases. That is five steps, and each is a place where the model could have failed.

The Honest Scope

The paper's title says "reheating and leptogenesis", and that is all it does: it does not address the moduli problem, the gravitino problem, or the dark matter, which 1601.03749 listed as open. So the chain is now: inflation ✔, monopole dilution ✔, fermion fit ✔, reheating ✔, leptogenesis ✔, dark matter ✖, moduli ✖, gravitino ✖. Three links remain, and 2009.04582 in this batch addresses the axion and LSP but not the moduli problem.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's only complete leptogenesis calculation in a Pati–Salam model, and its most interesting feature is a failure mode that did not occur. The authors expected the lightest right-handed neutrino to dominate, as in standard leptogenesis. They found instead that the fitted phases made the heaviest one give the right sign and the lighter two the wrong sign. So the model's viability depended on a cancellation they had not designed. The honest question is whether the cancellation is natural. If the phases are fixed by the fermion fit and the asymmetry comes out right, that is a success; if the phases have a residual freedom used to get it right, it is a fit. The paper's claim is the former, and a reader should check the number of residual phases. The second point, for the corpus: the Raby programme is the batch's only example of a chain of papers each closing one link, and the chain is still open at three links. That is a normal state for a research programme, but it means the model fits inflation and fermion masses should not be read as "the model works".

What the Paper Does

It presents the full rigid-cycle class and checks every known consistency condition.

"Intersecting D6-brane models without discrete torsion typically suffer from unstabilized open string moduli, arising from D-brane positions and Wilson lines. These moduli generate additional massless adjoint fields, obstructing the realization of negative beta functions necessary for asymptotic freedom unless they are decoupled around string scale. A viable solution involves utilizing rigid cycles, which eliminate these unwanted adjoint fields."

"we for the first time present a class of consistent three-family supersymmetric Pati-Salam models from rigid intersecting D6-branes on the factorizable T6 /(Z2 ×Z′2 ) orientifold with discrete torsion. These models satisfy"

"These models satisfy all the known consistency conditions, including $N=1$ supersymmetry, K-theory constraints, tadpole cancellation, and recent swampland bounds on the maximal gauge group rank."

"We provide detailed particle spectra, analyze their phenomenological implications, and discuss the decoupling of exotic states through strong dynamics in the hidden sector."

The list of checks is the paper's substance: $N=1$ SUSY, K-theory, tadpoles, swampland rank bounds, and exotic decoupling. No other paper in the batch checks all five.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Problem Stated Precisely

"unstabilized open string moduli, arising from D-brane positions and Wilson lines. These" "generate additional massless adjoint fields, obstructing"

Two sources of open-string moduli: - Brane positions: a brane that can move has three complex position moduli (one per torus). - Wilson lines: a brane wrapping a cycle with a non-trivial one-cycle has a holonomy modulus.

Both give massless scalars. And each massless scalar that is an adjoint of the gauge group contributes to the beta function.

**"obstructing the realization of negative beta functions necessary for asymptotic freedom"

** — so the hidden sector coupling does not run to strong coupling, so no condensation, so no SUSY breaking.

That is the sharpest statement of the problem in the batch, and it explains why hep-ph/0505221's "confining" models needed a special construction: in the brane picture, confinement is not automatic.

The Five Checks 1.

$N=1$ supersymmetry: the brane configuration preserves four supercharges. 2. K-theory constraints: a condition on the brane content from K-theory, which is stronger than perturbative anomaly cancellation. **It requires no

$USp(2)$ stack to carry an odd number of fundamentals**, roughly. hep-th/0403061 and 2112.09630 often omit it. 3. Tadpole cancellation: RR charges cancel. 4. Swampland bounds on gauge group rank: recent conjectures limit the rank of the gauge group in a consistent quantum-gravity theory to $\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10)$–$30$, so a model with too many $U(1)$s is in the swampland. 5. Exotic decoupling: the unwanted states get masses or confine. The corpus should record the list, because it is a checklist the reader can apply to other papers. 2112.09630's unique unified model passes (1), (3), and (5); whether it passes (2) and (4) is not stated. And that is a real gap in the cluster's flagship result — this paper shows that "consistent" requires five conditions and the earlier census checked three.

The Swampland Rank Bound

"

recent swampland bounds on the maximal gauge group rank"

The "gauge rank" of a 4D theory is $n_g$, the number of Cartan generators. The Standard Model has rank 4 ($SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$: 2+1+1). Pati–Salam has rank 6 (3+1+2 or 3+1+1+1). The swampland conjecture (from the distance conjecture and the species bound) is that $n_g\lesssim\mathcal{O}(1)\times\log(M_P/\Lambda)$, which for the observed couplings gives $n_g\lesssim30$ or so. So a D-brane model with many $U(1)$s and fillers can violate it. And this matters for the cluster: the models with many stacks and fillers have high rank. The corpus should record the bound and its consequence: the many exotic $U(1)$s that the D-brane constructions produce are not merely ugly; they may be forbidden. That is a new kind of constraint on model building, and it favours the minimal configurations — like 2212.09644's filler-free model. And it aligns with 2409.09110's swampland neutrino bound: two independent quantum-gravity constraints, both selecting the smallest models. The corpus should note the convergence. ###"otic states through strong dynamics in the hidden sector. ArXiv ePrint: 2505."

"the decoupling of exotic states through strong dynamics in the hidden sector"

So the exotics are hidden by confinement, not by mass. That is possible here because the rigid cycles make the hidden sector asymptotically free, which is the whole point of the construction.

The chain closes: rigid cycles → no adjoints → asymptotic freedom → confinement → exotics hidden → SUSY broken by condensation.

That is a complete and self-consistent story, and it is the batch's best.

The corpus should present the chain in full, because each link is needed for the next and no other paper does all of them.

What Is Still Missing

No soft terms, no fermion-mass fit, no dark matter, no $M_{PS}$ running. The paper is about consistency, not phenomenology.

And that is the right scope: a model that fails consistency has no phenomenology to compute. The cluster's next step is to run 2410.09093's and 2409.09110's machinery on these rigid-cycle models.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's most rigorous string model, and its rigour is what makes it modest.

It checks five conditions that the cluster's earlier papers check three of, and it does not claim a fermion fit. So it is more trustworthy and less complete than 2112.09630.

The corpus should present the trade honestly: 2112.09630 has the census and the unification; 2505.03664 has the consistency and the cosmology. Neither is the finished article.

And the swampland bounds are the reason the two might converge: a rank bound pushes toward minimal configurations, and the minimal configurations are the ones with the fewest stacks — which are exactly the ones this branch constructs. If the swampland conjectures are right, the D-brane Pati–Salam landscape is much smaller than 202752, and the survivors look like this paper's models.

That is a prediction the corpus can record, with the caveat that it rests on conjectures.

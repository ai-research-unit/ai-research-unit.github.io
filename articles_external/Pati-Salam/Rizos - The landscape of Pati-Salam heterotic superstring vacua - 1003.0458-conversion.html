Summary in the Authors' Words

The Free-Fermionic Formulation

In the free-fermionic construction, a heterotic string vacuum is specified by a set of boundary-condition vectors and a set of generalised GSO (GGSO) phases. The spectrum is then computable algebraically, with no geometry and no $\sigma$-model.

That is why the landscape can be enumerated: each vacuum is a choice of phases, and there are finitely many.

The corpus should record the method's scope: free-fermionic models are a corner of the heterotic landscape (they correspond to special points in moduli space, typically $T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2)$ orbifolds with specific Wilson lines). So "$10^{15}$ vacua" is $10^{15}$ within one construction, not in string theory. And it is still the largest count in the batch — against the D6-brane cluster's $2\times10^5$.

The Five Features

"the number of families, Pati–Salam breaking Higgs, Standard Model Higgs doublets, additional triplets and exotic charge states"

The classification is by these five, and each is a phenomenological requirement: 1. Three families: the observed number. 2. PS-breaking Higgs: the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ needed to break $SU(4)$. 3. SM Higgs doublets: at least one pair, and few enough to be realistic. 4. Additional triplets: the $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ and $(\mathbf{3},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$-type states, which can break PS but also mediate proton decay. 5. Exotic charge states: fractionally charged states, which are fatal because they would have been seen. The fifth is the sharpest filter: a fractionally charged particle is absolutely stable (no decay channel into SM particles), so even one at the string scale is excluded unless it is heavy enough to escape production. The corpus should record the filter's power: "exophobic" means no fractionally charged states at all, and only one in $10^6$ vacua passes.

Why This Matters for the Corpus

The paper is a proceedings, so it has no derivations. Its value is the numbers and the framing. And the framing is important: Rizos calls it a "landscape" in quotes, which is honest — it is a classification, not a vacuum-selection principle. The corpus should use the word the same way.

The contrast with the swampland literature is stark: the landscape papers count, the swampland papers bound. 2505.03664 and 2409.09110 apply swampland bounds to specific models; here there are $10^{15}$ models and no bound.

The corpus should note the tension: if the swampland bounds are right, most of these $10^{15}$ vacua are in the swampland, and the count is meaningless. That is the sharpest open question in the field, and the corpus should state it.

One observation about the corpus's map

This four-page proceedings is the batch's clearest statement of the landscape problem, and it should be quoted for its numbers.

$10^{15}$ heterotic Pati–Salam vacua. One in $10^6$ exophobic. $2\times10^5$ D6-brane configurations, 33 gauge-coupling classes, 17 viable Yukawa textures.

Those numbers, side by side, are the batch's real result about string model building: the space is astronomically large, the viable region is tiny but non-empty, and no principle selects within it.

And the corpus should draw the conclusion the field draws: string theory does not predict Pati–Salam; it permits it in many ways. That is not a failure — a theory that permits the Standard Model is already non-trivial — but it means the corpus cannot present Pati–Salam as a prediction of string theory.

The honest sentence: Pati–Salam is realised in string theory in at least $10^{15}$ ways, of which a tiny fraction are free of exotics, and no known principle chooses among those. The corpus should say exactly that, once, and then discuss the specific models.

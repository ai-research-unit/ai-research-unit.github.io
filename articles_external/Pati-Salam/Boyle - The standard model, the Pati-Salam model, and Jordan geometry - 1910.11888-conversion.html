What the Paper Does

The paper replaces the algebra of coordinates with a Jordan algebra instead of a non-commutative one, and calls the result "Jordan geometry". Two arguments lead there. Composition of two Hermitian operators is not Hermitian, but their anticommutator is, so the Hermitian operators close under ${\tfrac12}\{a,b\}$ to a Jordan algebra — and those are the observables. And non-commutative geometry "is the natural quantum generalization of the symplectic geometry of phase space, not the (Riemannian) geometry of configuration space", since it is $[q_i,p_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}$ that fails to commute while $[q_i,q_j]=0$.

With that substitution the construction becomes unusually economical. The finite algebra that most nearly matches the Standard Model is

$$ \mathcal A_F=J_3(\mathbb C)\oplus J_2(\mathbb C)\oplus S_2\oplus\mathbb R, $$

represented by an $8\times8$ block-diagonal Hermitian matrix, with the fermions of one generation in an $8\times8$ block-off-diagonal matrix and $J$ equal to ordinary Hermitian conjugation. That algebra delivers the Standard Model plus $U(1)_{B-L}$, three right-handed neutrinos, and a scalar $\phi$ of $B-L$ charge $2$ that Higgses the extra $U(1)$ — and it does so without the unimodularity condition that non-commutative geometry has to impose by hand. Enlarging to

$$ \mathcal A_F=J_2^L(\mathbb C)\oplus J_2^R(\mathbb C)\oplus J_4(\mathbb C) $$

gives Pati–Salam, $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)$, with the same $8\times8$ representation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Three Strategies

The paper sets out two long-standing goals and, for each, one standard strategy and its defect.

For unifying gravity with the other fields, the Kaluza–Klein strategy takes spacetime to be $M_4\times M_{\rm int}$ with $M_{\rm int}$ a manifold. Its defect is structural: because $\mathcal A_{\rm int}=C^\infty(M_{\rm int})$ is infinite-dimensional, the picture "predicts an infinite collection of 4D fields (in contrast to the finite handful of fields that we actually observe)", and stabilising $M_{\rm int}$ is hard. The modification is to make $M_{\rm int}$ finite, so that "the $\infty$-dimensional algebra $\mathcal A_{\rm int}=C^\infty(M_{\rm int})$ is replaced by a finite-dimensional algebra $\mathcal A_F$". The paper rejects "deconstruction" — a lattice — because "the algebraic/symmetry structure of the standard model is not properly reflected in the geometry of such an internal space."

For unifying gravity with quantum mechanics, the strategy is non-commutative geometry. Its defect is the one quoted above: it quantises the wrong space. The modification is to keep the Hermitian operators and drop the rest:

$$ H=\tfrac12(O+O^\dagger),\qquad A=\tfrac12(O-O^\dagger), $$

where the anti-Hermitian part "close[s] under the commutator product ... to form a Lie algebra, while the Hermitian operators close under the anti-commutator product ... to form a Jordan algebra." Why carry "all this excess, unobservable baggage"? Jordan showed quantum mechanics can be written in terms of the Hermitian algebra alone.

The paper is careful that this second argument is a motivation and not a derivation: it "w[as] originally led to Jordan geometry by a completely different line of argument", and "in principle, it may even turn out that our Jordan geometry is not quantum mechanical at all".

The Barrett Form, Which Everything Rests On

The review section presents the non-commutative Standard Model in Barrett's form, and this is the technical hinge. The finite algebra is $\mathcal A_F=M_3(\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb H\oplus\mathbb C$, with $a=(m,q,z)$, and it acts on $\mathcal H_F=\mathbb C^{96}$ — "96 corresponds to the total number of fermionic degrees of freedom in the standard model ... after including particles and anti-particles, left and right chiralities, right-handed neutrinos, 3 colors, and 3 families". The representation is the $8\times8$ block-diagonal matrix

$$ \pi(a)=\begin{pmatrix}q&&&\\&q_z&&\\&&m&\\&&&z\end{pmatrix}, \qquad q=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\-\bar\beta&\bar\alpha\end{pmatrix}, \qquad q_z=\begin{pmatrix}z&0\\0&\bar z\end{pmatrix}, $$

and the fermions are the $8\times8$ block-off-diagonal matrix whose upper-right block holds "the 16 Weyl spinors in a single generation" and whose lower-left block holds the antiparticles, with $J\psi=\psi^\dagger$.

Why this form matters is stated in a footnote: Barrett's contribution "was to notice that $\mathcal H_F$ could be elegantly represented as an $8\times8$ matrix (rather than as an $8\times4$ matrix): if one does so, then $J$ becomes ordinary hermitian conjugation, and the algebra is also represented by an $8\times8$ matrix, whose left and right representations on $\mathcal H_F$ is simply given by ordinary matrix multiplication."

The generation, written out with its entries named, is

$$ \psi=\begin{pmatrix} u_L&u_L&u_L&\nu_L\\ d_L&d_L&d_L&e_L\\ u_R&u_R&u_R&\nu_R\\ d_R&d_R&d_R&e_R\\ \bar u_L&\bar d_L&\bar u_R&\bar d_R\\ \bar u_L&\bar d_L&\bar u_R&\bar d_R\\ \bar u_L&\bar d_L&\bar u_R&\bar d_R\\ \bar\nu_L&\bar e_L&\bar\nu_R&\bar e_R \end{pmatrix}, $$

with "red, green and blue ... the three quark colors", the chirality operator $\gamma$ flipping the sign of the left-handed entries and of the right-handed antifermions, and the gauge group arising as the inner automorphisms, "generated by commutators $[\pi(a),\cdot]$".

Two remarks in this section are worth carrying away. The first: the inner automorphisms include the trace part of $\pi(a)$, which "yields an extra (unwanted, anomalous) gauge symmetry which must be removed by hand, and the so-called 'unimodularity condition' was introduced precisely for this reason." The second: $\mathcal B_F$ "also has one other anomaly-free outer automorphism, corresponding to $U(1)_{B-L}$".

Why the Spectral Action Is Rejected

Flatly: "we suspect this expression is incorrect, for both experimental and theoretical reasons: from the experimental standpoint, it predicts relationships between standard model couplings (including gravitational couplings) which disagree with observations, and from the theoretical standpoint, these relations are also not preserved by the known RG flow of the standard model (regarded as an effective field theory)."

The Jordan Algebra for the Standard Model

The classification used is Jordan–von Neumann–Wigner: the finite-dimensional Euclidean Jordan algebras are

$$ S_n,\quad J_n(\mathbb R),\quad J_n(\mathbb C),\quad J_n(\mathbb H),\quad J_3(\mathbb O), $$

with $J_n(\mathbb K)$ the Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices over the normed division algebra $\mathbb K$, product the anticommutator; $S_n$ the spin factor on $\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R^n$; and the isomorphisms $J_1(\mathbb K)\simeq\mathbb R$, $J_2(\mathbb K)\simeq S_{\dim\mathbb K+1}$. "Corresponding to the Jordan algebras (10), we have the automorphism groups $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$, $SO(n)$, $SU(n)$, $Sp(n)$ and $F_4$, respectively."

The requirements on $\mathcal A_F$ are listed as three: representable by an $8\times8$ block-diagonal Hermitian matrix acting by anticommutator on $\psi$; inner automorphism group $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$; and commuting with $\gamma$. "It is now straightforward to check that, up to isomorphism, there is a unique algebra/representation that does the trick":

$$ \mathcal A_F=J_3(\mathbb C)\oplus J_2(\mathbb C)\oplus S_2\oplus\mathbb R, \qquad \pi(a)=\mathrm{diag}(c_2,\,s_2,\,c_3,\,r). $$

The inner automorphisms are not commutators. Because $\mathcal A_F$ is Jordan, "the inner automorphisms of $\mathcal B_F$ are not generated by commutators with elements $\pi(a)$, but rather by associators with pairs of elements $\pi(a)$ and $\pi(a')$ — i.e. by terms of the form $[\pi(a),\cdot,\pi(a')]=\{\{\pi(a),\cdot\},\pi(a')\}-\{\pi(a),\{\cdot,\pi(a')\}\}$".

What Comes Out, and What Has to Be Added

With only the inner automorphisms, the fermions break into six representations with $U(1)$ charges

$$ q_L:0,\quad u_R:+\tfrac12,\quad d_R:-\tfrac12,\quad \ell_L:0,\quad \nu_R:+\tfrac12,\quad e_R:-\tfrac12, $$

"which does not agree with the observed charge assignments in the standard model". The outer automorphism fixes it: adding $U(1)_{B-L}$ with charges $\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1,-1,-1$ gives a table in which "the third column is just a linear combination of hypercharge and baryon-minus-lepton number, $X=Y+(B-L)/2$".

So the geometry predicts an extension: "an extra $U(1)_{B-L}$ gauge symmetry, and two extra bosonic fields: the new $U(1)_{B-L}$ gauge boson, and a new complex scalar field $\phi$ which does not couple to $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)_Y$, but does carry charge $+2$ under $U(1)_{B-L}$".

One advantage over non-commutative geometry is claimed here. Because the algebra is Hermitian, there is no anti-Hermitian trace part to generate an anomalous extra $U(1)$, "so no new rules are needed to excise them".

Pati–Salam

Enlarge the block structure to three factors and require the same properties:

$$ \mathcal A_F=J_2^L(\mathbb C)\oplus J_2^R(\mathbb C)\oplus J_4(\mathbb C), \qquad \pi(a)=\mathrm{diag}(c^L_2,\,c^R_2,\,c_4), $$

with $\psi$ in $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 2,\mathbf 1)$ and $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$ plus conjugates. "One can check that the automorphism group is now $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)$", and "the previous section's finite algebra (and representation) are contained within this section's". The verdict: "This is a particularly interesting (and experimentally viable) extension of the standard model, the Pati-Salam model, which is seen to arise very neatly and naturally as a Jordan geometry."

The Closing Speculation

The discussion raises the exceptional Jordan algebra $J_3(\mathbb O)$ as an obvious and appealing next step, and then flags a consequence that is easy to miss:

"if the algebra has the form $\mathcal A=C^\infty(M)\otimes\mathcal A_F$, where $\mathcal A_F$ is a finite-dimensional simple algebra, then the corresponding symmetry group $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal A)$ is given by the semi-direct product $G\rtimes\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ ... a Yang-Mills theory, with gauge group $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal A_F)$, coupled to gravity. On the other hand, in attempting to describe the standard model of particle physics or the Pati-Salam model, we are led to use a finite algebra that is the sum of several simple components ... But in this case, the corresponding symmetry group $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal A)$ actually contains multiple copies of $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$. Could this be a hint that this framework actually predicts multi-metric or multi-tetrad gravity?"

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. The fermion is a matrix and $J$ is the dagger — said exactly

The idea. One generation is an $8\times8$ matrix, block-off-diagonal, with particles in the upper block and antiparticles in the lower; the algebra is the block-diagonal $8\times8$ matrix; $J\psi=\psi^\dagger$.

What it would add. The precise form of the corpus's central structural claim, in a framework with published axioms behind it. The corpus asserts that the fermion is a matrix — that a biquaternion, or a $2\times2$ array of them, carries a generation — and states it as a property of its own algebra. Here is the same statement made about the finite Hilbert space of the Standard Model: the sixteen Weyl spinors are literally the entries of a matrix, the antiparticles are the transpose block, and the real structure is Hermitian conjugation. No bimodule, no left–right action, no grading bookkeeping. The corpus's claim is not idiosyncratic; it is a known presentation.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spectral-triple-of-the-biquaternion-algebra.md, in the section on the real structure, and articles_physics/introduction-to-the-biquaternion-universe.md where the matrix picture is introduced.

What stands in the way. Barrett's matrix is $8\times8$ over $\mathbb C$ for one generation, and the corpus's carrier is a biquaternion, which is $2\times2$ over $\mathbb C$ — four times smaller. So the corpus's matrix is a quarter of Barrett's, and the article would need to say which quarter: the upper-left particle block, plausibly, with the antiparticle block obtained by conjugation. That is a real comparison and it is worth making explicit, because a factor of $4$ in a carrier is the difference between "the same construction" and "a similar construction".

2. Replace the commutator by the anticommutator, and the algebra becomes the observables

The idea. The Hermitian operators do not close under composition but do close under $\tfrac12\{a,b\}$. Take that as the algebra. Then the derivation of the gauge group changes from commutators to associators, and the trace part that forced the unimodularity condition disappears.

What it would add. A cleaner foundation for the corpus's own split. The corpus's biquaternion $\mathbb B$ decomposes into a real scalar–pseudoscalar part and a vector part, and its state pairing is taken on the Hermitian sector. That is the same instinct — keep the Hermitian part, discard the rest — arrived at by a different route, and this paper supplies the argument that the instinct is forced rather than aesthetic: the anti-Hermitian part is a Lie algebra, the Hermitian part is a Jordan algebra, and only the second is observable.

Where it lands. The spectral-triple article's axiom section, and articles_physics/anomalies-and-anomaly-cancellation-in-biquaternionic-form.md, where the pairing on the Hermitian sector is used to compute anomaly coefficients. If a Jordan algebra is the right carrier, the anomaly computation has a new justification.

What stands in the way. The corpus's multiplication is associative and it uses that constantly. Jordan algebras are not associative, and any calculation that relies on regrouping products would need redoing. This is the single largest technical obstacle to the import, and it should be stated as one.

3. The classification lists exactly $\mathbb R,\mathbb C,\mathbb H,\mathbb O$ — and the corpus lives in the third slot

The idea. $J_n(\mathbb K)$ for $\mathbb K$ one of the four normed division algebras, with automorphism groups $SO(n)$, $SU(n)$, $Sp(n)$ and $F_4$, and the spin factors $S_n$ with $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$. The Standard Model's algebra uses $J_2(\mathbb C)$ and $J_3(\mathbb C)$; Pati–Salam uses $J_4(\mathbb C)$.

What it would add. A classification in which the corpus's algebra has a named address. The corpus argues for the quaternions as the natural carrier; here the quaternionic entry is $J_n(\mathbb H)$, with automorphism group $Sp(n)$, and $J_2(\mathbb H)\simeq S_5$ — the spin factor of five-dimensional Euclidean space. The complex entry, which is where the Standard Model actually sits, is $J_n(\mathbb C)$ with $SU(n)$. So the corpus can say precisely what it is claiming: that physics sits one division algebra up from where the standard constructions put it, at $\mathbb H$ rather than $\mathbb C$, and that the price is that the automorphism group moves from $SU(n)$ to $Sp(n)$.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-number-of-generations-and-the-biquaternion-algebra.md and articles_physics/squeezing-beyond-the-biquaternion-algebra.md, which already discuss the division algebras.

What stands in the way. $Sp(n)$ is not a symmetry of anything observed. The corpus's claim would have to be that the quaternionic structure is a property of the carrier and not of the gauge group — which is what the corpus already says, but the classification makes the cost visible.

4. The geometry predicts $B-L$, $\nu_R$ and a $B-L$ scalar — without a unimodularity condition

The idea. The unique $8\times8$ algebra with the right properties forces an extra $U(1)_{B-L}$, three $\nu_R$ and a scalar $\phi$ of charge $+2$ that Higgses it. The trace part that non-commutative geometry must excise by hand never appears, because the algebra is Hermitian.

What it would add. A comparison the corpus's Pati–Salam article is well placed to make. That article derives $B-L$ from $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf 4}(B-L)=0$ — a condition imposed on the generator. This paper gets $B-L$ as "the one other anomaly-free outer automorphism", with no condition on a trace. Two routes to the same $U(1)$, one by imposition and one by classification, and the second is stronger because it also fixes the scalar sector.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's charge-quantisation paragraph and its scalar-sector ledger.

What stands in the way. The Jordan route gets $B-L$ but not $SU(4)$ in the Standard-Model case — the colour group stays $SU(3)$ and the lepton stays out of it. So the same construction that gives the corpus its favourite $U(1)$ denies it the fourth colour, unless the algebra is enlarged to $J_4(\mathbb C)$, and then the extra $U(1)$ is inside $SU(4)$ again. The corpus should record both steps and which one costs what.

5. A sum of simple factors may force multi-metric gravity

The idea. If $\mathcal A_F$ is simple, $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal A)=G\rtimes\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ — ordinary Yang–Mills coupled to gravity. If $\mathcal A_F$ is a sum of simple factors, as it is for both the Standard Model and Pati–Salam in this framework, then $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal A)$ "actually contains multiple copies of $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$", which "could be a hint that this framework actually predicts multi-metric or multi-tetrad gravity".

What it would add. A cost of the product structure that no other paper in this corpus mentions. Every construction in the corpus takes a product $\mathcal A=C^\infty(M)\otimes\mathcal A_F$ and never asks what happens to the diffeomorphisms when $\mathcal A_F$ has several summands. This paper asks, and the answer is that each summand may carry its own metric.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's structural-cost paragraph, and the spectral-triple article's section on the product.

What stands in the way. It is posed as a question, not derived, and multi-metric gravity is a niche. Import it as a flag, with the paper's own hedging intact.

6. The spectral action is rejected on renormalisation-group grounds

The idea. $\mathrm{Tr}\,f(D/\Lambda)$ "predicts relationships between standard model couplings ... which disagree with observations, and ... these relations are also not preserved by the known RG flow".

What it would add. Independent support for a criticism the corpus already makes. The corpus's Pati–Salam article runs the couplings and shows $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38$ fails. This paper states the general form of the same objection — the relations are not RG-invariant, so they cannot hold at more than one scale — and it comes from authors who have no stake in the corpus's conclusion. A criticism that two independent programmes arrive at is worth more than one.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's The Number That Fails, as a citation of the general argument rather than only the numerical one.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

One tension recorded

The paper's central philosophical claim is that non-commutative geometry quantises the wrong space. That is a claim about what the framework is for, and the corpus's position is the opposite: the corpus uses non-commutativity as the source of the gauge structure and treats the algebra as the object of interest. Both positions are tenable, and the paper's argument — $[q_i,q_j]=0$ while $[q_i,p_j]\neq0$ — is a genuine difficulty for anyone who says the coordinates themselves fail to commute. It does not touch the corpus's construction, which is not a quantisation of phase space either, but it is the sharpest available statement of why the non-commutative route is not compulsory.

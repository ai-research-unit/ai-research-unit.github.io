What the Paper Does

It solves the strong CP problem without an axion, using Pati–Salam to make the Nelson–Barr mechanism automatic.

"We present a UV completion of the Standard Model in which quarks and leptons are unified under color $SU(4)$. A single fermionic representation, the real antisymmetric, provides the building blocks to address the strong CP problem via the Nelson–Barr mechanism, while simultaneously correcting the charged-lepton and down-quark mass relations predicted by Pati-Salam theories for the two heaviest generations."

Two jobs, one multiplet, and both are problems that Pati–Salam has on its own. That is the economical move the paper makes and it is worth recording precisely.

"The interplay between the quality of the Nelson–Barr mechanism and the non-observation of baryon-number-violating processes determines the scale of spontaneous CP violation and the mass of the new vector-like down quark. The theory predicts a distinctive baryon-number-violating decay mode of the neutron, $n\to K^+\ell^-$ (with $\ell=e,\mu$), which lies within the projected sensitivity of upcoming nucleon-decay experiments such as Hyper-Kamiokande, JUNO, and DUNE."

So a strong-CP solution ends up predicting a neutron decay mode. That chain is the paper's value: one assumption (Pati–Salam), one multiplet ($F_6$), and the output is a specific two-body decay with a rate that three planned experiments can reach.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Nelson–Barr Texture and Its Problem

The mechanism: CP is a symmetry of the Lagrangian, spontaneously broken by a scalar's VEV. Then the mass matrices have the form $$ \mathcal M_q=\begin{pmatrix}M_q&0\\ \mu_i&M_Q\end{pmatrix}, $$ "where $M_q$ denotes the $3\times3$ SM weak mass matrix (real), $M_Q$ the mass of the new vector-like quark (real), and $\mu_i$ the mixing mass terms between the SM quarks and the new state (complex)".

The determinant is $M_qM_Q$, which is real-vacuum-times-real, so $\arg\det(M_uM_d)=0$ and $\bar\theta_{\rm QCD}=0$ at tree level — while the CKM phase, which comes from $\mu_i$, is $O(1)$. That is the whole trick: the determinant only sees the real block; the phase lives entirely in the mixing.

"The minimal simplified model realizing this idea was proposed by Bento, Branco and Parada (BBP): a vector-like down-type quark $D_{L,R}\sim(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,-1/3)_{\rm SM}$ together with a complex scalar $S\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,0)_{\rm SM}$, both charged under a $\mathbb Z_2$."

And then the flaw:

"However, higher dimensional operators, appearing already at dimension five, $$\frac1\Lambda\bar Q_LH(SD_R)+\cdots,$$ reintroduce a small $\bar\theta_{\rm QCD}$ and spoil the mechanism unless the scale of spontaneous CP violation is below 40 TeV, assuming $\Lambda\sim M_{\rm Pl}$. Such a low scale is incompatible with high-scale leptogenesis ... and with inflationary models featuring a hot reheating phase, since the domain walls arising from the spontaneous breaking of CP must be inflated away."

So the minimal Nelson–Barr model is squeezed between two cosmological requirements. $v_{\rm CP}<40$ TeV is needed for the quality of the mechanism; leptogenesis and inflation want $v_{\rm CP}$ high. The window is empty.

That is the problem the paper solves, and it is a clean statement of a real obstruction. The corpus should record it: Nelson–Barr works in the infrared and fails in the ultraviolet, for reasons (leptogenesis, domain walls) that have nothing to do with flavour.

The Fix: Make $S$ Not a Singlet

"Automatic Nelson–Barr models have been proposed, in which the $\mathbb Z_2$ is an accidental remnant of the gauge symmetry and matter content. This ensures that the operator in Eq. (5) is generically forbidden and contributions to $\bar\theta_{\rm QCD}$ are further suppressed, thereby allowing for a higher spontaneous CP violation scale."

The logic, stated generally: if $S$ is a gauge singlet, nothing forbids $\bar Q_LH\,S\,D_R$. If $S$ carries gauge quantum numbers, that operator is not invariant, and the leading violation appears at higher dimension. That is exactly the same reasoning as the axion quality problem, solved by the same device — and the corpus should note the parallel, since 2005.00012 and 2505.08866 in this batch use it for PQ.

Why $F_6$ Is the Right Multiplet

"Within color $SU(4)$, the next-to-minimal nontrivial representation beyond the fundamental that can be added to the matter content is the antisymmetric representation, $F_6$. Remarkably, this representation is real and therefore does not spoil anomaly cancellation."

Three reasons at once, and each is a separate necessity: 1. It is the smallest new representation, so it is economical. 2. It is real, so it adds no gauge anomaly — which is what lets it be added to a complete, anomaly-free model at all. 3. It contains a vector-like down quark, which is what Nelson–Barr requires.

That coincidence is the paper's central observation. The multiplet that Pati–Salam naturally admits is the one that carries a vector-like down quark, and a vector-like down quark is what the strong CP solution needs.

The Second Job: Fixing $M_e=M_d$

"its mixing with the SM down-type quarks helps correct the Pati-Salam mass matrix relation $M_e=M_d$ for the second and third generations."

So the same mixing fixes a Pati–Salam defect. In minimal Pati–Salam with only a bidoublet, $M_d=M_e^T$ exactly, which is wrong by a factor of three for the strange quark. 2005.00012 fixes it with $\Phi_{15}$, 1608.04124 derives the $-3$ from the Georgi–Jarlskog direction, and here it is fixed by mixing with a vector-like down quark.

Three different mechanisms, one required correction. Worth recording, because the corpus should show that the $M_d=M_e$ failure is not a technicality — it is a constraint that shapes the field content.

The resulting mass ordering: $$ m_d^2\le\kappa_M^2m_e^2\le m_s^2\le\kappa_M^2m_\mu^2\le m_b^2\le\kappa_M^2m_\tau^2, $$ "Hence, the strange and bottom quark masses can be successfully reproduced from their charged-lepton partners after including renormalization-group running and mixing with the vector-like down quark. However, the experimental value for the down-quark mass satisfies $m_d(2\ {\rm GeV})\simeq9\,m_e$, which exceeds the maximal value attainable through fermion mixing alone ... higher-dimensional operators ... are expected to generate the splitting."

So the first generation needs an extra ingredient and the second and third do not. That is a specific, checkable statement: the $U(4)$ relation survives at $10$–$20\%$ for $s$ and $b$, and fails badly for $d$.

The Combination That Fixes the Scales

"The combined requirements of a high-quality Nelson–Barr and the non-observation of baryon number violation significantly restrict the viable parameter space, fixing the scale of spontaneous CP violation and the new vector-like down quark to be around $10^9$ GeV."

$10^9$ GeV, and it is fixed by two independent constraints crossing. The quality bound:

$$ \frac{\Delta\bar\theta_{\rm QCD}}{10^{-10}}\le3\left(\frac{10^9\ {\rm GeV}}{v_{\rm CP}}\right)^2\left(\frac{10^9\ {\rm GeV}}{M_D}\right), $$

and the neutron-decay bound:

$$ \left(\frac{v_{\rm CP}}{10^9\ {\rm GeV}}\right)^4\left(\frac{10^9\ {\rm GeV}}{M_D}\right)>0.34, $$

from $\tau/{\rm Br}(n\to K^+e^-)>3.2\times10^{31}$ yr and $\tau/{\rm Br}(n\to K^+\mu^-)>5.7\times10^{31}$ yr (Fréjus, 90% CL).

Look at the two inequalities: one bounds the ratio from above, the other from below, with the same combination of powers. That is why the scales are fixed rather than merely bounded, and it is the most elegant part of the paper. A CP-quality requirement and a proton-decay requirement carve out a corner of a two-dimensional plane, and the intersection is a point.

The Prediction, and the Experimental Situation

"the theory predicts a distinctive and, in principle, experimentally accessible signal of baryon number violation: the decay mode $n\to K^+\ell^-$, with $\ell=e,\mu$."

Why $n\to K^+\ell^-$ and not $p\to e^+\pi^0$: the model violates $B$ but conserves $B-L$ in the leading operators (as Pati–Salam does), so the final state must have the same $B-L$. $n\to K^+\ell^-$ has $\Delta B=1$, $\Delta L=-1$, so $\Delta(B-L)=2$... which means it is not the leading operator but a suppressed one, and that is why the rate is small enough to be interesting rather than fatal. The paper says the $B$-violating modes come in a single class, "a single class of decay channels, $n\to K^+(e,\mu)^-$, is expected to dominate, providing a distinctive smoking-gun signal."

And the experimental gap is real: "The limits on the decay modes $n\to K^+(e^-,\mu^-)$ are a couple of orders of magnitude weaker than the leading bounds on nucleon decay, and have not been updated for more than three decades." "Super-Kamiokande does not have a published bound on $n\to K^+\ell^-$." The reason is a detector limitation: "In these two-body neutron decays, the kaon momentum is below the Cherenkov threshold in water ... the $K^+$ is therefore not observed directly."

So the paper identifies a channel that is predicted, unexcluded, and unsearched — which is the most useful thing a theory paper can do. And it names the fix: "a dedicated SK analysis would therefore be needed."

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. Strong CP without an axion: the determinant only sees the real block

The idea. $\mathcal M_q$ block-triangular with a real $3\times3$ block and complex mixing; $\det$ real; CKM phase $O(1)$ from the mixing.

What it would add. The only non-axion solution to strong CP that the corpus would otherwise lack. And it is the natural alternative for a unified model, because it needs a vector-like quark — which unification supplies — rather than a global symmetry, which unification tends to break. The corpus's axion entries (2005.00012, 2505.08866, 1812.02747) all assume PQ; this shows the other road and its price.

What it would add. A worked example of a solution that works in the infrared and is destroyed by ultraviolet physics — the same shape as the axion quality problem, with different actors. Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's strong-CP section.

3. A real representation adds no anomaly; the $\mathbf6$ of $SU(4)$ is real

The idea. $F_6$ is real, so it can be added to an anomaly-free model without new fermions to cancel it.

What it would add. A selection rule that the corpus's group-theory discussion should carry. Adding a complex representation to a unified model requires adding its conjugate; a real one does not. That is why $F_6$ is the minimal extension and why the paper can say "a single fermionic representation."

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's representation table, with the reality of $\mathbf6$ marked.

What stands in the way. Nothing. $SU(4)$'s $\mathbf6$ is real; this is standard.

4. $10^9$ GeV is where CP-quality and proton-decay bounds cross

The idea. One inequality bounds $v_{\rm CP}^2/M_D$ from above, the other bounds $v_{\rm CP}^4/M_D$ from below; the intersection fixes both scales.

What it would add. A model of how two unrelated constraints can determine rather than merely bound a parameter. The corpus's phenomenology discussion is full of bounds; here is a case where the answer is a point, which is much stronger and is what makes the prediction sharp.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's phenomenology section.

What stands in the way. The crossing depends on $O(1)$ uncertainties in the operator coefficients.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the most economical construction in the batch, and it is worth contrasting with the twin-PS series directly.

The twin Pati–Salam needs two gauge groups, six vector-like families, two discrete symmetries, a colour octet, a $Z'$, and a tuned 2-3 structure to explain $B$ anomalies. It does not address strong CP at all.

Murgui needs one gauge group, one extra fermion multiplet, and one scalar, and gets strong CP, the $s$ and $b$ masses, and a neutron-decay prediction. It does not address $B$ anomalies.

So the two are answering different questions with the same group, and the comparison shows what Pati–Salam is actually good for: it is a machine for relating sectors, and the more sectors you ask it to relate the more field content you need. The batch's papers can be sorted by how many sectors they ask for, and the field content grows monotonically with the count.

The second thing: this paper and 2105.14029 are by the same author, five years apart, and both are about what Pati–Salam does to baryon number. The 2021 paper asks whether light leptoquarks are safe; the 2026 paper asks whether a heavy vector-like quark is safe, and finds a channel, $n\to K^+\ell^-$, that the earlier paper's logic would not have singled out. Reading them in order is a short course in how a field-content question gets answered.

What the Paper Does

It replaces the $\mathbf{15}$ by bifundamental $\mathbf4$s, and gets a conformal $SU(4)_C$ coupling for free.

"We present a supersymmetric Pati-Salam model with small representations as a potential candidate for physics beyond the Standard Model." "The model features a Higgs sector with bifundamental fields HR + H̄R = (4, 1, 2) + (4̄, 1, 2), HL + H̄L = (4, 2, 1)+(4̄, 2, 1) as well as a pair of bi-doublet fields ha = (1, 2, 2) where a = 1, 2, with three families of fermions accommodated in (4, 2, 1) + (4̄, 1, 2) as usual. The matter spectrum is augmented" "with three families of fermions accommodated in (4, 2, 1) + (4̄, 1, 2) as usual. The matter spectrum is augmented with three"

"Interestingly, the one-loop beta coefficient of the $SU(4)_C$ gauge coupling is zero in the minimal $Z_3$ model, rendering the corresponding gauge coupling near-conformal in the UV. Meanwhile, Landau poles are avoided up to the Planck scale and proton decay is suppressed, resulting in a proton lifetime beyond current experimental bounds."

"By virtue of the extended Higgs sector, the key advantage of this PS model is its ability to disentangle quark and lepton masses through higher-dimensional effective operators, addressing a common limitation in GUT models with small Higgs representations. This makes the model more economical and easier to be constructed from string theory, particularly in several heterotic and F-theory models where Higgses in the adjoint representation are absent."

So the paper's four results are: no $\mathbf{15}$ (hence no Landau pole), $b_4=0$ (near-conformal), proton decay suppressed, and quark–lepton masses split. And the motivation is string-theoretic: heterotic and F-theory models often lack adjoints, so a $\mathbf{15}$-based breaking is unavailable there.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Problem With the $\mathbf{15}$

"introducing large representations such as the adjoint 15 ∈ SU (4)C might lead to a Landau pole well below the Planck scale due to the rapid growing of the gauge couplings. Secondly"

"

several appealing string derived constructions do not contain the adjoint representation and therefore, the associated benefits cannot be reaped."

Two independent problems, and the papers in this batch split on which they take seriously:

  • The $\mathbf{15}$ is what makes the Georgi–Jarlskog factor possible — the $m_e/m_\mu$ correction — and 1608.04124, 1701.04460, 2305.19356, 2305.19020 all use it.
  • But the $\mathbf{15}$ has a large Dynkin index, so it drives $SU(4)_C$ toward a Landau pole, and it is often absent in string constructions.

So the choice is: use the $\mathbf{15}$ and get GJ, or do without and solve the mass relations another way. This paper chooses the second.

The corpus should frame the tension this way, because it recurs: the same representation that fixes the fermion masses destabilises the gauge coupling.

The Replacement: Bifundamental $\mathbf4$s

"most of the PS models built in the context of the various limits of string theories do predict an excess of SU (4)C × SU (2)L × SU (2)R bifundamental representations (4, 2, 1) + (4̄, 2, 1) where additional Higgs fields can be accommodated. Therefore, in this study, we draw inspiration from superstring"

So the string constructions supply $\mathbf4$-type Higgses abundantly — they are the matter multiplets' own representation — and the paper uses them.

"When these Higgs fields acquire vevs, they play a crucial role in breaking the equality of quark-lepton mass matrices which are typically predicted by GUTs."

"we demonstrate the ability to distinguish the down quark mass matrix from the charged lepton mass matrix. This distinction provides a unique opportunity to make accurate predictions of mass ratios at the electroweak scale."

That is the mechanism, and the corpus should record how it works. In the minimal Pati–Salam, $m_d=m_e$ at the PS scale because both come from the same $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ fields coupled to the same $\mathbf{10}$ Higgs. The GJ factor fixes $m_e/m_\mu$ but at the price of the $\mathbf{15}$.

Here, a $\mathbf4$-Higgs VEV of the form $\langle H\rangle\propto\text{diag}(0,0,1)$ or similar modifies the down-quark and charged-lepton Yukawas differently, because the Higgs is itself in a matter-like representation and couples asymmetrically.

And the effect is generated at higher dimension — "through higher-dimensional effective operators" — so it is suppressed by $1/M$ and controllable. That is the trade: no $\mathbf{15}$, but $1/M$ operators.

The corpus should note the parallel with 2305.19356's flip: there, $SO(10)$ and flipped $SU(5)$ embeddings give different $m_d/m_s$ and $m_e/m_\mu$ relations. Here, the operator structure does the same job without the adjoint. Two routes to the same fix, and neither uses the GJ factor.

$b_4=0$: Near-Conformal $SU(4)_C$

"

the one-loop beta coefficient of the $SU(4)_C$ gauge coupling is zero in the minimal $Z_3$ model, rendering the corresponding gauge coupling near-conformal in the UV."

That is a striking result and it deserves care. The one-loop coefficient is

$$b_4=-\frac{11}{3}C_2(SU(4))+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{\rm Weyl}T(R)+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\rm chiral}T(R),$$

and with the bifundamental Higgs content it can vanish. If $b_4=0$, the $SU(4)_C$ coupling does not run at one loop, so it neither hits a Landau pole nor runs to zero — it is marginal.

"Landau poles are avoided up to the Planck scale and proton decay is suppressed, resulting in a proton lifetime beyond current experimental bounds."

So the model is UV-consistent in the sense that no coupling blows up below $M_P$. That is a real virtue and it is rare in this batch — 1807.03669 (asymptotically safe Pati–Salam) is the other paper that takes UV consistency seriously.

The corpus should compare them: 1807.03669 makes the whole theory asymptotically safe via a UV fixed point; here one coupling is one-loop marginal by representation choice. The second is more modest and easier to arrange.

And the caveat: one-loop marginality is not asymptotic safety — two-loop terms will typically push $b_4$ away from zero, and the paper says "near-conformal", not "conformal". The corpus should record the qualifier.

Proton Decay

"proton decay is suppressed, resulting in a proton lifetime beyond current experimental bounds."

Why: with no $\mathbf{15}$ and the $Z_3$ symmetry forbidding the dangerous couplings, the proton-decay operators are absent at the renormalisable level and suppressed at higher dimension. That is the same conclusion 2604.00166 reaches with an accidental $U(1)_B$: remove the operator and the proton lives. And it is the opposite of 2105.14029 and 1905.04533, which find that diquark scalars destroy the proton. The corpus should tabulate the proton-decay outcomes across the batch, because they depend entirely on the field content and symmetry, not on Pati–Salam itself.

The Domain Walls

"we explore the implications of spontaneous breaking of the $Z_3$ symmetry, particularly the formation of topological defects such as domain walls, with carefully adjusted energy scales, and we investigate the collapse of these domain walls and the conditions under which they can produce detectable gravitational waves."

So the $Z_3$ that forbids the bad operators also produces domain walls, and the walls must be made to decay. That is the same trade-off as 2504.01893 (the bias that makes walls decay) and 1812.02747/2307.02544. And the payoff is the same: gravitational waves. The batch now has four papers extracting a gravitational-wave signal from Pati–Salam defects (2504.01893, 1812.02747, 2307.02544, 2004.02332, and here). That convergence is itself a finding.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's most economical use of string constraints, and it is worth reading as a response to a specific string-theory fact. The fact: heterotic and F-theory constructions often have no adjoint of $SU(4)_C$. So a Pati–Salam model that needs the $\mathbf{15}$ cannot be embedded there. The paper's whole design follows from taking that seriously. Compare hep-ph/0409098 and 1007.2268 — the heterotic Pati–Salam papers in this batch — which build the models from the string side. This paper works the other way: it asks what a string-compatible Pati–Salam model must look like and finds that it must do without the $\mathbf{15}$, hence without GJ, hence with higher-dimension operators. The convergence is the interesting thing: two independent routes — field theory worrying about Landau poles, and string theory lacking adjoints — lead to the same conclusion: avoid the $\mathbf{15}$. The corpus should say so, because it is evidence that the small-representation choice is not arbitrary. The second point: $b_4=0$ is presented as a curiosity ("Interestingly") and it is more than that. A gauge coupling that does not run is a coupling whose value at low energy is a choice — so the model must fix $\alpha_4$ by hand, which is a cost the paper does not dwell on. The corpus should note the cost as well as the benefit.

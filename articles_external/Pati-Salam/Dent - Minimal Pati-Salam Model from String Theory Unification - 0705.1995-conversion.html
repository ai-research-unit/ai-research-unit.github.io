What the Paper Does

A three-family supersymmetric Pati–Salam model from a $Z_3$ orbifold of type IIB on $AdS_5\otimes S^5$, with the breaking chain followed all the way down to the Standard Model and a stated philosophy:

"what we attempt to do is travel the least circuitous route from strings to the standard model while carrying the least amount of superfluous baggage. Hence, success according to this philosophy is measured in a way similar to success in a game of golf. Rather than exploring as much of the landscape as possible, one tries to reach a particular local minimum quickly while avoiding the many hazards along the way."

The chain is $SU(4)^3\to SU(4)\times SU(3)\times SU(3)\times U(1)_A\to SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_A\times U(1)_{A'}\to$ Standard Model, and at each step the paper lists the complete scalar spectrum with quantum numbers.

The reason to keep it is the chain itself. Every step is explicit, every scalar is counted, and the paper gives the hypercharge in a form that writes $(B-L)$ and the third component of right-handed isospin as two separate $U(1)$s before combining them. That is the decomposition the corpus asserts and rarely shows.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Orbifold and the Field Content

"Recall that the AdS/CFT correspondence for $AdS\otimes S^5$ yields a conformal, $N=4$ supersymmetric, $SU(N)$ gauge theory which is non-chiral. In the Pati-Salam model the three chiral families are

$$ 3[(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)]_F . $$

"

Two routes to chirality are described; the second is taken: "choose $\Gamma$ to be an Abelian group of order $n$, where we set $N=4$ to arrive at a gauge group $SU_n(4)$, and then break the symmetry to the Pati-Salam group and then to the standard model, while at the same time preserving three chiral families by judicious choice of embedding."

"We choose $n=3$, i.e., $\Gamma=Z_3$, and $N=4$ with the orbifold group embedding $\mathbf4=(1,\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)$. This yields an $N=1$ theory with chiral supermultiplet fields in the following bifundamental and adjoint representations of the gauge group $SU_3(4)$:

$$ 3[(\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf4)] \quad\text{and}\quad (\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf{15},\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf{15}). $$

"

The embedding $\mathbf4=(1,\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)$ is what gives three families: the $Z_3$ acts with three distinct phases plus one trivial phase on the four components of the $\mathbf4$ of the $R$-symmetry, and the mismatch between the three stacks is what produces exactly three copies. That is a genuinely minimal mechanism, and it is why "three" is not put in by hand.

The family count is then structural: "Of the initial fermions, only the $3[(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)]_F$ remain chiral. The remainder are vectorlike, so can pair up to become heavy at the Pati-Salam scale."

The Breaking Chain

Step 1. $\langle(\mathbf1,\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})\rangle=v\,\mathrm{diag}(0,0,0,1)$ breaks $SU(4)^3\to SU(4)\times SU(3)\times SU(3)\times U(1)_A$. The vev matrix is explicitly a rank-one projector, so the breaking is to the maximal subgroup.

Step 2. $\langle(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\bar{\mathbf3})\rangle=v'\,\mathrm{diag}(0,0,1)$ breaks to $SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_A\times U(1)_{A'}$. Again a rank-one projector on the remaining $SU(3)$.

Step 3. "Breaking the $SU(4)_C\to SU(3)_C\times U(1)_D$ and $SU(2)_R\to U(1)_E$ (using a $\mathbf{4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_S$, see below) and defining the normalizations

$$ B-L=-D, \qquad Y=-\tfrac12D-\tfrac12E, $$

gives the following content under $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\times U(1)_{B-L}\times U(1)_A\times U(1)_{A'}$."

This is the cleanest statement of the hypercharge decomposition in the batch. $D$ is the diagonal $U(1)$ of $SU(4)$'s $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-1)$-type direction — so $B-L=-D$ with that normalisation means the quark has $B-L=\frac13$ and the lepton $-1$ in units where $D=\mp1$. $E$ is the diagonal of $SU(2)_R$, so $T_{3R}=-E/2$. Then

$$ Y=T_{3R}+\frac{B-L}{2} $$

follows immediately. The signs are chosen so that the up-type quark gets $Y=+\frac23$ and the down-type $-\frac13$, and the table confirms it: $3(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{1/6,1/3,\ldots}$ for the quark doublet.

Step 4. "Now, the VEVs $\langle\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1\rangle_{\frac43,-1}$ and $\langle\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1\rangle_{\frac43,\frac12}$ break $U_A(1)$ and $U_{A'}(1)$ completely and we arrive at the Standard Model gauge group."

Step 5. "Once a VEV for a $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_S$ breaks the symmetry to the standard model, only three standard families remain massless. The three right handed neutrinos become massive at this stage, and are available for use in the see-saw mechanism. Finally we identify a $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-\frac12}$ scalar with the Higgs."

Note the ordering: the right-handed neutrinos get their mass before the Higgs gets its vev, and the paper treats the two vevs as distinct steps. That is the standard Pati–Salam picture and it is worth recording against the alternative where $B-L$ breaking and electroweak breaking are simultaneous.

The scalars are listed in full at each stage; the count at the $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ stage includes $3(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\bar{\mathbf2})_{0,0}$ — the three bidoublets, one per family — plus $3(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{1/3,1/2}$ and $3(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/3,-1/2}$ which are the three families themselves.

Proton Stability

"The unification into $SU_3(4)$ happens at a high scale $\sim10^{15}$ GeV, so if the VEVs that break to the standard model are given at a high enough scale, the proton is sufficiently stable to avoid the present bound on its lifetime."

The argument is a hierarchy, not a symmetry. There is no $R$-parity statement and no $B$ or $L$ conservation; the proton is stable because the leptoquark is heavy. That is the "dimension-six suppression" logic, and it is the weakest available: it forbids nothing, it only suppresses.

Anomalies and Supersymmetry Breaking

"Orbifolded string theories produce quiver gauge theories that are known to contain $U(1)$ gauge symmetries. The $U(1)$s are generic and usually anomalous at the level of the quiver gauge theories. However, the underlying string theory must be anomaly free. This implies that higher order terms arise in the gauge theories, or counter terms can be added to the theories, to cancel these anomalies."

The computation, with $A_3(\mathbf4)=1$ and $Q(\mathbf4)=-Q(\bar{\mathbf4})=-1$:

$$ A_3^{(1^3)}\big(3[(\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf4)]\big)=\sum Q_1Q_2^2=3[4(1)(-1)^2+4(0)(1)^2+4(-1)(0)^2]=12, $$

and the mixed $1^42$ anomaly coefficient has the same value $12$: "Because of the symmetry of the quiver for our Pati-Salam model, all the $1^3$ anomalies have equal coefficients."

The vanishing of $4(0)(1)^2$ and $4(-1)(0)^2$ is the content: the bifundamental has charge only under two of the three $U(1)$s, so each term picks out one non-zero product. Had the $\mathbf4$ been charged under all three, no coefficient would have vanished and the arithmetic would be different.

Then: "since our orbifold compactification generated these $U(1)$s they can be used to break SUSY through the generation of Fayet-Iliopoulos D-terms ... The full D-term contribution to the scalar potential is then

$$ V=\sum_p\Big(\kappa_pD_p-\tfrac12D_p^2-gD_p\sum_iq_i|\phi_i|^2\Big), $$

" and "We assume the $U(1)$s are broken via terms of the form $\sum_p(m_p^2\phi_p^2+\lambda_p\phi_p^4)$ generated at the string loop level, and so we do arrive at a three family string theory generated supersymmetric Pati-Salam that naturally breaks to the non-SUSY standard model at the electroweak scale."

So the $U(1)$s do triple duty: they are anomalous, hence cancelled by string loops; the loops generate the mass terms that break them; and the $D$-terms they carry break supersymmetry. One set of fields, three jobs. That is the paper's actual claim to minimality.

The Opening Comparison

"The Pati-Salam model, based on the gauge group $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$, is to $SO(10)$ what trinification, based on the gauge group $SU(3)\times SU(3)\times SU(3)$, is to $E_6$. They are both maximal subgroup models of the covering grand unified theory (GUT) and both have the same number of massless chiral fermions as there are in the fermion families of the corresponding covering GUT."

That is a clean structural statement and the corpus should have it. $SO(10)\supset SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$ with the $\mathbf{16}\to(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ — two multiplets per family, matching $SO(10)$'s one. Similarly $E_6\supset SU(3)^3$ with $\mathbf{27}\to(\mathbf3,\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)+(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\bar{\mathbf3})$ — three multiplets.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. $B-L=-D$, $Y=-\frac12D-\frac12E$: the hypercharge as a sum of two diagonals

The idea. Break $SU(4)\to SU(3)\times U(1)_D$ and $SU(2)_R\to U(1)_E$, then define $B-L=-D$ and $Y=-\frac12D-\frac12E$.

What it would add. The corpus states $Y=T_{3R}+(B-L)/2$ as a formula. Here the two ingredients are named as separate $U(1)$s before being combined, and the charges of every scalar and fermion are tabulated in both. That makes the formula checkable rather than assertable: the quark doublet is $(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{1/6,1/3}$, the lepton doublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)_{0,\ldots}$, and $Y$ is the linear combination that reproduces the measured charges. The corpus's Pati–Salam article should show the intermediate table, not just the final formula, because the table is what proves the formula.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the hypercharge section, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-the-neutral-current-and-the-z-coupling.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The decomposition is exact in any Pati–Salam model.

2. An embedding $\mathbf4=(1,\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)$ that produces three families

The idea. The $Z_3$ orbifold acts on the $\mathbf4$ of the $R$-symmetry with one trivial and three non-trivial phases. Three families come out because three of the four components transform non-trivially and one does not.

What it would add. A mechanism for the number three that is not a choice. The corpus's Pati–Salam article takes three families as given, and the standard objection to any family-counting argument is that the number is put in. Here it is the index of an orbifold action, which is a topological quantity. That is a different kind of answer and the corpus does not have one.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's family-count discussion.

What stands in the way. The mechanism is stringy, and the corpus's own three-generation arguments (if any) would have to be reconciled with it. Import it as an existence proof that a discrete symmetry can fix the count, not as a competing explanation.

3. Pati–Salam is to $SO(10)$ what trinification is to $E_6$

The idea. Both are maximal-subgroup models, and both have the same number of chiral fermions per family as the covering GUT.

What it would add. A placement of Pati–Salam in the classification of unified models, which the corpus's article lacks. The corpus discusses $SO(10)\supset SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$ as a subgroup relation; here the rank of the relation is stated — both are maximal, so neither can be extended without adding a $U(1)$ — and the fermion count is matched. Maximality is the property that makes a subgroup model a genuine alternative rather than a truncation, and the corpus should say so.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's introduction, as the structural placement.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

4. One set of $U(1)$s doing anomaly cancellation, symmetry breaking and supersymmetry breaking

The idea. The anomalous $U(1)$s are cancelled by string loops; the loops generate mass terms that break the $U(1)$s; the $D$-terms of the broken $U(1)$s generate Fayet–Iliopoulos terms that break supersymmetry.

What it would add. A worked case of a mechanism with three roles, which is the strongest available form of the minimality argument. The corpus's Pati–Salam article argues from economy — few parameters, few fields. This is a sharper form: not fewer fields, but the same fields used more than once. That distinction is worth making because "minimal" is otherwise a word with no content.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's economy discussion.

What stands in the way. The three roles are string-specific. The pattern is transferable: look for a sector that is required for one reason and turns out to be required for two more.

5. Proton stability from a hierarchy, with no symmetry

The idea. "if the VEVs that break to the standard model are given at a high enough scale, the proton is sufficiently stable to avoid the present bound."

What it would add. The weakest form of proton protection, stated plainly. The corpus's Pati–Salam article discusses leptoquark-induced proton decay and the need for a suppression; here there is no suppression mechanism at all, only a mass. Compare with Barbieri, where a $Z_3$ or a $U(1)$ forbids the dangerous couplings, and with the spectral papers, where the leptoquark mass is tied to $M_X$. Three different answers, and the corpus should rank them: symmetry > kinematics > nothing.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's proton-decay paragraph, as the limiting case.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

One observation about the corpus's map

The paper's scalar lists are its real content, and they show something the corpus's article cannot show in prose: the number of scalars needed grows at every step of the chain. The $SU(4)^3$ stage has three adjoints; the $SU(4)\times SU(3)^2$ stage has a long list of bifundamentals; the $SU(4)\times SU(2)^2$ stage has more still. Pati–Salam breaking is not cheap in scalars, and a model that breaks it in one step with one multiplet (as the corpus's article does, and as the spectral papers do with $A^{(2)}$) is making a strong assumption about the potential.

That is worth recording as a tension: the corpus's Pati–Salam article describes a one-scalar breaking, and no string construction in this batch achieves one. The spectral model has one field but a complicated spectral action; the field-theoretic models use a $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ or a bidoublet; this one uses five stages. The corpus should state which it is doing and why.

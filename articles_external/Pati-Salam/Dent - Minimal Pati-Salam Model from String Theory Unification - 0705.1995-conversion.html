What the Paper Does

A three-family supersymmetric Pati–Salam model from a $Z_3$ orbifold of type IIB on $AdS_5\otimes S^5$, with the breaking chain followed all the way down to the Standard Model and a stated philosophy:

"what we attempt to do is travel the least circuitous route from strings to the standard model while carrying the least amount of superfluous baggage. Hence, success according to this philosophy is measured in a way similar to success in a game of golf. Rather than exploring as much of the landscape as possible, one tries to reach a particular local minimum quickly while avoiding the many hazards along the way."

The chain is $SU(4)^3\to SU(4)\times SU(3)\times SU(3)\times U(1)_A\to SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_A\times U(1)_{A'}\to$ Standard Model, and at each step the paper lists the complete scalar spectrum with quantum numbers.

The reason to keep it is the chain itself. Every step is explicit, every scalar is counted, and the paper gives the hypercharge in a form that writes $(B-L)$ and the third component of right-handed isospin as two separate $U(1)$s before combining them. That is the decomposition the corpus asserts and rarely shows.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Orbifold and the Field Content

"Recall that the AdS/CFT correspondence for $AdS\otimes S^5$ yields a conformal, $N=4$ supersymmetric, $SU(N)$ gauge theory which is non-chiral. In the Pati-Salam model the three chiral families are $$ 3[(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)]_F . $$"

Two routes to chirality are described; the second is taken: "l is to choose Γ to be an Abelian group of order n, where we set N = 4 to arrive at a gauge group SU n (4), and then break the symmetry to the Pati-Salam group and then to the 3 standard model, while at the same time preserving three chiral families by judicious choice of embedding. We"

"We choose $n=3$, i.e., $\Gamma=Z_3$, and $N=4$ with the orbifold group embedding $\mathbf4=(1,\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)$. This yields an $N=1$ theory with chiral supermultiplet fields in the following bifundamental and adjoint representations of the gauge group $SU_3(4)$: $$ 3[(\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf4)] \quad\text{and}\quad (\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf{15},\mathbf1)+(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf{15}). $$"

The embedding $\mathbf4=(1,\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)$ is what gives three families: the $Z_3$ acts with three distinct phases plus one trivial phase on the four components of the $\mathbf4$ of the $R$-symmetry, and the mismatch between the three stacks is what produces exactly three copies. That is a genuinely minimal mechanism, and it is why "three" is not put in by hand. The family count is then structural:"Of the initial fermions, only the $3[(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)+(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)]_F$ remain chiral. The remainder are vectorlike, so can pair up to become heavy at the Pati-Salam scale."

The Breaking Chain

Step 1.

$\langle(\mathbf1,\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})\rangle=v\,\mathrm{diag}(0,0,0,1)$ breaks $SU(4)^3\to SU(4)\times SU(3)\times SU(3)\times U(1)_A$. The vev matrix is explicitly a rank-one projector, so the breaking is to the maximal subgroup. Step 2. $\langle(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\bar{\mathbf3})\rangle=v'\,\mathrm{diag}(0,0,1)$ breaks to $SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_A\times U(1)_{A'}$. Again a rank-one projector on the remaining $SU(3)$. Step 3."Breaking the $SU(4)_C\to SU(3)_C\times U(1)_D$ and $SU(2)_R\to U(1)_E$ (using a $\mathbf{4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_S$, see below) and defining the normalizations

$$ B-L=-D, \qquad Y=-\tfrac12D-\tfrac12E, $$

gives the following content under $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\times U(1)_{B-L}\times U(1)_A\times U(1)_{A'}$."

This is the cleanest statement of the hypercharge decomposition in the batch. $D$ is the diagonal $U(1)$ of $SU(4)$'s $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-1)$-type direction — so $B-L=-D$ with that normalisation means the quark has $B-L=\frac13$ and the lepton $-1$ in units where $D=\mp1$. $E$ is the diagonal of $SU(2)_R$, so $T_{3R}=-E/2$. Then $$ Y=T_{3R}+\frac{B-L}{2} $$ follows immediately. The signs are chosen so that the up-type quark gets $Y=+\frac23$ and the down-type $-\frac13$, and the table confirms it: $3(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{1/6,1/3,\ldots}$ for the quark doublet. Step 4. Now, the VEVs $\langle\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1\rangle_{\frac43,-1}$ and $\langle\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1\rangle_{\frac43,\frac12}$ break $U_A(1)$ and $U_{A'}(1)$ completely and we arrive at the Standard Model gauge group. Step 5." e Pati-Salam scale. Once a VEV for a (4, 1, 2)S breaks the symmetry to the standard model[13], only three standard families remain massless. The three right handed neutrinos become massive at this stage, and are available for use in the see-saw mechanism. Finally we identify a (1, 2) 1 scalar with the Higgs. Giving it a VEV completes the chain of spontaneous 2 symmetry breaking."Note the ordering: the right-handed neutrinos get their mass before the Higgs gets its vev, and the paper treats the two vevs as distinct steps. That is the standard Pati–Salam picture and it is worth recording against the alternative where $B-L$ breaking and electroweak breaking are simultaneous. The scalars are listed in full at each stage; the count at the $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ stage includes $3(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\bar{\mathbf2})_{0,0}$ — the three bidoublets, one per family — plus $3(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{1/3,1/2}$ and $3(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/3,-1/2}$ which are the three families themselves.

Proton Stability

"unification into SU 3 (4) happens at a high scale ∼ 1015 GeV, so if the VEVs that break to the standard model are given at a high enough scale, the proton is sufficiently stable to avoid the present bound on its lifetime. Breaking"

The argument is a hierarchy, not a symmetry. There is no $R$-parity statement and no $B$ or $L$ conservation; the proton is stable because the leptoquark is heavy. That is the dimension-six suppression logic, and it is the weakest available: it forbids nothing, it only suppresses.

So the $U(1)$s do triple duty: they are anomalous, hence cancelled by string loops; the loops generate the mass terms that break them; and the $D$-terms they carry break supersymmetry. One set of fields, three jobs. That is the paper's actual claim to minimality.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

  1. $B-L=-D$,

$Y=-\frac12D-\frac12E$: the hypercharge as a sum of two diagonals

The idea. Break $SU(4)\to SU(3)\times U(1)_D$ and $SU(2)_R\to U(1)_E$, then define $B-L=-D$ and $Y=-\frac12D-\frac12E$.

What it would add. The corpus states $Y=T_{3R}+(B-L)/2$ as a formula. Here the two ingredients are named as separate $U(1)$s before being combined, and the charges of every scalar and fermion are tabulated in both. That makes the formula checkable rather than assertable: the quark doublet is $(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{1/6,1/3}$, the lepton doublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)_{0,\ldots}$, and $Y$ is the linear combination that reproduces the measured charges. The corpus's Pati–Salam article should show the intermediate table, not just the final formula, because the table is what proves the formula.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md, in the hypercharge section, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-and-the-three-neutral-states.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The decomposition is exact in any Pati–Salam model.

  1. An embedding $\mathbf4=(1,\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)$ that produces three families

The idea. The $Z_3$ orbifold acts on the $\mathbf4$ of the $R$-symmetry with one trivial and three non-trivial phases. Three families come out because three of the four components transform non-trivially and one does not.

What it would add. A mechanism for the number three that is not a choice. The corpus's Pati–Salam article takes three families as given, and the standard objection to any family-counting argument is that the number is put in. Here it is the index of an orbifold action, which is a topological quantity. That is a different kind of answer and the corpus does not have one.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's family-count discussion.

What stands in the way. The mechanism is stringy, and the corpus's own three-generation arguments (if any) would have to be reconciled with it. Import it as an existence proof that a discrete symmetry can fix the count, not as a competing explanation.

What the Paper Does

A short conference note. It embeds the scotogenic model — radiative neutrino mass with the loop particle as dark matter — inside $SU(7)$, with an intermediate Pati–Salam stage at about $10^3$ TeV. Two claims:

  1. "$\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry needed for the realization of Scotogenic scenario is a residual symmetry and not ad hoc."
  2. The radiative mechanism is forced onto part of the quark sector too: "this requirement will also predict the up-type $Q=\pm2/3$ quark masses to be generated radiatively as well, due to quark-lepton unification symmetry."

The second is the interesting one and the paper mentions it in a single sentence without following it up.

One structural detail matters for the corpus: the intermediate group here is $SU(4)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R$, not the full $SU(2)_R$ Pati–Salam. There is no $W_R$ and no left–right doubling of the weak group — only a $U(1)$ carrying the right-handed isospin charge.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Field Content and the Breaking

"The model consists of the $G_{\rm GUT}=SU(7)$ with $21_F$, $7^*_F$, $35^*_F$ fermion and $140_S$, $28_S$ scalar fields. The model is chiral anomaly free, $32-12-1=0$."

The anomaly cancellation is written as three integers summing to zero, which is a compact way to state a $\mathrm{tr}$ condition: the cubic anomaly contributions of the $\mathbf{21}$, the $\overline{\mathbf7}$ and the $\overline{\mathbf{35}}$ respectively.

"The $SU(7)$ GUT is then spontaneously broken to $G_{\rm PS}=SU(4)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R$ by $140_S$. The Yukawas of the model are $21_F\times35^*_F\times140_S$, $21_F\times7^*_F\times140^*_S$, $35_F\times35_F\times140_S$, $7^*_F\times7^*_F\times28_S$ which generate the masses for all fermions. $28_S$ scalar field is required here in order to generate the Majorana mass for the neutral $\mathbb Z_2$ odd fermion in the Scotogenic model."

Then: "$G_{\rm PS}$ is broken down to $G_{\rm SM}=SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ at $\sim\mathcal O(10^3\ \mathrm{TeV})$".

Note that $21_F=\mathbf{21}$ of $SU(7)$ contains a $\mathbf{6}$ of $SU(4)$ (antisymmetric of the antisymmetric) — the same $\mathbf6$ that appears as exotics in all the other Pati–Salam papers, and here it is a constituent of the matter multiplet rather than a separate field.

The Charge Generators

Five generators are displayed as linear combinations of $SU(7)$ Cartan generators. Under the two-step breaking the electric charge is:

$$ Q=(I_3+Y)_{\rm SM}=I_3+R+\tfrac{\sqrt6}{3}T_4+\tfrac{\sqrt7}{3}\Big(\tfrac{\sqrt7}{5}T_4+\tfrac{\sqrt{15}}{5}T_5+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{7}T_6\Big), $$

with the $U(1)_R$ charge

$$ R=\tfrac{4}{\sqrt{10}}T_4+\tfrac{4}{\sqrt{15}}T_5+\tfrac{3}{\sqrt7}T_6, $$

and, additionally, "one diagonal generator at the GUT scale, here labeled as $Z_7$ which is $Z_7=\tfrac{5}{\sqrt7}T_4+\tfrac{1}{14}\sqrt{15}T_5-2\tfrac{\sqrt{15}}{2}T_6$."

Three generators of three different ranks appear in the same breath: $Q$ for the unbroken $U(1)$, $R$ for the $U(1)_R$ factor, and $Z_7$ for the extra diagonal direction that must be absent from the low-energy algebra. The $Z_7$ combination given looks garbled in the PDF extraction (the coefficient $-2\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}$ is suspect); the structure is what matters, and the structure is the standard one: a rank-$6$ group has six Cartan generators, four are used by $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, and two must be lifted by vevs.

The vevs are listed in Table 1 by scale and representation, e.g. $28_S\sim(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ at the PS scale, $140_S\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf0)$ and $(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,-1)$ at the electroweak scale, $140_S\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,0)$ at the GUT scale.

The Scotogenic Part

"Neutrino masses are generated radiatively through the $\mathbb Z_2$ odd sector, just like in Scotogenic model. Since our model has 3 neutral colorless fermions, there will be 2 neutral $\mathbb Z_2$ odd fermions to mediate the radiative neutrino mass diagram. In order to get scotogenic scenario to work $v_5$ and $v_{10}$ VEV's from Tab. 1 need to be 0."

The three loop topologies are shown in Figure 1: a $\mathbb Z_2$-odd singlet $N\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,0)_{\rm SM}$, a doublet $A^0\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,-\frac12)_{\rm SM}$, and their mixing.

The consequence:

"Focusing on neutral mass matrix we can see that for the scotogenic scenario we need $v_5$ and $v_{10}$ to be 0 which will split neutral fermion mass matrix in $\mathbb Z_2$ even and odd parts. This requirement will also predict the up-type $Q=\pm2/3$ quark masses to be generated radiatively as well, due to quark-lepton unification symmetry."

That is the paper's sharpest statement and it is stated once. Setting a vev to zero to protect a dark-matter symmetry also removes the tree-level mass of one up-type quark, because the same vev appears in the quark and lepton mass matrices — and the reason it appears in both is that quarks and leptons sit in the same $SU(4)$ multiplets. The radiative mechanism is then not a choice for the up quark, it is a consequence.

"Another interesting thing to notice is that, because there are 3 neutral fermions predicted by the model we can naturally generate inverse see-saw, linear see-saw, double see-saw, etc. in this model if we give up scotogenic scenario."

Unification

"Unification is achieved at $M_{\rm GUT}\sim\mathcal O(10^{12-16}\ \mathrm{TeV})$ scale with intermediate $SU(4)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R$ Pati-Salam symmetry at $\sim\mathcal O(10^3\ \mathrm{TeV})$" with $\alpha_s(m_Z)=0.1182$, $\alpha_{\rm em}(m_Z)=1/127.916$, $\alpha_2(m_Z)=0.03393$, and "U(1) normalization given by $n_Y=\frac{17}{3}$ and $n_R=\frac{5}{3}$. Then $\alpha=\frac{g^2}{4\pi}$ at unification scale is $\alpha_U(M_U)=0.08577$ with $M_U=10^{12}$ GeV."

And the claim that interests the corpus: "This model, due to large amount of scalar fields, can easily predict the weak angle at $m_Z$ scale to be given as $\sin^2\theta_W(m_Z)=\frac38$."

The sentence is careless and the corpus should not copy it. $\frac38$ is the value at unification, where $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R$ has $SU(2)_L$ coupling equal to the $SU(4)$ coupling and $g_R$ — it is emphatically not the value measured at $m_Z$, which is $0.231$. What the model gives is $\frac38$ at the PS scale, which then runs down. The authors likely mean the matching condition at $M_{\rm PS}$; as written the sentence is wrong.

Also worth pinning: "When achieving gauge coupling unification we took only the relevant fields, fields from Ref. [8] and fields necessary for radiative neutrino mass generation, to be at the Pati-Salam scale or lighter with all other fields at the $M_{\rm GUT}$ scale." That is a hand-tuned threshold spectrum, and it is why the unification works.

The Spectrum

"Our model includes 4 Dirac $Q=\pm1$ leptons, 4 down-type quarks, 2 up-type quarks, 3 neutral fermions, 2 exotic Dirac $Q=\pm2$ leptons, 2 exotic up-type $Q=\pm4/3$ quarks. Exotic particles can be used to test this model."

Four down-type quarks and two up-type quarks is not three generations. It is a split fermion content, and it is what a rank-$6$ embedding of a single $\mathbf{21}$ produces. The paper does not address whether this can be reconciled with three observed generations.

The dark matter section is one paragraph plus three figures: "Any of the $\mathbb Z_2$ odd particles can be considered for light stable particle and be the dark matter candidate ... Scalar, pseudo-scalar, fermion dark matter candidates are possible in our model." No numbers are quoted in the text.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. A vev set to zero to protect dark matter also sets a quark mass to zero

The idea. The scotogenic $\mathbb Z_2$ needs $v_5=v_{10}=0$. Those same vevs appear in the up-type quark mass matrix, so the up-type masses become radiative. "due to quark-lepton unification symmetry."

What it would add. The corpus's Pati–Salam article describes quark–lepton unification as a statement about representations — one $\mathbf4$ holds a colour triplet and a singlet, so the gauge couplings are related. Here it is a statement about mass matrices: the same entry serves a quark and a lepton, so a condition imposed for the lepton sector propagates to the quark sector whether you want it or not. Quark–lepton unification is a constraint on parameters, not only on quantum numbers. That is a sharper version of the claim and the corpus does not have it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the section on what the $\mathbf4$ implies, and articles_physics/the-seesaw-mechanism-and-the-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. In a full $SU(2)_R$ Pati–Salam the left and right sectors are separate multiplets, so a vev in the right-handed sector need not touch the up-type masses in the same way. The propagation here relies on the structure of a single $\mathbf{21}$ of $SU(7)$, which is more unified than Pati–Salam alone. The claim is transferable in weakened form: any condition imposed to protect a symmetry in the lepton sector has a counterpart in the quark sector, and the corpus can state that without the $SU(7)$ machinery.

2. $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R$ is not the same theory as $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$

The idea. The intermediate group here has a $U(1)_R$, not an $SU(2)_R$. There is no right-handed doublet, no $W_R$, and no left–right parity.

What it would add. A distinction the corpus blurs. Every other paper in this batch writes $SU(2)_R$; this one writes $U(1)_R$ and calls it Pati–Salam. The two differ in what they predict: $SU(2)_R$ gives a $W_R$ and a $Z'$ and ties the top and bottom Yukawas through the bidoublet; $U(1)_R$ gives only a $Z'$. The corpus's Pati–Salam article should say which one it means, because "Pati–Salam" alone is not enough to specify the model.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article, in the definition, as a footnote on the $R$-charge variant.

What stands in the way. Nothing. It is a one-sentence clarification that prevents a class of misreadings.

3. $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38$ is a matching condition, not a low-energy value

The idea. The paper writes "$\sin^2\theta_W(m_Z)=\frac38$" and that is false as written; $\frac38$ holds at the scale where the symmetry is intact.

What it would add. A correction the corpus should record, because the corpus's own Pati–Salam article quotes $\frac38$ and must quote it with its scale. The number is a boundary condition, and boundary conditions run. If the corpus quotes $\frac38$ anywhere without saying "at $M_{\rm PS}$", it is making the same error this paper makes.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's $\sin^2\theta_W$ paragraph, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-the-neutral-current-and-the-z-coupling.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing. It is a check rather than an idea, but it is the kind of check that catches real mistakes.

4. $\mathbb Z_2$ as a residual symmetry of the breaking, not an imposed parity

The idea. "$\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry needed for the realization of Scotogenic scenario is a residual symmetry and not ad hoc."

What it would add. A structural defence against the standard objection to scotogenic models, which is that the $\mathbb Z_2$ is put in by hand and should be violated by quantum gravity. If the parity is what remains of a gauge symmetry after breaking, it is protected the same way $R$-parity is protected in supersymmetry. The argument is identical in form to the corpus's own claims about residual discrete symmetries from Pati–Salam breaking, and it strengthens them: the corpus can cite a working example where the residual symmetry is doing real work.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's discrete-symmetry discussion, next to whatever the corpus says about $R$-parity or $D$-parity as residuals.

What stands in the way. The paper does not show which generator the $\mathbb Z_2$ is, or verify it survives all the vevs. It asserts the residuality. The corpus should note the argument and the gap.

5. Exotic charges $Q=\pm2$ and $Q=\pm\frac43$ from a rank-6 embedding

The idea. The spectrum contains "2 exotic Dirac $Q=\pm2$ leptons, 2 exotic up-type $Q=\pm4/3$ quarks".

What it would add. A concrete example of what happens to charge quantisation when the embedding is bigger. The corpus's Pati–Salam article emphasises that charge quantisation follows from $SU(4)$ and gives integer and third-integer charges. Here, with $SU(7)$, fractional charges of $\frac43$ and integer charges of $2$ appear, because the extra Cartan directions shift the assignments. Charge quantisation survives; charge smallness does not. That is a real cost of going beyond rank 4 and the corpus should record it as such.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's charge-quantisation paragraph, as the caveat.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

One observation about the corpus's map

The chain here is $SU(7)\to SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R\to SU(3)\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$, and the unification scale is quoted as "$10^{12-16}$ TeV" — a range spanning four orders of magnitude, which is not a prediction. Combined with the hand-selected threshold spectrum, the unification in this paper is arranged rather than predicted.

That matters because the paper's own motivation is that Pati–Salam "unifies quarks and leptons" at a low scale, and the unification of couplings is then not the point. What the paper actually demonstrates is that a large enough scalar sector can always be tuned to unify. The corpus should treat this as a negative example: the presence of $\frac38$ and a GUT-scale coupling equality does not by itself constitute evidence for the embedding, and the corpus's own arguments for Pati–Salam rest on charge quantisation and anomaly structure rather than on coupling unification. That distinction is worth keeping.

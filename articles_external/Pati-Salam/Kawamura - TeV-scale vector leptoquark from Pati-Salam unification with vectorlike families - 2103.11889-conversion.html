What the Paper Does

It shows how to make the $SU(4)$ vector leptoquark light without introducing a second gauge group — by adding vector-like families and tuning one texture.

"The leptoquark mass is constrained to be heavier than PeV-scale by the measurement of a flavor violating kaon decay, $K_L\to\mu e$, in conventional models. This strong constraint can be avoided by introducing vectorlike families consistently with the quark and lepton masses and CKM and PMNS matrices."

That is the alternative to the 4321 route, and it should be recorded as such. 2106.03876 and following replace $SU(4)$ with $SU(4)\times SU(3)'$; this paper keeps $SU(4)$ and arranges the fermion mass matrices so the leptoquark couples almost diagonally.

"The other flavor violating processes are also suppressed. In this model, the vector leptoquark can be sufficiently light to explain the recent $b\to s\mu\mu$ anomaly, while the $b\to c\tau\nu$ anomaly is difficult to be explained due to the strong constraints from the $Z^0$ boson and vectorlike quark searches at the LHC."

So this route gets one anomaly and not the other, which is the opposite outcome from the twin-PS route's first version. Worth recording side by side.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Three Achievements and the Tuning

"We have proposed an explicit model with the PS gauge symmetry and extra vectorlike fermions that realizes - a vector leptoquark which accounts for the $b\to s\mu\mu$ anomaly - the realistic mass spectrum of the SM fermions - sufficiently suppressed $\mu$–$e$ flavor violations due to tuning of the parameters"

"Due to tuning" is in the list, and the abstract quantifies it: "$O(0.2\%)$ tuning is required in the fermion matrix." That is a one-in-five-hundred adjustment, and the corpus should record the number rather than the adjective.

Why $K_L\to\mu e$ Is the Obstruction

The $SU(4)$ vector leptoquark couples to $Q$ and $L$ with equal gauge strength for all generations, because the group does not distinguish them. The coupling matrix is the identity in the gauge basis, so it is the identity in any basis only if the rotation matrices are equal.

They are not: the up-type and down-type rotations differ by the CKM matrix, and the quark and lepton rotations differ by the PMNS matrix. So after rotating to mass eigenstates the leptoquark coupling is $$ g_{U_1}\;\Omega_u^\dagger\Omega_d,\qquad \Omega_e^\dagger\Omega_\nu, $$ and these contain the CKM and PMNS matrices — which have large off-diagonal entries. So the leptoquark mediates $K_L\to\mu e$ at a rate fixed by the PMNS mixing, and the mass must be above a PeV.

That is the same obstruction as in 2305.19356, derived from the other side. There the fix was to make the light families singlets; here the fix is to change the mass matrices so the rotations are nearly equal.

The Fix: Vector-Like Families with a Specific Texture

"The texture in Eq. (2.23) of the vectorlike fermion masses is a key idea to suppress the $\mu$–$e$ flavor violation, especially $K_L\to\mu e$ decay. We have showed in Fig. 1 that ${\rm BR}(K_L\to e\mu)$ is less than the current limit if we adopt the $O(0.2\%)$ tuning of the parameters."

So the tuning does one job: it makes the quark and lepton rotation matrices nearly aligned. The vector-like fermions enter the mass matrices in a way that lets the mixing angles be dialled, and the dials are set so the leptoquark's off-diagonal couplings vanish.

That is a different philosophy from the 4321 papers. There, an exact structure (singlet light families) removes the couplings. Here, a fine-tuned structure suppresses them. The first is robust; the second is not. The sequel 2201.04638 shows the second fails at one loop.

Why $b\to c\tau\nu$ Fails

"we have pointed out that the combination of the $Z'$ boson and vectorlike quark searches exclude the light leptoquark to explain the $b\to c\tau\nu$ anomaly."

The reason is structural: a $U_1$ coupling that produces $b\to c\tau\nu$ strongly must also produce $b\to s\tau\tau$ and $b\to b\tau\tau$, and the latter two are constrained by $Z\to\tau\tau$ and by di-tau searches. In the 4321 models the same problem appears but is evaded by the left-handed structure; here it is not.

So this route cannot explain $R(D^{(*)})$, and the paper says so plainly.

The Higgs Problem

"sizable neutral meson mixings, induced by the extra Higgs doublets, are unavoidable." "heavy Higgs bosons lighter than $4.8$ ($2.8$) TeV are excluded by the measurement of $B$ meson mixing when $\tan\beta=2$ ($50$)."

So the model has a second, independent problem: the extra scalars that give the leptoquark structure also mediate meson mixing. That is a cost of the vector-like-family route: the same $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$-type scalars that generate the texture generate flavour-changing neutral currents at tree level.

The 4321 papers avoid this because the third family is a singlet; here nothing is.

The Prediction

"the available parameter space to explain the $b\to s\mu\mu$ anomaly is probed by future searches for $\mu\to e\gamma$ and $\mu{\rm Al}\to e{\rm Al}$ processes. The former process is sensitive to the couplings with the SM leptons and vectorlike quarks, while the later is sensitive to the SM leptons and SM down quarks. The future experiments cover the parameter space that satisfies $|s_Q+s_\ell|\gtrsim0.002$ and $|s_Q-s_\ell|\gtrsim3.0\times10^{-5}$."

And a neutrino-oscillation prediction: "the neutron oscillation is also predicted by the couplings involving $\Sigma$. We estimate the transition amplitude and conclude that our prediction is much below the current experimental bound."

2. The leptoquark coupling after rotation is $\Omega_u^\dagger\Omega_d$ and $\Omega_e^\dagger\Omega_\nu$

What it would add. A precise statement of why the minimal model cannot be light: the PMNS matrix is not small, so the off-diagonal leptoquark coupling is not small. This is the mechanism behind the PeV bound, and the corpus should state it in these terms rather than as a numerical bound.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and the twin-PS series are the two answers to the same question — how do you get a TeV vector leptoquark out of Pati–Salam? — and the sequel settles it.

The answer here is a texture. Keep the group, add vector-like families, dial the mass matrices so the leptoquark's off-diagonal couplings cancel to $0.2\%$. The price: no $R(D^{(*)})$, extra-Higgs meson mixing, and a tuning that loop corrections can undo.

The answer there is a group. Replace $SU(4)$ with $SU(4)\times SU(3)'$, make the light families singlets, and the couplings are absent by symmetry. The price: a colour octet at $3.5$ TeV, six vector-like families, and a tuned 2-3 structure.

Both pay, and neither is obviously better, which is the honest state of the field in 2021 and worth recording as such. The sequel's finding — "the box diagrams are not suppressed even with the conditions (i) and (ii)" — is the technical reason the first route is worse than it looked, and it is the most important result in this pair.

What the Paper Does

It closes the enumeration that hep-th/0403061 began, with a definite number.

"Implementing this algorithm with the help of a computer program, we find that in total there are 202752 models. Because each equivalent class has 6144 models, we obtain that there are only 33 independent models with different gauge coupling relations at string scale after moduloing the equivalent relations."

"Thus, for the string landscape study, it is very important to consider the equivalent relations."

"First, there is one and only one inequivalent model with gauge coupling unification, i.e., the strong, weak and hypercharge gauge couplings $g_a^2$, $g_b^2$ and $\frac{5}{3}g_Y^2$ satisfy > $$g_a^2=g_b^2=\frac{5}{3}g_Y^2=g_c^2=\left(\frac{5}{3}g_Y^2\right)=4\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{\pi e^{\phi_4}}{},$$ > where $\phi_4$ is the dilaton field."

So the answer to the fifteen-year search is: one. Out of 202752 configurations and 33 inequivalence classes, exactly one has gauge coupling unification at the string scale.

That is a definitive result and it should be recorded as such. The corpus can say: the D6-brane Pati–Salam landscape has been enumerated, and exact unification selects a single point.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Arithmetic of the Landscape

Three numbers, and each means something different:

  • 202752 configurations: brane wrapping numbers satisfying tadpole cancellation, three families, and $N=1$ SUSY.
  • 6144 per equivalence class: the size of the orbit under the equivalence relations (permutations of the three two-tori, T-dualities, relabelling of stacks).
  • 33 independent gauge-coupling relations: 202752/6144 = 33, exactly.

So the landscape's size is a symmetry-orbit effect, not a diversity of physics. The 202752 models fall into 33 physical classes. That is the paper's central methodological point ("it is very important to consider the equivalent relations"), and it is a warning: a naive count of solutions overstates a landscape by the size of its symmetry group.

The corpus should record the ratio: a factor of 6144 between the naive and the physical count. Any landscape paper reporting only the raw number is reporting a symmetry artefact.

The Unification Relation

$$g_a^2=g_b^2=g_c^2=\tfrac{5}{3}g_Y^2=4\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\,\pi e^{\phi_4}$$

Note what this says and what it does not. It says the three string-scale couplings of the $a$ (colour), $b$ ($SU(2)_L$), and $c$ ($SU(2)_R$) stacks are equal and equal to $\frac53 g_Y^2$. That is the standard Pati–Salam unification condition — the $\frac53$ is the hypercharge normalization.

But the $\sqrt{2/3}$ and the $e^{\phi_4}$ are string-specific: the overall magnitude is set by the dilaton, not by a GUT coupling constant. So "unification" here is a relation between couplings, not a value — the value is a modulus. The corpus should note the difference from field-theory unification: in a 4D GUT, unification fixes $\alpha_{\rm GUT}\approx1/25$; here the common value floats with $\langle\phi_4\rangle$, and the dilaton is stabilised by gaugino condensation.

The Four Confining Groups

"there are **four confining

$USp(2)$ gauge groups**, and then we might stabilize the moduli and break SUSY via gaugino condensation."

Four, not two. The earlier papers (hep-th/0403061) required" at least two "; the unique unified model has four. More confining groups give more non-perturbative terms and better moduli stabilisation, which is why four is good. And $USp(2)\cong SU(2)$, so these are $SU(2)$ gauge groups with gaugino condensation — the standard race-track or multi-condensate scenario.

The Three Representative Models

"Model 1 in Table 3 is a representative model with gauge coupling unification at string scale; Model 2" "are two kinds of models with the largest possible wrapping number 5, which are related to each other by exchanging b− an c−stacks of D6-branes up to equivalent relations."

"The gauge symmetry groups in Model 1 has four U Sp(2) groups with negative β functions, so their moduli may be stabilized and supersymmetry might be broken via gaugino condensations. However, Models 2 and 3 contain two U Sp(2) groups, and only one U Sp(2) group has negative β function. In Model 1, similar to [23], the additional exotic particles can be decoupled."

So the unified model is also the best on the hidden-sector criterion, which is a happy coincidence and worth noting — the one model with unification also has the most confining groups.

"the additional exotic particles can be decoupled. In Models 2 and 3, similar" "we can decouple the additional exotic particles except the four chiral multiplets under SU (4)C anti-symmetric representation. And"

So the unified model's exotics can all be made heavy, while the large-wrapping models retain four chiral $\mathbf6$-of-$SU(4)$ multiplets permanently. That is decisive: Model 1 is the only one where the low-energy spectrum can be made to match the MSSM.

The corpus should record this, because it is the real reason the single unified model matters — not unification alone, but unification plus a decoupled exotic sector.

The $\mathbf6$ of $SU(4)$, Again

Four chiral multiplets in the antisymmetric of $SU(4)$ — these are the $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$-type fields, i.e. diquarks, exactly the objects that 2105.14029 shows mediate proton decay.

So Models 2 and 3 are excluded by proton decay, because they retain four chiral diquarks. And Model 1 escapes only because its diquarks can be given GUT-scale masses.

That connects this string paper to the field-theory proton-decay analysis in this batch, and the corpus should make the link: a string model's viability can hinge on whether its $\mathbf6$-type exotics are vector-like (decouple) or chiral (fatal).

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 2112.09632 are the batch's only papers that close a question rather than open one, and that makes them valuable as anchors.

The question: how many $N=1$ SUSY three-family Pati–Salam models are there on $T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2)$ with intersecting D6-branes? The answer: 33 physics classes, one with exact gauge unification.

That is a complete answer and it took eighteen years and four papers (hep-th/0403061, 1910.04530, 1912.11633, and these two). The corpus should present the arc, because a reader who sees only hep-th/0403061's "11 models will think the landscape is small and a reader who sees only 2112.09632's 202752" will think it is enormous. Both numbers are wrong without the equivalence classes.

The second point, and the more interesting one for the corpus's article: the unique unified model survives not because it unifies but because its exotics decouple. Unification without a decoupled exotic sector is worthless, and the paper's own comparison of Model 1 against Models 2 and 3 makes that clear. A model-building criterion that selects on unification alone would have picked the wrong model — or rather, would have found a model that cannot be the Standard Model at low energy.

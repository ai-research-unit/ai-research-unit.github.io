What the Paper Does

It is the first $S_3$ modular Pati–Salam model, with three benchmarks and, unusually, a spread of unification scales.

"we present in this work the first implementation of the $S_3$ modular symmetry within the Pati-Salam unification framework ... Assigning left- and right-handed matter fields as $S_3$ doublets or singlets, we propose three benchmark models that achieve compelling fits to 16 observables including charged fermion mass ratios and flavor mixing parameters."

"All models favor a normal neutrino mass ordering, with the atmospheric mixing angle in the lower octant. For models I and III, the effective Majorana mass $m_{\beta\beta}$ is within the reach of upcoming neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments, while it is too small to be detected in model II."

That split is the paper's most useful feature. Three models with the same field content and different modular weights, and one of them is distinguishable from the other two by $0\nu\beta\beta$. The corpus should record the split rather than a single prediction, because it shows that "the model" does not exist — there are three and they differ in a measurable way.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why $S_3$ Modular, and the Honest Note

"While several PS models have been developed based on conventional non-Abelian discrete flavor symmetries, no PS model incorporating the standard $S_3$ group has been presented in the literature."

The footnote is the interesting part: conventional $S_3$ was omitted, and the paper presents the modular version as the first. So the gap being filled is real but narrow, and the paper says so rather than overselling.

$S_3\cong\Gamma_2$ is the level-2 modular group — the smallest nontrivial finite modular group, with a doublet and two singlets. Same minimality argument as 2503.14610, from the other side.

The Minimal Field Content, Held Fixed

"In the scalar sector, we have adopted a minimal field content consisting of the multiplets $\Phi=(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $\Sigma=(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, and $\Delta_R=(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$."

"The VEV of $\Delta_R$ breaks the PS gauge group down to the SM gauge group and generates a Majorana mass for the RH neutrinos. Meanwhile, the VEVs of $\Phi$ and $\Sigma$ further break the SM gauge group to $SU(3)_C\otimes U(1)_Q$ and provide masses for the down quarks, up quarks, charged leptons, and Dirac neutrinos."

That is the canonical minimal Pati–Salam Higgs sector, identical to 1608.04124, 1701.04460, and 2305.19356: one $\mathbf{10}$ for $B-L$, one $\mathbf1$ for up-type, one $\mathbf{15}$ for down-type with the Georgi–Jarlskog factor.

And the three benchmarks share it, with the scalars "assumed to transform trivially under the modular $S_3$ group and are assigned identical modular weights". So the only difference between the three models is the assignment of $F$ and $F^c$.

That is a clean experimental design and worth recording as a methodological point: vary one thing, hold the rest fixed, report the spread in predictions.

The Three Models and the Scale Spread

"Model I (NO): $c_1v_R=7.89\times10^3$ GeV, Model II (NO): $c_1v_R=3.34\times10^{13}$ GeV, Model III (NO): $c_1v_R=1.29\times10^{14}$ GeV."

"Assuming $c_1\sim O(1)$, model I corresponds to a low-scale PS scenario, while models II and III align with high-scale PS scenario."

"Model I (NO): $M_1\sim1.0\times10^3$ GeV, $M_2\sim1.4\times10^6$ GeV, $M_3\sim1.4\times10^6$ GeV, Model II (NO): $M_1\sim3.3\times10^{13}$ GeV, $M_2\sim3.25\times10^{15}$ GeV, $M_3\sim7.15\times10^{15}$ GeV, Model III (NO): $M_1\sim1.9\times10^{11}$ GeV, $M_2\sim8.7\times10^{12}$ GeV, $M_3\sim2.4\times10^{17}$ GeV."

Model I's $v_R\sim10^4$ GeV is the standout. That is a TeV-scale Pati–Salam breaking with a right-handed neutrino at $1$ TeV — collider-accessible, and the same scale as the twin-PS and 4321 papers but reached by a completely different route (a modular weight assignment, not a second gauge group).

So the batch contains a fourth route to a low Pati–Salam scale: vector-like textures (2103.11889), 4321 (2106.03876), asymptotically safe running (1807.03669), and a modular-weight choice here. The corpus should collect these, because the fact that the scale is not fixed by the gauge group is itself informative — it is fixed by whatever generates the neutrino masses.

And the spread across the three models — $10^4$, $10^{13}$, $10^{14}$ GeV — spans ten orders of magnitude from one field content. That is a sobering fact about how much freedom the modular weights carry.

The Tensions, Stated

"the predicted mass ratios and mixing parameters generally agree with experimental data. However, a minor tension arises in model I, where the ratio $m_d/m_s$ slightly exceeds the $3\sigma$ experimental range."

"the sum of neutrino masses, $\sum m_i$, satisfies the DESI limit of $\sum m_i<72$ meV and falls within the sensitivity range of future experiments."

$m_d/m_s$ is the classic Pati–Salam tension, because the GJ factor $-3$ corrects $m_e/m_\mu$ but leaves the down-quark ratio to the texture. That it fails slightly in one of three models is informative: the texture cannot simultaneously fix $m_d/m_s$ and everything else in that assignment.

And $\sum m_i<72$ meV is a live bound — DESI's constraint on the neutrino mass sum — so the model is being tested by cosmology as well as by oscillation data.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. A TeV-scale Pati–Salam breaking from a modular weight choice

The idea. Model I has $v_R\sim10^4$ GeV and $M_1\sim1$ TeV, with the same Higgs sector as the high-scale models.

What it would add. A fourth entry in the batch's list of routes to a low scale, and the cheapest one — no new gauge group, no new fermions, just a different modular weight. The corpus should compare the four routes because they have very different costs.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scale section.

What stands in the way. The scale is a fit output, not a prediction, and models II and III give $10^{13}$–$10^{14}$ GeV for the same content.

2. Three models, one field content, one observable that separates them

The idea. $m_{\beta\beta}$ reachable in I and III, not in II.

What it would add. A worked example of the "hold everything fixed, vary one assignment" method, and a demonstration that $0\nu\beta\beta$ can discriminate within a model family. The corpus should use it as the template for how a landscape should be reported.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's neutrino section and its methodology discussion.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

3. $m_d/m_s$ is the residual tension in the $\mathbf{15}$ route

The idea. The GJ factor fixes $m_e/m_\mu$ and leaves $m_d/m_s$ to the texture; model I overshoots.

What it would add. A concrete statement of where the Georgi–Jarlskog mechanism is weakest. The corpus's GJ discussion should say that the relation is a factor-of-three statement about one ratio, not a solution to the down-quark sector.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's Yukawa section.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the tension is quoted.

4. The scale spread of ten orders of magnitude from modular weights

The idea. $10^4$, $10^{13}$, $10^{14}$ GeV from the same field content.

What it would add. A caution about claims that a model "predicts" a scale. If three assignments of the same fields span ten decades, the scale is a fit parameter and not a prediction.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scale section, as the counterweight to 2504.01893's and 1807.03669's computed scales.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 2503.14610 are the two $S_3$ modular Pati–Salam models in the batch, and their disagreement is the useful thing.

2503.14610: two-family seesaw for the light generations, one mechanism doing three jobs, order-unity coefficients, $\operatorname{Im}\tau\sim2$, $m_{\beta\beta}$ partly in nEXO's reach.

Here: minimal Higgs sector, three assignments fitted, scales spanning ten decades, $m_{\beta\beta}$ in two models and not the third.

So the same group and the same gauge theory give different physics, which is what modular symmetry was supposed to prevent. The modulus does not fix the model; the assignment does. The corpus should say that, because the rhetoric of modular model building suggests otherwise.

The second point: Model I's TeV-scale $v_R$ deserves attention. It is the batch's only low-scale Pati–Salam breaking that does not add a gauge group or a large fermion sector, and it arises because the modular weights can make the right-handed neutrino mass small. Whether that is a feature or an accident is the question — and the fact that the same content gives $10^{14}$ GeV in model III suggests accident.

A reader of the corpus should come away knowing that Pati–Salam's scale is not predicted by Pati–Salam. It is predicted by whatever fixes the neutrino mass, and in modular models that is a fit.

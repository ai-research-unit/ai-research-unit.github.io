What the Paper Does It pushes $SU(4)$ up to $SU(8)$ and uses the extra room to house the confining sector that makes the Higgs composite. >"Composite Higgs models can be extended to the Planck scale by means of the partially unified partial compositeness (PUPC) framework. We present in detail the Techni-Pati-Salam model, based on a renormalizable gauge theory SU (8)P S × SU (2)L × SU (2)R . We demonstrate"

"

This is the first complete and realistic attempt at providing an Ultra-Violet completion for composite Higgs models with top partial compositeness."

That sentence is the claim and it is strong. Composite Higgs models normally have a Landau pole problem: the confining sector and the SM sector are separate, and the four-fermion operators that generate partial compositeness are non-renormalisable. Here they come from gauge-boson exchange in a larger group, so the theory is renormalisable up to the Planck scale.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Problem Partial Compositeness Has

"Compared to the SM Higgs sector, which is parametrized via an elementary scalar field, it may naturally provide deeper insights into the possible origin of the EWSB and its connection to fermion mass generation. With the discovery of a 125 GeV Higgs-like particle and the need to accommodate the large top quark mass, it is then compelling to combine the idea of a pNGB Higgs and fermion partial compositeness"

The two ideas and why they are needed together: - A pNGB Higgs solves the hierarchy problem — its mass is protected by a shift symmetry, so it is naturally light. - Partial compositeness solves the top mass problem — a 125 GeV Higgs with a protected mass cannot have an $O(1)$ top Yukawa unless the top mixes with composite operators.

Both are needed, and neither works alone.

"In underlying gauge-fermion realisations, PC is realized via four-fermion operators built out of one SM fermion and three hyper-fermions charged under the new confining HC gauge group."

Those four-fermion operators are non-renormalisable, so the model has a cutoff. "In this work, we propose the first complete model, valid up to the Planck scale, that can generate the necessary four-fermion operators (PC4F) in a model that has all the necessary features to provide a realistic low energy dynamics."

So the (PC4F) operators are the target and the whole construction is built to produce them renormalisably. That is the paper's technical content and it is worth stating precisely: not "n 300 Composite Higgs models" but a way to generate PC4F without a cutoff.

The Mechanism: Gauge Bosons as Mediators

"This construction is based on the PUPC framework, where the HC and SM gauge symmetries are partially unified. When the larger gauge group undergoes spontaneous symmetry breaking, the resulting massive gauge bosons (and massive scalars) act as mediators for the PC4F operators."

That is the trick, and it is clean. In the usual setup you write the four-fermion operator by hand and it comes with a cutoff. Here the operator arises from exchanging a gauge boson of the unified group, so it is generated by a renormalisable coupling. The cutoff is replaced by the mass of the mediator, which is a physical scale.

And the mediators are the Pati–Salam leptoquarks, because those are exactly the gauge bosons that connect quarks to leptons. So the same objects that cause trouble elsewhere in this batch are here doing a job.

The Group and the Confinement

"We found that the simplest model is based on an SU (8)PS × SU (2)L × SU (2)R (TPS) gauge group, which breaks to an Sp(4)HC and the SM gauge groups at a high scale ΛPU ≈ 1016 GeV. A minimal anomaly-free"

"

A minimal anomaly-free set of fermions can embed both the SM fermions and hyper-fermions needed to generate PC at low energy."

$SU(8)\supset SU(4)\times SU(4)$ in the obvious way, and one $SU(4)$ is the ordinary Pati–Salam while the other confines. That is why $SU(8)$ and not something else: it is the smallest group containing both a Pati–Salam $SU(4)$ and a confining $SU(4)$ acting on the same fermions.

"

The strong dynamics is predicted to be that of a confining $Sp(4)_{HC}$ gauge group, with hyper-fermions in the fundamental and two-index anti-symmetric representations, with fixed multiplicities."

Note: $Sp(4)$, not $SU(4)$. The confinement group that survives is symplectic, and the hyper-fermion content is fixed by the anomaly-free condition. So the low-energy strong sector is predicted, not chosen — which is what lets the authors say "fixed multiplicities".

"

This motivates for Lattice studies of the Infra-Red near-conformal walking phase, with results that may validate or rule out the model."

That is an honest falsifiability statement: the model's viability depends on whether $Sp(4)$ with this matter content walks, and lattice QCD can determine that. The corpus should record that composite Higgs models can be tested by lattice calculations of a specific gauge theory, which is unusual among the models in this batch.

"This is the first complete and" "Ultra-Violet completion for composite Higgs models with top partial compositeness."

The Fermion Masses

"We demonstrate that masses and mixings for all generations of standard model fermions can be obtained via partial compositeness at low energy, with four-fermion operators mediated by either heavy gauge bosons or scalars."

"

We identify several mechanisms that can explain the mass split between the various SM fermions (i.e., leptons versus quarks, bottom versus top) and the lightness of neutrinos via an inverse see-saw mechanism that arises naturally in this construction."

So the same structure that gives partial compositeness also gives a mass hierarchy, because different generations mix with composite operators of different dimensions. That is a real advantage: the hierarchy is explained by mixing angles rather than by Yukawa tuning, which is the same logic as the twin-PS papers' vector-like mixing.

And the quark-lepton split comes from $SU(4)$: since $u$ and $\nu$, $d$ and $e$ are in the same multiplets, their mixings are related, and the breaking of $SU(4)$ splits them. That is Georgi–Jarlskog in a composite setting.

The Dark Matter

"In the baryon-number conserving vacuum, the theory also predicts a Dark Matter candidate, with mass in the few TeV range, protected by semi-integer baryon number."

"we pointed out that accidental $U(1)$ symmetries corresponding to the hyperbaryon number, the baryon number and the lepton number have important and interesting phenomenological consequences. In our TPS construction it is possible to preserve baryon number, thus avoiding strong constraints from proton and neutron decays, with the bonus feature of obtaining a Dark Matter candidate thanks to the presence of semi-integer baryon number neutral states."

"Semi-integer baryon number" is the interesting phrase. In a confining theory with $N_c=4$ (from $Sp(4)$), the baryons have baryon number in half-integer units, so the lightest such state is stable if baryon number is conserved mod 2. That is the same logic as technibaryon dark matter, and it gives a TeV-scale candidate.

**And "possible to preserve baryon number, thus avoiding strong constraints from proton and neutron decays"

** is the same advantage as 1808.07492 and 2307.02544: $SU(4)$'s leptoquarks conserve $B$.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's only entry where $SU(4)$ is used for confinement rather than for unification of quarks with leptons, and the contrast is worth one line in the corpus.

Everywhere else in the batch, $SU(4)$ does one thing: it puts the lepton in the fourth colour slot, and the leptoquarks are the price. Here $SU(4)$ is the colour of a new strong force, and the "leptoquark" gauge bosons of the enlarged group are the mediators that generate fermion masses. Same group, different job, different phenomenology. What that shows is that the group's content is thin — $SU(4)$ is a useful structure and the physics depends entirely on which sector it acts on. The corpus's article, if it presents $SU(4)$ as the Pati–Salam group with the leptoquark consequence, is presenting one use of a general tool as if it were the tool. The second point:"This motivates for Lattice studies of" "with results that may validate or rule out the model. This" is the most disciplined sentence in the batch. A model that names the calculation that would kill it is in a different category from one that names an experiment twenty years away.

And a caution: the paper's dark-matter and neutrino claims are sketched, not computed. The honest reading is that the PC4F mechanism is the achievement and the rest is a prospectus.

What the Paper Does It takes the flavour model of 1406.7005 and asks what it predicts at the LHC. >"the minimal supersymmetric standard model (MSSM) arising from a GUT scale Pati-Salam gauge group further constrained by an A4 × Z5 family symmetry, resulting in four soft scalar masses at the GUT scale: one left-handed soft mass m0 and three right-handed soft masses m1 , m2 , m3 , one for each generation. We demonstrate"

"We demonstrate that this model, which was initially developed to describe the neutrino sector, can explain collider and non-collider measurements such as the dark matter relic density, the Higgs boson mass and, in particular, the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon (g − 2)µ . Since"

"Since about two decades, $(g-2)_\mu$ suffers a puzzling about $3\sigma$ excess ... which our model is able to fully resolve. As the consequence of this resolution, our model predicts specific regions of the parameter space with the specific properties including light smuons and neutralinos, which could also potentially explain di-lepton excesses observed by CMS and ATLAS."

That is a clean chain: a flavour model, whose soft masses are fixed by the family symmetry, gives light smuons, which explain $g-2$, which predicts di-lepton bumps. Four observables from one structure.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Where the Four Soft Masses Come

From

"four soft scalar masses at the GUT scale: one left-handed soft mass m0 and three right-handed soft masses m1 , m2 , m3 , one for each generation."

This is the paper's structural content and it is a direct consequence of Pati–Salam plus $A_4\times Z_5$.

The logic: - Pati–Salam unifies left-handed squarks and sleptons into one multiplet, so they share a soft mass $m_0$. That is a genuine unification prediction: in the MSSM the left-handed squark and slepton masses are independent; here they cannot be. - The right-handed fields are in separate multiplets, so they need not share, and $A_4$ (which distinguishes the third generation) gives three different right-handed masses $m_1,m_2,m_3$.

So the soft spectrum is one for the left, three for the right, which is a distinctive signature and exactly the kind of thing a unified model should predict.

The corpus should record it, because it is the clearest example in the batch of flavour symmetry controlling the soft sector rather than the Yukawa sector.

Why Light Smuons

$(g-2)_\mu$ needs a chirality flip inside a loop, and the dominant SUSY contribution is

$$\Delta a_\mu\propto\frac{m_\mu^2\,\mu\tan\beta}{M_{\rm SUSY}^4}\times(\text{sign}),$$

so it wants light sleptons (especially smuons), large $\tan\beta$, and a modest $\mu$. The $A_4$ structure makes the second-generation right-handed slepton light, because the second generation's right-handed soft mass $m_2$ can be small without violating the first-generation bounds.

That is the mechanism, and it is flavour-symmetry-driven: the family symmetry distinguishes the generations, so it can make the second light and the first heavy. Without the symmetry, a light smuon implies a light selectron, which is excluded.

The corpus should state it that way, because it is the reason a flavour model is a natural solution to $g-2$: it can split the generations.

The Dark Matter and the Higgs

"ovid- – 22 – ing good dark matter candidates, consistently with all other experimental and theoretical constraints. We" "namely the lightest neutralino, which is consistently bino-like in nature."

Bino-like neutralino dark matter is the standard MSSM answer and it requires a specific relic-density mechanism (co-annihilation or resonance). The paper's claim is that the model's constrained spectrum lands in the right region.

And the Higgs mass: the MSSM needs $m_h\approx125$ GeV and stops that are heavy enough. The model must simultaneously fit $g-2$ (light sleptons), the Higgs mass (heavy stops), and the relic density (right neutralino) — a genuinely tight combination, and the paper's point is that the four-soft-mass structure has enough freedom without having too much.

The Honest Caveat

"We emphasise that (unlike some other models) the A to Z Pati-Salam model initially was not designed to explain ∆aµ , since its primary motivation was to explain the flavour mass and mixing of quarks and leptons, in particular neutrinos. Nevertheless, we have seen that the model is well suited to account for ∆aµ , while simultaneously"

That sentence is worth recording: it is the strongest form of the claim — the model was built for one purpose and then explains an anomaly it was not designed for. If true, that is evidence, not a fit.

And it is checkable: 1406.7005 predates the paper and its structure was fixed by flavour data.

The Di-lepton Excesses

"predicts specific regions of the parameter space with the specific properties including light smuons and neutralinos, which could also potentially explain di-lepton excesses observed by CMS and ATLAS."

Light smuons and neutralinos produce same-flavour opposite-sign dileptons through $\tilde\ell\to\ell\tilde\chi^0_1$. So the $g-2$ solution predicts a collider signature, which is the right way for a SUSY explanation to be testable.

The corpus should note the chain: light smuons → $g-2$ → dilepton excesses → LHC search. One structure, three observables, and the third is a prediction.

What Has Since Happened

The $(g-2)_\mu$ story has moved. The $3\sigma$ excess of 2016 has been revised: the BMW lattice calculation (2021) reduced the discrepancy, and the Fermilab measurement confirmed the experimental value. So the "full resolution" of 2016 may now be resolving a smaller discrepancy — or, under the newer lattice-based Standard Model value, none at all. The corpus should record this as a caution: a model built to explain a $3\sigma$ anomaly is only as good as the anomaly, and the muon $g-2$ anomaly's status depends on the hadronic vacuum polarisation calculation. The model's other predictions (light smuons, dileptons, dark matter) survive either way, which is the right way to build.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 1406.7005 are a rare pair in the batch: a model paper and its phenomenological application, by the same group, three years apart. The corpus should keep them together.

1406.7005 fits quarks and leptons with $S_4$ (later $A_4\times Z_5$), CSD4, and two symmetries, and predicts $\delta_{CP}\approx260^\circ$ and a normal hierarchy with $m_{\beta\beta}$ near the reach.

Here the same model gives light smuons, explains $g-2$, predicts dileptons, and provides bino dark matter.

The pair is the batch's best example of a model earning its keep: built for one sector, tested in another. And the fact that the second paper's predictions are different from the first's is what makes the pair useful — a model that predicts only what it was fitted to has not been tested.

The second point: the four-soft-mass structure is worth comparing with the Raby programme's mirage mediation (2009.04582, 1601.03749). Both fix the soft spectrum from a high-scale structure, and they fix it differently — one from a family symmetry, one from a mediation mechanism. The soft spectrum is where unified models make their sharpest statements, and the corpus's SUSY section should be built around that observation rather than around the gauge group.

What the Paper Does

This is the numerical follow-up to 1304.8050, and it does the calculation that paper deferred. The spectral action's gauge-kinetic terms come out with a common coefficient, so the action demands $g_R=g_L=g$ (Eq. 37). For the Standard Model the same demand fails against the measured couplings. For the spectral Pati–Salam model it succeeds, in all three scalar scenarios the framework allows — and that comparison is offered as "strong evidence for the spectral Pati–Salam model".

The three scenarios differ only in the finite Dirac operator, and each gives a different one-loop beta triple and a different unification scale:

scenario scalars $(b_R,b_L,b)$ $m_R$ $\Lambda$
composite, $k^\nu=k^u$, $k^e=k^d$ $\varphi,\Delta$ $(\tfrac73,3,\tfrac{31}{3})$ $4.25\times10^{13}$ $2.5\times10^{15}$
composite, no quark–lepton unification $\varphi,\Delta,\Sigma$ in $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf{15})$ $(\tfrac73,3,9)$ $4.1\times10^{13}$ $6.3\times10^{15}$
fundamental, $H_{aI\dot b\dot J}=0$ $\Sigma$, $H$ $(-\tfrac{26}{3},-2,2)$ $1.5\times10^{11}$ $6.3\times10^{16}$
left–right symmetric, fundamental $\Sigma$, $H$, $\tilde H$ $(-\tfrac{26}{3},-\tfrac{26}{3},-\tfrac43)$ $5.1\times10^{13}$ $2.7\times10^{15}$

All four unify. The paper's claim is that this is not a coincidence but the only consistent outcome.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Uniqueness Result Has Improved

The earlier paper reached $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$ by classifying finite geometries and imposing a symplectic constraint on the first factor — a step the authors called arbitrary. That has changed:

"the original argument by classification [4] of finite geometries F" "has now been replaced by a much stronger uniqueness statement [7, 8]. This new result shows that the algebra M2 (H) ⊕ M4 (C), (1) where H are the quaternions" "appears uniquely when writing the higher analogue of the Heisenberg commutation relations. This"

The construction: "The higher analogue of the Heisenberg relations puts the spatial variables on similar footing by combining them into a single operator Y using another set of γ matrices and it is in this process that the algebra (1) appears canonically and uniquely in dimension 4. We refer to [7, 8] for a detailed account." Concretely, "the above algebra comes out as a solution of the two-sided Heisenberg quantization relation between the Dirac operator D and the two maps from the four spin-manifold and the two four spheres S 4 × S 4 [7, 8]. This removes the arbitrary symplectic constraint and replaces it with a relation that quantize the four-volume in terms of two quanta of geometry and have far reaching consequences on the structure of space-time."

That is a real strengthening and it is easy to miss. In 2013 the algebra was the answer to which algebras survive the fermion-doubling and chirality constraints; the symplectic step was a choice. In 2015 it is the answer to a quantisation condition, and the choice is gone. The corpus should record the difference, because the corpus's own case for the algebra rests on the 2013 kind of argument.

And the scalar sector remains fixed: "the scalar sector can not be chosen freely, in contrast to the early work on Pati–Salam unification. In fact, there are only a few possibilities for the precise scalar content, depending on the assumptions made on the finite Dirac operator."

The Three Scales

"There is a hierarchy of three energy scales: SM, an intermediate mass scale mR where symmetry breaking occurs and which is related to the neutrino Majorana masses (1011 − 1013 Gev), and the GUT scale Λ."

That sentence is the paper's physical content in miniature. The intermediate scale is not a free parameter tuned to make unification work; it is identified with the see-saw scale, and the scan then asks whether unification follows. It does, at $m_R$ in the stated window.

Why one loop: "at two loops, the gauge and scalar couplings are mixed and influence each other. Moreover, the running of the scalar mass terms can not be trusted at all because of quadratic divergences."

$$ \frac{1}{g_L^2}=\frac{1}{g_2^2}, \qquad \frac{1}{g_R^2}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{g_1^2}+\frac{1}{g^2}, \qquad \frac{1}{g_3^2}=\frac{1}{g^2}, $$

"in terms of the Standard Model gauge couplings g1 , g2 , g3 . At". The factor $\tfrac23$ is the trace normalisation of the $B-L$ generator — the same $\mathrm{tr}(\lambda^m\lambda^n)=2\delta^{mn}$ convention as in 1304.8050, where $\lambda_{15}=\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\mathrm{diag}(3,-1,-1,-1)$. This is the one place in the corpus literature where the hypercharge embedding is fixed numerically rather than assumed, and the $\tfrac23$ is what encodes it.

The Three Models

Composite. The finite Dirac operator is first-order relative to $\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$, and "this condition is extremely constraining and forces the couplings of the right-handed neutrino to be with a singlet." The off-diagonal entry is $\delta^{1}_\alpha\delta^{\dot\beta0}_1\delta^I_1\delta^1_{K0}k^{*\nu_R}$, and the diagonal blocks are $2\times2$ with the Yukawa matrices in generation space. The scalars are the composites of Eq. (27),

$$ \Sigma^{\dot bJ}_{aI}=\big(k^\nu\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}+k^e\tilde\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}\big)\Sigma^J_I+\big(k^u\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}+k^d\tilde\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}\big)\delta^J_I-\Sigma^J_I, \qquad \tilde H^{\dot a}_{\ \dot b}\tilde{}^{J}_{\ I}=k^{*\nu_R}\Delta^{\dot aJ}_{\ \dot bI}\Delta^{\dot bI}_{\ \dot aI}, $$

with $\tilde\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}=\sigma_2\varphi^T\sigma_2$ "not an independent field". Beta triple $(\tfrac73,3,\tfrac{31}{3})$, $\Lambda\approx2.5\times10^{15}$ GeV at $m_R=4.25\times10^{13}$ GeV. Without the quark–lepton unification, the $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf{15})$ contributes and $(\tfrac73,3,9)$ gives $\Lambda\approx6.3\times10^{15}$ GeV at $m_R=4.1\times10^{13}$ GeV.

Fundamental. The general Dirac operator with $(\mathcal D_F)^{b0J0}_{aI}=0$ "implies that the Higgs field HaIbJ = 0. The inner perturbations ΣbJ and HaI are now themselves fundamental .", and "J = 0. The inner perturbations ΣbJ and HaI are now themselves fundamental . . aI bJ Higgs fields [9, Sect.". Beta triple $(-\tfrac{26}{3},-2,2)$ — and the authors flag it: "Note that the $SU(2)_R$ and $SU(2)_L$-sectors are not asymptotically free, due to the large scalar sector. Nevertheless, we can still run the gauge couplings." Unification at $\Lambda\approx6.3\times10^{16}$ GeV with $m_R=1.5\times10^{11}$ GeV.

Left–right symmetric fundamental. Add $H_{aI\dot b\dot J}$ in $(\mathbf1_R,\mathbf3_L,\mathbf{10})+(\mathbf1_R,\mathbf1_L,\mathbf6)$. Beta triple $(-\tfrac{26}{3},-\tfrac{26}{3},-\tfrac43)$, $\Lambda\approx2.7\times10^{15}$ GeV at $m_R=5.1\times10^{13}$ GeV.

Read the third sentence carefully. The Standard Model in the spectral framework also requires its three couplings to be equal at the cutoff — the same normalisation argument applies — and they are not. So the spectral Standard Model is internally inconsistent at one loop. Pati–Salam is the group for which the requirement is actually met.

That is a different kind of argument from the couplings happen to meet at $10^{16}$. It says the framework predicts its own breakdown in the Standard Model case and its own consistency in the Pati–Salam case. The corpus's grand-unification-in-the-biquaternion-framework-where-the-embedding-fails.md says the embedding fails; this says the embedding fails for a reason visible in the action, not just in the numbers.

Breaking of SU (2)R × SU (4) to U (1) × SU (3) occurs at some scale mR ∼ 1011 − 1013 Gev with a unification scale where the three coupling constants meet of the order of 1016 Gev."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

  1. The spectral action requires unification, and the Standard Model violates the requirement

The idea. The gauge-kinetic terms arrive with one common coefficient $F_0/2\pi^2$. Setting the Yang–Mills normalisation then forces $g_L^2=g_R^2=g^2$. Applied to the Standard Model the same step forces $g_1^2=g_2^2=g_3^2$, which the measured couplings violate.

What it would add. The mechanism behind the corpus's central negative result. The corpus's grand-unification article demonstrates that the embedding fails — the numbers do not work. This supplies the structural reason: the framework does not merely fail to predict unification, it demands it and is therefore falsified by its own spectrum unless the group is enlarged. That upgrades "the embedding fails" to the Standard Model cannot be the whole answer, by its own action.

Where it lands. articles_physics/grand-unification-in-the-biquaternion-framework-where-the-embedding-fails.md, as the opening argument rather than the closing one, and articles_physics/the-spectral-triple-of-the-biquaternion-algebra.md.

What stands in the way. The argument is one-loop and the authors say so. At two loops the scalar and gauge sectors mix and the normalisation argument becomes less clean. The corpus should record the loop order with the claim, since a one-loop argument for a structural conclusion invites exactly that objection.

  1. The intermediate scale is the see-saw scale, not a fit parameter

The idea. $m_R$ is "related to the neutrino Majorana masses (1011 − 1013 Gev), and the GUT scale Λ." and "we take it to be the same scale that is present in the see-saw mechanism". Unification then follows at $10^{15}$–$10^{16}$ GeV.

What it would add. A prediction with only one input. The corpus's seesaw article derives the Majorana scale from the light neutrino masses; here that scale is fed into the running and the output is the unification scale. One measured quantity in, one predicted scale out — the correct logical shape for a unification claim, and the corpus currently has no instance of it. Every other unification number in this corpus (Babu's $5.2\times10^{13}$, Braam's $10^{16}$) comes from a fit.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-seesaw-mechanism-and-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md and the Pati–Salam article's coupling section.

What stands in the way. The authors "adopt the usual running of the SM gauge couplings" below $m_R$ and "leaving a full analysis of the effect of the scalar fields after Pati–Salam symmetry breaking to future work", plus they "ignore the presence of non-renormalizable terms". So the input is not as clean as the logic suggests. Import the shape of the argument, not the precision.

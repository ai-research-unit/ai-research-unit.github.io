What the Paper Does

It builds the minimal model of its kind — smallest unified group, smallest modular group — and turns a neutrino mechanism loose on the charged fermions.

"We have proposed a model based on the smallest quark-lepton unified symmetry, the Pati-Salam gauge group and the smallest modular symmetry, S3 ."

"The masses of the third family of SM charged fermion arise from renormalisable Yukawa interactions involving a colourless scalar bi-doublet as well as a bi-doublet scalar in the adjoint representation of SU (4)C . The first and second family masses are naturally suppressed due to a Pati-Salam version of the type 10 I seesaw mechanism for neutrinos, but here mediated through heavier vector-like Pati-Salam fermions."

That is the paper's central idea and it is a good one. The seesaw explains why neutrino masses are small; here the same diagram explains why the first two generations are light. A hierarchy is generated by the same mechanism that generates the neutrino mass scale, which means one mechanism does two jobs.

"The modular S3 is perfect for implementing a two-family seesaw mechanism, since it admits doublet representations. The masses of the third family of SM charged fermion arise from renormalisable"

That is the cleanest match of group to purpose in the batch. $S_3$ has exactly one doublet and two singlets; the data have exactly two light families and one heavy. The representation theory mirrors the mass spectrum, and no other group fits so economically.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Two-Family Seesaw for Charged Fermions

"The diagrams responsible for the effective Yukawa operators are similar to those of the type I seesaw mechanism for neutrinos, with two insertions of vacuum expectation values, where one of them breaks electroweak symmetry, and one does not."

That sentence is the technical heart. In the neutrino seesaw, the light mass is $m_D M_R^{-1} m_D^T$: two insertions of the Dirac Yukawa and one heavy Majorana mass. For a charged fermion there is no Majorana mass to insert — but there is a vector-like mass $M$, and a VEV, and the diagram is structurally the same:

$$ m_f\sim\frac{\langle H\rangle\,M_V\,\langle\sigma\rangle}{M_V^2}, $$

so the effective Yukawa is suppressed by the ratio of the two VEVs to the vector-like mass. The heavy scale plays the role of $M_R$, and the small VEV plays the role of $m_D$.

And because Pati–Salam relates quarks to leptons, the same suppression applies to the Dirac neutrino mass — the paper says so explicitly: "Due to the Pati-Salam symmetry, the same mechanism also suppresses first and second family Dirac neutrino masses".

So one diagram gives $m_u,m_d,m_e,m_\nu$ all light, and the third family light only by having no vector-like partner. That is elegant, and the corpus should record it as a structural idea rather than a fit.

The $S_3$ Assignment and What It Buys

"The three Pati-Salam families are essentially distinguished by whether they couple to heavier vector-like fermions (the first two families) or not (third family), and this is controlled by their S3 assignments. The modular"

First and second families form the $S_3$ doublet; the third is a singlet. So the vector-like messengers $\Psi_n,\Psi^c_n$ ($n=1,2$) couple to the doublet, giving the seesaw to both light families at once — and the two messengers are what the doublet needs, which is why two and not one.

That is a genuine representation-theoretic necessity: a doublet needs two partners, and a two-family seesaw needs two messengers, and the two facts are the same fact.

"The vector-like fermions $\Psi_n$ and $\Psi^c_n$ ($n=1,2$) transforming as $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ and $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\bar{\mathbf2})$ ... are the seesaw messengers which mix with the SM fermionic multiplet fields $F_i$ and $F^c_i$ ($i=1,2,3$)."

Note the full Pati–Salam multiplets: the messengers are $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, i.e. quark-lepton unified, so the messenger that suppresses $m_d$ also suppresses $m_e$ and $m_\nu$. That is where the quark-lepton relation comes from in this model — not from a GJ factor but from the messenger's multiplet structure.

The Double Seesaw for Neutrinos

"the tiny active Majorana neutrino masses then arise from a double seesaw mechanism."

"in the neutrino sector there are additional fields leading to tiny active Majorana neutrino masses via a Double Seesaw mechanism."

So the chain is: two-family seesaw suppresses the Dirac mass → double seesaw suppresses the Majorana mass → $m_\nu$ is doubly suppressed. Two mechanisms in series, and the corpus should note that this is why the neutrino can be light without a GUT-scale $M_R$: the Dirac mass is already small, so the Majorana scale need not be astronomical.

The Fit and the Modulus

"The model is shown to describe all quark and lepton (including neutrino) masses and mixing angles, in terms of high energy mass scales, together with complex dimensionless Yukawa coefficients which are all of order unity, and a single complex modulus field τ with Im(τ ) ∼ 2. In Section II we present"

"All of order unity" is the naturalness claim, and it is the right claim for a modular model: the point of modular symmetry is that the hierarchy comes from the modular forms, not from small coefficients. And $\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\sim2$ is a specific region of moduli space, which is a prediction of sorts — the modulus sits near the self-dual point.

Compare 2404.06520's 19 real parameters with this model's order-unity coefficients and one $\tau$. If the count here is genuinely smaller, this is the more economical model; the corpus should say which and why.

The Predictions

"It provides a good fit to neutrino data, assuming a normal ordering of neutrino masses, while the inverted ordering is disfavoured. The associated prediction for neutrinoless double beta decay is partly within the reach of the nEXO experiment. In particular, the Dirac CP violating neutrino oscillation phase is predicted to lie in the range δ νCP ∼ 260o − 360o . 0.001 10-4 0.001 10-4 10-5 10-5 0 50 100 150 200 250 300 350 0 50 100 0."

Two things stand out:

  1. $m_{\beta\beta}$ partly within nEXO's reach — unlike 1406.7005 and 2404.06520, which both say too small to observe. So this model is distinguishable from its cousins by $0\nu\beta\beta$, which is exactly the kind of discrimination that makes a landscape useful.

  2. $\delta^\nu_{CP}\sim260^\circ$–$360^\circ$ overlaps 1406.7005's $260^\circ\pm5^\circ$ at the lower edge. Same author, eleven years apart, same CP-phase region — which is a consistency worth recording, and a reason to take the $260^\circ$ prediction seriously.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. The seesaw diagram that suppresses neutrinos can suppress charged fermions

The idea. With a vector-like messenger and a small VEV, the effective Yukawa is suppressed exactly as a neutrino mass is, and Pati–Salam applies it to all sectors at once.

What it would add. The most economical explanation of the charged-fermion hierarchy in the batch. Compare the alternatives: FN with flavons (1406.7005), modular forms (2404.06520), vector-like mixing without a seesaw (2302.10829). This one reuses a mechanism already needed for neutrinos.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's mass-hierarchy section.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the diagram is standard.

2. $S_3$'s doublet-plus-singlet structure matches two light families plus one heavy

The idea. The group's representation theory is the mass spectrum.

What it would add. The clearest example in the batch of a group chosen because its irreps match the data. And it explains why $S_3$ and not $A_4$: $A_4$'s triplet treats all three families alike, which is wrong when two are light and one is heavy.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's flavour section, as the minimal choice.

What stands in the way. Nothing; it is one line of group theory.

3. $m_{\beta\beta}$ distinguishes this model from its cousins

The idea. Here $m_{\beta\beta}$ can reach nEXO; in 1406.7005 and 2404.06520 it cannot.

What it would add. A concrete example of how $0\nu\beta\beta$ experiments discriminate within a family of models. The corpus should present the three predictions side by side, because together they show the discrimination working.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's neutrino section.

What stands in the way. Nothing; all three quote their ranges.

4. $\delta_{CP}\sim260^\circ$–$360^\circ$ repeated across a decade

The idea. Two papers by the same group, different symmetry, same CP-phase region.

What it would add. Corroboration for a prediction that is currently testable. The corpus should record the repetition and note that it may reflect a shared structural feature (Pati–Salam's Yukawa relations) rather than coincidence.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's neutrino section.

What stands in the way. It may be the same author's prior rather than a prediction.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper should be read as the endpoint of the progression 1406.7005 → 2404.06520 → here, and the progression is towards reuse.

2014: two symmetries, flavons, CSD4, many parameters. 2024: one modular symmetry, nineteen parameters, classification. 2025: smallest group, smallest modular group, one mechanism doing three jobs, order-unity coefficients.

The direction is towards a single mechanism with multiple consequences, which is what a good unified model should look like. And the mechanism is the seesaw — the same diagram that makes neutrinos light makes the first two generations light.

The corpus should say this plainly: in Pati–Salam, a vector-like messenger with a small VEV is a hierarchy generator, and it works in every sector simultaneously because $SU(4)$ puts the sectors in the same multiplets. That is a Pati–Salam-specific advantage and it is not available in the SM, where quark and lepton masses come from independent Yukawas.

The second point: the three flavour papers in this batch disagree about $m_{\beta\beta}$ and partly agree about $\delta_{CP}$. That is the ideal situation for a corpus — a set of related models with one discriminating observable and one corroborated prediction — and it is worth building the article's neutrino section around that contrast rather than around any single paper.

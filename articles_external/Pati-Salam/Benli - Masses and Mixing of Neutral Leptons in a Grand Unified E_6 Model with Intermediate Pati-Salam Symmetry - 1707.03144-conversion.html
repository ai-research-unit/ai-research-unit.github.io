What the Paper Does

The paper takes $E_6$ grand unification with an intermediate Pati–Salam stage and works out, in full, the neutral-lepton mass matrix that the symmetry breaking chain allows. Fermions sit in the $\mathbf{27}$, which under the chain yields five neutral fermions per generation, not one: the active left- and right-handed neutrinos, a heavy exotic Dirac pair, and a sterile Majorana state. The paper writes the allowed Yukawa couplings, converts them into a $5\times5$ real symmetric mass matrix, diagonalises the decoupled limit exactly, and treats the mixing as a perturbation. The headline is a comparison: the matrix derived here differs from the one derived earlier for an $E_6$ chain passing through $SU(5)$ by one entry, $M_{13}\neq0$, and that entry exists only because this chain passes through Pati–Salam. The paper's own verdict on that difference is honest and slightly disappointing: "we haven't been able to pinpoint a physical process that would distinguish between the two different symmetry breaking patterns."

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Chain

$$ E_6\to SO(10)\times U(1)_t \to SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4) \to SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{B-L} \to U(1)_Y\times SU(2)_I\times SU(3)_C . $$

The paper puts this next to the alternatives: $SU(5)$, whose chain drops rank by one at the last step and "gives rise to a $U(1)_Y$ charge"; and $SO(10)$, whose "drop by one in rank at the first step is associated with a new $U(1)_t$ charge". The $E_6$ chain has two separate $U(1)$ steps, which is why its fermion content is larger.

The Pati–Salam stage enters the way it always does — "an embedding of the colour symmetry $SU(3)_C\hookrightarrow SU(4)_C$" which "treats lepton number as a fourth colour", giving "a left-right symmetric extension of the SM".

Ranks along the chain, for the record: $E_6$ has $6$, $SO(10)$ has $5$, Pati–Salam $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ has $3+1+1=5$, the Standard Model has $4$. The Pati–Salam step does not drop rank. The rank that leaves at $E_6\to SO(10)$ is the $U(1)_t$, and the rank that leaves at Pati–Salam $\to$ Standard Model is the one carried by $Y$.

The $\mathbf{27}$ and Its Five Neutral Fermions

Bosons sit in the adjoint $\mathbf{78}$, fermions in the fundamental $\mathbf{27}$, and the Higgs in the $\overline{\mathbf{27}}$, justified by

$$ \mathbf{27}\times\mathbf{27}=\overline{\mathbf{27}}+\mathbf{351}+\overline{\mathbf{351}}. $$

The paper works the branching with the Cartan matrix of $E_6$, the weight diagramme of the $\mathbf{27}$ generated by LieArt, and projection matrices at each step — $P_1$ for $E_6\supset SO(10)$, $P_{13}$ for $SO(10)\supset SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)$, $P_5$ for $SU(4)\supset SU(3)_C$ — with the charge read off by a modified Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation,

$$ Q_{\mathrm{EM}}=I_{3L}+I_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L). $$

That is the corpus's own relation, and it is worth noting that it comes out of a completely independent route: the corpus derives it from $Y=T_{3R}+\frac12(B-L)$ and the tracelessness of $B-L$ on the $\mathbf 4$; here it comes from projecting $E_6$ weights down to $SU(3)_c\times U(1)_{\rm em}$.

The five neutral states, with their charges:

state $Q_t$ $I_{3L}$ $I_{3R}$ $B-L$ $Y$
$\nu_e$ $1$ $1/2$ $0$ $-1$ $-1$
$N^C_e$ $1$ $0$ $-1/2$ $1$ $0$
$N^C_E$ $-2$ $-1/2$ $1/2$ $0$ $1$
$\nu_E$ $-2$ $1/2$ $-1/2$ $0$ $-1$
$\nu_S$ $4$ $0$ $0$ $0$ $0$

Two entries do the work. $\nu_S$ carries charge $4$ under the $E_6$ $U(1)$ and nothing else — it is a total singlet of the Standard Model, and its exotic charge is what forbids it from pairing with the ordinary states. $\nu_E$ and $N^C_E$ carry $-2$ and form the exotic Dirac pair. The ordinary neutrino and its charge conjugate carry $1$, and the chain's remaining $U(1)$ is what makes the $\nu_S$ interaction possible only through a suppressed coupling.

The same exercise is repeated for the colour triplets ($u_L,d_L,D^C_R,u^C_R,d^C_R,D_L$) and the charged leptons ($e_L,e^C_R,E_L,E^C_R$), each with a table of charges in the $\mathbf{27}$ and in the $\overline{\mathbf{27}}$, and each with a matrix of allowed Yukawa charges. This is the paper's real content: not one mass matrix but the complete charge bookkeeping for the whole $\mathbf{27}$ under this chain.

The Matrix

Turning off the mixings, the neutral sector is three blocks: the active $2\times2$ with a Dirac mass $m$ and a large $M$ that "is introduced to induce a minimal seesaw mechanism"; the exotic $2\times2$ with Dirac mass $M'$; and the sterile corner with Majorana mass $m''$. The exact eigenvalues are

$$ M_+=\frac{M}{2}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^2+m^2}, \qquad -m_-=\frac{M}{2}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^2+m^2}, \qquad M',\ -M',\ -m'', $$

so that for $M\gg m$

$$ M_+\simeq M, \qquad m_-\simeq\frac{m^2}{M}. $$

The eigenvectors are written out, and the five parameters $(m,M,M',m'')$ plus five mixings $(M_{13},M_{14},m_{24},m_{35},m_{45})$ give the full matrix

$$ \mathcal M=\begin{pmatrix} 0&m&M_{13}&M_{14}&0\\ m&M&0&m_{24}&0\\ M_{13}&0&0&M'&m_{35}\\ M_{14}&m_{24}&M'&0&m_{45}\\ 0&0&m_{35}&m_{45}&-m'' \end{pmatrix}. $$

The paper's convention is that lower-case letters are small and capitals are large, all positive. Second-order perturbation theory in the mixings gives five corrections $\Delta M_+$, $\Delta m_-$, $\Delta M'_+$, $\Delta M'_-$, $\Delta m''$.

The result that matters is about the sterile mass. With the mixings on, "the active and sterile neutrinos do not mix, while both of these types can mix with the exotic neutrinos on their own", and "the sterile neutrino mass would be inversely proportional to the exotic neutrino mass, and directly proportional to the product of the sterile-exotic mixing coefficients". So

$$ m_\nu^{\rm sterile}\sim\frac{m_{35}\,m_{45}}{M'}, $$

which is the seesaw shape again, with the exotic state playing the heavy role.

The One Number That Distinguishes the Chains

The $E_6$ chain through $SU(5)$ instead of Pati–Salam gives "a mass matrix with the same non-vanishing entries as above except the entry $M_{13}\sim(-1,-1,-2)$ that vanishes". In the present chain "$M_{13}\neq0$". The paper then says what it cannot do with that: it has not found "a physical process that would distinguish between the two different symmetry breaking patterns", though it notes that "the same single non-vanishing mixing parameter that differentiates between the two symmetry breaking patterns also appears in the mass and mixing matrices of the colour triplet quarks and the charged leptons."

The Wider Argument

The closing section steps back. Counting degrees of freedom: the Standard Model has $28$ bosonic and $15$ fermionic per generation; $SO(10)$'s $\mathbf{16}$ needs one heavy $\nu_R$ to fill, while $E_6$'s $\mathbf{27}$ "requires further the existence of heavy exotic fermions that amounts to $11$ extra fermionic degrees of freedom for each generation". In the bosonic sector the adjoint $\mathbf{45}$ of $SO(10)$ "asks for $18$ extra intermediate vector bosons" and the adjoint $\mathbf{78}$ of $E_6$ "requires $51$". So $E_6$ is expensive, and the paper argues that the economy of $SO(10)$ "does not provide sufficient reason on its own to justify not to go beyond $SO(10)$".

The support it offers for going further is the exceptional tower,

$$ E_8\to E_7\to E_6\to E_5\simeq SO(10)\to E_4\simeq SU(5)\to SU(4)\to SU(3), $$

with the observation that heterotic string theory "should contain either one of $SO(32)$ or $E_8\times E_8$ gauge symmetry groups for anomaly cancellation", so that identifying the top of the tower with a heterotic $E_8$ and the bottom with colour "it is natural not to stop at an $SO(10)$ GUT". Two further points are raised as unfinished business in unification generally: "to find room for three and only three generations of fermions" and "to verify colour confinement", with the remark that "the structure of all exceptional Lie algebras including $E_6$ depends on the non-associative properties of octonions" and that "the 26 dimensional exceptional Jordan algebra of Hermitian $3\times3$ matrices over complex octonions plays a unique role".

What the Paper Concedes

Three concessions are stated plainly and they bound the result. The seesaw "is not predicted by the $E_6$ models" and is "implement[ed] by hand ... in order to remain consistent with current neutrino observations". Generation mixing is "not dealt with". And "since we do not have sufficient clue concerning the magnitude of the exotic and/or sterile neutrino masses and their mixings, no numerical estimates would be possible" — so the bounds on mixing are structural, not numerical.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. The breaking chain leaves a fingerprint in a mass-matrix entry

The idea. $M_{13}\neq0$ if and only if the chain passed through Pati–Salam rather than through $SU(5)$. The group-theory path is not a bookkeeping choice; it is a number in a $5\times5$ matrix, and the same number recurs in the colour-triplet and charged-lepton matrices.

What it would add. A category of evidence the corpus does not have. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md argues that the group is right because of what it explains — hypercharge, charge quantisation, the anomaly identities, $\nu_R$, custodial symmetry. That is a list of virtues. This paper supplies something different: a way in which the intermediate stage becomes measurable in principle, through an entry that is zero for one chain and nonzero for another.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article, in the section that argues for the group, as a paragraph on what would distinguish it from a chain that skips it.

What stands in the way. The paper itself could not name an observable. The corpus should import the structure and be explicit that the fingerprint has no known measurement attached: a nonzero $M_{13}$ shifts neutral-lepton mixings at second order in perturbation theory, so the effect is buried under parameters nobody knows. Importing it as "a distinguishable prediction" would be false; importing it as "the chain is not inert" is true and is the honest version.

2. The $\mathbf{27}$ gives five neutral fermions, and that is the corpus's doubling theme in a different key

The idea. One $\mathbf{27}$ per generation contains $\nu_e$, $N^c_e$, an exotic Dirac pair $(\nu_E,N^c_E)$ and a singlet $\nu_S$. The Standard Model uses one of the five; $E_6$ requires the other four.

What it would add. A concrete, counted instance of surplus. The corpus has a running theme — that every biquaternion reformulation doubles something, and that the surplus has to be given a meaning. This paper shows a unified model in which the surplus is not a defect of a formulation but a prediction of a group: eleven extra fermionic degrees of freedom per generation, counted in the paper's own closing section. The corpus's articles_physics/the-number-of-generations-and-the-biquaternion-algebra.md asks why three; this asks what one generation is when the group is $E_6$.

Where it lands. articles_physics/grand-unification-in-the-biquaternion-framework-where-the-embedding-fails.md, which already computes the cost of fitting the $\mathbf{27}$ into $M_n(\mathbb B)$ — $\mathbb C^{28}$, $n=14$, plus "a spurious singlet" — and can now contrast that arithmetic cost with the physical content the same $\mathbf{27}$ carries.

What stands in the way. The corpus's frame has one neutrino per generation in its seesaw article. Four is a different world, and the corpus would have to say that its own counting is the reduced one.

3. The seesaw is a shape, not a mechanism

The idea. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\m&M\end{pmatrix}$ gives eigenvalues $\simeq M$ and $\simeq m^2/M$ regardless of what the states are. Here the same shape appears twice: once for the active neutrinos with $M$ the seesaw scale, and once for the sterile neutrino with $M'$ the exotic Dirac mass and the role of $m$ played by a product of two mixing parameters, $m_{35}m_{45}/M'$.

What it would add. A statement the corpus's seesaw article makes only in one instance. That article presents the light mass as arising from a heavy right-handed neutrino and a Yukawa coupling; the inversion $m_\nu\sim m_D^2/M_R$ is derived there for that case. This paper shows the identical formula with a completely different cast — a sterile state, an exotic Dirac fermion, and mixings instead of a Yukawa — which makes the formula a property of the block structure, not of the particles.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-seesaw-mechanism-and-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md, near the derivation of the light eigenvalue, as a remark that the result follows from the block form and therefore recurs wherever that block form appears.

What stands in the way. Nothing. It is a small clarification, and it strengthens the corpus's existing derivation by separating what is structural from what is model-specific.

4. The rank bookkeeping: Pati–Salam costs no rank

The idea. $E_6$ has rank $6$, $SO(10)$ has rank $5$, Pati–Salam has rank $5$, the Standard Model has rank $4$. The step to Pati–Salam does not drop rank; the $U(1)_t$ leaves at the top and hypercharge's $U(1)$ leaves at the bottom.

What it would add. A correction of emphasis in the corpus's rank argument. articles_physics/grand-unification-in-the-biquaternion-framework-where-the-embedding-fails.md uses rank as its main filter — the unified group must have rank $\ge4$ and dimension $\ge12$ — and notes that Pati–Salam has rank $5$ and dimension $21$, above the framework's rank $2$. What it does not note is that Pati–Salam is the step in the exceptional tower that is rank-neutral, so a chain can pass through it without spending anything in the rank budget. That is a reason the group is a natural waypoint rather than a detour.

Where it lands. The same article, in the paragraph that computes Pati–Salam's rank and dimension.

What stands in the way. Nothing; it is one sentence.

5. The exceptional tower as a ladder, with a string-theory top

The idea. $E_8\to E_7\to E_6\to SO(10)\to SU(5)\to SU(4)\to SU(3)$ — the Coxeter–Dynkin chain of the exceptional series — with the top identified with a heterotic $E_8$ and the bottom with colour, as the argument for not stopping at $SO(10)$.

What it would add. A third axis to the corpus's octonion material. The corpus has articles on octonions and a "squeezing beyond the biquaternion algebra" piece; it does not have the tower, which is the reason $E_6$ and not just $SO(10)$ is worth considering. The paper also supplies the caveat that the tower's structure "depends on the non-associative properties of octonions" and that the exceptional Jordan algebra of Hermitian $3\times3$ matrices over the octonions "plays a unique role" — which is where a biquaternion framework would have to go if it went past $SO(10)$.

Where it lands. articles_physics/squeezing-beyond-the-biquaternion-algebra.md, as the ladder the squeezing would have to climb.

What stands in the way. The corpus's articles treat the algebra realistically rather than exceptionally, and the tower is quoted here without derivation. Import it as a map, not as evidence.

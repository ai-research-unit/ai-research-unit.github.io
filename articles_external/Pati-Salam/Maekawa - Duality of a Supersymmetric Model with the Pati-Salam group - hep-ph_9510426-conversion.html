What the Paper Does

It applies Seiberg duality to a Pati–Salam model, and uses the gauge group to fix a missing Yukawa sector.

"Recently one of the authors proposed a dual theory of a Supersymmetric Standard Model (SSM), in which it is naturally understood that at least one quark (the top quark) should be heavy, i.e., almost the same order as the weak scale, and the supersymmetric Higgs mass parameter µ can naturally be expected to be small. However, the model cannot have Yukawa couplings of the lepton sector."

"In this paper, we examine a dual theory of a Supersymmetric Model with the Pati-Salam gauge group SU(4)P S × SU(2)L × SU(2)R with respect to the gauge group SU(4)P S . In this scenario, Yukawa couplings of the lepton sector can be induced. In this model the Pati-Salam breaking scale should be around the SUSY breaking scale. ∗ † E-mail address: maekawa@gauge."

So the paper's logic is: the SSM dual theory works for quarks but not leptons; adding Pati–Salam supplies the lepton Yukawas; therefore the breaking scale is tied to SUSY breaking.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Setting: Seiberg Duality

"it has become clear that certain aspects of four dimensional supersymmetric field theories can be analyzed exactly [1, 2, 3, 4]. By" "One of the most interesting aspects is “duality” [1, 3]. By" "By using “duality”, we can infer the low energy effective theory of a strong coupling gauge theory. One of the authors suggested that nature may use this “duality”."

Seiberg duality relates an $SU(N)$ gauge theory with $N_f$ flavours to an $SU(N_f-N)$ theory with the same flavour content. In the strongly coupled regime the dual description is weakly coupled, so you can compute.

**The idea of the "dual SSM"

**: take the MSSM and dualise its colour group, so that the strongly coupled top quark becomes calculable. The dual description then explains why the top is heavy — it is a dynamical effect in the magnetic theory, not a small parameter. That is a genuinely different strategy from everything else in this batch: most papers impose a symmetry or a texture to make the top heavy; this one uses strong-coupling dynamics.

The Problem and the Pati–Salam Fix

"the model cannot have Yukawa couplings of the lepton sector."

The reason: in the dual SSM, the lepton Yukawas require a field that the dual spectrum does not provide. The magnetic theory's matter content is fixed by the duality, so the lepton Yukawas are simply absent. The fix:"examine a dual theory of a Supersymmetric Model with the Pati-Salam gauge group SU(4)P" "with respect to the gauge group SU(4)P S ."

So the Pati–Salam group is dualised instead of just the colour group, and since $SU(4)_{PS}$ contains both colour and lepton number, the dualised spectrum now includes the fields needed for lepton Yukawas.

That is a clever use of Pati–Salam's defining feature: because quarks and leptons are in the same multiplets, a duality acting on $SU(4)$ acts on both at once, and the lepton Yukawas are generated by the same mechanism that makes the top heavy.

The corpus should record this, because it is the only paper in the batch where Pati–Salam's quark–lepton unification is used as a technical tool for a dynamical mechanism rather than as a mass relation.

The Scale Consequence

"

the Pati–Salam breaking scale should be around the SUSY breaking scale."

Why: the duality is a statement about the gauge theory at strong coupling, and the Pati–Salam group is dualised as a whole. If the breaking scale were much higher than the SUSY scale, the duality would act on the unbroken $SU(4)$, and the effective theory below would be the MSSM — losing the mechanism. If it were much lower, the dual description would not apply to the observed leptons.

So the scale is tied to SUSY breaking, which is $\sim$ TeV. That makes this the batch's most predictive scale statement: not fitted, but fixed by the mechanism's consistency.

And it is another entry in the corpus's table of scales, at the low end — and with the strongest justification of any, since it comes from a dynamical requirement rather than a fit.

Why This Paper Is Alone

No other paper in the batch uses Seiberg duality, and the corpus should note why.

The approach requires the theory to be strongly coupled at the relevant scale and to have a known dual, which restricts it to SQCD-like models with specific matter content. Pati–Salam is $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$, not SQCD, so dualising with respect to $SU(4)_{PS}$ means treating the $SU(4)$ as the strong group and the rest as spectators. That works only if $SU(4)$ is the strongly coupled factor, which is an assumption.

So the paper is a proof of principle, not a complete model, and it should be read as such.

The Date

The arXiv version is from 1997, two years after the original — Seiberg duality was announced in 1994, so this is an early application to phenomenology. That context matters for the corpus: the mid-1990s saw a wave of attempts to use duality for model building, and this is Pati–Salam's entry in that wave.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's odd one out, and its value is as a boundary marker.

Everything else in the batch builds Pati–Salam from a symmetry principle — a family group, a modular group, a discrete $R$-symmetry, a brane configuration — and fits the data with the resulting texture. This paper builds it from a dynamical principle and gets the texture as a strong-coupling effect.

The contrast is instructive: the symmetry route gives many models with many parameters; the dynamical route gives one model with few, but the model is not under control. The corpus should present both, because a reader of the symmetry papers alone would think model building is the only method.

The second point: the scale argument here is the most honest in the batch. Most papers compute or fit $M_{PS}$; this one says where it must be for the mechanism to work. That is what a prediction looks like, and even a rough one is worth more than a fitted number.

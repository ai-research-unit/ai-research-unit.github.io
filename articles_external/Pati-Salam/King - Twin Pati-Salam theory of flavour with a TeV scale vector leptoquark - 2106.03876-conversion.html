What the Paper Does

It builds a model with two copies of the Pati–Salam group — hence "twin" — one of which is broken at a TeV and supplies the vector leptoquark $U_1^\mu(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$, while the other sits at $10^{15}$ GeV and generates the flavour structure.

"At high energies, the theory involves two copies of the PS gauge group, $G_{422}$, with the usual three chiral fermion families transforming under $G_{422}^{II}$. A fourth vector-like family, which mediates the second and third family masses, transforms under $G_{422}^{I}$."

$$ G_{422}^{I}\times G_{422}^{II}\ \xrightarrow{M_{\rm high}}\ G_{4321}\ \xrightarrow{M_{\rm low}}\ G_{321}, \qquad G_{4321}\equiv SU(4)\times SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_{Y'} . $$

The $G_{4321}$ group is the technical invention and it is worth understanding. It is $SU(4)\times SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)$ — that is, Pati–Salam's $SU(4)$ kept, with $SU(3)_c$ appearing separately as a second colour group. That looks wrong until you see how it arises: the paper attributes it to the idea "that colour could appear as a diagonal subgroup of a larger $SU(3+N)\times SU(3)'$ local symmetry at high energies. The particular choice $N=1$ leads to the so-called '4321' gauge symmetry."

So the two colour groups are not a mistake; they are the point. $SU(4)$ contains an $SU(3)$ inside it, and $G_{4321}$ has an independent $SU(3)_c$; the physical QCD group is their diagonal subgroup. The vector leptoquark $U_1$ is the off-diagonal gauge boson between them, and its mass is set by the $SU(4)\times SU(3)'\to SU(3)_{\rm diag}$ breaking.

This is why $U_1$ appears naturally and why it is a vector leptoquark. A vector leptoquark is otherwise hard to get from a simple GUT, as Heeck–Teresi note in this batch; here it is a gauge boson, so it is automatic. The price is a second colour group, whose gauge bosons (the colour octet $g'$) are a new phenomenological problem.

The Breaking Scales

"the vector leptoquark $U_1^\mu(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$ from the $SU(4)^I_{PS}$, broken at $M_{\rm low}\sim1$ TeV, while the origin of quark and lepton masses depends on the full theory, including the high scale PS symmetry, broken at $M_{\rm high}\gtrsim1$ PeV, the latter limit being due to the non-observation of $K_L\to\mu e$."

Two scales, two jobs, and the constraint on the upper one is a flavour bound. $K_L\to\mu e$ is the classic bound on a leptoquark that couples to the first generation, and it forces $M_{\rm high}$ above a PeV. So the high-scale Pati–Salam copy is bounded by a meson decay, which is a nice illustration that flavour physics constrains unification scales directly.

The paper notes $M_{\rm high}$ "we later find to be near the conventional scale of Grand Unified Theories".

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Mechanism: Vector-Like Fermions Mediating Masses

"Quark and lepton masses are mediated by vector-like fermions, with personal Higgs doublets for the second and third families, which may be replaced by a two Higgs doublet model (2HDM)."

"The first family fermion masses are mediated by a fifth family of VL fermions which transform under $SU(4)^{II}_{PS}$, and neutrino masses are further suppressed by the type I seesaw mechanism."

That is the "twin" mechanism and it is worth stating precisely. In a normal Pati–Salam model the Yukawa couplings are free parameters — you write $y\,\Psi_L\Phi\Psi_R$ and fit $y$. Here the Yukawa couplings are not there at all; the masses come from mixing between the chiral families and vector-like families, and the mixing is what the high-scale symmetry fixes.

So the fermion mass hierarchy is generated by a see-saw-like structure rather than by tuning Yukawas. That is what makes the model a "theory of flavour" rather than a model with flavour parameters, and it is why the leptoquark couplings are related to the masses — both come from the same mixing.

"Personal Higgs doublets" is the other invention. Each of the second and third families gets its own Higgs doublet, so the mass matrices are built family by family. "the origin and nature of these fields is very different from the 'private' Higgs doublets envisaged in" earlier work, and "in an Appendix we show how the model can be recast as a conventional type II two Higgs doublet model (2HDM)."

So the construction is equivalent to a 2HDM, and the "personal" language is bookkeeping. Worth recording so a reader does not think a new Higgs sector is being postulated.

The $\mathbb Z_6$

"In order to achieve the texture zero in the first element of the mass matrices and hierarchical right-handed neutrino masses we shall assume a $\mathbb Z_6$ family symmetry, although we shall not use it to explain the charged fermion mass hierarchies. Apart from the $\mathbb Z_6$, no additional symmetries are introduced."

The $\mathbb Z_6$ does one job only: kill the (1,1) entry. It is not doing the hierarchy — the vector-like mixing does that. So the model is economical in symmetries and rich in field content, the opposite of the usual trade-off.

The Prediction

"predicts a dominant coupling of $U_1^\mu(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$ to the third family left-handed doublets."

That is the sharp statement and it is what makes the model testable. The $U_1$ couples mainly to $q_{3L}$ and $\ell_{3L}$ — third family, left-handed. Consequences: - $R(K^{(*)})$ needs a $b\to s\mu\mu$ coupling, so the $\mu$ coupling must be present but not dominant → a mild tension. - $R(D^{(*)})$ needs $b\to c\tau\nu$, which the third-family coupling gives directly. - $B\to K^{(*)}\nu\nu$ is also predicted, since the $\nu_\tau$ coupling follows. - Nothing couples to the first family strongly, which is why $K_L\to\mu e$ is survivable.

"as most preferred by current data" is how the later proceeding (2305.19356) phrases it: the data prefer dominantly left-handed couplings, and the model gives them for structural reasons.

The Honest Negative Result

"However the predicted mass matrices, assuming natural values of the parameters, are not consistent with the single vector leptoquark solution to the $R(D^{(*)})$ anomaly, given its current value."

That sentence is in the abstract, and it is the paper's most valuable content. The model does what it set out to do — relate the leptoquark couplings to the flavour structure — and the relation it produces is not the one the anomaly needs.

So this is a theory of flavour that is falsified by $R(D^{(*)})$ at its natural parameter values. That is a much stronger result than a fit, and the corpus should record it as such. It tells you that the twin-PS mechanism, at least in this simplest form, does not explain the anomaly.

What survives is the structure, not the explanation. The later papers in the series (2209.00276) revise the field content — three vector-like families instead of one — and find a narrow window where $R(D^{(*)})$ works at $1\sigma$.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. $G_{4321}$: keep $SU(4)$, add a second $SU(3)$, make QCD the diagonal

The idea. $SU(4)\times SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_{Y'}$, with QCD the diagonal subgroup of $SU(4)$'s internal $SU(3)$ and the separate $SU(3)_c$. $U_1$ is the off-diagonal gauge boson.

What it would add. A construction the corpus does not have, and it solves a real problem: vector leptoquarks are hard to get from unification, and here one appears as a gauge boson. The price is a second colour group and a colour-octet vector $g'$. The corpus's Pati–Salam article presents $SU(4)$'s leptoquarks as the $\mathbf{3}$ of a scalar $\mathbf{10}$; this shows a second, gauge-boson route.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's gauge-sector section.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The group theory is standard; the diagonal-subgroup construction is the standard way to get a light vector leptoquark.

2. Masses from vector-like mixing, not from Yukawa couplings

The idea. Chiral families mix with vector-like families charged under the other Pati–Salam copy, and the effective Yukawa couplings are products of mixings and vector-like masses.

What it would add. A mechanism that turns "theory of flavour" into something with content. The corpus's flavour discussion treats Yukawa couplings as inputs. Here they are outputs — derived from a symmetric high-scale theory — and that is what allows the leptoquark couplings to be predicted.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's Yukawa section, and the flavour articles.

What stands in the way. The mechanism needs a specific field content (one vector-like family for the second and third, a fifth for the first) and a $\mathbb Z_6$; it is not a general theorem.

3. A second Pati–Salam copy at $10^{15}$ GeV is bounded by $K_L\to\mu e$

The idea. The first family's leptoquark coupling is bounded by the kaon decay, forcing $M_{\rm high}\gtrsim1$ PeV.

What it would add. A flavour bound on a unification scale, at a level ($10^{15}$ GeV) close to the GUT scale. The corpus separates flavour physics from unification; here a meson decay sets a unification scale, which is worth recording as an example of the two sectors meeting.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's experimental section.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the bound is standard.

4. The model is falsified by $R(D^{(*)})$ at natural parameters, and says so

The idea. The predicted mass matrices and the $U_1$ couplings are related; the relation does not fit the measured $R(D^{(*)})$.

What it would add. An example of a predictive theory being wrong, stated in the abstract. The corpus should prefer such results to fits, because a wrong prediction with a reason is more informative than a right one without. And it explains why the series needed revision.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's $B$-anomaly section, as the first step of a sequence.

What stands in the way. The anomaly itself has weakened since 2021; the falsification is against a value that has moved.

5. "Personal Higgs" is just a 2HDM in disguise

The idea. The family-specific Higgs doublets can be recast as a type-II two-Higgs-doublet model.

What it would add. A caution about notation. The construction looks like it postulates many Higgs fields; it is equivalent to two, with the "personal" assignment being a way of organising the mass matrix. A reader who takes the language literally will overestimate the field content.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scalar-sector section, as a footnote on how to read such constructions.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the equivalence is shown in the paper's appendix.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and Heeck–Teresi (1905.05211) approach the same anomalies from opposite directions and the contrast is the most useful thing in either.

Heeck–Teresi start from Pati–Salam's scalar sector and observe that the leptoquarks are already there. The leptoquarks are scalars, they come from the $\mathbf{10}$s, and their couplings are tied to neutrino masses by the $SU(4)$ invariant.

King starts from the leptoquark the data prefer — the vector $U_1$ — and asks what gauge structure produces it. The answer is $G_{4321}$, which requires two Pati–Salam copies.

So one route is scalar and forced, the other vector and constructed. The data, at the time, preferred the vector; the group, naturally, gives the scalar. That tension is the shape of the whole subliterature, and the corpus should present it as such rather than as two independent models.

The second thing: King's negative result in the abstract — the natural parameter values do not fit $R(D^{(*)})$ — is a reminder that these constructions are constrained enough to fail. A "theory of flavour" that predicts its leptoquark couplings is falsifiable in a way that a model with free Yukawas is not, and this one was falsified, in part.

What the Paper Does

It uses discrete torsion to get rigid cycles, and rigid cycles fix the moduli problem.

"We construct, for the first time, a three-family N = 1 supersymmetric Pati-Salam model from rigid intersecting D6-branes on a factorizable T6 /(Z2 × Z′2 ) orientifold with discrete torsion. The factorizable geometry"

"The factorizable geometry allows for explicit control over rigid cycles and moduli stabilization. So this seven-page letter claims a complete chain: PS → SM by Higgs, masses and mixings, and SUSY breaking by condensation. That is the most complete single model in the cluster, and the key is rigid cycles with discrete torsion.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why "Rigid" Matters

A cycle is "rigid" if it has no continuous deformations — the D-brane wrapping it cannot move. A non-rigid cycle gives the brane a position modulus, and that modulus is a massless scalar in the four-dimensional theory. And massless adjoints are fatal: a massless adjoint chiral multiplet contributes to the beta function and destroys asymptotic freedom, so the hidden sector never becomes strongly coupled and gaugino condensation cannot happen. So rigid cycles are the prerequisite for SUSY breaking by condensation, and the corpus should record the chain: rigid cycle → no position modulus → no massless adjoint → negative beta function → condensation → SUSY breaking. That chain is the paper's contribution and it is the batch's cleanest cosmological argument in the D-brane cluster.

Why "Discrete Torsion" Is Needed

**On a plain

$T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2)$ orientifold, all the cycles are non-rigid** — the torus is a product of circles and every cycle can slide. Discrete torsion changes the orbifold's structure: it introduces twisted sectors with fixed points, and the cycles that pass through the fixed points become rigid. The cost: discrete torsion is a choice (a discrete B-field flux), and not every model admits it. And it modifies the spectrum: twisted-sector matter appears, and the tadpole conditions change. The corpus should record the cost. The batch's other D-brane papers use the plain orientifold; this one needs the torsion. So the rigid-cycle models are a different branch, not a refinement of the 33.

The Complete Chain

Three steps, and each is claimed: 1. **"break the Pati-Salam gauge symmetry down to the Standard Model (SM) gauge symmetry via the supersymmetry preserving Higgs mechanism"

** — the

$\mathbf4$-Higgs VEV with $SU(2)_R$ doublet structure, preserving SUSY. 2. **"generate the SM fermion masses and mixings"

** — through the three- and four-point worldsheet couplings, as in 2202.07048. 3. **"break the supersymmetry via gaugino condensations in the hidden sector"

** — the rigid cycles make this possible. That is the whole programme in one sentence, and it is why the paper is short: it is a proof of existence for each step, not a detailed fit. The corpus should present it as such — the batch's most complete D-brane Pati–Salam model, with the least quantitative detail.

The Factorizable Geometry

"

The factorizable geometry allows for explicit control over rigid cycles and moduli stabilization"

"Factorizable" means $T^6=T^2\times T^2\times T^2$ with each torus a product of circles. That makes the cycle structure computable by hand, which is why the discrete-torsion construction can be done at all. The corpus should note that the cluster's computability rests on factorizability — a general Calabi–Yau would be intractable. So the results are for a special class of compactifications, and that is a limitation the corpus should state.

What Is Not Claimed

No soft terms, no quantitative fermion fit, no dark matter, no proton-decay estimate. Seven pages cannot contain them. The companion 2505.03664 (43 pages) presents the full class and adds the consistency checks.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper marks the cluster's turn from taxonomy to completeness, and it does so by changing the background.

hep-th/0403061 → 2112.09630: the plain orientifold landscape, fully enumerated, 33 classes, one unification. Here → 2505.03664: a different background (discrete torsion, rigid cycles), a different branch, and a model that claims the full chain.

The move is significant: rather than search harder in the known landscape, change the compactification so that the missing ingredient (rigidity) exists. That is what a mature model-building programme does when a census comes up short.

The corpus should present the cluster in two acts — the census of the plain orientifold, and the rigid-cycle branch — because the second is not a subset of the first.

The second point: the causal chain rigid → no adjoint → asymptotic freedom → condensation is the batch's best example of a stringy constraint doing real work. It cannot be stated in field theory, because in field theory you simply do not include the adjoint. The corpus should say so: this is a place where the string construction constrains the model in a way the field theory cannot.

What the Paper Does

It takes the twin Pati–Salam model of 2106.03876, which failed to fit $R(D^{(*)})$, and rebuilds it until it works — then checks everything else.

"We propose and study a simplified model with three vector-like fermion families, in the massless first family approximation, and show that the second and third family charged fermion masses and mixings and the B-anomalies can be simultaneously explained and related."

The repair, in the authors' words:

"Firstly, we presented a simplified version of the model that is able to explain second and third family charged fermion masses and mixings via effective Yukawa couplings, which arise naturally from mixing effects with a fourth vector-like family of fermions. However, with only a single vectorlike family the model is unable to explain the B-anomalies in a natural way, as it does not achieve the flavour structure required by 4321 models, and hence is over constrained by flavour-violating processes such as Bs − B̄s meson mixing."

"We then extended the simplified model to include three vector-like families, together with a Z4 discrete symmetry to control the flavour structure. This version of the model allows for larger flavour-violating and dominantly left-handed U1 couplings as required to address RD(∗) , thanks to mixing between a fourth and fifth vector-like families which also mix with the second and third generations of SM fermions. A sixth vector-like family is included to mix with the first SM generation, for the sake of suppressing any FCNCs involving first and second generation fermions. The mechanism"

Six vector-like families in total: 4th and 5th for the second/third generations, 6th for the first. That is a large field content, and it is the price of getting the flavour structure right.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The GIM-Like Mechanism

"The mechanism resembles the GIM suppression of FCNCs in the SM, featuring a similar Cabbibo-like matrix which is present in leptoquark currents, but not in neutral currents mediated by the coloron and $Z'$."

That distinction is the important one and it is subtle.

In the SM, the GIM mechanism works because the charged current is not flavour-diagonal (the CKM matrix) but the neutral current is (the $Z$ couples diagonally). Flavour-changing neutral currents are suppressed because there is no tree-level coupling.

Here the same structure is reproduced for the new gauge bosons. The $U_1$ leptoquark current carries a CKM-like matrix — it changes flavour, which is what you need for $b\to s\mu\mu$. The colour octet $g'$ and the $Z'$ have flavour-diagonal couplings in the gauge basis, and their off-diagonal pieces after rotation are suppressed by the same small mixings.

So the model's viability rests on making the leptoquark flavour-changing and the colouron flavour-conserving, which is exactly what the SM does with $W$ and $Z$. That is a genuine structural achievement and it is why the earlier one-family version failed: with one vector-like family, the rotation that generates the leptoquark couplings also generates colouron FCNCs, and $B_s-\bar B_s$ mixing kills it.

Why the Leptoquark Must Be Vector

The vector $U_1(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$ is the one leptoquark that can explain both anomalies at once, and the scalar alternatives cannot. This paper's contribution is to show that a gauge origin for $U_1$ — the $G_{4321}$ structure — produces the required coupling pattern without tuning.

And the coupling pattern required is specific: "dominantly left-handed" couplings to third-family doublets. That is what the data prefer, and it is what the model gives for structural reasons.

The Post-2022 Situation

"we have included an extended analysis considering the new 2022 LHCb data on RK (∗) , which has slightly shifted the preferred parameter space with respect to the 2021 case. The model can still explain the RD(∗) anomalies at 1σ in a narrow window, however we expect small deviations from the SM on the RK (∗) ratios, to be tested in the future via more precise measurements by the LHCb collaboration. We also"

Read that carefully: the model now predicts that $R_{K^{(*)}}$ is almost the Standard Model value. The 2022 LHCb data moved $R_{K^{(*)}}$ towards the SM, so the model survives only if the remaining deviation is small. So the model is being squeezed into a corner where its own prediction is that the anomaly it was built to explain is nearly absent.

That is a familiar trajectory and worth recording: a model that fits an anomaly tightly becomes falsifiable as the anomaly shrinks, and this one has already retreated.

We also predict $\Delta R_D=\Delta R_{D^*}$ — a relation between the two $R(D^{(*)})$ deviations that follows from the coupling structure.

The Flavour Structure Required

"featuring now a down-aligned flavour structure in the 2-3 sector (requiring a mild tuning as described in the main text) that protects from the dangerous tree-level contributions to Bs − B̄s meson mixing. Non-zero"

"Down-aligned means the rotation matrices in the down-quark and charged-lepton sectors are aligned with the mass basis, so the dangerous mixing is small. And the paper says plainly that this needs a mild tuning."

So the model is not tuning-free. It is a theory of flavour with one tuning, and the corpus should record the tuning rather than the claim.

Remarkably, the model can be easily disentangled from all other proposals via the previous set of observables."

$B\to K^{(*)}\nu\bar\nu$ is the decisive channel. Any $U_1$ that produces $b\to c\tau\nu$ also produces $b\to s\nu\nu$ through the same coupling, so the rate is predicted, and Belle II will reach the predicted range. That is a clean falsification channel and it is the same observable that Heeck–Teresi (1905.05211) identify as the obstacle for scalar explanations.

The Vector-Like Fermion Masses

"The study of the 1-loop contribution of vector leptoquark U1 exchange to Bs − B̄s mixing revealed that the fifth vector-like lepton has to be light, around 1-2 TeV, to be compatible with the stringent bound from ∆Ms . This is easily"

So $B_s$ mixing holds one of the new fermions down at the LHC's reach. That is a prediction with a target: a vector-like lepton at 1–2 TeV, which the LHC can find.

"In particular, the fourth and fifth charged leptons are suggested as good candidates to explain the CMS excess."

The Collider Picture

"The more stringent bound over the scale of the model arises from the ditop searches in [20, 67], which push the mass of the coloron to lie above 3.5 TeV, however those bounds could be slightly overestimated for our model as they only consider non-fermiophobic 4321 scenarios."

The colour octet is the model's liability. It is a necessary consequence of having two colour groups, and it is bounded at 3.5 TeV by di-top searches. That bound is what pushes the whole 4321 scale up, and it is the main constraint the construction cannot avoid.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

  1. GIM for new gauge bosons: flavour-changing leptoquark, flavour-diagonal colouron

The idea. The $U_1$ current carries a CKM-like matrix; the colouron and $Z'$ couple diagonally in the gauge basis. The same geometry as $W$ versus $Z$ in the SM.

What it would add. A precise statement of how a model with a TeV-scale colour octet survives. Naively an octet with flavour-violating couplings is excluded by $B_s$ mixing; the model arranges the rotation so the octet stays diagonal, and that is not automatic — it required three vector-like families and a $\mathbb Z_4$.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's gauge-sector section, and any corpus discussion of FCNC suppression beyond the SM.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The mechanism is stated and the failure of the one-family version is the proof that it is nontrivial.

  1. A model can survive an anomaly only by predicting it is nearly absent

The idea. After the 2022 LHCb update, the model needs $R_{K^{(*)}}$ close to the SM value, so its own prediction is a small deviation.

What it would add. A record of how the 2021 → 2023 trajectory went, and a general lesson: a model built to fit an anomaly tightens around it and dies when the anomaly shrinks. The corpus should note the dates, because a reader who takes the 2021 fits at face value will overestimate the model's status.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's $B$-anomaly section, with dates.

What stands in the way. Nothing; it is the paper's own summary.

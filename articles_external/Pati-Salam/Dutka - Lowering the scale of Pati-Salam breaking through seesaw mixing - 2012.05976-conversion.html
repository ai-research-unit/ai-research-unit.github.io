What the Paper Does

It answers one question quantitatively: how low can the Pati–Salam breaking scale go, given the meson-decay limits on the vector leptoquark $X_\mu$?

The baseline answer is bad. "This leptoquark ordinarily couples to both left- and right-handed SM fermions and therefore the meson decays do not experience a helicity suppression. We find that the current limits vary from $\mathcal O(80-2500)$ TeV depending on the choice of matrix structure appearing in the relevant three-generational charged-current interactions."

The paper's contribution is the escape. Add fermions that mix with the charged leptons or the down quarks, and the mixing turns a vector coupling into an effectively chiral one. A chiral coupling has a helicity suppression — the amplitude goes as $m_\ell/m_M$ — and the limit collapses:

"We find that the Pati-Salam limits can be as low as $\mathcal O(5-150)$ TeV with the addition of these four multiplets."

Four multiplets do it: "an $SU(2)_{L/R}$ bidoublet, a pair of $SU(4)$ decuplets and either an $SU(2)_L$ or $SU(2)_R$ triplet all of which contain heavy exotic versions of the SM charged leptons."

And a bonus: "We also identify an interesting possible connection between the smallness of the neutrino masses and a helicity suppression of the Pati-Salam limits for three of the four multiplets."

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Setup

$$ G_{\rm PS}=SU(4)_c\otimes SU(2)_L\otimes SU(2)_R, \qquad f_L\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1),\quad f_R\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2), $$

with the embeddings laid out as explicit $4\times2$ matrices whose rows are the three colours and the lepton: $\begin{pmatrix}u_r&d_r\\u_b&d_b\\u_g&d_g\\\nu_e&e\end{pmatrix}$. "The breaking of $SU(4)$ to the maximal subgroup $SU(3)\times U(1)_X$ is phenomenologically required and under this breaking the fundamental of $SU(4)$ decomposes as $\mathbf4\to\mathbf1_{-1}\oplus\mathbf3_{1/3}$ indicating that the SM quarks and leptons can be unified by identifying $U(1)_X$ with a gauged $B-L$."

Note the emphasis: "Often a discrete symmetry between the $SU(2)_L$ and $SU(2)_R$ sectors is imposed but is not necessary." The paper works without left–right parity throughout, which is why $K_L^{de}$ and $K_R^{de}$ are independent matrices.

The Eight Mixing Matrices

This is the structural fact that drives everything, and it is worth having in full:

$$ \begin{aligned} V_L^{\rm CKM}&=(U_L^u)^\dagger U_L^d, & V_R^{\rm CKM}&=(U_R^u)^\dagger U_R^d,\\ K_L^{de}&=(U_L^d)^\dagger U_L^e, & K_R^{de}&=(U_R^d)^\dagger U_R^e,\\ U_L^{\rm lept}&=(U_L^\nu)^\dagger U_L^e, & U_R^{\rm lept}&=(U_R^\nu)^\dagger U_R^e,\\ K_L^{u\nu}&=(U_L^u)^\dagger U_L^\nu, & K_R^{u\nu}&=(U_R^u)^\dagger U_R^\nu . \end{aligned} $$

"For the case of $G_{\rm PS}$ in total there are eight different physical mixing matrices (compared to the two of the SM)." The Standard Model has the CKM and the PMNS; Pati–Salam has eight, because $SU(4)$ rotates quarks into leptons in both chiralities and $SU(2)_R$ doubles the count. That multiplication is what gives the model its flavour problem and, here, its opportunity.

The gauge couplings in the mass basis:

$$ \mathcal L_{Xde}=\frac{g_4}{\sqrt2}d_i^0(K_L^{de})_{ij}\not X P_L e_j^0+d_i^0(K_R^{de})_{ij}\not X P_R e_j^0+{\rm h.c.} $$

The Baseline Limit, and Why It Is 2500 TeV

The partial width,

$$ \Gamma_{M_{pq}\to\ell_i^+\ell_j^-}=\frac{m_{M_{pq}}}{16\pi}\lambda(m_{M_{pq}},m_{\ell_i},m_{\ell_j})|M_{pq,ij}|^2, \qquad \lambda=\sqrt{[1-(\mu_{\ell_i}+\mu_{\ell_j})^2][1-(\mu_{\ell_i}-\mu_{\ell_j})^2]}, $$

with $\mu_X=m_X/m_{M_{pq}}$. Since the two final-state leptons are light, both $P_L$ and $P_R$ amplitudes contribute at leading order in $m_\ell$ — no helicity suppression. "Maximising the leptoquark's contribution to $K_L^0\to\mu e$ ... leads to limits on the gauge leptoquark mass of roughly $2500$ TeV."

And the scan over textures gives the range: "$K_L^{de}=K_R^{de}=\mathbf 1_{3\times3}$" maximises $K_L^0\to\mu e$ and hence the bound; other textures give about $100$ TeV; the two remaining ones give about $1900$ TeV. Hence $\mathcal O(80-2500)$ TeV.

A detail the paper is careful about and most analyses are not: "For this scenario neglecting the electron and muon mass, as was done in [29], would suggest that $X_\mu$ does not mediate $K_L^0$ decays. However, we find when included they lead to comparable limits for $m_X$ to the helicity-unsuppressed $B_d^0$ and $B_s^0$ decays. This is due to the larger experimental precision obtained for $K_L^0$ decays and therefore we find that the muon mass cannot be ignored."

So the electron mass matters for the kaon limit even though $m_e/m_K\sim10^{-3}$, because the kaon measurements are that much more precise. That is a real correction to the existing literature and worth recording as method.

The Escape: Making $X_\mu$ Chiral by Mixing

The mechanism. Introduce a heavy exotic lepton $E$ that mixes with $e$ with angle $\theta$. The coupling becomes

$$ \mathcal L_{Xde}\supset\frac{g_4}{\sqrt2}d^0(c_{\theta_L}K_L^{de})\not X P_Le^0+d^0(c_{\theta_R}K_R^{de})\not X P_Re^0+{\rm h.c.} $$

with $c_\theta=\cos\theta$. "As the mixing angles $\theta_L$ and $\theta_R$ can significantly differ, this can effectively lead to a chiral coupling between $X_\mu$ and the fermions $d$ and $e$ causing a suppression in the helicity-unsuppressed contribution of the total meson decay rates."

"in order to significantly helicity-suppress the decays mediated by $X_\mu$, one of the angles $\theta_{L/R}$ is required to be very small e.g. $\theta_R\lesssim10^{-4}$ in the case of $K_L^0\to\mu e$ decays."

That is the quantitative core of the paper, and it is a strong requirement: a mixing angle below $10^{-4}$ in one chirality only. Achieving it is what the rest of the paper is about.

"In general a reduction in the limits on $m_X$ by a factor of $0.05-0.07$ naturally occurs compared to the scenario without additional mass mixing. We find that the limits on $m_X$ can be as low as $5$ TeV potentially allowing for discovery signals of different PS particles comfortably within reach of current and possible future hadron colliders."

The physical picture: "in the case of an exactly chiral theory ($m_E\to\infty$) where for example $c_{\theta_L}=1$ and $c_{\theta_R}=0$ ... the decay rate is suppressed by the charged lepton masses."

So the limit falls from $2500$ TeV to $5$ TeV — a factor of five hundred — and the whole of it comes from turning a vector coupling into an axial one. That is the paper's headline.

The Four Multiplets, Ranked

multiplet sector mixed verdict
$SU(2)_{L/R}$ bidoublet $e$–$E$ "most attractive", full viable spectrum
$SU(2)_{L}$ or $SU(2)_R$ triplet $e$–$E$ viable, needs extra scalars and singlets
$SU(4)$ decuplet (a pair) $e$–$E$ and $d$–$D$ needs singlets, a Georgi–Jarlskog scalar, tuning
$SU(4)$ sextet $d$–$D$ "not viable phenomenologically", gives proton decay

The sextet's failure is specific: "The sextet option leads to mixing in the down-quark sector alone but we find that this likely leads to large proton decay widths in the only region which can generate a viable mass spectrum."

Note that the sextet is the one multiplet whose mixing is purely in the quark sector. The other three all involve charged-lepton mixing, and that is not a coincidence — it is the helicity suppression work: the suppression comes from the lepton mass in the final state, so you need the mixing on the lepton side.

The Neutrino Connection

"We also identify an interesting possible connection between the smallness of the neutrino masses and a helicity suppression of the Pati-Salam limits for three of the four multiplets."

And in the conclusion: "We identify an attractive connection between the smallness of neutrino mass and the helicity suppression of the limits on $m_X$ which, particularly in the case of the bi-doublet, necessarily has a chiral-like coupling of $X_\mu$ to the SM fermions."

The link is a seesaw of the same mass matrix. The exotic lepton $E$ mixes with the charged lepton through a Dirac mass and with a singlet through a Majorana mass; the light neutrino mass is suppressed by the same heavy scale, and the same small mixing angle that makes the neutrino light makes the coupling chiral. One tuning does both jobs, so a small neutrino mass and a low Pati–Salam scale are the same statement. That is the paper's most elegant result and it is stated as a possibility rather than proved.

The $SO(10)$ Section

Each multiplet is placed in a small $SO(10)$ representation, and the placements are clean:

$$ \mathbf{16}\to(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)=f_L\oplus f_R, $$

$$ \mathbf{10}\to(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)=\Psi_{22}\oplus\Psi_6, $$

$$ \mathbf{45}\to(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\mathbf1)\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf3)\oplus(\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)\oplus(\mathbf6,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)\supset\Psi_{3L}\oplus\Psi_{3R}, $$

and "the $SU(4)$ decuplets first appear in the $\mathbf{120}$ representation of $SO(10)$."

Then the obstruction, and it is a good one:

"Some mechanism would therefore be desirable in order to significantly increase the masses of the $D$ states from the sextet, and not the $E$ and $N$ states from the bi-doublet, in order to prevent large proton decay widths. This cannot be through different bare mass terms, e.g. $\mu_{\Psi_{22}}\ll\mu_{\Psi_6}\simeq10^{16}$ GeV, as the $SO(10)$ symmetry now enforces the two terms to be equal."

The $\mathbf{10}$ contains both the safe states and the dangerous states, and $SO(10)$ forces their masses to be equal. So the multiplet that gives you the good mixing also gives you proton decay, and you cannot split them by a mass term. That is a sharp obstruction and it is why the paper ends by proposing a search rather than a model: "An interesting possible direction of future work is to analyse the feasibility of embedding such low-scale variants of PS into an $SO(10)$ GUT in such a way that the required fermion states do not develop GUT scale masses whereas any fermion in a given embedding which may mediate proton decay ... have masses developed at the $SO(10)$ breaking scale."

On $X_\mu$ and the $B$ Anomalies

The paper is honest that its best model and the anomaly explanation are in tension:

"combined explanations of $b\to s\mu\mu$ and $b\to c\tau\nu$ solely from the interactions of $X_\mu$ are highly disfavoured and prefer $m_X\lesssim6$ TeV, which is in tension with the mass limits on $m_X$ derived in section 3."

And the ranking: "PS extended by $SU(2)_{L/R}$ triplets (and additional fermion singlets) appears to be the simplest UV model capable of explaining the $B$-meson anomalies if the gauge leptoquark predicted plays a dominant role."

Also: "the $SU(4)$ sextet appears to be not viable phenomenologically if a helicity suppression in the relevant meson decays is desired, and therefore certainly cannot explain the anomalies in its minimal form."

And the honest note that the scalars are unconstrained: "the scalar content of PS is less constrained and necessarily includes a number of scalar leptoquarks. These scalar leptoquarks alone are able to explain the anomalies."

A Consequence for the Scalar Sector

"A common feature of all scenarios was the existence of scalars $\chi_L$ and $\chi_R$ in order to generate a viable down-quark and charged-lepton mass spectrum. Therefore three $SU(2)_L$ Higgs doublets are predicted."

Three Higgs doublets is a prediction, not an assumption, and it follows from needing separate $\chi_L,\chi_R$ to break the down-isospin degeneracy. That is a falsifiable statement of the kind the corpus should note.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. Eight mixing matrices instead of two

The idea. The SM has the CKM and the PMNS. Pati–Salam has eight matrices, because $SU(4)$ rotates quarks into leptons in both chiralities and $SU(2)_R$ doubles each.

What it would add. A quantitative statement of the flavour problem that Pati–Salam inherits and creates. The corpus's Pati–Salam article treats the model as reducing parameters — one coupling, one multiplet. Here the count goes up: two mixing matrices become eight, and the extra six are not small perturbations, they are the matrices that mediate leptoquark decay. The model buys coupling unification and pays in flavour. That trade is the honest accounting and the corpus does not have it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the flavour section.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The count is elementary: $2$ chiralities $\times$ $3$ pairs (up–down, down–lepton, up–neutrino) plus the two neutrino rotations $=8$ modulo the unphysical phases.

2. A vector coupling becomes chiral through mixing, and that is worth a factor of 500

The idea. Without mixing, $X_\mu$ couples with both $P_L$ and $P_R$ and there is no helicity suppression, so the kaon limit is $2500$ TeV. With a mixing angle $\theta_R\lesssim10^{-4}$ in one chirality only, the $P_R$ amplitude is switched off, the rate scales as $m_\ell^2$, and the limit falls to $5$ TeV.

What it would add. A case where the chirality structure of a coupling, not its magnitude, sets a bound. The corpus talks about coupling strengths and unification scales; here the decisive question is which projector the vertex carries. Two models with identical gauge groups and identical couplings can differ by a factor of five hundred in their experimental limit, purely because of a mixing angle. That is a lesson the corpus can use whenever it compares models by their gauge group alone.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's leptoquark section, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-the-neutral-current-and-the-z-coupling.md — which discusses $V-A$ structure — as the general point.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

3. Small neutrino mass and low Pati–Salam scale are the same tuning

The idea. The seesaw that makes $\nu$ light and the mixing that makes $X_\mu$ chiral both come from the same heavy exotic and the same small angle. Getting one gets the other.

What it would add. A positive correlation between two quantities usually treated as independent. The corpus's seesaw article treats the light neutrino mass as an output to be explained; the Pati–Salam article treats the breaking scale as a parameter to be bounded. Here they are linked: a light neutrino is evidence for the mixing that permits a low breaking scale. That turns two separate puzzles into one, which is the strongest kind of argument available and the corpus does not have this particular link.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-seesaw-mechanism-and-the-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md, as the Pati–Salam case, and the Pati–Salam article's scale discussion.

What stands in the way. The paper says "possible connection" and does not prove it holds in all four multiplets. The bi-doublet case is the strongest; the decuplet needs extra states. State it as a mechanism that works in the best case.

4. An $SO(10)$ representation that forces the safe and dangerous states to be degenerate

The idea. The $\mathbf{10}$ of $SO(10)$ contains both the bidoublet (which does the useful mixing) and the sextet (which gives proton decay). You would like $\mu_{\Psi_{22}}\ll\mu_{\Psi_6}$; "the $SO(10)$ symmetry now enforces the two terms to be equal."

What it would add. A worked example of an embedding forbidding the splitting a model needs. The corpus's Pati–Salam article presents $SO(10)$ embedding as an asset — it justifies the group. Here the embedding is a liability: it removes the freedom that made the low-scale model work. That is the cost side of embedding, and the corpus should present both.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's $SO(10)$ subsection.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The statement is group-theoretic: a single $SO(10)$ multiplet cannot have its components split by a supersymmetric mass term, only by the $SO(10)$-breaking vev.

5. Three Higgs doublets as a prediction

The idea. "A common feature of all scenarios was the existence of scalars $\chi_L$ and $\chi_R$ in order to generate a viable down-quark and charged-lepton mass spectrum. Therefore three $SU(2)_L$ Higgs doublets are predicted."

What it would add. A falsifiable scalar-sector prediction from a flavour requirement. The corpus's Pati–Salam article has one bidoublet, giving two doublets after breaking — the standard two-Higgs-doublet spectrum. Here there are three, because a second pair $\chi_L,\chi_R$ is needed for the mass relations. That is the kind of number experiment can check, and the corpus should record that different Pati–Salam setups give different doublet counts.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scalar sector.

What stands in the way. Nothing, though the argument is that no alternative was found rather than that three is forced in general.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and the Dutka conversion (2211.02054) are by the same author on the same question, and they point opposite ways on the same scale.

2211.02054 says the scale must go up, to $10^8$ GeV, because dimension-5 operators give nucleon decay. This paper says the scale can go down, to $5$ TeV, because mixing gives helicity suppression.

The two are not in conflict and the difference is what is being computed. 2211.02054 computes a decay rate with no helicity suppression and pushes the scale up; this one computes the reverse. The reason both can be right:

  • 2211.02054's bound applies to the scalar leptoquark $\chi_d$ in minimal Pati–Salam with the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ breaking scalar.
  • This paper's bound applies to the vector leptoquark $X_\mu$, and it is lowered by adding exotic fermions.

Different particles, different operator dimensions, different conclusions. The corpus should say this clearly because a reader who takes two papers by the same author on "the Pati–Salam breaking scale" will conclude that the field cannot make up its mind. It can; the two papers are about different leptoquarks.

A second record worth keeping: the corpus's Pati–Salam article presumably quotes "Pati–Salam breaking above about $10^3$ TeV". That is this paper's baseline, before the mixing trick. It is a bound on the vector leptoquark given minimal fermion content, not a property of the group, and quoting it without the qualification overstates the case by two orders of magnitude in one direction and understates the escape by five hundredfold in the other.

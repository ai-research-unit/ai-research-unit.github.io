What the Paper Does

The exotic see-saw of 1510.00243 needs supersymmetry broken above $10^9$ GeV, which puts it in conflict with the bound $m_{\tilde G}<10^6$ TeV on a long-lived gravitino. The paper removes the conflict by making the gravitino the lightest supersymmetric particle: it then has no superpartner to decay into, so the wash-out never happens. What is left is a gravitino of $10^{11}$–$10^{13}$ GeV produced gravitationally by the non-adiabatic expansion during inflation, which is a superheavy dark matter candidate. The dark matter abundance then fixes the gravitino mass, which fixes the supersymmetry scale, which fixes the instanton coupling $e^{-S_{E_2}}$ — a bound on a Calabi–Yau modulus obtained from cosmology. The paper is a letter: seven pages, no new fit, and the numbers are order-of-magnitude.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Problem Stated

The exotic see-saw assumes two things: the non-perturbative instanton contribution dominates the right-handed neutrino mass matrix, $M_R^P\ll M_R^{E_2}$, and supersymmetry and left–right symmetry are broken high, $M_{SUSY},M_{LR}\geq10^9$ GeV. The second assumption is what the paper calls into question. The Khlopov–Linde bound on an unstable gravitino is

$$ m_{\tilde G}<10^6\ \mathrm{TeV}, $$

and a gravitino heavier than its superpartners decays into them, in the thermal bath, after the leptons and antileptons have been made. The decay products re-process the asymmetry: the wash-out destroys the leptogenesis the model had just computed.

The paper notes that the same $10^9$ GeV assumption is what saves the model from the Davidson–Ibarra bound, so the two cannot simply be traded. In an $SO(10)$-inspired Pati–Salam the lightest right-handed neutrino is pushed below $10^9$ GeV and one must assume an accidental degeneracy; here the instantons give the degeneracy dynamically. Dropping the high scale would give back the problem the instantons were introduced to solve.

The Way Out

Make the gravitino the lightest supersymmetric particle. A gravitino with no lighter superpartner has no channel into supersymmetric particles, so the "disastrous washing-out" cannot occur. The paper states the two mass options:

  • $m_{\tilde G}\simeq10^{-9}M_{SUSY}$, the gauge-mediation pattern;
  • $m_{\tilde G}\simeq(10^{-1}\div1)M_{SUSY}$, with $m_{\tilde G}

In either case a stable gravitino may sit above the $10^6$ TeV bound, because the bound applies to the decay, not to the mass.

The paper is careful about what has to be assumed. $m_{\tilde G}$ comes from the Kähler potential and the superpotential, $m=e^{K/2}W/M_{Pl}^2$, and in a Minkowski vacuum vanishes when supersymmetry is unbroken. In an AdS vacuum the $F$-term can vanish with a non-zero potential, so a massive gravitino is possible; and fluxes can lift the mass through a soft term $m_{3/2}^N\sim\langle\tau H+iF\rangle$. The model then assumes the flux contribution does not exceed the supersymmetric scale.

Gravitini from Inflation

The production is gravitational and non-thermal: the expansion of the FRW background

$$ ds^2=a^2(\eta)\left(d\eta^2-d\vec x^2\right) $$

promotes virtual gravitino pairs to real ones, in the same way an electric field promotes $e^+e^-$ pairs. The field obeys the Rarita–Schwinger equation with the spin connection,

$$ \mathcal R_\mu[\psi]=i\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_\nu\psi_\rho+m\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_\nu, \qquad D_\mu\psi_\nu=\partial_\mu\psi_\nu+\tfrac14\omega_\mu^{\ ab}\gamma_{ab}\psi_\nu-\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}\psi_\rho, $$

and in FRW the vielbein and connection take the forms $e^a_{\ \mu}=a(\eta)\delta^a_\mu$, $\omega_\mu^{\ ab}=2\dot a a^{-1}e_\mu^{\ [a}e^{0b]}$. Expanding in modes and applying a Bogoliubov transformation from the surface $\eta_0$ to $\eta_1>\eta_0$,

$$ c^k_\mu=\alpha_k c^k_\mu(\eta_0)+\beta_k c^{C\,k}_\mu(\eta_0), $$

the produced density is $\rho_{\tilde G}=m_{\tilde G}n_{\tilde G}$ with the occupation number $n_{\tilde G}\propto H\int dk\,k^2|\beta_k|^2/(2\pi^2)$. The mass itself is taken to follow the expansion, $m=m(\eta)$. The paper states that the case is formally different from the scalar one treated by Chung, Kolb and Riotto, and that it had not been treated before.

The Abundance and the Two Bounds

The relic ratio is diluted by the expansion after reheating,

$$ \frac{\rho_{\tilde G}(t_0)}{\rho_R(t_0)}=\frac{\rho_{\tilde G}(t_{Re})}{\rho_R(t_{Re})}\frac{T_R}{T_0}, $$

and with $H^2(t_e)\sim m_\phi^2$, $\rho(t_e)\sim m_\phi^2M_{Pl}^2$ this gives

$$ \Omega_{\tilde G}h^2\sim10^{17}\left(\frac{T_{Rh}}{10^9\ \mathrm{GeV}}\right)\frac{\rho_{\tilde G}(t_e)}{\rho_c(t_e)}. $$

With the inflaton mass near $10^{13}$ GeV and $T_{Rh}/T_0\simeq4.2\times10^{14}$ for $\Omega_{\tilde G}h^2\lesssim1$, the requirement that the gravitini make up a dark matter abundance of order one fixes

$$ m_{\tilde G}\simeq(10^{-2}\div2)H\simeq10^{11}\div10^{13}\ \mathrm{GeV}. $$

The gravitino mass must be of order the Hubble rate at the end of inflation. Two consequences follow. First, $M_{SUSY}>m_{\tilde G}\simeq10^{11}$–$10^{13}$ GeV, which is compatible with the $10^9$ GeV of the leptogenesis computation. Second, no superpartner may be heavier than the gravitino, or the decay channel reopens.

The Bound on the Instanton

The chain continues into the geometry. Instanton calculus is trustworthy only above the supersymmetry scale, so $M_S\geq M_{SUSY}\simeq10^{11}$–$10^{13}$ GeV. The right-handed masses are $M_{1,2,3}^{RH}\simeq e^{-S_{E_2}(\rho_i)}M_S\simeq10^9$ GeV, where $\rho_i$ are the moduli of the three-cycles the E2-brane wraps. Dividing,

$$ e^{-S_{E_2}}\simeq\frac{M_{RH,1,2,3}}{M_S}<10^{-2}\div10^{-3}. $$

The paper calls this a bound on the E2-brane, and says it "sets important constraints on the Calabi-Yau compactifications and on the string scale".

If the Gravitino Decays

The stable gravitino is the assumption, not a theorem. Trilinear R-parity breaking, $y\,h_\alpha L H_\alpha$, generates $\tilde G\to\gamma\nu$ with

$$ \Gamma(\tilde G\to\gamma\nu)=\frac{\cos^2\theta_W\,m_\nu\,m_{\tilde G}^3}{32\pi\,m_\chi M_{Pl}^2} \left(1-\frac{m_\nu^2}{m_{\tilde G}^2}\right)\left(1+\frac{m_\nu^2}{3m_{\tilde G}^2}\right), $$

through the photino–neutralino mixing induced by the interaction $\mathcal L_{int}=-i\,\bar\psi_\mu[\gamma^\nu,\gamma^\rho]\gamma^\mu\lambda F_{\nu\rho}/(8M_{Pl})$. With $m_\chi\simeq10^{13}$ GeV and $m_{\tilde G}\simeq10^{11}$ GeV the rate is $\Gamma\simeq10^{-20}$ eV, that is $\tau\simeq10^5$ s — far too fast to be dark matter. To reach $1$–$10$ Gyr the rate must be suppressed by about $10^{-13}$, which the paper attributes to a higher-dimension operator $(S/\Lambda)^n$ with a flux scale $\Lambda$, giving $(hS/\Lambda)^2\simeq10^{-13}$ for $n=1$.

The signature is stated plainly: a two-body decay peak in neutrinos or photons at $10^{11}$–$10^{13}$ GeV, which IceCube and ANTARES could see as a very rare event.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. Spin-$\tfrac32$ in curved spacetime: the spin connection, not the algebra

The idea. The Rarita–Schwinger field in an FRW background needs the spin connection $\omega_\mu^{\ ab}$ and the Christoffel term $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}\psi_\rho$, and its mass is promoted to a function of conformal time, $m=m(\eta)$. The mode expansion is then Bogoliubov-rotated between two surfaces, and the produced density is a sum over $|\beta_k|^2$.

What it would add. The corpus has three Rarita–Schwinger articles — the-rarita-schwinger-equation-spin-3-2-in-biquaternionic-form.md, the-lagrangian-and-the-rarita-schwinger-equation.md, canonical-quantization-of-the-biquaternion-rarita-schwinger-field.md — and all three are flat-space. A search of the first for "curved", "FRW" and "cosmology" returns nothing. The corpus's covariant derivative on a curved background exists for spin-$\tfrac12$ and spin one; the spin-$\tfrac32$ case is the one not carried over.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-rarita-schwinger-equation-spin-3-2-in-biquaternionic-form.md, as a new section, and the cosmology article for the mode expansion.

What stands in the way. The corpus's construction is algebraic: the field is an element of $\mathbb B^4$ and the equation is built from left and right multiplication. A spin connection is a connection on a frame bundle, and the corpus's frame article fixes one metric and one frame. Extending the algebra to a curved background is possible — the cosmology article already does it for the metric — but the spin-$\tfrac32$ case requires a vierbein-valued object, which the corpus has not built. This is a real construction, not a paragraph.

2. A dark matter candidate at $10^{11}$–$10^{13}$ GeV, and the corpus's closed budget

The idea. Gravitini produced by the non-adiabatic expansion are superheavy dark matter, with $m_{\tilde G}\simeq(10^{-2}\div2)H_{end}$ and $\Omega_{\tilde G}h^2\lesssim1$ reached at $T_{Rh}/T_0\simeq4.2\times10^{14}$.

What it would add. A candidate with a derived mass and a derived abundance, in a corpus that states the dark matter failure as a closed negative result.

Where it lands. articles_physics/what-the-standard-model-does-not-explain-dark-matter-baryogenesis-and-the-sphaleron.md. That article records $\Omega_{DM}h^2=0.1200$ against $\Omega_bh^2=0.02237$, the ratio $5.36$, the Standard Model accounting for $15.7\%$ of the budget, and "no knob to turn because the spectrum is closed". This paper's candidate is a knob: a particle outside the Standard Model spectrum, with a mass set by the inflationary scale rather than by a coupling.

What stands in the way. Everything. The candidate needs local supersymmetry, inflation and a reheating history, and the corpus has none of the three. The importable content is the shape of the argument — a particle whose mass is fixed by $H_{end}$ rather than by a coupling gets its abundance for free — and that is a sentence, not a derivation.

3. A cosmological measurement of a Calabi–Yau modulus

The idea. $\Omega_{\tilde G}h^2\lesssim1$ gives $m_{\tilde G}$, which gives $M_{SUSY}$, which gives $M_S$, which gives $e^{-S_{E_2}}<10^{-2}\div10^{-3}$. The chain runs from the dark matter abundance to the size of a three-cycle.

What it would add. The corpus's seesaw article lists as Open Question 2 whether the heavy scale is a symmetry-breaking scale or an exponential. This paper answers operationally: the exponential can be bounded from outside, by cosmology, without measuring a neutrino mass.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-seesaw-mechanism-and-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md, Open Question 2, and idea 1 of the 1510.00243 conversion.

What stands in the way. The chain has five links and each is an order-of-magnitude estimate; the paper says so. A bound obtained this way is a consistency check, not a measurement, and the corpus should record it as a conditional: if the gravitino is the dark matter, then the modulus is bounded. That conditionality is the honest content.

4. The gravitino bound as a constraint on any high-scale completion

The idea. $m_{\tilde G}<10^6$ TeV applies to any model with a gravitino heavier than its superpartners. A model that needs $M_{SUSY}>10^9$ GeV must therefore either make the gravitino the LSP or suppress its couplings.

What it would add. A general constraint the corpus does not carry: the corpus has no supersymmetry, so it has no gravitino problem. If the corpus ever imports a supersymmetric completion — and several conversions propose one — this is the first obstacle it meets.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the section that records what the frame does not contain. The article already has a ledger of assumptions and failures; a line "supersymmetric completions inherit the gravitino problem" belongs there.

What stands in the way. Nothing, but it is a caveat rather than an import. It is worth one row in a table.

5. A two-body peak as the falsification of a superheavy candidate

The idea. A superheavy decaying dark matter particle produces a monochromatic line, not a continuum: $\tilde G\to\gamma\nu$ at $10^{11}$–$10^{13}$ GeV is a peak, and the absence of a peak bounds the R-parity breaking.

What it would add. A worked example of how a superheavy candidate is tested, as opposed to a WIMP candidate. The corpus's dark matter discussion is entirely about the missing mass, not about the shape of a spectrum.

Where it lands. articles_physics/what-the-standard-model-does-not-explain-dark-matter-baryogenesis-and-the-sphaleron.md, as one paragraph on how a candidate is excluded.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no indirect detection and no decay. The import is the logic — a line is a signature, a continuum is a background — and that logic is short enough to state without the model.

One tension recorded

The paper's premise, "TeV-MSSM and TeV-Left-Right scenarios seem to be ruled out by recent data", was a 2016 reading. Supersymmetry has not appeared at the LHC, so the premise holds, but the paper's motive for high-scale breaking is now the absence of signals rather than a specific excess. No corpus article rests on either reading.

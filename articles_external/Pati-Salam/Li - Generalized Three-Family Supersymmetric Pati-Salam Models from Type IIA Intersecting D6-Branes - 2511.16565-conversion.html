Summary in the Authors' Words

The Assumption That Was Hidden

The earlier censuses (2112.09630, 2112.09632) assumed $I_{ac}=-3$ and $I_{ac'}=0$ — i.e. the $a$-stack intersects the $c$-stack three times and its image not at all. That gives three generations of $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ from the $a$–$c$ intersection.

**And they assumed "the $a$ stack of D6-branes must be parallel to the orientifold image of the $c$-stack along one of the three two-tori"

** — a simplifying geometric condition that makes the intersection numbers easy to compute.

This paper relaxes both. The general condition is $I_{ac}=-(3+h)$, $I_{ac'}=h$, which still gives three net generations (because the chiral index is $I_{ac}+I_{ac'}=-3$), but distributes them differently between the brane and its image.

And it drops the parallelism assumption, which allows genuinely new geometry.

The corpus should record this as a methodological lesson: "complete enumeration" means complete within a set of assumptions, and the assumptions are stated in equations, not in headlines. Two papers after the "complete" census (2303.02864, this one) have each found new models.

The Four New Classes

"four new classes ... with $I_{ac}=3$, $I_{ac'}=-6$ and $I_{ac}=-1$, $I_{ac'}=-2$"

Note the signs and the pattern: $I_{ac}=3$, $I_{ac'}=-6$ gives $I_{ac}+I_{ac'}=-3$, and $I_{ac}=-1$, $I_{ac'}=-2$ also gives $-3$. So the chiral index is preserved and the distribution changes. That is the right way to generalise: keep the physics (three generations) and vary the realisation. And it shows that the "33" was the count for one distribution, not all. The corpus should say the number is assumption-dependent.

The Canonical Normalisation Trick

"the canonical normalization requirement of the gauge kinetic term provides an alternative approach that can be imposed before the renormalization group equation evolution for $SU(2)_{L'}$ gauge coupling"

In these models $SU(2)_L$ sometimes arises from $SU(2)_{L1}\times SU(2)_{L2}$ broken to the diagonal. The diagonal coupling is then $1/g_L^2=1/g_{L1}^2+1/g_{L2}^2$, and if $g_{L1}\neq g_{L2}$ the tree-level gauge kinetic term is not canonical. The standard procedure is to run the RGEs and then check. This paper imposes the canonical normalisation first, which is equivalent to a field redefinition, and finds that the resulting relation is easier to satisfy. **"mmetric Pati-Salam models with large gb /ga ratio. We"

** — so the trick works exactly where the standard approach failed: the models with a large hierarchy between the $b$ and $a$ couplings. The corpus should record it as a technical device, and note that it is a normalisation choice, not new physics — but a choice that changes which models "unify". That is important: the unification criterion is partly a convention. The corpus should be careful with any claim that a model unifies at the string scale.

Why This Matters for the Cluster's Story

The cluster's narrative was: 33 classes, all enumerated, one unifies. Now: 33 was incomplete; four more classes exist; and even the unification criterion depends on normalisation. The corpus should present the cluster as an ongoing enumeration rather than a finished census. The honest statement is: the landscape has at least 37 classes and more assumptions remain to be relaxed.

The Rest of the Abstract

The paper also discusses the RGE evolution and the string-scale relations for the new models, along the lines of 2212.05875. The technical content is in those sections and the corpus does not need it in detail — the structural point is that generalising the generation condition produces new models that also need the unification machinery.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 2303.02864 together show that the D6-brane census is a moving target, and the corpus should present it that way. 2021: 33 classes, complete. 2023: hidden sectors were not scanned; the count is complete only for fixed hidden content. 2025 (here): generalised generation conditions; four more classes. 2025 (2512.09057): a fourth stack; 28 more classes. So four papers, four extensions, and the "complete landscape has grown from 33 to at least 65. The corpus's string section should say this plainly, because a reader who takes complete" at face value will misjudge the field.

And the deeper lesson is about what a census is: a count of solutions to a stated system of equations. The system is a modelling choice, and modelling choices are revisable. That is not a criticism of the cluster — it is how string model building works — but the corpus should not present any of its numbers as final.

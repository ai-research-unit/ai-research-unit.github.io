What the Paper Does

It explains the pulsar-timing-array signal with Pati–Salam strings, and adds inflation and leptogenesis to make a complete cosmology.

"A series of pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) recently observed gravitational waves at the nanohertz frequencies. Motivated by this remarkable result, we present a novel class of Pati-Salam models that give rise to a network of metastable cosmic strings, offering a plausible explanation for the observed PTA data."

"Besides, we introduce a hybrid inflationary scenario to eliminate magnetic monopoles that arise during the subsequent phase transitions from the Pati-Salam symmetry to the Standard Model gauge group."

"The resulting scalar spectral index is compatible with Planck data, and the tensor-to-scalar ratio is anticipated to be extremely small."

"Moreover, we incorporate a non-thermal leptogenesis to generate the required baryon asymmetry."

"the gravitational wave spectra generated by the metastable cosmic strings not only correspond to signals observed in recent PTAs, including NANOGrav, but are also within the exploration capacity of both present and future ground-based and space-based experiments."

This is the batch's only paper that does inflation, monopoles, strings, baryogenesis, and a GW signal at once. The corpus should treat it as the complete cosmology package.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Metastable Cosmic Strings

An ordinary cosmic string is stable; a metastable one can decay, via monopole–antimonopole pair nucleation on the string — the string breaks when a monopole and antimonopole appear on it and pull it apart.

The decay rate is controlled by a suppression factor $\kappa$, and the resulting GW spectrum has a characteristic cutoff at a frequency set by $\kappa$. A metastable string network produces a spectrum that rises at low frequency and cuts off — exactly the shape PTA data prefer.

And the corpus should record why metastability matters: a stable string network would over-produce GWs at high frequency (constrained by LIGO), so the metastability is what makes the scenario viable.

That is the key technical point: the network must decay, and PTA frequencies are where it decays.

The Monopole Problem and Hybrid Inflation

"we introduce a hybrid inflationary scenario to eliminate magnetic monopoles that arise during the subsequent phase transitions from the Pati-Salam symmetry to the Standard Model"

This is the batch's recurring problem: a $SU(4)\to SU(3)\times U(1)$ breaking produces monopoles (since $\pi_2(SU(4)/[SU(3)\times U(1)])\neq0$), and they would overclose the universe unless inflated away.

The standard fix is inflation after the breaking, and the paper uses hybrid inflation — a waterfall field triggered when the inflaton crosses a critical value.

And crucially: "Magnetic monopoles could still be generated at the end of inflation", so the inflation must end before the PS breaking, or the monopoles must be produced before the last $e$-folds.

The corpus should record the ordering problem, because it is the sharpest constraint on a Pati–Salam cosmology: the sequence must be $$\text{PS breaking} \to \text{inflation} \to (\text{reheating}) \to \text{EW breaking},$$ with the PS monopoles diluted and the EW monopoles (if any) absent.

And 2601.14580 (Okada) has a different answer: symmetry non-restoration means PS never restores, so no monopoles are regenerated after inflation. The two papers solve the same problem in opposite ways, and the corpus should pair them.

The String Network and Inflation Together

A subtlety: inflation dilutes the string network too. So the strings that produce the PTA signal must be formed after inflation — which means the PS breaking that makes them must also be after inflation.

And then the monopoles from that same breaking are not diluted — which is why the paper needs the metastable strings: **the strings decay, so they do not overclose; and the monopoles are... **

This is the tension the paper manages, and the corpus should present it as a fine-tuning of the ordering: the breaking must be after inflation (for strings), the monopoles must be diluted or absent (which needs either inflation after them, or non-restoration).

The resolution in this paper is a hybrid-inflation scenario where the monopoles are produced before and diluted, and the strings form in a later transition. The corpus should say that the ordering is the model's substance, because that is where the physics is.

Non-Thermal Leptogenesis

"non-thermal leptogenesis to generate the required baryon asymmetry"

Non-thermal leptogenesis means the right-handed neutrinos are produced by the inflaton's decay, not thermally — so the reheating temperature can be lower than the thermal leptogenesis bound ($T_{\rm RH}\gtrsim10^9$ GeV).

That matters because a low reheating temperature is preferred (it avoids gravitino overproduction, and it is compatible with 2601.14580's non-restoration).

The corpus should record the chain: inflaton → $N_R$ → lepton asymmetry → sphalerons → baryons. And note that it ties the inflation sector to the neutrino sector, which hep-ph/9602414-type models with seesaw can supply.

The PTA Signal

"The GW spectra ... not only correspond to signals observed in recent PTAs, including NANOGrav, but are also within the exploration capacity of both present and future ground-based and space-based experiments"

The nanohertz signal (NANOGrav 2023) is fit, and the same spectrum's high-frequency tail is within LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA, Einstein Telescope, LISA, and BBO reach.

So the model makes a two-band prediction: nanohertz now, and higher frequencies soon.

The corpus should record that as the batch's most testable cosmological prediction, and compare with 2504.01893's domain-wall GW: two different PS defects, two different frequency bands, both testable.

Why This Completes the Picture

The corpus's earlier cosmology notes found the field fragmented: 2504.01893 does domain walls, 2410.09093 does reheating, 2004.02332 does inflation and dark matter, hep-ph/0505221 does inflation, and none does all.

This paper does: inflation, monopoles, strings, GWs, and baryogenesis. It is the batch's only complete cosmology, and the corpus should say so and use it as the template.

And note what it still omits: dark matter. The PS dark matter is 2606.29811 and 2603.24152. So even this paper is not complete — the corpus should note the remaining gap.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's best answer to "what would Pati–Salam look like if it were true?", and the answer is a sequence, not a parameter.

The sequence: 1. $E_6$ or $SO(10)$ or the string scale breaks to PS. Monopoles form. 2. Inflation dilutes them. Hybrid inflation, with the waterfall after the breaking. 3. Reheating produces the right-handed neutrinos. Non-thermal leptogenesis makes the asymmetry. 4. A later transition forms the strings — metastable, so they decay. 5. The decay's GWs reach us at nanohertz. 6. The EW breaking happens last, with no monopoles (or diluted ones).

Six steps, each constrained, and the PTA data fixes step 5. That is a cosmological history, which is more than any other paper in the batch offers.

And it is falsifiable: if the nanohertz signal is not from strings, or if LISA sees nothing in the predicted band, step 5 fails and the model must change.

The corpus should present this as the template for what a complete Pati–Salam cosmology would have to do — and note that even this one leaves out dark matter, which 2606.29811 supplies separately.

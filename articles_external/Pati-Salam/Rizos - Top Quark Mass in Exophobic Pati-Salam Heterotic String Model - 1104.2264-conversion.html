What the Paper Does

It tests the exophobic model against the top quark, and finds that most such models fail.

"We analyse the phenomenology of an exemplary exophobic Pati–Salam heterotic string vacuum, in which no exotic fractionally charged states exist in the massless string spectrum. Our model also contains the Higgs representations that are needed to break the gauge symmetry to that of the Standard Model and to generate fermion masses at the electroweak scale."

"We show that the requirement of a leading mass term for the heavy generation, which is not degenerate with the mass terms of the lighter generations, places an additional strong constraint on the viability of the models."

"

In many models a top quark Yukawa may not exist at all, whereas in others two or more generations may obtain a mass term at leading order."

"In our exemplary model a mass term at leading order exist only for one family. Additionally, we demonstrate the existence of supersymmetric $F$– and $D$–flat directions that give heavy mass to [the exotics]."

So the rank-one structure is the feature, not the bug: only one family gets a leading mass, and that is the top. The problem is that many models get two or more, which is fatal.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Rank-One Requirement, Inverted

In field theory, a rank-one Yukawa matrix is a problem: only the top gets a mass, and the others need FN suppression (1406.7005) or radiative generation.

Here it is a requirement: **"the requirement of a leading mass term for the heavy generation, which is not degenerate with the mass terms of the lighter generations"

** — i.e. exactly one family must get a renormalisable mass.

And the failure mode is the opposite: **"in others, two or more generations may obtain a mass term at leading order"

** — which would give degenerate heavy generations, excluded by observation.

The corpus should record the inversion, because it is a clean illustration of how the same structure is a problem or a solution depending on the framework: field theory wants rank three and gets rank one; string theory gets rank one for free and must avoid rank two.

And hep-th/0601064's distinction between its two model kinds is exactly this: the first kind generates all three masses at tree level; the second gives only the third. So the two papers disagree about which is desirable — and they are right to, because the question is what the data allow.

The Statistic

**"In many models a top quark Yukawa may not exist at all"

** — so the Yukawa can vanish, because the three-point coupling requires three fields at a common fixed point, and the GGSO projection can remove any of them.

**"whereas in others two or more generations may obtain a mass term at leading order"

** — so the coupling can be degenerate.

Both failure modes are common. So the exophobic filter is necessary but not sufficient: the survivors must also pass the flavour test.

The corpus should add this filter to the funnel: - $10^{11}$ Pati–Salam - 1 in $10^6$ exophobic - of those, a fraction with exactly one leading Yukawa - of those, a fraction with a viable CKM and mass ratios

The funnel now has four stages, and only the first two are quantified. The corpus should say so.

The $F$- and $D$-Flat Directions

"

supersymmetric $F$– and $D$–flat directions that give heavy mass to [the exotics]"

That is how the exotics are removed even in the exophobic model: some remain at the massless level but can be given mass along a flat direction, i.e. by moving to a different point in moduli space where they pair up and get a VEV-induced mass.

The corpus should record the technique: flat directions are the heterotic analogue of the D6-brane's decoupling, and both are give the exotics a mass by moving in moduli space.

And the requirement that the direction be $F$- and $D$-flat is non-trivial: otherwise SUSY is broken at a high scale.

Why the Top Specifically

The top is the only fermion with a renormalisable Yukawa in most of these models, because its coupling is the one that survives the projections with order-one strength.

That is the string version of the "top is heavy" observation: it is heavy because its coupling is the only unsuppressed one. And it explains why the models can fit the top and not the others: the others need non-renormalisable operators, whose coefficients are exponentially suppressed by worldsheet areas or by the string scale. The corpus should record the mechanism, because it is the same as 2202.07048's three- and four-point couplings: the hierarchy is geometric.

The Exemplary Model

"In our exemplary model a mass term at leading order exist only for one family."

So the model passes. And it is the model from 1007.2268 — the first exophobic, quasi-realistic heterotic Pati–Salam vacuum. The corpus should note the chain: the classification found it, and this paper checked that its flavour sector is not obviously wrong. That is as far as the heterotic branch gets in this batch. What is missing: the actual numerical fit of the quark and lepton masses. The paper establishes the structure, not the numbers.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper shows that the heterotic branch's "success" is a structural check, not a fit, and the corpus should be careful not to overstate it.

What is established: an exophobic vacuum exists; it has three generations; it has the right Higgses; exactly one family gets a leading Yukawa; the exotics can be lifted by flat directions.

What is not: the masses and mixings. No numerical comparison with data appears.

So the heterotic branch is at the stage the D6-brane branch reached with 2202.07048 — the couplings are identified but not fitted. And the D6-brane branch needed 2409.09110 (126 pages) to do the fit for 33 models.

The corpus should say where each branch stands: - D6-branes: fit done for 33 models, 17 viable. - Heterotic free fermions: structure established for one model, fit not done.

That is a fair comparison and it should be in the corpus, because the literature's tone suggests the heterotic models are more successful, and on the evidence they are only earlier.

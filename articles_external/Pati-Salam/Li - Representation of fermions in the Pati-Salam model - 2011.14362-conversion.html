What the Paper Does

It puts the fermions in a $4\times4$ matrix and gauges the original Pati–Salam group, with both chiral factors at full $SU(4)$.

"a representation of fermions in the Pati-Salam model is suggested. The semi-leptonic and beyond standard model flavor changing neutral currents of the Lagrangian in this representation of fermions are discussed. A pair of possible Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa and Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata matrices are defined."

"The well-established Pati-Salam model has the following gauge group $G=SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\times SU(4')$ where $SU(4)_L$ and $SU(4)_R$ are the chiral flavor gauge groups, $SU(4')$ is the color group."

"the chiral flavor group $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ degenerates into the chiral group $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ and reproduces the NC and CC weak interactions transported by $Z$ and $W^\pm$"

"the $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\times SU(4')$ Pati-Salam model is anomaly free"

The important point for the corpus: this paper uses Pati–Salam's original three-$SU(4)$ group, not the familiar $SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Two Pati–Salam Groups, Not One

This is the structural fact the corpus most needs from this paper.

The familiar group is $$G_{PS}=SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R .$$ The original 1974 group is larger: $$G=SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\times SU(4') ,$$ with $SU(4')$ the colour group and $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ a chiral flavour group acting on the fermion families.

And the reduction is: "the chiral flavor group $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ degenerates into the chiral group $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$."

So the familiar model is the broken phase of the original one. The corpus's frame article uses the familiar group and derives it from the two-sided action of $\mathbb B\cong M_2(\mathbb C)$ — and $M_2(\mathbb C)$'s unitary group is $U(2)$, whose two-sided action is $U(2)\times U(2)$, which is the degenerate case. The $U(4)\times U(4)$ of De-Sheng Li's programme is the unbroken case.

The corpus should record the distinction, because it explains why the biquaternion framework lands on $SU(2)\times SU(2)$ and not on $SU(4)\times SU(4)$: the algebra is two-by-two, so its two-sided unitary action is $U(2)\times U(2)$. Getting $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ needs a four-dimensional carrier with a matrix action, which is De-Sheng Li's construction, not the biquaternion one.

That is a genuine and clarifying point, and the corpus should make it.

The $4\times4$ Fermion Matrix

The fermions fill a $4\times4$ matrix, with the rows and columns carrying the two $SU(4)$ indices.

So one entry is a fermion labelled by a colour index and a flavour index — a "quark-lepton" matrix.

And the Lagrangian is a trace: $$\mathcal L=\operatorname{Tr}\left[i\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi+f\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu V_\mu\Psi-g\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\Psi W_\mu\right],$$ with $V_\mu$ the colour and $W_\mu$ the flavour connection.

The trace structure is forced: only a trace is invariant under both $SU(4)$s, because the matrix transforms as $\Psi\to U_L\Psi U_R^\dagger$, so $\Psi$ is a bifundamental.

The corpus should record the bifundamental structure, because it is the honest content of "the fermions are a matrix": a field in $(\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})$ of $SU(4)\times SU(4)$.

And note the consequence: a bifundamental decomposes under the diagonal subgroup into adjoint plus singlet plus others, which is why the spectrum is rich and why the reduction to $SU(2)\times SU(2)$ is needed.

The Nine Gauge Bosons Problem

"If we gauge the flavor symmetry according to $SU(6)$ group ... nine gauge bosons at least ... the experimental data only showed us three flavor gauge interaction bosons, $W^+,W^-,Z$. The problem relates to how to reduce the nine flavor gauge bosons naturally to three"

The paper states the problem clearly: a large flavour gauge group predicts many new vector bosons, and only three are seen. So the reduction to $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ is not optional; it is required.

That is the same problem every extended gauge group faces, and the corpus should note that this paper's answer is the degeneration of $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\to SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$, i.e. a breaking, with the extra bosons heavy.

Semi-Leptonic FCNC and the $X$ Bosons

"The semi-leptonic processes preserve $B-L$ symmetry and violate baryon lepton number conservation ... transported by $X$ bosons"

The $X$ bosons are the leptoquarks, and they mediate $q\to\ell$ transitions — which is the Pati–Salam signature (1210.3697, 2103.11889).

And "semi-leptonic ... FCNC" means flavour-changing neutral currents with a quark and a lepton, which are constrained by $K\to\pi\nu\bar\nu$, $B\to K^{(*)}\ell\ell$, and muon conversion.

The corpus should record that this paper's new physics is the standard Pati–Salam leptoquark physics, and its FCNC discussion is a contribution to the batch's flavour section — because the bifundamental structure makes the FCNC automatic, not optional.

That is an interesting point: in the matrix representation, the flavour-changing couplings come from the off-diagonal entries of $\Psi$ and $W_\mu$, so the FCNC structure is determined by the fermion matrix's texture — a different language for the same physics as 2103.11889's leptoquark mixing.

The CKM and PMNS Pair

"A pair of possible CKM and PMNS matrices are defined"

"A pair" — because the matrix representation allows both a left-handed and a right-handed mixing matrix, and in the $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ language there are two.

The corpus should note it: the right-handed mixing matrix is a prediction of the left-right structure, and it is what makes the model testable — right-handed currents are constrained by $b\to s\gamma$, $B\to\tau\nu$, and the neutron EDM.

The batch's hep-ph/0302216-type left-right analyses apply directly.

The Anomaly-Free Claim

"the $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\times SU(4')$ Pati-Salam model is anomaly free"

A nontrivial statement for a chiral group with three factors. The corpus should note it and note that it is checked by the triangle diagrams in the paper — the standard test.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's clearest statement that Pati–Salam has two groups, and the corpus's frame article should be read in that light.

The frame article derives $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ from a two-by-two algebra. De-Sheng Li's programme derives $U(4)\times U(4)$ from a four-dimensional carrier. And Pati–Salam's original paper used $SU(4)\times SU(4)$.

So the three are the same idea at three different points on a breaking chain: $$SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\times SU(4')\ \longrightarrow\ SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\ \longrightarrow\ \text{SM}.$$

The corpus should draw the chain once, and place each paper on it: - hep-ph/0409098, hep-ph/9602414, and the whole field-theory branch: the middle group. - 2011.14362, 1412.2578, 2208.05942: the top group. - The biquaternion frame article: the middle group, derived from $M_2(\mathbb C)$.

That single diagram organises the corpus's gauge-structure discussion, and it makes the "why $SU(2)$ and not $SU(4)$" question answerable: because $M_2(\mathbb C)$ is two-by-two.

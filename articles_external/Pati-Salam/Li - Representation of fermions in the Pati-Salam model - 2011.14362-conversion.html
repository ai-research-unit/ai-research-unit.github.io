What the Paper Does

It puts the fermions in a $4\times4$ matrix and gauges the original Pati–Salam group, with both chiral factors at full $SU(4)$.

"a representation of fermions in the Pati-Salam model is suggested. The semi-leptonic and beyond standard model flavor changing neutral currents of the Lagrangian in this representation of fermions are discussed. A pair of possible Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa and Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata matrices are defined."

"The well-established Pati-Salam model [1] has the following gauge group G = SU (4)L × SU (4)R × SU (4′ ) (1) where SU (4)L and SU (4)R are the chiral flavor gauge groups, SU (4′ ) is the color group. Fermions have six flavors of quarks and leptons."

"paper [1], the chiral flavor group SU (4)L × SU (4)R degenerates into the chiral group SU (2)L × SU (2)R and reproduces the NC and CC weak interactions transported by Z and W ± weak gauge bosons, respectively."

"same time. So the SU (4)L × SU (4)R × SU (4′ ) Pati-Salam model is anomaly free. CONCLUSIONS"

The important point for the corpus: this paper uses Pati–Salam's original three-$SU(4)$ group, not the familiar $SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Two Pati–Salam Groups, Not One

This is the structural fact the corpus most needs from this paper.

The familiar group is $$G_{PS}=SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R .$$ The original 1974 group is larger: $$G=SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\times SU(4') ,$$ with $SU(4')$ the colour group and $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ a chiral flavour group acting on the fermion families.

And the reduction is: **"paper [1], the chiral flavor group SU (4)L × SU (4)R degenerates into the chiral group SU (2)L × SU (2)R and reproduces"

**

So the familiar model is the broken phase of the original one.

⚠️ FALSE — PURGED

An earlier version of this section said: "$M_2(\mathbb C)$'s unitary group is $U(2)$, whose two-sided action is $U(2)\times U(2)$, which is the degenerate case … Getting $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ needs a four-dimensional carrier with a matrix action." Both sentences are false, and the claim that they explain "why the biquaternion framework lands on $SU(2)\times SU(2)$ and not on $SU(4)\times SU(4)$" is false with them. They are retained here only so the error is not silently reintroduced.

Why they are false. The operator algebra of $\mathbb B$ is $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)\cong M_4(\mathbb C)$, and the sixteen words $L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$ span it at rank $16$ — the corpus's own frame article says so. Its compact group is therefore $U(4)$, of dimension $16$, with $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ of dimension $15$. The carrier is already four complex dimensions. $U(2)\times U(2)$, of dimension $8$, is only the subgroup generated by unitary left and right multiplication; it is not the compact group of the algebra. The mistake was to read the group of the units acting by multiplication as the group of the algebra.

And the weak pair is inside the colour algebra, not beside it. With the carrier basis $1,e_x,e_y,e_z$ and the form $\operatorname{tr}(X^\dagger Y)$, the six operators $L(\vec e_a)$, $R(\vec e_a)$ are skew-adjoint and traceless, hence elements of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, and they are independent:

$$ L(\vec e_x)\,e_t=\vec e_x,\qquad L(\vec e_x)\,\vec e_x=-e_t . $$

So $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\subset SU(4)$ as a six-dimensional subalgebra, and nothing has to be added to reach either it or $SU(4)$. The sum $\mathfrak{su}(4)+\mathfrak{su}(2)_L+\mathfrak{su}(2)_R$ is $15$-dimensional, not $21$. The true decomposition of the carrier's algebra is

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)=\underbrace{9}_{\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)_{B-L}}\oplus\underbrace{6}_{\text{leptoquark coset}}, $$

and the six weak generators straddle the two pieces, three in each, none of them purely one or the other — half of each $L(\vec e_a)$ is a colour rotation and half is a leptoquark direction. That is the exact reason the product is not a subgroup of $U(4)$: the factors overlap. It is a count, not a defect, and the frame article already answers it by declaring the formal copy the group acts on.

What survives from the retracted passage, and it is the useful part. $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)=M_4(\mathbb C)$ is a bimodule under $X\mapsto U_LXU_R^\dagger$, a bifundamental $(\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})$ of $U(4)\times U(4)$, and a trace Lagrangian is invariant for exactly that reason. Li's $4\times4$ fermion matrix and the corpus's array of sixteen words $L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$ have the same shape, the same index pair, and the same two-sided $U(4)$ action. One array, two readings: as a matrix of generators (the address space, Conway's length bound) or as a matrix of fermions (the content). The entry is the same sixteen matrices in both readings, and whether a generator and a fermion can share an entry is the real question the paper raises.

The $4\times4$ Fermion Matrix

The fermions fill a $4\times4$ matrix, with the rows and columns carrying the two $SU(4)$ indices.

So one entry is a fermion labelled by a colour index and a flavour index — a "quark-lepton" matrix. And the Lagrangian is a trace: $$\mathcal L=\operatorname{Tr}\left[i\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi+f\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu V_\mu\Psi-g\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\Psi W_\mu\right],$$ with $V_\mu$ the colour and $W_\mu$ the flavour connection. The trace structure is forced: only a trace is invariant under both $SU(4)$s, because the matrix transforms as $\Psi\to U_L\Psi U_R^\dagger$, so $\Psi$ is a bifundamental. The corpus should record the bifundamental structure, because it is the honest content of the fermions are a matrix: a field in $(\mathbf4,\bar{\mathbf4})$ of $SU(4)\times SU(4)$. And note the consequence: a bifundamental decomposes under the diagonal subgroup into adjoint plus singlet plus others, which is why the spectrum is rich and why the reduction to $SU(2)\times SU(2)$ is needed.

The Nine Gauge Bosons Problem

"If we gauge the flavor symmetry according to SU (6) group, the fermions should fill in a 4 × 6 matrix." "nine gauge bosons at least that transport flavor gauge interactions." "the experimental data only showed us three flavor gauge interaction bosons, which are W + , W − and Z. The problem relates to how to reduce the nine flavor gauge bosons naturally"

The paper states the problem clearly: a large flavour gauge group predicts many new vector bosons, and only three are seen. So the reduction to $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ is not optional; it is required.

That is the same problem every extended gauge group faces, and the corpus should note that this paper's answer is the degeneration of $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\to SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$, i.e. a breaking, with the extra bosons heavy.

Semi-Leptonic FCNC and the $X$ Bosons

"The semi-leptonic processes preserve B − L symmetry and violate baryon lepton number conservation. Topics" "transported by X bosons. The"

The $X$ bosons are the leptoquarks, and they mediate $q\to\ell$ transitions — which is the Pati–Salam signature (1210.3697, 2103.11889).

**And "semi-leptonic ... FCNC"

** means flavour-changing neutral currents with a quark and a lepton, which are constrained by $K\to\pi\nu\bar\nu$, $B\to K^{(*)}\ell\ell$, and muon conversion.

The corpus should record that this paper's new physics is the standard Pati–Salam leptoquark physics, and its FCNC discussion is a contribution to the batch's flavour section — because the bifundamental structure makes the FCNC automatic, not optional.

That is an interesting point: in the matrix representation, the flavour-changing couplings come from the off-diagonal entries of $\Psi$ and $W_\mu$, so the FCNC structure is determined by the fermion matrix's texture — a different language for the same physics as 2103.11889's leptoquark mixing.

The CKM and PMNS Pair

"a pair of possible CKM and PMNS matrix formulations were illustrated."

**"A pair"

** — because the matrix representation allows both a left-handed and a right-handed mixing matrix, and in the $SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R$ language there are two. The corpus should note it: the right-handed mixing matrix is a prediction of the left-right structure, and it is what makes the model testable — right-handed currents are constrained by $b\to s\gamma$, $B\to\tau\nu$, and the neutron EDM. The batch's hep-ph/0302216-type left-right analyses apply directly.

The Anomaly-Free Claim

"same time. So the SU (4)L × SU (4)R × SU (4′ ) Pati-Salam model is anomaly free. CONCLUSIONS"

A nontrivial statement for a chiral group with three factors. The corpus should note it and note that it is checked by the triangle diagrams in the paper — the standard test.

$SU(4)\times SU(4)$ and the familiar $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$

What it would add. A chronological point the corpus does not make anywhere: the group it uses is the second of two, and the first is larger. Where it lands. The frame article's "where the group comes from" section.

What stands in the way. Nothing — but the reason the corpus sits on the second group is not the size of its algebra. The corpus sits on the second group because it declares a formal copy for the chiral assignment, not because a two-by-two algebra forbids the larger one.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's clearest statement that Pati–Salam has two groups, and the corpus's frame article should be read in that light.

The three groups stand on one chain, and Li's own reduction is the top step: $$SU(4)_L\times SU(4)_R\times SU(4')\ \longrightarrow\ SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\ \longrightarrow\ \text{SM}.$$

The corpus should draw the chain once, and place each paper on it: - hep-ph/0409098, hep-ph/9602414, and the whole field-theory branch: the middle group. - 2011.14362, 1412.2578, 2208.05942: the top group. - The biquaternion frame article: the middle group — by declaration, since its algebra's endomorphisms contain the middle group's colour factor and its two $SU(2)$ factors at once.

That single diagram organises the corpus's gauge-structure discussion. What it does not do is answer "why $SU(2)$ and not $SU(4)$" from the algebra. The algebra admits both: $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)\cong M_4(\mathbb C)$ has $U(4)$ as its compact group, and the six weak generators sit inside the fifteen. The answer is the declared formal copy, not the size of the algebra, and an earlier version of this note got that backwards; the error is flagged in Two Pati–Salam Groups, Not One above.

What the Paper Does

It makes the CP phases of both mixing matrices come from one spontaneously broken symmetry.

"Recent neutrino oscillation experiments suggest that the PMNS matrix in the lepton sector has a CP violating phase as the CKM matrix in the quark sector. However, [the] origin of these phases in both matrices [is] not clarified."

"Although complex Yukawa couplings could induce these phases, they remain as free parameters ... If the CP symmetry is considered to be spontaneously broken, they are expected to be determined by some physics at a much lower energy scale than the Planck scale."

"We study such a possibility in a framework of Pati-Salam type unification."

Plus: a Peccei–Quinn symmetry to protect $\bar\theta$, and the two phases related because the same CP-breaking sector couples to quarks and leptons.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Spontaneous CP Violation

The idea: the Lagrangian is CP-invariant, and the CP violation comes from complex VEVs.

Then the phases are computed from the scalar potential's minimum, not chosen. That is the attraction: CP violation becomes a dynamical question.

And in a unified theory the same VEV can feed both the CKM and the PMNS phases, so the two are related.

The corpus should record the ambition: one source for both phases, which is what a unified theory ought to give.

And note the difficulty: spontaneous CP violation tends to produce a large $\bar\theta$, because the same mechanism that makes the CKM phase also makes the QCD angle. The paper's whole structure is a response to that.

The BBP Mechanism and the $Z_2$

"A minimal simple example ... has been proposed by Bento, Branco and Parada (BBP). In this model, extra heavy vector-like down-type quarks are introduced, and a $Z_2$ symmetry is imposed ... which controls the down-type quark mass matrix so as not to bring about a contribution to $\bar\theta$ through $\arg(\det M_uM_d)$"

The logic: $\bar\theta=\theta_{\rm QCD}+\arg\det(M_uM_d)$, so if $\arg\det(M_uM_d)=0$ the QCD angle is protected.

And a symmetry that makes the determinant real — a $Z_2$, or a $U(1)$ — does the job.

The corpus should record the formula, because it is the heart of every strong-CP discussion: the physical angle is the sum of a gauge term and a fermion-mass determinant's phase. And the corpus's proton-decay and axion sections both need it.

The PQ Upgrade

"If we impose a global $U(1)$ symmetry instead of the $Z_2$ and assign its charge to these extra heavy quarks ... the model has [a] similar structure to an invisible axion model by KSVZ ... If the introduced global $U(1)$ works as the PQ symmetry, the contribution to $\bar\theta$ through radiative corrections to $\arg(\det M_uM_d)$ could be erased"

So the paper improves the BBP mechanism: a $Z_2$ protects $\bar\theta$ at tree level but not radiatively; a PQ $U(1)$ protects it at all orders, because the axion relaxes $\bar\theta$ dynamically.

And the extra heavy quarks are KSVZ-like, so they generate the axion-gluon coupling.

The corpus should record the upgrade, because it is a genuine technical improvement: a discrete symmetry is not enough against radiative corrections.

The Two Phases Related

"We study this scenario in a Pati–Salam type unified model, in which the CP phases in the CKM matrix and the PMNS matrix could be related."

In PS the quarks and leptons share multiplets, so a CP-breaking VEV that enters the quark Yukawas also enters the lepton ones. Hence the phases are correlated.

That is the model's distinctive prediction, and the corpus should record it: a relation between $\delta_{\rm CKM}$ and $\delta_{\rm PMNS}$.

And it is testable, because $\delta_{\rm PMNS}$ is being measured (T2K, NOvA, DUNE) and $\delta_{\rm CKM}$ is known.

The corpus should note that the paper does not give a sharp numerical relation in the abstract, and the conversion should not invent one.

The Practical Problem

Spontaneous CP violation with a PQ symmetry is a tightrope: the CP-breaking scale must be high enough to hide the scalars, low enough to be "much lower than the Planck scale", and the axion must not overclose the universe.

The corpus should note the tension and that the paper's section 3 addresses "several phenomenological issues".

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper fills a gap the corpus's flavour chapter had: it addresses the phases.

The corpus's flavour papers fit the magnitudes — masses and mixing angles — and leave the CP phases as free parameters. This paper makes them dynamical.

That is important because the phases are where the next measurements are: $\delta_{\rm PMNS}$ is the target of DUNE and Hyper-K, and the leptonic CP violation is the gateway to leptogenesis.

And the paper's structure ties three things together: the CKM phase, the PMNS phase, and the axion. One sector, three consequences.

The corpus should say that, and should also say the limitation: the relation between $\delta_{\rm CKM}$ and $\delta_{\rm PMNS}$ is qualitative in the abstract, and a sharp prediction needs the potential's minimum computed. The corpus should mark it as a promised relation, not a delivered one.

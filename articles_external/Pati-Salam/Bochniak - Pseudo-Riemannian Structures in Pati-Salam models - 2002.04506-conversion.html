What the Paper Does The paper asks what happens to the family of Pati–Salam spectral triples when one adds the pseudo-Riemannian structure — an extra grading $\beta$ carrying a signature — that Bochniak and Sitarz had already shown the Standard Model's finite triple possesses. The answer is destructive and clean. For the full Pati–Salam algebra $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$ with the natural grading, no compatible $\beta$ exists that leaves a physically acceptable Dirac operator, so "arXiv:2002.04506v1 [math-ph] 11 Feb 2020 Prepared for submission to JHEP Pseudo-Riemannian Structures in Pati-Salam models A. Bochniak, T.E. Williams, P. Zalecki Institute of Theoretical Physics, Jagiellonian University in Kraków, prof." For the reduced algebra $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus\mathbb C\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ exactly one $\beta$ survives, and it is the same one that in the Standard Model case forbids leptoquarks. The consequence: $\beta$ breaks $SU(4)$ to $U(1)\times U(3)$, "leptons are separated from quarks, i.e. they are not the fourth color, so there are no leptoquarks", and what remains is a generalised left–right symmetric model. This is the sharpest internal critique of the non-commutative Pati–Salam programme in the corpus. The Aydemir papers take $\mathcal A_F=\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$ and read leptoquarks out of the spectral action. This paper accepts the same algebra and shows that a further axiom, one the Standard Model itself satisfies, removes the leptoquarks.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Algebra and the Fermions

$$ \mathcal A=\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C), $$ on $\mathcal H=F\oplus F^*$, with $F$ presented as a $4\times4$ array, $$ v=\begin{pmatrix} \nu_R&u_{1R}&u_{2R}&u_{3R}\\ e_R&d_{1R}&d_{2R}&d_{3R}\\ \nu_L&u_{1L}&u_{2L}&u_{3L}\\ e_L&d_{1L}&d_{2L}&d_{3L} \end{pmatrix}, $$ so that $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal H)\cong M_4(\mathbb C)\otimes M_2(\mathbb C)\otimes M_4(\mathbb C)$, with the representation acting by $\alpha v\beta^t$ on one factor and $M_2(\mathbb C)$ on the other. The rows are $(\text{chirality}\otimes\text{isospin})$ and the columns are the $SU(4)$ index, which makes lepton number the fourth colour a statement about the shape of an array: the first column is the lepton column and the other three are colour. The paper distinguishes two chiral structures — $\gamma$ and $\gamma_*$ — because $\gamma_*$"explicitly breaks the SU(4)-symmetry into U(1)×U(3), it is not". That remark is the seed of the whole result.

The Pseudo-Riemannian Structure

A pseudo-Riemannian spectral triple is $(\mathcal A,\mathcal H,D,\gamma,J,\beta)$ of signature $(p,q)$, where $\beta=\beta^*$, $\beta^2=1$, $\beta$ commutes with the representation of $\mathcal A$,"and defines a Krein structure on the Hilbert space", subject to $$ \beta\gamma=(-1)^p\gamma\beta, \qquad \beta J=(-1)^{\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}J\beta, \qquad D^*=(-1)^p\beta D\beta . $$ The triple is time-oriented if $\beta$"can be presented as the image of a certain Hochschild $p$-cycle". The Riemannian restriction is $D_+=\frac12(D+D^*)$ with $\beta D_+=(-1)^pD_+\beta$, "of the same KO-dimension as the one we started". The physical motivation is stated in the introduction: the Standard Model's finite triple can be seen as "the shadow of some pseudo-Riemannian triple in such a way that the pseudo-Riemannian structure leads to the existence of some nontrivial grading on the Riemannian triple". The signature is taken to be $(0,2)$ because the Pati–Salam triple must have KO-dimension $6$.

The Resulting Model

The reduced Pati–Salam model is $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus\mathbb C\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$, i.e. gauge group

$$ SU(2)_R\times SU(2)_L\times SU(3)\times U(1)_{B-L}, $$

with

$$ q_L=(1,2,3,\tfrac13),\quad q_R=(2,1,3,\tfrac13),\quad l_L=(1,2,1,-1),\quad l_R=(2,1,1,-1), $$

and $Q=I_{3L}+I_{3R}+\frac{B-L}{2}$. The paper's summary: this "generalizes both the Left-Right Symmetric Models and also the Chiral Electromagnetism Model", the latter being the $U(1)_R\times U(1)_L$ toy model of the Connes–Lott scheme.

One point is recorded as surprising: "the pseudo-Riemannian structure still allows for the existence of $SU(2)$-doublets of right-handed particles. This is an interesting feature."

The Historical Obstacle It Recalls

In the left–right survey the paper repeats a result the corpus needs: "In [17] it was argued that in the almost-commutative Yang–Mills–Higgs models, parity cannot be spontaneously broken. This followed from the requirement that Poincaré duality must be satisfied."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

  1. There is an axiom that removes the leptoquark, and the Standard Model already obeys it

The idea. Add the pseudo-Riemannian structure $\beta$ to the finite triple. On the unreduced algebra the Yukawa block is forced to vanish; on the reduced algebra the only surviving $\beta$ is one that splits $SU(4)$ into $U(1)\times U(3)$ and thereby makes the lepton a singlet rather than a fourth colour.

What it would add. A derivation where the corpus currently has a choice. articles_physics/the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md treats $SU(4)$ as the right group because of what it explains, and the corpus's spectral-triple article says the biquaternion algebra is not a Connes triple because it fails the first-order condition and the KO-dimension selection. This paper supplies a third fact of the same kind, and a stronger one: within the spectral-triple family the Pati–Salam algebra and the pseudo-Riemannian axiom are incompatible, and the axiom wins because the Standard Model satisfies it. The leptoquark is not excluded by data or by taste; it is excluded by the same requirement that gives the Standard Model its fermion content.

Where it lands. The spectral-triple article, in the section on the axioms — as the sharpest available statement of what a spectral-triple construction can and cannot deliver — and the Pati–Salam article, in its boundary statement.

What stands in the way. It cuts against the corpus's own Pati–Salam article, which treats the leptoquarks as a prediction. The corpus can hold both, but only by being explicit: the leptoquark survives only if the pseudo-Riemannian structure is dropped, and the price of dropping it is that the Standard Model's own triple loses the axiom that selects its fermion content. That is a real trade-off and the corpus should name it rather than pick a side silently.

  1. $M_4(\mathbb C)\to\mathbb C\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ is the algebraic form of the lepton is the singlet and colour is the triplet

The idea. The reduced algebra represents $\mathbb C\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ on $\mathbb C^4$ block-diagonally, as $\mathrm{diag}(\lambda,N)$ with $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ and $N\in M_3(\mathbb C)$. The four-dimensional space splits as $\mathbf 1\oplus\mathbf 3$; the singlet carries $B-L$ and the triplet carries colour.

What it would add. The exact algebra behind a phrase the corpus uses descriptively. The corpus's Pati–Salam article says lepton number is "the fourth colour" and proves charge quantisation from $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$. The reduced algebra says the fourth direction is not a colour at all — it is the one-dimensional summand, and $B-L$ is the $U(1)$ that acts on it. Both statements are true of the same $4\times4$ array; they differ in whether the array's four columns are one orbit or a singlet plus a triplet.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's charge-quantisation paragraph, and articles_physics/the-spectral-triple-of-the-biquaternion-algebra.md where $M_2(\mathbb H)\cong M_4(\mathbb C)$ is discussed.

What stands in the way. The corpus's frame treats the four directions as one algebra acting transitively. Splitting them is a reduction of the algebra, and the corpus should present it as such: not the fourth colour is wrong but the fourth colour and a separate $B-L$ are two readings of the same array, and an axiom decides between them.

3. A generation is literally a $4\times4$ matrix

The idea. $F$ is written as a $4\times4$ array whose rows are $(\text{chirality},\text{isospin})$ and whose columns are the four $SU(4)$ directions. Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's fermion-assignment section, as the display of the array rather than a list of multiplets.

4. Poincaré duality forbids spontaneous parity breaking in almost-commutative models

The idea. Girelli's result, quoted here:"in the almost-commutative Yang–Mills–Higgs models, parity cannot be spontaneously broken. Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's parity section, where the three-paper sequence is assembled.

5. The surviving model is a generalised left–right symmetric model, not Pati–Salam

The idea. The endpoint of the reduction is $SU(2)_R\times SU(2)_L\times SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{B-L}$, which "generalizes both the Left-Right Symmetric Models and also the Chiral Electromagnetism Model". Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's comparison of the two groups, and the grand-unification article's rank discussion.

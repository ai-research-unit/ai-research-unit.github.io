What the Paper Does

It is a systematic classification: all Pati–Salam models with $A_4$ modular symmetry, fitted, with four benchmarks.

"We perform a systematic classification of Pati-Salam models according to the transformations of matter fields under the $A_4$ modular symmetry, and the general form of the fermion mass matrix is given. We present four phenomenologically viable benchmark models which provide excellent descriptions of masses and flavor mixing of quarks and leptons, including neutrinos."

"In such models we find that the normal ordered neutrino mass spectrum is preferred over the inverted case, with neutrinoless double beta decay predicted to be too small to be observed by the next generation of experiments."

What is new relative to 1406.7005 is the word "classification". King's 2014 model chose one assignment; this paper enumerates all of them, fits each, and reports which survive. That is the right response to a model-building landscape and the corpus should note the genre.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why Modular, and Why $A_4$

"In comparison with the PS models based on traditional flavor symmetries, both flavons and driving fields are absent so that the modular invariant PS models are much more simpler, with $A_4$ being the minimal modular group which admits triplet representations."

Two claims, both about economy: - No flavons. In a traditional model the Yukawa couplings are $y\,(\phi/\Lambda)^n$ with a flavon $\phi$ getting a VEV. In a modular model the coupling is a modular form $Y(\tau)$, a fixed function of the modulus. So the flavon and its alignment are replaced by one complex number $\tau$. - No driving fields. Traditional $A_4$ models need a separate "driving" sector to align the flavon VEV. Modular models need none, because there is no VEV to align.

And "$A_4$ is the minimal modular group admitting triplets" — the same ladder argument as 1705.06320, but here the relevant rung is the lowest one that has a triplet at all. $\Gamma_2\cong S_3$ has a doublet, $\Gamma_3\cong A_4$ has a triplet, and $A_4$ is where three generations can be a single irreducible.

That is the sharpest statement in the paper: modular symmetry does not just replace flavons, it removes the largest source of arbitrariness (the alignment). Whether it also removes enough parameters is the question the classification answers.

The Answer on Parameters, Which Is Sobering

"We find that many PS $\times A_4$ models are compatible with the experimental data, and the phenomenologically viable models depend on at least 19 real parameters including the real and imaginary parts of the modulus $\tau$."

"We notice that the models would be strongly constrained by modular symmetry and a large number of parameters are required to accommodate the data, if both $F_i$ and $F_i^c$ transform as triplet under $A_4$."

19 real parameters is not a small number, and the paper says so without spin. The corpus should record it, because the selling point of modular symmetry is economy and this paper's own count shows the economy is partial: you remove the flavons but you keep the coupling constants $\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i$ as free complex numbers, each with a modulus and a phase.

So the modular framework removes flavons but not freedom. The freedom migrates into the coefficients of the modular forms. That is the honest assessment and the paper's classification is what makes it visible — a single benchmark model could hide it.

The Structure They Impose

"The three generations of the left-handed and right-handed fermions are assigned to be triplet or singlets of $A_4$."

"we have dropped the assignment that both $F_i$ and $F_i^c$ are $A_4$ singlets, since the flavor symmetry would be essentially the $Z_3$ subgroup generated by modular generator $T$ and the different entries of the Yukawa couplings would be loosely correlated for this kind of assignment."

That is a useful technical exclusion, and it explains why the classification is short. If both left and right are singlets, only the $T$ generator acts nontrivially, and $T$ is Abelian — so the "non-Abelian" symmetry has collapsed to $Z_3$ and nothing relates the generations. The surviving models are those where at least one side is a triplet.

So the classification space is: $F_i$ triplet and $F_i^c$ singlets, or $F_i$ singlets and $F_i^c$ triplet. Two families, and the four benchmarks sit in them.

The Georgi–Jarlskog Factor, Again

"Two PS Higgs multiplets $\Phi\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $\Sigma\sim(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ are introduced to trigger the electroweak symmetry breaking, and $\Sigma$ can lead to the Georgi-Jarlskog factor $-3$ so that the difference in the mass spectrum of down type quarks and charged leptons can be accommodated."

Note the sign: $-3$, not $+3$. The negative factor is what makes the charged-lepton mass come out larger than the down quark at the relevant scale, and it is the same structure as 1608.04124's minimal model. The corpus should record the sign, because it distinguishes the $\mathbf{15}$ route from the $45$-route which gives a different factor.

And in a modular model the GJ factor is a consequence of the gauge structure, not a fitted number — the modular forms fix the texture and the $\mathbf{15}$ fixes the relative normalization. That is the division of labour the paper is exploiting: Pati–Salam for the inter-sector relation, $A_4$ modular for the intra-sector texture.

The Predictions

"The neutrino mass spectrum is determined to be normal ordered in all these four models, since the best fit values of certain flavor observables lie outside the $3\sigma$ regions for inverted ordering."

"The effective mass $|m_{\beta\beta}|$ of the neutrinoless double beta decay is expected to be in the range of $0.1$–$2$ meV which is below the sensitivities of the future tonn-scale experiments."

"The predictions of the normal neutrino mass ordering, the atmospheric mixing angle $\theta_{23}^l$ and leptonic Dirac CP violation phase $\delta_{CP}^l$ are expected to be either confirmed or excluded by the forthcoming neutrino facilities such as DUNE and T2HK."

$0.1$–$2$ meV is below every planned $0\nu\beta\beta$ sensitivity ($\sim5$–$15$ meV for nEXO/LEGEND-1000). So the prediction is "no signal, ever" — which is a form of falsifiability: if $0\nu\beta\beta$ is seen at the next generation, these models die.

And that matches 1406.7005's conclusion exactly. Two papers, ten years apart, different symmetry (discrete $A_4\times Z_5$ versus modular $A_4$), same answer: hierarchical normal ordering, $m_{\beta\beta}$ too small to see. That agreement is informative — it suggests the conclusion follows from the Pati–Salam seesaw structure rather than from the flavour symmetry.

That is a claim the corpus could make: if two very different flavour models built on the same gauge group give the same $0\nu\beta\beta$ prediction, the prediction is about the gauge group.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. Modular symmetry removes flavons but not free parameters — 19 of them here

The idea. The selling point is economy, but the coupling coefficients of the modular forms remain free.

What it would add. A balanced assessment of the modular programme, with a number. The corpus should not describe modular models as parameter-free, and this paper's own count is the evidence.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's flavour section, when discussing modular symmetry.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the count is stated.

2. If both left- and right-handed fields are singlets, the symmetry is only $Z_3$

The idea. Only the $T$ generator acts, and $T$ is Abelian, so nothing relates generations.

What it would add. A structural reason the classification is small, and a general lesson: a non-Abelian flavour symmetry is only non-Abelian if a triplet appears. That is transferable to any modular model.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's flavour section.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

3. The agreement of $0\nu\beta\beta$ predictions across two very different flavour models

The idea. 1406.7005 and this paper both give normal ordering and $m_{\beta\beta}$ below reach.

What it would add. An argument that the prediction comes from the gauge group's seesaw structure, not from the flavour symmetry — which is a stronger claim and a more useful one. The corpus should test it by checking whether any model in the batch gets inverted ordering.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's neutrino section.

What stands in the way. Both papers use type-I seesaw with hierarchical right-handed neutrinos, so the agreement could come from that choice instead.

4. Classification is the right genre for a landscape

The idea. Enumerate all assignments, fit each, report the survivors, give benchmarks.

What it would add. A methodological contrast with single-model papers. In a batch where most papers propose one model and show it works, this one asks how many work — and the answer ("many") is itself a result.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's methodology discussion.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's best guide to the modular programme's actual state, because it is a census rather than a showcase.

The census says: many assignments work, each needs 19 real parameters, all prefer normal ordering, all predict unobservable $0\nu\beta\beta$. That is not a triumph and it is not a failure; it is a landscape, and the honest question is whether the landscape has any structure.

One piece of structure it does have: the excluded case (both singlets) is excluded for a clear reason, and the surviving cases all have one triplet. So at least one binary choice is fixed by logic rather than by fit.

The second point, for the corpus: 1406.7005 and this paper are the same author ten years apart, and the difference is the replacement of flavons by a modulus. If the 2024 model needs 19 parameters and the 2014 model needed a comparable number, the modular programme has bought simplicity of structure rather than of fitting. That distinction is worth making precisely, because the papers' rhetoric tends to blur it.

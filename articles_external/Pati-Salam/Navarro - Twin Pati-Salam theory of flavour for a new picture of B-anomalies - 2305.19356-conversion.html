What the Paper Does

Six pages that state the twin Pati–Salam logic more clearly than the 67-page paper does.

"We discuss a theory of flavour which can simultaneously explain and connect the origin of the Standard Model flavour hierarchies with the $U_1(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$ leptoquark explanation of the B-decay flavour anomalies."

The paper's real contribution is the argument for why the third family must be a singlet under $SU(4)$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why $U_1$ and Why It Connects the Two Anomalies

"The vector leptoquark $U_1(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$ contributes at tree-level to the $R_{D^{(*)}}$ ratios via the contact interaction $(\bar c_L\gamma^\mu b_L)(\bar\tau_L\gamma_\mu\nu_L)$. This contribution is also connected to the contact interaction $(\bar s_L\gamma^\mu b_L)(\bar\tau_L\gamma_\mu\tau_L)$ via $SU(2)_L$ invariance. Remarkably, at the 1-loop level the latter provides a contribution to the LFU operator $(\bar s_L\gamma^\mu b_L)\bar\ell\gamma_\mu\ell$. Therefore, a vector leptoquark $U_1(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$ explaining the $R_{D^{(*)}}$ anomalies naturally delivers a relevant LFU contribution to $b\to s\ell\ell$ that can reduce the tension with the experiment."

That is the mechanism that makes one leptoquark do both jobs, and it is worth recording precisely.

The chain: $U_1$ gives $b\to c\tau\nu$ at tree level (fixing $R(D^{(*)})$). $SU(2)_L$ invariance ties that coupling to $b\to s\tau\tau$. Then a loop closes the $\tau$ pair into a $Z$ or photon, giving $b\to s\ell\ell$ — the operator that $R(K^{(*)})$ measures.

So the two anomalies are connected by one coupling and one loop, not by two independent couplings. That is why a single $U_1$ can explain both, and why no scalar leptoquark can (the scalar $S_3$ has the right $b\to s\mu\mu$ coupling but no $b\to c\tau\nu$).

The Kaon Problem and the 4321 Solution

"the explanation of the $R_{D^{(*)}}$ anomalies requires a TeV scale $U_1$. Such a light vector leptoquark arising from traditional Pati-Salam (PS) embeddings would be at odds with data from flavour-violating kaon decays."

That is the obstruction and it is fatal for the obvious approach. A TeV-scale $SU(4)$ leptoquark couples to all three generations with equal strength (the group does not distinguish them), so it mediates $K_L\to\mu e$ at a rate that a TeV mass cannot suppress. The experimental bound forces the scale to a PeV or above, which is too heavy for $R(D^{(*)})$.

The escape: "the so-called '4321' gauge group ... which can provide a TeV scale $U_1$ as long as the first and second fermion families are singlets under $SU(4)$."

So the resolution is to make most fermions blind to the leptoquark. $U_1$ then couples only to whatever transforms under $SU(4)$, and if the light families are singlets, the kaon decay is automatically absent.

Why the Third Family Must Also Be a Singlet — the Key Argument

"In recent years, several theories of flavour have been proposed assuming that the third SM family transforms non-trivially under $SU(4)$. However, all of them predict a sizable coupling of third family right-handed (RH) fermions to $U_1$, which is tightly constrained by direct searches at LHC. Instead, if the third family is a singlet under $SU(4)$, then the $U_1$ currents can be dominantly left-handed (LH), a scenario compatible with current high energy data."

That is the paper's sharpest point. If $b_R$ transforms under $SU(4)$, then $U_1$ couples to $b_R$ with gauge strength, and the LHC's $pp\to U_1\to t\bar t$ or $\tau\bar\tau$ searches exclude it at a TeV. If $b_R$ is a singlet, the coupling is generated only through mixing with vector-like fermions, and it is naturally left-handed and small.

So the model must be "fermiophobic": every SM fermion is a singlet under $SU(4)$, and all $U_1$ couplings come from mixing. That is a strange-looking requirement — the leptoquark does not couple to anything at the renormalisable level — and it is what the data force.

"In this so-called 'fermiophobic' scenario, the couplings of $U_1$ to SM fermions are all induced through mixing with vector-like fermions that transform non-trivially under $SU(4)$. Nevertheless, the flavour structure of such a model is ad-hoc, worsening the flavour puzzle with a bunch of extra parameters that give no information about the origin of flavour in the SM."

So the fermiophobic construction solves the LHC problem and creates a flavour problem. And that is exactly what the twin Pati–Salam is for:

"We will show in the following a theory of flavour featuring a low scale fermiophobic 4321 model, which addresses the origin of flavour hierarchies in the SM and predicts an even richer phenomenology."

The Twin Structure as a UV Completion

"Given the structure of the fermiophobic 4321 group ... where a set of vector-like fermions transforms under $SU(4)$ while SM-like fermions transform under $SU(3)'$, a natural UV completion would be that of two commuting Pati-Salam groups, one for vector-like fermions and another one for SM-like fermions."

That sentence is the cleanest justification of the twin construction in the literature. The 4321 group has two colour factors; the natural completion is two full Pati–Salam groups; the vector-like fermions live under one, the SM fermions under the other, and a scalar sector links them.

So the "twin" is not a doubling for its own sake — it is what the 4321 structure becomes when you complete it. That is a much better motivation than the original paper gives, and it is worth recording.

The Predictions

"the enhancement of $B\to K^{(*)}\nu\bar\nu$ over the SM prediction, which is within the future reach of the Belle II collaboration. Remarkably, given the dominantly LH nature of $U_1$ couplings in the Twin PS model, this signal is much more enhanced with respect to alternative constructions."

So the left-handedness makes $B\to K^{(*)}\nu\bar\nu$ larger, which is both a prediction and a discriminator: the twin model gives a bigger rate than competing 4321 constructions with right-handed couplings. That is a way to tell the models apart with one measurement.

And $\tau\to3\mu$ from both $Z'$ and $U_1$, with the $Z'$ dominating.

One observation about the corpus's map

This six-page proceeding contains the best argument in the batch for the twin construction, and it is buried in a conference report.

The argument has three steps and each is a constraint from data rather than a preference:

  1. $R(D^{(*)})$ needs a TeV $U_1$.
  2. A TeV $SU(4)$ leptoquark is excluded by $K_L\to\mu e$, so light families must be $SU(4)$ singlets.
  3. A $U_1$ coupling to $b_R$ is excluded by the LHC, so the third family must be a singlet too.

That leaves a group in which the leptoquark couples to nothing at tree level, and then "the flavour structure ... is ad-hoc" — unless the couplings come from mixing with vector-like fermions, which needs a second Pati–Salam group to organise.

So the twin Pati–Salam is the answer to a sequence of exclusions, not a construction someone liked. That is the strongest form of model building and the corpus should present it that way. It also explains why the field content is so large: each exclusion closed off a simpler option.

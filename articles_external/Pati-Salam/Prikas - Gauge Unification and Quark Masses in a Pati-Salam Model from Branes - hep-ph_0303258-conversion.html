What the Paper Does

It asks how low the string scale can be in a brane-derived Pati–Salam model, and finds a large gap between the SUSY and non-SUSY answers.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why a Low String Scale at All

The motivation is the hierarchy problem in its brane form: if the string scale is near the TeV scale, the hierarchy between the electroweak and Planck scales is solved by large extra dimensions (the Arkani-Hamed–Dimopoulos–Dvali picture), not by SUSY.

And the attraction is testability: a 10 TeV string scale means string resonances at the LHC, and Kaluza–Klein modes of the gauge bosons at accessible energies.

The corpus should record this as another route to a low Pati–Salam scale, distinct from the vector-leptoquark papers (2103.11889) and the 4321 model (2106.03876): here the scale is low because the string scale is low.

And it is the batch's only non-SUSY low-scale Pati–Salam proposal. 1807.03669 is non-SUSY but at $10^{15}$ GeV; the twin-PS and 4321 models are non-SUSY but TeV-scale for different reasons. This one is non-SUSY and TeV-scale because of large extra dimensions.

Why SUSY Pushes the Scale Up by Three Orders

The argument, in plain terms. In a SUSY model the gauge couplings unify at the string scale via the MSSM RGEs, and the unification scale is fixed by the measured couplings and the spectrum. Adding the Pati–Salam exotics changes the running, and the required string scale moves up.

**"The limited energy region for RGE running demands a large tan β in order"

** — because with the string scale at $2\times10^7$ TeV the running is short, so the $b$ and $t$ Yukawa couplings have little room to diverge, and a large $\tan\beta$ compensates by making $Y_b$ comparable to $Y_t$ at the string scale.

So the SUSY version is squeezed twice: the unification condition pushes $M_S$ up, and the short running then requires a large $\tan\beta$ to fit $m_b/m_t$.

The corpus should record the pattern: the mass ratio $m_b/m_t$ is the constraint that fights a low string scale, because it needs either a long running distance or a large $\tan\beta$. That is the same tension as in hep-ph/0303192, where exact Yukawa unification forced large $\tan\beta$ and heavy sfermions.

The Non-SUSY 10 TeV Option

The non-SUSY version avoids the unification constraint because there is no SUSY desert: the couplings run from 10 TeV to $M_Z$ with the SM content, and the string scale can be as low as the experimental bounds allow.

But the cost is the hierarchy problem, which large extra dimensions must solve, and the Pati–Salam breaking scale must be at or below the string scale — so $M_{PS}\sim10$ TeV.

That puts the leptoquark at 10 TeV, which is below the ~40 TeV bound from 1210.3697 unless the mixing matrix $D$ is tuned. The corpus should note the tension: a 10 TeV Pati–Salam leptoquark needs the small-$D$ corner that the rare-decay papers identify.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is short and its value is in a single comparison: the same brane construction supports a 10 TeV non-SUSY model and a $2\times10^7$ TeV SUSY model.

A factor of $2\times10^6$ between the two, from the same gauge group and the same brane origin, differing only in whether supersymmetry is present. That is the cleanest single illustration of the corpus's central point about the scale.

And it explains the literature's confusion: papers quote the Pati–Salam scale as though it were a property of the group, when it is a property of the spectrum and the mediation of SUSY breaking. 2504.01893 derives $10^{15}$ GeV from two-loop running; 2501.00302 fits $10^4$ GeV from modular weights; this paper gets 10 TeV non-SUSY and $10^7$ TeV SUSY. All are consistent with $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$.

The corpus should make the group/scale distinction explicit in its first section, because every later section depends on it.

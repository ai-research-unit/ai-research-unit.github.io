Summary in the Authors' Words

Quark–Lepton Complementarity

The observation: the solar angle plus the Cabibbo angle is about $45°$, and the atmospheric angle minus the corresponding quark angle is about $-45°$.

So the lepton mixing looks like the complement of the quark mixing. That is a striking numerical coincidence, and it suggests a common origin.

Why it happens in a GUT: if the charged-lepton and down-quark mass matrices are similar (as in $SU(5)$-type relations, $\ell\leftrightarrow d$), then their diagonalisation angles are similar, and the PMNS built from them inherits the CKM.

So QLC is a consequence of a unification relation plus a flavour symmetry. That is why the paper uses PS: PS relates quarks and leptons too, through the $\bar{\mathbf4}$ rather than the $\bar{\mathbf5}$.

The corpus should record the logic, because it is the batch's cleanest example of a unification prediction for flavour. The relation is not imposed; it follows from the multiplet structure.

And the corpus should note the status: QLC is approximate and somewhat dated (the measurements have moved), but the mechanism is sound.

The $S_4$ Structure

$S_4$ — the permutation group of four objects, order 24 — has a three-dimensional irrep, which is why it fits three families.

And it has a natural embedding in $SO(3)$-like structures, so it can arise from the geometry of a tetrahedron — and hence from orbifold or flux compactifications.

The corpus should note: $S_4$ is the batch's most-used discrete group (1605.02072, 1111.3952, here), and the reason is the three-dimensional irrep. The corpus's flavour section should say that once.

The Interplay: GUT and Flavour Together

"Both symmetries put constraints on the high energy behaviour of the theory." "This can give rise to unexpected interplay when building models that possess both symmetries."

What the interplay is: - The GUT fixes relations between quark and lepton Yukawas at the GUT scale ($y_d=y_e$ etc.). - The flavour symmetry fixes the pattern of the Yukawas. - Both must hold simultaneously, and the combined constraints can be over-determined.

And the paper's finding: **"the Higgs sector and the running Yukawa couplings are strongly affected."

**

So the combination is not free: the flavour symmetry's predictions must survive the GUT running, and the running depends on the Higgs content.

The corpus should record this as a methodological point: flavour models must be run from the GUT scale, and most of the batch's flavour papers fit at the weak scale and ignore the running.

That is a genuine criticism the corpus can make, and 1003.4502 is the exception that proves the rule.

The Higgs Sector Cost

**"the Higgs sector ... strongly affected"

** — because the flavour symmetry must be broken, and the flavons must not spoil the GUT relations.

So the model has a large flavon sector, and the corpus should note the cost: the flavour symmetry's predictivity is bought with many new scalars.

And 1010.3406 is the companion paper that develops this, so the corpus should pair them.

The Neutrino Sector

The $S_4$ model predicts near-tri-bimaximal mixing, with deviations from the symmetry breaking.

And TB is now excluded at high confidence ($\theta_{13}\ne0$, and $\theta_{23}$ not maximal), so the model must be perturbed.

The corpus should note the historical position: 2010, when TB was viable, and the paper fits within it. The later data (1111.3952's relatively large $\theta_{13}$) has moved past it.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's most careful flavour-and-unification analysis, and its lesson is a warning.

The lesson: a flavour symmetry that fits the weak-scale data may not survive the GUT running, and checking that requires the full RGE with the model's Higgs content. Most papers skip it.

So the corpus should add a criterion to its flavour comparison: does the model run? 1003.4502 does; 1406.7005, 1605.02072, and 1111.3952 mostly do not.

And the corpus should say what the running changes: the GUT relations ($y_b=y_\tau$, $y_\mu=y_s$) are themselves running-dependent, and a flavour fit at the weak scale that does not respect them is fitting the wrong numbers.

That is a technical point with real consequences, and it is the kind of thing a corpus for readers should say once, clearly, rather than leave implicit.

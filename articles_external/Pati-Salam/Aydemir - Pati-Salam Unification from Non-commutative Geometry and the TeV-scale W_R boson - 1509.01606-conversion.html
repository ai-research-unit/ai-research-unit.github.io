What the Paper Does

The paper asks whether the Pati–Salam models that come out of noncommutative geometry can host a right-handed $W_R$ at a few TeV, as the 2015 LHC diboson excess was taken to suggest. The models fix unification at $M_U\sim10^{15}$ GeV and, in their original form, break left–right symmetry at $M_R\sim10^{13}$ GeV. The paper adds intermediate scales and relaxes the survival hypothesis, then solves the one-loop equations. The answer is a qualified no: the best configuration it can reach is $M_R\sim2\times10^5$ GeV with $g_R\simeq0.43$, which is neither a few TeV nor the coupling the excess wanted. The value of the paper for the corpus is elsewhere: it states precisely what D-parity is, why $g_L\ne g_R$ is possible, and what the minimal noncommutative model predicts for the Higgs mass.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Models and Their Two Scales

The gauge group emerging from the noncommutative geometry at the unification scale is

$$ G_{224}=SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)_C, \qquad g_L(M_U)=g_R(M_U)=g_4(M_U), $$

and one of the three versions has $G_{224D}=G_{224}\times D$, with $D$ parity. Breaking to the Standard Model at $M_R$ uses the matching conditions

$$ \frac{1}{g_1^2(M_R)}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{g_4^2(M_R)}+\frac{1}{g_R^2(M_R)}, \qquad \frac{1}{g_2^2(M_R)}=\frac{1}{g_L^2(M_R)}, \qquad \frac{1}{g_3^2(M_R)}=\frac{1}{g_4^2(M_R)}. $$

In their original form the models satisfy these with $M_U\sim10^{15}$ GeV and $M_R\sim10^{13}$ GeV. That high $M_R$ is the problem, because a $W_R$ at a few TeV would mean $M_R$ at a few TeV.

The paper's device is a chain with two extra steps and a separate D-parity scale,

$$ \mathrm{NCG}\Longrightarrow G_{224D}\xrightarrow{M_D}G_{224}\xrightarrow{M_C}G_{2213}\xrightarrow{M_R}G_{213}\xrightarrow{M_Z}G_{13}, $$

with $G_{2213}=SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_{B-L}\times SU(3)_C$ and the ordering $M_Z\leq M_R\leq M_C\leq M_D\leq M_U$ strictly maintained. Adjacent scales may coincide, which collapses a step.

The One Degree of Freedom the Corpus Does Not Have

The paper separates two things that a left–right model usually ties together: D-parity breaking at $M_D$ and $SU(2)_R$ breaking at $M_R$. Between them, $g_R\ne g_L$ is allowed even though the high-energy theory had $g_R=g_L$. The paper states the reason in a footnote that is worth quoting:

D-parity is slightly different from the usual Lorentz ($P$) parity in that the former interchanges the $SU(2)_L$ and $SU(2)_R$ sectors completely (including the scalars), while the latter does not transform the scalars. For example, the D-parity interchanges the $SU(2)_L$ Higgs fields and their $SU(2)_R$ counterparts, and transforms the bidoublet $\varphi$ into $\varphi^\dagger$ (and vice versa), while the $P$-parity leaves them unchanged.

That distinction is what makes the whole analysis possible. If the only parity available were $P$, the two couplings would be tied; with $D$ as a separate breaking event, they are not.

The Target and the Inputs

The target comes from the 2015 LHC data: ATLAS reported a $WZ$ excess near $2$ TeV with a local significance of $3.4\sigma$ and a global $2.5\sigma$, with smaller excesses in dijets and in $WH$. Fits to a left–right interpretation gave a $W_R$ with

$$ g_R(M_R)\simeq0.4, $$

against the Standard Model value $g_L(5\ \mathrm{TeV})\simeq0.63$. The required $g_R$ is smaller than $g_L$, which is exactly the asymmetry that $M_D\ne M_R$ permits.

The infrared inputs are $\alpha(M_Z)=1/127.9$, $\alpha_s(M_Z)=0.118$, $\sin^2\theta_W(M_Z)=0.2312$, translating to $g_1=0.36$, $g_2=0.65$, $g_3=1.22$, at $M_Z=91.1876$ GeV. The running is one-loop,

$$ \frac{1}{g_i^2(M_A)}-\frac{1}{g_i^2(M_B)}=\frac{a_i}{8\pi^2}\ln\frac{M_B}{M_A}, $$

with

$$ a_i=-\frac{11}{3}C_2(G_i)+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{R_f}T_i(R_f)\prod d(R_f)+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{R_s}T_i(R_s)\prod d(R_s), $$

and all couplings required to stay perturbative from $M_U$ down.

The Answer

Under the extended survival hypothesis, the three models give $M_R$ too large and $g_R$ too large. Relaxing the hypothesis by letting extra colour-triplet components of $\Delta_R$ survive in the intermediate interval improves both. The three best points, for Model C with sets of surviving fields, are

$$ g_R=0.51,\ M_R=5\times10^3\ \mathrm{GeV},\ M_C=8\times10^{15},\ M_U=M_D=6\times10^{17}, $$

$$ g_R=0.45,\ M_R=8\times10^5\ \mathrm{GeV},\ M_C=9\times10^{10},\ M_U=M_D=10^{19}, $$

$$ g_R=0.43,\ M_R=2\times10^5\ \mathrm{GeV},\ M_C=4\times10^9,\ M_U=M_D=10^{19}, $$

with the unified coupling $g_L=g_R=g_4\simeq0.51$–$0.52$. The paper's conclusion is stated without softening: generating a TeV-scale $W_R$ with $g_R=0.4$ "is not trivial and places strong constraints on the particle content and symmetry breaking scales", and the first point, the only one at $5$ TeV, needs $M_C\sim8\times10^{15}$ GeV and $M_U$ at $6\times10^{17}$ GeV, which is inside the Planck bound but on the wrong side of the pattern the model prefers.

The paper also checks proton stability, $\Delta B=2$ neutron–antineutron and hydrogen–antihydrogen oscillation, and inflationary constraints, and reports that none of them is prohibitive.

The Higgs Mass, and a Repair

The paper's review of the noncommutative program contains two numbers the corpus should keep. The first is a failure: the minimal version of the noncommutative Standard Model "predicts a Higgs mass of ∼ 170 GeV, in clear contradiction with experiment.12". The second is the repair: the fault is one off-diagonal entry of the Dirac operator, the one responsible for the neutrino Majorana mass, and turning that entry into a field — a singlet coupled to the Standard Model Higgs — brings the prediction to $126$ GeV. The paper notes that the same is achieved by extending the geometry to the left–right symmetric Pati–Salam type, "which automatically involves this singlet field".

The paper draws the moral: the necessity to enlarge the gauge symmetry to accommodate the Higgs mass "can be considered a strength rather than a weakness. It tells us that the approach is restrictive enough for the models to be confronted by experiment."

The Hierarchy Remark

The closing discussion argues that, because the noncommutative unification scale sits close to the Planck scale and the approach produces a gravity sector alongside the Standard Model, the hierarchy problem should be treated gravitationally rather than within effective field theory. One observation is geometric: the Higgs scale ($\sim1$ TeV) is the geometric mean of the Planck and Hubble scales, and in Connes' geometry all three appear as geometric scales. The paper also notes a scalar on the gravity side — a Brans–Dicke–Jordan-like field — which can warp the discrete Higgs direction, "similar to the infinite extra dimensional scenarios, however, without infinite extra dimensions".

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

  1. D-parity is not

$P$: the exchange includes the scalars

The idea. $D$ interchanges the two $SU(2)$ sectors completely, scalars included, and sends the bidoublet $\varphi\to\varphi^\dagger$. $P$ acts on the fermions and leaves the scalars alone.

What it would add. The exact content of the operation missing from the corpus's chiral wall. The corpus says the algebra does not choose a chirality. It does not say what "choosing" would mean, i.e. which operation would have to be a symmetry and is not. This paper supplies the distinction in a footnote, and the distinction is the whole point: the exchange that would relate the two chiralities has to move the scalars too, and the observed world is one in which the scalar sector is not paired that way.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md, the chiral-wall section, next to the D-parity material from the Aulakh conversion (hep-ph/0204097), which gives the explicit generator $D=\exp(-i\pi J_{23})\exp(i\pi J_{67})$ and the sign rules.

What stands in the way. Nothing conceptually; the corpus's frame has a bidoublet and could say what $\varphi\to\varphi^\dagger$ does to it. That is a concrete sentence the corpus can write today.

  1. One group does not have to mean one coupling

The idea. $g_L(M_U)=g_R(M_U)$ at unification, and $g_L(M_R)\ne g_R(M_R)$ at the breaking scale, because D-parity breaking at $M_D$ is a separate event from $SU(2)_R$ breaking at $M_R$. The chain has five scales and the couplings are equal only at the top.

What it would add. A way out of the corpus's single-coupling assumption, which is the source of its $\sin^2\theta_W$ failure. The Pati–Salam article assumes $g_3=g_L=g_R$ at $M_{PS}$ and gets $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38$. Here the equality holds only at $M_U$, and two intermediate steps separate $M_U$ from $M_Z$.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article, the paragraph stating the unification condition and its assumption.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no running and no scales. What it can record is the logical point, which is short: symmetry at the top does not imply equality at the bottom; between the two the symmetry may be broken in stages. That belongs in the ledger as a note on why the failure may be an artefact of the single-step reading.

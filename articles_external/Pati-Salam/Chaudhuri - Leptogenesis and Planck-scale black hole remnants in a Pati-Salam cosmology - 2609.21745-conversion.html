What the Paper Does

The paper puts primordial black holes inside a Pati–Salam cosmology and finds that the gauge structure is what constrains the cosmological parameters — not the other way round.

The setup: Pati–Salam breaks before inflation, so monopoles are diluted. A singlet inflaton with a near-inflection potential enhances the small-scale curvature power spectrum enough to form a narrow black hole population that briefly dominates the energy density. The black holes evaporate, emitting heavy right-handed neutrinos, and a lepton asymmetry forms. If quantum-gravitational backreaction halts evaporation at the Planck scale, each hole leaves a stable remnant that is a dark-matter candidate.

Two results carry the paper.

First, the embedding is a constraint, not a decoration. Because the right-handed neutrino mass is $M_{N}=y_Rv_R$ with $v_R$ the $SU(2)_R$ breaking scale, and because the plasma must not restore the symmetry, both $v_R$ and $M_{N}$ are tied to the black-hole mass through the formation temperature. That forces

$$ y_R\lesssim 2.3\times10^{-5}\left(\frac{M_{N1}}{10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV}}\right)\left(\frac{M_{\rm in}}{1\,\mathrm{g}}\right)^{1/2}, $$

which is quoted as $y_R\lesssim 0.11$ and $y_R\lesssim 6.5\times10^{-4}$ at the two benchmarks. "The RHN mass is thus required to sit well below the $SU(2)_R$ breaking scale, which is a genuine prediction of embedding the mechanism in a pre-inflationary Pati–Salam cosmology rather than an assumption."

Second, the dark matter and the asymmetry cannot both come from the same black holes unless the thermal channel is used. The remnant fraction scales as $M_{\rm in}^{-5/2}$, so suppressing it needs heavier holes; non-thermal leptogenesis needs lighter ones (below about $10^2$ g). "Remnant dark matter and non-thermal leptogenesis are mutually exclusive." With the thermal channel, a single mass near $10^6$ g does both.

The Chain and the Fermions

$$ G_{\rm PS}=SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R, \qquad F_L\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1),\quad F_R\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2), $$

"where the numbers denote representations under SU (4)C , SU (2)L , and SU (2)R , respectively. Because the right-handed multiplet FR = (uR , dR , νR , eR ) explicitly contains the right-handed neutrino νR ≡ NR without additional assumptions, the existence of heavy Majorana righthanded neutrinos is a rigorous structural prediction of the gauge symmetry rather than an ad hoc extension of the Standard Model. Thus, the PS framework"

The breaking chain is the standard two-step one,

$$ SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\to SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\to SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{\rm em}, $$

with "the first step ... at a scale $v_R$ associated with the breaking of $SU(2)_R$ ... driven by a scalar multiplet $\Delta_R\sim(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$" and "the hypercharge generator $Y=T_{3R}+(B-L)/2$ arising from the appropriate combination of the $SU(2)_R$ and $B-L$ generators".

Note that the $\Delta_R$ here is in the $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ — a tensor of $SU(4)$, not a fundamental. That is a different choice from the spectral papers, which use a $(\mathbf{2},\mathbf1,\mathbf4)$ or a bidoublet, and it is what allows the renormalisable Majorana coupling

$$ \mathcal L_Y\supset y_R\,F_R^T\,C^{-1}\Delta_R\,F_R+{\rm h.c.} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M_N=y_Rv_R,\quad \langle\Delta_R\rangle=v_R. $$

A $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ times itself is $\mathbf{10}+\mathbf6$, so a $\mathbf{10}$ is the smallest scalar that can give a renormalisable Majorana mass. The paper says so indirectly, by noting that Eq. (2.5) holds "up to an O(1) normalization factor arising from the triplet convention, which –4– we absorb into the definition of yR . Thus"

D-parity is broken explicitly at a high scale to suppress the left-handed triplet $\Delta_L\sim(\mathbf{10},\mathbf3,\mathbf1)$, so "we therefore work in the type-I seesaw-dominated limit". The Dirac mass is $m_D=y_\nu v_{\rm EW}/\sqrt2$ and

$$ m_\nu\simeq-m_D^TM_N^{-1}m_D \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y_\nu^2\simeq\frac{m_\nu M_{N1}}{v^2},\qquad v\simeq174\ \mathrm{GeV}, $$

so

$$ y_\nu\simeq1.3\times10^{-2}\left(\frac{M_{N1}}{10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{m_\nu}{0.05\,\mathrm{eV}}\right)^{1/2}. $$

The Cosmological Consistency Conditions

Three inequalities do all the work.

Symmetry not restored. Breaking before inflation requires $H_{\rm inf}<v_R$, but the authors are careful that this is not enough: "Since the PS-breaking transition occurs before inflation, any topological defects regenerated after inflation would no longer be diluted by subsequent inflation. The relevant requirement is therefore that the post-inflationary plasma never reaches a temperature high enough to restore the PS symmetry", i.e.

$$ v_R\gtrsim\max(T_{\rm RH},T_f), $$

and with $T_{\rm RH}\simeq T_f$ this becomes $v_R\gtrsim T_f$ where

$$ T_f\simeq4.36\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{GeV}\left(\frac{1\,\mathrm{g}}{M_{\rm in}}\right)^{1/2}. $$

That is the paper's central inequality, and it is a lower bound on the unification-type scale that tightens as the black hole gets lighter. At $M_{\rm in}\simeq1.2\times10^6$ g it requires $v_R\gtrsim4\times10^{12}$ GeV; at $1.3\times10^2$ g, $v_R\gtrsim3.8\times10^{14}$ GeV. "Sub-gram PBHs push $v_R$ to within an order of magnitude of the Planck scale and are therefore disfavoured within a pre-inflationary breaking scenario."

Black holes hot enough. $T_{\rm BH}=M_p^2/M_{\rm BH}\simeq10^{13}\,\mathrm{GeV}(1\,\mathrm{g}/M_{\rm BH})$, and $T_{\rm BH}\gtrsim M_{N1}=y_Rv_R$ gives

$$ M_{\rm in}\lesssim\frac{M_p^2}{y_Rv_R}\simeq10^2\,\mathrm{g}\left(\frac{10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV}}{y_Rv_R}\right), $$

with the caveat that "18) is an efficiency criterion and not a hard kinematic cut. Emission above threshold is Boltzmann suppressed rather than forbidden"

The Remnants

"stable, non-radiating remnant may form with mass mrem = rMPl , where r ≥ 1 is an order-one coefficient, whose precise value is not predicted by any of the candidate frameworks and which we therefore retain as a free parameter throughout." "we therefore retain as a free parameter throughout.". One remnant per hole, so $n_{\rm rem}(t_{\rm ev})=n_{\rm BH}(t_{\rm ev})$, and the present fraction goes as

$$ f_{\rm rem}\propto\frac{m_{\rm rem}}{M_{\rm in}}\propto M_{\rm in}^{-5/2}. $$

Suppose the remnants are all the dark matter; then $r=1$ requires $M_{\rm in}\simeq1.1\times10^6$ g, and in general

$$ r=\left(\frac{M_{\rm in}}{1.1\times10^6\,\mathrm{g}}\right)^{5/2}. $$

"The coefficient $r$ is not predicted by any of the frameworks in which remnants arise. Hence it can be tuned according to the initial mass to achieve the correct DM relic density." And the tuning is one-sided: "for lighter black holes, it would demand $r<1$ which is not allowed and hence the remnants overclose the Universe. Because the remnant fraction falls as the five-halves power of the initial mass, a reduction of the mass by only ten percent is enough to overclose the Universe for any permitted $r$".

The exponent $5/2$ is the paper's sharpest number. It comes from $f_{\rm rem}\propto1/M_{\rm in}$ times the scaling of the relic abundance with mass. A ten-percent change in $M_{\rm in}$ changes the dark matter density by about twenty-five percent, and there is no free parameter to absorb it because $r\ge1$ is bounded below.

Hence the mutual exclusion: non-thermal leptogenesis needs $M_{\rm in}\lesssim10^2$ g, and $10^2$ g gives $f_{\rm rem}$ larger than one by a factor of $(10^6/10^2)^{5/2}=10^{10}$.

The Gravitational Waves

Three channels, and the paper is clear that two are hopeless.

Scalar-induced. $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim\mathcal P_\zeta^2$ at the peak, redshifted to "$10^4$–$10^8$ Hz ... far above the band of any current or planned interferometer".

  1. The Majorana mass is the breaking scale, so the see-saw scale is not free

The idea. $M_N=y_Rv_R$ with $y_R$ a Yukawa coupling, and the paper then shows $y_R\ll1$ from cosmology, so $M_N\ll v_R$. But the identification $M_N\sim v_R$ at order one is the input, and everything else is a correction to it.

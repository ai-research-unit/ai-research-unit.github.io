What the Paper Does

It takes the minimal renormalisable Pati–Salam theory — the one with doublet Higgs fields doing the breaking — and works out what it implies for cosmology.

"We discuss a minimal renormalizable Pati-Salam theory based on the SU (4)C × SU (2)L × SU (2)R gauge group, with unification scale Higgs multiplets taken as SU (2)L and SU (2)R doublets, which lead to neutrino Dirac picture. Although a number"

"Although a number of scalar particles could be light, even lying at the LHC energies, the unification scale is hopelessly out of reach in any foreseeable future. Moreover, phase transition in the early Universe leads to the production of magnetic monopoles and domain walls, both incompatible with the standard cosmological model."

So the theory is unobservable at colliders and excluded by cosmology — and the paper's contribution is that the exclusion is diagnosable and the repair is predictive.

"A small explicit breaking of the discrete left-right symmetry allows the domain walls to decay, and in the process possibly sweep away the monopoles, analogously" "This leaves an important imprint of gravitational waves, within the reach of next generation searches, correlated with monopole detection and new light particles at collider energies."

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why the Choice of Higgs Multiplet Matters So Much

"with unification scale Higgs multiplets taken as $SU(2)_L$ and $SU(2)_R$ doublets, which lead to neutrino Dirac picture."

This is the crux and it is worth stating carefully. In Pati–Salam the $B-L$ breaking can be done by different scalar representations, and the choice determines the neutrino mass mechanism:

  • A $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ or $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ gives a large Majorana mass to $\nu_R$ → type-I seesaw → Majorana neutrinos.
  • A $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ or doublet-like field gives no large $\nu_R$ mass → neutrinos are Dirac, with the small mass coming from a small Yukawa.

So the Higgs sector choice is not cosmetic; it decides whether neutrinos are their own antiparticles. The corpus's article treats the seesaw as automatic in Pati–Salam, and this paper shows it is not: the minimal renormalisable model with doublets gives Dirac neutrinos.

"the Dirac neutrino picture, the latter to the Majorana one based on the seesaw mechanism. Over the years" "in the absence of any experimental hint of the neutrino Majorana feature, it is worthwhile to also consider the Dirac picture after all, it was the original one [4, 5]. 1 We choose P for definiteness."

That is a fair historical point: the seesaw was introduced because it explains smallness, not because data demanded Majorana. Given that $0\nu\beta\beta$ has not been seen, the Dirac option deserves the same attention.

And a Dirac neutrino mass needs a tiny Yukawa ($\sim10^{-12}$), which is a different naturalness problem than the seesaw's large $M_R$. The paper accepts it.

The Unification Scale, and Why

It Is Hopeless

"PS = MXPS scale we found in the 1-loop analysis is lowered, at most, by roughly 10%. Namely, assuming 2−loop all the new scalar states are at MPS , we find MPS ≃ 1−loop 13 4.6×10 GeV as opposed to the MPS ≃ 5×1013 GeV. ACKNOWLEDGMENTS"

"r ones are also light, around TeV scale, we get the lower bound on PS scale We thank Borut Bajc for useful"

So the scale is $10^{11}$–$10^{13}$ GeV — high enough that no collider reaches it, low enough to be interesting for cosmology. That combination is exactly what makes the paper work: a scale too high to produce directly is still low enough to have left a phase-transition imprint.

"WLEDGMENTS For arbitrary scalar mass thresholds, a light (3C , 2L )7/6Y has the largest effect in lowering MPS , just like in the one-loop."

A scalar with electric charge $7/6$ — that is a doubly-charged-plus-one object, a distinctive prediction, and it is the state that pulls the unification scale down. The corpus should record it: a $7/6$ scalar is a signature that would identify this model at a collider even though the unification scale is unreachable.

The Cosmological Disaster

"phase transition in the early Universe leads to the production of magnetic monopoles and domain walls, both incompatible with the standard cosmological model."

Both, and for different reasons: - Monopoles: $SU(4)\to SU(3)\times U(1)$ leaves a monopole, and the Kibble mechanism produces them in abundance unless inflation or a later dilution removes them. - Domain walls: the discrete $L\leftrightarrow R$ parity is spontaneously broken, and the resulting walls would dominate the energy density.

The domain-wall problem is the worse one because walls do not dilute with expansion the way monopoles do — they scale as $1/a$ in energy density while matter scales as $1/a^3$, so they come to dominate. A stable domain wall is fatal, not merely problematic.

The Repair, and Why

It Gives Gravitational Waves

"A small explicit breaking of the discrete left-right symmetry allows the domain walls to decay, and in the process possibly sweep away the monopoles."

The mechanism is worth spelling out: a small bias term makes the walls unstable, they annihilate, and as they do the monopoles are swept up and annihilated at the wall boundaries. One bias fixes both problems.

And the wall annihilation produces gravitational waves, because the walls carry energy and their collapse is violent:

$$ \Omega_{\rm GW}h^2|_{\rm peak}=3\times10^{-7}\left(\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{\rm ann})}{100}\right)^{-4/3}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\rm Wall}}{10^{10}\ {\rm GeV}^3}\right)^2\left(\frac{10^{13}\ {\rm GeV}}{T_{\rm ann}}\right)^2, $$

$$ 4\times10^{11}\ {\rm GeV}\lesssim\sigma_{\rm Wall}\sim M_{PS}\lesssim10^{13}\ {\rm GeV},\qquad 10^8\ {\rm GeV}\lesssim T_{\rm ann}\lesssim10^{10}\ {\rm GeV}, $$

$$ 10\ {\rm Hz}\lesssim f_{\rm peak}\lesssim10^3\ {\rm Hz}. $$

That frequency range is the important output: 10 Hz to 1 kHz is where DECIGO, BBO, the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer operate, and it is above LISA's band. So the prediction is aimed at the next generation of ground-based and space-based detectors, and it is a range rather than a point.

"the amplitude of the signal becomes independent of the underlying model parameters and is represented by the horizontal blue line in Fig. 2."

That is the strongest form of prediction: not a curve depending on inputs, but a plateau that follows from the cosmology alone. **And the paper notes that moving off it "likely reintroduces the monopole problem"

**, so the model's own consistency pins the signal.

The corpus should record the logic: a theory too heavy to test at colliders becomes testable through a phase transition, and the phase transition's consistency with monopole bounds fixes the gravitational-wave amplitude.

The Genesis Problem, Stated Honestly

"Since the authors do not adhere to any of the religious paradigms of genesis (they are open minded though), they are compelled to look for alternative explanations."

"The former one is absent here - we are in the neutrino Dirac picture with conserved baryon and lepton numbers. The only other alternative is the electroweak genesis, and it is surely possible." "all that is needed then is an additional light doublet - which can then account for the first order phase transition at the electroweak temperatures. We"

That is a real consequence of the Dirac choice: leptogenesis is unavailable, because lepton number is conserved. So the baryon asymmetry must come from electroweak baryogenesis, which needs a first-order transition, which needs a light doublet — and the theory has plenty. One choice (doublet Higgs) propagates to neutrino nature, to the absence of leptogenesis, and to the baryogenesis mechanism.

The joke about religious paradigms is the author's style and worth keeping — it is also a fair comment on how the field talks about "genesis".

What the Paper Does

A string construction. Seven stacks of D6-branes wrap rigid 3-cycles on the $T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2')$ orientifold, and the gauge group that comes out is four-generation Pati–Salam. Because the cycles are rigid, "there are no extra massless fields in the adjoint representation, arising as open-string moduli" — and that is the point, because those adjoints "would create difficulties with asymptotic freedom as well as the prediction of gauge unification."

The model then breaks to the MSSM, and the breaking is the interesting part: since there are no adjoints, the standard brane-splitting mechanism is unavailable and the breaking has to happen through the Higgs mechanism with bifundamental states from a non-chiral sector. The hypercharge that survives is orthogonal to four $U(1)$ factors that become massive by a generalised Green–Schwarz mechanism, so those four survive as global symmetries rather than gauge symmetries. All MSSM Yukawa couplings are allowed by those global symmetries, and at tree level $\frac53g_s^2=g_w^2=g_Y^2$.

The paper is a string-theory existence proof with one defect the authors name themselves: four generations, not three.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why Rigid Cycles

The motivation is stated as a problem with earlier models:

"For Pati-Salam models constructed from bulk D-branes wrapping non-rigid cycles, the gauge symmetry may be broken to the MSSM by the process of brane splitting, which corresponds to assigning a VEV to an adjoint scalar in the field theoretic description. However, this option is not available in the present construction since the adjoint fields have been eliminated due to the rigidization of the cycles."

So rigidity buys the absence of adjoints and costs the breaking mechanism. That trade is the paper's structure: everything follows from it.

The consolation is that "light non-chiral matter in the bifundamental representation may still appear between pairs of fractional branes", and "these non-chiral states smoothly connect the configuration of fractional D-branes to one consisting of non-rigid D-branes." The computation of the overlap,

$$ \tilde A_{a_ia_j}=\int_0^\infty\!dl\,\langle a_i|e^{-2\pi lH_{\rm cl}}|a_j\rangle+\int_0^\infty\!dl\,\langle a_j|e^{-2\pi lH_{\rm cl}}|a_i\rangle, $$

with loop-channel amplitude $\int_0^\infty\frac{dl}{l}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{ij+ji}\frac{1+\theta+\omega+\theta\omega}{4}e^{-2\pi lH_{\rm cl}}$, gives "one massless hypermultiplet ... Thus, the required states to play the role of the Higgs fields are present in this non-chiral sector."

And the honest caveat: "In principle, one should determine that there are flat directions that can give the necessary VEV's to these states. This process would correspond geometrically to a particular brane recombination, where the CFT techniques fail and only a field theory analysis of D- and F-flat directions is applicable ... Such computations are technically very involved and beyond the scope of the present work."

The Breaking, and the Hypercharge

The stacks separate as

$$ \alpha\to\alpha_B+\alpha_L, \qquad \beta\to\beta_{r1}+\beta_{r2}, $$

but "This does not mean that the stacks are located at different points in the internal space. After all, there are no adjoint scalars which may receive a VEV. Rather, this separation reflects that there has been a spontaneous breaking of the Pati-Salam gauge symmetry down to the MSSM by the Higgs mechanism, where we have identified the Higgs states with $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ representations of $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ present in the non-chiral sector."

That sentence is worth keeping. A split of stacks usually means a geometric separation, which requires an adjoint. Here the split is a bookkeeping device for a Higgs vev, and the paper says so explicitly so that no one reads geometry into it.

The resulting group is $SU(3)\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\times SU(2)_4\times U(1)_8$ with

$$ Q_Y=\frac16\big(U(1)_{\alpha_B}-3U(1)_{\alpha_L}-3U(1)_{\beta_{r1}}+3U(1)_{\beta_{r2}}\big), $$

"Of course, this is just

$$ Q_Y=\frac{Q_B-Q_L}{2}+Q_{I3R}, $$

where $Q_B$ and $Q_L$ are baryon number and lepton number respectively, while $Q_{I3R}$ is like the third component of right-handed weak isospin."

Note the coefficients: $+1$ on the colour stack, $-3$ on each of the three others, all over $6$. That is $B-L$ in a non-standard normalisation, and the $-3$ is the $U(1)$ charge of the lepton under $SU(4)$'s $\lambda_{15}$ — the same arithmetic as $\mathrm{diag}(3,-1,-1,-1)$ in the spectral papers, written in a different basis.

The four massless combinations are orthogonal to $Q_Y$, and "these $U(1)$'s remain to all orders as global symmetries". The explicit forms are given, e.g.

$$ U(1)_A=3U(1)_{\alpha_B}-U(1)_{\alpha_L}-U(1)_{\beta_{r1}}-U(1)_{\beta_{r2}}+2U(1)_\gamma-2U(1)_1+2U(1)_2+2U(1)_3+2U(1)_4, $$

with three more of the same shape differing in signs. Four global $U(1)$s, each a different sign pattern over the same eight stacks — that is why the Yukawa couplings survive: the charges are not all the same, so a product of fields can be neutral.

Yukawa Couplings

$$ W_Y=y_uH_uQU^c+y_dH_dQD^c+y_lH_dLE^c, $$

"keeping in mind that all of the MSSM fields are charged under the global symmetries ... Typically, this results in the forbidding of some if not all of the desired Yukawa couplings. In this case, all of the Yukawa couplings are allowed by the global symmetries including a trilinear Dirac mass term for neutrinos, $W_D=\lambda_\nu LNH_u$."

And the neutrino sector: "By itself, such a term would imply neutrino masses of the order of the quarks and charged leptons. However, if in addition there exist a Majorana mass term for the right-handed neutrinos, $W_m=M_mNN$, a see-saw mechanism may be employed. Such a mass term may in principle be generated by $E2$ instanton effects. This mechanism may also be employed to generate a $\mu$-term of the order of the EW scale."

Both the Majorana mass and the $\mu$ term from the same instanton source — a coincidence the paper notes without explaining.

The exotics: "there is additional vector-like matter transforming under the 'hidden' gauge group $U(2)_1\otimes U(2)_2\otimes U(2)_3\otimes U(2)_4$. By choosing appropriate flat directions, we may deform the fractional cycles ... such that $U(2)_1\otimes U(2)_2\to U(1)$; $U(2)_3\otimes U(2)_4\to U(1)$." And crucially: "The remaining eight pairs of exotic colour triplets present in the model resulting from the breaking $\mathbf6\to\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}$, while not truly vector-like due to their different charges under the global symmetries, may in principle become massive via instanton effects."

Gauge Coupling Unification

"in intersecting D-brane models, the gauge groups arise from different stacks of branes, and so they will not generally have the same volume in the compactified space. Thus, the gauge couplings are not automatically unified."

The gauge kinetic function for a stack $P$ is

$$ f_P=\frac{1}{2\pi l_s}\Big(\int_{\Pi_P}\Omega_3-i\int_{\Pi_P}C_3\Big) $$

in general, and for $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ with $\theta_P=0$,

$$ f_P=n^1_Pn^2_Pn^3_Ps-n^1_Pm^2_Pm^3_Pu_1-n^2_Pm^1_Pm^3_Pu_2-n^3_Pm^1_Pm^2_Pu_3, $$

with $g_{D6,P}^{-2}=|\mathrm{Re}(f_P)|$.

Then the identification: "$SU(3)$ holomorphic gauge function with stack $\alpha_B$, the $SU(2)$ ... with stack $\gamma$", and the hypercharge function as the linear combination

$$ f_Y=\tfrac16f_{\alpha_B}+\tfrac12f_{\alpha_L}+\tfrac12f_{\beta_{r1}}+\tfrac12f_{\beta_{r2}}, $$

so "the tree-level MSSM gauge couplings will be unified at the string scale

$$ \tfrac53g_s^2=g_w^2=g_Y^2, $$

since each stack will have the same gauge kinetic function."

The $\tfrac53$ is the standard $SU(5)$ normalisation, and it appears here by accident of the coefficients in $f_Y$ rather than by grand unification. The corpus's Pati–Salam article discusses $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38$ and $\frac53$; here the $\frac53$ falls out of brane wrapping numbers.

The Admission

"The main drawback of this model is that there are four generations of MSSM matter. However, the existence of a possible fourth generation is rather tightly constrained, although it is not completely ruled out." And then the speculation: "The emergence of three light generations may in fact be correlated with the existence of three twisted sectors."

Also flagged as future work: "the presence of discrete torsion will complexify the Yukawa couplings and thereby introduce CP violation into the CKM matrix".

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. Rigid cycles kill the adjoints, and the breaking mechanism dies with them

The idea. Non-rigid cycles give adjoint moduli, which spoil asymptotic freedom and unification, but which also allow brane splitting — the geometric way to break the group. Rigid cycles remove the moduli and the mechanism, so the breaking must be done by bifundamental Higgses from a non-chiral sector.

What it would add. A worked example of a trade the corpus's frame makes silently. The corpus's Pati–Salam article wants both a clean adjoint sector (no extra scalars) and a breaking mechanism. Here the two are shown to be the same object: the adjoint is what breaks the group geometrically, and removing it removes the geometry and forces a Higgs. You cannot have an adjoint-free model and a geometric breaking. That is a structural constraint the corpus should state.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the breaking section, and articles_physics/the-spectral-triple-of-the-biquaternion-algebra.md — the spectral papers get their breaking from $A^{(2)}$, which is the same trade made a different way.

What stands in the way. The argument is specific to intersecting-brane model building, and "rigid cycle" has no corpus analogue. Translate it: the general statement is that a scalar that breaks a group by a vev is either an adjoint (which comes from moduli and is therefore not optional) or a bifundamental (which must be put in by hand). That general statement is corpus-ready.

2. A stack split that is not a geometric split

The idea. $\alpha\to\alpha_B+\alpha_L$ and $\beta\to\beta_{r1}+\beta_{r2}$ are written as though the stacks separate, but "This does not mean that the stacks are located at different points in the internal space ... Rather, this separation reflects that there has been a spontaneous breaking".

What it would add. A warning about a notation that misleads. The corpus's Pati–Salam article writes $SU(4)\to SU(3)\times U(1)$ as a decomposition of a group; here the same step is written as a decomposition of a brane stack, and the two notations look alike while meaning different things. The decomposition is algebraic; the geometry is unchanged. That distinction is worth one sentence in the corpus because it is easy to get wrong when reading either literature.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's breaking section.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

3. The hypercharge coefficients $+\frac16,-3\times\frac16$ and the $\frac53$

The idea. $Q_Y=\frac16(Q_{\alpha_B}-3Q_{\alpha_L}-3Q_{\beta_{r1}}+3Q_{\beta_{r2}})$ and, at tree level, $\frac53g_s^2=g_w^2=g_Y^2$.

What it would add. A third independent derivation of the $\frac53$. The corpus has it from $SU(5)$ normalisation and from $\mathrm{tr}(\lambda_{15}^2)=2$; here it comes from the coefficients in $f_Y$, which are fixed by the requirement that $Q_Y$ be $B-L+T_{3R}$. Three routes, one number — and the fact that the brane wrapping numbers deliver it without any grand-unified input is a check the corpus can cite.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's normalisation paragraph, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-the-neutral-current-and-the-z-coupling.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The $-\frac{3}{6}=-$ the lepton charge matches the spectral paper's $\mathrm{diag}(3,-1,-1,-1)$ up to the overall $1/6$ and a sign convention, which the corpus can check and state.

4. Four global $U(1)$s from a Green–Schwarz mechanism, and Yukawas that survive

The idea. Up to twelve $U(1)$s can become massive; four remain massless because they are orthogonal to $Q_Y$ and to each other. They act as global symmetries, and the MSSM Yukawas are allowed because the fields' charges are not all equal.

What it would add. A different, and in a sense cheaper, answer to the flavour problem than the corpus's continuous symmetries. Barbieri uses $U(2)^5$; Blanke uses alignment; Braam uses H-parity; here the symmetries are accidental byproducts of anomaly cancellation and are global rather than gauged. Protection without a symmetry postulate — the symmetry exists because the string demanded it, not because a model builder added it.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's flavour paragraph, now with four distinct mechanisms listed.

What stands in the way. Global symmetries are expected to be violated by quantum gravity, and the paper does not address this. The corpus should record the mechanism and the objection together.

5. Eight exotic colour triplets from $\mathbf6\to\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}$

The idea. The $SU(4)$ antisymmetric $\mathbf6$ of the PS spectrum breaks as $\mathbf6\to\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}$ under $SU(3)$, giving exotic colour triplets which are "not truly vector-like due to their different charges under the global symmetries" and must be made heavy by instantons.

What it would add. A concrete realisation of the corpus's leptoquark/diquark bookkeeping. The $\mathbf6$ of $SU(4)$ is the $(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$-like object, and its colour-triplet components have diquark-type couplings. Here they are not optional — a $\mathbf6$ is in the spectrum, and it must be given a mass. That is exactly the proton-decay problem the corpus discusses, met in a string model where the $\mathbf6$ is not a choice.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's exotic-sector discussion.

What stands in the way. The paper's solution (instantons) is not a solution the corpus can use. Import the problem, note the solution is string-specific.

One observation about the corpus's map

This is the first paper in the batch where the Pati–Salam group arises from a compactification rather than from a gauge-theory postulate. The group is not chosen; it is what seven stacks of D6-branes on a specific orientifold produce, and the four generations are what their intersection numbers produce. That makes the four-generation problem a prediction rather than a failure of model building — the authors cannot change it without changing the compactification.

The corpus's Pati–Salam article treats the group as an ansatz and asks what it explains. This paper treats it as an output and asks what it costs. Both are legitimate, and the corpus should record which question it is answering, because the answer to "why Pati–Salam?" is different in the two cases: in the corpus it is charge quantisation and the anomaly identities; here it is that the orientifold gives it.

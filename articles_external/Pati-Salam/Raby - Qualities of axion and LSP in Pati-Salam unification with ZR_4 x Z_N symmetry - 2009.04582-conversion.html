Summary in the Authors' Words

The Axion Quality Problem, Which Is the Real Subject

"The axion quality problem" is named as the target, and it is the reason for the whole construction.

The problem, stated plainly: the PQ symmetry must be exact to about one part in $10^{10}$ for the axion to solve strong CP. But quantum gravity is expected to violate all global symmetries, so Planck-suppressed operators like $\Phi^n/M_P^{n-4}$ should appear and shift the axion potential minimum away from $\theta=0$. Unless something forbids them, the axion does not solve the problem it was invented for.

A gauge symmetry can forbid such operators because gauge symmetries are respected by gravity. So the standard fix is to make the PQ symmetry arise accidentally from a gauged discrete symmetry. That is exactly what this paper does, and it is the correct approach.

The corpus should record the logic, because it is a constraint on any Pati–Salam axion model (including 2005.00012 in this batch, the "Pati-Salam Axion" paper): $$\text{PQ must be exact to }10^{-10}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{a global symmetry cannot be exact}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{PQ must be accidental from a gauge symmetry.}$$ That syllogism is the reason Z_4^R\times Z_N appears, and it is more important than the model's specific field content.

Why $Z_4^R$ Specifically "The

$Z_4^R$ symmetry is a unique anomaly-free symmetry consistent with the PS unification which can forbid the dimension-4 and dimension-5 operators responsible for proton decay, as well as the mass term of the Higgs doublets at the Planck scale."

Three jobs again, and the word" unique "is the striking one. Among discrete $R$-symmetries compatible with $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$ and anomaly-free, $Z_4^R$ is the only one that forbids dim-4 and dim-5 proton decay and the Planck-scale $\mu$ term. That is a genuine derivation rather than a choice, and the corpus should record it: Pati–Salam plus anomaly freedom plus proton stability plus no $\mu$ problem selects $Z_4^R$ uniquely. Very few discrete symmetries in this literature are forced; this one is." Without the PQ field, R-parity exists exactly if the ZR 4 symmetry is broken by non-perturbative effects associated with SUSY breaking, and thus the Lightest Supersymmetric Particle (LSP) will contribute to the Dark Matter (DM)."

So $R$-parity is not imposed; it is a residual of $Z_4^R$ after SUSY breaking. That is the same argument as the automatic Nelson–Barr in 2604.00166: an accidental symmetry from a broken discrete symmetry is more robust than one imposed by hand.

The Tension Between the Axion and

$R$-Parity "the PQ field carries charges under both ZR 4 and ZN symmetries, the non-zero Vacuum Expectation Value (VEV) of it will break the discrete symmetries. Hence, there can be R-parity Violation (RPV) due to the spontaneous breaking of the ZR 4 symmetry, if the PQ field carries odd R-charge."

That is the paper's central tension and it is a real one. The axion needs a field with a large VEV that breaks the discrete symmetries. If that field has odd $R$-charge, breaking $Z_4^R$ breaks $R$-parity, and the LSP decays. So the axion and the dark matter candidate are in conflict, structurally, and the paper's two models are the two resolutions:"t the RPV effect is so suppressed that the lifetime of the LSP is much longer than the age of universe. In this case, the low-energy R-parity is accidental, but is high quality such that the LSP contributes to the DM."

"t the RPV is so large that the LSP decays before Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN), and thus the LSP is not the DM, i.e. the R-parity is low quality. The intermediate case is excluded by experiments [29–33]."

"

The intermediate case is excluded by experiments."

That last sentence is the sharpest physics statement in the paper. An LSP with a lifetime between $10^{-2}$ s and $10^{13}$ s disrupts BBN: it decays during or after nucleosynthesis and destroys the light-element abundances. So there is a window of RPV that is simply forbidden, and the model must sit at one end or the other.

The corpus should record that window, because it constrains every model with an unstable LSP and it is not obvious: partial R-parity violation is worse than none.

The Two Models and the Surprise

"odels. In the minimal model, without H, Σ, P , the anomaly conditions require that the MSSM particles do not carry ZN charges, so that the R-parity is respected very accurately and the LSP is sufficiently stable to be the DM."

So anomaly freedom, not preference, forces the MSSM to be $Z_N$-neutral in the minimal model. That is a nice example of anomaly conditions doing model selection: you want the MSSM charged to have freedom, and the anomaly conditions say no.

"An interesting feature of the RPV case is that the exotic vector-like triplets (σ, σ) and bi-doublet H are predicted to have SUSY breaking scale masses. Since the vector-like triplets may decay through the Yukawa couplings which are also induced by the SUSY breaking effects, the SUSY breaking scale is predicted to be larger than O (100 TeV). With the discrete"

That is a genuine prediction from a failure mode. If you take the RPV branch, the exotic states must be at the SUSY-breaking scale, and their non-observation pushes that scale above $100$ TeV — which excludes the standard low-scale SUSY spectrum.

So the two branches give: low-scale SUSY with the LSP as dark matter, or high-scale SUSY ($>100$ TeV) with the LSP decaying. The paper does not choose, and the choice is experimentally resolvable.

The Forward Look

"there are self-couplings of the PQ field $P$ at very high-order. It was recently proposed that the baryon asymmetry can be produced through the motion of a PQ field when kicked by an A-term of the self-coupling $P^n$, so-called lepto-axiogenesis. Our models may provide concrete examples which can accommodate the lepto-axiogenesis scenario."

Lepto-axiogenesis is the modern alternative to leptogenesis, and this model can host it. So the Raby programme has two baryogenesis routes in this batch: right-handed sneutrino leptogenesis (1612.04382) and lepto-axiogenesis here, depending on which model.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 2005.00012 are the batch's two axion-in-Pati–Salam papers and they differ in exactly the way that matters.

2005.00012 (Luzio et al.): the axion is built into the Pati–Salam structure, and the question is whether the axion's couplings respect the unified symmetry.

Here: the axion is added as an invisible-axion sector, and the question is whether the PQ symmetry itself survives Planck-suppressed operators — the quality problem.

Both questions are necessary and neither paper asks the other's. A complete Pati–Salam axion model would have to answer both: does the axion multiplet fit the unified representations, and is its shift symmetry protected from gravity? The corpus should present the two together and note that neither paper is sufficient alone.

The second point: the BBN window for LSP decay is the sort of bound that should be in the corpus's summary rather than in a single paper's section, because it silently rules out a whole class of models that appear viable in isolation. Partial $R$-parity violation looks like a small perturbation and is actually fatal.

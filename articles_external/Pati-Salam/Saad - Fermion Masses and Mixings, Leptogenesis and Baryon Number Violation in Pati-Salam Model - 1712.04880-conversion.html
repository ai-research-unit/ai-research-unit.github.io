What the Paper Does

It adds a Peccei–Quinn symmetry to Pati–Salam and gets the axion, the fermion masses, leptogenesis, and observable baryon violation from one model.

"a predictive model based on a partially unified theory possessing the gauge symmetry of the Pati-Salam group, SU (2)L × SU (2)R × SU (4)C supplemented by a global Peccei-Quinn symmetry, U (1)P Q . A comprehensive"

"A comprehensive analysis of the Higgs potential is carried out in a minimal set-up."

"The assumed Peccei-Quinn symmetry along with solving the strong CP problem, can provide axion as the dark matter candidate."

"This minimal set-up with limited number of Yukawa parameters can successfully incorporate the hierarchies in the charged fermion masses and mixings."

"The automatic existence of the heavy Majorana neutrinos generate the extremely small light neutrino masses through the seesaw mechanism, which is also responsible for producing the observed cosmological matter-antimatter asymmetry"

"processes mediated by the scalar diquarks and leptoquarks" "eptoquarks in this framework are found to be, n, p → + m, c + m (m = meson, = lepton, c = antilepton) and n, p → + c + `c . For some choice of the parameters of the theory, these decay rates can be within the observable range."

That last point is the surprising one: observable baryon-number violation, which the usual PS construction forbids.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The PQ Extension

Pati–Salam plus a global $U(1)_{PQ}$, with the PQ charges assigned to the fermions and scalars so that the strong CP angle is dynamical.

And the axion is the dark matter. That makes this the batch's second axion paper (after 1108.1771), and the more complete one, because it does the Higgs potential and the Yukawa fit.

The corpus should note the pattern: PQ keeps appearing in PS models, because PS has a natural place for it — the $\bar{\mathbf4}$'s components and the PS Higgs carry the charges, and the PQ-breaking can be tied to the PS breaking.

And the axion's mass and coupling are fixed by the PQ scale, which the corpus should note is a free parameter (between $10^9$ and $10^{12}$ GeV for the usual window).

The Higgs Potential Analysis

"

A comprehensive analysis of the Higgs potential is carried out in a minimal set-up."

That is the paper's technical core, and the corpus should value it, because the PS Higgs potential is rarely analysed completely: three or four multiplets, all the cross-quartics, and the vacuum alignment.

The output is a set of vacuum-alignment conditions, which determine whether the breaking pattern is the desired one. That is the kind of check most papers skip.

The corpus should record it as the batch's most careful breaking-sector analysis, and note that hep-ph/9602414 and hep-ph/9811469 are the other two.

The Yukawa Economy

"

limited number of Yukawa parameters can successfully incorporate the hierarchies in the charged fermion masses and mixings"

**"Limited"

** — the paper claims a small number of Yukawa couplings fits the masses and mixings. That is a genuine success, and it is what the corpus's flavour section needs: a PS model that fits without a family symmetry. The corpus should record the approach: the mass hierarchies come from the Higgs sector's structure (which VEVs couple to which operators), not from a flavour symmetry. So the batch now has three flavour strategies: 1. Family symmetry imposed (1406.7005, 1111.3952, 1605.02072). 2. Localisation (hep-ph/0409098, 2012.10492). 3. Higgs-sector structure with few parameters (here). That is a useful taxonomy, and the corpus should present it.

Observable Baryon Violation

"processes mediated by the scalar diquarks and leptoquarks" "on, c = antilepton) and n, p → + c + c . For some choice of the parameters of the theory, these decay rates can be within the observable range."

This is the paper's most striking claim. Pati–Salam's leptoquarks mediate proton decay, and the usual assumption is that the rate must be suppressed.

The paper says the rate can be observable — at the level of existing or near-future experiments — for some parameter choices.

That is a significant and unusual statement, and the corpus should record it carefully: it means the model does not assume the PS scale is superheavy, and it predicts proton decay in reach.

And note the channels: $n,p\to\ell+m$ (two-body, the classic), $n,p\to\ell+\ell^c+\ell^c$ (three-body, with a dilepton). The second is the more distinctive.

The corpus should compare with 2502.15614's $Z_3$ symmetry: that model forbids the dangerous operators; this one allows them and predicts a rate. Two opposite strategies for the same problem.

The Automatic Seesaw

"the automatic existence of the heavy Majorana neutrinos"

Same point as hep-ph/9602414: $N_R$ is in the $\bar{\mathbf4}$, so the seesaw needs nothing added.

And here the same $N_R$ does leptogenesis, so one field, two jobs.

Why This Paper Matters

It is the batch's most complete field-theory PS model: Higgs potential, Yukawa fit, neutrino masses, leptogenesis, axion dark matter, and proton decay.

And its predictions are sharp: the axion, the proton decay channels, and the leptogenesis scale.

The corpus should present it as a benchmark, alongside 2603.24152/2606.29811 for the SUSY branch and 2012.10492 for the flavour branch.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's best example of a PS model that predicts rather than accommodates, and the corpus should say why.

It predicts: an axion (with a mass and coupling), a proton-decay rate (possibly observable), a leptogenesis scale, and the Yukawa pattern from few parameters.

And it does not tune the PS scale to be superheavy, which is what makes the proton-decay prediction possible.

That is a genuinely different stance from the rest of the field-theory branch, where the PS scale is pushed up until the proton decays away.

The corpus should present the trade plainly: a high PS scale hides the proton decay and makes the model untestable; a lower one predicts it and risks the bounds. 1712.04880 takes the second option, and that is braver.

And the corpus should note which experiments decide: Hyper-Kamiokande and DUNE for $p\to e^+\pi^0$ and $p\to K^+\bar\nu$; the axion haloscopes for the dark matter. Both are coming.

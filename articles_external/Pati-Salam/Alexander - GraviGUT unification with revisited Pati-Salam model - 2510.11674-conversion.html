What the Paper Does

The paper removes the second weak $SU(2)$ of the Pati–Salam group by identifying it with one chiral half of the Lorentz group, so that it belongs to gravity and not to a second weak force. The identification is made inside the complexified orthogonal group $SO(1,9,\mathbb C)$, whose algebra splits as $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)_\mathbb C\oplus\mathfrak{so}(6)_\mathbb C\oplus(\mathbb C^{1,3}\otimes\mathbb C^6)$, with $\mathfrak{so}(6)\cong\mathfrak{su}(4)$ giving the Pati–Salam colour factor. A single gauge-invariant action on that connection, with a McDowell–Mansouri ansatz soldering the coset to the tetrad, reduces to Einstein–Cartan plus a Yang–Mills term for the surviving chirality. Matching to first-order gravity fixes the couplings: the weak coupling comes out $g_W\sim0.707$, against $g_2\simeq0.65$ measured. The paper is a construction with a phenomenology section that is explicitly left for later: birefringence of gravitational and electromagnetic waves, and graviton–gauge vertices whose coefficients are fixed by $G$ and $\Lambda$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Problem the Paper Targets

Pati–Salam unifies quarks and leptons in $SU(4)$ and puts left and right chiralities in two separate $SU(2)$ factors, $SU(4)\times SU(2)_+\times SU(2)_-$. The second factor predicts right-handed charged currents and $W_R$ bosons, and the LHC excludes $W_R$ up to about $6.4$ TeV under standard assumptions. The usual response is to break $SU(2)_-$ at a high scale by hand.

The paper's move is to refuse the breaking and reinterpret the factor. One $SU(2)$ is the weak force. The other is the chiral half of the Lorentz group — a part of gravity, not a force. The observable consequence is immediate: there is no second weak interaction in the infrared, because there was never a second weak interaction.

The Group and the Split

The gauge group is $SO(1,9,\mathbb C)$, gauged over four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime. Its algebra splits as

$$ \mathfrak{so}(1,9)_\mathbb C=\underbrace{\mathfrak{so}(1,3)_\mathbb C}_{(\mathrm{Adj},1)} \oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak{so}(6)_\mathbb C}_{(1,\mathrm{Adj})} \oplus\underbrace{\mathbb C^{1,3}\otimes\mathbb C^6}_{(4,6)}, $$

so $45=6+15+24$ generators, with $M_{AB}$, $A,B=0,\dots,9$, splitting into $a,b=0..3$ and $I,J=4..9$. The reason for this group and not a larger one is stated: $SO(1,9,\mathbb C)$ is the smallest simple group containing $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)_\mathbb C\oplus\mathfrak{so}(6)_\mathbb C$ as a subalgebra whose coset carries bifundamental indices of the right shape to be a vierbein.

The connection splits as $A=\omega+A+\phi$, with $\omega$ the $SO(1,3)$ part, $A$ the $SO(6)$ part and $\phi=\phi^{aI}M_{aI}$ the coset. The curvature components are

$$ F^{ab}=\mathcal R^{ab}+\phi^{aI}\wedge\phi_I^{\ b},\qquad F^{IJ}=F^{IJ}+\phi^{Ia}\wedge\phi_a^{\ J},\qquad F^{aI}=d\phi^{aI}+\omega^{a}_{\ c}\wedge\phi^{cI}+\phi^{aJ}\wedge A_J^{\ I}. $$

The Soldering Ansatz

The coset field is not left free. The paper imposes

$$ \phi^{aI}=\frac{1}{\ell}\,e^a n^I, \qquad \phi^{Ia}=-\frac{1}{\ell}\,e^a n^I, $$

with $e^a$ the tetrad, $\ell$ a constant and $n^I$ a normalized internal vector, $n^2=1$. The count drops from $6\times4\times4=96$ to $6+(4\times4)=22$ components. Then

$$ F^{ab}=\mathcal R^{ab}-\ell^{-2}e^a\wedge e^b, \qquad F^{aI}=\frac{1}{\ell}\left(T^a n^I+e^a\wedge P^I\right), $$

with torsion $T^a\equiv De^a$ and the internal analogue $P^I\equiv Dn^I$. The cosmological constant is not put in; it arrives as $\ell^{-2}$, the curvature of the soldered coset.

The Action and the Chiral Selection

The action is built from three terms — a curvature-squared kinetic term, a Stelle–West term with a five-index Higgs $U^{A_1\dots A_5}$, and a Pontryagin-like term with a pseudoscalar $\phi$:

$$ S=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int F^{AB}\wedge*F_{AB} +\frac{\gamma\alpha}{4}\int\phi\,\epsilon_{A_1\dots A_{10}}F^{A_1A_2}\wedge*F^{A_3A_4}U^{A_5\dots A_{10}} +\gamma\alpha\int\phi\,F^{AB}\wedge F_{AB}+\int[V(\phi)+V(U)]*1 . $$

Parity acts as $\phi'=-\phi$, and the potentials $V(\phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}(\phi^2-1)^2$ and $V(U)=\frac{\beta}{4}(U\cdot U-1)^2$ give $\langle\phi\rangle=\pm1$ and $U_0^{I_1\dots I_6}=\epsilon^{I_1\dots I_6}/6!$.

The chirality choice is dynamical. With $\gamma=i$ and $\langle\phi\rangle=1$, the Stelle–West term recombines with the leading term,

$$ \tfrac12F^{ab}\wedge*F_{ab}+\tfrac{i}{4}\epsilon_{abcd}F^{ab}\wedge*F^{cd} =F^{(+)ab}\wedge*F^{(+)}_{ab}, $$

so the surviving combination is the self-dual one. One chirality becomes the self-dual gravitational connection; the other becomes the Yang–Mills connection of the weak force.

The Resulting Lagrangian

Integrating out the topological terms and dropping torsion,

$$ \mathcal L=\frac{1}{32\pi G}\epsilon_{abcd}e^a\wedge e^b\wedge\mathcal R^{(-)cd} -\frac{\Lambda}{6}e^a\wedge e^b\wedge e^c\wedge e^d -\frac{3}{32\pi G}P^I\wedge*P_I -\frac{3}{64\pi G\Lambda}\mathcal R^{(+)}_{ab}\wedge*\mathcal R^{(+)ab} +\tfrac12F^{IJ}\wedge*F_{IJ}, $$

with

$$ \alpha=-\frac{3}{32\pi G\cdot2\Lambda}, \qquad \frac{1}{\ell^2}=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}. $$

The first two terms are Einstein–Cartan with a cosmological constant. The next is a kinetic term for the internal one-form. The last two are kinetic terms for the surviving chiral connection and for the $SO(6)$ field strength.

The Coupling Comes Out Right

The Yang–Mills coupling is read off as $\alpha=-1/(8g_W^2)$. With $\ell\sim\ell_{Pl}=\sqrt{8\pi G}$ this gives

$$ g_W\sim0.707, $$

against the measured $g_2\simeq0.65$. The paper runs it: with $b_2^{\text{MSSM}}=+1$ above $M_{SUSY}\sim1$ TeV,

$$ \frac{1}{g^2(\mu)}=\frac{1}{g^2(\mu_0)}-\frac{b_2}{8\pi^2}\ln\frac{\mu}{\mu_0} $$

from $\mu_0=\bar M_{Pl}$ to $M_Z$ gives $g_2(M_Z)\simeq0.65$. A single geometric length controls the normalisation of the gauge terms and the cosmological constant at once. The bare value $\Lambda_{bare}=-3/\ell^2$ is distinguished from the effective $\Lambda$.

$SO(6)$ to $SU(4)$ to $SU(3)\times U(1)$

The map is made with a Clifford realisation: six matrices with $\{\Gamma^I,\Gamma^J\}=2\delta^{IJ}$ and $\sigma^{IJ}=\frac14[\Gamma^I,\Gamma^J]$, which satisfy the $\mathfrak{so}(6)$ relations and act on the chiral spinor as $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. The same connection then has two equivalent expansions, $A=\frac12A_{IJ}\sigma^{IJ}=A_iT_i$, and the same holds for the curvature. Decomposing the adjoint,

$$ 15\to8\oplus1\oplus6, $$

the connection components are $A_i=W^{\hat i},\psi^{\hat a},B$, with the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ field strength acquiring a coset bilinear, $F^{\hat i}=dW^{\hat i}+\frac12f^{\hat i}_{\ \hat j\hat k}W^{\hat j}\wedge W^{\hat k}+\frac12f^{\hat i}_{\ \hat a\hat b}\psi^{\hat a}\wedge\psi^{\hat b}$, and the abelian one likewise, $F^{15}=dB+\frac12f^{15}_{\ \ \hat a\hat b}\psi^{\hat a}\wedge\psi^{\hat b}$.

The Surplus Coset Degrees of Freedom

The ansatz leaves 22 components where the tetrad needs 16. The paper splits them covariantly,

$$ \phi^{aI}=\frac{1}{\ell}e^a n^I+\tau^{aI}, \qquad \tau^{aI}\equiv Q^{I}_{\ J}\phi^{aJ},\quad n_I\tau^{aI}=0, $$

with projectors $P^{IJ}=n^In^J$ and $Q^{IJ}=\delta^{IJ}-n^In^J$. The field $\tau^{aI}$ is the surplus, and it is not discarded: it mediates interactions between sectors that would otherwise decouple, because it appears in every curvature component,

$$ F^{ab}=\mathcal R^{ab}-\ell^{-2}e^a\wedge e^b+\tau^{a}_{\ I}\wedge\tau^{Ib}, \qquad F^{aI}=\frac{1}{\ell}\left(T^an^I+e^a\wedge P^I\right)+d\tau^{aI}+\omega^a_{\ c}\wedge\tau^{cI}+\tau^{aJ}\wedge A_J^{\ I}. $$

Integrating $\tau$ out at tree level with mass $m_\tau$ from the adjoint Higgs gives

$$ \mathcal L_{eff}\simeq\frac{c_1}{m_\tau^2}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma} +\frac{c_2}{m_\tau^2}R_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\alpha}F^\alpha_{\ \nu} +\frac{c_3}{m_\tau^2}RF^2, $$

with $c_i\sim\alpha^2$. The first term is a graviton–gauge–gauge vertex, contributing to $h\to\gamma\gamma$, $h\to gg$, $h\to ZZ$ and $h\to W^+W^-$; the paper says the full computation is left for later work.

Birefringence

With $\phi=1+\delta\phi$ the parity-odd term becomes a Chern–Simons correction to Einstein–Cartan gravity. Linearising around a torsionless FRW background,

$$ \Box h_{ij}\pm i\frac{\dot\phi}{M_{Pl}}\epsilon_{ikl}\partial^k\dot h^{l}_{\ j}=0, $$

so the two helicities obey $\omega_\pm^2=k^2\pm k\dot\phi/M_{Pl}$ and accumulate a phase

$$ \Delta\Phi=\int^t\frac{\dot\phi}{M_{Pl}}\,dt'. $$

The same happens for the gauge fields, where the $SO(6)$ parity-odd term projects onto the $U(1)$ of $SU(4)$ as $\frac{\kappa_\gamma}{4}\phi F\wedge F$, giving $A''_\pm+(k^2\pm k\kappa_\gamma\phi')A_\pm=0$. Gravitational and electromagnetic birefringence have one origin, the same field $\phi$ that chooses the chirality.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. The second $SU(2)$ is the other chirality of the Lorentz group

The idea. $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ is not two forces. It is $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)_+\oplus\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)_-$ — the two chiral halves of the Lorentz group — with one half gauged as the weak force and the other left as the gravitational connection. The selection between them is made dynamically by a pseudoscalar whose vacuum expectation value can be $+1$ or $-1$, and the choice of sign is the choice of which chirality becomes which.

What it would add. This is the closest thing in the whole corpus's external literature to what the corpus already asserts. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md derives $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ as $\exp L(\mathfrak k)\times\exp R(\mathfrak k)$ — the two-sided action of the algebra — and then records the consequence as a defect: the algebra "does not choose a chirality", and the assignment $(4,2,1)\oplus(\bar4,1,2)$ is declared, not derived. The corpus calls this the chiral wall.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article, in the section Where Each Factor Comes From, and How Firmly, whose table ends with the two declared rows. The paper supplies exactly the missing argument: if the two $SU(2)$ are the two chiral halves of one Lorentz structure, then the second one is not a gauge freedom to be broken but a gravitational chirality, and the question "why do we not see $W_R$" has an answer that is not a scale.

What stands in the way. The paper needs $\mathfrak{so}(1,9)_\mathbb C$, a complexified group gauged over real spacetime, and a Higgs in a five-index antisymmetric representation. The corpus's algebra is $\mathbb B=\mathbb C\otimes_\mathbb R\mathbb H$, whose compact part is at most $\mathrm{U}(2)$ — not $\mathrm{SO}(1,9)$. So the identification cannot be imported as a derivation. But the statement can be imported as a reading of the corpus's own two-sided action, and that is worth more than the model: the corpus's $L$ and $R$ are a Lorentz pair, and the corpus has never said so in those words.

2. A gauge coupling fixed by the Planck length

The idea. $\alpha=-1/(8g_W^2)$ together with $\ell\sim\ell_{Pl}$ and $1/\ell^2=2\Lambda/3$ gives $g_W\sim0.707$, running to $g_2(M_Z)\simeq0.65$.

What it would add. A route to a gauge coupling that the corpus does not have, and a competitor to the one that fails. The corpus's Pati–Salam article takes $g_3=g_L=g_R$ as an assumption and tests it by $\sin^2\theta_W=\frac38$, which gives $0.31$ against $0.231$ — a $36\%$ failure that the article records. Here the weak coupling is not a free parameter at all: it is the inverse square root of the cosmological constant in Planck units.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, the paragraph that states the unification condition and its assumption, and the ledger row that records the $\sin^2\theta_W$ failure.

What stands in the way. The corpus's one-coupling statement is a boundary condition at $M_{PS}$, not at $M_{Pl}$. The paper's value is at the Planck scale, and reaching $M_Z$ needs a desert and MSSM matter content. The corpus has neither. What travels is the form of the claim — a coupling set by a length — and the corpus should record it as an alternative it cannot yet evaluate.

3. Parity broken by a pseudoscalar, not declared away

The idea. Parity is a symmetry of the high-energy action, realised as spatial inversion combined with an exchange of the two chiral sectors. It is broken spontaneously when $\phi$ takes $\langle\phi\rangle=\pm1$, and the two signs are two vacua. Nothing in the action prefers one.

What it would add. A mechanism where the corpus has a wall. The corpus states the chiral assignment as declared and does not offer a way it could be otherwise. Here the asymmetry of the world is a choice of vacuum among two, which is a different kind of statement: not "the algebra does not choose" but "the algebra offers two and the vacuum picks one".

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's chiral-wall row, and articles_physics/the-electroweak-pair-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-quotient-and-the-parity-lock.md, which treats the discrete symmetry the weaker partner of the pair.

What stands in the way. The corpus's algebra has no pseudoscalar with the right quantum numbers, and no potential to minimise. The importable kernel is the logical form: a discrete symmetry broken by a vacuum expectation value leaves two degenerate vacua and no preferred sign. That is one sentence, and the corpus can hold it without the model.

4. Gravitational birefringence from one field

The idea. $\omega_\pm^2=k^2\pm k\dot\phi/M_{Pl}$ for gravitational waves, and $A''_\pm+(k^2\pm\kappa_\gamma k\phi')A_\pm=0$ for light, both driven by the same $\phi$. The accumulated rotation is $\Delta\Phi=\int\dot\phi/M_{Pl}\,dt$.

What it would add. A parity-odd gravitational effect with the same structure as the electromagnetic one. The corpus's axion articles treat $aF\tilde F$ and cosmic birefringence for photons. The gravitational analogue, $R\tilde R$, is the same identity applied to a different curvature, and the corpus has never written it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-axion-and-the-peccei-quinn-mechanism-in-biquaternionic-form.md, and articles_physics/the-conformal-anomaly-in-biquaternionic-form.md, whose subject is a curvature response.

What stands in the way. The corpus's birefringence treatment is electromagnetic and its gravitational side is the linearised Einstein equation. $R\tilde R$ needs the Riemann tensor's dual, which the corpus's frame article does not build. A real addition, not a paragraph.

5. Counting: 45 generators, 24 in the coset, 22 after soldering

The idea. The coset $\mathbb C^{1,3}\otimes\mathbb C^6$ has 24 real components, the ansatz removes all but 22, the tetrad needs 16, and the remaining 6 are a field $\tau^{aI}$ that cannot be discarded because it appears in every curvature component.

What it would add. A worked instance of the corpus's recurring count problem: how many components a connection has, how many the geometry needs, and what to do with the surplus. The corpus's frame article counts $21=15+3+3$ for Pati–Salam and records which are massive; here the count is $45=6+15+24$ with the surplus carried by a named field.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, the boson table, and the corpus's frame article.

What stands in the way. Different group, different dimension. The method — count, solder, name the remainder — transfers; the numbers do not.

6. The Immirzi parameter as a dynamical field

The idea. $\gamma\phi$ appears where the Immirzi parameter appears in self-dual gravity, so $\phi$ is a dynamical Immirzi parameter, and its vacuum expectation value is what makes the self-dual sector the gravitational one.

What it would add. A bridge between the corpus and loop-quantum-gravity language that the corpus has never used: the Immirzi parameter is normally a constant of the quantisation, here it is a field with a potential.

Where it lands. No corpus article. It would be a new one, or a paragraph in a future article on self-dual formulations.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no spin-foam and no Ashtekar variables. This is a forward-looking item and the paper itself calls the self-dual formulation a direction for later work.

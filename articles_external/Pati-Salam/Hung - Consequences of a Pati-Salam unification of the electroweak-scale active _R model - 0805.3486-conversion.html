What the Paper Does

It takes a model in which the right-handed neutrino is not a Standard Model singlet and asks what happens when you unify quarks and leptons on top of it.

"If right-handed neutrinos are not singlets under the electroweak gauge group as it was proposed in a recent model, they can acquire electroweak scale masses and are thus accessible experimentally in the near future. When this idea is combined with quark-lepton unification à la Pati-Salam, one is forced to introduce new neutral particles which are singlets under the Standard Model. These 'sterile neutrinos' ... can have their own seesaw with masses in the keV range for the lighter of the two eigenstates."

The logic in one line: a non-singlet $\nu_R$ cannot fit into an $SU(4)$ quartet without a partner, and the partner is a new sterile state, and the sterile state ends up at keV. Three steps, each forced, and the last gives warm dark matter for free.

"And argument is presented on why, in this model, it is natural to have four families which can obey existing constraints."

Four families is normally fatal (the $Z$ invisible width allows three light neutrinos, and a fourth chiral family is excluded). The paper's claim is that in this structure a fourth family is natural and survives, and that argument is the most interesting part.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Starting Point: an Active $\nu_R$

"The model of [4] is based on the assumption of the existence of mirror fermions (both quarks and leptons) in the SM: For every $SU(2)_L$ SM left-handed doublet (e.g. $(\nu,e)_L$) there is a mirror right-handed doublet (e.g. $(\nu,e^M)_R$) and for every $SU(2)_L$ right-handed singlet (e.g. $e_R$), there is a mirror left-handed singlet (e.g. $e^M_L$)."

So the model has a full mirror sector, and the mirroring is what makes $\nu_R$ a doublet member. In the mirror assignment, $(\nu,e^M)_R$ is a doublet of a right-handed $SU(2)$, and $\nu_R$ sits in it.

"the right-handed neutrino masses are 'confined' to a rather 'narrow' range $M_Z/2

That window is not arbitrary: $M_R>M_Z/2$ keeps the $Z$ from decaying to $\nu_R\bar\nu_R$ invisibly, and $M_R<\Lambda_{\rm EW}$ is what makes the state "electroweak scale." Fine-tuning the mass into the window is the model's premise, and the corpus should note that it is a premise and not a consequence.

"Electroweak scale SM active right-handed neutrinos have an appealing aspect to them: They can be proved or disproved at colliders. One of the characteristic signals is like-sign dilepton events."

Why Pati–Salam Forces New Sterile States

"Because our model contains mirror fermions, it turns out that unification à la Pati–Salam with the minimal particle content of [4] cannot be achieved unless one introduces new neutral leptons which are singlets under the SM: the 'sterile' neutrinos."

The representation counting is the argument. The $SU(2)_L$-singlet content is $d_R$, $d^M_L$, $u_R$, $u^M_L$, $e_R$, $e^M_L$. To make $SU(4)$ quartets you pair them: $(d_R,e_R)$ and $(d^M_L,e^M_L)$ work. But then $u_R$ and $u^M_L$ have no partner — the fourth slot of their quartets is empty, because the usual partner ($\nu_R$ and $\nu^M_L$) is not a singlet here; it is inside a doublet.

So you must add a singlet $N$ to fill the slot, giving $(u_R,N_R)$ and $(u^M_L,N_L)$. And that $N$ comes in both helicities, because you needed two quartets.

This is the paper's central observation and it is a good one: the very assumption that makes the model interesting (active $\nu_R$) is the assumption that breaks the Pati–Salam completion, and the repair introduces exactly the kind of particle the assumption was meant to avoid.

Why the Sterile Neutrinos Are Not Constrained

"unlike generic models of sterile neutrinos where they are usually thought of as SM-singlet right-handed particles, these new neutral fermions come in both helicities: $N_L$ and $N_R$."

"Unlike a typical model containing sterile neutrinos where there is usually an intrinsic link — and hence a tight constraint — between the active and sterile sectors, it will be seen below that this link is at best very weak in our model and one is not bound by constraints from the active sector."

That is the mechanism that makes keV sterile neutrinos viable. In a normal seesaw, active-sterile mixing is $\theta\sim m_D/M_R$ and the X-ray and structure-formation bounds on a keV sterile state constrain $\theta$ tightly. Here the mixing is suppressed by the mirror structure, so the state is allowed.

And that is what makes it warm dark matter. A keV fermion with weak mixing is exactly the Dodelson–Widrow candidate, and it has the astrophysical consequences the abstract lists: "structure formation, supernova asymmetries, pulsar kicks".

The Four-Family Argument

"The structure of the aforementioned gauge group and its fermionic representations is very suggestive of the way spinors of some orthogonal group decompose into spinors of its orthogonal subgroup. For instance, a spinor of $SO(2m+4)$ decomposes into $2^{m-1}\psi_+ + 2^{m-1}\psi_-$ of $SO(4)$ or $2^{m-1}$ families of $SO(4)$. With $SO(4)\approx SU(2)\otimes SU(2)$, it is argued in Section (VII) why the simplest, anomaly-free case where $m=3$ which corresponds to the group $SO(10)$ and to four families is an appealing scenario."

"The group that gives rise to this feature is argued to be $SU(4)_{PS}\otimes SO(10)\otimes SO(10)'$ which breaks down to $SU(4)_{PS}\otimes SU(4)_H\otimes SU(2)_L\otimes SU(2)_R\otimes SU(4)'_H\otimes SU(2)'_L\otimes SU(2)'_R$ with $SU(4)_H$ and $SU(4)'_H$ being family gauge groups."

That is a genuinely different route to a family number and it deserves recording: the number of families is $2^{m-1}$ from a spinor decomposition, and $m=3$ gives four.

Whether that is convincing is another matter — the "$2^{m-1}$" counting requires an $SO(2m+4)$ ultraviolet group whose existence is assumed — but it is the kind of explanation the corpus's article should mention, because the question "why three generations" otherwise has no answer beyond "because."

"It is argued in Section (VII) how one might expect the fourth neutrino to be heavy."

And that is how the fourth family survives the $Z$ width: the fourth neutrino is heavy, so the invisible width sees only three.

The Gauge Group

"the group $SU(4)_{PS}\otimes SU(2)_L\otimes SU(2)_R\otimes SU(2)'_L\otimes SU(2)'_R$ ... This group is reminiscent of the Petite Unification model of [14] but differs from it in a major way, in terms of fermionic assignments and patterns of symmetry breaking."

"The computation of $\sin^2\theta_W(M_Z)$ reveals the Pati-Salam scale to be of a typical GUT size. (In this sense, it has 'grown up' and is no longer 'Petite'.)"

Four $SU(2)$s, so a "doubly left-right" structure, and the unification analysis puts the scale at $10^{15}$ GeV. The joke about growing up is worth keeping — it is the author admitting that the original "petite" idea of a low unification scale did not survive contact with $\sin^2\theta_W$.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. A non-singlet $\nu_R$ breaks the Pati–Salam completion and forces a new sterile state

The idea. If $\nu_R$ lives in a doublet, the $SU(4)$ quartets $(u_R,\nu_R)$ are not allowed, and a singlet $N$ must be added; $N$ then comes in both helicities.

What it would add. A clean example of a unified completion punishing an extension: the assumption that made the model attractive is the one that costs a new sector. The corpus should record this pattern, because it recurs (the twin-PS papers pay with six vector-like families for the same kind of reason).

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's fermion-assignment section.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the quartet counting is explicit.

2. keV sterile neutrinos with suppressed active mixing as warm dark matter

The idea. $N_{L,R}$ both present, active–sterile mixing weak because of the mirror structure, mass in the keV range.

What it would add. A dark-matter candidate that is a consequence rather than an addition, and one with distinctive astrophysics (pulsar kicks, supernova asymmetry). The corpus's dark-matter discussion should include it as the fermionic alternative to the WIMP-ish options in 2504.01893.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's cosmology section.

What stands in the way. keV sterile dark matter has its own tensions (structure formation, X-ray bounds), which the paper does not resolve.

3. $2^{m-1}$ families from an $SO(2m+4)$ spinor decomposition

The idea. $m=3$ gives four families, and this is why four is natural here.

What it would add. A candidate answer to "why this many generations" that is not numerology. The corpus should present it as a candidate, with the caveat that the ultraviolet group is assumed.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's generation-count discussion, if it has one.

What stands in the way. The argument is heuristic; no anomaly-free $SO(16)$-like completion is constructed.

4. "Petite" unification grows up

The idea. The original low-scale unification idea gives way to a $10^{15}$ GeV scale once $\sin^2\theta_W$ is computed.

What it would add. A datapoint on the recurring failure of low-scale unification, which is the same lesson as 2305.19356's PeV bound and 1812.02747's $10^{11}$ GeV constraint. Three papers in this batch, one conclusion: $SU(4)$ wants to be heavy.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's unification-scale section.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's clearest example of a completion forcing new states, and reading it next to the twin-PS series is instructive.

Hung wants an active $\nu_R$ at the electroweak scale. The Pati–Salam completion says: then add singlets. The result is a four-$SU(2)$ group, a mirror sector, keV dark matter, and four families.

King wants a TeV vector leptoquark. The completion says: then add a second $SU(4)$, six vector-like families, and a colour octet.

In both cases the unification does not accommodate the desired low-energy feature; it exacts a price for it, and in both cases the price is a large new sector. That is the honest pattern of Pati–Salam model building and the corpus should show it twice rather than once.

The second point: the "grown up, no longer petite" remark is the most quotable line in the batch and it records a real retreat. Petite unification promised a low scale; the calculation delivered $10^{15}$ GeV. The corpus's article should note that Pati–Salam's scale is not a free choice but an output of $\sin^2\theta_W$ and the proton-decay bounds, and it comes out high.

What the Paper Does

It asks the question every leptoquark paper should answer and most avoid: if you put a TeV leptoquark in, what happens to the proton?

"One or more scalar leptoquarks with masses around a few TeV may provide a solution to some of the flavor anomalies that have been observed. We discuss the impact of such new degrees on baryon number violation when the theory is embedded in a Pati-Salam model. The Pati-Salam embedding can suppress renormalizable and dimension-five baryon number violation in some cases."

The logic of the problem, stated plainly:

"Treating the SM as an effective theory it contains higher dimension operators that give rise to baryon number violation at dimension six. There are a number of flavor anomalies that can be addressed by adding to the SM scalar leptoquarks with masses around a few TeV. However, adding such leptoquarks to the SM effective theory one finds that baryon number violation can now occur at the renormalizable and/or dimension-five levels. Experimental limits on baryon number violating processes force the leptoquark mass to be too large to explain the flavor anomalies even when the cutoff of the SM effective theory is the Planck mass."

That paragraph states a problem that is fatal and little discussed.

In the SM, baryon number is violated only at dimension six, so the rate goes as $1/\Lambda^2$ with $\Lambda>10^{15}$ GeV and the proton survives. Add a TeV scalar leptoquark that couples to quarks and leptons, and you can write a renormalizable coupling $\lambda\,q\,q\,\phi$ — dimension four. Then the proton decays at a rate set by the leptoquark mass, and a TeV leptoquark gives a lifetime of order a second.

Unless the leptoquark's diquark coupling vanishes. And the SM gives no reason for that: an EFT leptoquark has arbitrary couplings, and setting the diquark one to zero is a choice that nothing protects (it is not enforced by any symmetry, so loops regenerate it).

So the "leptoquark explanation of the $B$ anomalies" has a hidden fine-tuning problem, and the Pati–Salam embedding is one of the few things that can remove it.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Answer Depends on the Seesaw

"When the Pati-Salam model is broken at a high scale and the neutrino masses arise from the type-I seesaw mechanism we found by using experimental constraints on baryon number violation that $\Lambda_{\rm PS}\gtrsim10^{18}$ GeV and the vev defining the Pati-Salam breaking is constrained to be in the window $10^{13}\lesssim v_\Delta\lesssim10^{15}$ GeV."

Note the two scales: the Pati–Salam breaking VEV at $10^{13}$–$10^{15}$ GeV, and the cutoff $\Lambda_{\rm PS}$ at the Planck scale. So the model is one in which Pati–Salam is broken at a very high scale and the TeV leptoquarks are... still at a TeV? That works only if the leptoquarks get their mass from a different, lower source than the Pati–Salam breaking — which is exactly the splitting described in 1808.07492.

"When the Pati-Salam model (with minimal particle content) is broken at the low scale, neutrinos get mass through the inverse seesaw mechanism. In this case, in the Pati-Salam effective field theory baryon number violating operators involving the leptoquarks first occur at dimension seven. Baryon number violation arising from dimension-six operators involving four fermions will dominate in this case and provide a bound $\Lambda_{\rm PS}\gtrsim10^{15}$ GeV."

So the low-scale version is safer, and the reason is a dimension count. With the minimal content and a low breaking scale, the leptoquark's dangerous couplings require more fields to be gauge invariant, so the leading violating operator moves from dimension four to dimension seven, and dimension seven is suppressed enough.

The corpus should record this as the general lesson: whether a light leptoquark is safe depends on the operator dimension, which depends on the field content, which depends on which seesaw you use. Three things linked by one representation-theory calculation.

The Case That Fails

"We also studied a case where the Pati–Salam model had non-minimal particle content. Adding a scalar to the SM that has both leptoquark and diquark couplings gives rise to renormalizable baryon number violation through the tree-level exchange of that scalar."

That is the mixed scalar, and it is the object to avoid. A single scalar that couples both to $qq$ (diquark) and to $q\ell$ (leptoquark) mediates $qq\to q\ell$ at tree level through $s$-channel exchange, which is proton decay at dimension four.

"We showed in an example that embedding the SM and such a scalar in the Pati-Salam effective field theory can protect the theory for having unacceptably large baryon number violation" — but only in a specific embedding, and:

"It is worth emphasizing that our conclusions depend on the field content of the model. Adding more degrees of freedom, several of which are at the TeV scale, can lead to unacceptably large baryon number violation."

So the protection is not automatic and it is field-content-dependent. That is the paper's honest summary, and it is a warning to the model builders in this batch who add leptoquarks freely.

The Example That Shows Pati–Salam Does Not Always Help

"According to table I, $\phi_{\rm LQ}$ and $\phi_{\rm DQ}$ are mixed scalars. This is also true in Pati-Salam models as can be seen explicitly in their Yukawa couplings," $$ -\mathcal L=Y_6F_{uA}F_{dB}(\Phi_6^\dagger)^{AB}+Y_6'\epsilon_{ABCD}F_{uA}F_{dB}\Phi_6^{CD}+\dots $$ $$ =Y_6\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}u_{\alpha R}d_{\beta R}(\varphi_{\rm DQ}^\dagger)_\gamma+2Y_6'u_{\alpha R}e_R\varphi_{{\rm DQ}\alpha}+\dots $$

So a single Pati–Salam multiplet $\Phi_6\sim(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ contains both a diquark and a leptoquark component, with couplings of the same order. Embedding in Pati–Salam does not separate them; it guarantees they are both present.

That is a genuinely negative result about the group and it should be recorded. The $\mathbf6$ of $SU(4)$ is antisymmetric, so it decomposes into a $\bar{\mathbf3}$ and a $\mathbf3$ under $SU(3)$, which are the diquark and the leptoquark. One multiplet, both couplings, unavoidable.

So Pati–Salam protects baryon number only for the $\mathbf{10}$ (whose content is symmetric) and not for the $\mathbf6$. The corpus's article should say which representations are safe.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. A TeV leptoquark in the SM EFT violates baryon number at dimension four

The idea. An EFT leptoquark has an arbitrary diquark coupling; nothing forbids $\lambda\,qq\phi$, and loops regenerate it if you set it to zero.

What it would add. The problem that makes the whole leptoquark programme questionable, stated in one paragraph. The corpus should carry this as a caveat on every leptoquark paper in the batch: the couplings are not protected unless a symmetry or a unified structure removes them.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's leptoquark section, as the opening problem.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The dimension counting is trivial and decisive.

2. The protection depends on the representation, and the $\mathbf6$ is not safe

The idea. $\Phi_6\sim(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ decomposes into a diquark and a leptoquark with comparable couplings. The $\mathbf{10}$ does not.

What it would add. A sharp selection rule for model building. Pati–Salam does not automatically protect the proton; it protects it for some representations and not others, and the criterion is whether the multiplet's $SU(3)$ decomposition mixes the two couplings.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scalar-sector section, with the representation criterion.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the Yukawa expansion is in the paper.

3. The seesaw choice moves the leading violating operator from dimension four to seven

The idea. High-scale type-I seesaw needs $\Lambda_{\rm PS}\gtrsim10^{18}$ GeV and $10^{13}\lesssim v_\Delta\lesssim10^{15}$ GeV; low-scale inverse seesaw gives dimension-seven leptoquark operators and the bound relaxes to $\Lambda_{\rm PS}\gtrsim10^{15}$ GeV.

What it would add. A demonstration that "how badly is baryon number violated" is not a property of the group but of the field content and the neutrino mechanism. That is a methodological point the corpus should have: in unified models, the most dangerous operators are often determined by an unrelated sector's choices.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's proton-decay section, next to the point that $SU(4)$ leptoquarks do not mediate proton decay.

What stands in the way. Nothing; both bounds are quoted.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the one that says the quiet part aloud, and it should be read before any of the leptoquark papers rather than after.

Every other paper in this batch that uses Pati–Salam leptoquarks for $B$ anomalies states, as an advantage, that proton decay is avoided. That is true of the $\mathbf{10}$'s leptoquark components and false of the $\mathbf6$'s, and the difference is one line of $SU(3)$ decomposition. So the claim needs a qualifier, and Murgui–Wise supply it.

The broader point: "unification protects the proton" is a claim about representations, not about groups. $SU(4)$ contains both safe and unsafe multiplets, and a model that uses the unsafe ones has a fatal problem however unified it is. That is worth stating once in the corpus, with this paper as the citation, because otherwise the reader will take the recurring "no proton decay" claim at face value for every construction.

The second thing: the paper's closing warning — "our conclusions depend on the field content ... Adding more degrees of freedom, several of which are at the TeV scale, can lead to unacceptably large baryon number violation" — applies directly to the twin-PS constructions in this batch, which add six vector-like families and a colour octet at the TeV scale. Those papers do not perform this check. The corpus should note the gap rather than assume the check was done.

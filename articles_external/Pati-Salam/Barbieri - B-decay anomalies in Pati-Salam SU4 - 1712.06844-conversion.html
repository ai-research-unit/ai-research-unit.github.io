What the Paper Does

The paper explains both $B$-decay anomaly families with one object: the leptoquark in the adjoint of Pati–Salam $SU(4)$, which is the vector $\hat V_\mu(3,1)_{2/3}$. Two problems stand in the way and both are solved the same way. The tree-level $K_L\to\mu e$ rate is unacceptable if all three generations couple equally, and the LHC has not seen the state. The answer is to make $SU(4)$ a global symmetry of a new strongly interacting sector rather than a gauge symmetry, to make the leptoquark and the necessary heavy Dirac quartets composite, and to let the $U(2)^5$ flavour symmetry of the Standard Model make the coupling generation-dependent — large for the third generation, small for the first two. The same strong sector produces the Higgs as a pseudo-Goldstone boson.

For the corpus the value is threefold: it gives the explicit matrix form of the $B-L$ generator, it shows that the $K_L\to\mu e$ bound the corpus pays $250$ TeV for can instead be evaded by non-universality, and it explains why one anomaly is tree-sized and the other loop-sized.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Constraints That Must Be Met Together

The paper begins by listing what any combined explanation has to survive:

  • the couplings of $b_L$, $\tau_L$, $\nu_\tau$ to the $Z$ and of $\tau$ to the $W$, none of which deviates at the $10^{-3}$ level;
  • the absence of deviations in $\Delta B=2$ transitions;
  • $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)<4.7\times10^{-12}$;
  • the absence of direct production at the LHC of the mediators needed for the $20$–$30\%$ deviation in $R_D^{(*\tau l)}$.

Why $U(2)^5$ Does Most of the Work

The argument is the paper's best paragraph and is worth reproducing in substance. The gauge sector of the Standard Model has a $U(3)^5$ symmetry, which is the origin of minimal flavour violation. With Yukawa couplings switched on, the surviving approximate symmetry is $U(2)^5$, "which acts on the first two generations of matter as doublets and on the third generation as singlets", and which "is an observed approximate symmetry both of the masses of the charged fermions as of the mixing angles in the quark sector".

Then: "Suppose now that a leptoquark be the mediator responsible for the B-anomalies, singlet under U(2)5 . In absence of U(2)5 breaking the leptoquark can only couple to the third generation. This hypothesis provides a natural first-order explanation for the different size of the charged current versus the neutral current effects, about equally deviating from the tree-level versus loop-level SM amplitudes respectively: b → cτ ν only involves a single second generation particle, whereas b → sµµ has three light generation fermions [20]. As we shall"

That is the structural point: the two anomalies differ in size because they differ in how many light-generation fermions they contain, and a third-generation-only coupling reproduces the difference without tuning.

Why the Group Must Be Composite

The vector leptoquark in the adjoint of $SU(4)$ transforms as $(3,1)_{2/3}$. Two features constrain it. It "cannot even be too light to have escaped detection at LHC so far", and "since in a low energy exchange what counts is the ratio of coupling versus mass, its coupling must be sizeable, at least 3÷4. Furthermore" Furthermore, "Furthermore the process KL → µe is mediated at tree level by V̂µ -exchange at an unacceptable rate, if (s, µ) and (d, e) form quartets of SU(4) with equal coupling as the third generation (b, τ ). A possible"

The way out: "invoking the presence of heavy Dirac quartets of $SU(4)$ to which the standard quarks and leptons are suitably mixed." A strongly coupled leptoquark plus heavy Dirac fermions "lead us to consider both of them as composite particles of a new strong dynamics, assumed to exist, with $SU(4)$ as a global symmetry group". And then the natural extension: the same dynamics produces the Higgs as a pseudo-Goldstone boson. The symmetry breaking pattern is

$$ G=SU(4)\times SO(5)\times U(1)_X \longrightarrow H=SU(4)\times SO(4)\times U(1)_X . $$

The resonances are

$$ G^A_\mu=(15,1)_0,\quad \rho^a_\mu=(1,6)_0,\quad X_\mu=(1,1)_0,\quad \Psi_\pm=(4,4)_{\pm\frac12},\quad \chi_\pm=(4,1)_{\pm\frac12}. $$

The Hypercharge Formula, Stated Explicitly

The Standard Model is introduced by explicit breaking of $H$: QCD is an $SU(3)_c$ subgroup of $SU(4)$, electroweak $SU(2)$ sits inside $SO(4)$, and

$$ Y=T_{15}+T^3_R+X, \qquad T_{15}=\frac{\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)}{\sqrt{24}}, $$

with $T^3_R$ a generator of the $SU(2)_R$ inside $SO(4)$. The tracelessness is visible in the entries: $1+1+1-3=0$.

The decomposition of the adjoint is

$$ G^A_\mu\to\hat G(8,1)_0\oplus\hat V(3,1)_{\frac23}+\mathrm{c.c.}\oplus\hat B(1,1)_0, $$

which names the three pieces: a colour octet gluon, the leptoquark and its conjugate, and a $B-L$ gauge boson.

The Fit

The parameters that control the charged-current anomaly together with the $Z$ and $W$ couplings are

$$ \left(\frac{\hat g_G}{m_G},\ \frac{\hat g_\rho}{m_\rho},\ \frac{\hat g_X}{m_X}\right)s_{q3}s_{l3}, \qquad C_{l1}=-C_{l3}, $$

and the preferred value is $\hat g_Gs_{q3}s_{l3}\approx2m_G/\mathrm{TeV}$. The $U(2)^n$-breaking parameters control the neutral-current anomaly together with $\Delta C=2$, $\tau\to3\mu$ and $\tau\to\mu\gamma$, and the constraints are "all closely satisfied by"

$$ s_{l3}\approx s_{q3}, \qquad s_{l2}/s_{l3}\approx0.04, \qquad E_{\mu3}\approx0.1 . $$

The paper's closing line on this is a prediction: Any improvement of the current sensitivity on $\Delta C=2$, $\tau\to3\mu$, $\tau\to\mu\gamma$ is likely to produce a visible signal.

The LHC Target

The most powerful search is for the heavy gluon $\hat G_\mu$, whose "most relevant features of" "are the dominant decays of the gluon resonance into tt̄ and bb̄ and its broadness. Because". The paper is careful that its figure "must be seen as indicative of the potential of further studies".

What the Paper Says About

Its Own Status

The description "is far from being possibly considered as 'UV complete'", and the paper says so plainly, comparing itself to composite Higgs models. It notes that reproducing the anomalies with an elementary $SU(4)$ gauge symmetry is a separate and active programme, and proposes that "one could focus on making explicit the different phenomenological expectations between an elementary and a composite $SU(4)$ symmetry picture."

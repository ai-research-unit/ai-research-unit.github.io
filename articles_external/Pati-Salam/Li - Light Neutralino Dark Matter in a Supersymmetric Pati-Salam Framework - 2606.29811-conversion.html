What the Paper Does

It finds a light dark matter candidate — below 120 GeV — in the same framework.

"We investigate the low-energy phenomenology of the MSSM arising from the supersymmetric $SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ Pati–Salam framework, focusing on neutralino dark matter in the bulk annihilation and Higgs/$Z$-funnel regions."

"We identify a viable bulk region characterized by a bino-like neutralino LSP and a light right-handed stau as the next-to-lightest supersymmetric particle (NLSP). The allowed bulk solutions satisfy all present experimental constraints ... and predict robust upper bounds $m_{\tilde\chi^0_1}\lesssim110$ GeV and $m_{\tilde\tau_1}\lesssim120$ GeV."

"Our this parameter space lies within the projected reach of future lepton colliders such as CEPC and FCC-ee."

"We further analyze the Higgs- and $Z$-funnel regions and show that current direct-detection limits strongly constrain light Higgsino-assisted resonance solutions for $\mu>0$. In contrast, for $\mu<0$, destructive interference in the Higgs-mediated spin-independent scattering amplitude suppresses the direct-detection cross section, allowing a narrow but viable $Z$-funnel region to survive below the projected sensitivity of the 1000-day LUX-ZEPLIN exposure."

So the light window survives for $\mu<0$, through destructive interference. That is the paper's specific and interesting result.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Light Window

The MSSM's natural dark matter is a bino of a few hundred GeV, and the LHC's null results have pushed it up — which is why 2603.24152 found 1–4 TeV.

But a light window survives: if the LSP is $\lesssim110$ GeV and the NLSP is a right-handed stau at $\lesssim120$ GeV, the coannihilation and the bulk annihilation can give the right relic density without violating the LHC bounds, because light staus are poorly constrained.

The corpus should record this: the Pati–Salam boundary conditions do not force a heavy spectrum. Both a light and a heavy window exist, and the corpus should present the SUSY section with both.

And that is a correction to the common statement that "the LHC has excluded SUSY". It has excluded the simplest spectra; constrained GUT spectra survive in two windows.

The Right-Handed Stau

Why a right-handed stau specifically. In Pati–Salam the right-handed sleptons sit in the $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ multiplets with the right-handed quarks, so their soft masses are related to the squarks' at the GUT scale. The RGE running then makes the right-handed stau the lightest slepton.

That is a genuine Pati–Salam prediction: $\tilde\tau_R$ light, $\tilde e_R$ and $\tilde\mu_R$ heavier — because the third family's Yukawa coupling drives its mass down.

So the NLSP being $\tilde\tau_R$ is not a choice but a consequence. The corpus should record it, because it is one of the few places where a Pati–Salam boundary condition makes a distinctive collider prediction.

Destructive Interference and $\mu<0$

"for $\mu<0$, destructive interference in the Higgs-mediated spin-independent scattering amplitude suppresses the direct-detection cross section"

The mechanism, in plain terms. The neutralino's spin-independent scattering on a nucleus goes through Higgs exchange (and squark exchange), and the amplitude has contributions of opposite sign — from the up-type and down-type quarks' couplings, and from the Higgsino and bino components.

For $\mu<0$ the signs conspire to cancel, so the cross-section drops below the LZ sensitivity and the light window survives.

That is a well-known MSSM effect, and the paper applies it in the Pati–Salam-constrained subspace, where the cancellation is more constrained because the soft terms are related.

The corpus should record it as a mechanism with a name: "blind spot" is the standard term — a point in parameter space where the direct-detection cross-section vanishes. The corpus should use the term and explain it.

The $Z$-Funnel and the Higgs Funnel

$Z$-funnel: $2m_\chi\approx m_Z$, resonant annihilation through the $Z$. Needs $m_\chi\approx45$ GeV, which is below LEP's bound unless the coupling is suppressed. Higgs funnel: $2m_\chi\approx m_h\approx125$ GeV, so $m_\chi\approx62$ GeV.

Both are light windows, and both are tightly constrained by the invisible $Z$ width and the Higgs invisible width.

The paper finds the $Z$-funnel survives for $\mu<0$ because of the same interference. So the light windows and the blind spot are connected.

The Future Colliders

"within the projected reach of future lepton colliders such as CEPC and FCC-ee"

A 110 GeV neutralino and a 120 GeV stau are within a lepton collider's reach but not the LHC's, because the stau's production cross-section at a hadron collider is small and the backgrounds large.

So the light window is a target for CEPC/FCC-ee, and the heavy windows are targets for the LHC and LZ.

The corpus should note the complementarity: two windows, two kinds of machine, and both tests are coming. That is a healthy situation for a model — it will be tested either way.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper completes the batch's dark-matter picture, and the picture has two halves.

Half one (2603.24152): a heavy spectrum, 1–4 TeV, tested by the LHC and LZ. Half two (here): a light spectrum, $\lesssim120$ GeV, tested by CEPC and FCC-ee.

Both are consistent with the same Pati–Salam boundary conditions. So the framework does not predict the scale — the same lesson as the corpus's breaking-scale table, now in the soft sector.

The corpus should say it once, clearly: Pati–Salam fixes relations among parameters, not their values. Both a 110 GeV and a 3 TeV neutralino fit. The framework is a constraint, not a prediction of scale.

And that is why the future colliders matter: they will cover both halves, and one of them will decide.

What the Paper Does

The paper puts the Pati–Salam gauge group in a five-dimensional Randall–Sundrum bulk and breaks it by boundary conditions on the ultraviolet brane, with no scalar responsible for the breaking. Two consequences follow at once. The vector leptoquark and the vector-like fermions come out at the same scale, both being Kaluza–Klein resonances, so the leptoquark is naturally at a few TeV rather than at the PeV scale. And the $K_L\to\mu e$ problem is solved geometrically, by localising the Standard Model fermions at different points of the bulk, so that their couplings to the resonance are not universal. With those two problems gone, the model addresses the $B$-anomalies. It explains $b\to s\mu^+\mu^-$ including $R_K$ and $R_{K^*}$, and it cannot explain $b\to c\tau\nu$ because $D-\bar D$ mixing forbids the required size.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Problem the Model Is Built Around

The paper states it plainly: "In conventional PS models, the bounds on the breaking scale from KL → µe and K → πµe are so strong (at the PeV scale) [30, 31] that any other observable effect in flavour physics is ruled out. Nonetheless". So a conventional Pati–Salam model cannot be used for the anomalies at all — the leptoquark that would do the job is forced to be far too heavy. The two known escapes are adding vector-like fermions (which can lower the scale to a few TeV) and putting the group in a warped background. This paper takes the second route and gets the first for free.

The Breaking Mechanism

The metric is

$$ ds^2=e^{-2ky}\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu-dy^2, \qquad 0\le y\le\pi R, $$

with bulk gauge symmetry $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$, "broken to its SM subgroup by means of boundary conditions on the UV brane". The paper notes that the unbroken hypercharge "is a linear combination of $U(1)_{B-L}$ (contained in $SU(4)$) and $U(1)_R$ of $SU(2)_R$", and that because of this "relaxing the assumption of a discrete left-right symmetry, one can always account for the measured values of $g_Y$". That is a small but important remark: the group does not force $g_L=g_R$, because hypercharge is a mixture and the mixing angle is free.

The Standard Model fermions are bulk fields whose zero modes are the ordinary particles, "their localizations are determined by their 5D bulk masses". The Higgs is different — "a 4D field confined to the UV brane, hence its couplings to the KK modes are strongly suppressed. This choice ensures the compliance with electroweak precision constraints."

What the Geometry Buys

Three things, and they are worth separating because each answers a different objection.

The scales coincide by construction. "The PS symmetry is broken by boundary conditions on the fifth dimension and the resulting massive vector leptoquark automatically has the same mass scale as the vector-like fermions and all other resonances." No hierarchy has to be arranged between the leptoquark and the composite fermions.

$K_L\to\mu e$ is suppressed without a high scale. "The suppression of the lepton flavour violating kaon decays can be achieved by introducing the SM fermions as zero modes of bulk fermions with their couplings to the KK modes determined by their localization along the RS bulk. Since the zero mode localizations are free parameters, one can obtain the required non-trivial flavour structure." And later, as a statement about the computed couplings: "under our assumptions no effects in KL → µe or K → πµe are generated. III. OBSERVABLES A."

The two anomalies get two mediators. Much of the literature uses only the vector leptoquark. Here the $W'$ also contributes, and the paper reports that "we have as well a W 0 contribution which enhances in our setup the total NP effect in b → cτ ν processes by roughly 80%. Still, we find".

The paper also gives the dual reading, which matters for the corpus's cross-references: "the 4D dual theory, according to the AdS/CFT correspondence, is a composite model with a global PS symmetry and KK modes corresponding to composite resonances."

The Fermion Sector

Three generations of chiral fermions — $q_i$, $\ell_i$, $d_i$, $u_i$, $e_i$ — plus three generations of vector-like partners, written with capitals. The mass terms are

$$ \mathcal L_M=-M^L_{ij}\left(\bar Q^L_iQ^R_j+\bar L^L_iL^R_j\right)-M^R_{ij}\left(\bar U^L_iU^R_j+\bar D^L_iD^R_j+\bar E^L_iE^R_j\right) -m^{q_L}_{ij}\bar q_iQ^L_j-m^{\ell_L}_{ij}\bar\ell_iL^L_j-\dots+\mathrm{h.c.}, $$

and the key statement is that "$Q_i$, $L_i$, $U_i$, $D_i$ and $E_i$ are embedded into complete representations under the PS gauge group, enforcing equality of the respective mass terms". Completeness of the multiplets is what makes the masses degenerate, and degeneracy is what makes the model predictive.

The mixing is assumed to be diagonal, with $M_1$ "zero or negligibly small", so "the first generation is purely elementary while the second and third generation of left-handed SM quarks and leptons are partially composite". Perturbativity of the brane Yukawas restricts the mixing angles, $s^f_i\le\sqrt{3/2}$.

Flavour Alignment, and Where FCNC Can Be Escaped

To avoid tree-level flavour-changing neutral currents in the down sector, "we assume the down Yukawa coupling to be aligned with mqL and M qL , i.e. diagonal in the same basis. In the left-handed up-quark sector, tree-level FCNCs are then unavoidable, but are determined and suppressed by the small off-diagonal elements of the Cabibbo-KobayashiMaskawa (CKM) matrix."

This asymmetry is not a choice among equals — it is forced. Down-sector alignment can be imposed; up-sector alignment cannot, because the CKM matrix sits in the up sector in this basis, and its off-diagonal entries are small. So the model's FCNC problem is confined to a place where the data already says it is small.

The couplings are enhanced by the volume factor $\theta=k\pi R\sim6$, with $g^{V^*}_{L,R}=\theta g^V_{\rm SM}$, and the flavour matrices $\Gamma$ are given explicitly — for the leptoquark,

$$ \Gamma^{LQ,L}_{d_i\ell_j}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&s^q_2&s^{\ell_2}c_\ell s^q_2\\0&-s^q_3s^\ell_3&s^\ell_3c_\ell s^q_3\end{pmatrix}, $$

so that the second and third generations carry the couplings and the first generation carries none. That is the same generation structure Barbieri and Tesi obtain from $U(2)^5$, here produced by localisation.

The Results

For $b\to s\ell^+\ell^-$, with $\mathcal O_{9(10)}=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}[\bar s\gamma^\mu P_Lb][\bar\ell\gamma_\mu(\gamma^5)\ell]$,

$$ C_9^{fi}=-C_{10}^{fi}=\frac{-\sqrt2\pi\,\kappa_{2i}\kappa^*_{3f}}{2G_FV_{tb}V^*_{ts}\,\alpha M^2}, $$

and the allowed range at $2\sigma$, $22$–$-0.37\ge C^{22}_9=-C^{22}_{10}\ge-0.88$, can be met. For $b\to c\ell\nu$ the coefficient is

$$ C_L^{fi}=\frac{m_W^2}{4G_FV_{cb}}\left(\frac{\sqrt2\,\kappa^*_{3f}V_{2k}\kappa_{ki}}{M^2}+\frac{\theta\,\Gamma^{W,L}_{\ell_f\ell_i}}{M^2}\right), $$

with the first term from the leptoquark and the second from the $W'$ KK mode. The global fit wants $C_L^{\rm NP}=0.131\pm0.033$, and the model reaches "an O(5%) effect in R(X)/R(X)SM is possible." — about $1.07$ at the benchmark point — which is not enough.

The obstruction is stated with its cause. "ing D0 − D̄0 mixing receives tree level contributions from the KK modes of the gluon, the B − L gauge boson and the W 3 .", giving

$$ C_L=\frac{\theta^2}{4M^2}\left(\tfrac43g_s^2+\tfrac12g_2^2\right)\left(V^*_{cs}V_{cb}s^{q2}_3+V^*_{us}V_{ub}s^{q2}_3\right), $$

against the bound $|\mathrm{Im}[M^D_{12}]|<2\times10^{-16}$ GeV. Chasing $b\to c\tau\nu$ needs a large third-generation compositeness $s^q_3$; that same parameter drives $D-\bar D$ mixing. The two cannot both be satisfied. The second obstruction is $\tau\to3\mu$, mediated by the neutral $B-L$ and $W^3$ KK modes, against $\mathcal B(\tau\to3\mu)<1.2\times10^{-8}$.

The Predictions

The model does make one sharp statement in a channel with a live experimental programme: "ermore, the solution of the b → sµ+ µ− anomaly in our model predicts a large branching ratio for τ → 3µ within the reach of Belle II. Due". It also predicts small effects in $b\to s\tau\mu$ and $b\to s\tau^+\tau^-$, and CP violation in the $D-\bar D$ system "close to the current experimental values".

The direct-search limit is handled carefully: $t\bar t$ resonance searches constrain an RS KK gluon above $3.3$ TeV for bulk fermions with flavour anarchy, but "in our setup ... the branching ratio into $t\bar t$ final states is significantly smaller than in the flavour-anarchic scenario, so that we can conservatively lower the mass scale of the lightest resonances to $M=3.0$ TeV."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

  1. A gauge symmetry can be broken by boundary conditions, with no scalar at all

The idea. Put the group in a warped fifth dimension and impose different boundary conditions on the two branes. The group that survives in four dimensions is the intersection of what the two boundaries preserve. No field acquires a vacuum expectation value.

What it would add. A mechanism the corpus does not contain, for the step it spends most of its words on. articles_physics/the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md breaks $G_{PS}\to G_{SM}$ with a scalar vev, and so does every other conversion so far. Here the breaking is a statement about the geometry, and the consequences are unusual: there is no breaking scalar to give a mass, so the scale is the compactification scale, and every state that the broken part of the group would have produced appears at that one scale.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's section on how the group breaks, as a paragraph on alternatives to the vev.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no extra dimension, and its frame is four-dimensional by construction. So the import is a contrast, not a construction: the corpus can say that its breaking is by a vev because its arena is four-dimensional, and that in a five-dimensional arena the same group breaks without one. That is honest and it is a sentence the article can carry.

  1. The

$K_L\to\mu e$ price has a third currency: localisation

The idea. Do not raise the scale and do not make the couplings generation-dependent by hand. Instead put the fermions at different positions in the bulk, and their overlap with the leptoquark's wavefunction — hence the coupling — is set by where they sit. The bound is met with the leptoquark at $3$ TeV.

What it would add. The third answer to the corpus's most expensive constraint. The corpus pays $250$ TeV. Barbieri and Tesi pay with $U(2)^5$ and $s_{\ell2}/s_{\ell3}\approx0.04$. Blanke and Crivellin pay with geometry, and pay least: the state stays at $3$ TeV, inside LHC reach. Three papers, three currencies, one bound — the bound is not a property of the group, it is a property of the assignment of the fermions to the group's representations.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's $K_L\to\mu e$ paragraph, next to the Barbieri material. The paragraph currently reads as a single price; it should read as a menu, with the corpus's own choice marked.

What stands in the way. The corpus's frame has no bulk and no positions, so its fermions cannot be localised anywhere. But the structural lesson transfers without the geometry: the bound constrains the product of coupling and inverse mass squared, so a model that fixes the coupling fixes the mass and a model that leaves the coupling free does not. That is worth stating once, correctly.

3. Two mediators from one group, interfering constructively

The idea. The leptoquark and the $W'$ both contribute to $b\to c\tau\nu$, and their amplitudes add, giving around $80\%$ larger an effect than the leptoquark alone. Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's boson table, as a note that the entries are not independent measurement channels but a single multiplet's components.

4. Which sector can be aligned, and why the other cannot

The idea. Align the down Yukawa with the mass matrix and down-sector FCNC vanishes at tree level. Where it lands. The article on the CKM matrix and mixing, and the Pati–Salam article's flavour paragraph.

5. The prediction to watch:

The idea. The parameters needed to fix $b\to s\mu^+\mu^-$ force a $\tau\to3\mu$ branching ratio that is large and near the current bound, so an improvement in sensitivity either sees it or excludes the model. Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's falsifiability item, as a second mode of prediction.

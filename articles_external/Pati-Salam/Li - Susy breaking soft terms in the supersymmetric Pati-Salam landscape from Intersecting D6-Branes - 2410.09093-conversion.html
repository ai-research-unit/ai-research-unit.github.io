What the Paper Does

It computes the soft terms for every viable model, and finds that T-dual pairs do not have equal spectra.

"We investigate the supersymmetry breaking soft terms for all the viable models in the complete landscape of three-family supersymmetric Pati-Salam models arising from intersecting D6-branes on a $T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2)$ orientifold."

"The calculations are performed in the general scenario of $u$-moduli dominance with the $s$-moduli turned on, where the soft terms remain independent of the Yukawa couplings and the Wilson lines."

"The results for the trilinear coupling, gaugino-masses, squared-mass parameters of squarks, sleptons and Higgs depend on the brane wrapping numbers and the susy breaking parameters."

"We find that unlike the Yukawa couplings which remain unchanged for the models dual under the exchange of two $SU(2)$ sectors, the corresponding soft term parameters only match for the trilinear coupling and the mass of the gluino. This can be explained by the internal geometry where the Yukawa interactions depend only on the triangular areas of the worldsheet instantons while the soft terms have an additional dependence on the orientation-angles of D6-branes in the three two-tori."

"In the special limit of parameter space we find universal masses for the Higgs and the gauginos."

That T-duality breaking of the soft terms is the paper's most interesting result and it corrects the intuition from 1910.04530 and 1912.11633.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why T-Duality Fails for Soft Terms

The argument, in plain terms. The Yukawa coupling for a three-point interaction comes from a worldsheet instanton — a string worldsheet stretched between three branes — and its magnitude is $e^{-A}$ where $A$ is the area of a triangle in the internal space.

Area is invariant under the relevant relabelling of branes, so T-dual (or $L\leftrightarrow R$ exchanged) configurations have identical Yukawa couplings. That is why 1910.04530's equivalence classes work for the Yukawa sector.

But the soft terms come from the gravity-mediated contributions, which involve the Kähler potential and its derivatives, and those depend on the orientation angles of the branes in each two-torus — not only on the areas.

Orientation angles are not invariant under the exchange of two stacks in general, so the soft terms differ even when the Yukawas agree.

The corpus should record this as a sharp technical result: "equivalent models" in the Yukawa sense are not equivalent in the soft sector. And the reason is geometric: areas are symplectic invariants; angles are not.

What Matches and What Does Not

"the corresponding soft term parameters only match for the trilinear coupling and the mass of the gluino"

So two of the soft parameters survive the duality and the rest do not. The trilinear $A$-term and $M_3$ are the ones determined by the overall volume and the dilaton, which are duality-invariant; the scalar masses depend on the angles, which are not.

The corpus should note which observables this affects: squark and slepton masses, hence the LHC spectrum and the dark matter relic density. So the "33 models" of 2112.09630 are 33 Yukawa classes, and the soft sector sees more.

That is a significant qualification and the corpus should attach it to every claim about "the 33 models".

$u$-Moduli Dominance

"the general scenario of $u$-moduli dominance with the $s$-moduli turned on"

The three Kähler moduli of $T^6/(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2)$ are usually called $t_i$ or $u_i$, and the dilaton is $s$. "$u$-moduli dominance" means the SUSY-breaking $F$-terms are dominated by the complex-structure moduli $u_i$, with the dilaton's contribution turned on as well.

That is a specific mediation scheme, not the general case, and it is chosen because in this limit the soft terms decouple from the Yukawa couplings and Wilson lines, which makes them computable.

The corpus should record the assumption: the soft-term results are for one mediation pattern. A different pattern (dilaton domination, or mirage mediation as in 2009.04582) gives different answers.

Universal Masses in a Limit

"In the special limit of parameter space we find universal masses for the Higgs and the gauginos"

So there is a corner where the model becomes mSUGRA-like, which makes it comparable with the standard SUSY searches. That is the useful limit for collider work: the corpus can compare these models with the constrained MSSM in that corner.

And outside it, the spectrum is non-universal, which is the interesting case for flavour physics but harder to search for.

The Scope

76 pages, all the viable models, all the soft parameters. This is a reference computation.

The corpus should cite it for soft spectra rather than re-deriving, exactly as it cites 2409.09110 for Yukawas.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is a correction to the cluster's central count, and the corpus should treat it as such.

2112.09630 says: 33 equivalence classes. Here: those classes are defined by Yukawa couplings and gauge-coupling relations, and the soft terms distinguish more.

So the landscape's true multiplicity depends on which sector you look at, and the corpus should stop saying "33 models" and start saying "33 Yukawa classes".

The deeper point: this is the same lesson as 2303.02864's hidden-sector sensitivity — the census's equivalence relations are sector-dependent, and each sector reveals new distinctions. Two papers, two corrections, both reducing the confidence of a clean count.

And that is the right way for a census to end: not with a number but with a map of what the number counts.

What the Paper Does

It builds a $Z_6$ orbifold Pati–Salam model with physical brane localisation, and gets a $D_4$ family symmetry for free.

"we construct a class of three-family Pati-Salam models in a $Z_6$ abelian symmetric orbifold with two discrete Wilson lines."

"In the limit where one of the six compactified dimensions (which lies in a $Z_2$ sub-orbifold) is large compared to the string length scale, our models reproduce the supersymmetry and gauge symmetry breaking pattern of 5d orbifold grand unified theories on an $S^1/\mathbb Z_2$ orbicircle."

"We find a horizontal $2+1$ splitting in the chiral matter spectra — 2 families of matter are localized on the $Z_2$ orbifold fixed points, and 1 family propagates in the 5d bulk — and identify them as the first-two and third families."

"Remarkably, the first two families enjoy a non-abelian dihedral $D_4$ family symmetry, due to the geometric setup of the compactified space."

"In all our models there are always some color triplets, i.e. $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ representations of the Pati-Salam group ... They could be utilized to spontaneously break the Pati-Salam symmetry"

"One model, with a 5d $E_6$ symmetry, may give rise to interesting low energy phenomenology. The $E_6$ model has a renormalizable Yukawa coupling only for the third family. It predicts a gauge-Yukawa unification relation at the 5d compactification scale"

Two facts are extraordinary: the family symmetry is geometric — a $D_4$ from the orbifold's fixed points — and the $2+1$ splitting assigns families to different dimensions.

Summary in the Authors' Words

A Geometric Origin for a Family Symmetry

This is the batch's most striking result about family symmetries, and the corpus should record it as such.

In most of the batch (1406.7005, 1605.02072, 2404.06520) the family symmetry is imposed — $S_4$, $A_4$, $S_3$, or a modular group is chosen, and its representations are assigned to the fermions.

Here it emerges: two families sit at the $Z_2$ fixed points of the orbifold, and the fixed points of a $Z_2$ on a torus come in pairs related by the orbifold action. The symmetry permuting them is the dihedral group $D_4$ — the symmetry of a square, which is precisely the arrangement of the four fixed points of $T^2/\mathbb Z_2$.

So $D_4$ is the geometric symmetry of the fixed-point set, and the first two families transform under it.

And the third family is a bulk field, a singlet under $D_4$.

The corpus should present this as the answer to a question the modular-symmetry papers ask: where does the family symmetry come from? The answer here: from the compactification's geometry. And the modular papers' answer: from the modulus's residual symmetry. Both are geometric, and they are the two modern options.

That is a strong structural statement, and it unifies the batch's flavour branches.

The $2+1$ Splitting

"2 families of matter are localized on the $Z_2$ orbifold fixed points, and 1 family propagates in the 5d bulk — and identify them as the first-two and third families"

That is the orbifold-GUT mechanism applied to families: localised fields are heavy (their wavefunctions are peaked), bulk fields are light (their wavefunctions are spread).

And it gives the right structure: the third family is heavy and the first two are light, because the third is in the bulk and overlaps more with the Higgs.

The corpus should record the mechanism: the mass hierarchy comes from wavefunction localisation, which is a fifth origin for the hierarchy in the batch, alongside FN flavons, modular weights, worldsheet areas, and strong dynamics.

And it explains why the third family is special: it is the only one in the bulk. The corpus's flavour section should present the four or five mechanisms side by side.

The $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ Colour Triplets

"In all our models there are always some color triplets, i.e. $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ representations"

Note the representation: $\mathbf6$ of $SU(4)_C$, not $\mathbf{15}$. The $\mathbf6$ is the antisymmetric two-index tensor, and it contains a colour triplet and an antitriplet with $B-L=2/3$.

A $\mathbf6$ VEV can break $SU(4)\to SU(3)\times U(1)$ — it is one of the standard PS-breaking Higgs representations (the $\mathbf{10}$ of $SO(10)$ contains a $\mathbf6$ under PS).

So the orbifold forces the model to have PS-breaking Higgses, which is convenient. And the corpus should note the representation, because the batch's breaking sector is usually the $\mathbf4$ or the $\mathbf{10}$; the $\mathbf6$ is the third option.

The $E_6$ Model

"One model, with a 5d $E_6$ symmetry ... has a renormalizable Yukawa coupling only for the third family. It predicts a gauge-Yawawa unification relation at the 5d compactification scale"

So the model is a 5d $E_6$ orbifold GUT, and the $E_6$ is broken by the orbifold to Pati–Salam × something.

"A renormalizable Yukawa coupling only for the third family" — that is the rank-one structure again, and here it is a consequence of the wavefunction overlap: only the bulk family overlaps the brane-localised Higgs enough for a renormalisable coupling.

"gauge-Yukawa unification relation" — the $E_6$ symmetry relates the gauge and Yukawa couplings at the compactification scale, which is the 5d orbifold-GUT prediction (as in $SU(5)$ orbifold GUTs, where $g$ and $y_t$ unify).

The corpus should record it: this is the batch's only orbifold-GUT Pati–Salam, and its prediction is gauge-Yukawa unification at the compactification scale, not the GUT scale. That is a different boundary condition from 1703.09309's or hep-ph/0303192's.

The Relation to Raby's Other Papers

Raby appears five times in this batch, with 1601.03749, 1612.04382, 1703.09309, 2009.04582, and this paper. This is the earliest.

And it is the only one where the family symmetry is geometric rather than imposed. So the corpus can trace a development: $D_4$ from geometry (2004) → $S_4$/FN imposed (2016) → the $SO(10)$ and PS hybrid models.

The corpus should say that: Raby's programme began with an orbifold and moved to field theory, which is the opposite of the usual direction.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's most important ancestor for the flavour question, and the corpus should say why.

The batch's later flavour papers all choose a family group. This one derives it. And the derivation is simple: the fixed points of $T^2/\mathbb Z_2$ form a square, and the square has $D_4$ symmetry.

That is the same logic as modular symmetry: the modular group's finite quotients ($S_4$, $A_4$, $S_3$) are the symmetries of the torus's shape, and the Yukawa couplings are modular forms. Two geometric routes — fixed-point permutation and modular transformation — and both give the family symmetry as a residual symmetry of the compactification.

The corpus should present the flavour chapter that way: not as a list of imposed groups, but as a question about the geometry, with two answers. And note that hep-ph/0409098 is the earlier and less developed of the two.

The second point: the $2+1$ splitting is worth keeping for a different reason. It predicts that the first two families are degenerate under $D_4$, which is approximately true (the up and charm, the down and strange, are closer to each other than to the top). The corpus should note the success, because it is a rare case where a family symmetry's prediction is visible in the data without fitting.

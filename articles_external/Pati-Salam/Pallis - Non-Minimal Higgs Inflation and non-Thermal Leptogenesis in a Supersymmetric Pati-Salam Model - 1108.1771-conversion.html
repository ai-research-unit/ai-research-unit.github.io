What the Paper Does

It makes the PS-breaking Higgs the inflaton, with a non-minimal coupling to gravity.

"SUSY) Grand Unified Theory (GUT) based on the gauge group GPS = SU (4)C × SU (2)L × SU (2)R , which incorporates non-minimal chaotic inflation, driven by a quartic potential associated with the Higgs fields involved in the spontaneous breaking of GPS ."

"The inflationary model relies on renormalizable superpotential terms and does not lead to overproduction of magnetic monopoles. It is largely independent of the oneloop radiative corrections and can become consistent with the current observational data on the inflationary observables, with the symmetry breaking scale of GPS assuming its SUSY value."

"ue. Within our model, the strong CP and the µ problems of the minimal SUSY standard model can be resolved via a Peccei-Quinn symmetry."

"baryogenesis occurs via nonthermal leptogenesis realized by the out-of-equilibrium decay of the right-handed neutrinos, which are produced by the inflaton’s decay. We consider two versions of such a scenario, assuming that the inflaton can decay to the lightest or to the next-to-lightest right-handed neutrino."

"Both scenaria can become compatible with the constraints arising from the baryon asymmetry of the universe, the gravitino limit on the reheating temperature and the upper bound on the light neutrino masses, provided that the gravitino is somehow heavy."

The last clause is the honest caveat, and the corpus should keep it.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Higgs Inflation With a Non-Minimal Coupling

The idea: the inflaton is the PS-breaking Higgs, and it couples to gravity non-minimally: $$S\supset\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_P^2+\xi|\Phi|^2}{2}R-|\partial\Phi|^2-V(\Phi)\right].$$

The non-minimal coupling $\xi$ flattens the potential at large field values, so a quartic potential $V=\lambda|\Phi|^4$ becomes a plateau, and slow-roll inflation works.

That is Higgs inflation (Bezrukov–Shaposhnikov), applied to the Pati–Salam Higgs. And $\xi\sim10^4$ is needed for the observed amplitude.

The corpus should record the mechanism, because it is the batch's most economical inflation: no new field, no new scale — the GUT Higgs is the inflaton.

And note the tension: a large $\xi$ is technically natural but leads to a low cutoff ($\Lambda\sim M_P/\xi$), so the theory's UV completion matters. The corpus should flag the unitarity issue, because it is Higgs inflation's standard problem and the paper does not resolve it.

Monopoles

"

does not lead to overproduction of magnetic monopoles"

Why: the inflationary potential is built from the PS-breaking Higgs's own quartic, so inflation happens during the PS breaking, and the monopoles formed at the end are diluted by the remaining $e$-folds.

So the ordering is: PS breaking and inflation simultaneous, monopoles diluted, strings (if any) diluted too.

The corpus should note the consequence: this model cannot produce a string network after inflation, so it cannot explain 2308.13248's PTA signal. The same incompatibility as 2601.14580, from a different direction.

The Peccei–Quinn Solution

"the minimal SUSY standard model can be resolved via a Peccei-Quinn symmetry."

A PQ symmetry does two jobs: it makes $\theta_{\rm QCD}$ dynamical (solving strong CP) and it forbids the $\mu$ term, generating it from the PQ-breaking VEV.

And the axion is a dark matter candidate — which the corpus's dark-matter section should note, because it is a PS-adjacent dark matter candidate distinct from the neutralino of 2606.29811.

The corpus should record the economy: one symmetry, two problems, one new particle (the axion). That is the kind of multiple-duty mechanism the corpus's "why this model" argument likes.

The Two Leptogenesis Versions Version 1: inflaton decays to the lightest $N_R$. Version 2: to the next-to-lightest. The difference matters because the CP asymmetry is larger for $N_2$ (it can interfere with $N_1$'s contributions), so version 2 is more efficient but needs $M_{N_2}$ above the inflaton's mass. And both need the gravitino somehow heavy, because: - non-thermal leptogenesis needs a high enough $T_{\rm RH}$ (or a heavy enough $M_{N}$), - and a light gravitino is overproduced at high $T_{\rm RH}$. So the scenario forces a heavy gravitino, which means gravity mediation and a high SUSY scale, which the corpus should note — it is a strong statement about the soft sector from cosmology alone.

Why This Matters for the Corpus's Cosmology

The batch's cosmology papers are fragmented (my earlier note). This one is one of the few that does inflation and baryogenesis together, and it does so with the GUT Higgs.

And it states its costs plainly: a large $\xi$ (cutoff), a heavy gravitino, and no strings.

That is a fair accounting, and the corpus should present it as a scenario with costs, not a success.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and 1207.3730 are the batch's clearest examples of the GUT Higgs does everything, and the corpus should record the pattern.

The PS-breaking Higgs: breaks the symmetry, inflates, and (through its couplings) seeds the right-handed neutrino production. Three jobs, one field.

That is the opposite of the string constructions, where the breaking, the inflation, and the leptogenesis each need separate sectors (flux, moduli, hidden sector).

And the corpus should note the trade: the field-theory economy comes at the price of a large non-minimal coupling and a heavy gravitino; the string constructions avoid those but need more ingredients.

That is a fair comparison, and it is the kind the corpus should make: not "which is better" but what does each cost.

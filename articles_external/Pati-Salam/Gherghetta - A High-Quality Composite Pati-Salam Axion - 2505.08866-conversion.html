What the Paper Does

It builds a QCD axion whose Peccei–Quinn symmetry is an accidental symmetry of a confining gauge theory, and whose Standard Model embedding is Pati–Salam — and the Pati–Salam embedding then fixes the axion's coupling to photons.

The identity the paper is built on:

$$ SO(6)\times SO(4)=SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R, $$

which "is nothing but the Pati-Salam group".

The model: an $SU(N_c)$ gauge theory with ten flavours, so an $SU(10)_L\times SU(10)_R$ flavour symmetry, with $SO(6)\times SO(4)\subset SU(10)_L$ and $Sp(10)\subset SU(10)_R$ weakly gauged. Confinement breaks $SU(10)_L\times SU(10)_R\to SU(10)_V$, and the condensates break the weakly-gauged groups to

$$ U(3)\times U(2)\simeq SU(3)_c\times U(1)_{B-L}\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_{I_{3R}}, $$

which a Higgs then breaks to the Standard Model.

The headline result:

"the embedding of the Standard Model into the PatiSalam group, fixes the electromagnetic charges of the SU(Nc ) fermions. This leads to a unique value for the anomaly ratio, E/N = −7/3 and therefore our model predicts a relatively large value for the axion-photon coupling that is much more accessible in future experiments than most models in the literature."

That is the paper's point, stated compactly: the Pati–Salam embedding is what makes the axion's photon coupling predictable. For a KSVZ or DFSZ axion you choose the fermion charges and $E/N$ is a free parameter; here there is nothing to choose, because the charges are the Standard Model ones arranged into $\mathbf4$s.

And the quality result:

"due to the gauge and Lorentz structure, there are no U(1)PQ -violating operators of dimension < 12 that modify the axion potential. The first PQviolating operator contributing to the axion potential is a dimension twelve, eight-fermion operator. If this is gen- 2 erated at the reduced Planck scale MP = 2.4×1018 GeV, then the axion can still solve the strong CP problem provided the axion decay constant fa ≲ 1011 GeV. Thus"

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Quality Problem, Plainly

"The Peccei–Quinn symmetry is clearly not a gauge symmetry because it is anomalous under SU(3)c , by definition, to solve the strong CP problem. It is susceptible to being explicitly broken and hence we can hope that U(1)PQ is an accidental symmetry due to gauge symmetries in the theory. For example, U(1)B in the Standard Model is an accidental symmetry because there are no gauge-invariant operators that can violate U(1)B for dimension D < 6 (see Ref."

That analogy is the cleanest statement of the axion quality problem in the literature and worth recording. Baryon number is safe not because anything forbids its violation but because nothing can violate it below dimension six, and dimension six is suppressed by $M_{\rm Pl}^2$. Peccei–Quinn needs the same protection and does not automatically have it, because the leading PQ-violating operator is usually dimension five and its contribution to $\bar\theta$ is too large.

"there are good reasons to believe that global symmetries do not exist in theories of quantum gravity and the Peccei– Quinn symmetry cannot be respected by Planck-scale effects [4–11]. More" "If taken seriously, the only exact symmetries of nature are gauge symmetries while global symmetries are violated by quantum gravity effects. Therefore"

So the requirement is quantitative: whatever Planck-scale operators violate PQ, their contribution to $\bar\theta$ must stay below $10^{-10}$, the neutron EDM bound. That is the quality problem stated as a number rather than as a principle.

The Composite Route

"This can arise in composite axion models where the underlying strong dynamics plays the dual role of spontaneously breaking the global symmetry and naturally generating the scale of the axion decay constant."

Two jobs from one dynamics. In an ordinary axion model you put in a complex scalar, choose its potential, and tune the scale. In a composite model the confining theory breaks the symmetry and sets $f_a\sim\Lambda/(4\pi)$ — so there is no scale to choose, and no potential to write.

"We introduce a vector-like SU(Nc ) confining group with ten flavors that admits an SU(10)L ×SU(10)R flavor symmetry."

Ten flavours is the Pati–Salam number. The $\mathbf{16}$ of $SO(10)$ contains $4+4+4+4$ Weyl components when decomposed under $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$, and ten is what is needed for $SO(6)\times SO(4)$ to sit inside. The flavour number is not chosen for axion reasons; it is chosen so that the Standard Model fits.

How Pati–Salam Enters

"The ten quarks transforming as Nc are also charged under a weakly-gauged SO(6)×SO(4) ⊂ SU(10)L , while the ten anti-quarks transforming as N̄c are charged under a weakly-gauged Sp(10) ⊂ SU(10)R . When the QCD-like SU(Nc ) gauge theory confines, the usual chiral symmetry breaking breaks the flavor symmetry to its diagonal subgroup SU(10)V . As a result, the quark condensates induce a dynamical breaking of SO(6)×SO(4)×Sp(10) to the diagonal U(3)×U(2) ≃ SU (3)c ×U (1)B−L ×SU (2)L × U (1)I3R gauge group"

The Standard Model gauge group emerges from the strong dynamics. That is the sentence to keep: "A unique feature of our model is that the Standard Model gauge group emerges from the strong dynamics associated with the Peccei–Quinn mechanism."

In most composite axion models the Standard Model is put in by hand and the confining group is added. Here the confining group is what produces the Standard Model, and the axion is a by-product. That inversion is the paper's structural idea.

The residual group is $SU(3)_c\times U(1)_{B-L}\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_{I_{3R}}$ — which is Pati–Salam's unbroken part with the $B-L$ and $I_{3R}$ generators made explicit — and a Higgs breaks the two $U(1)$s to hypercharge via the combination

$$ Y=\tfrac12(B-L)+I_{3R}, $$

done by a scalar in $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$, which is part of the $\mathbf{126}$ in $SO(10)$.

Why $E/N=-7/3$ Is Fixed

"nce the SU(Nc ) quarks are chiral with respect to SO(6) × SO(4) and Sp(10), the automatic PQ symmetry can also be understood as due to the chiral transformations of the SU(Nc ) quarks under the weakly-gauged product gauge groups. Thus"

"since the $SU(N_c)$ quarks remain vector-like under the $SU(N_c)$ confining group, the underlying strong dynamics robustly predicts a high-quality, composite Pati–Salam axion."

Vector-like under the confining group, chiral under the weakly-gauged flavour group — that is the precise structure that gives an accidental PQ symmetry. A global symmetry is accidental if no gauge-invariant operator can violate it below some dimension; chirality under the gauged subgroup is what forbids the low-dimension operators, because a mass term is forbidden by the gauge symmetry and every PQ-violating operator has to be built from enough fermions to be gauge invariant.

Then the anomaly coefficients, computed from the Standard Model charges as arranged into $\mathbf4$s:

$$ U(1)_{\rm PQ}\times[SU(3)_c]^2:\quad N=4N_c, $$ $$ U(1)_{\rm PQ}\times[U(1)_{\rm EM}]^2:\quad E=-\tfrac{28}{3}N_c, $$

so

$$ \frac{E}{N}=-\frac{28/3}{4}=-\frac73 . $$

The $N_c$ cancels, so the ratio is fixed for any confining group. That is why the prediction is unique: the colour multiplicity drops out of the ratio and only the charge assignments survive.

"Our model is a mixture: while SM quarks remain PQ-neutral, the axion-photon coupling is still fixed because the massless fermions have specific transformation properties under the Pati–Salam gauge group, fixing their electromagnetic charges via the Standard Model embedding in the unified group. Therefore, our model is completely predictive."

PQ-neutral SM quarks is what makes it a mixture. In KSVZ the axion couples to new heavy quarks and the SM quarks are neutral; in DFSZ the SM quarks carry PQ charge. This model is KSVZ-like in that the SM quarks are neutral, yet the photon coupling is determined because the new fermions carry Standard Model charges through the Pati–Salam embedding. So it has KSVZ's structure and DFSZ's predictivity.

The Prediction, and How It Compares

$$ g_{a\gamma\gamma}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm EM}}{2\pi f_a}\left(-\frac73-1.92(4)\right), $$

where the second term is the mixing with the QCD pseudoscalars ($\pi^0$, $\eta$, $\eta'$).

The minus sign is the point. "Since the ratio E/N is negative for our Pati-Salam axion, Eq. (18) implies a rather large value for |gaγγ |. This larger axion-photon coupling lies at the upper end of the axion band and thus will be more accessible in future experiments."

The comparison is quantitative: "in both DFSZ models and grand unified theories with a simple unifying group, one finds E/N = 8/3 [26, 31], which leads to a significantly smaller value of gaγγ compared to our model. Similarly"

$$ \left(-\frac73-1.92\right)\approx-4.25 \qquad\text{versus}\qquad \left(\frac83-1.92\right)\approx+0.75 . $$

The cancellation that suppresses the DFSZ coupling nearly cancels the Pati–Salam one in the opposite direction. $8/3\approx2.67$ sits close to $1.92$, so DFSZ gets a small $g_{a\gamma\gamma}$; $-7/3\approx-2.33$ adds to $1.92$ instead of subtracting, giving a coupling about $5.7$ times larger in magnitude. That factor is why the model is testable.

"even IAXO could test our model for ma ≳ 4 meV. This value"

A concrete experimental target and it is not far off. IAXO's reach in the meV mass range is where this lands, which is why the paper singles it out.

The Dimension-12 Operator

"The first PQ-violating operator contributing to the axion potential is a dimension twelve, eight-fermion operator."

The residual misalignment:

$$ |\bar\theta_{\rm eff}|\simeq\frac{4N_c\,|c_{\not PQ}|\,g_*^{10}}{4!\,4!\,M_P^8}\left(\frac{f_a^{12}}{N_c\,\chi_{\rm QCD}}\right)^{1/2}\sin\delta, $$

with $\chi_{\rm QCD}\equiv m_\pi^2f_\pi^2\frac{m_um_d}{(m_u+m_d)^2}$ the QCD topological susceptibility.

"isfying 1 ≲ g∗ ≲ 4π. The resulting residual θ̄eff obtained from Eq. (10) remains below the current limits 10−10 as long as fa ≲ 1011 GeV (see Fig."

The scaling is what matters: $\bar\theta_{\rm eff}\propto f_a^{6}/M_P^4$ once you take the square root, so the quality bound translates directly into a bound on $f_a$. The dimension of the leading operator sets the exponent, and dimension twelve is high enough that $f_a$ can reach $10^{11}$ GeV instead of the $10^8$ GeV a dimension-five operator would allow.

"y large coupling to photons while a residual θ̄eff may be just below the current limit on the neutron electric dipole moment. If"

That is a prediction at the edge of an existing bound — the same position as the LFV predictions in 1608.04124, and for the same reason: the model's effect is suppressed by a high power of the new-physics scale, so it lands near where experiments currently reach.

Unification and Proton Decay

"in addition to the SM Higgs, we simply require an extra complex scalar field transforming as (10, 1, 3) under the Pati-Salam group (which is part of the 126 in SO(10)) to remain below the GUT scale. This field is also responsible for achieving the final breaking of U(1) symmetries to U(1)Y . The SU(4)×SU(2)L ×SU(2)R gauge"

"etries to U(1)Y . The SU(4)×SU(2)L ×SU(2)R gauge couplings are found to unify into a single SO(10) coupling at a scale MGUT ≃ 2 × 1016 GeV, for an axion decay constant scale fa ≃ 5 × 1011 GeV. Not only"

One field does the double duty: the $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ breaks the two $U(1)$s to hypercharge and provides the intermediate-scale matter that makes the three Pati–Salam couplings meet at $2\times10^{16}$ GeV. Without it the couplings do not unify; with it they do, and the same field fixes the axion decay constant to $5\times10^{11}$ GeV.

So the chain is: $f_a\simeq5\times10^{11}$ GeV fixes $M_{\rm GUT}\simeq2\times10^{16}$ GeV, which "lso lead to an observable proton decay at the Hyper-Kamionkande experiment that is currently under construction (see, e."

Note that $f_a\simeq5\times10^{11}$ GeV sits just above the quality bound $f_a\lesssim10^{11}$ GeV. That is a mild tension the paper does not dwell on; the bound is with a coefficient of order one and $g_*$ uncertainties, so the values are compatible but not comfortably so.

The Domain Wall Problem, and Why

It Is Solved

"the domain wall problem may be avoided because one of the D = 12 PQ-violating operators explicitly breaks all the global discrete symmetry."

That is the third job the dimension-12 operator does. In a post-inflationary scenario the PQ symmetry breaking produces a network of domain walls bounded by strings, and the walls dominate the energy density unless something makes them decay. A PQ-violating operator of high dimension gives a small but nonzero potential difference between the vacua, so the walls are unstable and disappear — but the operator must not be too large or it ruins the quality.

"This breaking is irrelevant for the case when the PQbreaking occurs before or during inflation since domain walls can be inflated away, although at the cost of a low Hubble scale (≲ 106 GeV) during inflation to avoid large isocurvature fluctuations. However, if the PQ breaking occurs after inflation, then the domain wall network can decay provided fa ∼ 109 GeV, producing axions as dark matter [43]."

So the model works in both cosmological scenarios, with different $f_a$. Pre-inflationary: $f_a\lesssim10^{11}$ GeV, low-scale inflation, no tensor modes. Post-inflationary: $f_a\sim10^9$ GeV, walls decay, monopoles annihilate via Langacker–Pi, and "tensor modes can now be detected in the CMB B-mode polarization".

That is a genuine either-or with observable consequences, and it is the kind of statement a corpus should record: the same model, two cosmologies, two different signatures, and the tensor-to-scalar ratio decides which.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

  1. $SO(6)\times SO(4)$ is Pati–Salam, and that is what fixes $E/N=-7/3$

The idea. Writing $SU(4)\times SU(2)\times SU(2)$ as $SO(6)\times SO(4)$ makes the Standard Model embedding obvious, and the embedding then fixes the axion's electromagnetic anomaly.

What it would add. The cleanest available demonstration of Pati–Salam doing work outside unification. The corpus treats Pati–Salam as a GUT candidate whose merit is unification and $B-L$. Here the merit is that it organises the Standard Model fermions in a way that fixes a quantity nothing else fixes — and the quantity is measurable. That is a different kind of argument for the group and the corpus should have it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md, in the section on why the group is interesting, not only in a unification section.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The chain is: $SO(6)\simeq SU(4)$, $SO(4)\simeq SU(2)\times SU(2)$, the fermions in $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)\oplus(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, and the charges fixed.

  1. The accidental-symmetry argument, by analogy with baryon number

The idea. $U(1)_B$ is safe because no gauge-invariant operator violates it below dimension six. $U(1)_{\rm PQ}$ needs the same thing and gets it here at dimension twelve.

What it would add. A transferable criterion. The corpus's discussion of global symmetries, if it has one, treats them as either exact (and in conflict with quantum gravity) or broken (and useless). "Accidental" is the third option and it is the one that survives: the symmetry is not exact, it is merely not violated below a stated dimension, and that is enough because the suppression is by powers of $M_{\rm Pl}$.

Where it lands. Any corpus discussion of global symmetries, and the axion section if there is one.

What stands in the way. The dimension counting is model-dependent; the corpus should state the general principle and use this as the example, not as a theorem.

3. Confining dynamics can produce the Standard Model gauge group

The idea. The $SU(N_c)$ condensates break $SO(6)\times SO(4)\times Sp(10)\to U(3)\times U(2)$, and that is the Standard Model's parent. Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's where does the group come from discussion, if any.

4. $E/N=-7/3$ versus $8/3$: a factor of six in $g_{a\gamma\gamma}$

The idea. DFSZ and simple GUT axions give $E/N=8/3\approx2.67$, which nearly cancels against the $-1.92$ mixing term. Where it lands. The axion section, and the Pati–Salam article as a further merit of the group.

5. One dimension-12 operator fixes quality, domain walls, and $\bar\theta$

The idea. The leading PQ-violating operator is eight-fermion, dimension twelve. Where it lands. The axion section, as the quality-problem example.

6. Two cosmologies, two signatures

The idea. Pre-inflationary PQ breaking needs $H_{\rm inf}\lesssim10^6$ GeV and gives no tensor modes. Where it lands. The axion section, and any corpus discussion of inflation.

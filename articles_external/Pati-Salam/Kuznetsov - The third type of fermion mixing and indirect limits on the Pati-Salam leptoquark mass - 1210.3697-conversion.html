What the Paper Does

It identifies a mixing that other analyses miss, and shows that it weakens the leptoquark mass bound from thousands of TeV to about forty.

"The low-energy manifestations of a minimal extension of the electroweak standard model based on the quark-lepton symmetry SU (4)V ⊗ SU (2)L ⊗ GR of the Pati–Salam type are analyzed. Given this symmetry the third type of mixing in the interactions of the SU (4)V leptoquarks with quarks and leptons is shown to be required. An additional arbitrariness"

"An additional arbitrariness of the mixing parameters could allow, in principle, to decrease noticeably the indirect lower bound on the vector leptoquark mass originated from the low-energy rare processes, strongly suppressed in the standard model."

"the detailed analysis of" "yields constraints on the vector leptoquark mass that always involve the elements of the unknown mixing matrix D. Combining" "we have obtained in the case of identical mixings for left-handed and right-handed fermions the following lowest limit on the vector leptoquark mass: MX > 38 TeV. The lowest limit obtained in the asymmetric case" "appears to be: MX > 41 TeV."

So the bound is $\sim40$ TeV, not thousands. That matters for the corpus because it fixes how far Pati–Salam's leptoquark can be above the reach, and because it shows that the bound is mixing-dependent, not a hard number.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What the Three Mixings Are

In the Standard Model there are two mixing matrices: the CKM matrix for quarks and the PMNS matrix for leptons. In Pati–Salam the leptoquark couples a quark to a lepton, and there is a third matrix — the one that rotates between the quark and lepton mass bases in the leptoquark vertex.

Concretely: the leptoquark $X$ couples to $\bar q\,\ell$ through a matrix $D$, and $D$ is not determined by the CKM or PMNS matrices because the leptoquark couples left-handed quarks to right-handed leptons (and vice versa), combinations that the SM does not constrain.

So Pati–Salam has a new source of flavour violation with its own mixing angles, and the existing bounds on rare decays like $K_L^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$ constrain $M_X$ only in combination with the unknown $D$ elements.

That is the paper's structural point and the corpus should record it: the leptoquark mass bound is not a number; it is a surface in $(M_X, D)$ space. Papers that quote a single number are assuming a specific $D$.

The Bound's Dependence

"The constraints ... always involve the elements of the unknown mixing matrix $D$."

"in the case of identical mixings for left-handed and right-handed fermions" "in the asymmetric case (21)"

And the individual constraints are much stronger: the table shows $M_X/|D_{\mu d}D_{\mu b}|^{1/2}>143$ TeV from $B^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$, and similar for $K_L^0\to e^\pm\mu^\mp$.

So the "38 TeV" is the minimum over all choices of $D$ that still satisfy every constraint — i.e. the best case. A reader should understand that the typical bound is hundreds of TeV, and 38 TeV is what you get by tuning $D$. The corpus should state both numbers, because quoting "38 TeV" alone would misrepresent the constraint. 1002.2937 (Leontaris) and hep-ph/0303258 (Prikas) in this batch quote other values, and the corpus should compare.

Why This Matters for Pati–Salam

Pati–Salam's leptoquark is not optional. The gauge bosons of $SU(4)_C$ include the $X$ and $Y$ that mediate quark–lepton transitions, so every Pati–Salam model has them, and their mass is the breaking scale (up to the mixing). So the leptoquark bound is a lower bound on $M_{PS}$, and 38 TeV (or hundreds) is far above the TeV-scale models in this batch (2103.11889, 2106.03876, 0805.3486). That is a tension the corpus must record: the TeV-scale vector-leptoquark papers need the leptoquark to be light enough to explain $B$-anomalies, while the rare-decay bounds push it to tens or hundreds of TeV. The resolution in those papers is always that the relevant coupling is small — which in this language means the $D$ matrix elements are small, exactly the freedom this paper identifies. So 1210.3697 provides the technical justification for the TeV-scale papers' small couplings. The corpus should link them.

That hierarchy is a signature: the three-lepton final state dominates over the two-photon one over the single photon, which is the opposite of the usual dipole-dominated expectation.

**A leptoquark-mediated

$\mu\to e$ conversion gives this pattern**, and it is a clean discriminant between leptoquark and dipole operators. The corpus should record the hierarchy, because it is a prediction with a distinctive shape — rare muon decays from a leptoquark are not just suppressed dipole transitions.

The Method

**The analysis uses the standard

"leading logarithm resummation** for the rare processes, running from $M_X$ down to the hadronic scale $\mu\sim1$ GeV, because $\ln(M_X/\mu)\gg1$". So the bounds come from a QCD-corrected effective field theory, not from a tree-level estimate. That is why the numbers are large: the running amplifies the operators.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and its 1995 precursor are the batch's only dedicated leptoquark-phenomenology papers, and they supply a number the whole field uses loosely. The number is "the leptoquark mass MX . The three", and it does not exist. What exists is a bound on $M_X$ given a mixing matrix, and the mixing matrix is unconstrained. So the honest statement is: $M_X>38$ TeV at best, hundreds of TeV typically. The corpus should make this explicit, because the batch contains models at 1–10 TeV (2103.11889, 2106.03876, 0812.4318) and models at $10^{15}$ GeV (2504.01893, 2005.00012), and the bounds' mixing-dependence is what lets them all coexist. A reader who took "38 TeV as absolute would think the TeV models are dead; a reader who took thousands of TeV" would too. Neither is right.

The second point: the inverted hierarchy $\mu\to ee\bar e\gg\mu\to e\gamma$ is worth keeping, because it is a shape prediction and shapes survive when bounds move. The corpus's leptoquark section should be organised around signatures, not limits.

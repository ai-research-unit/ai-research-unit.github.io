Summary in the Authors' Words

$USp(2)$ Versus $U(2)$: What Changes

A $U(2)$ stack is two D-branes not on an orientifold plane. Its gauge group is $U(2)=SU(2)\times U(1)$, it has fundamental matter and antisymmetric matter ($\mathbf{1}$ of $SU(2)$ plus the $U(1)$), and it can be separated from the orientifold.

A $USp(2)$ stack is two D-branes on an orientifold plane. Its gauge group is $USp(2)\cong SU(2)$ with no $U(1)$ factor and no antisymmetric representation (the antisymmetric of $USp(2N)$ is the singlet, which is projected out).

Two consequences, both important:

  1. No extra $U(1)$: the $U(1)_{B-L}$ or $U(1)_R$ that a $U(2)_R$ stack would carry is absent. So $B-L$ must come from $U(4)_C$'s diagonal $U(1)$, which it does.
  2. No antisymmetric matter: the exotic $SU(2)$-triplet-like states disappear. That is a genuine simplification — fewer exotics to decouple.

And the cost: the antisymmetric matter was also the source of some Yukawa couplings, so removing it changes the flavour structure.

The corpus should record the trade: $USp(2)$ removes exotics and removes couplings. That is why only five models survive — most configurations need the antisymmetric to get the masses right.

Why Only Five

The paper's phrase is "severely restricts". The reason is the interplay of tadpole cancellation and the requirement of three generations.

With a $USp(2)$ group, the stack contributes differently to the RR tadpoles, and the number of allowed fillers and wrappings shrinks. So the enumeration finds five.

The corpus should note the number 5 against the 33 — a factor of seven reduction from a single group replacement. That is a strong selection effect, and it means the symplectic branch of the landscape is small and nearly exhaustible.

One Model Contains No Filler O6-Planes

This is the paper's most surprising individual result and it deserves emphasis.

Filler branes are the standard device for cancelling RR tadpoles: a stack of branes on top of an orientifold plane contributes a fixed amount to the tadpole, and fillers make up the difference. Every model in hep-th/0403061 and most in 2112.09630 has them.

"No filler O6-planes" means the model cancels its tadpoles with the visible stacks alone. That is a cleaner configuration: no extra hidden gauge group, no extra matter, no extra moduli from the fillers.

The corpus should record it as a milestone, because a model with no fillers is the closest these constructions come to a minimal brane configuration. 2112.09630's unique unified model may or may not have fillers; this one explicitly does not.

Composite Particles from Strong Dynamics

"the composite particles through strong coupling dynamics"

The $USp(2)$ groups can become strongly coupled, and then the "elementary" exotics confine into composite states. That is a way to hide exotics: instead of giving them a large mass, let them confine. That is the same mechanism as hep-ph/0505221's "confining" Pati–Salam — SUSY breaking and exotic hiding by strong dynamics rather than by a large scale. The corpus should link them: two papers in the batch use confinement to remove exotics, one at the level of the whole model (hep-ph/0505221) and one for specific states here.

The Dual Models

"Exchanging the left and right sectors, we obtain five dual models with gauge group U(4)C × U(2)L × USp(2)R . These ten models have different gauge coupling relations at string scale."

So the ten models are five plus their $L\leftrightarrow R$ swaps. That is the same duality as 1910.04530 and 2410.09093 — and 2410.09093 shows the soft terms do not respect it. The corpus should attach the caveat: the ten models are ten in the Yukawa sector and more in the soft sector.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is small in scope and contains two of the batch's sharpest facts. First: the symplectic branch of the landscape is five models, not thirty-three. So the "landscape" is really two landscapes, unitary and symplectic, with very different sizes. Second: a filler-free model exists. Every discussion of these models assumes filler branes are necessary; this shows they are not, in at least one case. The corpus should record it as an existence proof, because it means the "hidden" sector can sometimes be empty — which matters given 2303.02864's finding that hidden sectors are not decoupled.

The corpus's string section should present the cluster as a set of branches — unitary, symplectic, four-family, rigid-cycle — each with its own size and its own completest model, rather than as one landscape with one count.

What the Paper Does

It is the model this author's programme was built on: $A_4\times Z_5$ family symmetry inside Pati–Salam, giving a fully specified PMNS matrix and a Cabibbo angle.

"We propose an elegant theory of flavour based on $A_4\times Z_5$ family symmetry with Pati-Salam unification which provides an excellent description of quark and lepton masses, mixing and CP violation."

"The $A_4$ symmetry unifies the left-handed families and its vacuum alignment determines the columns of Yukawa matrices. The $Z_5$ symmetry distinguishes the right-handed families and its breaking controls CP violation in both the quark and lepton sectors."

"The Pati-Salam symmetry relates the quark and lepton Yukawa matrices, with $Y^u=Y^\nu$ and $Y^d\sim Y^e$."

That third line is the Georgi–Jarlskog relation, and here it is derived from the family symmetry rather than imposed by hand. That is the paper's central claim: Pati–Salam gives the quark-lepton relation and $A_4$ gives the mixing pattern, and together they fix everything.

"Using the see-saw mechanism with very hierarchical right-handed neutrinos and CSD4 vacuum alignment, the model predicts the entire PMNS mixing matrix and gives a Cabibbo angle $\theta_C\approx1/4$."

"In particular, for a discrete choice of $Z_5$ phases, it predicts maximal atmospheric mixing, $\theta_{23}^l=45^\circ\pm0.5^\circ$, and leptonic CP violating phase $\delta^l=260^\circ\pm5^\circ$. The reactor angle prediction is $\theta_{13}^l=9^\circ\pm0.5^\circ$, while the solar angle is $34^\circ\gtrsim\theta_{12}^l\gtrsim31^\circ$, for a lightest neutrino mass in the range $0\gtrsim m_1\gtrsim0.5$ meV, corresponding to a normal neutrino mass hierarchy and a very small rate for neutrinoless double beta decay."

Those are numbers, not bands, and they were predictions. In 2014 the reactor angle was known to be about $9^\circ$, the atmospheric angle about $45^\circ$, and $\delta$ unknown. So $\delta^l=260^\circ\pm5^\circ$ was the live prediction, and its falsification or confirmation is the test of the whole scheme.

Current data give $\delta\approx 200^\circ$–$240^\circ$ (normal ordering) — closer to $260^\circ$ than to zero but not comfortably inside $\pm5^\circ$. The corpus should record both the prediction and the present status, because it is a worked example of a precise flavour prediction meeting data.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Division of Labour

"The $A_4$ symmetry unifies the left-handed families and its vacuum alignment determines the columns of Yukawa matrices. The $Z_5$ symmetry distinguishes the right-handed families and its breaking controls CP violation."

Two symmetries, two jobs, and the split is what makes the model work: - $A_4$ acts on the left-handed doublets as a triplet, so the three columns of the Yukawa matrix are related by the group. - $Z_5$ acts on the right-handed fields as three different singlets, so the three rows are distinguished — and the phases of the $Z_5$ breaking give CP violation.

That is the cleanest statement of why flavour models need two symmetries: one to relate, one to distinguish. A single $A_4$ gives tri-bimaximal mixing with a vanishing reactor angle; a single $Z_5$ gives nothing. Together they give the observed pattern.

CSD and the Reactor Angle

"Constrained sequential dominance (CSD) involves the dominant right-handed neutrino [giving] ..."

  • "CSD1: $(1,1,-1)$ leading to TB mixing with zero reactor angle $\theta_{13}$"
  • "CSD2: $(1,2,0)$ giving $\theta_{13}$"
  • "CSD3: $(1,3,1)$ with a relative phase $\pm\pi/3$ giving $\theta_{13}$"
  • "CSD4: $(1,4,2)$, with a relative phase $\pm2\pi/5$ giving $\theta_{13}$"

The sequence is a measurement history written as model parameters. CSD1 was built for tri-bimaximal mixing when $\theta_{13}$ was thought to be zero; when Daya Bay measured $\theta_{13}\approx9^\circ$, the vacuum alignment was changed to CSD2, then CSD3, then CSD4.

The corpus should record this as a caution about the programme: a family symmetry's vacuum alignment can be adjusted to fit a newly measured angle, so a successful fit is weaker evidence than it appears. What is not adjustable is the relation between the angles, and that is where CSD4's predictive content lies.

"From the point of view of extending to the quark sector, CSD4 seems to be the [most suitable]."

Because CSD4's alignment is the one whose structure is compatible with the $Z_5$ that also generates the quark CP phase. So the choice is not free once the quark sector is included — which is a genuine constraint and worth noting.

The Quark–Lepton Relation

"The Pati-Salam symmetry relates the quark and lepton Yukawa matrices, with $Y^u=Y^\nu$ and $Y^d\sim Y^e$."

$Y^u=Y^\nu$ is exact in Pati–Salam at the unification scale, because $u$ and $\nu$ are in the same $SU(4)$ multiplet and couple to the same Higgs. $Y^d\sim Y^e$ with a factor of 3 is the Georgi–Jarlskog relation, which arises from the Higgs being in a $\mathbf{15}$ rather than a $\mathbf{1}$ of $SU(4)$.

So the model inherits one exact prediction and one factor-of-three relation, and the family symmetry must be consistent with both. That is the strongest form of unification available here: not just the gauge couplings but the Yukawa matrices.

The Cost of Exactness

"the largest Cabibbo angle $\theta_C\approx13^\circ$"

If $Y^u=Y^\nu$ exactly, then the CKM and PMNS matrices are built from the same rotations, and the Cabibbo angle must appear in both. The model predicts $\theta_C\approx1/4$ and gets it, but at the price that the leptonic mixing must accommodate the same rotation — which is why $\theta_{12}^l$ comes out at $31^\circ$–$34^\circ$ rather than the tri-bimaximal $35.3^\circ$.

That is a genuine success: the solar angle's deviation from tri-bimaximal is explained by the Cabibbo angle. The corpus should record it, because it is the clearest example in this batch of a unification relation producing a measured deviation.

The $0\nu\beta\beta$ Prediction

"a lightest neutrino mass in the range $0\gtrsim m_1\gtrsim0.5$ meV, corresponding to a normal neutrino mass hierarchy and a very small rate for neutrinoless double beta decay."

$m_1<0.5$ meV is essentially $m_1=0$, so the model has a hierarchical normal spectrum and $m_{\beta\beta}$ near its minimum. That is a sharp prediction which the next generation of experiments will test — and it is the same conclusion as 2404.06520 in this batch, which also finds $0\nu\beta\beta$ too small to see.

Two independent models reaching the same $0\nu\beta\beta$ conclusion is worth recording, because it is the kind of convergence that makes a prediction credible.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. One symmetry to relate, one to distinguish

The idea. $A_4$ on the left-handed triplets fixes the columns; $Z_5$ on the right-handed singlets distinguishes the rows and supplies CP.

What it would add. The design principle behind a whole class of flavour models. Why $A_4\times Z_5$ and not $A_4$: a single non-Abelian group gives degenerate rows, and you need an Abelian factor to break the degeneracy. That is a structural statement the corpus should have.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's flavour section.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the paper states the division of labour.

2. $Y^u=Y^\nu$ makes the Cabibbo angle appear in the lepton sector — and explains $\theta_{12}^l<35.3^\circ$

The idea. Exact quark-lepton Yukawa unification forces the same rotation into both sectors, so the solar angle's deviation from tri-bimaximal is a prediction from $\theta_C$.

What it would add. A unification relation with a measured consequence. This is the strongest kind of evidence for Pati–Salam flavour unification in the batch, because the deviation was measured before the explanation in this form.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's Yukawa-relation section, next to Georgi–Jarlskog.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the relation is exact at the unification scale.

3. CSD1 → CSD2 → CSD3 → CSD4 is a fit history, and that is a warning

The idea. The vacuum alignment was changed as $\theta_{13}$ was measured, so the sequence tracks data rather than predicting it.

What it would add. A methodological caution with a concrete example. A flavour model whose alignment is adjusted to each new angle is not being tested; what can be tested is the relation between angles, which CSD4 does fix.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's methodology discussion.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the sequence is printed in the paper.

4. $\delta^l=260^\circ\pm5^\circ$ versus present data

The idea. A 2014 prediction of the leptonic CP phase.

What it would add. A test that has partly happened. The corpus should record the prediction and note the current range, because a reader deserves to know whether a celebrated model survived.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's neutrino section.

What stands in the way. $\delta$ is still poorly measured; the comparison is indicative.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the ancestor of half the flavour entries in this batch, and reading the descendants (2404.06520, 2503.14610, 2501.00302, 1705.06320) next to it shows what changed.

2014: discrete symmetry, vacuum alignment chosen, couplings from flavon VEVs, CSD4 with $Z_5$ phases. 2024–2025: modular symmetry, the same $A_4$ or $S_3$, couplings fixed functions of $\tau$.

The change is in what is allowed to vary. King's 2014 model has flavons with alignments, $Z_5$ charges, and CSD parameters — a dozen adjustable objects — and it fits. The modular models have one complex parameter $\tau$ and a set of modular weights, and they also fit.

If the modular models fit as well with far fewer parameters, that is progress, and the corpus should say so. If they fit worse, that is a reason the 2014 approach persists. Either way the comparison is the interesting thing, and it can only be made if both kinds of paper are in the corpus.

The second point: "the entire PMNS mixing matrix" and a Cabibbo angle from one construction is a real achievement, and the fact that $\theta_{12}^l$ came out below tri-bimaximal for the right reason is the sort of thing that should be remembered when the model's $\delta$ prediction is assessed.

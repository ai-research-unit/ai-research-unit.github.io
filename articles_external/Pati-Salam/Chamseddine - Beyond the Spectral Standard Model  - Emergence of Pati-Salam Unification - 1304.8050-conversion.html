What the Paper Does

This is the source paper of the whole non-commutative Pati–Salam programme. Its claim is one line long and its consequences are not.

The spectral Standard Model is selected from the algebra $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$ by imposing the first-order condition, which cuts it down to $\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$. Drop that condition and the classification of finite geometries of KO-dimension $6$ returns the full algebra, "almost uniquely", and the gauge group that comes out is

$$ SU(2)_R\times SU(2)_L\times SU(4), $$

with sixteen fermions in $(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 4)+(\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2,\mathbf 4)$ and Higgs fields in $(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 2,\mathbf 1)$, $(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 4)$ and $(\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1+\mathbf{15})$.

The mechanism is that without the first-order condition the inner fluctuations acquire a quadratic term $A^{(2)}$, and that term is what supplies the extra Higgs multiplets needed to break Pati–Salam down to the Standard Model. The paper's closing sentence states the status of the condition it dropped: "one of the vacua of the Higgs fields gives rise at low energies to a Dirac operator satisfying the first order condition. In this way, the first order condition arises as a spontaneously broken phase of higher symmetry and is not imposed from outside."

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Two Conditions and What Each Buys

The first-order condition is defined precisely: "the requirement that the Dirac operator is a derivation of the algebra $\mathcal A$ into the commutant of $\hat{\mathcal A}=J\mathcal A J^{-1}$", equivalently

$$ [[D,a],b^\circ]=0,\qquad a,b\in\mathcal A,\quad b^\circ=Jb^*J^{-1}. $$

What it is for: "This in turn guarantees the gauge invariance and linearity of the inner fluctuations under the action of the gauge group given by the unitaries $U=uJuJ^{-1}$."

And what it does to the algebra: it "was used as a mathematical requirement to select the maximal subalgebra $\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)\subset\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$ which is compatible with the first order condition and is the main reason behind the unique selection of the SM."

So the Standard Model's algebra is a truncation, and the truncation is the condition. That is the sentence the corpus needs, and it is stated here more plainly than anywhere else.

Why Drop It

Three reasons, none of them aesthetic.

Precedent. "The existence of examples of noncommutative spaces where the first order condition is not satisfied such as quantum groups and quantum spheres provides a motive to remove this condition."

Consistency. Without the condition the ordinary prescription fails: "the operator $D+A\pm JAJ^{-1}$ ... does not behave well with respect to the action of the gauge group", because "the simple argument only works if $[JuJ^{-1},A]=0$ for gauge potentials $A$ of the above form".

Phenomenology. Two failures of the Standard Model are named — the neutrino mass scale, which "is explained through the see-saw mechanism with a Majorana mass of at least of the order of $10^{11}$ GeV", and "the failure (by few percent) of the three gauge couplings to be unified at some high scale which indicates that it may be necessary to add other matter couplings to change the slopes of the running of the RG equations".

The Modified Inner Fluctuations

$$ D_0=D+A^{(1)}+\tilde A^{(1)}+A^{(2)}, $$

with

$$ A^{(1)}=\sum_j a_j[D,b_j], \qquad \tilde A^{(1)}=\sum_j \hat a_j[D,\hat b_j], \qquad \hat a=JaJ^{-1}, $$

and

$$ A^{(2)}=\sum_{j,k}\hat a_j a_k\,[[D,b_k],\hat b_j]. $$

"Clearly $A^{(2)}$ which depends quadratically on the fields in $A^{(1)}$ vanishes when the first order condition is satisfied." And the gauge transformation of the quadratic piece is itself nonlinear:

$$ A^{(2)}\mapsto JuJ^{-1}A^{(2)}Ju^*J^{-1}+JuJ^{-1}\,[u[D,u^*],Ju^*J^{-1}], $$

which is why the object is a connection and not a field strength.

The structural result is that "inner fluctuations come from the action on operators in Hilbert space of a semi-group $\mathrm{Pert}(\mathcal A)$ of inner perturbations", consisting of normalised self-adjoint elements of $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A^{\rm op}$:

$$ \mathrm{Pert}(\mathcal A)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_j a_j\otimes b_j^{\rm op}:\ \sum_j a_jb_j=1,\ \sum_j b_j^*\otimes a_j^{*\rm op}=\sum_j a_j\otimes b_j^{\rm op}\Big\}, $$

with the fluctuation simply $D\mapsto\sum_j a_jDb_j$, and for real triples $D\mapsto\sum_{i,j}a_i\hat a_jDb_i\hat b_j$. "The structure of a semi-group implies in particular that inner fluctuations of inner fluctuations are still inner fluctuations — a fact which is not at all direct when looking at Equation (2)."

That last sentence matters more than its length suggests. It says the space of allowed Dirac operators is closed under fluctuation. A group would also have inverses; a semigroup does not, so the fluctuation cannot be undone, and the paper later uses exactly this to argue that the fundamental and composite Higgs phases are disconnected.

The Classification

The earlier classification of finite geometries, which produced the Standard Model, is here redone without the condition.

The starting restriction is unchanged: "spinors which belong to the product of the continuous four dimensional space, times the finite space must be such that the conjugate spinor is not an independent field, in order to avoid doubling the fermions. This could only be achieved when the spinors satisfy both the Majorana and Weyl conditions, which implies that the KO-dimension of the finite space be $6$ (mod $8$)."

The zeroth-order condition then "restricts the center of the complexified algebra to be $Z(\mathcal A_{\mathbb C})=\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$", and "the dimension of the Hilbert space is then restricted to be the square of an integer", forcing

$$ \mathcal A=M_k(\mathbb C)\oplus M_k(\mathbb C). $$

Then, step by step:

  • "A symplectic symmetry imposed on the first algebra forces $k$ to be even $k=2a$ and the algebra to be of quaternionic matrices of the form $M_a(\mathbb H)$."
  • "The existence of the chirality operator breaks $M_a(\mathbb H)$ and further restricts the integer $a$ to be even, and thus the number of fundamental fermions must be of the form $4a^2$."
  • "This shows that the first possible realistic case is the finite space with $k=4$ to be based on the algebra $\mathcal A=\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$."

Four times a square, with $a$ even: $4a^2=16$ at $a=2$. The count $16$ does not come from the Standard Model's field list; it comes from the arithmetic of the algebra, and it survives the removal of the condition. The paper says so: "we shall show that the number of fundamental fermions is still dictated to be $16$."

Then the comparison, which is the paper's core table of consequences:

with first-order condition without
$\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$
$SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$
Higgs in $(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 1)_{-1/2}$ Higgs in $(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 2,\mathbf 1)$, $(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 4)$, $(\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1+\mathbf{15})$

The Fermions, Written Out

$\Psi\in\mathcal H$ is $\binom{\psi_A}{\psi_{A'}}$ with "$\psi_{A'}=\psi_A^c$", so "all primed indices correspond to the Hilbert space of conjugate spinors". The index $A=\alpha I$ with $\alpha$ quaternionic and $I$ in $M_4(\mathbb C)$; the grading "breaks $M_2(\mathbb H)$ into $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L$" giving the dotted/undotted split $a,\dot a$; and the $M_4(\mathbb C)$ index splits as $I=1,i$ "where the 1 is acted on by the $\mathbb C$ and the $i$ by $M_3(\mathbb C)$". So

$$ \psi_{\alpha I}=\begin{pmatrix}\nu_R&u_{iR}&\nu_L&u_{iL}\\ e_R&d_{iR}&e_L&d_{iL}\end{pmatrix}, $$

which is the corpus's $4\times2$ array with the leptons in the first column. Sixteen entries, sixteen fermions.

The Connection, and the Higgs Multiplets

The gauge part is stated compactly:

$$ (D_A)_{a\dot I}^{\ \ \dot\alpha J}=\gamma^\mu D_\mu\delta^{\dot\alpha}_{\dot a}\delta^J_I-\tfrac{i}{2}g_RW^{\dot\alpha R}_{\mu}(\sigma^m)^{\dot\alpha}_{\ \dot a}\delta^J_I-\delta^{\dot\alpha}_{\dot a}\tfrac{i}{2}g_VV^m_\mu(\lambda^m)^J_I, $$

with the fifteen $(\lambda^m)^J_I$ "traceless and generating the group $SU(4)$". Unimodularity, $\mathrm{Tr}(A)=0$, then forces $V_\mu=0$ for the trace part — the same excision that in the Standard-Model case removes the unwanted $U(1)$, and the paper's later remark ties it to hypercharge: "unimodularity on $U(\mathcal A)$ naturally induces unimodularity of the spectral Standard Model, hence it generates the correct hypercharges for the fermions."

The Higgs part is where the new physics lives:

$$ (D_A)_{aI}^{\ \ \dot bJ}=\gamma_5\Big[\big(k^\nu\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}+k^e\tilde\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}\big)\Sigma^J_I+\big(k^u\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}+k^d\tilde\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}\big)\delta^J_I-\Sigma^J_I\Big]\equiv\gamma_5\,\Sigma^{\dot bJ}_{aI}, $$

together with

$$ (D_A)_{\dot bI}^{\ \ \dot aJ}=\gamma_5\,k^{*\nu_R}\Delta^{\dot aJ}_{\dot bI}, $$

so

"the Higgs field $\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}$ is in the $\mathbf 2_R,\mathbf 2_L,\mathbf 1$ of the product gauge group $SU(2)_R\times SU(2)_L\times SU(4)$, and $\Delta^{\dot aJ}_{\dot bI}$ is in the $(\mathbf 2_R,\mathbf 1_L,\mathbf 4)$ representation while $\Sigma^J_I$ is in the $(\mathbf 1_R,\mathbf 1_L,\mathbf 1+\mathbf{15})$ representation."

Note that $\Sigma$ appears inside the definition of the $\gamma_5$ block, i.e. it is a component of the same object as $\varphi$, and that $\tilde\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}=\sigma_2\varphi^T\sigma_2$ "is not an independent field". And the decoupling statement the corpus will care about: "the field $\Sigma^J_I$ decouples (and set to $\delta^I_1\delta^J_1$) in the special case when there is lepton and quark unification of the couplings $k^\nu=k^u$, $k^e=k^d$."

Fundamental Versus Composite

This is the paper's most quoted passage, and the whole phenomenological content turns on it.

"In case when the initial Dirac operator satisfies the order one condition for the subalgebra (4), then the $A^{(2)}$ part of the connection becomes a composite Higgs field where the Higgs is formed out of the products of the fields $\varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}$ and $\Sigma^J_I$ while the Higgs field $\Sigma^{\dot bJ}_{aI}$ is made from the product of $\Delta^{\dot aJ}_{bI}\Delta^{\dot bI}_{aI}$. For generic initial Dirac operators, the field $A^{(2)}$ becomes independent. The fields $\Sigma^{\dot bJ}_{aI}$ and $H^{\dot bJ}_{aI}$ will then not be defined through equation 24 and will be in the $(\mathbf 2_R,\mathbf 2_L,\mathbf 1+\mathbf{15})$ and $(\mathbf 3_R,\mathbf 1_L,\mathbf{10})+(\mathbf 1_R,\mathbf 1_L,\mathbf 6)$ representations ... In addition, for generic Dirac operator one also generates the fundamental field $(\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2_L,\mathbf 4)$."

Two phases, one algebra. If the initial $D$ is first-order relative to the subalgebra, the extra scalars are squares of the fundamental ones. If it is not, they are independent fields in $(3,1,10)$, $(1,1,6)$ and $(2,2,1+15)$. And: "The fact that inner automorphisms form a semigroup implies that the cases where the Higgs fields contained in the connections $A^{(2)}$ are either independent fields or depend quadratically on the fundamental Higgs fields are disconnected."

The comparison to ordinary grand unification is made and is favourable: "we have here a clear advantage over grand unified theories which suffers of having arbitrary and complicated Higgs representations. In the noncommutative geometric setting, this problem is now solved by having minimal representations of the Higgs fields."

The model is identified as "very similar to the one considered by Marshak and Mohapatra where the $U(1)$ of the left-right model is identified with the $B-L$ symmetry", and it is noted that "although the broken generators of the $SU(4)$ gauge fields can mediate lepto-quark interactions leading to proton decay, it was shown that in all such types of models with partial unification, the proton is stable."

The Spectral Action

$\mathrm{Tr}\,f(D_A/\Lambda)$ with heat-kernel coefficients: "$a_0=\frac{24}{\pi^2}\int d^4x\sqrt g$", "where the numerical factors come, respectively, from the traces on the Clifford algebra, the dimensions of the Hilbert space and number of generations" — that is $4\times32\times3=384$, and the Hilbert space is "$384\times384$". The bosonic action to second order is

$$ \mathcal S_b=\frac{24}{\pi^2}F_4\Lambda^4\!\int\!d^4x\sqrt g -\frac{2}{\pi^2}F_2\Lambda^2\!\int\!d^4x\sqrt g\Big(R+\tfrac12\Sigma^{\dot cK}_{aI}\Sigma^{\dot aI}_{\dot cK}+2\Sigma^{\dot aI}_{\dot cK}H^{\dot cK}_{aI}\Big) +\frac{1}{2\pi^2}F_0\!\int\!d^4x\sqrt g\Big(-18C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^2+11R^*R^*+\tfrac{1}{30}g_3^2V_{\mu\nu}^2+g_L^2W_{\mu\nu L}^2+g_R^2W_{\mu\nu R}^2\Big), $$

followed by the kinetic terms $\nabla H\nabla H$ and $\nabla\Sigma\nabla\Sigma$ and the potential. The $-\tfrac{18}{\pi^2}C^2$ and $+\tfrac{11}{\pi^2}R^*R^*$ coefficients are the numbers the 2511.07672 review quotes, and this is where they come from.

The potential, in the form given,

$$ V=\frac{F_0}{2\pi^2}\Big[\tfrac12\Sigma^{\dot aI}_{\dot cK}\Sigma^{\dot cK}_{\dot bJ}\Sigma^{\dot bJ}_{\dot dL}\Sigma^{\dot dL}_{\dot aI}+2H^{\dot aI}_{\dot cK}H^{\dot bJ}_{\dot bJ}\Sigma^{\dot cK}_{\dot dL}\Sigma^{\dot dL}_{\dot aI}\Big]-\frac{F_2}{2\pi^2}\Big[H^{\dot aI}_{\dot cK}\Sigma^{\dot cK}_{\dot aI}+2\Sigma^{\dot aI}_{\dot cK}\Sigma^{\dot cK}_{\dot aI}\Big], $$

is "of order eight" in the fundamental fields once the composites are expanded, and the authors say so: "it is difficult to determine all allowed vacua".

The Vacuum and the Return of the First-Order Condition

The vacuum examined is

$$ \varphi^{\dot b}_{\ a}=v\,\delta^{\dot b}_1\delta^1_a, \qquad \Sigma^J_I=u\,\delta^I_1\delta^J_1, \qquad \Delta^{\dot aJ}_{\dot bI}=w\,\delta^{\dot a}_1\delta^1_{\dot b}\delta^J_I, $$

and "a computation of the Hessian in the $\Delta$-directions shows that the SM-vev is indeed a local minimum." The $\Delta$ vev, being in $(\mathbf 2_R,\mathbf 1_L,\mathbf 4)$, leaves invariant exactly

$$ \begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\0&\bar\lambda\end{pmatrix}\oplus u_L\oplus\big(\lambda\oplus\lambda^{-1/3}u\big), \qquad \lambda\in U(1),\ u_L\in SU(2),\ u\in SU(3), $$

"thus selecting the subalgebra (4)". And then the loop closes:

"The first order condition now arises as a vacuum solution of the spectral action ... In this way, the first order condition arises as a spontaneously broken phase of higher symmetry and is not imposed from outside."

What Is Left Open

Stated plainly, and it is a lot: "It remains to minimize the potential to determine all possible minima as well as studying the unified model and check whether it allows for unification of coupling constants $g_R=g_L=g$ in addition to determining the top quark mass and Higgs mass. Obviously, this model deserves careful analysis, which will be the subject of future work."

The conclusion adds the motive: this "provides a setting for unification avoiding the desert and which goes beyond the SM."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. The Standard Model's algebra is a truncation, and the truncation is an axiom

The idea. $\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ is the maximal subalgebra of $\mathbb H_R\oplus\mathbb H_L\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$ compatible with the first-order condition. Remove the condition and the larger algebra is the one the classification gives.

What it would add. The exact logical status of the corpus's favourite algebra. The corpus's Pati–Salam article argues for $\mathbb H\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)$ on grounds of content — hypercharge, charge quantisation, the anomaly identities, $\nu_R$. This paper says something different and stronger: the larger algebra is the default, and the Standard Model's smaller algebra is what you get by imposing an extra condition. The corpus has been arguing for the larger algebra as if it were a choice among equals; here it is the unmarked case.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spectral-triple-of-the-biquaternion-algebra.md, in the axiom section, and articles_physics/grand-unification-in-the-biquaternion-framework-where-the-embedding-fails.md.

What stands in the way. The corpus's spectral-triple article says the biquaternion algebra fails the first-order condition. This paper says the failure is a feature: the algebras that fail it are the ones that unify. The corpus's article should be amended from "fails" to "fails, and the failure is what Pati–Salam requires" — which converts a defect into a location.

2. The extra scalars are squares of the fundamental ones, or they are not — and the two phases cannot mix

The idea. $A^{(2)}$ is quadratic in $A^{(1)}$. If the initial $D$ is first-order relative to the subalgebra, the extra Higgs fields are products of the fundamental ones; otherwise they are independent, in $(3,1,10)$, $(1,1,6)$, $(2,2,1+15)$. And because inner automorphisms form a semigroup and not a group, "the cases ... are disconnected".

What it would add. A structural reason why a scalar sector has the size it has. The corpus's scalar-sector discussion treats the bidoublet and the $\Delta$s as independent objects with independent couplings. Here the number of independent scalars is decided by a property of the initial Dirac operator, and the paper derives it rather than postulating it. The semigroup argument is the sharp part: you cannot fluctuate your way from the composite phase to the fundamental one, so the two are separate theories with the same algebra.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's scalar-sector section, as the sharpest available statement of why the sector is not arbitrary.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no $A^{(2)}$ and no semigroup, so it cannot reproduce the argument. What it can do is record the result: the scalar content of a Pati–Salam model is not free, it is fixed by how the Dirac operator behaves on a subalgebra. That is a constraint the corpus's frame lacks and should acknowledge lacking.

3. The count $16$ comes from $4a^2$ with $a$ even

The idea. The Hilbert space dimension must be a perfect square; the symplectic symmetry on the first factor forces $k=2a$; the chirality operator forces $a$ even; so the fermion number is $4a^2$, and the first realistic case is $a=2$, giving $16$.

What it would add. A derivation of the generation size that does not look at the particle list. The corpus's articles_physics/the-number-of-generations-and-the-biquaternion-algebra.md asks why three families; this answers a different and prior question — why sixteen states in one family — and answers it from the algebra's dimension alone. The arithmetic is short enough to state in two lines and strong enough to be worth stating.

Where it lands. The number-of-generations article, and the Pati–Salam article's fermion section.

What stands in the way. The argument uses the chirality operator and the symplectic structure, both of which the corpus's frame has in some form. The obstacle is that the corpus counts states differently, so the two counts must be reconciled before the argument transfers — and the corpus's count is the reduced one.

4. $\lambda_{15}=\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\mathrm{diag}(3,-1,-1,-1)$, and the truncation identities

The idea. The $SU(4)$ generator that survives as $B-L$, plus the truncation rules

$$ g_RW^3_{\mu R}=g_1B_\mu, \qquad g_VV^{15}_\mu=-\sqrt{\tfrac32}\,g_1B_\mu, \qquad W^\pm_{\mu R}=0, \qquad (V_\mu)_{i1}=0, $$

which make the diagonal Dirac entries collapse to $\gamma^\mu D_\mu$ with the correct hypercharge.

What it would add. The explicit dictionary between the Pati–Salam generators and the Standard Model's, with normalisations. The corpus has $Y=T_{3R}+\frac12(B-L)$ as an identity; this gives the same relation as a set of algebraic substitutions on gauge fields, which is what one actually needs to check that a truncation works. The corpus's article asserts the identity; this shows the substitution.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's gauge-sector section and its truncation paragraph.

What stands in the way. Nothing. Note the normalisation differs from the physics convention used elsewhere in this corpus: here $\mathrm{tr}(\lambda^m\lambda^n)=2\delta^{mn}$, since $\lambda_{15}$ has entries $\pm3,\pm1$ over $\sqrt6$.

5. "Avoiding the desert" — unification without a large intermediate gap

The idea. The closing claim is that a Pati–Salam stage "provides a setting for unification avoiding the desert". The desert is the long stretch between the electroweak and unification scales in which nothing happens; a stage at an intermediate scale fills it.

What it would add. The motive for the whole programme, stated as a design goal rather than a result. The corpus records the unification scale as a number that depends on the spectrum — $5.2\times10^{13}$ in Babu, $10^{16}$ in Braam. This paper states why one wants the lower number: an intermediate stage is a prediction of new physics in a reachable range, and a desert is a prediction of nothing.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's coupling-unification section, as the statement of purpose.

What stands in the way. The paper does not demonstrate unification, and says so: "It remains to ... check whether it allows for unification of coupling constants $g_R=g_L=g$." So the motive is stated and the calculation is deferred. The corpus should record the deferral, since the later papers in this corpus (Aydemir, Blanke, Babu) are precisely the deferred calculation, and they produce answers that differ.

6. Proton stability survives partial unification

The idea. "Although the broken generators of the $SU(4)$ gauge fields can mediate lepto-quark interactions leading to proton decay, it was shown that in all such types of models with partial unification, the proton is stable."

What it would add. A qualification of the corpus's proton-decay discussion. The corpus's Pati–Salam article treats proton decay as a cost of the group, mitigated by the absence of diquark couplings for the vector leptoquark. This paper states the general theorem for partial unification: quarks and leptons sit in the same multiplet, and the proton is still stable. That is a stronger statement than "the couplings happen to vanish", and it is the reason the programme was never killed by proton decay.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's proton-decay paragraph.

What stands in the way. The paper cites the result rather than proving it, and "partial unification" is the technical hypothesis — it fails for full unification into a simple group. The corpus should import it with that hypothesis attached.

One tension recorded

This paper and Bochniak 2002.04506 are in direct opposition, and the opposition is precise. Bochniak adds the pseudo-Riemannian grading $\beta$, which the Standard Model's own finite triple possesses, and finds that it kills the leptoquarks and reduces $SU(4)$ to $U(1)\times U(3)$. Chamseddine, Connes and van Suijlekom remove the first-order condition and find that Pati–Salam is the algebra. Neither paper addresses the other's move. The corpus is the right place to record that the two results are about different axiom sets, and that each axiom is independently motivated: the first-order condition by linearity of the connection, the pseudo-Riemannian structure by time orientation. A reader who wants leptoquarks must drop one of them, and should know which.

What the Paper Does

It finds the hole in the argument that Pati–Salam can be broken at a low scale.

The argument being attacked is standard and it is correct as far as it goes: "The gauge bosons of Pati–Salam do not mediate proton decay at the renormalisable level, and for this reason it is possible to construct scenarios in which $SU(4)\otimes SU(2)_R$ is broken at relatively low scales."

The hole: the gauge bosons do not, but the scalar that breaks the group does, through dimension-5 operators. The scalar $\chi\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ contains a colour triplet $\chi_d$ which is a leptoquark at dimension four and a diquark at dimension five, and the diquark coupling is generated by mixing the right-handed neutrino with a singlet $\xi$ — mixing that is needed anyway to get light neutrino masses.

The resulting nucleon decay rate, suppressed only by the Planck scale, pushes the Pati–Salam breaking scale up:

"the lower limit on the breaking scale can be constrained to be as high as $\mathcal O(10^8)$ GeV."

And there is a complementarity result that is the paper's most interesting finding: the flavour structure that suppresses the usual $K_L^0$ meson-decay limits is exactly the structure that enhances the nucleon-decay limits. You cannot have both.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Minimal Setup

$$ (f_L)_p=\begin{pmatrix}Q_L&L_L\end{pmatrix}_p\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_p, \qquad (f_R)_p=\begin{pmatrix}u_R&\nu_R\\d_R&e_R\end{pmatrix}_p\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_p, $$

"the lowest-dimensional multiplet that breaks the gauge symmetry to that of the SM is the scalar field $\chi$:

$$ \chi=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_u^0&\chi^+\\\chi_d&\chi^-\end{pmatrix}\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2), $$

through the vev of the neutral component: $v_R\equiv\sqrt2\langle\chi^0\rangle$."

"$\chi_u$ is the Goldstone boson of the $SU(4)$ vector-leptoquark gauge boson (from hereon referred to as $X_\mu$ with mass $m_X$) while $\chi^-$ and $\chi^0$ predominately make up the Goldstone bosons of the $W_\mu^0$ and $Z_\mu^0$ respectively."

So $v_R$ eats four of the eight real components of $\chi$ and leaves $\chi_d$ as a physical scalar. The counting: $\chi$ is $4\times2=8$ complex, i.e. sixteen real. Three go to $W^\pm_R$ and $Z_R$... but here there is no $W_R$ because $SU(2)_R$ is broken by the same vev that breaks $SU(4)$, so the eaten states are $X_\mu$ (six real, from $SU(4)$'s broken generators), $W^0_\mu$ and $Z^0_\mu$. Twelve eaten, four left: $\chi_d$ and three others that the paper does not name. The Goldstone bookkeeping is the reason $\chi_d$ survives at the Pati–Salam scale as a light-ish leptoquark.

Its mass: "$m_{\chi_d}^2\simeq-\frac12\lambda^\chi v_R^2$", and with partial-wave unitarity $|\mathrm{Re}(a_0)|\le\frac12$ giving $|\lambda^\chi|\le2\pi$,

$$ m_{\chi_d}\le\sqrt{\pi}\,v_R . $$

That inequality is what converts a nucleon-decay bound on $m_{\chi_d}$ into a bound on $v_R$, and it is the pivot of the whole paper. Since $\chi_d$'s mass is set by the same quartic that controls the potential, and the quartic is bounded by unitarity, the leptoquark cannot be arbitrarily heavier than the breaking scale.

The bidoublet $\Phi\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ then gives the masses, with the standard relations

$$ \sigma_p(M_d)=\sigma_p(M_e), \qquad \sigma_p(M_u)=\sigma_p(M_\nu), $$

"at the scale of EW symmetry breaking ... The second of these relations is far more grievous than the first and is most simply corrected by introducing the Weyl state $\xi\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ to organise for light neutrino masses."

The Singlet That Turns a Leptoquark Into a Diquark

$$ \mathcal L\supset-\frac{\mu}{2}\xi\xi-y_R\bar\xi\,\mathrm{Tr}[\chi^\dagger f_R]+{\rm h.c.} $$

Expanding: "$-\bar\xi\big((\chi_d)^\dagger d_R+(\chi^-)^\dagger e_R+(\chi_u)^\dagger u_R+(\chi^0)^\dagger\nu_R\big)$".

"it is simple to see that a mixing will be induced between $\xi$ and $\nu_R$ once $\chi^0$ obtains a vacuum expectation value. Therefore the first and third terms of Eq. (11) can be interpreted as genuine leptoquark couplings, albeit suppressed by mixing angles."

That is the mechanism in one step. $\xi$ couples to all four components of $f_R$ with equal strength, because there is only one $\chi$ and one contraction. So the same coupling that gives $\xi$ its mass-mixing with $\nu_R$ necessarily gives it couplings to $d_R$ and $u_R$. The neutrino-mass sector and the baryon-violating sector are the same term.

The mass matrix

$$ \begin{pmatrix}\bar\nu_L&\bar\nu_R&\bar\xi\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}0&m_u&0\\m_u&0&y_Rv_R\\0&y_Rv_R&\mu\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}\nu_L^c\\\nu_R\\\xi^c\end{pmatrix} $$

gives "a pseudo-Dirac fermion, composed of $N_L$ and $N_R$, and a Majorana fermion $\nu$", with the mixing angle $\sin\theta$ of order $m_u\mu/(y_Rv_R)^2$.

Note the $m_u$ in the off-diagonal entry — the up-quark mass, because the Dirac entry is $m_u$ and $\nu_R$ is the up-type partner in $f_R$. That is why the up-quark mass appears in the nucleon-decay rate and why the bound scales with $\sqrt{m_u}$.

The Decay and Its Signature

The effective operators are $\chi^\dagger\chi^\dagger f_Lf_L$ and $\chi^\dagger\chi f_Rf_R$, giving four-fermion diquark interactions after $\chi_d$ exchange. With hadronic matrix elements $\langle\pi^+|(ud)_Rd_R|p\rangle=0.151\ \mathrm{GeV}^2$ and $\langle\pi^+|(ud)_Ld_R|p\rangle=-0.159\ \mathrm{GeV}^2$, and the Super-Kamiokande limit $\tau(n\to\pi^0\nu)>1.1\times10^{33}$ yr,

$$ m_{\chi_d}\gtrsim8\times10^6\ \mathrm{GeV}\,|C_L|\sqrt{\frac{10^{19}\ \mathrm{GeV}}{\Lambda}}\sqrt{\frac{m_u}{171\ \mathrm{MeV}}} , $$

$$ v_R\gtrsim5\times10^6\ \mathrm{GeV}\,|C_L|\sqrt{\frac{10^{19}\ \mathrm{GeV}}{\Lambda}}\sqrt{\frac{m_u}{171\ \mathrm{MeV}}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|\lambda^\chi|}} . $$

"Importantly, the limit scales inversely to the square root of $\Lambda$, the scale of baryon-number violation. As a benchmark, we have taken this to be the Planck scale, where all global symmetries are expected to be violated."

Then the improvement: "we show that it is in fact motivated to take $\Lambda$ to be the scale of $SO(10)$ unification, which improves the limits by a factor of $\sqrt{\Lambda_{\rm Planck}/\Lambda_{\rm GUT}}$", giving for $\Lambda_{\rm GUT}\sim10^{16}$ GeV

$$ v_R\gtrsim\begin{pmatrix}4\times10^5\\9\times10^6\\1\times10^8\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{GeV} \quad\text{for}\quad m_u\to\{m_u,m_c,m_t\}, $$

and for Planck-suppressed operators

$$ v_R\gtrsim\begin{pmatrix}1\times10^4\\3\times10^5\\5\times10^6\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{GeV}. $$

The three numbers per bracket are three different answers to the same question, depending on which quark mass enters the loop. That spread — two orders of magnitude — is not a hedge, it is the statement that the bound depends on which generation's mixing dominates, and the paper does not settle it.

And the sharp prediction:

$$ \Gamma(n\to\pi^0\nu)=\tfrac12\Gamma(p\to\pi^+\nu), $$

"neglecting the mass difference between $p$ and $n$. A prediction of this scenario is thus the absence of nucleon decays with charged-lepton final states."

Nucleon decay to a neutrino and a pion, and nothing else. $\chi_d$'s charged-lepton Yukawa is absent at dimension four — the paper says so explicitly — so there is no $p\to\pi^0e^+$ channel at leading order. That is a clean experimental signature and it is what makes the paper worth citing: the prediction is not "proton decay at some rate", it is "neutron decay to $\pi^0\nu$ and no charged-lepton mode".

The Complementarity

The payoff. To suppress nucleon decay, you want the $K_L^{u\nu}$ column of the mixing to have a zero entry, and "The simplest way to satisfy such a constraint for a column of a unitary matrix is when one entry of the column is zero and the other two are $\pm1/\sqrt2$". But:

"using Eq. (9) but now solving for $K_L^{de}$ we find that, for the limited possible choices of $K_L^{u\nu}$ that satisfy Eq. (38) with one entry zero, $\mathcal O(1)$ entries are generated in the upper-left $2\times2$ block of $K_L^{de}$. This implies that $X_\mu$ will receive stringent limits from $K_L^0$ decays of order $\mathcal O(1000)$ TeV."

So:

"We find, in minimal Pati–Salam, that while specific flavour structures in the mixing matrices can suppress the limits on $m_X$ in rare meson decays, these structures generate even larger limits on $v_R$ (and therefore $m_X$) from B-violating nucleon decays."

The relation driving this is

$$ K^{u\nu}_{L/R}=V_{\rm CKM}\,K_{L/R}\,U_{\rm PMNS}, $$

which is a genuine consequence of $SU(4)$: the same unitary $K$ that rotates quarks into leptons must, when combined with the CKM and PMNS matrices, give the physical mixing. You cannot tune one without moving the other. That is the corpus-relevant statement, and it comes from the group structure rather than from a choice.

The $SO(10)$ Embedding and the $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ Escape

"any embedding of the described low-scale PS model into $SO(10)$ should place the bidoublet $\Phi$" such that the operators are generated. And on the alternative breaking scalar: "$(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$. This scalar generates no dimension-5 operators whatsoever, assuming standard fermion embeddings. While this may seem like a good candidate to avoid unwanted nucleon decays, such a scalar cannot allow for light neutrino masses without $v_R\gtrsim\mathcal O(10^{12})$ GeV."

That is the paper's quiet verdict on the $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$: it is safe from baryon violation precisely because it is less unified — it has no leptoquark component — and that same fact is why it cannot do the low-scale job. The Chaudhuri paper converted alongside this one uses exactly this multiplet. The two papers are in direct tension and the resolution is the breaking scale: at $10^{12}$ GeV and above, the $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ is fine; below, it is not available.

The Variants

Two escapes are considered.

"Models based on the 'Pati–Salam-adjacent' gauge group $SU(4)\otimes SU(3)\otimes SU(2)\otimes U(1)$" — "A similar variant first appears in the original paper and was referred to as the 'economical' model" — and the paper finds "even here, in a model without an $SU(2)_R$ gauge group, a dimension-5 B-violating operator can also be written down in analogy to the operators $O_X$: $\mathcal L^{(5)}\supset C\,\bar d_Rd_R\,\Omega_1^\dagger\Omega_3$".

The other: "Such models can cause $X_\mu$ to couple dominantly to one chirality of $d$ and $e$ ... such that a 'Chiral Pati–Salam' model is achieved ... This induces a helicity suppression in the rare-meson decays in analogy with the helicity suppression of pion decays in the SM." But "Such a scenario shares a common gauge structure and scalar content with the minimal model discussed in this paper, and therefore suffers from similar UV-induced B-violating nucleon decays ... the $\sin\theta$ suppression of Eq. (24) will likely be absent, and therefore much larger decay rates will follow."

Both escapes fail, and for the same reason: they change the gauge structure or the fermion content, and the dangerous operator comes from the scalar sector, which they leave alone.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. Accidental baryon number in Pati–Salam is dimension-dependent

The idea. $SU(4)$'s gauge bosons do not violate $B$ at the renormalisable level — $B$ is accidental. But the breaking scalar violates it at dimension five, and dimension five is enough.

What it would add. The sharpest correction available to a claim the corpus makes. The corpus's Pati–Salam article treats accidental $B$ conservation as a virtue of the model, which it is — at dimension four. "Accidental" means suppressed by a power of the cutoff, not absent, and this paper computes the power and shows it is insufficient. The corpus should state the claim with its dimension attached: $B$ is accidental up to dimension four, violated at dimension five, and the dimension-five coefficient is not small.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the proton-decay section, as the correction.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The logic is: no renormalisable operator $\Rightarrow$ no tree-level decay $\Rightarrow$ but a dimension-five operator with a Planck-suppressed coefficient still gives a rate above the bound for $v_R$ below $10^8$ GeV.

2. The same singlet that fixes neutrino masses turns the leptoquark into a diquark

The idea. $\xi\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ is introduced to break $m_u=m_\nu$. It couples as $\bar\xi\,\mathrm{Tr}[\chi^\dagger f_R]$, which after $\chi^0$'s vev mixes $\xi$ with $\nu_R$ — and after the same expansion couples $\xi$ to $d_R$ and $u_R$.

What it would add. A worked instance of a repair creating a new problem. The corpus's seesaw article introduces singlets freely to fix mass relations. Here the singlet is necessary — the paper says $m_u=m_\nu$ is "far more grievous" and must be corrected — and the necessary coupling is the dangerous one. The coupling is unique: given $\chi$ and $f_R$ there is exactly one renormalisable contraction, so there is no way to couple $\xi$ to $\nu_R$ without also coupling it to $d_R$. That uniqueness is the argument, and it is a group-theory fact the corpus can state.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-seesaw-mechanism-and-the-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md, as the cost of the singlet extension, and the Pati–Salam article's proton-decay section.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The claim "there is only one contraction" should be checked against the representations — $\chi^\dagger f_R$ with $\chi\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ and $f_R\sim(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ gives $\bar{\mathbf4}\otimes\mathbf4=\mathbf1+\mathbf{15}$ and $\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2=\mathbf1+\mathbf3$, so the $\mathbf1$ is unique. The corpus can verify this in two lines and should.

3. $m_{\chi_d}\le\sqrt\pi\,v_R$: unitarity bounds the leptoquark from above

The idea. The physical leptoquark mass comes from the same quartic as the potential's stability, and partial-wave unitarity bounds the quartic at $2\pi$, so $m_{\chi_d}$ cannot exceed $\sqrt\pi v_R$.

What it would add. A two-sided constraint on a scalar mass that the corpus's Pati–Salam article treats as free. Usually a heavy scalar is a way to suppress a rate — "make the leptoquark heavy and the proton is stable". This says you cannot: the leptoquark's mass is bounded above by the breaking scale times $\sqrt\pi\approx1.77$. So a nucleon-decay bound on the leptoquark becomes a bound on $v_R$ directly, with no freedom to decouple.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's leptoquark-mass discussion.

What stands in the way. The bound assumes only the $\lambda^\chi(\chi^\dagger\chi)^2$ contribution to the quartic and a specific unitarity argument. It is a benchmark rather than a theorem, and the paper presents it as such.

4. $n\to\pi^0\nu$ and the absence of charged-lepton modes

The idea. $\Gamma(n\to\pi^0\nu)=\frac12\Gamma(p\to\pi^+\nu)$, and no $p\to\pi^0e^+$.

What it would add. A clean experimental discriminant, which the corpus's proton-decay discussion lacks — the corpus typically says "proton decay is suppressed" without saying what the final state is. Here the final state is specified and one common mode is forbidden. That is what makes a prediction testable rather than merely consistent, and the corpus should adopt the habit.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's proton-decay paragraph.

What stands in the way. The absence of the charged-lepton mode holds "at dimension four" — the paper says the $\chi_d$ Yukawa to charged leptons is absent there. Loop corrections and higher operators would generate it. State it as a leading-order statement.

5. Suppressing $K_L^0$ decay forces nucleon decay: the mixing identity

The idea. $K^{u\nu}_{L/R}=V_{\rm CKM}K_{L/R}U_{\rm PMNS}$, so tuning the quark–lepton mixing to hide the leptoquark from meson decays necessarily produces $\mathcal O(1)$ entries in the block that mediates nucleon decay.

What it would add. An anticorrelation with a proof. The corpus discusses flavour alignment — Blanke's alignment, Barbieri's $U(2)^5$ — as a way to suppress dangerous couplings. Here is a case where the suppression mechanism has a mandatory side effect in another sector, and the identity that forces it is a product of the CKM and PMNS matrices, both measured. That is as model-independent as flavour statements get.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's flavour section, next to the alignment mechanisms, as the counter-example.

What stands in the way. The argument assumes minimal Pati–Salam fermion content. The paper's own "Chiral Pati–Salam" variant changes the content and the conclusion shifts. State the assumption.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and the Chaudhuri conversion are a matched pair, and the corpus should read them together because they reach opposite conclusions about the same scalar sector.

Chaudhuri takes the $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ as the breaking scalar and $v_R$ above $10^{12}$ GeV, and the model is safe. Dutka and Gargalionis take the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ and ask what happens if $v_R$ is small, and they find it is not allowed below $10^8$ GeV.

The two agree: the $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ is safe because it costs $v_R\gtrsim10^{12}$ GeV, and the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ is dangerous because it permits a low scale. There is no contradiction, and the apparent one is resolved by the scalar representation. That resolution is exactly what the corpus's Pati–Salam article should say, because a reader who takes the two papers separately will conclude that the literature disagrees about proton decay in Pati–Salam when it does not.

A second thing worth recording: the paper's bound is stated three times with three different numbers ($4\times10^5$, $9\times10^6$, $10^8$ GeV) and the spread comes from which quark mass enters. The corpus should quote the range, not the largest figure, because the largest figure assumes top-quark dominance of the mixing, which the paper does not justify.

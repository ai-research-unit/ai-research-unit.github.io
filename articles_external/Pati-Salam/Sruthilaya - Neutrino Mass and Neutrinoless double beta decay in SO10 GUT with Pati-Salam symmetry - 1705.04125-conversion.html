What the Paper Does

It runs $SO(10)$ down through an asymmetric left-right group and predicts $0\nu\beta\beta$.

"We demonstrate how a class of non-supersymmetric $SO(10)$ GUT with asymmetric left-right theory $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R\times U(1)_{B-L}\times SU(4)_C$ [as] ... $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)_C$ as intermediate symmetry breaking steps leads to successful gauge coupling unification satisfying proton decay constraints."

"firstly to study the RGEs for gauge couplings by keeping right-handed neutral gauge boson $Z_R$ around LHC energy range, leading [to] interesting dilepton searches at [the] collider while [keeping] $W_R$ at [a] very high scale"

"secondly to explain neutrino masses and associated lepton number violating process like neutrinoless double beta decay in three possible cases"

"The presence of Pati-Salam symmetry and Pati-Salam symmetry with D-parity (discrete left-right symmetry leading to $g_L=g_R$) at the highest scale is to allow two gauge couplings and thereby ensuring precision unification"

"We include one extra fermion singlet per generation in order to implement gauged extended seesaw where light neutrino mass is governed by natural type-II seesaw mechanism whereas type-I seesaw contribution is exactly canceled out."

"Since light neutrino mass formula is independent of Dirac neutrino mass matrix, the value of Dirac neutrino mass is taken to be up-type quark mass matrix which is a characteristic of Pati–Salam symmetry"

The key structural move is the asymmetric left-right group: $Z_R$ light, $W_R$ heavy.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Asymmetric Left-Right Group

In a symmetric left-right model, $g_L=g_R$ and $W_R$, $Z_R$ have comparable masses. In the asymmetric version, $SU(2)_R\to U(1)_R$ at a high scale, leaving a light $Z_R$ from the $U(1)_R\times U(1)_{B-L}$ mixing.

So the spectrum separates: $W_R$ heavy (out of collider reach), $Z_R$ light (in reach).

That is a real phenomenological advantage, and the corpus should record it: the model can be discovered by a dilepton resonance without the $W_R$-mediated $K_L\to\mu e$ problem that kills minimal left-right models.

And it links to hep-ph/0302216's "accessible parity breaking": two ways to make the left-right sector testable — low $M_R$ with symmetric couplings (hep-ph/0302216), or high $W_R$ with a light $Z_R$ (here).

D-Parity and Precision Unification

"Pati–Salam symmetry with D-parity (discrete left-right symmetry leading to $g_L=g_R$) at the highest scale is to allow two gauge couplings and thereby ensuring precision unification"

D-parity is the discrete symmetry exchanging $SU(2)_L\leftrightarrow SU(2)_R$, which forces $g_L=g_R$.

And the paper's point is that having D-parity at the highest scale allows the couplings to split below it, so unification can be precise without forcing $g_L=g_R$ at low energy.

That is the standard resolution of the left-right unification problem, and the corpus should note it: the $g_L=g_R$ relation is a high-scale statement, not a low-scale one.

The Extended Seesaw With Exact Type-I Cancellation

"one extra fermion singlet per generation ... where light neutrino mass is governed by natural type-II seesaw mechanism whereas type-I seesaw contribution is exactly canceled out."

The "extended seesaw" (or "double seesaw"): the mass matrix is enlarged by a singlet $S$ per generation, and the parameters can be chosen so that the type-I and type-III contributions cancel, leaving a type-II.

Why that matters: the light neutrino mass is then independent of the Dirac mass matrix $m_D$, so it does not inherit the up-quark mass hierarchy.

That is a genuine simplification, and the corpus should record it: the model's neutrino masses are not tied to the quark masses, which sidesteps the $m_D\sim m_u$ tension.

And the paper uses the freedom: "the value of Dirac neutrino mass is taken to be [the] up-type quark mass matrix, which is a characteristic of Pati–Salam symmetry."

So $m_D=m_u$ is assumed (the PS relation), and the light masses come from type-II instead. That is coherent: the PS relation is kept for structure, and the phenomenology is decoupled from it.

$0\nu\beta\beta$ and the Lower Bound on the Lightest Neutrino Mass

"We present analytic relation for effective Majorana mass parameter and corresponding half-life arising from new physics contributions due to purely left-handed currents through exchange of heavy right-handed neutrinos and sterile neutrinos."

"We numerically estimate ... and derive [a] lower bound on [the] lightest neutrino mass by saturating with experimental bounds like GERDA Phase-II, KamLAND-Zen and EXO."

So the new physics adds to the standard $0\nu\beta\beta$ rate, and the experimental bounds therefore exclude a region of the lightest neutrino mass.

That is the correct logic and the corpus should use it: an upper bound on the half-life becomes a lower bound on $m_{\rm lightest}$ when the new physics contribution is included.

The corpus should note the result's form: the model predicts a floor on the lightest neutrino mass, which is testable by the next generation of $0\nu\beta\beta$ experiments (nEXO, LEGEND-1000).

And it is a welcome change from the batch's usual vague neutrino predictions.

The Three Breaking Chains

"three possible cases depending on how $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_R\times U(1)_{B-L}\times SU(3)_C$ breaks down to SM" — the paper analyses all three and focuses on one.

The corpus should record that the analysis is systematic: the chain's ordering is a choice, and each choice gives different phenomenology.

And that is the right way to do it, because the intermediate scales are not predicted.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. Asymmetric left-right: $Z_R$ light, $W_R$ heavy

The idea. Breaking $SU(2)_R$ early leaves a light $Z_R$ and a heavy $W_R$.

What it would add. A testable left-right sector without the $K_L\to\mu e$ problem. And a second route to "accessible parity breaking".

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's gauge-sector section.

What stands in the way. $Z_R$ mass bounds.

2. D-parity at the high scale allows $g_L\neq g_R$ at low energy

The idea. The equality is a boundary condition, not a low-energy relation.

What it would add. The resolution of the left-right unification tension.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's unification section.

What stands in the way. Nothing.

3. Extended seesaw cancels type-I and leaves type-II

The idea. Extra singlets decouple the light masses from $m_D$.

What it would add. A way to keep the PS relation $m_D=m_u$ without inheriting the wrong neutrino spectrum.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's neutrino section.

What stands in the way. The cancellation is a tuning.

4. $0\nu\beta\beta$ bounds become a lower bound on the lightest neutrino mass

The idea. New physics adds to the rate, so the null results exclude the light region.

What it would add. A testable prediction, and the correct logic for interpreting $0\nu\beta\beta$ in extended models.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's neutrino section.

What stands in the way. Hadronic uncertainties.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's most careful $0\nu\beta\beta$ analysis, and its logic is worth generalising in the corpus.

The logic: the standard light-neutrino contribution and the new-physics contribution add, so a null result bounds their sum, which converts into a lower bound on the lightest mass when the new physics is fixed.

Most papers in the batch either ignore the new physics or assume it dominates. This one keeps both and derives a bound.

The corpus should adopt the method for its neutrino section: for each model, state the $0\nu\beta\beta$ rate as standard-plus-new, and derive what the null results imply.

That is a service the corpus can perform across the batch, and it is more useful than repeating each model's claim.

The second point: the asymmetric left-right group deserves a note because it is not the textbook left-right model. The corpus's reader will assume $g_L=g_R$ unless told. So the corpus should say that the testable version of left-right may be the asymmetric one, and that 1705.04125 and hep-ph/0302216 together define the two options.

What the Paper Does

A short, sharp numerical note. It asks whether one intermediate Pati–Salam stage is enough to fit the three measured couplings, without supersymmetry, and answers yes with a specific scale.

"We demonstrate to two-loop order that an intermediate symmetrically embedded Pati-Salam $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)$ level of symmetry is all that is necessary to accommodate empirical values of $\alpha(M_Z)$, $\alpha_s(M_Z)$ and $\sin^2\theta_w(M_Z)$ within a grand unification context but with a high ($10^{14}$ GeV) intermediate mass scale and with a concomitant higher GUT scale."

The numbers:

$$ \log(M/M_Z)=27.10\ \Longrightarrow\ M=5.90\times10^{11}M_Z\simeq5.38\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{GeV}, $$

$$ M'=6.05\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{GeV},\qquad \log(M'/M)=4.72 . $$

An intermediate scale of $5\times10^{13}$ GeV and a GUT scale of $6\times10^{16}$ GeV, with no supersymmetry. That is the whole content. The title's "trailing edge" is the point: the Pati–Salam stage sits just below the GUT scale, not in the middle, because that is the only place the couplings will have it.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Charge Relations

The mixing is written with two angles, and their values are fixed by requiring the photon to couple correctly:

$$ A_\mu=W_{3L\mu}\sin\theta_w+W_{3R\mu}\cos\theta_w\cos\phi+V_{15\mu}\cos\theta_w\sin\phi, $$

$$ Z_\mu=W_{3L\mu}\cos\theta_w-W_{3R\mu}\sin\theta_w\cos\phi-V_{15\mu}\sin\theta_w\sin\phi, $$

$$ Z'_\mu=W_{3R\mu}\sin\theta_w\sin\phi-V_{15\mu}\sin\theta_w\cos\phi . $$

"Correct charge assignments for fermions coupling to the photon are obtained by requiring that

$$ g_L\sin\theta_w=e,\qquad g_R\cos\theta_w\cos\phi=e,\qquad g_4\cos\theta_w\sin\phi=\sqrt{2/3}\,e . $$

The third relation is the one with content: it says the $SU(4)$ coupling carries a factor $\sqrt{2/3}$ relative to $e$, which is the statement that the $U(1)_X$ generator inside $SU(4)$ is normalised as $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)/\sqrt{24}$ — the $\sqrt{24}=\sqrt{8\cdot3}$ from the trace. The paper writes that generator explicitly in the Lagrangian as $\frac{1}{\sqrt{24}}\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)$.

Then the sum rule, "a result quoted in Eq. (17) of ref. [2]":

$$ \frac{e^2}{g_L^2}+\frac{e^2}{g_R^2}+\frac{2e^2}{3g_4^2}=1, $$

and

$$ \frac{1}{g'^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{2}{3g_4^2}. $$

That second equation is the whole of Pati–Salam electroweak mixing in one line: the hypercharge coupling is the $SU(2)_R$ coupling and the $SU(4)$ coupling in parallel, with the $\frac23$ from the generator normalisation. Compare the Standard Model, where $1/g'^2$ is a free parameter. Here it is a sum of two other couplings, which is what makes the model predictive — and what forces the intermediate scale when you demand all three meet.

The authors verify the charges explicitly, e.g. for the lepton doublet

$$ \begin{pmatrix}\nu_e\\e^-\end{pmatrix}_L:\ g_L\sin\theta_w\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\-1/2\end{pmatrix}+\frac{g_4\cos\theta_w\sin\phi}{\sqrt{24}}(-3)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\-e\end{pmatrix}, $$

using $\sqrt{2/3}\cdot(-3)/\sqrt{24}=-3e\cdot\sqrt{2}/(\sqrt{3}\cdot2\sqrt6)=-e/2$ in the lower component and $+e/2$ in the upper, so that the neutrino is neutral. For the quark doublet the same computation gives $\begin{pmatrix}2e/3\\-e/3\end{pmatrix}$ "for fractionally charged quarks".

The check is worth recording because it pins the normalisation: with conventional quark charges the sum rule has $\frac23$; with integer-charge quarks it would differ. The paper notes the alternative in passing — "it could certainly be formulated with conventional charges ... Although it is generally known for its proposal of integer charged quarks" — and then uses the fractional ones.

The Hierarchy and the Counting Argument

$$ \begin{array}{c}G\\\downarrow\\ SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)\ \ \text{at }M\\\downarrow\\ SU(2)_L\times U(1)\times SU(3)_c\ \ \text{at }M_Z\end{array} $$

"We assume at the intermediate mass scale $M$ the $SU(2)$ coupling constants are smaller than that of $SU(4)$; i.e., that $g_L(M)=g_R(M)

Then the counting observation that makes the one-loop run work:

"We also note to one loop order that the fermionic content to the evolution of the $SU(2)$ coupling constants $g_L(\mu)$ and $g_R(\mu)$ per generation is the same as the fermionic content of $g_4(\mu)$. Four chiral doublets of $SU(2)_{L}$ or $R$ contributes equivalently to two non-chiral fundamental representations of $SU(4)$."

A generation contributes $\mathbf4+\bar{\mathbf4}$ worth of $SU(2)$ doublets — four of them — and a $\mathbf4$ of $SU(4)$ has four components. So the beta functions of $g_L$, $g_R$ and $g_4$ get equal fermion contributions per generation, and $g_L=g_R=g_4$ is preserved by the running above $M$ even though the groups differ. That is a small miracle of the Pati–Salam fermion assignment and it is why a symmetric embedding can survive to a high scale.

The paper notes the analogy: "The same situation occurs in the calculation of Georgi, Quinn and Weinberg, where two fundamental representations of $SU(3)_c$ contributions equivalently to the $\beta$-function as four chiral $SU(2)_L$ doublets."

The Equations That Fix $M$

Three relations, each an inverse-coupling difference:

$$ \frac{4\pi}{\alpha_s(M_Z)}=\frac{4\pi}{g_4^2(M)}-2\beta_3\log\frac{M}{M_Z},\qquad \beta_3=\frac{33-2n_f}{48\pi^2}, $$

$$ \frac{1}{\alpha(M_Z)}=4\pi^2\left[\frac{2}{g^2(M)}+\frac{10}{3g_4^2(M)}\right]-\left(\frac{2}{3}\beta_1+2\beta_2\right)\log\frac{M}{M_Z}, $$

and the mass-independent combination

$$ \frac{4\pi}{g_L^2(M_Z)}+\frac{4\pi}{g_R^2(M_Z)}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{4\pi}{g_{15}^2(M_Z)}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{4\pi}{\alpha_s(M_Z)}+\frac{2\sin^2\theta_w(M_Z)-1}{\alpha(M_Z)}+\cdots $$

"Given these (central) values, we can solve Eq. (16) for $\log(M/M_Z)$; note that $n_f$ cancels explicitly from the final righthand side of Eq. (16)."

That cancellation is the paper's technical trick. The number of flavours drops out of the relation that determines the intermediate scale, so the answer does not depend on how many quarks are active — only on the measured couplings at $M_Z$. That is why the result is clean, and it is worth noting because it means the prediction is insensitive to the threshold structure between $M_Z$ and $M$.

The Honest Ending

"Since the intermediate mass scale $M$ is so very large, however, there remain severe hierarchy problems in this scenario. For example, one might have to appeal to a radiative breaking of electroweak symmetry, with a concomitant exact symmetry preventing $\phi^2$ terms in the Higgs potential, in order to avoid contamination of the Higgs mass from scales $M$ and $M'$."

"We note in conclusion, though, that gauge coupling constant unification is seen to occur in a sensible hierarchy without supersymmetry, as is generally assumed to be required, a scenario also not entirely immune from hierarchy problems."

The last clause is the fair one: non-supersymmetric unification does not solve the hierarchy problem, and neither does supersymmetric unification.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

The rank is a judgement, ordered by how directly the corpus needs the object.

1. $1/g'^2=1/g_R^2+\frac{2}{3}\cdot1/g_4^2$: hypercharge as two couplings in parallel

The idea. In Pati–Salam the hypercharge coupling is not free. It is the $SU(2)_R$ coupling and the $SU(4)$ coupling combined with a $\frac23$ from the generator normalisation.

What it would add. The single most compact statement of what Pati–Salam does to the electroweak sector. The corpus's Pati–Salam article says hypercharge is a combination of $B-L$ and $T_{3R}$, which is right but qualitative. This gives the coefficient: $1/g'^2=1/g_R^2+(2/3)(1/g_4^2)$, and the $\frac23$ comes from $\mathrm{Tr}(T_{15}^2)=\frac{24}{6}$ versus $\mathrm{Tr}(T_{3R}^2)=\frac12$. That number should be in the corpus with its derivation, because it is the one place Pati–Salam makes a numerical prediction about a Standard Model parameter.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pati-salam-model-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md, in the hypercharge section, and articles_physics/the-weinberg-angle-the-neutral-current-and-the-z-coupling.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The derivation is two lines of trace arithmetic: $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-3)/\sqrt{24}$ has $\mathrm{Tr}(T^2)=\frac{1+1+1+9}{24}=\frac12$, equal to $\mathrm{Tr}(T_{3R}^2)$ on a doublet, and the $\frac{2}{3}$ follows from normalising $g_4$ to match $g_3$ on the colour subgroup.

2. One intermediate Pati–Salam stage suffices, at $5\times10^{13}$ GeV, without supersymmetry

The idea. Three measured couplings, one intermediate stage, one GUT scale, no SUSY, and it works.

What it would add. A worked, checkable answer to "does non-supersymmetric unification work?" The corpus's unification articles are built around the standard result that the three SM couplings miss in the SM and meet in the MSSM. Here they meet in a third way: not by adding superpartners but by inserting a Pati–Salam stage that changes the running between $M$ and $M'$. That is a third option and the corpus should have it, with the numbers: $M=5.4\times10^{13}$ GeV, $M'=6.1\times10^{16}$ GeV.

Where it lands. articles_physics/asymptotic-freedom-and-the-running-of-the-strong-coupling.md and the Pati–Salam article's unification section.

What stands in the way. Nothing, but note the paper does not solve the hierarchy problem — it says so — so the corpus should not present it as an alternative to SUSY on those grounds, only on the unification grounds.

3. Four doublets equal two fundamentals: the beta functions match

The idea. Per generation, four chiral $SU(2)$ doublets contribute to $g_2$ exactly as two non-chiral fundamentals of $SU(4)$ contribute to $g_4$. So $g_L=g_R=g_4$ is preserved above $M$ even though the groups differ.

What it would add. The reason a symmetric embedding can survive. Naively, $SU(2)$ and $SU(4)$ run differently and any equality at $M'$ is destroyed by $M$. It is not, because the fermion content is arranged so the coefficients agree, and that is a property of the $\mathbf4+\bar{\mathbf4}$ assignment: $4$ components, each carrying $SU(2)$ index, matches two fundamentals of four. The corpus can state this as a counting identity.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's unification section, and the asymptotic-freedom article.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The identity is $4\times\frac12=\frac{4}{2}$ per doublet versus $\frac{4}{2}$ per fundamental — it is the same Dynkin index, which is what the paper means by "contributes equivalently".

4. If $g_1^{-2}(M)

The idea. Above $M$, $g_3^{-2}$ runs exactly as $g_4^{-2}$ because $SU(3)\subset SU(4)$, while $g_1^{-2}$ runs as a mixture. If the ordering is wrong at $M$, the two can never cross.

What it would add. A sharp criterion of the form "this model can or cannot unify", derived from the embedding rather than from a fit. The corpus's unification discussion is numerical — do the lines cross? This is structural: the crossing is guaranteed or forbidden by which group sits inside which, and it can be decided before any running is done.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's unification section, and the asymptotic-freedom article as a general technique.

What stands in the way. Nothing. The statement is: $SU(3)\subset SU(4)$ means $g_3=g_4$ at all scales above $M$, so $g_3$'s trajectory is fixed once $g_4$'s is.

5. $n_f$ cancels from the equation that fixes $M$

The idea. The number of flavours drops out of the combination that determines $\log(M/M_Z)$, so the intermediate scale depends only on the measured couplings.

What it would add. A robustness statement. Thresholds between $M_Z$ and $M$ are the usual source of uncertainty in a unification prediction, and here they do not enter. The prediction is therefore cleaner than a typical GUT-scale estimate, and the corpus can say why: the equation is a difference of inverse couplings that share the same $n_f$ dependence.

Where it lands. The Pati–Salam article's numerical claims.

What stands in the way. The cancellation is at one loop, in this specific combination. At two loops the paper finds only a small shift, so the statement holds numerically but not as an identity.

One observation about the corpus's map

This is the second paper in this batch with a high Pati–Salam scale, and the third distinct answer to "at what scale does Pati–Salam break?":

paper scale reason
Elias–Steele (this) $5\times10^{13}$ GeV to unify the couplings
Dutka–Gargalionis (2211.02054) $\gtrsim10^8$ GeV to suppress dimension-5 nucleon decay
Dolan–Dutka–Volkas (2012.05976) $\gtrsim5$ TeV lower bound from meson decays, with mixing
Dent (0802.0180) $5\times10^3$ GeV chosen to fit the couplings in that model

Four papers, four scales, spanning ten orders of magnitude, all called Pati–Salam. The corpus's article needs to make clear that the breaking scale is not a property of the group. It is set by whichever constraint is binding: unification, proton decay, meson decays, or a fit. Each of these four papers is answering a different question and the answers do not compete.

The Elias–Steele numbers are worth keeping precisely because they are the high end and they are derived rather than assumed: $M=5.4\times10^{13}$ GeV follows from the measured couplings, given the hierarchy. That makes it the one scale in the batch that is a prediction rather than a bound.

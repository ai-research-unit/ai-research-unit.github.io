What the Paper Does

It turns the Higgs-inflation scenario into predictions for the right-handed neutrino masses.

"mechanism of non-thermal leptogenesis in the framework of an inflationary model based on a supersymmetric (SUSY) Pati-Salam Grand Unified Theory (GUT)."

"inflation is driven by a quartic potential associated with the Higgs fields involved in the spontaneous GUT symmetry breaking, in the presence of a non-minimal coupling of the inflaton field to gravity."

"The inflationary model relies on renormalizable superpotential terms and does not lead to overproduction of magnetic monopoles. It is largely independent of one-loop radiative corrections, and it can be consistent with current observational data on the inflationary observables, with the GUT symmetry breaking scale assuming its SUSY value."

"Non-thermal leptogenesis is realized by the out-of-equilibrium decay of the two lightest right-handed (RH) neutrinos, which are produced by the inflaton decay."

"we constrain the masses of the RH neutrinos in the range (1010 − 1015 ) GeV and the Dirac neutrino masses of the two first generations to values between 0."

So the paper's output is two mass windows — and the corpus should record them, because they are testable by leptogenesis and by neutrino experiments.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What the Paper Adds

Three things beyond 1108.1771: 1. Both lightest right-handed neutrinos decay (not one), which increases the CP asymmetry. 2. A numerical scan, giving the mass windows. 3. **The confrontation with "the GUT prediction for the heaviest Dirac neutrino mass"

** — the PS relation $m_{\nu,\rm Dirac}^{\rm heaviest}\sim m_t$ at the GUT scale, which is a genuine prediction.

The corpus should record the third point, because it is a Pati–Salam-specific relation: the top quark and the heaviest Dirac neutrino share a multiplet, so their masses are related at the PS scale. That is a statement no other group makes, and it constrains the seesaw.

The Mass Windows

$M_{N_{1,2}}\in(10^{10},10^{15})$ GeV, and $m_{D_{1,2}}\in(0.1,20)$ GeV.

The lower end of $M_N$ is the thermal-leptogenesis-like bound; the upper end is the GUT scale.

And $m_{D}\sim0.1$–$20$ GeV for the first two generations is a specific and unusual prediction: it is not the up- and down-quark masses, so the Dirac neutrino masses are not simply related to the charged-fermion masses.

The corpus should record that, because it contradicts the naive PS expectation that all four components of a $\bar{\mathbf4}$ share a mass. The paper's window says the neutrino Dirac masses are heavier than the quark masses of the same generation — so the $\bar{\mathbf 4}$'s components are split.

And the corpus should note that this splitting must come from the PS-breaking, which is a constraint on the Higgs sector.

The Two-Neutrino Decay

"the out-of-equilibrium decay of the two lightest right-handed (RH) neutrinos, which are produced by the inflaton decay."

Why two: the CP asymmetry from a single $N_R$ requires interference with another, so at least two are needed.

And using both increases the asymmetry and relaxes the $T_{\rm RH}$ bound, which is why it helps with the gravitino problem.

The corpus should note the mechanism: leptogenesis needs at least two right-handed neutrinos, and the PS model has three (one per family). So the model naturally supplies what leptogenesis needs — another instance of a Pati–Salam structure doing double duty.

The Monopole Claim

"

does not lead to overproduction of magnetic monopoles"

Same as 1108.1771: inflation is simultaneous with the breaking, so the monopoles are diluted.

And note what this paper adds: **"it can be consistent with current" "the GUT symmetry breaking scale assuming its SUSY value. Non-thermal"

** — so the breaking scale is fixed at $M_{\rm GUT}\approx2\times10^{16}$ GeV, not a free parameter.

That is important: the scenario does not tune $v_{PS}$; it uses the SUSY value. So its predictions are sharp.

The Unitarity Problem, Again

The non-minimal coupling is still there, and the paper does not address its cutoff.

The corpus should note it in both conversions, because it is the scenario's main theoretical weakness, and the hype-free way to present the model is: economical, predictive, and with a known UV problem.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper is the batch's most predictive cosmology because it does not tune, and the corpus should present it as the benchmark.

The inputs: the PS group, its SUSY breaking scale, the PS Higgs as inflaton, and the PS relation between the top and the heaviest Dirac neutrino.

The outputs: $n_s$, $r$, the baryon asymmetry, and the windows for $M_N$ and $m_D$.

Nothing is tuned except the non-minimal coupling $\xi$, which the amplitude fixes. That is a real prediction, and it is falsifiable: if the right-handed neutrinos are outside $(10^{10},10^{15})$ GeV, the scenario dies.

The corpus should say so, and should also say what the scenario cannot do: no strings (inflation dilutes them), no axion unless PQ is added, and a heavy gravitino. The costs are stated, the prediction is sharp, and the UV problem is known.

That is what a good model in this field looks like, and the corpus should use it as the standard against which the others are read.

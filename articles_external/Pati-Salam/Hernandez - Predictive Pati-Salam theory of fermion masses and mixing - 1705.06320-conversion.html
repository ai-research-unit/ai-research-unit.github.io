What the Paper Does

It is the first fully realistic flavour realisation of Pati–Salam, and the authors say so with appropriate modesty.

"this is the first time that a fully realistic and to some extent predictive flavor realization of a Pati-Salam scenario is given."

The construction: Pati–Salam with a $\Delta(27)$ family symmetry, giving a Froggatt–Nielsen-like quark mixing pattern and a predictive neutrino sector via inverse seesaw.

"The theory realizes a realistic Froggatt-Nielsen picture of quark mixing and a predictive pattern of neutrino oscillations. We find that, for normal neutrino mass ordering, the atmospheric angle must lie in the higher octant, CP must be violated in oscillations, and there is a lower bound for the $0\nu\beta\beta$ decay rate. For the case of inverted mass ordering, we find that the lower atmospheric octant is preferred, and that CP can be conserved in oscillations."

Those are sharp, falsifiable statements and they are the paper's value. Each is a binary or one-sided prediction, which is much stronger than a fit.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why $\Delta(27)$

"The $\Delta(27)$ discrete group is a subgroup of $SU(3)$, has 27 elements divided into 11 conjugacy classes ... These irreducible representations are: two triplets ... and 9 singlets, i.e., $\mathbf1_{k,l}$ ($k,l=0,1,2$), where $k$ and $l$ correspond to the $Z_3$ and $Z_3'$ charges."

"The $\Delta(27)$ discrete group is a simple group of the type $\Delta(3n^2)$ with $n=3$ and is isomorphic to the semi-direct product group $(Z_3'\times Z_3'')\rtimes Z_3$. Indeed, the simplest group of the type $\Delta(3n^2)$ is $\Delta(3)\equiv Z_3$. The next group is $\Delta(12)$, which is isomorphic to $A_4$. Thus, the $\Delta(27)$ discrete group is the next simplest nontrivial group of the type $\Delta(3n^2)$."

That is the reason to use it, stated as a sequence: $Z_3$, then $A_4$, then $\Delta(27)$. Each step up the $\Delta(3n^2)$ ladder buys one more triplet representation — and a model that needs to assign three generations to a triplet and also needs several distinct singlets for the Froggatt–Nielsen charges needs both.

The corpus should record the ladder, because it explains why $A_4$ (used in 2404.06520 in this batch) and $\Delta(27)$ appear in the same literature: they are consecutive rungs.

Nine singlets is a lot, and that is the point — Froggatt–Nielsen needs a hierarchy of charges, and nine singlets give you a fine-grained grading. $A_4$ has three singlets, which is too coarse.

The Quark Sector

"The theory realizes a realistic Froggatt-Nielsen picture of quark mixing."

A Froggatt–Nielsen picture, not a derivation, and the distinction matters. The charges are assigned to fit the observed hierarchy, so the "prediction" is the pattern rather than the numbers. The corpus should not treat FN fits as derivations, and this paper is honest about it in the phrase "picture of".

The Neutrino Sector, and Why It Is Predictive

"neutrino masses arise from an inverse seesaw mechanism and the allowed ranges for the atmospheric mixing angle $\theta_{23}$ and CP violating phase $\delta_{CP}$ are rather restricted once one takes into account the precise measurements of the remaining oscillation parameters. This makes the model rather predictive."

Inverse seesaw is the reason the predictions survive. In a type-I seesaw the light neutrino mass matrix is $m_DM_R^{-1}m_D^T$, and the freedom in $M_R$ absorbs almost anything. In inverse seesaw the mass matrix has a $\mu$ parameter that is small by construction (it is the $L$-violating entry), so the structure of the Dirac sector shows through.

So the predictivity comes from choosing the seesaw that has fewer free parameters, which is the same logic as 2305.19356's fermiophobia: restrict the structure until the model says something.

The resulting claims, which the paper states as conditional on the ordering: - Normal ordering: $\theta_{23}$ in the upper octant, $\delta_{CP}\neq0$, and $m_{\beta\beta}$ bounded below. - Inverted ordering: lower octant preferred, $\delta_{CP}$ may be zero.

The upper-octant prediction for normal ordering is the sharpest. Current data favour the upper octant slightly, so this is a live test, and the correlation with $0\nu\beta\beta$ makes it doubly checkable.

The Cost, Admitted

"Admittedly, the model is rather complex, especially in its scalar sector. However, it serves as a 'proof-of-concept' attempt."

That is the fair summary and it should be kept. A $\Delta(27)$ with nine singlets and enough scalars to break Pati–Salam and generate the FN structure is a large field content, and the paper does not pretend otherwise.

The LHC Portal

"neutrino masses arise from a low-scale seesaw mechanism, whose messengers can be produced by a $Z'$ portal at the LHC."

Because the inverse seesaw is at a low scale, the messengers are light enough to produce. The $Z'$ is the Pati–Salam $U(1)_{B-L}$-like gauge boson, and it decays to the pairs. So the model has a collider signature connected to the neutrino mass mechanism, which is the same structure as the twin-PS papers: a TeV vector boson whose couplings encode the flavour model.

What it would add. A map of the discrete groups used in this literature and the reason each is chosen: the number of singlets sets how fine a Froggatt–Nielsen grading you can impose. The corpus's flavour discussion should carry this, because otherwise $A_4$, $S_4$, $\Delta(27)$ look like arbitrary choices.

The idea. Inverse seesaw's $\mu$ is small by construction, so the Dirac structure shows through and the oscillation parameters are correlated.

What it would add. A mechanism-level explanation for why some flavour models predict and others do not. The corpus's seesaw discussion treats the three types as alternatives with different scales; here the choice determines whether the model is falsifiable.

One observation about the corpus's map

This paper and the modular-symmetry papers in this batch (2404.06520, 2503.14610, 2501.00302) are two generations of the same programme, and the difference is instructive.

Here the flavour symmetry is discrete and non-modular: you assign charges to fields and write higher-dimensional operators suppressed by a flavons' VEVs. The predictive content comes from the charge assignment, which is chosen.

There the symmetry is modular: the same group, but the Yukawa couplings are fixed functions of a modulus $\tau$, so the "charges" are replaced by modular weights and the couplings are determined. The predictive content comes from the modulus, which is a single complex parameter.

So the modular papers are an attempt to remove exactly the freedom that this paper uses. Whether that succeeds is a separate question, but the corpus should present the two as a progression rather than as unrelated model-building choices — the same groups, a different way of fixing the couplings.

The second point: "first time that a fully realistic and to some extent predictive flavor realization of a Pati-Salam scenario" is a claim worth dating. Pati–Salam was proposed in 1974 and took forty-three years to get a realistic flavour sector, which is a fact about how hard the flavour problem is and not about the group.

What the Paper Does

Claims a mathematical framework, built inside an extended classical mechanics, from which the quantum nature of matter and the relation $E=h\nu$ can be derived, and from which Schrödinger's equation follows. The aim is stated as removing the incompatibility between classical and quantum mechanics; the status is a proposed derivation, not a theorem.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim

"we introduce a mathematical framework, developed within the extended classical mechanics set-up, which helps us derive quantum nature and the relation E = hν from it. By doing so, we give a solid mathematical foundation to quantum nature and remove the inherent incompatibilities between classical mechanics and quantum mechanics. We also derive Schrödinger’s equation with the help of this framework to establish the validity of this framework. Keywords"

The historical frame

The introduction recounts Planck's 1900 introduction of quantised energy: "Planck proposed that electromagnetic energy could only be emitted or absorbed in discrete packets, which he called “quanta”. He suggested that the energy of each quantum is directly proportional to the frequency of the radiation, with the constant of proportionality being what we now know as Planck’s constant (h). Mathematically, this relationship is expressed as E = hν, where E is energy, h is Planck’s constant, and ν is frequency [1]."

Planck's own view

"Initially, Planck himself regarded his proposal as merely a mathematical trick to fit the experimental data of black body radiation. Planck found that his relation is in 'direct contadiction' and 'incompatible' with the classical theory." The misspelling is as printed. The paper's premise is that this incompatibility can be removed rather than accepted.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Derive

$\hbar$ instead of axiomatising it

The idea. If $E=h\nu$ follows from an extended classical framework, then the quantum of action is a consequence of a structure and not a postulate. This is a mechanism claim, and a strong one.

What it would add. Exactly the alternative the corpus's canonical-commutation article argues against: the corpus holds $\hbar$ to be an independent axiom and says so. A worked derivation, even a flawed one, is the counter-argument that article would have to answer.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-biquaternion-framework-and-the-canonical-commutation-relation-an-independent-axiom.md, as the opposing position.

What stands in the way. The paper is a manuscript without peer review, and its framework is not stated in a form the corpus's algebra can absorb; the derivation would have to be checked step by step before it could be cited as a challenge.

2. The classical limit is not a limit but a special case

The idea. Classical mechanics is recovered by restricting the framework, not by taking $\hbar\to0$. That reverses the usual direction of the correspondence.

What it would add. A third option for the corpus's quantum-classical article, which lists the limit and the projection.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-quantum-classical-divide-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md.

What stands in the way. The paper's restriction is not written as a condition on the algebra, so the corpus cannot state it as an operation on its own objects.

This is a ten-page manuscript; the framework, the derivation of $E=h\nu$ and the derivation of Schrödinger's equation are its content.

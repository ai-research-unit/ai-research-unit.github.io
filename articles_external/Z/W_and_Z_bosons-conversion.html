What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the W and Z bosons: the three vector bosons mediating the weak interaction, their charges and masses, their discovery at CERN in 1983, and their place in the electroweak theory as the massive partners of the photon after symmetry breaking.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The particles

"the W and Z bosons are vector bosons that are together known as the weak bosons or more generally as the intermediate vector bosons. These elementary particles mediate the weak interaction; the respective symbols are W+, W−, and Z0. The W± bosons have either a positive or negative electric charge of 1 elementary charge and are each other's antiparticles. The Z0 boson is electric[ally neutral]."

The masses

The W has a mass of about 80 GeV and the Z about 91 GeV, which is why the weak interaction is short-ranged. The masses are not parameters of the symmetric theory: they arise from the Higgs mechanism, and the ratio of W to Z mass is fixed by the weak mixing angle, $m_W=m_Z\cos\theta_W$.

The neutral current

The Z boson carries a neutral current, which is what makes neutrino scattering possible without charge exchange. That process is the experimental evidence that the Z exists as a separate particle and not as a mixture of the charged ones.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Mass is what breaks the symmetry, and the ratio is a prediction

The idea. The symmetric theory has four massless gauge fields; one combination stays massless (the photon) and three acquire mass. The ratio $m_W/m_Z=\cos\theta_W$ is fixed by the mixing, so the masses are not independent.

What it would add. A concrete prediction from a broken symmetry, which the corpus's electroweak article asserts structurally but does not put as a number relation.

2. A neutral current needs a neutral mediator

The idea. Neutrino scattering without charge exchange cannot proceed by W exchange, so a separate neutral boson is required. The existence of the Z is forced by the observed neutral-current processes before it is seen directly.

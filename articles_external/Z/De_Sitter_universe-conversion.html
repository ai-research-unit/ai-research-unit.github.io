What the Paper Does

A two-page encyclopedia entry on the de Sitter universe as a cosmological model: spatially flat, matter-free, dominated by a positive cosmological constant, with the expansion rate scaling as the square root of $\Lambda$, and with the observation that the concordance model places the real universe in this class both very early and very late.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The model

"A de Sitter universe is a cosmological solution to the Einstein field equations of general relativity, named after Willem de Sitter. It models the universe as spatially flat and neglects ordinary matter, so the dynamics of the universe are dominated by the cosmological constant, thought to correspond to dark energy in our universe or the inflaton field in the early universe."

When it applies

"According to the models of inflation and current observations of the accelerating universe, the concordance models of physical cosmology are converging on a consistent model where our universe was best described as a de Sitter universe at about a time 𝑡 = 10−33 s after the fiducial Big Bang singularity, and far into the future. Mathematical"

The one relation

"A de Sitter universe has no ordinary matter content but with a positive cosmological constant (Λ) that sets the expansion rate, 𝐻. A larger cosmological constant leads to a larger expansion rate: 𝐻 ∝ √Λ, where the constants of proportionality depend on conventions. It is common to describe a patch"

That proportionality is the entry's only equation, and the caveat about conventions is worth keeping: the numerical coefficient depends on how $H$ and $\Lambda$ are normalised, so the statement is a scaling law rather than a formula.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The same model at both ends of the history

The idea. A matter-free model describes the universe at $10^{-33}\,\mathrm{s}$, when the inflaton dominates, and again in the far future, when everything else has diluted. The intermediate epoch, where matter and radiation matter, is the exception.

What it would add. A framing for the corpus's cosmology: an algebra-carried metric that reproduces a matter-free expanding phase would already cover the two epochs in which the real universe is simplest, and the matter era is the harder test rather than the first one.

Where it lands. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md, in the choice of test case.

What stands in the way. The corpus's missing source is precisely what a matter-free model does not need, so passing the de Sitter test would not address the article's complaint.

2. $H\propto\sqrt{\Lambda}$ is a scaling law, not a formula

The idea. The proportionality constant depends on conventions, so the content is that the expansion rate is set by the square root of the constant and by nothing else.

What it would add. A precise statement of what a carried metric must reproduce: not a number but a scaling, which is weaker and therefore easier to satisfy.

Where it lands. articles_physics/flrw-in-the-frame-route.md, at the Friedmann relation.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; it is a matter of stating the target correctly.

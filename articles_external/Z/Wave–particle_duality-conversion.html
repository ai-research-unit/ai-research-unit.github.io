What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on wave–particle duality: the statement that quantum objects show wave or particle behaviour according to the experiment, the historical route from Huygens and Newton through Planck, Einstein and de Broglie to the double-slit experiments, and the modern reading through complementarity and the uncertainty relation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The statement

"Wave–particle duality is the concept in quantum mechanics that fundamental entities of the universe, like photons and electrons, exhibit particle or wave properties according to the experimental circumstances.[1]: 59 It expresses the inability of the classical concepts such as particle or wave to fully describe the behavior of quantum objects.[2]"

The historical route

During the 19th and early 20th centu[ry] light was settled as a wave and matter as particles, and the two identifications were then reversed in turn: Planck's quanta, Einstein's light quanta, and de Broglie's matter waves. The article's point is that each reversal was forced by an experiment, not by a preference.

The modern reading

The duality is not a superposition of two natures but a statement about what a given measurement asks. Which-path information and interference are complementary: a measurement that reveals the path destroys the fringes, and the quantitative trade-off is the Englert–Greenberger relation.

"The experiment shows wave interference revealed a single particle at a time—quantum mechanical electrons display both wave and particle behavior."

"Physical systems exhibiting wave behavior and described by the mathematics of wave equations include water waves, seismic waves, sound waves, radio waves, and more."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Duality is about the question, not about the object

The idea. Nothing in the object is both wave and particle; the description is selected by the measurement, and the two descriptions are complementary rather than simultaneous. This is a reading.

What it would add. A precise statement of what the corpus's wave-particle articles assert, which they sometimes put as a property of the object.

2. Which-path information and visibility trade off quantitatively

The idea. The loss of fringe visibility is bounded by the available path information, $V^2+D^2\le1$ in the Englert–Greenberger form. The trade-off is a formula, not a slogan.

What it would add. A quantitative statement the corpus's duality articles could adopt in place of the qualitative one.

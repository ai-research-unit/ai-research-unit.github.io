What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the strong interaction: the force confining quarks into hadrons and binding nucleons into nuclei, its description by quantum chromodynamics with colour charge and gluons, asymptotic freedom, confinement, and the origin of most of a nucleon's mass.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The scope

"the strong interaction, also called the strong force or strong nuclear force, is one of the four known fundamental interactions. It confines quarks into protons, neutrons, and other hadron particles, and also binds neutrons and protons to create atomic nuclei, where it is called the nuclear force."

The mass

"Most of the mass of a proton or neutron is the result of the strong interaction" — the binding energy of the gluon field and the quarks' kinetic energy, not the quarks' rest masses.

The two regimes

At short distance the coupling is weak and quarks behave as free particles (asymptotic freedom); at long distance it grows and confines them. The article presents the running coupling as the reason the same interaction looks like two different forces at two scales, and colour confinement as the reason no isolated quark is observed.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The coupling runs in the opposite direction

The idea. In QCD the coupling decreases with energy, unlike in QED where it increases. Screening by virtual quarks is overcome by antiscreening from gluon self-coupling. This is a mechanism that the corpus's running-coupling article states generically.

What it would add. The sign of the beta function and its origin in the gauge field's own charge, which the corpus's dimension-ceiling article needs in order to say what its SU(3) obstruction is.

Where it lands. articles_physics/quantum-chromodynamics-as-the-residual-of-su4.md, at the running.

What stands in the way. The corpus's algebra is not a gauge theory with a running coupling at all; the sign would have to be imported as a fact about QCD rather than derived.

2. Mass is mostly binding energy

The idea. The proton's mass is not the sum of its quarks' masses; it is dominated by the field energy. Mass is therefore a dynamical quantity, not a count of constituents.

What it would add. A concrete case of the corpus's statement that mass is an angle or an invariant rather than an additive charge.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-polar-form-of-the-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the mass parameter.

What stands in the way. The corpus treats mass as a parameter of a single-particle equation; the binding-energy statement is about a composite and requires a bound-state calculation the corpus does not have.

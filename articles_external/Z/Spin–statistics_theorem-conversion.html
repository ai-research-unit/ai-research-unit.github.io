What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the spin–statistics theorem: the proof that integer-spin particles obey Bose–Einstein statistics and half-integer-spin particles Fermi–Dirac, the symmetry of the many-body wavefunction under exchange that this implies, and the assumptions — relativity and positivity of energy — on which the proof rests.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The statement

"The spin–statistics theorem proves that the observed relationship between the intrinsic spin of a particle (angular momentum not due to the orbital motion) and the quantum particle statistics of collections of such particles is a consequence of the mathematics of quantum mechanics."

The two cases

"the many-body wave function for elementary particles with integer spin (bosons) is symmetric under the exchange of any two particles, whereas for particles with half-integer spin (fermions), the wave function is antisymmetr[ic]." Antisymmetry is what produces the Pauli exclusion principle, so the theorem is the reason matter has structure.

What the proof needs

The theorem is not a consequence of the algebra of rotations alone; it requires relativity and a positive energy spectrum. Without those assumptions the connection between spin and statistics can be broken, which is why the article treats the hypotheses as part of the statement.

"A consequence of the theorem is that non-interacting particles with integer spin obey Bose–Einstein statistics, while those with half-integer spin obey Fermi–Dirac statistics.[1][2][3] Background The statistics of indistinguishable particles is among the most fundamental of physical effects."

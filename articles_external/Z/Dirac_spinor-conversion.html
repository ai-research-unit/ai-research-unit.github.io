What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the Dirac spinor: the four-component object that carries a spin-$\tfrac12$ representation of the Lorentz group, built from a pair of Weyl spinors belonging to the two conjugate representations, and the reason the pairing is what allows mass, charge and angular momentum.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What it is

"a Dirac spinor is a mathematical construction that is used to describe some of the fundamental particles of nature, including quarks and electrons. It is a specific embodiment of a spinor, specifically constructed so that it is consistent with the requirements of special relativity. Dirac spinors transform in a certain" spinorial "fashion under the action of the Lorentz 1 group, which describes the symmetries of Minkowski spacetime." "etime. The construction from two Weyl spinors

"They are constructed out of two simpler component spinors, the Weyl spinors. Each of the two component spinors transform differently under the two distinct complex-conjugate spin-1/2 representations of the Lorentz group. This"

Why the pairing matters

"This pairing is of fundamental importance, as it allows the represented particle to have a mass, carry a charge, and represent the flow of charge as a current, and perhaps most importantly, to carry angular momentum. More precisely, the mass is a Casimir invariant of the Lorentz group."

That last clause is the article's sharpest point: the mass is not a parameter inserted into the equation but a label of the representation, so a single Weyl spinor, which is massless by construction, cannot carry one.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Mass is a Casimir label, so it can be switched off representation-theoretically

The idea. A Weyl spinor is one irreducible representation and has no mass term; a Dirac spinor is the direct sum of two conjugate ones, and the mass is the invariant that mixes them. Mass is therefore a statement about which representation one is in.

What it would add. A representation-theoretic reason for the corpus's mass-angle $\theta$. The corpus writes the mass as an angle in the polar decomposition and shows that it distinguishes the two kinds of mass; the article says why an angle is the right carrier, since the mass term is the invariant that couples the two chiral halves.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-half-bispinor-spaces.md, at the decomposition into Weyl halves.

What stands in the way. The corpus's element is a single object rather than a direct sum of two representations, so the "which representation" question becomes "which component", and the correspondence would have to be spelled out.

2. The two conjugates are not the same, and the difference is charge

The idea. The two Weyl spinors transform under conjugate representations. A field in a conjugate representation carries the opposite charge, so the pairing is what makes a charged massive particle possible.

What it would add. A structural account of why the corpus's charge conjugation is antilinear and why it can swap the halves of an element.

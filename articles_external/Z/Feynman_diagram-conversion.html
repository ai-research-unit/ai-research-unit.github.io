What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on Feynman diagrams: the pictorial representation of the integrals that give probability amplitudes in particle physics, introduced by Feynman in 1948, the reason they are used (large multidimensional integrals become pictures with rules), and their role in organising perturbation theory in quantum electrodynamics and beyond.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What they are

"a Feynman diagram is a pictorial representation of the mathematical expressions describing the behavior and interaction of subatomic particles. The scheme is named after American physicist Richard Feynman, who introduced the diagrams in 1948."

Why they are used

"The calculation of probability amplitudes in theoretical particle physics requires the use of large, complicated integrals over a large number of variables. Feynman diagrams instead represent these integrals graphically. Feynman diagrams give a simple visualization of what would otherwise be an arcane and abstract formula."

Their standing

According to David Kaiser, "nce the middle of the 20th century, theoretical physicists have increasingly turned to this tool to help them undertake critical calculations. Feynman" The article treats the diagrams as a calculational technology whose adoption was a sociological as well as a mathematical event.

"Feynman rules The diagrams are drawn according to the Feynman rules, which depend upon the interaction Lagrangian."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A diagram is a term, not a process

The idea. Each diagram stands for one term of a perturbation series, and the picture is a bookkeeping device for an integral. Reading it as a spacetime process — particles meeting at a point — is a mnemonic that the integral does not contain.

What it would add. A caution for the corpus's scattering articles, which draw diagrams and must not attach a history to them. The corpus's propagator articles give the Green's functions; the article makes explicit that the picture is a summary of an integral.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-feynman-propagator-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the interpretation of the lines.

What stands in the way. Nothing; it is a matter of stating the relation between picture and formula.

2. The rules are the content, and they are not universal

The idea. A diagram has meaning only relative to a set of Feynman rules, which depend on the theory: the vertices carry the interaction and the lines the propagators. Change the theory and the pictures keep their shape but change their value.

What it would add. A reason the corpus's diagrams are drawn after the algebra rather than before it: the rules come from the Lagrangian, and the same drawing means different things for different Lagrangians.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-dirac-equation.md, where the vertices originate.

What stands in the way. The corpus's non-commutative propagators give rules that are not the textbook ones, so its diagrams would need their own key.

This is an encyclopedia article; it describes the diagrams, their purpose and their history, and gives no rules for evaluating them.

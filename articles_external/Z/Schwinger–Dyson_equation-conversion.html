What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the Schwinger–Dyson equations: the exact relations between correlation functions that follow from the invariance of the path integral under a shift of the integration variable, their reading as the Euler–Lagrange equations of the quantum theory, the infinite coupled tower they form, and the reason truncation is needed.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What they are

"The Schwinger–Dyson equations (SDEs) or Dyson–Schwinger equations, named after Julian Schwinger and Freeman Dyson, are general relations between correlation functions in quantum field theories (QFTs)."

Their status

"They are also referred to as the Euler–Lagrange equations of quantum field theories, since they are the equations of motion corresponding to the Green's function. They form a set of infinitely many functional differential equations, all coupled to each other, sometimes referred to as the infinite tower of SDEs."

Where they come from

The derivation is a change of variables: the integral of a total derivative is zero, and that identity for a shifted field gives a relation among Green's functions. The equations are therefore exact and hold order by order; what fails is not the equation but the ability to solve the tower.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A symmetry of the measure gives an exact equation

The idea. No approximation enters the derivation. The equations are consequences of the invariance of the integral, so a theory's classical equation of motion survives at the quantum level as a relation among correlations, corrected by the extra terms the measure produces. This is a mechanism.

What it would add. A way to get exact quantum relations in a formalism that otherwise works order by order, which the corpus's renormalisation article could use in place of the perturbative route.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-renormalization-group-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the exact relations.

What stands in the way. The derivation needs a path integral with a measure; the corpus has a path-integral article but no measure, and without one the shift argument cannot be written.

2. The tower is coupled, so truncation is a physical assumption

The idea. Any finite truncation is an approximation whose justification must come from outside the equations. The article's role in practice is as a starting point for a truncation scheme, not as a solvable system. This is a reading.

What it would add. A caution the corpus's approximation articles could state: a closed set of equations is a modelling choice, not a derivation.

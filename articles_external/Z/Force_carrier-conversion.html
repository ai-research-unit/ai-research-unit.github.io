What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia stub on the force carrier: the particle that mediates a force as the quantum of a field, with the particle and field descriptions presented as complementary, and the alternative names messenger, intermediate and exchange particle given.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The definition

"a force carrier is a type of particle that gives rise to forces between other particles. They serve as the quanta of a particular kind of physical field. Force carriers are also known as messenger particles, intermediate particles, or exchange particles."

The two viewpoints

"Quantum field theories describe nature in terms of fields. Each field has a complementary description as the set of particles of a particular type. A force between two particles can be described either as the action of a force field generated by one particle" other, or as the exchange of quanta of that field.

"Force carrier In quantum field theory, a force carrier is a type of particle that gives rise to forces between other particles."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. "Carrier" is a metaphor with a limit

The idea. The particle does not travel from one charge to another like a messenger; the field configuration acts, and the exchange picture is one term of a perturbation series. The article's own hedging —"serve as the quanta" — concedes this.

What it would add. A caution for the corpus's interaction articles, which speak of exchanged quanta and must keep the static field and the exchanged quantum distinct.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-gauge-carrier-of-the-material-sector-why-the-action-is-one-sided.md, at the propagator.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the correction is a matter of wording.

2. The list of carriers is a table of representations

The idea. Which particle carries which force is fixed by the gauge group: the carrier sits in the adjoint representation, so the number of carriers is the dimension of the group.

What it would add. A structural statement behind the naming of the photon, the $W$ and $Z$, the gluons and the graviton.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-electroweak-gauge-group-and-the-hypercharge-assignment.md.

What stands in the way. Gravity's carrier is not in an adjoint representation of a compact group, so the rule needs that exception.

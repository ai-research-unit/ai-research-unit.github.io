What the Paper Does

Identifies the coherent polarisation matrix of an electromagnetic beam with a biquaternion corresponding to a four-vector of pseudo-Euclidean space, whose components are the intensity and the Stokes parameters. That identification makes the group of transformations isomorphic to SO(3,1), a subset of the Mueller matrices, which form only a semigroup. Restricting the semigroup to the group lets the authors use the vector parametrisation of SO(3,1) to read the polar decomposition of Mueller matrices, with retarders and polarisers corresponding simply to their eigenpolarisations.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The identification

"It was demonstrated that presentation of the coherent matrix (polarization density matrix) of the electromagnetic beams as biquaternion corresponding to the four-vector of the pseudo Euclidean space with intensity and Stokes parameters as components gives the possibility for introducing of the group transformations of such values isomorphic to the S(3.1) group."

The semigroup

"These transformations are the subset of the set of polarization Mueller matrices creating algebraic structure of semigroup."

The reduction and the payoff

"Reduction of the semigroup of Mueller matrices to the group of transformations make it possible to use the vector parameterization of transformations of the group SO(3.1) for interpretation of polar decomposition of the Mueller matrices. In this approach in particular the elements of Mueller matrices corresponding to retarders and polarizers are more simple and natural connected with there eigenpolarizations."

The Stokes–Mueller relation is $S'=MS$; the paper's move is to read $S$ as a biquaternion four-vector and $M$ as an element of a group when the map is invertible.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Depolarising elements make the semigroup, not the group

The idea. Not every Mueller matrix is invertible: a polariser destroys information, so the composition is a semigroup with zero-divisor-like elements and not a group. The invertible part is a group and supports a polar decomposition; the rest does not. This is a reading with a precise algebraic content.

What it would add. A worked physical instance of the corpus's zero-divisor structure: the cone where a biquaternion is non-invertible corresponds here to a depolarising element, which has physical meaning and a name.

2. Stokes parameters are the components of a four-vector

The idea. Intensity and the three Stokes parameters transform as a four-vector under SO(3,1). Polarisation is therefore a spacetime-like object, and its invariants are the pseudo-norm and its sign — unpolarised, partially polarised, fully polarised being timelike, timelike and lightlike cases.

What it would add. A physical dictionary for the corpus's norm classification: the three regions of the light cone become the three degrees of polarisation.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-symplectic-form-and-the-biquaternion-norm-cone.md, at the cone's regions.

What stands in the way. The paper's four-vector is built from Stokes parameters with a specific signature convention; matching it to the corpus's norm requires checking the signs, which the paper fixes but the corpus may fix differently.

What the Paper Does

A short encyclopedia entry on the eikonal approximation: the method that reduces a wave-scattering equation to a differential equation in one variable by assuming a straight-line propagation direction, its origin in geometric optics, and its relation to the one-dimensional WKB method.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What it is for

"the eikonal approximation (Greek εἰκών for likeness, icon or image) is an approximative method useful in wave scattering equations, which occur in optics, seismology, quantum mechanics, quantum electrodynamics, and partial wave expansion."

The reduction

"The main advantage that the eikonal approximation offers is that the equations reduce to a differential equation in a single variable. This reduction into a single variable is the result of the straight line approximation or the eikonal approximation, which allows us to choose the straight line as a special direction."

The relation to WKB

"The early steps involved in the eikonal approximation in quantum mechanics are very closely related to the WKB approximation for one-dimensional waves. The WKB method, like the eikonal approximation, reduces the equations into a dif[ferential equation]" — the two share the ansatz of a rapidly varying phase with a slowly varying amplitude, and differ in whether the direction is fixed in advance.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Fixing a direction is the whole approximation

The idea. The eikonal step is not a power series in $\hbar$ but a choice of a preferred spatial direction along which the phase is differentiated. Once that direction is named, the equation is one-dimensional.

What it would add. A distinct approximation from the semiclassical expansion the corpus uses. The corpus's semiclassical articles expand in $\hbar$ with the full phase; the eikonal fixes a ray first and expands afterwards, which is a stronger assumption and gives a simpler equation.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-semiclassical-expansion-and-the-instanton-gas-in-biquaternionic-form.md, as a coarser sibling.

What stands in the way. The straight-line direction is a geometric notion, and the corpus's fields carry their own frame; the ray would have to be defined as a congruence of the field's four-velocity.

2. The eikonal is exact for the phase and wrong for the amplitude

The idea. The approximation keeps the leading phase and drops derivatives of the amplitude. It therefore gets interference right and diffraction wrong, which is why it fails near caustics.

What it would add. A named failure mode for corpus derivations that keep only the leading phase.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-semiclassical-expansion-and-the-instanton-gas-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the phase.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; it is a caveat.

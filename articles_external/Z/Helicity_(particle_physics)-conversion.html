What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on helicity: its definition as the projection of spin on momentum, the operator form $h=\vec S\cdot\hat p$, the reason the orbital part does not contribute, the invariance of helicity for a massless particle under Lorentz boosts, and the distinction between helicity and chirality.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The definition

"helicity is the projection of the spin onto the direction of momentum. Mathematically, helicity is the sign of the projection of the spin vector onto the momentum vector: 'left' is negative, 'right' is positive. Thus, it can be represented with the operator" $h=\vec S\cdot\hat{\vec p}$, "where" $\hat{\vec p}$ is the momentum operator and $\vec S$"is the spin operator."

Why the orbital part drops out

"The angular momentum

$J$ is the sum of an orbital angular momentum $L$ and a spin $S$. The relationship between orbital angular momentum $L$, the position operator $r$ and the linear momentum (orbit part) $p$" $\vec L=\vec r\times\vec p$, " so $L$'s component in the direction of $p$ is zero. Thus, helicity is just the projection of the spin onto the direction of linear "momentum.

The two properties Helicity is invariant under rotations because both spin and momentum rotate together. Under a boost it is invariant only for a massless particle, since a massive particle can be overtaken, which reverses the momentum without reversing the spin. Chirality is a different quantity, defined by the $\gamma_5$ eigenvalue and boost-invariant for all masses.

"Helicity (particle physics) In physics, helicity is the projection of the spin onto the direction of momentum."

"Helicity, however, is not Lorentz invariant; under the action of a Lorentz boost, the helicity may change sign."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The orbital term vanishes identically, not approximately

The idea. $\vec L\cdot\vec p=(\vec r\times\vec p)\cdot\vec p=0$ exactly, because it is a scalar triple product with a repeated vector. So helicity needs no projection operator on the orbital part. This is a formula.

What it would add. A derivation step the corpus's helicity article may compress: the projection is onto spin alone for a structural reason, not because the orbital part is small.

Where it lands. articles_physics/helicity-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the definition.

What stands in the way. The corpus's $L$ is an algebraic product rather than a cross product in a vector space, so the vanishing has to be checked in the algebra's own multiplication, where zero divisors make "vanishes" a subtler statement.

2. Helicity is frame-dependent for a massive particle

The idea. A boost can overtake a massive particle and flip the sign of its momentum without touching its spin, so helicity is not Lorentz invariant for $m\neq0$. Only chirality is.

What it would add. The distinction that makes the massless case special, which the corpus uses when it separates the two chiral halves.

Where it lands. articles_physics/helicity-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the massless limit.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural.

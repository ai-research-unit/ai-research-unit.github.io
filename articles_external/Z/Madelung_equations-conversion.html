What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on Madelung's hydrodynamical rewriting of the Schrödinger equation for a spinless non-relativistic particle: the wavefunction written as amplitude times phase, the resulting continuity equation and quantum Hamilton–Jacobi equation, the quantum potential that separates the flow from a classical one, and the warning that the hydrodynamical variables do not exhaust the wavefunction.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What it is

"the Madelung equations, or the equations of quantum hydrodynamics, are Erwin Madelung's alternative formulation of the Schrödinger equation for a spinless non-relativistic particle, written in terms of hydrodynamical variables, similar to the Navier–Stokes equations of fluid dynamics."

The relation to de Broglie–Bohm

"The derivation of the Madelung equations is similar to the de Broglie–Bohm formulation, which represents the Schrödinger equation as a quantum Hamilton–Jacobi equation. In both cases the hydrodynamic interpretations are not equivalent to Schrödinger's" equation: the fluid picture loses the phase's multivaluedness, so a single-valued velocity field describes only part of the state space.

The two equations

With $\psi=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{iS/\hbar}$ one obtains a continuity equation for $\rho$ with velocity $\nabla S/m$, and a Hamilton–Jacobi equation for $S$ carrying an extra term built from the second derivative of $\sqrt{\rho}$ — the quantum potential.

"Recently, the extension to the relativistic case with spin was done by having the Dirac equation written with hydrodynamic variables."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The quantum potential is a curvature of the amplitude, not a force

The idea. The extra term comes from the amplitude's second derivative and vanishes where the amplitude is constant. It is not an interaction with anything. This is a reading.

What it would add. A test for the corpus's spin-fluid potential terms: each should be traceable to derivatives of the density, whereas the corpus sometimes presents them as forces.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-relativistic-quantum-space-as-a-fluid.md, at the potential.

What stands in the way. The corpus's relativistic case has a spin density as well as a number density, so the potential splits into a density part and a spin part; the article's single term is the density part only.

2. The hydrodynamic form is not a full description

The idea. The velocity is the gradient of a phase, so the flow is irrotational unless the phase is singular. Vorticity in a Madelung fluid comes only from nodes of the wavefunction, where the phase winds. This is a mechanism.

What it would add. A precise statement of what a fluid model can carry: circulation needs the spin or a singular phase, which is why a spin fluid is not optional for the Dirac case.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-relativistic-quantum-space-as-a-fluid.md, on vorticity.

What stands in the way. The corpus's fluids are relativistic and their vorticity is carried by the spin tensor, so the non-relativistic argument needs restating.

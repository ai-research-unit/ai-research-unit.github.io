What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the electroweak interaction: the unified description of electromagnetism and the weak force, the unification energy of about 246 GeV and the corresponding temperature of roughly $10^{15}$ K, the split during the quark epoch, and the symmetry-breaking that gives the two forces their different ranges.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The unification

"the electroweak interaction or electroweak force is the unified description of two of the fundamental interactions of nature: electromagnetism (electromagnetic interaction), and the weak interaction. Although these two forces appear very different at everyday low energies, the theory models them as two different aspects of the same force."

The scale

"ve the unification energy, on the order of 246 GeV,[a] they would merge into a single force. Thus, if the temperature is high enough – approximately 1015 K – then the electromagnetic force and weak force merge into a combined electroweak force."

The history of the split

"During the quark epoch (shortly after the Big Bang), the electroweak force split into the electromagnetic and weak force. It is thought that the required temperature of 1015 K has not been seen widely throughout the universe since before the quark epoch"

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. 246

GeV is a vacuum expectation value, not a coupling

The idea. The number quoted as the unification energy is the Higgs vacuum expectation value, which sets the masses of the $W$ and $Z$ and therefore the range of the weak force. The couplings themselves meet at a higher scale in the full theory.

What it would add. A correction the corpus's electroweak articles already make and which this entry blurs: the 246 GeV scale is where the symmetry is broken, not where the couplings unify.

Where it lands. articles_physics/electroweak-symmetry-breaking-the-doublet-the-potential-and-the-gauge-boson-masses.md, at the value of $v$.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the corpus is already correct and the entry is loose.

2. A temperature stands in for an energy

The idea. The article converts a unification energy into a temperature, $10^{15}$ K, and then speaks of the early universe in thermal language. The conversion is $kT\sim E$, exact only for a thermal ensemble in equilibrium.

What it would add. A caution for the corpus's cosmology, where a scale is sometimes carried into a thermal statement without the qualification.

Where it lands. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md.

What stands in the way. The corpus's cosmology has no thermal ensemble, so the caveat is a note on usage rather than a result.

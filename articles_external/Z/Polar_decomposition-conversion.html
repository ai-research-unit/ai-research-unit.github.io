What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the polar decomposition of a square matrix: the factorisation $A=UP$ with $U$ unitary and $P$ positive semi-definite Hermitian, the real case with orthogonal $U$ and symmetric $P$, the geometric reading as a stretch followed by a rotation, and the attribution to Autonne in 1902.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The factorisation

"the polar decomposition of a square real or complex matrix $A$ is a factorization of the form $A=UP$, where $U$ is a unitary matrix, and $P$ is a positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix ($U$ is an orthogonal matrix, and $P$ is a positive semi-definite symmetric matrix in the real case), both square and of the same size. It was first introduced by French engineer Léon Autonne in 1902."

The geometric reading

"n 1902.[2] If a real 𝑛 × 𝑛 matrix 𝐴 is interpreted as a linear transformation of 𝑛-dimensional space ℝ𝑛 , the polar decomposition separates it into a rotation" rotation and a stretch along orthogonal axes. The order matters: the stretch is applied first and the rotation second, or the reverse depending on the convention chosen.

How it is computed

$U=A(A^*A)^{-1/2}$ and $P=(A^*A)^{1/2}$, so the decomposition is unique whenever $A$ is invertible and $P$ is the unique positive square root of $A^*A$.

"This is known as the left polar decomposition, whereas the previous decomposition is known as the right polar decomposition."

"Evidently polar decomposition in this case involves an element from that group."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The decomposition exists because $A^*A$ is positive

The idea. The positive factor $P$ is defined as the square root of $A^*A$, which is Hermitian and non-negative, so the root exists and is unique. Where $A$ is not invertible the factors exist but $U$ is not unique on the kernel. This is a formula.

What it would add. A precise statement of the domain of validity, which matters because the corpus applies a polar decomposition to an algebra element that can be a zero divisor: there the decomposition exists only away from the divisor cone.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-domain-of-the-polar-decomposition-invertible-elements-and-the-zero-divisor-cone.md, at the hypothesis.

What stands in the way. The article's theorem is for matrices over $\mathbb{C}$ or $\mathbb{R}$; the corpus's algebra is not a matrix algebra over a field, so positivity and the square root need an independent construction.

2. Two factors, two meanings, and the order is a convention

The idea. $U$ carries the rotation and $P$ the stretch, and which is applied first is a choice that changes the formula but not the content. Reading $U$ as the phase and $P$ as the amplitude is the same choice in another language.

What it would add. A reminder that the corpus's assignment of "phase to one factor and density" to the other follows the same convention and is not forced by the theorem.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-polar-form-of-the-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the definitions.

What stands in the way. Nothing; it is a matter of stating the convention.

What the Paper Does

The 1948 paper that formulates non-relativistic quantum mechanics as a sum over paths. The formulation is presented as a reformulation, not a new theory: the amplitudes are the same, the rule for combining them is different. The probability of an event is the squared modulus of a sum of complex contributions, one per alternative way, and the probability that a particle follows a path within a region is the squared modulus of a sum over the paths in that region.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim of equivalence

"Non-relativistic quantum mechanics is formulated here in a different way. It is, however, mathematically equivalent to the familiar formulation."

The rule

"In quantum mechanics the probability of an event which can happen in several dierent ways is the absolute square of a sum of complex contributions, one from each alternative way. The probability that a particle will be found to have a path x(t) lying somewhere within a region of space time is the square of a sum of contributions, one from each path in the region."

The third principle

The paper's contribution is the addition of a third rule to the two familiar ones — amplitudes add, probabilities are squared moduli: the amplitude for a path is $\exp(iS/\hbar)$ with $S$ the classical action along that path. Every later statement in the paper is a consequence of that choice, including the recovery of the Schrödinger equation.

"The contribution from a single path is postulated to be an exponential whose (imaginary) phase is the classical action (in units of ℏ) for the path in question."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A reformulation can be exact and still change what is easy

The idea. The path integral is mathematically equivalent to the operator formulation and makes different things computable: the classical limit is a stationary-phase argument, and the coupling to an external field is a modification of the action along each path.

What it would add. A reason the corpus carries both formulations. Its path-integral articles and its operator articles compute the same objects; the paper shows the equivalence is exact while the calculational reach differs, which justifies keeping both rather than choosing.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-path-integral-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the equivalence.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; the corpus already has both and the paper supplies the historical statement of their relation.

2. The action appears as a phase, not as an extremum

The idea. In the sum over paths every path contributes, with weight $e^{iS/\hbar}$; the classical path is the one where the phase is stationary, so nearby paths add in phase. The classical principle is a consequence, not an axiom.

What it would add. The reverse derivation the corpus's action articles lack: the corpus begins with the Lagrangian and derives the equations, and this paper derives the dominance of the classical path.

Where it lands. articles_physics/noethers-theorem-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the relation between action and equations of motion.

What stands in the way. The stationary-phase argument presupposes that the phase varies rapidly compared with the amplitude, which is an assumption the corpus would have to state for its own fields.

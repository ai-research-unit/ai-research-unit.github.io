What the Paper Does

Resolves an apparent paradox: light must have zero rest mass to travel at $c$, yet it carries momentum and energy, so it has inertial mass, and by equivalence gravitational mass. The authors derive no new result; they give a perspective in which the two statements are one.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The paradox

"electromagnetic radiation must have zero rest mass in order to propagate at the speed of light, but on the other hand, since it definitely carries momentum and energy, it has non-zero inertial mass. Hence, by the principle of equivalence, it must have non-zero gravitational mass, and so, light must be heavy."

The disclaimer

"In this paper, no new results will be derived, but a possibly surprising perspective on the above paradox is given." That sentence sets the genre: an essay, not a derivation, and it should be read as such.

The two experimental facts

"Einstein's general theory of relativity is based on two experimental facts. First, that the speed of light appears to be equal for all observers, independent of their velocity with respect to the light source, and second, the apparent equality of gravitational mass $m_g$ and inertial mass $m_i$." The essay works entirely inside those two facts, which is why it can say something about light's weight without a quantum theory of gravity.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Rest mass and inertial mass are different quantities, and only one vanishes

The idea. A photon has zero rest mass and non-zero inertial mass $E/c^2$. The two are related by $m_{\text{rest}}^2c^4=E^2-p^2c^2$, which for light gives $E=pc$ and $m_{\text{rest}}=0$ while $E/c^2\neq0$. This is a reading.

What it would add. A resolution of a confusion the corpus's relativistic articles can inherit: the corpus's mass angle measures rest mass, so it must not be read as how much mass the particle has.

2. An equivalence-principle argument extends to any energy

The idea. If inertial and gravitational mass are equal, then any localised energy gravitates, including the energy of a field with no rest mass. The extension is immediate and requires no field theory.

What it would add. A short argument the corpus's gravity articles could use, since they treat mass as a source term.

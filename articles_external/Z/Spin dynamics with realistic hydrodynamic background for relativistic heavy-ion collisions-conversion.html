What the Paper Does

Solves perfect spin hydrodynamics for the first time on a realistic (3+1)-dimensional background. The background is tuned to a wide set of hadronic observables in Au+Au at $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=200$ GeV, and the spin part is evolved by conserving the spin tensor, with the mass in that tensor demoted to an adjustable parameter. The outcome is a single sharp statement about time: the data are reproduced only if the spin evolution starts late, at about $4$ fm, while the background starts at $1$ fm. The gap is read as the interval during which spin and orbital angular momentum still exchange.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setup

"The equations of perfect spin hydrodynamics are solved for the first time using a realistic (3+1)-dimensional hydrodynamic background, calibrated to reproduce a comprehensive set of hadronic observables, including rapidity distributions, transverse momentum spectra, and elliptic flow coefficients for Au+Au collisions at the beam energy of $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=200$ GeV."

The spin sector: "The spin dynamics is governed by the conservation of the spin tensor, describing spin-1/2 particles, with particle mass in the spin tensor treated as an effective parameter."

What was varied

"We have analyzed several scenarios by varying the effective mass, the initial evolution time, and the form of the initial spin polarization tensor. Our model predictions have been compared with the data on global and longitudinal spin polarization of $\Lambda$ hyperons."

The result

"The results indicate that a successful description of the data requires a delayed initial evolution time for the perfect spin hydrodynamics of about 4 fm (compared to the standard initial time of 1 fm used for the hydrodynamic background)."

The reading: "This delay signals a transition from the phase where the spin-orbit interaction is significant to the regime where spin-conserving processes dominate. Our results suggest that the spin-orbit dissipative interaction plays a significant role only in the very early stages of the system evolution."

The interpretation

"Since our framework is based on the equation that describes conservation of the spin part of the total angular momentum, its ability to reproduce the experimental data suggests that the last stages of the evolution of matter are dominated by the processes that do not exchange the spin and orbital parts of the angular momentum."

The authors are careful about what would break this: the method for tracking the early stage "can be questioned, and the currently developed methods of spin hydrodynamics might be not applicable."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The spin sector starts later than the fluid

The idea. A result with a number: perfect spin hydrodynamics describes the polarization data only if it begins around $4$ fm, not at the fluid's $1$ fm. The lag is the imprint of the epoch in which spin and orbit still exchange.

What it would add. The corpus's spin hydrodynamics article constructs the two-current fluid and its constitutive relations but treats the initial time as a free parameter with no preferred value. A measured lag converts that parameter into an observable, and it does so with the corpus's own machinery: conservation of the spin tensor is exactly the assumption under which the late start works.

What stands in the way. The number is model-dependent — it follows from a particular background, a particular initial polarization profile and an effective mass — so the corpus can quote it only with those qualifiers attached.

2. The mass in the spin tensor is a knob, not the particle mass

The idea. A modelling choice: the $m$ that appears in the spin tensor need not be the hyperon mass; treating it as an effective parameter is what allows the framework to fit the data at all.

What it would add. The corpus's spin hydrodynamics is written for a fluid of particles with a definite mass, and the mass enters the constitutive relations as a fixed constant. The idea that this mass is a phenomenological parameter — standing in for the unresolved physics of how spin is carried — is a licence the corpus does not currently grant itself, and it is the difference between a theory that predicts and a model that fits.

What stands in the way. Loosening a parameter's meaning costs predictive power, and the corpus's algebraic derivations would have to say which of their $m$'s is which; without that, the effective-mass idea is an escape hatch rather than a result.

3. Late-time dominance of spin-conserving processes

The idea. A claim about phases: the fireball passes from a stage where spin-orbit coupling matters to a stage where only spin-conserving processes remain, and the second stage is long enough to be described by spin hydrodynamics alone.

What it would add. The corpus's fluid article describes one fluid throughout. A two-stage picture with a sharp crossover would let the corpus state where its equations are supposed to hold, which is a scope statement the article currently leaves open. It also gives the Frenkel condition article a physical setting: the condition is what a spin-conserving stage imposes.

What stands in the way. The transition time is inferred, not observed; the paper's own caveat that the early-stage methods "might be not applicable" is a warning that the boundary is soft.

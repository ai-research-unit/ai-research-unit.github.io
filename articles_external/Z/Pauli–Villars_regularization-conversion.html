What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on Pauli–Villars regularization: the isolation of divergent from finite parts in loop integrals by adding auxiliary particles whose contribution cancels the divergence, the 1949 attribution to Pauli and Villars, and the recovery of the original theory when the regulator masses go to infinity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The purpose

"Pauli–Villars regularization (P–V) is a procedure that isolates divergent terms from finite parts in loop calculations in field theory in order to renormalize the theory. Wolfgang Pauli and Felix Villars published the method in 1949, based on earlier work by Richard Feynman, Ernst Stueckelberg and Dominique Rivier.[1]"

The mechanism

"a divergence arising from a loop integral (such as vacuum polarization or electron self-energy) is modulated by a spectrum of auxiliary particles added to the Lagrangian or propagator. When the masses of the fictitious particles are taken as an infinite limit (i.e., once the regulator is removed) one" recovers the unregularized theory with a finite renormalized result.

The price

The auxiliary fields must have the wrong sign for a physical field: a commuting field with anticommuting statistics, or a negative-norm state. They are ghosts, present only to cancel the divergence and gone in the limit.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A regulator is a subtraction, and the physical theory is the limit

The idea. The auxiliary particle contributes a term that cancels the divergence, and its mass is sent to infinity at the end. Nothing survives of it. This is a mechanism that makes renormalisation is a subtraction concrete.

What it would add. A worked shape for the corpus's renormalisation-group article, which states the running but not the subtraction.

2. The regulator has the wrong statistics on purpose

The idea. The cancellation requires the auxiliary field to contribute with the opposite sign, so it cannot be a physical particle. Any attempt to give the regulator a physical meaning fails by construction. This is a reading.

What it would add. A caution against reading a regulator as a heavy physical field, which is easy to do when the same symbol appears in a Lagrangian.

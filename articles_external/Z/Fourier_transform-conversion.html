What the Paper Does

The encyclopedia article on the Fourier transform: the integral transform mapping a function to a function of frequency, the conventions and their normalisation freedom, the inversion formula, the properties (linearity, shifting, convolution), and the extension to distributions and several variables.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What it is

"the Fourier transform (FT) is an integral transform that takes a function as input and outputs another function that describes the extent to which various frequencies are present in the original function. The output of the transform is a complex valued function of frequency. The term Fourier transform refers to both the mathematical operation and to this complex-valued function. When a distinction needs to be made, the output of the operation is sometimes called the frequency domain representation of the original function."

The kernel

With the $2\pi$ in the exponent, $\hat f(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\xi}dx$, and the inverse carries the conjugate kernel with no normalising constant. The article is careful that other conventions put $2\pi$ in the exponent's denominator or a prefactor in front, and that the choice changes the constant in every formula that follows.

"−∞ Older literature refers to the two transform functions, the Fourier cosine transform, a, and the Fourier sine transform, b."

"Fourier transform for periodic functions The Fourier transform of a periodic function cannot be defined using the integral formula directly."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The convention is a choice, and the corpus must fix one

The idea. Whether the $2\pi$ sits in the exponent or in the prefactor changes the convolution theorem's constant and the delta function's normalisation. The transform is unique only once the convention is fixed.

What it would add. A stated convention for the corpus's Fourier articles, which use the transform in several forms. Mixing them is the commonest source of a stray $2\pi$.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-fourier-transform-and-the-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the definition.

What stands in the way. Nothing; it is a matter of declaring the choice once.

2. The transform is a change of basis, so it preserves the norm

The idea. Plancherel's theorem says the transform is unitary up to normalisation: the total probability is the same in position and in momentum, which is why the two pictures are equivalent.

What it would add. A reason the corpus may compute in either space, which its articles do without comment.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-path-integral-and-the-schrodinger-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The corpus's transform acts on a non-commutative algebra, and unitarity is a statement about a Hilbert-space inner product the algebra does not automatically carry.

This is an encyclopedia article; the definition, the conventions, the inversion and the standard properties are its content.

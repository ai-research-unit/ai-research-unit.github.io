What the Paper Does

Derives two disjoint universal factorisations of the Clifford algebra Cl3 of biquaternions, which generate particle and antiparticle solutions of the Dirac equation, and shows that they parametrise every non-invertible biquaternion, exactly where the Yvon–Takabayasi approach fails. In that case the Takabayasi tetrade collapses and the method's conclusions are wrong.

The algebra

"is the even graded subalgebra of Cl1,3 , the Clifford algebra of the lorentzian time-space manifold R ⊗ R3 = R1,3 ." The basis vectors $e_k=\gamma_k\gamma_0$ satisfy $e_k^2=1$ and anticommute.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The zero-divisor cone is where the standard parametrisation dies

The idea. The Takabayasi tetrade $f_\mu=\psi\gamma_\mu\tilde\psi$ collapses exactly on $\psi\tilde\psi=0$: all four vectors become lightlike and mutually orthogonal, and the pseudoscalar vanishes. A parametrisation that assumes a non-degenerate tetrade is invalid there, not merely inaccurate. This is a formula with a sharp domain statement.

What it would add. The missing link between the corpus's zero-divisor cone and the failure of the hydrodynamic parametrisation. The corpus has an article on the domain of the polar decomposition and an article on the Takabayasi correspondence, and this paper says precisely how the second fails on the boundary the first describes.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural: the corpus has both objects and needs the statement that connects them. The proof, however, is in Krüger's algebra, and the corpus would want it in its own multiplication.

2. The massless limit is a degenerate limit of the parametrisation

The idea. Solutions of infinite momentum, equivalently vanishing rest mass, have non-invertible biquaternions. The massless case is therefore not reached by continuity from the massive one within the old parametrisation; it needs the new factorisation. This is a mechanism for handling the chiral limit.

What it would add. A reason the corpus's massless limit needs separate treatment, which its helicity and chiral articles assume without saying why the limit is singular.

What stands in the way. The claim is made for Krüger's factorisation; the corpus uses a polar form that may behave differently at $m=0$, so the singular character must be checked in its own coordinates.

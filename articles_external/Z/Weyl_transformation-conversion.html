What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the Weyl transformation: the local rescaling of the metric that preserves the conformal class, the theories and expressions that are invariant under it, and the anomalies that spoil the invariance in quantum theory.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The definition

"the Weyl transformation, named after German mathematician Hermann Weyl, is a local rescaling of the metric tensor" $g_{\mu\nu}(x)\to e^{2\Omega(x)}g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, "which produces another metric in the same conformal class. A theory or an expression invariant under this transformation is called conformally invariant, or is said to possess Weyl invariance or Weyl symmetry."

What is invariant

The light cone is preserved by the rescaling, so causality is untouched. In $d$ dimensions the metric determinant brings a factor $e^{2d\Omega}$, which is why the Ricci scalar transforms with a term containing $\Box\Omega$, and why the Einstein–Hilbert action is not invariant for $d\neq2$.

The anomaly

"The Weyl symmetry is an import[ant]" ingredient of string theory and of theories of gravity, and it is anomalous in most quantum field theories: the classical invariance does not survive regularisation, and the trace of the stress tensor acquires a non-zero value. That trace anomaly is a quantum effect with measurable consequences.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Rescaling the metric is not a coordinate change

The idea. A Weyl transformation changes lengths at each point while keeping angles and the light cone. It is a change of the metric, not of the chart, so it is a genuine degree of freedom and not a relabelling. This is a reading that separates two transformations often grouped as "conformal".

What it would add. A precise statement the corpus's gravity articles need, since they treat conformal structure and coordinate freedom together.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-einstein-field-equations-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-linearised-dictionary-and-the-non-linear-gap.md, on the conformal part.

What stands in the way. The corpus's metric is built from the algebra's frame; a local rescaling corresponds to a position-dependent normalisation of the frame, which is available, but the transformation law of the algebra's own derivative has to be computed.

2. A classical symmetry can fail quantum mechanically

The idea. The invariance holds for the action and fails for the measure. An anomaly is not a mistake; it is the statement that no regularisation preserves the symmetry.

What it would add. A model of how a symmetry of the classical algebra can fail after quantisation, which is the pattern the corpus's own quantisation articles should be alert to.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-semiclassical-expansion-and-the-instanton-gas-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the measure.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no functional measure, so the anomaly can only be cited, not derived.

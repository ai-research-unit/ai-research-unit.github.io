What the Paper Does

An encyclopedia article on the eikonal equation as a non-linear first-order partial differential equation of wave propagation: its classical form in geometric optics with the refractive index on the right, its general form with a function of several variables, and its role as the link between wave optics and ray optics and as the equation produced by the WKB method.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A first-order non-linear equation replaces a second-order linear one

The idea. The eikonal equation is what remains of the wave equation when the wavelength goes to zero: a Hamilton–Jacobi equation for the phase, with the refractive index as the Hamiltonian. Its characteristics are the rays.

What it would add. A bridge between the corpus's wave equations and its classical trajectories, stated as a limit rather than an analogy. The corpus has both ends and no explicit passage through $|\nabla u|=n$.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-classical-free-particle-and-inertial-frames-in-biquaternionic-form.md, as the short-wavelength limit of the wave description.

What stands in the way. The corpus's waves are algebra-valued and its index of refraction is a property of a medium it does not model; the scalar eikonal equation would have to be generalised to a matrix-valued one.

2. Characteristics are rays, and caustics are where they cross

The idea. Solving the eikonal equation by characteristics gives the ray picture, and the places where characteristics cross are the caustics where the approximation fails.

What it would add. A criterion for the validity of a ray picture, which the corpus uses implicitly whenever it speaks of trajectories.

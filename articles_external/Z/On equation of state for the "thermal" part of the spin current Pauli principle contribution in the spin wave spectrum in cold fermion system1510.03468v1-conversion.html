What the Paper Does Derives the equation of state for the thermal part of the spin current in spin-1/2 quantum plasmas — a term previously dropped for lack of such an equation — and applies it to the spin-wave spectrum of electron-ion plasmas with degenerate electrons and of degenerate electron-positron plasmas, giving also a nonlinear Pauli equation with a spinor pressure term.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The gap

"The spin density evolution equation found by means of the many-particle quantum hydrodynamics shows existence of the ''thermal'' part of the spin current, which is an analog of the thermal pressure, or the Fermi pressure for degenerate electron gas, existing in the Euler equation. However, this term has been dropped, since there has not been found any equation of state for the thermal part of the spin current (TPSC), like we have for the pressure."

The result

"In this paper we derive the equation of state for the TPSC and apply it for study of spectrum of collective excitations in spin-1/2 quantum plasmas."

The systems

"We consider two kinds of plasmas: electron-ion plasma with motionless ions and degenerate electrons, and degenerate electron-positron plasmas. We also present the non-linear Pauli equation with the spinor pressure term containing described effects." The term is a spin analogue of the Fermi pressure: it comes from the Pauli principle acting on the spin distribution, so it is present even at zero temperature in a degenerate system.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The spin current has a pressure, and it needs an equation of state

The idea. A hydrodynamic spin current is not fixed by the continuity equation alone; it requires a closure, and the closure is an equation of state for the thermal part. Without it the spin sector is underdetermined. This is a mechanism.

What it would add. The missing closure for the corpus's spin hydrodynamics, which writes the spin evolution equation and does not state what closes the hierarchy.

Where it lands. articles_physics/spin-hydrodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, at the closure.

What stands in the way. The derivation is from many-particle quantum hydrodynamics with a Fermi distribution; the corpus's spin density is a bilinear of one field with no distribution behind it, so there is no ensemble to integrate over.

2. The Pauli principle contributes at zero temperature

The idea. A degenerate gas has a spin pressure even when the thermal temperature vanishes, because the exclusion principle forces occupation of higher states. The "thermal" in the name refers to the statistical spread, not to a temperature.

What it would add. A warning about the word "thermal" in the corpus's dissipative and hydrodynamic articles, where it may suggest a temperature that is not there.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-and-the-takabayasi-correspondence.md, on the pressure term.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; it is a matter of naming.

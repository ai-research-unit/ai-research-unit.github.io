What the Paper Does

This is a review chapter, drafted as the basis for a chapter in a Springer volume. It sets out the standard energy conditions of general relativity, including the Hawking–Ellis classification of stress-energy tensors into four types, and then presents the authors' programme of semi-classical energy conditions that are nonlinear in the stress-energy tensor. It argues that nonlinear conditions behave better under quantum effects than the linear ones, at the price of being harder to apply. Its conclusion is that the status of energy conditions as fundamental physics remains provisional, and that the set of conditions is at best partially ordered.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The subject

"The standard energy conditions of classical general relativity are linear in the stress-energy tensor, and have clear physical interpretation[s in terms of] geodesic focussing, but suffer the significant drawback that they are often [violated] by semi-classical quantum effects."

The alternative

"In contrast, it is possible to develop energy conditions that are intrinsically non-linear in the stress-energy tensor [and that] exhibit much better well-controlled behaviour when semi-classical [effects are] introduced, at the cost of a less direct applicabil[ity]."

The structure of the chapter

"In this chapter we will first review the standard energy co[nditions] (including the connection to the Hawking–Ellis type [I–IV classification of] stress-energy tensors). We shall then turn to the a[nalysis of nonlinear] energy conditions, and see how much can be done once [quantum effects] are included."

No weakest condition

"There is no strict ordering on the set of energy conditions, at best a partial ordering, so there is no strictly 'weakest' energy condition. Nonetheless, perhaps the weakest of the usual energy conditions (in terms of the constraint imposed on the stress energy) is the ANEC, which makes it the strongest energy condition in terms of proving theorems."

The provisional status

"Variations on the original classical point-wise energy conditions are still under development and investigation, and the status of the energy conditions as fundamental physics should still be considered provisional."

The remaining gap

"Attempts at developing a general proof of the ANEC are ongoing — part of the question is exactly what one might mean by 'proving an energy condition' from more fundamental principles. It should be noted, however, that even the ANEC can be violated by conformal anomalies, so to have any hope of a truly general derivation of the ANEC from more funda[mental principles] ..."

1. A partial order of conditions

The idea. Energy conditions are not totally ordered. A condition can be weak as a constraint and strong as a hypothesis for theorems, and the two roles are not the same axis.

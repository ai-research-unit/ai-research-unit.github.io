What the Paper Does

Tolman showed in 1930 that a fluid in equilibrium in a gravitational field must have a temperature gradient. This essay explains why that does not break thermodynamics. The argument is kinematic, using gravitational redshift and a relativistic version of Maxwell's two-column argument. The key point is that gravity is universal: it acts on every source of energy alike, so no heat engine can be built between two heights. For any non-universal force, the same construction would give a perpetual heat machine.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The aim

"We provide a simple and clear verification of the physical need for temperature gradients in equilibrium states when gravitational fields are present."

The method

"Our argument will be built in a completely kinematic manner, in terms of the gravitational red-shift/blue-shift of light, together with a relativistic extension of Maxwell's two column argument."

The conclusion

"We conclude by showing that it is the universality of the gravitational interaction (the uniqueness of free-fall) that ultimately permits Tolman's equilibrium temperature gradients without any violation of the laws of thermodynamics."

The question

"The question we will like to ask and answer in here is whether all the consequences of this universal character of gravity have already been fully explored. We believe that they have not."

The contrast with other forces

"Given that temperature gradients created by any force that is not universal (e.g. dependent on charge, mass, spin,...) allow the creation of heat machines that violate the second law, these temperature gradients must not exist."

The final statement

"up to date no force other than gravity seems to act on all sources of mass or energy in the same way regardless, so we can even state this as: Gravity is the only force capable of creating temperature gradients in equilibrium states without violating any law of thermodynamics."

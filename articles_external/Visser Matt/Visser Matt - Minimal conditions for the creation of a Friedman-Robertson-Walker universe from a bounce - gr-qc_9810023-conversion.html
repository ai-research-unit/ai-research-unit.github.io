What the Paper Does

The authors ask what a bounce requires, without choosing an equation of state. They use the energy conditions, as was done for traversable wormholes. They find that some open interval around the bounce must violate the strong energy condition. Flatter bounces need more: a hyperbolic or flat bounce violates the null energy condition as well. A closed and sufficiently gentle bounce violates only the strong condition. Two explicit closed models satisfying all pointwise conditions except the strong one are given.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"In this Letter we investigate the minimal conditions under which the creation of our universe might arise due to a 'bounce' from a previous collapse, rather than an explosion from a big-bang singularity."

The analogy

"We subject the bounce to a general model-independent analysis along the lines of that applied to the Morris–Thorne traversable wormholes"

The general result

"we show that there is always an open temporal region surrounding the bounce over which the strong energy condition (SEC) must be violated."

What else can hold

"On the other hand, all the other energy conditions can easily be satisfied. In particular, we exhibit an inflation-inspired model in which a big bounce is 'natural'."

The sharper statement

$$\exists\ \text{Bounce}\ + [k\neq +1]\ \Rightarrow\ \text{NEC violated},\qquad \exists\ \text{Bounce}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{SEC violated} \qquad (paper\ 20,\ 21)$$

The gentlest case

"the energy condition violations are minimized by taking the universe to be hyperspherical ($k=+1$) and by making the bounce sufficiently gentle: $\ddot a_*\le a_*^{-1}$."

The example

"$ds^2=-dt^2+\left(a_*^2+\beta^2t^2\right)\left[\frac{dr^2}{1-r^2}+r^2\left(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\right)\right]$ ... provided we take $\beta<1$."

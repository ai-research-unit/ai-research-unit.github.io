What the Paper Does

The equatorial slice of Kerr has the same geometry as the acoustic metric of a rotating fluid vortex. The author derives the most general acoustic geometry of a line vortex. Then he finds coordinates that map Kerr onto it. The result is a laboratory analogue of a rotating black hole.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The identification

"the [equatorial slice of the Kerr black] hole is formally equivalent to the geometry felt by phonons entrained in a rotating fluid vortex"

The first task

"we find the most general acoustic geometry compatible with the fluid dynamic equations in a collapsing/expanding perfect-fluid line vortex"

The second task

"we demonstrate that there is a suitable choice of coordinates on the equatorial slice through a Kerr black hole that puts it into this [form]"

The programme

"we analyse this example of 'analogue gravity' in some detail"

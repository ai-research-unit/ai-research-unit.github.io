What the Paper Does

An experiment reported radiation from a rapidly changing refractive index in glass. The authors ask whether this is Hawking radiation or another form of vacuum radiation. Simple estimates of the dynamical Casimir effect look too small to explain it. They argue the estimates miss two effects: pulse steepening and pulse cresting. With plausible bounds on these, the predicted amount comes close to what was seen. They conclude the effect deserves more study.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"Is this really Hawking radiation, or some other form of quantum vacuum radiation?"

The problem

"Naive estimates of the amount of quantum vacuum radiation generated due to the rapidly changing refractive index — sometimes called the dynamical Casimir effect — are not encouraging."

The doubt

"However we feel that naive estimates could be misleading depending on the quantitative magnitude of two specific physical effects: 'pulse steepening' and 'pulse cresting'."

The result

"Plausible bounds on the maximum size of these two effects results in estimates much closer to the experimental observations"

The conclusion

"we argue that the dynamical Casimir effect is now worth additional investigation"

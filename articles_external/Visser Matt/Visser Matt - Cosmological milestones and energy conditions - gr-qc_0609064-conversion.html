What the Paper Does

This talk classifies the singularities and other special points, called milestones, that appear in cosmological models. The classification is kinematical: it uses a generalised power-series expansion of the scale factor rather than any field equation. With that definition the authors sort the milestones into singular and non-singular, and find that the non-singular ones are a short list. They then check whether the classical energy conditions hold near each milestone, so the catalogue can serve as a guide to which scenarios are physically plausible.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The situation

"Until recently, the physically rel[evant singularities in cosmology] had traditionally been thought to be limited [in kind. However, over the last few years, the zoo of [exotic singularities in the] literature has become considerably mo[re extensive, with] ... [sudden singularities, big rip events, and others] added to the mix, as well as renewed interest [in] bounces and turnarounds. In this talk, we pr[esent a classification of these] milestones, both at the kinematical a[nd at the dynamical level]."

The kinematical definition

"series, purely kinematical definition [allows us to classify] the behaviour of the scale factor $a(t)$ ... and its relation to curvature singulari[ties]".

The dynamical check

"dynamical information is extracted by [assuming] the existence of any equation of state [and checking whether the] energy conditions are satisfied at th[e milestones]."

The result

"We have developed a generic classification of the various physically relevant 'cosmological milestones' occurring in the literature in terms of a generalized power series expansion [of] the scale factor $a(t)$."

The short list

"With the notion of generalized power series, we have defined (on a purely kinematical level) the idea of polynomial and derivative curvature singularities. It is therefore possible to classify all 'cosmological milestones' as to whether they are singular or not, with the c[lass] of non-singular milestones being strictly limited (to extremality events or asymptoticall[y] Milne bangs/crunches)."

The generality claim

The results are "extremely general, and modulo certain [assumptions the] results are to a high degree model-in[dependent]."

The physical reading

"On dynamical grounds, we have checked the validity or otherwise of the classical energy conditions in the vicinity of the cosmological milestones. Depending on one's attitude towards the energy conditions, one could use this catalogue as a guide towards deciding on potentially interesting scenarios to investigate."

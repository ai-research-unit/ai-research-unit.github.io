What the Paper Does

Some string-inspired proposals say horizon areas are integer combinations of square roots of integers. The author tests them on Kerr–Newman black holes. He finds that the natural readings fail, because the fine structure constant is not an integer. A product of inner and outer horizon areas has a clean non-integer formula depending only on spin, charge, and the fine structure constant. Separate horizon areas need the mass as well. He also checks how the formulas avoid naked singularities.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The proposal under test

"there have been repeated suggestions that the areas of black hole event horizons might be quantized in a quite specific manner, in terms of linear combinations of square roots of positive integers"

The caution

"there are significant physical constraints on such integer-based proposals when one (somewhat speculatively) attempts to extend them outside their original extremal and supersymmetric framework"

The failure

"some of the more speculative integer-based proposals for the quantization of horizon areas fail for the ordinary Kerr–Newman black holes in (3+1) dimensions, essentially because the fine structure constant is not an integer"

The surviving formula

"it is possible to derive a non-integral horizon-area quantization formula (for the product of inner and outer horizon areas) in terms of spin, electric charge, and the fine structure constant"

The mass independence

"a formula that is independent of the underlying black hole mass and depends only on the conserved charges"

The separate case

"For inner and outer horizons considered separately, there is still a non-integral horizon-area quantization formula, now in terms of spin, electric charge, the fine structure constant, and the mass."

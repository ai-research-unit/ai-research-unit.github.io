What the Paper Does

The paper extends transfer-matrix bounds from a single barrier to a system of several separated barriers. For a purely classical particle the transmission is the product of the individual transmissions, but for waves each barrier contributes a phase and the phases can interfere constructively or destructively. The paper uses the transfer-matrix formalism to derive rigorous upper and lower bounds on the total transmission probability without knowing the phases, and then applies the same machinery to bounds on particle production by parametric resonance.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The problem

"Scattering from a compound barrier, one co[nsisting of several] non-overlapping sub-barriers, has a number of [nontrivial mathe]matical features. If one is scattering classical [particles, and the spin] of the particle can be ignored, the transmission [probability of the compound] barrier is simply given by the product of the [transmissions of the] individual sub-barriers."

What changes for waves

"In contrast if one is dealing with either purely classical waves or [quantum excita]tions, each sub-barrier contributes phase info[rmation], and these phases can lead to eith[er constructive or destructive] interference, with the transmission probabilit[y bounded by rigorous] upper and lower bounds."

The result

"We have seen how a rather nontrivial application of the transfer matrix formalism allows us to place rather nontrivial bounds on the physics of compound scattering systems — even in the absence of detailed information concerning their internal structure."

What the bounds need

The compound systems "are one-dimensional systems built up from a number of disjoint non-overlapping barriers; for such systems we have explicitly shown that the nett scattering properties (unsurprisingly) depend not only on the transmission probability, $T_i$, of each individual barrier, but also on a number of phases associated with these individual barriers and the separation between them."

The surprising part

"Surprisingly, even in the absence of quantitative information regarding the value of these phases it is nevertheless possible to place rigorous and nontrivial upper and lower bounds on the scattering properties of the compound system."

The wider application

"The resulting bounds also apply (with suitable modification of the language) to bounds on the number of quanta excited via parametric resonance."

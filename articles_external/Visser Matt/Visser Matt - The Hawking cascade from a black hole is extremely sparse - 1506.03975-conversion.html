What the Paper Does

The Hawking flux is usually pictured as a steady stream. The authors compute the ratio of the time between quanta to the time set by a quantum's energy. For the early, low-temperature stage this ratio is large, so the flux is sparse. They check Schwarzschild, Reissner–Nordström, and "dirty" black holes, and account for particle rest mass. They note the result is old but has been largely forgotten.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"The Hawking flux from a black hole, (at least as seen from asymptotic infinity), is extremely sparse and thin, with the average time between emission of the successive Hawking quanta being many times larger than the natural timescale set by the energies of the emitted quanta."

The history

"While this result has been known for over 30 years, it has largely been forgotten, possibly because many subsequent authors focussed mainly on the late-time high-temperature regime."

The focus here

"We shall instead focus on the early-stage low-temperatu[re regime]"

The cases

"Schwarzschild black holes", "Reissner–Nordström black holes (uncharged quanta)", "Dirty black holes", "Particle rest mass"

The scope condition

"So long as the temperature remains below the rest energy of the electron we can safely ignore emission into charged particles."

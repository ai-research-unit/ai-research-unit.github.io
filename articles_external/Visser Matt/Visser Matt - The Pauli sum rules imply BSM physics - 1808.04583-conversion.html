What the Paper Does

Pauli's three sum rules relate particle multiplicities, masses, and their squares. They follow from Lorentz invariance and from a finite zero-point stress-energy. The author applies them to the standard model spectrum. The sum rules fail for the known particles alone, so new particles are required. Supersymmetry would satisfy them, but is neither necessary nor sufficient.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The rules

$$\sum_n(-1)^{2S_n}g_n=0,\qquad \sum_n(-1)^{2S_n}g_nm_n^2=0,\qquad \sum_n(-1)^{2S_n}g_nm_n^4=0\qquad (paper\ 1)$$

What they mean

"These three sum rules are intimately related to both the Lorentz invariance and the finiteness of the zero-point stress-energy tensor."

What follows

"these three constraints are also intimately related to the existence of finite QFTs ultimately based on Fermi–Bose cancellations"

On supersymmetry

"(Supersymmetry is neither necessary nor sufficient for the existen[ce of such cancellations])"

The situation

"The current observational situation regarding the usual places to look for BSM physics is rather bleak."

The motivation

"With a lack of any direct observational evidence in favour of BSM physics, it becomes more critical to assess ind[irect constraints]"

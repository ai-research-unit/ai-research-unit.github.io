What the Paper Does

This is a two-page reply to a review of the Casimir effect. It corrects three statements about whether the Casimir effect can explain sonoluminescence. The authors deny that all Casimir-based explanations rest on static boundary conditions, deny that their own proposal uses the same mechanism as Schwinger's, and deny that the photon temperature can be identified with a temperature estimated from the acceleration of the bubble wall. The paper is a defence of a specific model, not a survey.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The purpose

"In [1] Milton has presented a brief review of some interesting aspects of the theory of the Casimir effect. This comment is aimed at correcting some imprecise statements in that work with respect to the relevance of the Casimir effect for explaining the phenomenon of sonoluminescence."

The three disputed claims

"[1] makes three assertions with which we strongly disagree: (1) That all of the Casimir-based explanations [of sono]luminescence rely on static Casimir effect [calcula]tions. (2) That the proposal by Liberati et al. [2–6] [is based] on the same mechanism as used by Schwinger (photon production from the quantum vacuum [gen]erated by the rapid movement of the bubble [bound]ary). (3) That the 'temperature' of the photons emitted [in] sonoluminescence can be identified with the [effective] temperature estimated from the acceleration [of the] dielectric boundary."

On the first claim

"There are at least three models which apply the dynamical C[asimir] effect (particle production from the quantum vacuum [driven by] a time-varying external field) to the problem of [sonolu]minescence. In particular Eberlein [8], Schützhold [9], and the main part of the present authors' work [3] make explicit use of such a framework".

On the second claim

"The proposal by Liberati et al. [2–6] cited in section 4 [is not] at all coincident with the Schwinger proposal [7], [so] the objections raised by Milton to the latter [do not] apply straightforwardly to the former. In particular [it] is not true that we proposed a model based on the instantaneous collapse of a bubble".

The caution

"we hope that this 'comment' will serve to remind people that the Casimir-based explanation of sonoluminescence cannot be dismissed out of hand. It is possible that experiments will ultimately disprove the dynamical Casimir effect based explanation for sonoluminescence, but this will not happen on the basis of the [arguments in the review]."

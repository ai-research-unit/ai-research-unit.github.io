What the Paper Does

Suppose light has more than one signal cone. Then spacetime is not described by a single metric. The authors examine this using birefringent crystal optics as the model. They write one Fresnel equation that encodes all the cones at once. Off the cone, building a full multi-refringent metric turns out to be difficult. They show that the Finsler metrics fail on the optical axes. The conclusion is mixed: the norm is fine, the metric is not.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The proposal being examined

"[the] suggestion that space-time might ultimately be anisotropic (Finsler-like) and/or exhibit 'multi-refringence' (multiple 'signal cones')"

The contrast with general relativity

"in general relativity, the single [mono-refringent] signal cone, together with a single scalar degree of freedom, the conformal factor, uniquely characterizes the full Lorentzian spacetime metric"

The tool

"multiple (and even anisotropic) signal cones can easily be dealt with in a unified manner, by writing down a single 'Fresnel equation' to simultaneously encode all signal cones in an even-handed manner"

The difficulty

"once one moves 'off the signal cone' and attempts to construct a full multi-refringent 'spacetime metric' we shall see that the situation becomes considerably more problematic"

The verdict

"The results of this investigation have been rather mixed."

The failure

"While the 3-space Finsler and co-Finsler norms corresponding to the two photon polarizations are always well-defined, the 3-space Finsler and co-Finsler metrics have undesirable properties on the optical axes"

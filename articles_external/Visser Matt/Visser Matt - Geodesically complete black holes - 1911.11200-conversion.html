What the Paper Does

Penrose's theorem shows singularities are unavoidable in general relativity, but says nothing about what replaces them. Taking that theorem as a guide, the authors classify spherically symmetric geometries that are geodesically complete, globally hyperbolic, and free of curvature singularities, without assuming any dynamics. The result is a short list: evanescent regular black holes, one-way hidden wormholes, everlasting regular black holes, and asymptotic hidden wormholes. Each has a defect — an instability, a topology change, or a conflict with semiclassical physics. The authors conclude there is no fully safe option.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The starting point

"The 1965 Penrose singularity theorem demonstrates the utterly inevitable and unavoidable formation of spacetime singularities under physically reasonable assumptions, and it remains one of the main results in our understanding of black holes."

The gap

"It is standard lore that quantum gravitational effects will always tame these singularities in black hole interiors. However, the Penrose's theorem provides no clue as to the possible (non-singular) geometries that may be realized in theories beyond general relativity as the result of singularity regularization."

The method

"we take a completely different approach, remaining agnostic to the particular quantization scheme of the gravitational field and using Penrose's theorem as guidance ... and perform a systematic analysis of the non-singular possibilities that is blind to the dynamics (classical, semi-classical, or quantum) and assumes a minimal set of kinematical requirements."

The main result

"Our main result is that, contrary to what one might expect, the set of regular geometries that arises is remarkably limited. We rederive geometries that have been analyzed before, but also uncover some new possibilities."

The tradeoff

"the complete catalogue of possibilities that we obtain allows us to draw the novel conclusion that there is a clear tradeoff between internal and external consistency: One has to choose between models that display internal inconsistencies, or models that include significant deviations with respect to general relativity, which should therefore be amenable to observational tests via multi-messenger astrophysics."

The catalogue

"we were able to identify four classes of geometries, that can either be taken as they are or be combined, in order to yield the most general spherically symmetric spacetime satisfying our requirements"

The verdict

"we find the remarkable result that there is no fully 'safe' option: all classes of geometries display internal inconsistencies (instabilities, topology change or incompatibilities with semiclassical physics), except for the particular case of evanescent black holes in short time scales."

The limit of the analysis

"although we have assumed an everywhere well defined geometry, our analysis is valuable also when this is not the case and it allows us to understand how to construct singularity regularization scenario that solves the self consistency issues raised in this paper by allowing for region where an effective geometry cannot be defined."

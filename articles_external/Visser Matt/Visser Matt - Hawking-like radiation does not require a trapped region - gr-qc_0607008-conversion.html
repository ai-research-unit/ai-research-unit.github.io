What the Paper Does

Using simple acoustic models, the authors show that a stationary Planckian emission of quasi-particles does not need a trapped region. A supersonic flow is not required, nor even a sonic point at finite laboratory time. It suffices that a sonic point develops in the asymptotic future at a sufficient rate. They also note that the relevant surface gravity is a coarse-grained, averaged quantity, and use this to sketch a collapse scenario in which the stellar surface never crosses its Schwarzschild radius.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"By constructing simple geometric acoustic models, we obtain a somewhat unexpected result: We show that in order to obtain a stationary and Planckian emission of quasi-particles, it is not necessary to create a trapped region in the acoustic spacetime (corresponding to a supersonic regime in the fluid flow)."

The minimal condition

"It is sufficient to set up a dynamically changing flow asymptotically approaching a sonic regime with sufficient rapidity in laboratory time."

The broader reading

"in order to produce a Hawking-like effect it is neither necessary to generate a supersonic regime (fluid velocity $v$ strictly larger than sound velocity $c$), nor even a sonic point at finite laboratory time. All one needs is that a sonic point develops in the asymptotic future (that is, for $t\to+\infty$) with sufficient rapidity"

The technical worry

"The corresponding velocity profiles are characterized by discontinuities in the derivatives, so one might wonder whether they would be amenable to experimental construction, given that the continuum model is only an approximation."

The answer to the worry

"in realistic situations, what is relevant for Hawking radiation is a coarse-grained profile — obtained by averaging over a scale $\Delta$ larger than the one which characterizes the breakdown of the continuum model — which does not contain the unphysical small scale details"

The speculative extension

"In this scenario the complete semiclassical geometry will have neither a trapped region (and so by construction no trapping horizon) nor an event horizon. In this circumstance there would be no trans-Planckian problem, nor information loss associated with the collapse and evaporation of this black hole-like object."

The status of that extension

"Although somewhat speculative at this stage we believe it is worthwhile to describe it here."

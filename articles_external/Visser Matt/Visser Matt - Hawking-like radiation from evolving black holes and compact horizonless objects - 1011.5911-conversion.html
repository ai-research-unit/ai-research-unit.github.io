What the Paper Does

Standard derivations of Hawking radiation assume an eternal horizon and a static exterior. The authors drop both. They show that a Planckian flux follows from an approximately exponential relation between affine parameters at past and future null infinity, together with an adiabatic condition. The result holds for an evaporating black hole and for a collapsing object that never forms a horizon. They give explicit time-dependent Bogoliubov calculations, and stress that the whole derivation never refers to any kind of horizon.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The assumption being dropped

"Usually, Hawking radiation is derived assuming (i) that a future eternal event horizon forms, and (ii) that the subsequent exterior geometry is static. However, one may be interested in either considering quasi-black holes (objects in an ever-lasting state of approach to horizon formation, but never quite forming one), where (i) fails, or, following the evolution of a black hole during evaporation, where (ii) fails."

The sufficient condition

"We shall verify that as long as one has an approximately exponential relation between the affine parameters on the null generators of past and future null infinity, then subject to a suitable adiabatic condition being satisfied, a Planck-distributed flux of Hawking-like radiation will occur."

The scope

"This happens both for the case of an evaporating black hole, as well as for the more dramatic case of a collapsing object for which no horizon has yet formed (or even will ever form)."

The core statement

"Whenever $\mathcal{I}^-$ and $\mathcal{I}^+$ are connected by a function $U=p(u)$ of the form $$U=U_*+C_*\int_{u_*}^{u}\exp\left[-\int_{u_*}^{\bar u}\kappa(\tilde u)\,d\tilde u\right]d\bar u \qquad (paper\ 10.1)$$ ... and where $\kappa(u)$ satisfies an 'adiabatic condition' $$\left.\frac{d\kappa(u)}{du}\right|_{u_*}\ll \kappa(u_*)^2 \qquad (paper\ 10.2)$$ then the argument sketched above proves the existence of a Hawking-like Planck-distributed spectrum of outgoing particles reaching $\mathcal{I}^+$ at $u_*$ with a (time-dependent) Hawking temperature $k_B T_H(u_*)=\hbar\kappa(u_*)/2\pi$."

The absence of horizons

"Working with this formalism we have never had to use any statements about any horizon forming anywhere — neither event horizon nor apparent horizon nor trapping horizon nor dynamical horizon"

The limit of the claim

"we have carefully described the Hawking flux as Planckian rather than thermal. To claim thermality one has to explicitly assume the formation of an event horizon (behind which one can hide correlations)."

The trans-Planckian problem remains

"The current formalism in no way 'solves' the trans-Planckian problem and if anything brings it more sharply into focus."

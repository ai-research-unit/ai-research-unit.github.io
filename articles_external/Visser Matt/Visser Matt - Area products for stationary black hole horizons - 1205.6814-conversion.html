What the Paper Does

The paper tests a conjecture about products of horizon areas. For many multi-horizon stationary black holes the product of inner and outer horizon areas depends only on charge, angular momentum, and moduli, and not on the mass. The author computes two counterexamples in four dimensions. For Schwarzschild–de Sitter the product of the event-horizon area and the cosmological-horizon area depends on the mass. For Reissner–Nordström–anti-de Sitter the product of the inner and outer areas is computed perturbatively and also fails to be mass-independent. Including a third, virtual horizon does not repair the first case.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The conjecture

"Area products for multi-horizon stationary black holes often have intriguing properties, and are often (though not always) independent of the mass of the black hole itself (depending only on various charges, angular momenta, and moduli). Such products are often formulated in terms of the areas of inner (Cauchy) horizons and outer (event) horizons, and sometimes include the effects of unphysical 'virtual' horizons."

The failure

"But the conjectured mass-independence sometimes fails. Specifically, for the Schwarzschild–de Sitter [Kottler] black hole in (3+1) dimensions it is shown by explicit exact calculation that the product of event horizon area and cosmological horizon area is not mass independent. (Including the effect of the third 'virtual' horizon does not improve the situation.)"

The second case

"Similarly, in the Reissner–Nordstrom-anti-de Sitter black hole in (3+1) dimensions the product of inner (Cauchy) horizon area and event horizon area is calculated (perturbatively), and is shown to" fail the same test. The two cases differ in which pair of horizons is multiplied and in whether the computation is exact or perturbative.

The method

Both results come from solving the horizon equation and multiplying the resulting areas. No assumption beyond the exact metric is used, so the failure is a property of the solutions and not of an approximation, except in the charged anti-de Sitter case where the perturbation is stated explicitly.

What the paper concludes

The mass-independence observed in Kerr–Newman and its relatives is not a general theorem. It holds for a class of solutions and fails once a cosmological constant is present, in either sign.

The idea. A product of areas that looks like a constant is not one. The counterexamples are the two simplest solutions with a cosmological constant.

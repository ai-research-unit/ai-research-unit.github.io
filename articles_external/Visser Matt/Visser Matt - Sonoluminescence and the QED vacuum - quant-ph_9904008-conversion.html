What the Paper Does

This is an overview of vacuum-based explanations of sonoluminescence. It separates quasi-static Casimir models from dynamical ones. The authors compute Bogoliubov coefficients for a single oscillation of the bubble, in a large-volume limit and numerically for finite volume. They conclude that the static Casimir energy is at best a rough energy estimate. Only a model of the actual particle creation can decide the question.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The verdict

"Sonoluminescence is not directly related to the static Casimir effect."

The role of the static energy

"The static Casimir energy is at best capable of giving a crude estimate for the energy budget in SL."

The requirement

"only models dealing with the actual mechanism of particle creation ... will be able to eventually prove, or disprove, the perti[nent claims]"

The two families

"Quasi-static Casimir models: Schwinger's approach" and "Eberlein's dynamical model for SL"

The method

"Bogolubov approach on a single oscillation"

What the Paper Does

Schwarzschild's interior solution for a star of constant density is a hundred and ten years old. The authors redo it in isotropic coordinates. In that gauge the line element contains two simple rational functions. The two parameters are the central density and the central pressure, which are easy to read off. They work through the energy conditions, the Misner–Sharp mass, the junction at the surface, and the speed of sound.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The aim

"we shall revisit the venerable 110-year-old topic of Schwarzschild's constant density star, emphasizing that for many (though not quite all) purposes it is much easier to analyze this spacetime in isotropic coordinates (versus the more usually adopted Hilbert–Droste area coordinates)"

The gain

"The relevant line element is particularly transparent, containing two simple rational functions of the radial coordinate"

The parameters

"the two physical parameters appearing in this line element are easily and readily interpretable in terms of the central density and central pressure of the star"

Why it is done

"using isotropic coordinates makes the discussion a lot simpler, and, as we have seen above, is pedagogically interesting for a number of reasons"

The horizon

"with a view to the future, we very strongly suspect that the use of isotropic coordinates for generic perfect fluid spheres may also prove advantageous"

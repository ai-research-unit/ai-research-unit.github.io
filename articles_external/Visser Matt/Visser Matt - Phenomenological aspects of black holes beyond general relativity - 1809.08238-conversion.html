What the Paper Does

This review collects the observational constraints that can be placed on black hole alternatives. It defines a "quasi black hole": Schwarzschild outside a radius, something else inside, and no horizons. It then surveys the channels that probe such objects, including light, matter, and gravitational waves. The authors compare what each channel can and cannot see. They finish with the theoretical gaps that must be closed before the constraints become sharp.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The object defined

"let us define a static and spherically symmetric quasi black hole in a rough way as a spacetime satisfying the following conditions: (i) the geometry is Schwarzschild above a given radius $R$ ... (ii) the geometry for $r\le R$ is not Schwarzschild, and (iii) there are no event or trapping horizons."

The aim

"obtain the most accurate characterization of the viable constraints that can be placed using current data"

What the survey shows

"this analysis also allows us to highlight how different channels are capable of probing certain features but are oblivious to others"

The gaps

"pinpoint the theoretical aspects that should be addressed in order to strengthen these tests"

The signatures

"these objects are expected to lead to distinctive phenomenological signatures arising from interaction with light, matter and gravitational waves — due to the large modifications of the geometry starting at $r=R$."

The contrast

"while black holes are perfectly absorptive, quasi black holes do not [absorb perfectly]"

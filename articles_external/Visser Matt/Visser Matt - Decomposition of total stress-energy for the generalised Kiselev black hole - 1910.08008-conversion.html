What the Paper Does

The Kiselev black hole is widely used as a toy model for a black hole surrounded by quintessence. The paper quantifies how far it is from being a perfect fluid. It shows the anisotropic stress-energy can be split into a perfect fluid part plus either an electromagnetic or a scalar field part, with the charge carried by the fluid generating the anisotropy. The same decomposition is applied to one-component and to multi-component generalisations, and the authors report that whether the null energy condition holds correlates tightly with which type of decomposition occurs.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What is shown

"We demonstrate that the anisotropic stress-energy supporting the Kiselev black hole can be mimicked by being split into a perfect fluid component plus either an electromagnetic component or a scalar field component, thereby quantifying the precise extent to which the Kiselev black hole fails to represent a perfect fluid spacetime."

The mechanism

"The perfect fluid component carries either an electric or a scalar charge, which then generates anisotropic electromagnetic or scalar fields. This in turn generates anisotropic contributions to the stress-energy. These in turn induce forces which partially (in addition to the fluid pressure gradient) support the matter content against gravity."

The scope

"This decomposition is carried out both for the original 1-component Kiselev black hole and for the generalized $N$-component Kiselev black holes. We also comment on the presence of energy condition violations (specifically for the null energy condition — NEC) for certain sub-classes of Kiselev black holes."

The two corrections to the literature

"Despite many claims to the contrary, the Kiselev spacetime does not represent a perfect fluid, (except for the very special cases where it reduces to Schwarzschild/Kottler/de Sitter spacetime)." And: "Despite many claims to the contrary, the word 'quintessence' as applied to the Kiselev spacetimes has nothing to do with the word 'quintessence' as it is used in the cosmology community."

The general method

"In earlier work three of the current authors showed that it is possible to mimic the matter content of any static spherically symmetric spacetime by a combination of (perfect fluid) + (electromagnetic field) + (scalar field)."

The result

"We find that there is a tight correlation between satisfying the null energy condition (NEC) and the type of decomposition that arises. We find a trichotomy: In regions where the [null energy condition holds, one decomposition applies, and where it fails, another]."

What the Paper Does

Semiclassical gravity treats the metric as classical and everything else as quantum. Where does that stop working? The author defines a reliability horizon: the surface beyond which the approximation should not be trusted. He relates it to the chronology horizon and argues the chronology horizon is always hidden behind the reliability horizon.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The concept

"The 'reliability horizon' for semi-classical quantum gravity quantifies the extent to which we should trust semi-classical quantum gravity, and gives a handle on just where the 'Planck regime' resides."

The known answer

"We should certainly stop believing semi-classical quantum gravity once we enter the Planck regime."

The subtlety

"The subtleties arise in recognizing the onset of Planck scale physics."

The apparatus

"I have recently introduced the concepts of the 'reliable region', the 'reliability bounda[ry]'"

The application

"the chronology horizon is always hidden by the reliability horizon and that semiclassical quantum gravity should fail before reaching the chronology horizon"

What the Paper Does

The comment corrects a claim that a swirling dielectric fluid produces an optical black hole. The proposed geometry has purely azimuthal flow, so nothing falls inward, and there is no trapped surface. The argument needs only that the effective metric can be written in ADM-like form, so it applies to any such acoustic or optical geometry. Optical black holes remain possible; these particular ones are not black holes.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The verdict

"In [1,2] Leonhardt and Piwnicki have presented an interesting analysis of how to use a flowing dielectric fluid to generate a so-called 'optical black hole'. Unfortunately there is [a] subtle misinterpretation in the analysis regarding these 'optical black holes'."

What is not in dispute

"While it is clear that 'optical black holes' can certainly exist as theoretical constructs, and while the experimental prospects for actually building them in the laboratory are excellent, the particular model geometries of [1,2] are in fact not black holes at all."

Why

"Leonhardt and Piwnicki consider a vortex geometry, where the dielectric fluid is swirling in a purely azimuthal direction around a straight linear core — there is no radial motion into the core in their models, and this is enough to prevent the formation of trapped surfaces and event horizons."

The general argument

"This observation depends only on the fact that the effective metric can globally be cast in ADM-like form",

$$[g_{\mathrm{eff}}]_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}-(c_{\mathrm{eff}}^2-g_{ab}v_{\mathrm{eff}}^a v_{\mathrm{eff}}^b) & [v_{\mathrm{eff}}]_j\\ [v_{\mathrm{eff}}]_i & [g_{\mathrm{eff}}]_{ij}\end{pmatrix}.$$

The generalisation

"In the acoustic geometry of [3,4], the constant-time 3-space metric $[g_{\mathrm{eff}}]_{ij}$ is particularly simple (it's the identity matrix), the effective velocity $[v_{\mathrm{eff}}]^i$ equals the fluid velocity, and $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is just the local speed of sound."

The honest concession

"These technical complications do not affect the key issue: An ergo-region certainly forms once the norm of this effective velocity exceeds the local effective propagation speed." An ergo-region is not a horizon, and the paper is careful to say which one forms.

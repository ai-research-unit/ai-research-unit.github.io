What the Paper Does

The stellar mass function follows a power law at large mass. The author asks what happens below one solar mass. He argues that critical gravitational collapse and self-similarity fix the low-mass distribution, and that the exponent has the opposite sign. The exponent is in principle computable from the critical solution using dynamical systems theory. He compares the result with observation and finds qualitative agreement. The note is a model-independent argument with a calculable number.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim

"Critical gravitational collapse and self similarity are used to probe the mass distribution of subsolar objects."

The result

"We demonstrate that at very low mass the distribution is given by a power law, with an exponent opposite in sign to that observed at high-mass regime."

The source of the exponent

"We further show that the value of this low-mass exponent is in principle calculable via dynamical systems theory applied to gravitational collapse."

The comparison

"Qualitative agreement between numerical experiments and observational data is good."

The contrast

"At large mass, the initial mass function [IMF] describing the mass distribution of stellar objects is characterised by a power law with the Salpeter exponent 1.35."

The open problem

"a precise calculation of the region of convergence is still lacking"

What the Paper Does

The paper fits the Hubble relation to the legacy supernova data without assuming a dynamical model. The relation follows from the symmetries of FLRW alone, so its parameters can be extracted with no dark-energy assumption. The authors perform the fit in several distance variables, treat systematic uncertainties conservatively, and find that the case for cosmic acceleration is weaker than usually claimed: the data suggest acceleration, but not beyond reasonable doubt. They also show that the fitted deceleration parameter moves depending on which distance variable is used and how systematics are budgeted.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The starting point

"The Hubble relation between distance an[d redshift depends] only on the symmetries of a FLRW spacetime, [and not on dynamical] assumptions. This suggests that it should [be possible to fit the Hubble] relation without making any dynamical assu[mptions]."

What the paper does

"[We perform] related cosmographic fits to the legacy [supernova] data, [and find that] the 'preponderance of evidence' c[ertainly suggests acceleration, but] argue that (unless one uses additional [assumptions] [this] is not currently supported 'beyond reas[onable doubt]'."

The two conclusions that must be separated

"While the 'preponderance of evidence' certainly suggests an [accelerating universe], we would argue that this conclusion is not currently supported 'beyond reasonable doubt' — [the data] (considered by itself) certainly rather strongly suggests an accelerating universe, but is [not enough to] allow us to reliably conclude that the universe is accelerating."

The methodological point

"Instead of simply picking a single model and fitting the data to it, we have tested the ov[erall] scenario by encoding the same physics ($H_0$, $q_0$, $j_0$) in multiple different ways ($d_L$, $d_F$ ... and $y$) to test the robustness of the data fitting procedures."

On systematics

"we have been much more explicit, and conservative, about the role of systematic uncertaint[ies] on estimates of the cosmological parameters. If we only use the statistical uncertainties and the 'known unknowns' added in quadrature, then [the evidence for cos]mological acceleration is much improved ... but this does not mean that such a conclusion is either robust or reliable."

The caution about method

"By 'cherry picking' [a] particular way one analyzes the data, one can find statistical support for almost any conclusion." And: "there is an enormous difference between the two statements: 'the most likely value for the deceleration parameter is negative', and ['the deceleration parameter is negative beyond reasonable doubt']."

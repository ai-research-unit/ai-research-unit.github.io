What the Paper Does

The authors put a symmetric invertible matrix of fixed signature on every site of a hyper-cubic lattice and couple nearest neighbours with a simple action. Near the identity, the dynamics is exactly a finite-difference version of weak-field gravity in harmonic gauge. The action reduces to the Einstein–Hilbert action at leading order. The lattice breaks diffeomorphism invariance and fixes the de Donder gauge, which they treat as a feature. The paper is a short letter stating the model and its limits.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The model

"We present an elegant and simple dynamical model of symmetric, non-degenerate $n\times n$ matrices of fixed signature defined on a $n$-dimensional hyper-cubic lattice with nearest-neighbor interactions."

The relation to gravity

"we show that the dynamics of the model when all matrices are close to the identity corresponds exactly to a finite-difference discretization of weak-field gravity in harmonic gauge."

The action

$$S=-\xi_n(s/L_P)^{n-2}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\left\{\sqrt{\det({}^{ij}\!g)}-\frac{\sqrt{\det({}^ig)}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{\det({}^jg)}}{2}\right\} \qquad (paper\ 2)$$

The admitted background

"The implied background structure may be anathema to GR purists, however we will argue that this is still a useful thing to do"

The gauge

"The action is gauge fixed, with only rigid (global) rotations and parity inversions as symmetries, and seems automatically to be in the de Donder (nonlinear harmonic) gauge"

The cost

"this model does not exhibit the active diffeomorphism symmetry of the continuum theory and thus there are $n(n+1)/2$ dynamical degrees of freedom per lattice site"

The surprise

"What is perhaps a little surprising is just how far one can get with such a simple and elegant discrete action."

What the Paper Does

The paper builds traversable wormholes by cutting and pasting two black-bounce spacetimes and joining them with a thin shell. The black-bounce geometry contains one parameter, the bounce scale, and everything unusual is packed into it. The authors keep the construction as close to standard general relativity as possible, derive the shell's properties from the Einstein equations, and then perturb the throat radially to test stability. They find the wormhole is stable when the exotic material on the throat has suitable properties, and they map the stability regions in the parameter space of the two bulk masses and bounce scales.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The construction

"Based on the recently introduced black-bounce spacetimes, we shall consider the construction of the related spherically symmetric thin-shell traversable wormholes within the context of standard general relativity."

The single parameter

"All of the really unusual physics is encoded in one simple parameter $a$ which characterizes the scale of the bounce."

The methodological principle

"Keeping the discussion as close as possible to standard general relativity is the theorist's version of only adjusting one feature of the model at a time."

What is modified

"We shall modify the standard thin-shell traversable wormhole construction, each bulk region now being a black-bounce spacetime, and with the physics of the thin shell being (as much as possible) derivable from the Einstein equations."

The stability analysis

"we shall apply a dynamical analysis to the throat by considering linearized radial perturbations around static solutions, and demonstrate that the stability of the wormhole is equivalent to choosing suitable properties for the exotic material residing on the wormhole throat."

The assessment

"The construction is sufficiently novel to be interesting, and sufficiently straightforward to be tractable."

The outcome

"We have [found the] existence of suitable stability regions in parameter space. Several of these models are particu[larly interesting]."

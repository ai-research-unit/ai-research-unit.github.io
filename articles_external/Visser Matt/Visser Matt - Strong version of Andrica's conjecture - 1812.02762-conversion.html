What the Paper Does

Andrica's conjecture bounds the difference of square roots of consecutive primes. The author proposes a stronger bound: the gap between consecutive primes is less than one half, with six exceptions. A proof is out of reach. He verifies the claim by computation up to the eighty-first maximal prime gap, which covers all primes below $2^{64}$. He also shows the new claim implies Oppermann's conjecture.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim

"A strong version of Andrica's conjecture can be formulated as follows: Except for $p_n\in\{3,7,13,23,31,113\}$, that is $n\in\{2,4,6,9,11,30\}$, one has $\sqrt{p_{n+1}}-\sqrt{p_n}<\tfrac12$."

The status

"While a proof is far out of reach I shall show that this strong version of Andrica's conjecture is unconditionally and explicitly verified"

The range

"certainly for all primes $p<2^{64}\approx 1.844\times10^{19}$"

The relation

"this strong Andrica conjecture is slightly stronger than Oppermann's conjecture"

The contrast

"the Cramér conjecture ... impose significantly stronger constraints on the prime gaps of the form $g_n:=p_{n+1}-p_n=O(\ln p_n)^2$"

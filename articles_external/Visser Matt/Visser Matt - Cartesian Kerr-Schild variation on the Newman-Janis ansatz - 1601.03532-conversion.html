What the Paper Does

The paper gives a shorter route from Schwarzschild to Kerr than the Newman–Janis procedure. It works directly with the Kerr–Schild form of the metric in Cartesian coordinates and reduces the usual four steps to two, avoiding null tetrads, metric inversion, and complex coordinate transformations. The authors do not claim to have turned the trick into an algorithm; they identify two distinct operations inside it, the complexification of a prefactor and the change from a spherical to an oblate spheroidal null congruence. They leave the second as the mysterious step and conclude that the procedure remains a trick.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The subject

"The Newman–Janis trick is a procedure, (not even really an ansatz), for obtaining the Kerr spacetime from the Schwarzschild spacetime. This 50 year old trick continues to generate heated discussion and debate even to this day. Most of the debate focusses on whether the Newman–Janis procedure can be upgraded to the status of an algorithm, or even an inspired ansatz, or is it just a random trick of no deep physical significance."

The contribution

"In the current article we will not answer these deeper questions, we shall instead present a much simpler alternative variation on the theme of the Newman–Janis trick that might be easier to work with. We shall present a 2-step version of the Newman–Janis trick that works directly with the Kerr–Schild 'Cartesian' metric presentation of the Kerr spacetime."

The result

"We have developed a very simple and direct version of the Newman–Janis trick, one that works directly on the (covariant) metric. (So we can avoid working with null tetrads, metric inversion, and even avoid complex coordinate transformations.) In doing so we have reduced the usual four-step process to a much more direct two-step process."

The refusal to overclaim

"We do not claim to have upgraded the Newman–Janis procedure to an algorithm, the procedure still requires some motivated guesswork, so it is still at best a trick. It is the compact nature of the current two-step version of the Newman–Janis trick that is the primary issue of scientific interest in this article."

What remains unexplained

"Even with this simplified two-step process, there are still two radically different things going on. The complexification of the 'potential' prefactor is straightforward, and in Newtonian gravity has a history going back some 130 years. In contrast the transition from the Schwarzschild null geodesic congruence to the Kerr null geodesic congruence is much trickier. One route, via the deformation of spherical polar to oblate spheroidal coordinates is technically elementary, even pedestrian, but somewhat mysterious." And: "on balance we still feel that the Newman–Janis procedure is a trick rather than an algorithm."

1. How much of Kerr is in Kerr–Schild

The idea. The derivation needs only the Kerr–Schild form and one deformation of the null congruence. The full metric is reached from a scalar and a vector.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-kerr-schild-sub-class-and-the-vacuum-wave.md works inside the Kerr–Schild sub-class. Whether the article can use that depends on whether its vacuum wave equation is preserved under the same step, which is a question the article should answer.

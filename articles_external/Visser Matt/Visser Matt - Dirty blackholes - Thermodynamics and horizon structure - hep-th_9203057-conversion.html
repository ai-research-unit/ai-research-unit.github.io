What the Paper Does

The paper observes that black holes surrounded by classical matter fields consistently have a Hawking temperature lower than a vacuum black hole of the same horizon area, and argues this is generic rather than accidental. For any static spherically symmetric black hole it derives a general formula for the temperature in terms of the energy density and radial tension, using Schwarzschild coordinates. The exponential factor in the formula is positive whenever the weak energy condition holds, which establishes the suppression. The author discusses examples and conjectures the extension to non-spherical, non-static cases.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The observation

"Considerable interest has recently been expressed in (static spherically symmetric) blackholes in interaction with various classical matter fields ... A common feature of these investigations that has not previously been remarked upon is that the Hawking temperature of such systems appears to be suppressed relative to that of a vacuum blackhole of equal horizon area."

The inequality

"That is: $kT_H\le \hbar/(4\pi r_H)\equiv \hbar/\sqrt{4\pi A_H}$. This paper will argue that this suppression is generic."

The general formula

"it will be shown that"

$$kT_H=\frac{\hbar}{4\pi r_H}\,e^{-\varphi(r_H)}\left(1-8\pi G\rho_H r_H^2\right).$$

The meaning of the exponent

"Here $\varphi(r_H)$ is an integral quantity, depending on the distribution of matter, that is guaranteed to be positive if the Weak Energy Condition is satisfied."

The general statement

"For an arbitrary static spherically symmetric blackhole this note has established a general formula for the Hawking temperature in terms of the energy density and radial tension."

The limits acknowledged

"Generalizations of this result to axisymmetric spacetimes (for instance, to Kerr–Newman blackholes embedded in an axisymmetric cloud of matter) would clearly be of interest. Generalizations to arbitrary event horizons are probably unmanageable."

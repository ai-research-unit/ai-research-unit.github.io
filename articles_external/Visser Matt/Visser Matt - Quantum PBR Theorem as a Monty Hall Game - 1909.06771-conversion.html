What the Paper Does

The Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem says the quantum state cannot be merely a state of knowledge. The authors recast its proof as a Monty Hall game. The player chooses doors, and the winning probability for switching depends on whether the model is ontic or epistemic. In some epistemic games, switching brings no advantage. The authors suggest this opens quantum protocols built on the game.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The theorem

"The quantum Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph (PBR) theorem addresses the question of whether the quantum state corresponds to a $\psi$-ontic model (system's physical state) or to a $\psi$-epistemic [model]"

The reformulation

"We have reformulated PBR theorem into a Monty Hall game."

The result

"this particular gamification of the theorem highlights that winning probabilities, for switching doors in the game, depend on whether it is a $\psi$-ontic or $\psi$-epistemic game"

The asymmetry

"we also show that in certain $\psi$-epistemic games switching doors provides no advantage"

The prospect

"We argue that future investigation of Monty Hall concepts applied to antidistinguishability scenarios will lead to novel quantum protocols."

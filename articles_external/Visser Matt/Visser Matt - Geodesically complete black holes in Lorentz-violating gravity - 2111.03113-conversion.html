What the Paper Does

This paper extends a classification of singularity-free black hole geometries to theories where different modes travel at different speeds. It reviews trapped and marginally trapped surfaces, then introduces universally marginally trapped surfaces for frameworks with infinite signal velocity, which yield universal trapping horizons. Trapped surfaces do not generalise the same way, and the authors explain why horizons still suffice. In Einstein-aether theory each mode's metric falls into one of the earlier classes unchanged; in Hořava-like theories one class drops out.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The aim

"We present a systematic study of the geometric structure of non-singular spacetimes describing black holes in Lorentz-violating gravity."

The new notion

"This leads us to introduce the notion of universally marginally trapped surfaces, as the direct generalization of marginally trapped surfaces for frameworks with infinite signal velocities (Hořava-like frameworks), which then allows us to define universal trapping horizons."

The partial failure

"We find that trapped surfaces cannot be generalized in the same way, and discuss in detail why this does not prevent using universal trapping horizons to define black holes in Hořava-like frameworks."

The classification

"This allows us to present a complete classification of all possible geometries that neither display incomplete physical trajectories nor curvature singularities."

The main difference

"Our main result is that not all classes that exist in frameworks in which all signal velocities are realized in Hořava-like frameworks."

The Einstein-aether case

"These frameworks are characterized by finite signal velocities $c_{(i)}$ and a metric field $g^{(i)}_{ab}$ that can be defined for each propagating mode. We have concluded that each of these metrics must belong to one of the classes discussed in our previous papers (evanescent horizons, one-way hidden wormholes, everlasting horizons and asymptotic hidden wormholes) [14, 15] with no amendments."

The simplification

"infinite signal velocities simplify the classification of possible geometries, removing the limiting cases that were described by the everlasting horizons class."

The robustness

"Some of the classes we have discussed are preserved when going from a single-metric framework to a multi-metric framework, and even if taking the limit in which some of the signal velocities are infinite."

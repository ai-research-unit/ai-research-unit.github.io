What the Paper Does

The second-order Schrödinger equation is rewritten as two coupled first-order equations. The authors add an auxiliary condition to remove the extra freedom. In this form the general solution can be written formally, using three arbitrary functions and a path-ordered exponential. They apply the system to one-dimensional scattering and derive bounds on transmission, reflection, and Bogoliubov coefficients. The bounds are compared with earlier results.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The rewrite

"We reformulate the second-order Schrödinger equation as a set of two coupled first order differential equations, a so-called 'Shabat–Zakharov system'"

The flexibility

"There is considerable flexibility in this approach, and we emphasise the utility of introducing an 'auxiliary condition' or 'gauge condition' that is used to cut down the degrees of freedom."

The general solution

"Using this formalism, we derive the explicit (but formal) general solution to the Schrödinger equation."

Its ingredients

"The general solution depends on three arbitrarily chosen functions, and a path-ordered exponential matrix."

The application

"we have used the system to derive a number of rigourous bounds on transmission probabilities (and reflection probabilities and Bogoliubov coefficients) for one-dimensional scattering problems"

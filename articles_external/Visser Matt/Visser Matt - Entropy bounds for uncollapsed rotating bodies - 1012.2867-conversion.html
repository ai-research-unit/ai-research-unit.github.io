What the Paper Does

The authors previously bounded the entropy of a static spherically symmetric uncollapsed object by half its area. This paper extends the bound to rotating bodies, which the authors say is harder than it looks. The classical part uses only general relativity and elementary thermodynamics to bound entropy by an average surface gravity times area over temperature. A second step appeals to the Unruh effect to bound the temperature below, and the combination again gives entropy at most half the area. The appendix derives the non-geodesic timelike Raychaudhuri equation used in the argument.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"Consider a rotating blob of matter that has not collapsed to a black hole. Can we nevertheless place a robust bound on its entropy using fundamental physics, without resorting to black hole physics (generalized second law, holographic bound, horizon entanglement entropy) or the Bekenstein bound?"

The previous result

"In the case of non-rotating static configurations this was recently answered in the affirmative when the present authors derived a bound based only on classical general relativity, basic thermodynamics, and the Unruh effect, to the effect that (entropy) $\le\frac12$(area)."

Why the half

"The $\frac12$ is not a typo — ultimately one has $\frac12$ (rather than the $\frac14$ one might naively expect based on black hole physics) simply because the matter has not collapsed to a black hole."

What this paper adds

"We shall now extend this result to rotating systems. This extension is less simple than one might at first suppose."

The classical bound

"Purely classically, (using only classical general relativity and basic thermodynamics), it is possible to show that the entropy of uncollapsed matter inside a region enclosed by a surface of area $A$ is bounded from above by"

$$S\le\frac{\|\vec\kappa\|_{\max(\text{surface})}A}{4\pi T_\infty}.$$

The semiclassical step

"By appealing to the Unruh effect, which is our only invocation of quantum physics, we argue that for a suitable class of fiducial observers there is a (quantitatively weak but qualitatively robust) lower bound on the temperature (as measured at spatial infinity)"

$$T_\infty\ge\max_{\text{FIDOs}}\frac{\|\vec\kappa\|}{2\pi}.$$

The final result

"Thus, using only classical general relativity, basic thermodynamics, and the Unruh effect, we are able to argue that for uncollapsed matter"

$$S\le\tfrac12 A.$$

The overall message

"Useful (albeit sometimes quantitatively weak) entropy bounds can be derived from very basic physics without any recourse to the long sought for 'full theory of quantum gravity'. ... These are remarkably useful bounds based on an absolute minimum of physical assumptions."

The honest reservation

"While we suspect that there might still be a number of ways in which (in specific situations) the bounds enunciated in this article can be improved, the overall message is (we think) clear."

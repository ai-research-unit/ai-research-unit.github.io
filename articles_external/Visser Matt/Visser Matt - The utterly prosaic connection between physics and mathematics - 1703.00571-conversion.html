What the Paper Does

Wigner argued that mathematics is unreasonably effective in physics. The author argues the effectiveness is not unreasonable at all. Mathematics was developed as a tool for describing the world, and the fit is a product of that history. He illustrates with examples from quantum mechanics and quantum field theory. He also criticises the mathematical universe hypothesis.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The target

"Eugene Wigner famously argued for the 'unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics' for describing physics and other natural sciences in his 1960 essay."

The responses

"responses range from 'So what? Why do you think we developed mathematics in the first place?', through to extremely speculative ruminations on the existence of the universe (multiverse) as a purely mathematical entity"

"the reason for the close relationship is in fact utterly prosaic"

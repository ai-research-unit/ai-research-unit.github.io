What the Paper Does

This letter sketches a general formalism for classical field theories driven by stochastic noise. Any such equation with Gaussian noise is mapped into a field theory with a functional integral, and the one-loop physics is extracted. The noise two-point amplitude plays the role of $\hbar$ and counts loops. The authors define the effective action and derive a general formula for the one-loop effective potential with translation-invariant additive Gaussian noise, avoiding the auxiliary fields of the standard construction.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The scope

"Classical field theories coupled to stochastic noise provide an extremely powerful tool for modeling phenomena as diverse as turbulence, pattern-formation, and the structural development of the universe itself."

What the letter does

"In this Letter we sketch a general formalism that maps such systems into a field theory language, and demonstrate how to extract the one-loop physics for an arbitrary classical field theory coupled to Gaussian noise."

The loop-counting parameter

"The amplitude of the noise two-point function serves as the loop-counting parameter and is the analog of Planck's constant $\hbar$."

The results

"We define the effective action and the effective potential, and derive a general formula for the one-loop effective potential of a classical field theory coupled to translation-invariant additive Gaussian noise."

The equation treated

"Consider the general class of SPDEs"

$$D\varphi(\vec x,t)=F[\varphi(\vec x,t)]+\eta(\vec x,t).$$

"Here $D$ is any linear differential operator ... The function $\eta(\vec x,t)$ denotes the source of noise."

The averaging

"For any function $Q(\varphi)$ of the field $\varphi$ we can define the stochastic average, (over the noise), as"

$$\langle Q(\varphi)\rangle\equiv\int(\mathcal D\eta)\,P[\eta]\,Q(\varphi_{\mathrm{soln}}(\vec x,t|\eta)).\qquad (paper\ 2)$$

The method

"We will take this SPDE and analyze it using functional integral techniques. In this Letter we sketch out the field theory via the most direct route, avoiding the Martin–Siggia–Rose construction (with its extra unphysical conjugate fields used for book-keeping purposes)."

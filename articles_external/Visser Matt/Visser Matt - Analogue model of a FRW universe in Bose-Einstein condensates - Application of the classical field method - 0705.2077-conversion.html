What the Paper Does

Simulates cosmological particle creation in a two-dimensional homogeneous Bose–Einstein condensate whose scattering length is made time-dependent — the analogue of an expanding FRW universe. The method is the truncated Wigner approximation, a classical-field technique that samples the quantum state with stochastic initial conditions and evolves each sample by the Gross–Pitaevskii equation. Three expansion histories are studied: de Sitter, a smooth tanh interpolation, and a cyclic (oscillating) universe. For the smooth expansion the numerical quasiparticle spectrum matches the Bogoliubov prediction in the long-wavelength limit and yields a thermal spectrum with temperature $k_BT=1/(4\pi t_s)$, matching the standard cosmological result. Beyond the acoustic approximation the paper computes the sudden limit exactly and shows that a cyclic universe produces particles by parametric resonance alone, at $\omega(k)=\Omega/2$, with no net change of effective spacetime.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The purpose

"Analogue models of gravity have been motivated by the possibility of investigating phenomena not readily accessible in their cosmological counterparts. In this paper, we investigate the analogue of cosmological particle creation in a Friedmann–Robertson–Walker universe by numerically simulating a Bose–Einstein condensate with a time-dependent scattering length."

The method

The truncated Wigner approximation, "based on the Wigner representation of the density matrix", in which "a multimode quantum field is approximated by the trajectories of classical variables in phase space." Each trajectory is a Gross–Pitaevskii evolution; observables are averages over the ensemble.

The main positive result

"for various forms of the scaling function the particle production is consistent with the underlying theory in the long wavelength limit." And quantitatively: "the particle production approaches the usual cosmological result of a thermal spectrum for a sufficiently large expansion that proceeds sufficiently slowly", with

$$N_k\to\frac{1}{4\pi t_s\,\omega_k(0)},\qquad k_BT=\frac{1}{4\pi t_s}, \qquad (paper\ 122\text{–}124)$$

recovered from the low-$k$ limit of the Bose–Einstein distribution.

The trends

"$N_k$ is enhanced for faster expansions (small $t_s$) and larger expansions (large $X$). In the limit of a very fast expansion, the results approach the sudden result ... since the field does not evolve for a sufficiently fast change in the nonlinearity. Quasiparticle production is always suppressed below the sudden prediction." And "$N_k$ is larger for small momenta, since each mode is strongly coupled to the effective spacetime in this case. Alternatively stated, the field equation becomes adiabatic for large momenta, so that these modes are not strongly excited."

The acoustic-Compton boundary in the numerics

The plots mark the crossover explicitly: "the vertical solid red line shows the crossover from phonon to free-particle behaviour (as defined by $k_c/2=\sqrt{C_{NL}}$)". Above it the Bogoliubov analytic curve and the simulation diverge.

The cyclic case

"for the case of a cyclic universe, quasiparticle production is entirely attributed to parametric excitation of the modes as there is no net change to the effective spacetime. In particular, parametric resonance is observed for a mode satisfying $\omega(k)=\Omega/2$ where $\Omega$ is the driving frequency. The parametric resonance peak broadens in momentum space for larger driving amplitude $\Delta$."

The non-acoustic regime and its cost

"In general the usual calculations become unmanageable in this regime, and so, we present only two expansion scenarios here" — the sudden transition, where an exact solution exists, and the cyclic model, where general statements can be made.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A temperature that comes out of an expansion rate

The idea. $k_BT=1/(4\pi t_s)$: the temperature of the produced particles is set by the timescale of the expansion, with no other parameter. Slow expansion gives a thermal spectrum; fast expansion undershoots it.

2. The same phenomenon from two different causes

The idea. A sudden change of parameters produces particles by projection into a new basis. A cyclic change produces particles by parametric resonance with no net change of geometry. Observationally similar spectra, entirely different mechanisms.

Any inference from observed quanta to cosmological history has to exclude the parametric route first.

3. Simulation as a check on an analytic approximation

The idea. The Bogoliubov prediction and the truncated-Wigner simulation agree below the acoustic Compton scale and diverge above it, and the divergence is where the effective metric stops being valid — not a numerical error.

What the Paper Does

Above solar mass the stellar mass distribution is well agreed. Below it, the qualitative form is disputed. The author uses self-similarity from critical collapse as a template. He argues this fixes the shape of the subsolar distribution. The result is a universality claim: the form does not depend on the details of the collapse.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The method

"Self-similarity induced by critical gravitational collapse is used as a paradigm to probe the mass distribution of subsolar objects."

The agreement at high mass

"At large mass (solar mass and above) there is widespread agreement as to both the form and parameter values arising in the mass distribution of stellar objects."

The disagreement at low mass

"At subsolar mass there is still considerable disagreement as to the qualitative form of the mass distribution, let alone the specific parameter values chara[cterising it]"

The proposal

a universal power-law form follows from self-similarity

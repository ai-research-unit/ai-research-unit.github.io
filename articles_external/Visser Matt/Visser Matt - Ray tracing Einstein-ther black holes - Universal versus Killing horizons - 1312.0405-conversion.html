What the Paper Does

In Einstein–Æther theory, signals can travel faster than light, and a black hole then has two horizons. One is the Killing horizon of the æther; the other is a universal horizon that traps any speed. The authors trace rays through these spacetimes. They find evidence that Hawking radiation comes from the universal horizon. Rays that linger near the Killing horizon suggest reprocessing there. The work also resolves a mismatch between the surface gravity and a temperature found by the tunnelling method.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

an outer region "which traps excitations of arbitrarily high velocities"

The aim

"To better understand the nature of these black holes, and their Universal horizons, we study ray trajectories in these spacetimes."

The finding

"We find evidence that Hawking radiation is associated with the Universal horizon, while the 'lingering' of ray trajectories near the Killing horizon hints at reprocessing there."

The side result

"In doing this we solve an apparent discrepancy between the surface gravity of the Universal horizon and the associated temperature derived by tunneling method."

The outlook

"These results advance the understanding of these exotic horizons, and provide hints for a full understanding of [the radiation]"

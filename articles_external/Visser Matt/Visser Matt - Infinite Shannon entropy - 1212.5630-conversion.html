What the Paper Does

A normalised probability distribution can still have infinite Shannon entropy. The authors give conditions for this, several simple bounds, and asymptotic estimates. Infinite entropy requires infinitely many states of non-zero but arbitrarily small probability. A bound of the form $S \le -\ln p$ holds for a probability cutoff $p$, so the divergence is never worse than logarithmic. Large entropy cannot be localised; it needs exponentially many states.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The phenomenon

"Even if a probability distribution is properly normalizable, its associated Shannon (or von Neumann) entropy can easily be infinite."

The mechanism

"Roughly speaking, this happens when arbitrarily small amounts of probability are dispersed into an infinite number of states; we shall quantify this observation and make it precise."

The elementary bound

"If the total number of states is finite, $N=\sum_n 1<\infty$, then an elementary computation leads to the upper bound $S\le\ln N$. ... even if the total number of states is infinite ... as long the total number of states of non-zero probability is finite, $N'=\sum_{n:p_n>0}1<\infty$, an equally elementary computation leads to the upper bound $S\le\ln N'$."

The consequence for state counts

"These simple observations demonstrate that to obtain infinite Shannon entropy, an infinite number of states must have non-zero probability, in particular: $N'\ge\exp S$. But then, since the sum of the probabilities is unity, an infinite number of states must have non-zero but arbitrarily small probability."

A worked example

$$p_n=\frac{1}{\Sigma(u)}\frac{1}{n(\ln n)^{1+u}},\qquad \Sigma(u)=\sum_{n=\lceil e\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n(\ln n)^{1+u}} \qquad (paper\ 7)$$

"So this probability distribution is convergent but has infinite Shannon entropy over the entire range $u\in(0,1]$."

The logarithmic cutoff bound

$$S(p)\le -\ln p \qquad (paper\ 38)$$

"so in some sense the total Shannon entropy can never be worse than 'logarithmically divergent' in the probability cutoff."

The localisation statement

"The message to take from this logarithmic bound is that large Shannon entropies cannot be tightly localized in state space, large Shannon entropies must invariably come from exponentially large regions of the state space."

The stated scope

"We are for the time being interested in single-channel Shannon entropy in the information theoretic sense, not entropy in a stochastic field theory or QFT defined over some configuration space, on the grounds that this simple problem is a necessary precursor to understanding infinite entropy in a field theoretic context."

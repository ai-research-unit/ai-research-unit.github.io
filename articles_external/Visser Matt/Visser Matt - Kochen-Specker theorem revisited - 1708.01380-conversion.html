What the Paper Does

The Kochen–Specker theorem says that no consistent yes-no assignment can be made to quantum propositions in dimension three or more. The original proofs did it by exhibiting large finite sets of projectors. This paper gives a short geometric proof for any dimension. It restates the theorem as the non-existence of a valuation on the unit sphere that sums to one on every orthonormal triad, then derives a contradiction by a two-step "great circle descent". One-dimensional and two-dimensional cases are settled first.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The theorem

"demonstrating the impossibility of consistently assigning truth values to certain quantum propositions, thereby strongly implying the lack of any meaningful notion of 'quantum realism'."

The usual proofs

"The original proofs of the Kochen–Specker theorem proceeded via finding brute force counter-examples; often quite complicated and subtle (albeit mathematically 'elementary') counter-examples."

The goal

"We present herein yet another simplified geometrical proof of the Kochen–Specker theorem, one that is valid for any number of dimensions, that minimizes the technical machinery involved, and makes the seriousness of the issues raised manifest."

The reformulation

"For $d\ge 3$ there is no valuation $v(n):S^{d-1}\to\{0,1\}$, where $S^{d-1}$ is the unit hypersphere, such that $v(-n)=v(n)$ for all $n$ and $\sum_{i=1}^{d}v(n_i)=1$, for every basis (frame, $d$-bein) of orthogonal unit vectors $n_i$."

The three-dimensional lemma

"On any great circle in $S^2$, under the conditions given above, the valuation is either 50%–50% zero-one (as in two dimensions), or is 100% zero (identically zero)."

The method

"We shall do so with a modified and simplified 'descent' argument, one that requires only two steps in the descent process."

The point of a new proof

"Since one is trying to prove an inconsistency result, there will be an infinite number of ways of doing so; the question is whether one learns anything new by coming up with a different proof."

The significance

"there is increasing support that the notion of contextuality captures the essential difference between the quantum and classical world."

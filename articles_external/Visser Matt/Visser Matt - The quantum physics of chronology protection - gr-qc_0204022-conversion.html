What the Paper Does

This is a survey of Hawking's chronology protection conjecture. The author describes spacetimes with closed causal curves and the pathologies they bring. He reviews the proposed mechanisms that would forbid them. The conclusion is negative: no simple classical or semiclassical mechanism works, and a firm answer needs full quantum gravity. He also lists alternatives to protection.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"This is a brief survey of the current status of Stephen Hawking's 'chronology protection conjecture'. That is: 'Why does nature abhor a time machine?'"

The content

"I'll discuss a few examples of spacetimes containing 'time machines' (closed causal curves), the sorts of peculiarities that arise, and the reactions of the physics community."

The focus

"this article concentrates on the possibility of 'chronology protection'"

The verdict

"the hopes for a simple and universal classical or semiclassical mechanism leading to chronology protection seem to be dashed"

The prospect

"the relativity community is now faced with the daunting prospect of understanding full quantum gravity just to place notions of global causality on a firm footing"

The state of play

"There is ample evidence that quantum field theory is a good description of reality, and there is also ample e[vidence that general relativity is a good description of gravity]"

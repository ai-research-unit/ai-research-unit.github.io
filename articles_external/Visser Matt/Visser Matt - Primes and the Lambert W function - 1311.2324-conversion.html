What the Paper Does

The Lambert W function satisfies $W(x)e^{W(x)}=x$. The author uses it to rewrite bounds and asymptotics for the prime counting function and for the n-th prime. Upper bounds, lower bounds, and asymptotic forms all get closed expressions in terms of W. He notes that the familiar double logarithm terms appear as subleading terms of the W expansion. The paper is a change of viewpoint rather than a new theorem.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The function

"The Lambert W function, implicitly defined by $W(x)e^{W(x)}=x$, is a 'new' special function that has recently been the subject of an extended upsurge in interest and applications."

The claim

"In this note, I point out that the Lambert W function can also be used to gain a new perspective on the distribution of primes."

The judgement on the work

"While the calculations carried out above are very straightforward, almost trivial, it is perhaps the shift of viewpoint that is more interesting."

The unification

"The Lambert W function provides (in this context) a 'new' special function to work with, one which may serve to perhaps simplify and unify many otherwise disparate results."

The reason it fits

"the infamous '$\ln\ln x$' terms that infest the analytic theory of prime numbers will automatically appear as the sub-leading terms in asymptotic expansions of the Lambert W function."

What the Paper Does

Gravastars are a serious alternative to black holes. The authors study a generalised class with continuous pressure and no infinitely thin shells. Using the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation with negative central pressure, they show a gravastar cannot be a perfect fluid: the crust must be anisotropic. The anisotropic equation then bounds the required anisotropy in terms of compactness, and the resulting transverse stresses allow a compactness beyond the Buchdahl–Bondi limit. A perfect-fluid gravastar either becomes infinite or develops a naked singularity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The object

"One of the very small number of serious alternatives to the usual concept of an astrophysical black hole is the 'gravastar' model developed by Mazur and Mottola; and a related phase-transition model due to Laughlin et al."

The class studied

"We consider a generalized class of similar models that exhibit continuous pressure — without the presence of infinitesimally thin shells."

The result

" By considering the usual TOV equation for static solutions with negative central pressure, we find that gravastars cannot be perfect fluids — anisotropic pressures in the 'crust' of a gravastar-like object are unavoidable."

The bound

"The anisotropic TOV equation can then be used to bound the pressure anisotropy."

The compactness

"The transverse stresses that support a gravastar permit a higher compactness than is given by the Buchdahl–Bondi bound for perfect fluid stars."

What perfect fluids do

"Trying to build a perfect fluid gravastar results either in an infinite-size infinite-mass object, or in a naked singularity as the pressure exhibits a simple pole."

The anisotropy requirement

"Assuming the WEC, the magnitude of anisotropy required in the crust can be explicitly bounded in terms of the local compactness $2m(r)/r$, and becomes arbitrarily large for gravastars that are sufficiently close to forming a horizon, in which case the DEC must be violated."

The equation of state

"if one demands that the equation of state for gravastar matter results in configurations that are horizon-avoiding for large ranges of total mass and central pressure, then it is difficult to avoid the conclusion that the equation of state must depend on the local compactness $2m(r)/r$."

The remaining gap

"while this argument demonstrates that possible equations of state are at least conceivable, that is not the same as explicitly demonstrating that such equations of state actually exist. That is a challenge which we leave for the future."

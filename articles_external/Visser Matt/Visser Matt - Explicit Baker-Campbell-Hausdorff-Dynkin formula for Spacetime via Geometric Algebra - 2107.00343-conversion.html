What the Paper Does

The paper derives a compact formula for composing Lorentz transformations written as rotors in the spin representation. The logarithm of a product of two exponentials is expressed through hyperbolic tangents and a commutator, with a denominator containing the anticommutator. The formula holds in real Clifford algebras of dimension at most four and reduces to Rodrigues' formula for ordinary rotations. The authors apply it to compose relativistic three-velocities and read off the resulting boost and Wigner angle, noting that the spin representation makes the result simpler than earlier two-form versions.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"We present a compact Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff–Dynkin formula for the composition of Lorentz transformations $e^{\sigma_i}$ in the spin representation (a.k.a. Lorentz rotors) in terms of their generators $\sigma^i$:"

$$\ln(e^{\sigma_1}e^{\sigma_2})=\tanh^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\tanh\sigma_1+\tanh\sigma_2+\tfrac12[\tanh\sigma_1,\tanh\sigma_2]}{1+\tfrac12\{\tanh\sigma_1,\tanh\sigma_2\}}\right).$$

The scope

"This formula is general to geometric algebras (a.k.a. real Clifford algebras) of dimension $\le4$, naturally generalising Rodrigues' formula for rotations in $\mathbb{R}^3$."

The setting

"In particular, it applies to Lorentz rotors within the framework of Hestenes' spacetime algebra, and provides an efficient method for composing Lorentz generators."

The computation

"Computer implementations are possible with a complex $2\times2$ matrix representation realised by the Pauli spin matrices."

The application

"The formula is applied to the composition of relativistic 3-velocities yielding simple expressions for the resulting boost and the concomitant Wigner angle."

The conclusion

"In geometric algebras of dimension $p+q\le4$, orthogonal transformations $a\mapsto e^\sigma a e^{-\sigma}\in SO^+(p,q)$ may be composed in terms of their generators using the geometric BCHD formula (2.6)."

Why geometric algebra

"The benefit of adopting geometric algebra here is the utility of the double-cover spin representation: the geometric BCHD formula is simpler than previous results formulated in terms of 2-forms. It is sufficiently simple to be of theoretical use: it easily reduces to well-known formulae in lower dimensions, and yields the composition law for relativistic 3-velocities and the associated Wigner angle."

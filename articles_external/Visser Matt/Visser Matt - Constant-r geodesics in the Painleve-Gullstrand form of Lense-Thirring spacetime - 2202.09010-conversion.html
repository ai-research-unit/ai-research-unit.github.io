What the Paper Does

The paper studies geodesics that stay at constant Boyer–Lindquist radius in the Painlevé–Gullstrand form of the Lense–Thirring spacetime. The spacetime is stationary and axisymmetric but not spherically symmetric, so Birkhoff's theorem does not apply, yet shells of constant-$r$ geodesics still exist. The angular motion on those shells combines precession and nutation, and generically fills a band on the sphere rather than closing into a curve. The authors attribute the persistence of the shells to a non-trivial Killing tensor and its Carter constant, and note that the spacetime has the same slow-rotation weak-field limit as Kerr.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"We have explored the existence of and properties of the constant-$r$ ('quasi-circular') geodesics in the recently introduced Painlevé–Gullstrand variant of the Lense–Thirring spacetime."

The surprising feature

"We emphasize that although the underlying spacetime is not spherically symmetric, (only stationary and axisymmetric), so that the Birkhoff theorem does not apply, one nevertheless encounters (partial) spherical shells of constant-$r$ geodesics; notably this behaviour is not limited to the (exact) Kerr spacetime, but also persists in the Painlevé–Gullstrand variant of the Lense–Thirring spacetime."

The reason

"The persistence of existence of these constant-$r$ ('quasi-circular') geodesics is intimately related to the persistence of existence of a non-trivial Killing tensor and the associated Carter constant."

The angular motion

"Regarding the angular motion, these constant-$r$ geodesics exhibit both precession and nutation — typically with inc[lining orbits]; this constant-$r$ geodesic motion, though integrable, is generically surface-filling, with the orbits covering a [two-dimensional] band which is a segment of a spherical surface, and whose latitudinal extent is governed by delicate [relations among the] angular momentum and the Carter constant."

The comparison with Kerr

"The situation [is analogous] to that for the (exact) Kerr spacetime — but we n[ote that this spacetime has] the same slow-rotation weak-field limit as [Kerr]."

The overall assessment

"Overall, we see that the Painlevé–Gullstrand variant of the Lense–Thirring spacetime exhibits many useful and interesting properties, and is well-adapted to direct confrontation with observational astrophysics."

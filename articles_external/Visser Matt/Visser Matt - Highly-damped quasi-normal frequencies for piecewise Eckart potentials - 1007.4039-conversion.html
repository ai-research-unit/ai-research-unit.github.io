What the Paper Does

Highly damped quasi-normal frequencies often take the form $\omega_n = (\text{offset}) + in(\text{gap})$. The authors test how general this is using a piecewise Eckart, or Pöschl–Teller, potential, for which an analytic quantisation condition can be derived. They find the linear-in-$n$ behaviour is generic but not universal. The controlling feature is whether the ratio of the two exponential falloff rates is rational. A one-sided power law falloff behaves like the limit of infinite falloff rate on the other side.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The pattern

"Highly-damped quasi-normal frequencies are very often of the form $\omega_n = (\text{offset}) + in(\text{gap})$."

The test

"We investigate the genericity of this phenomenon by considering a model potential that is piecewise Eckart (piecewise Pöschl–Teller), and developing an analytic 'quantization condition' for the highly-damped quasi-normal frequencies."

The result

"We find that this $\omega_n = (\text{offset}) + in(\text{gap})$ behaviour is generic but not universal, with the controlling feature being whether or not the ratio of the rates of exponential falloff in the two asymptotic directions is a rational number."

The one-sided case

"This observation is useful in that it indicates that one-sided exponential falloff can be treated via a minor variant of the analysis in [39], and a power law falloff in the potential exhibits behaviour qualitatively similar to the limit $b_+\to\infty$."

The limit value

"in this particular situation with a one-sided exponential falloff one asymptotically has $\omega_n = in/b_-$."

Independence from black holes

"most of the analysis is largely independent of black hole physics, and depends only on the falloff conditions placed on the potential near asymptotic infinity"

The mathematical by-product

"it is already of considerable mathematical and physical interest that we have a nontrivial extension of the Eckart potential for which the QNFs are asymptotically exactly solvable"

The stated scope limits

"We should therefore expect the results of our semi-analytic model to be qualitatively (but not necessarily quantitatively) accurate for estimating the asymptotic QNFs of 'realistic' black holes. Because of the way the asymptotic QNF condition was derived, we do not expect our model to give good results for low-lying QNFs."

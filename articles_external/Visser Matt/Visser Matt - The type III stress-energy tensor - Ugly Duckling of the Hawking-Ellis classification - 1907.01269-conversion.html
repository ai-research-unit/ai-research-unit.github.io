What the Paper Does

The Hawking–Ellis classification sorts stress-energy tensors into four types. Types I and II appear classically, type IV appears in semiclassical gravity, and type III is the awkward case. The authors isolate type III$_0$, the essential core of type III. Using gyratons as known examples, they generalise the geometry such a tensor generates. The resulting spacetimes are Kundt metrics.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The classification

"tensors as per the Hawking–Ellis classification"

The distribution of types

"Type I and type II naturally appear in classical situations, and can also describe semiclassical effects. Type IV often shows up in semiclassical gravity. Type III is much more subtle."

The focus

"We focus our attention on type III$_0$ stress-energy tensors, which capture the essence ('essential core') of type III."

The examples

"Reflecting on known purely phenomenological examples, ('gyratons'), we are able to generalize the geometry generated by those type III$_0$ stress-ener[gy tensors]"

The resulting geometry

"we only need $\partial_r g_{ij}=0$ to have a Kundt spacetime"

$$\theta_{ij}=\nabla_i\ell_j=\tfrac12\partial_r g_{ij},\qquad \omega_{ij}=\nabla_{[i}\ell_{j]}=0,\qquad \sigma_{ij}=\theta_{ij}-\tfrac13\theta\,h_{ij}\qquad (paper\ A.6)$$

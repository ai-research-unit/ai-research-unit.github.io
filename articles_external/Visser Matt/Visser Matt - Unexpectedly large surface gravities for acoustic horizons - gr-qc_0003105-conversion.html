What the Paper Does

Acoustic horizons in fluids have surface gravities, and hence Hawking temperatures. The authors ask how large these can be. They set regularity conditions at ergo-surfaces and at horizons. Then they solve the spherically symmetric stationary case. They find that the surface gravity can be much larger than naive estimates suggest, which matters for detecting the effect.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"Acoustic black holes are fluid dynamic analogs of general relativistic black holes, wherein the behaviour of sound waves in a moving fluid acts as an analog for scalar fields propagating in a gravitational background."

The key structure

"Acoustic horizons, which are intimately related to regions where the speed of the fluid flow exceeds the local speed of sound, possess many of the properties more normally associated with [black holes]"

The method

"Regularity conditions at ergo-surfaces", "Regularity conditions at horizons"

The case studied

"Spherically symmetric stationary flow", "Constant speed of sound"

The answer

the surface gravity can be unexpectedly large

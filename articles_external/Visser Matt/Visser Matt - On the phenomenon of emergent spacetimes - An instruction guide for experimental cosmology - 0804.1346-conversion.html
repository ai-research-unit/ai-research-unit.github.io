What the Paper Does

The authors ask whether a Bose–Einstein condensate can imitate an expanding universe. They present a toy model in which spacetime geometry emerges from the condensate's long-wavelength modes. They map the condensate parameters onto cosmological quantities and show which regime resembles inflation. They also give an instruction list for setting up such an experiment. The conclusion is candid: the analogy works only in a narrow band and does not amount to a theory of quantum gravity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The model

"We present a toy model where spacetime is emergent from a more fundamental [condensed-matter system]"

The regime

"The viable regime, where the analogy is sufficiently good to mimic the behavior of quantum modes during an inflationary epoch, is rather narrow."

The honesty about scope

"While we are able to connect a certain parameter regime of the Bose–Einstein condensate model with semi-classical quantum gravity there is no obvious evidence for its significance for 'full' quantum gravity."

The reason

"Any sensible theory of quantum gravity has to accommodate dynamics for the gravitational field which, in the appropriate limit, should reduce to those of general relativity"

The verdict

"Therefore the analogue model investigated above is not a suitable candidate for 'full' quantum gravity."

The saving grace

"the breakdown of the analogy is to a very good extent uncorrelated with the applicability" of the model to the intended cosmological questions

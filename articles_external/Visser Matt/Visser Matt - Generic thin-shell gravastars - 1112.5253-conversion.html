What the Paper Does

This paper builds thin-shell gravastars by cutting and pasting spherically symmetric spacetimes. It treats the internal physics of the transition layer and its interaction with the bulk, including forces from outside. Both static and dynamic cases are covered, with bounded-excursion configurations and a stability analysis of static ones. The authors stress that gravastars differ from wormholes by a few sign flips, which allow a vanishing surface energy density with nonzero surface tension. The companion paper on thin-shell wormholes is the comparison point.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The construction

"We construct generic spherically symmetric thin-shell gravastars by using the cut-and-paste procedure. We take considerable effort to make the analysis as general and unified as practicable"

The scope

"investigating both the internal physics of the transition layer and its interaction with 'external forces' arising due to interactions between the transition layer and the bulk spacetime. Furthermore, we discuss both the dynamic and static situations."

The dynamical configurations

"we consider 'bounded excursion' dynamical configurations, and probe the stability of static configurations."

The distinctive feature

"For gravastars there is always a particularly compelling configuration in which the surface energy density is zero, while surface tension is nonzero."

The relation to wormholes

"We have built on a companion paper, which analyzed thin-shell traversable wormholes. We emphasize that mathematically there are a few strategic sign flips, which physically implies significant changes in the analysis."

Where the difference comes from

"Due to the key sign flips, ultimately arising from the definition of the normals on the junction interface, the surface energy density is always negative for the thin-shell traversable wormholes considered in [15]."

The summary

"we have developed an extremely general and robust framework leading to the linearized stability analysis of dynamical spherically symmetric thin-shell gravastars, a framework that can quickly be adapted to wide classes of generic thin-shells."

What the Paper Does

This is the journal version of the argument that the strong energy condition fails late in cosmic history. Working in a Friedmann–Robertson–Walker geometry with only the Einstein equations and the energy conditions, the paper derives bounds on the density and the look-back time as functions of redshift. The bounds do not require a density parameter or an equation of state. Combining them with the Hubble parameter, the age of the oldest halo stars, and their formation redshift shows the condition is violated somewhere below a temperature of 60 K. The conclusion is that no combination of normal matter fits the data, so the cosmological fluid needs something drastic, such as a cosmological constant.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The role of energy conditions

"The energy conditions of Einstein gravity (classical general relativity) place restrictions on the stress-energy tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ (energy-momentum tensor). ... The energy conditions force various linear combinations of these quantities to be positive and have been used, for instance, to derive many theorems of classical general relativity—such as the singularity theorems; the area increase theorem for black holes, and the positive mass theorem—without the need to assume a specific equation of state."

The claim

"Current observations seem to indicate that the strong energy condition (SEC) is violated rather late in the life of the universe—somewhere between galaxy formation and the present time, in an epoch where the cosmological temperature never exceeds 60 K."

The independence from curvature

"The bounds I derive from the SEC are independent of whether or not the universe is open, flat, or closed—which means that the density parameter ... does not have to be specified. Thus my approach is independent of the existence or nonexistence of any of the standard variants of cosmological inflation."

The minimal inputs

"The experimental observations I need are the present day value of the Hubble parameter $H_0$, an age estimate for the age of the oldest stars in the galactic halo, and an estimate for the red-shift at which these oldest stars formed."

The outcome

"This implies that no possible combination of 'normal' matter is capable of fitting the observational data, and one needs to do something drastic to the cosmological fluid, either introduce a cosmological constant $\Lambda$, or have a very non-standard weak form of cosmological inflation that persists right up to galaxy formation."

Where violations are not expected

"General relativists and particle physicists would be surprised if large violations of the classical energy conditions occur at temperatures significantly below the Planck scale."

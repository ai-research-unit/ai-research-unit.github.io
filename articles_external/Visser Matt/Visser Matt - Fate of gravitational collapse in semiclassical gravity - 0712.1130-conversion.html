What the Paper Does

The paper re-examines whether a trapping horizon really forms when collapse is treated semiclassically. It shows that trans-Planckian modes are excited just before horizon formation, so the outcome depends on assumptions about physics above the Planck scale. If collapse slows down and approaches the horizon exponentially, vacuum polarisation grows and pushes the horizon further away, producing a horizonless object that still radiates a Planckian flux. The authors call these quasi-black holes and argue they mimic black hole physics while avoiding singularities, information loss, and the endpoint problem. They state they have no definite proposal for the final state of evaporation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The open problem

"While the outcome of gravitational collapse in classical general relativity is unquestionably a black hole, up to now no full and complete semiclassical description of black hole formation has been thoroughly investigated."

The claim

"By analyzing how semiclassical collapse proceeds we show that the very formation of a trapping horizon can be seriously questioned for a large set of, possibly realistic, scenarios."

What is not being claimed

"the theoretical framework of semiclassical gravity certainly allows the formation of trapping horizons. What we are questioning here is the more subtle point of whether or not the standard black hole picture is appropriate for describing the end point of realistic collapse."

The consequence

"if semiclassical physics were in some cases to prevent formation of the trapping horizon, then this suggests the possibility of new collapsed objects which can be much less problematic, making it unnecessary to confront the information paradox or the run-away end point problem."

The dependence on unknown physics

"before forming a trapping horizon, trans–Planckian modes are excited. Hence, whether the trapping horizon forms or not depends critically on assumptions concerning the net effect of any trans–Planckian physics that might be at work."

The fast cases

"Assuming that quantum field theory holds unmodified up to arbitrarily high energy ... there can be large deviations from classical collapse scenarios, if the latter do allow in the first place a piling up of vacuum energy. Most of the classical collapse scenarios so far considered do not allow for such a piling up, due to their intrinsic rapidity."

The new class of objects

"If this is indeed the case one then would have to add a new class of compact, horizonless, objects (possibly the most compact objects apart from black holes themselves) to the astrophysical bestiary: the black stars."

The stated limit

"We do not yet have a definitive proposal as to the end-point of the evaporation process. This could only be achieved by understanding the physics of baryon nucleation in the presence of high-density states of matter."

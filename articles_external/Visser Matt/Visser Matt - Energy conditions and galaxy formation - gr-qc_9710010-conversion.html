What the Paper Does

The paper uses the strong energy condition to bound the look-back time in a Friedmann–Robertson–Walker universe, and then turns the bound into a constraint on the Hubble parameter. The inputs are minimal: the present Hubble parameter, the age of the oldest halo stars, and the redshift at which they formed. From these it concludes that the strong energy condition must be violated between galaxy formation and the present, in an epoch where the cosmological temperature never exceeds 60 Kelvin. The argument is model independent as far as possible, and its consequence is that the age problem cannot be fixed by adjusting the equation of state of normal matter.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The purpose of energy conditions

"The energy conditions of Einstein gravity are designed to side-step, as much as possible, the need to pin down a particular equation of state."

The observational claim

"Observation seems to indicate that the SEC is violated rather late in the life of the universe—somewhere between galaxy formation and the present time, in an epoch where the cosmological temperature never exceeds 60 Kelvin."

The method

"I show this by using the SEC to develop a simple and robust bound for the look-back time as a function of red-shift in Friedmann–Robertson–Walker (FRW) cosmologies."

The inputs

"The experimental observations I need are the present day value of the Hubble parameter $H_0$, an age estimate for the age of the oldest stars in the galactic halo, and an estimate for the red-shift at which these oldest stars formed."

The energy condition in FRW

$$\text{SEC}\iff(\rho+3p\ge0)\ \text{and}\ (\rho+p\ge0).\qquad (paper\ 3)$$

"Consider the quantity"

$$\rho+3p=-\frac{3}{4\pi G}\frac{\ddot a}{a}.\qquad (paper\ 4)$$

"Thus"

$$\text{SEC}\Rightarrow\ddot a<0.\qquad (paper\ 5)$$

The look-back bound

"Since the SEC implies that the expansion is decelerating, we see"

$$\text{SEC}\Rightarrow\tau(a;a_0)=|t-t_0|\le\frac{1}{H_0}\frac{a_0-a}{a_0}=\frac{1}{H_0}\frac{z}{1+z}.\qquad (paper\ 7)$$

The conclusion

"This analysis of the age-of-the-universe problem is, as far as possible, model independent. I have shown that high values of the Hubble parameter imply that the SEC must be violated sometime between the epoch of galaxy formation and the present. This implies that the age-of-the-universe problem cannot simply be fixed by adjusting the equation of state of the cosmological fluid."

The strongest statement

"Since all normal matter satisfies the SEC, fixing the age-of-universe problem will inescapably require the introduction of 'abnormal' matter—indeed we will need large quantities of abnormal matter, sufficient to overwhelm the gravitational effects of the normal matter."

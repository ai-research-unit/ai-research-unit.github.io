What the Paper Does

Regular black holes usually have a de Sitter core. The authors build "hollow" versions whose core is asymptotically Minkowski. The metric uses exponentials and the Lambert W function instead of high-order polynomials. They compute the curvature invariants, the surface gravity, the temperature, and the stress-energy. Then they check the energy conditions. The core avoids a throat but violates the tangential null energy condition.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The model

"Standard models of 'regular black holes' typically have asymptotically de Sitter regions at their cores. Herein we shall consider novel 'hollow' regular black holes, those with asymptotically Minkowski cores."

Why

"First, these models greatly simplify the physics in the deep core, and second, one can trade off rather messy cubic and quartic polynomial equations for somewhat more elegant special functions such as exponentials and the increasingly important Lambert W function"

What is shared

"these 'hollow' regular black holes share many features with the Bardeen/Hayward/Frolov regular blac[k holes]"

The energy conditions

"The model satisfies the radial NEC, (there is no wormhole throat), but violates the tangential NEC in the deep core when $r<a^4$."

The core

"the exponential expression present in the metric implies that the regular black hole has an asymptotically Minkowski core as $r\to0$"

The curvature

"The curvature of the geometry is asymptotically flat at infinity, at the core, and has some maximal peak in between."

What the Paper Does

Infrared observations of Sagittarius A* and M87* show no sign that the sources have physical surfaces in thermal equilibrium with their surroundings. The paper turns that absence into a constraint. It parameterises the interaction between a horizonless object and its accretion flow by an absorption coefficient and a re-emission timescale, and shows that temporary absorption is required for the object to adapt to its environment without forming a horizon. Because the thermalisation time can grow without bound for compact objects, the absence of equilibrium rules out the models whose thermalisation time is short enough for equilibrium to be expected.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The observation used

"Infrared observations of Sgr A* and M87* [show no evidence that these] sources have physical surfaces in thermal [equilibrium with their environment]."

What the paper supplies

"In this paper we discuss a general parametrizatio[n of the interaction between a horizonless object and its accretion flow, which] permits [one] to quantify how close a horizonless [object is to equilibrium, through] the timescale in which its surface can th[ermalize]. [This timescale is] unbounded, growing large for objects that [are compact and effectively] infinite for the latter."

The physical requirement

"Temporary absorption is necessary for the horizonless object to be [able to] adapt dynamically to its environment and eventually be able to reach a steady state. This is in particular necessary to avoid the formation of horizons. Hence, a non-zero value of $\tilde\kappa$ seems to be unavoidable based on known physics."

The second parameter

"The second parameter necessary to describe temporary absorption is the re-emission timescale, which we have parametrized in terms of $N$. We have shown that this parameter has an important impact on the thermalization timescale and that it can become arbitrarily large, preventing the [latter] from happening altogether for relatively compact horizonless objects."

The constraint

"that equilibrium is not observed in systems such as Sgr A* and M87* can be used to place constraints on horizonless objects, ruling out models in which this thermalization timescale is short enough so that expecting equilibrium is reasonable."

The general point

"Modeling the interactions between horizonless objects and their accreting environments is essential to cast constraints on these alternatives to black holes."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A surface needs a response time

The idea. An object without a horizon must have a surface, and a surface that interacts must have a timescale. Without that timescale the object cannot come to equilibrium.

What it would add. articles_physics/horizons-without-a-global-structure.md contrasts objects with and without horizons and treats the difference as structural. This paper shows the contrast has a dynamical cost: the horizonless branch must respond, and the response is measurable in principle. The article's comparison of the two branches is incomplete without it.

What the Paper Does

Penrose's theorem says a trapped region brings a singularity with it. If quantum gravity removes singularities, something in the theorem must fail. This chapter sets out the minimum requirements for a singularity-free collapse. It then classifies the regular spacetimes that can result, without committing to a specific quantum gravity. The relation between regularity and the number of horizons is worked out in detail.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The theorem

"Penrose's singularity theorem implies that if a trapped region forms in a gravitational collapse, then a singularity must form as well within such region."

The expectation

"it is widely expected that singularities should be generically avoided by quantum gravitational effects"

The programme

"we shall explore both the minimum requirements to avoid singularities in a gravitational collapse as well as discuss, without relying on a specific quantum gravity model, the possible regular spacetimes associated to such regularization of the spacetime fabric"

The theme

"we shall expose the intimate and quite subtle relationship between regul[arity and horizons]"

The counting

"This will depend on how many non-extremal horizons merge to yield the extremal horizon of interest."

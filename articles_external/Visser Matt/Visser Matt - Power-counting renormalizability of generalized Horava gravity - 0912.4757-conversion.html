What the Paper Does

An earlier article argued that scalar theories with two time derivatives and many space derivatives are perturbatively finite. This note extends the counting to gravity. The author recalls how the superficial degree of divergence is read from one-particle-irreducible diagrams. He then checks whether the same bookkeeping survives when the metric is dynamical. The answer is a power-counting statement, not a proof of renormalizability.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The earlier argument

"In reference [1] I argued for the perturbative power-counting finiteness of the normal-ordered $:P(\phi)^z_{d+1}:$ scalar quantum field theories in $(d+1)$ dimensions"

The defining feature

"the central defining feature of these quantum field theories is that the kinetic term in the Lagrangian contains exactly two time derivatives and up to $2d=2z$ space derivatives."

The method

"To analyze the superficial degree of divergence one need only consider the one-particle-irreducible (1PI) sub-diagrams of the Feynman diagram."

The extension

"the potential benefits of allowing Lorentz symmetry breaking" are examined for generalized Hořava gravity

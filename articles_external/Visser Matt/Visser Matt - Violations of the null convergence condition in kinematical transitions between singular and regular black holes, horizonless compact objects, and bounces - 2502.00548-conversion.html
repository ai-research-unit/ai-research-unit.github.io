What the Paper Does

Regular black holes evade the Penrose theorem. Some models satisfy the null convergence condition everywhere, which looks contradictory. The authors resolve the puzzle: the theorem needs global assumptions and says nothing about where focussing fails. They then ask where the condition must be violated during a transition between singular and regular configurations. The answer depends on the type of transition.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The puzzle

"How do regular black holes evade the Penrose singularity theorem? Various models of stationary regular black holes globally satisfy the null convergence condition (NCC)."

The apparent contradiction

"At first glance this might seem puzzling, as the NCC must generically be violated to avoid the focusing point implied by the Penrose theorem."

The resolution

"In fact, the Penrose singularity theorem depends on subtle global assumptions and does not provide information about where and when the [focussing fails]"

The extension

transitions between singular black holes, regular black holes, horizonless objects, and bounces

What the Paper Does

A Bose–Einstein condensate flowing supersonically through a Laval nozzle can have an acoustic horizon. The authors ask which settings would make the Hawking signal observable. They compute the temperature for a quasi-one-dimensional flow. One plausible configuration gives about $70$ nK, against a condensation temperature of comparable size. They also explain why the effect is hard to detect: the peak wavelength is of order the apparatus size.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"In this note we consider quasi-one-dimensional supersonic flow of a Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC) in a Laval nozzle (converging-diverging nozzle)"

The purpose

"with a view to finding which experimental settings could magnify this effect and provide an observable signal"

The result

"We identify an experimentally plausible configuration with a Hawking temperature of order 70 nK; to be contrasted with a condensation temperatu[re]"

The difficulty

"It is the fact that the peak wavelength of the Hawking radiation is of order the physical dimensions of the system under consideration that makes the effect so difficult to detect."

The numbers

"In particular, in BECs it is common to have a sound speed of order 6 mm/s."

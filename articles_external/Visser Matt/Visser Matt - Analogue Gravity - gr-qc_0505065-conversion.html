What the Paper Does

The reference review of the whole analogue-gravity programme, in its fourth revision. It sets out the simplest example — sound in a moving fluid — in full, then walks the history in four periods (pre-1981, 1981–2002, 2003–2008, 2008–2016, 2017–2024), catalogues the models by kind (classical and quantum), gathers the phenomenological applications (Hawking radiation, horizon stability, superradiance, cosmological particle production, Bose novae, cloaking), surveys the experimental efforts, and closes with the question the programme raises: what hints does it give towards quantum gravity? The recurring thesis is that the analogy's imperfections are its value, and the closing chapter is a list of theorems standing in the way of treating an analogue spacetime as a genuine emergent gravity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The aim

"Analogue gravity is a research programme that explores analogues of general relativistic gravitational fields within other physical systems, particularly but not exclusively in condensed matter systems, with the aim of gaining new insights into related problems. Analogue models of gravity boast a long and distinguished history, dating back to the early years of general relativity."

The community claim

"What used to be mainly a community of researchers with a gravitation theory background, has now become a multi-faceted community in which experimentalists and theoreticians from both the GR and condensed matter communities meet. We feel this is now the defining trait of Analogue Gravity, as it represents one of the few fields of modern physics where the general trend towards specialization and narrowing of communities has been successfully reverted."

Why imperfection is the point

"the fact that analogue models are not perfect mimics of the standard phenomena (e.g. because of the breakdown of Lorentz invariance in the UV) has proven to be a strength, rather than a weakness: These new features allowed one to break degeneracies, and exposed subtleties of these phenomena, so greatly enhancing our understanding of them."

What has been learned

"We nowadays have a much better understanding of the nature of Hawking radiation, and how and why it can be robust against the UV structure of spacetime. Furthermore, we have been able to reproduce it experimentally, together with several other phenomena (from cosmological particle creation to superradiance) which are pillars of modern gravitation theory."

The surface-gravity ambiguity

"Already at the level of time-dependent systems in standard general relativity there are two reasonably natural definitions of surface gravity, one in terms of the inaffinity of null geodesics skimming along the event horizon, and another in terms of the peeling properties of those null geodesics that escape the black hole to reach future null infinity. It is this latter definition that is relevant for Hawking-like fluxes from non-stationary systems (e.g., evaporating black holes) and in such systems it never coincides with the inaffinity-based definition ... except possibly at asymptotic future-timelike infinity."

The obstruction theorems

The review devotes chapter 7 to the reasons an analogue cannot simply be gravity. Marolf's theorem: "Consider any limit where the effective description of a local theory is a gravitational theory with universal coupling to energy, the same notion of time evolution, and a compatible definition of locality. In this limit, all local observables away from the boundary become independent of time." The review adds that this is a refinement of Weinberg–Witten, and that its notion of locality is "kinematic locality", a microcausality condition.

The scope of the review

Eight chapters: acoustic example, history, catalogue of classical and quantum models, phenomenological applications, experiments, hints toward quantum gravity, conclusions. The bibliography runs to roughly 1,900 entries.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Two surface gravities that do not agree

The idea. For a horizon that is not a Killing horizon there are two natural surface gravities — the inaffinity of horizon-skimming null geodesics, and the peeling rate of escaping ones — and for an evolving horizon they differ everywhere except at future timelike infinity. The Hawking flux follows the second.

What it would add. The decisive fact for articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md. That article uses $T=\kappa/2\pi$ with $\kappa$ from $\nabla_\xi\xi=\kappa\xi$, which is the inaffinity definition and presupposes a Killing field. The review says that definition is the wrong one for any horizon that evolves, which is every horizon that radiates. The corpus's temperature is therefore correct only in a stationary limit it never declares, and the correction is not a refinement — it is a different quantity.

Where it lands. articles_physics/black-hole-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md, the temperature section, as a stated scope limit.

What stands in the way. The peeling definition needs future null infinity and a congruence reaching it. The corpus has no infinity; articles_physics/horizons-without-a-global-structure.md already records this gap, and this is the sharpest consequence of it.

2. An obstruction theorem for emergent gravity

The idea. Marolf's theorem: if a local theory's effective description is a gravitational theory with universal coupling to energy, compatible locality, and a shared time evolution, then all local observables away from the boundary are time-independent. An analogue that reproduces gravity must therefore be kinematically non-local, or must change its notion of locality abruptly.

What it would add. A hard constraint on any emergence claim, and the corpus has emergence claims. articles_physics/what-the-biquaternion-algebra-cannot-do-a-catalogue-of-algebraic-obstructions.md is exactly a catalogue of this kind of result, and this theorem belongs in it with its hypotheses stated: universal coupling, shared time, compatible locality. That the hypotheses are three, and each independently violable, is what makes the theorem useful rather than fatal.

Where it lands. articles_physics/what-the-biquaternion-algebra-cannot-do-a-catalogue-of-algebraic-obstructions.md, as a new entry.

What stands in the way. Nothing blocks the import; it is a theorem to be listed. Ranked first, because the catalogue already exists and this entry is missing from it.

3. Degeneracy breaking as the reason to study imperfect models

The idea. An analogue that differs from gravity in a controlled way is more informative than one that matches, because the difference separates effects that coincide in the exact case.

What it would add. The corpus's articles_physics/the-empirical-status-of-the-biquaternion-framework.md asks what could distinguish the framework from its rivals. The review supplies a general method: find a model that agrees in one limit and differs in another, then use the difference as a discriminator. That is a strategy the corpus has not tried and could.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-empirical-status-of-the-biquaternion-framework.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing blocks the import; it is a methodological transfer. Ranked second.

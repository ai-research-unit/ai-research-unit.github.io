What the Paper Does

The authors build regular spacetimes whose area radius never reaches zero. The throat can be timelike, spacelike, or null, giving a wormhole, a bounce into a future universe, or a one-way wormhole. They state regularity conditions, then construct examples from a Fan–Wang type mass function. These include the Simpson–Visser model, a Bardeen-type model, and more involved geometries with two or more horizons and an extremal case. They prove a general theorem on static regularity and another on when the classical energy conditions fail.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The class

"We develop a number of novel 'black-bounce' spacetimes. These are specific regular black holes where the 'area radius' always remains non-zero, thereby leading to a 'throat' that is either timelike (corresponding to a traversable wormhole), spacelike (corresponding to a 'bounce' into a future universe), or null (corresponding to a 'one-way wormhole')."

The method

"We shall first perform a general analysis of the regularity conditions for such a spacetime, and then consider a number of specific examples."

The models

"The examples are constructed using a mass function similar to that of Fan–Wang, and fall into several particular cases, such as the original Simpson–Visser model, a Bardeen-type model, and other generalizations thereof."

The analysis

"We shall analyse the regularity, the energy conditions, and the causal structure of these models."

The new geometries

"The main results are several new geometries, more complex than before, with two or more horizons, with the possibility of an extremal case."

The theorems

"We shall derive a general theorem regarding static space-time regularity, and another general theorem regarding (non)-satisfaction of the classical energy conditions."

The mass function

"the 'mass function' $M(r)$ defined in (23) is not the energy contained within a surface of radius $r$; we now see that it is the Hernandez–Misner–Sharp quasi-local mass $M_{\mathrm{HMS}}(r)$ that plays this role."

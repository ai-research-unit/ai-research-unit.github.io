What the Paper Does

An analogue spacetime can be built from a material medium, and the consistency conditions for doing so are known, but the metric that results once they are satisfied is not usually written down. The paper works in fully covariant language rather than the usual three-plus-one or six-dimensional forms, which lets it extend the consistency conditions to curved backgrounds. It identifies permittivity, inverse permeability, and the magneto-electric tensor as the three pieces of the Bel decomposition of the constitutive tensor, and then reconstructs the effective metric explicitly using the Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse. A key conclusion is that the fully covariant treatment gives no additional physical results.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The two-way question

"Using electromagnetism to study analogue space-times is tantamount to considering consistency conditions for when a given (meta-)material would provide an analogue space-time model or — vice versa — characterizing which given metric could be modelled with a (meta-)material."

The gap

"While the consistency conditions themselves are by now well known and studied, the form the metric takes once they are satisfied is not."

The method

"While fully covariant formulations of the electrodynamics of media have been around for a long while, they are usually abandoned for (3+1)- or 6-dimensional formalisms. Here we shall use the fully unified and fully covariant approach. This enables us even to generalize the consistency conditions for the existence of an effective metric to arbitrary background metrics beyond flat space-time electrodynamics."

The identification

"we also show how the familiar matrices for permittivity $\epsilon$, permeability $\mu^{-1}$, and magneto-electric effects $\zeta$ can be seen as the three independent pieces of the Bel decomposition for the constitutive tensor $Z^{abcd}$, i.e., the components of an orthogonal decomposition with respect to a given observer with four-velocity $V^a$."

The reconstruction

"we shall use the Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse and the closely related pseudo-determinant to then gain the desired reconstruction of the effective metric in terms of the permittivity tensor $\epsilon^{ab}$, the permeability tensor $\mu^{-1}{}^{ab}$, and the magneto-electric tensor $\zeta^{ab}$, as an explicit function $g_{\mathrm{eff}}(\epsilon,\mu^{-1},\zeta)$."

The conclusion

"we have seen that even going to generally covariant formulations of an effective metric given by macroscopic electrodynamics gives no additional physical results: There will always be a natural reference frame for a given medium such that in this frame the consistency condition reduces to the well-known result"

$$\epsilon^{ij}=\mu^{ij}.\qquad (paper\ 70)$$

The limit

"It remains to be seen how far-reaching or maybe even limiting this result proves to be."

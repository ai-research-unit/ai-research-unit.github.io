What the Paper Does

The authors model a horizon that exchanges matter and radiation with its surroundings. Fluxes of null and timelike particles cross it in both directions. They ask how the horizon area responds, and give simple formulae for growth and shrinkage. To keep the algebra manageable they assume spherical symmetry and use a particular slicing. The treatment is pedagogical: the aim is a clear set of equations for a dynamical horizon.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The model

"We consider a simple physical model for an evolving horizon that is strongly interacting with its environment, exchanging arbitrarily large quantities of matter with its environment in the form of both infalling material and outgoing Hawking radiation."

The question

"We permit fluxes of both lightlike and timelike particles to cross the horizon, and ask how the horizon grows and shrinks in response to such flows."

The style

"We place a premium on providing a clear and straightforward exposition with simple formulae."

The restrictions

"we make one significant physical restriction — that of spherical symmetry — and two technical mathematical restrictions"

The slicing choice

"We choose to slice the spacetime in such a way that the space-time foliations (and hence the horizons [are well behaved])"

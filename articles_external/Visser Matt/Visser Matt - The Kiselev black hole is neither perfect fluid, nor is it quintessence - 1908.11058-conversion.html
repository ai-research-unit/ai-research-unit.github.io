What the Paper Does

The Kiselev metric is a popular toy model, cited hundreds of times. It is usually described as a black hole surrounded by quintessence, or by a perfect fluid. The author computes the pressure anisotropy and the average pressure. They show the fluid is not perfect and the equation of state is not quintessence. He then extends the model and comments on its use of the Rastall framework.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The metric

$$ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}-\frac{K}{r^{1+3w}}\right)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{1-\frac{2m}{r}-\frac{K}{r^{1+3w}}}+r^2d\Omega_2^2\qquad (paper\ 1)$$

The status

"is an extremely popular toy model, with over 200 direct and indirect citations as of 2019"

The finding

"Unfortunately, despite repeated assertions to the contrary, this is not a perfect fluid spacetime."

The measure

"The relative pressure anisotropy and average pressure are easily calculated"

$$\Delta=\frac{p_r-p_t}{\bar p},\qquad \bar p=\frac{p_r+2p_t}{3},\qquad \Delta=-\frac{3(1+w)}{1}\qquad (paper\ 3)$$

Why it matters

"Terminology is important — only when there is widespread agreement in terminology can useful scientific progress be made."

The concern

"Having some 200 articles ... use such basic concepts as 'perfect fluid' and 'quintessence' in a manner that is at best completely orthogonal to the usage in the bulk of the scientific community is somewhat alarming."

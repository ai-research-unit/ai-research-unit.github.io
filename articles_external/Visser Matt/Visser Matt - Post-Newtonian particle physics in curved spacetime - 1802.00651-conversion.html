What the Paper Does

Three papers claimed that a gravitational effect explains the muon magnetic moment anomaly. Visser answers them. He shows the calculations use the absolute gravitational potential, which the equivalence principle forbids. If one accepted that logic, the effect from the Sun would be fifteen times the Earth's, and the Galaxy's two thousand times. Under general relativity, the effect is suppressed by the size of the laboratory. The note is a correction, not a model.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim being answered

"it has been argued that the observed experimental anomaly in the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon might be explained using general relativity."

The verdict

"It is my melancholy duty to report that these articles are fundamentally flawed in that they fail to correctly implement the Einstein equivalence principle of general relativity."

The reductio

"Insofar as one accepts the underlying logic behind these calculations (and so rejects general relativity) the claimed effect due to the Earth's gravity will be swamped by the effect due to Sun (by a factor of fifteen), and by the effect due to the Galaxy (by a factor of two thousand)."

The diagnosis

"Insofar as one obtains results depending on the absolute gravitational potential, then one is not doing general relativity, (and the gravitational effect of the Galaxy dominates over the Sun which in turn dominates over the Earth — this is not a healthy state of affairs)."

The correct behaviour

"insofar as one working with standard general relativity, the Einstein equivalence principle implies the presence of overwhelmingly small suppression factors dependent on the size of the laboratory."

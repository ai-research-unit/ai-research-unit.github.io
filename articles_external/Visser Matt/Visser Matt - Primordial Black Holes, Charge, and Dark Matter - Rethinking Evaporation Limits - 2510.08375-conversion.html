What the Paper Does

Evaporation limits for primordial black holes are usually computed for a Schwarzschild hole. The authors point out that astrophysical holes are better modelled by Kerr, and that charge changes the story for early-universe holes. Dark matter models can leave a charge on a primordial black hole even after the charged particles have gone. They redo the evolution with charge included and give the conditions under which the standard limits hold. The result is a revised constraint rather than a new mechanism.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The starting point

"Hawking radiation are predominantly derived from the assumption of a Schwarzschild black hole evaporating."

The correction

"astrophysical black holes are usually much more realistically modelled by the rotating Kerr black hole solution."

The charge argument

"Extensions of this model thus can lead to charges present in the early universe which remain preserved in the charge of primordial black holes — even when the corresponding particles have disappeared from the particle content of the present epoch of the universe."

The regime assumed

"Evolution is assuming the following three conditions, involving the characteristic charge scale for the Schwinger effect, $Q_0=\hbar e_\chi/(\pi m_\chi^2)$"

The motivation

"Dark matter is not just a possible solution to issues of astrophysics and cosmology, but also to issues of the standard model of particle physics."

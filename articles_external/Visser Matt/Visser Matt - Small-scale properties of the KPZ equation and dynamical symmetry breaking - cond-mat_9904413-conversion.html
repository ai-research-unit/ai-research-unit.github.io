What the Paper Does

The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation describes a growing interface with noise. The authors apply a functional integral method to its short-distance behaviour. They compute the one-loop effective potential. The result indicates that a symmetry is broken dynamically at small scales. The KPZ equation appears in turbulence, interface growth, and directed polymers, so the result reaches several fields.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The equation

"A functional integral technique is used to study the ultraviolet or short distance properties of the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation with white Gaussian noise."

The calculation

"We apply this technique to calculate the one-loop effective potential for the KPZ equation."

The result

"The effective potential is (at least) one-loop ultraviolet renormalizable in 1, 2, and 3 space dimensions, but non-renormalizable in 4 or higher space dimensions."

The reach

"problems ranging from models of turbulence, interface growth, driven diffusion and flame fronts, directed polymers in a random medium, certain lattice gases with hard-core exclusion"

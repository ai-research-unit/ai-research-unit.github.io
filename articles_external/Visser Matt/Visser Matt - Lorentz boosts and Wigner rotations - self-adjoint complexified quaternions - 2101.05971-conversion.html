What the Paper Does

The authors write boosts and Wigner rotations with complexified quaternions. A self-adjoint complex quaternion carries the four-velocity, and a boost becomes conjugation by its square root. From that follow simple formulas for composing velocities and for the Wigner angle. They tie the Wigner rotation to the failure of associativity for three velocities and give a necessary and sufficient condition for associativity. They also connect the composition to the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"Herein we shall consider Lorentz boosts and Wigner rotations from a (complexified) quaternionic point of view."

The boost as conjugation

$$\sqrt{V}\,X\,\sqrt{V},\qquad L=BR=e^{i\xi\hat n/2}e^{\theta\hat m/2} \qquad (paper\ 8.1,\ 8.2)$$

The composition law

$$V_{1\oplus 2}=\sqrt{V_2}\,\sqrt{V_1}\,\sqrt{V_2} \qquad (paper\ 8.3)$$

The Wigner rotation

$$R=\sqrt{V_{1\oplus 2}^{-1}}\sqrt{V_2}\sqrt{V_1} \qquad (paper\ 8.4)$$

Non-associativity

"we subsequently relate the Wigner rotation to the generic non-associativity of the composition of three 4-velocities, and develop a necessary and sufficient condition for associativity to hold."

Why use quaternions

"As compared to ordinary $4\times 4$ Lorentz transformations, the use of self-adjoint complexified quaternions leads, from a computational view, to storage savings and more rapid computations, and from a pedagogical view to relatively simple and explicit formulae."

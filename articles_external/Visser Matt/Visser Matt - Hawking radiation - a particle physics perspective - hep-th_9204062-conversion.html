What the Paper Does

Taking seriously the idea that black holes are elementary particles, the author builds a particle-physics estimate of the decay rate into a massless quantum. Two free parameters are fixed by matching the high-energy end of the spectrum to a blackbody at the Hawking temperature. From this he argues three things. Hawking radiation needs only that matter couples to gravity. Its thermal character follows from a huge number of internal states. And the formula still gives finite answers for small black holes, which supports complete evaporation with no remnant.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The suggestion

"It has recently become fashionable to regard black holes as elementary particles. By taking this suggestion seriously it is possible to cobble together an elementary particle physics based estimate for the decay rate (black hole)$_i$ → (black hole)$_f$ + (massless quantum)."

The parameters

"This estimate of the spontaneous emission rate contains two free parameters which may be fixed by demanding that the high energy end of the spectrum of emitted quanta match a blackbody spectrum at the Hawking temperature."

First implication

"The existence of Hawking radiation from black holes is ultimately dependent only on the fact that photons (and all other forms of matter) couple to gravity."

Second implication

"The thermal nature of the Hawking spectrum depends only on the fact that the number of internal states of a large mass black hole is enormous."

Third implication

"Remarkably, the resulting formula for the decay rate gives meaningful answers even when extrapolated to low mass black holes. The analysis strongly supports the scenario of complete evaporation as the endpoint of the Hawking radiation process (no naked singularity, no stable massive remnant)."

The independence of geometry

"These conclusions are insensitive to the precise nature of the black hole: Schwarzschild, Reissner–Nordstrom, Kerr–Newman, or dilatonic."

The coherence claim

"Because the model treats Hawking radiation as a cascade of quite ordinary particle physics decays, the model automatically preserves quantum coherence. Any initially pure quantum state will evolve to some pure (but complicated) final state."

The open question

"Insofar as the ideas presented in this paper make any sense it now becomes absolutely critical to face head on the question: 'What exactly are the internal states of a black hole?'."

What the Paper Does

This paper extends the authors' earlier static result to stationary but rotating horizons. It shows the Einstein tensor block-diagonalises into transverse and parallel parts at any non-extremal Killing horizon, whatever the horizon geometry, and that the transverse part is proportional to the transverse metric. Two independent arguments establish this, one from regularity of the horizon geometry and one from the structure of a bifurcate Killing horizon. The consequence is that the stress-energy near the horizon is constrained, with the sum of energy density and transverse pressure going to zero.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"We establish that the Einstein tensor takes on a highly symmetric form near the Killing horizon of any stationary but non-static (and non-extremal) black hole spacetime."

The relation to the earlier paper

"This follows up on a recent article by the current authors, gr-qc/0402069, which considered static black holes."

The content

"Specifically, at any such Killing horizon — irrespective of the horizon geometry — the Einstein tensor block-diagonalizes into 'transverse' and 'parallel' blocks, and its transverse components are proportional to the transverse metric."

The two arguments

"Our findings are supported by two independent procedures; one based on the regularity of the on-horizon geometry and another that directly utilizes the elegant nature of a bifurcate Killing horizon."

The physical consequence

"It is then argued that geometrical symmetries will severely constrain the matter near any Killing horizon." And: "Most notably, the sum of the energy density and the transverse pressure will tend to zero as the horizon [is] approach[ed]. It should be re-emphasized that such constraints would apply to all forms of matter or energy in the proximity of the horizon; including any quantum-induced fluctuations."

The honest limit

"The present analysis, however, does not help in computing the actual value of the Einstein tensor. Such a computation would require something like the analysis of the previous section."

The motivation

"Let us recall Carlip's proposal that the black hole entropy is controlled by an asymptotic near-horizon conformal symmetry, as this idea served as one of the principal motivation[s]."

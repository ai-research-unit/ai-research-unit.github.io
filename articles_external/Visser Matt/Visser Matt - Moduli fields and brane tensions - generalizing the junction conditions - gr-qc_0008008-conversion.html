What the Paper Does

The authors extend the Israel–Lanczos–Sen thin-shell conditions to a bulk containing several scalar fields with arbitrary coupling to curvature. They derive the standard conditions from the action first, then repeat the derivation with the extra fields. The resulting conditions are intertwined: a shell can produce a jump in the scalar field even when it does not couple to it, and the metric jump can feed back into the scalar jump. They show that the form of the conditions depends on which conformal frame is used. For exceptional field configurations a kink survives with no shell at all.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"Taking the Randall–Sundrum models as background scenario, we derive generalized Israel–Lanczos–Sen thin-shell junction conditions for systems in which several bulk scalar fields are non-minimally coupled to gravity."

The frame dependence

"We demonstrate that the form of the junction conditions (though arguably not the physics) depends on the choice of frame."

The unexpected jump

"We show that generally (in any frame except the Einstein frame) the presence of a thin shell induces discontinuities in the normal derivative of the scalar field, even in the absence of any direct interaction between the thin shell and the scalar field."

The feedback

"For some exceptional scalar field configurations the discontinuities in the derivatives of the metric and the scalar fields can feed back into each other and so persist even in the absence of any thin shell of stress-energy."

The scope

"Our formalism is capable of dealing with the dilaton field, in the string and/or Einstein frames, metric-scalar theories à la Brans–Dicke, and non-minimally coupled scalars."

The interlocking

"The existence of couplings between the moduli fields and the curvature scalar makes the generalized junction conditions interconnected."

The exceptional case

"These exceptional field configurations are characterized by the vanishing of the kinetic term of some of the scalar fields when transforming to the Einstein frame."

The caveat

"the form of the junction conditions (though arguably not the physics) depends on the choice of frame"

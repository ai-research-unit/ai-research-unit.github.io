What the Paper Does

Adding two relativistic velocities at an angle is messy in vector notation. The authors use half-velocities and treat them as quaternions. The composition then becomes a simple product with an inverse. The same formula gives the Wigner angle, which is the rotation that appears when two boosts do not commute. They check the case of three velocities as well.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The history

"Quaternions have an (over a century-old) extensive and quite complicated interaction with special relativity."

The hope

"Since quaternions are intrinsically 4-dimensional, and do such a good job of handling 3-dimensional rotations, the hope has always been that the use of quaternions would simplify some of the algebra of the Lorentz transformations."

The result

"Herein we report a relatively nice result for the relativistic combination of non-collinear 3-velocities."

The variables

"If we work with the relativistic half-velocities $w$ defined by $v=\frac{2w}{1+w^2}$, and promote them to quaternions using $\mathbf{w}=w\,\hat{n}$"

The formula

$$w_{1\oplus2}=w_1\oplus w_2=(1-w_1w_2)^{-1}(w_1+w_2)=(w_1+w_2)(1-w_2w_1)^{-1} \qquad (paper\ 5.1)$$

The Wigner angle

$$e^{\Omega}=e^{\Omega\hat{\Omega}}=(1-w_1w_2)^{-1}(1-w_2w_1) \qquad (paper\ 5.2)$$

What the Paper Does

The paper computes the Casimir energy of a finite dielectric body by summing zero-point energies over all electromagnetic states. Extending earlier work on the bulk contribution, it approximates the sum over states by an integral over the density of states and keeps the finite-volume corrections. The leading correction for a finite dielectric is shown to be proportional to the surface area of the body and to a combination of the permeability and permittivity. The result is a surface term in the same expansion as the bulk term, with the next term expected to involve the integrated trace of the extrinsic curvature.

The extracted text of the abstract is partly garbled by the two-column layout; only the legible phrases are quoted below.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The approach

"In this paper we take a deeper look at the technically elementary but physically robust viewpoint in which the Casimir energy in dielectric media is interpreted as the change in the total zero point energy of the electromagnetic vacuum summed over all states."

The extension

"Extending results presented in previous papers ... we approximate the sum over states by an integral over the density of states, including finite volume corrections", and for a finite dielectric the first correction "is shown to be proportional" to the surface area.

The surface term

The surface contribution is expressed through the dielectric interface and through the permeability and permittivity, so the coefficient of the area term is a material property rather than a geometric constant.

The expected pattern

"In general, we can expect these to be the first two terms of a more general expansion that includes terms proportional to various geometrical invariants of the body. By analogy with the situation for non-dispersive Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin boundary conditions we expect the next term to be proportional to the trace of the extrinsic curvature integrated over the surface of the body."

The stated restriction

"The analysis of the present paper has been limited to situations of real refractive index (loss-free insulating dielectrics). Generalizing to lossy conducting media is clearly of interest but will require a careful re-assessment of the entire formalism."

What the Paper Does

Hořava gravity is a quantum field theory of gravity that breaks Lorentz invariance at very high energy. The author gives a personal overview of the programme, its motivations, and its variants. He stresses the difficulty of keeping the symmetry breaking small enough to pass experiment. He ends with a caution: the scalar mode is not understood well enough to support detailed phenomenology.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What the theory is

"Hořava gravity is a relatively recent (Jan 2009) idea in theoretical physics for trying to develop a quantum field theory of gravity."

What it is not

"It is not a string theory, nor loop quantum gravity, but is instead a traditional quantum field theory that breaks Lorentz invariance at ultra-high (presumably trans-Planckian) energies, while retaining approximate Lorentz invariance at low and medium (sub-Planckian) energies."

The challenge

"The challenge is to keep the Lorentz symmetry breaking controlled and small — small enough to be compatible with experiment."

The scope

"I will give a very general overview of what is going on in this field"

The caution

"considerably more work needs to go into understanding the fundamentals of these models, specifically the role played by the scalar mode"

The reason

"Without a deeper understanding of the fundamental framework one is operating in, detailed phenomenological studies ... [are premature]"

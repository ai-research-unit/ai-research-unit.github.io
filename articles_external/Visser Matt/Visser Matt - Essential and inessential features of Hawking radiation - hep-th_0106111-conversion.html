What the Paper Does

The paper strips the derivation of Hawking radiation down to what is needed. Using Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates, an eikonal approximation, and matching modes across a slowly evolving future apparent horizon, it recovers the thermal flux without the Einstein equations and without Bekenstein entropy. The author calls the two beliefs that radiation requires gravity, and that it implies entropy, myths produced by historical accident. The discussion lists what can be relaxed further and separates the kinematic content of the effect from the dynamical content that needs the field equations.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The two myths

"Perhaps the two most pernicious myths attached to this effect are: 'Hawking radiation has something to do with gravity', and, 'Hawking radiation automatically implies Bekenstein entropy'."

Why the myths arose

"These myths were engendered by two historical accidents: (1) the Hawking effect was first encountered within the context of general relativity, and (2) the fact that it was discovered shortly after the notion of Bekenstein entropy (geometric entropy) had been formulated."

The minimal requirements

"This article presents a 'minimalist' argument, and strips the derivation of as much excess baggage as possible. All that is really necessary is quantum physics plus a slowly evolving future apparent horizon (not an event horizon)."

The strongest statement

"In particular, neither the Einstein equations nor Bekenstein entropy are necessary (nor even useful) in deriving Hawking radiation."

The zeroth law caveat

"A tricky point for general analog model geometries is that without the Einstein equations, and something like the dominant energy condition, there is no longer any reason to believe in the zero'th law: the surface gravity and Hawking temperature can then in principle vary over different parts of the apparent horizon; these complications were suppressed in the current article via the simple expedient of enforcing spherical symmetry."

The limit

"One obvious question is the use of spherical symmetry, which precludes direct application of the current approach to Kerr and other rotating black holes. This is a technical problem, not a fundamental problem, and working in axisymmetric geometries will be do-able but somewhat messier."

Where the energy comes from

"The general message is that the Hawking effect steals energy from whatever process is used to set up the effective geometry in question."

The scope claim

"Hawking radiation is not a test of quantum gravity. Instead, searching for Hawking radiation is a test of the general principles of quantum field theory in curved spacetimes."

The reason for the separation

"To start to address the dynamics of general relativity black holes one needs the Einstein equations (or some approximation thereto), in which case one can begin to discuss Bekenstein entropy (or some approximation thereto). This requires a whole extra layer of theoretical superstructure, and a key point of this paper is that it is important and useful to keep these notions logically distinct."

What the Paper Does

In unit-lapse form the time-time component of the metric is minus one. Then the "rain" geodesics can be read off directly. The Painlevé–Gullstrand form does this for Schwarzschild. The authors construct unit-lapse forms of Kerr, starting from Boyer–Lindquist and applying coordinate transformations. They classify which transformations preserve the symmetries and which break them. Several rain metrics result.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The motivation

"The Kerr spacetime is perhaps the most astrophysically important of the currently known exact solutions to the Einstein field equations."

The benefit

"Whenever spacetimes can be put in unit-lapse form it becomes possible to identify some very straightforward timelike geodesics, (the 'rain' geodesics), making the physical interpretation of these spacetimes particularly clean and elegant."

The precedent

"The most well-known of these unit-lapse formulations is the Painlevé–Gullstrand [form]"

The method

"Symmetry-preserving coordinate transformations", "Temporal-only coordinate transformations", "Azimuthal-only coordinate transformations"

The construction

"Enforcing unit lapse — rain metrics for Kerr spacetime", "Boyer–Lindquist-rain metric"

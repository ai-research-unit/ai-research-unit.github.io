What the Paper Does

The paper asks how much of cosmology depends on the Einstein equations. It separates kinematics from dynamics and argues that the cosmological principle plus the FLRW symmetries are enough to analyze the Hubble law, without committing to a stress-energy model. It then shows a counting obstruction: in the expansion of the luminosity distance, the number of free parameters always exceeds the number of measurable coefficients by one, because of the scale factor or spatial curvature. Extracting the jerk and higher derivatives therefore requires an independent bound on that curvature.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"How much of modern cosmology is real[ly model-independent? How much of] modern cosmology is independent of the Einste[in equations (and the] Friedmann equations?) These questions are becoming i[mportant because] the models cosmologists use for the stress-energy co[ntent are] increasingly baroque."

The proposal

"it behoves us to step back a l[ittle and separate] cosmological kinematics from cosmological dyn[amics. Symmetry] principles (such as the cosmological principl[e]) permit one to analyze] without ever having to address the vexatious issues [of] 'dark matter', 'quintessence', and/or 'phantom [energy]' [or] the Einstein equations. This is the sub-secto[r of cosmology called] 'cosmography'."

What it covers

"in this article I will exp[lain what cosmography is and argue that it is] sufficient for analyzing the Hubble law and s[ome related observations of the] universe around us."

The counting problem

"even at $O(z^3)$ there is a problem in that the number of free parameters in the Hubble law exceeds the number of measurable coefficients. This arises because of the fact that the $O(z^3)$ term depends explicitly on both the jerk $j_0$ and the scale factor $a_0$."

The persistence of the problem

"This problem persists at $O(z^4)$ and higher, with the number of free parameters in the Hubble law always exceeding the number of measurable coefficients by one."

What would be needed

"If (and only if) one has some independent method for bounding the scale factor $a_0$ (or more precisely the space curvature $k/a_0^2$) can one even in principle use the observational Hubble law to bound the jerk, snap, and higher time derivatives of the scale factor."

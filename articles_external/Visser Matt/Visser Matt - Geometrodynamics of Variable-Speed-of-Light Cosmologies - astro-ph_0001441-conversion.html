What the Paper Does

Variable-speed-of-light cosmologies are an alternative to inflation. The authors set out a two-metric formalism in which photons and gravitons see different metrics, with the difference driven by a scalar field, so Lorentz invariance is broken softly rather than by hand. This answers the question "variable with respect to what". The models solve the kinematic puzzles but not flatness, since the strong energy condition still holds. A mixed model with inflation is also considered, giving signatures in lensing, birefringence, and the rotation of polarisation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The motivation

"Variable-Speed-of-Light (VSL) cosmologies are currently attracting interest as an alternative to inflation."

The approach

"We investigate the fundamental geometrodynamic aspects of VSL cosmologies and provide several implementations which do not explicitly break Lorentz invariance (no 'hard' breaking). These 'soft' implementations of Lorentz symmetry breaking provide particularly clean answers to the question 'VSL with respect to what?'."

The compatibility claim

"The class of VSL cosmologies we consider are compatible with both classical Einstein gravity and low-energy particle physics."

What they achieve

"These models solve the 'kinematic' puzzles of cosmology as well as inflation does, but cannot by themselves solve the flatness problem, since in their purest form no violation of the strong energy condition occurs."

The mixed model

"We also consider a heterotic model (VSL plus inflation) which provides a number of observational implications for the low-redshift universe if $\chi$ contributes to the 'dark energy' either as CDM or quintessence."

The signatures

"These implications include modified gravitational lensing, birefringence, variation of fundamental constants and rotation of the plane of polarization of light from distant sources."

The summary

"In this article, we have argued for the usefulness of a two-metric approach. We have sketched a number of two-metric scenarios that are compatible with laboratory particle physics, and have indicated how they relate to cosmological puzzles."

The caution

"We emphasise that there is considerable freedom in these models, and that a detailed confrontation with experimental data will require the development of an equally detailed VSL model. ... once $c_{\mathrm{photon}}\neq c_{\mathrm{gravity}}$ complications may appear in rather unexpected places."

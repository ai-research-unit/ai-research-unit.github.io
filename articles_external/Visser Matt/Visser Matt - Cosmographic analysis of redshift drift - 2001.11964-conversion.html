What the Paper Does

Redshift drift is the slow change in the redshift of a comoving source as the expansion rate changes. The paper analyses it cosmographically, using only the symmetries of FLRW and Taylor expansions, without the Friedmann equations. It expresses the drift and its first four derivatives, and the Hubble, deceleration, jerk, snap, crackle, and pop parameters, as expansions in redshift with present-epoch coefficients. Because the expansions converge badly at large redshift, the paper rewrites everything in the $y$-redshift, $y=z/(1+z)$. It also gives a general result for the $n$-th derivative of the redshift at lowest order.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The effect

"Redshift drift is the phenomen[on whereby the redshift of light emitted by] an emitter and observer comoving with th[e expansion] will slowly evolve — on a timescale [that is long, but] nevertheless [the subject of] serious astrometric proposa[ls]."

The approach

"[We] pursue a more abstract theoretic[al] analysis of this effect, eschewing (for [the most part]) [dynamics and relying on] kinematic symmetry considerations and Ta[ylor expansions in] FLRW spacetimes."

What is computed

"We have explicitly included present-epoch values of the jerk, snap, crackle, and pop parameters in the redshift-dependent cosmographic expansion for all of the Hubble, deceleration, jerk, snap, crackle, and pop parameters, and for the redshift drift and its derivatives $\dot z$, $\ddot z$, $\dot{\ddot z}$, and $z^{(5)}$."

The convergence fix

"Since in applications one often wants to work at large redshift ($z>1$) we have shown how to ameliorate problematic convergence issues by rephrasing the discussion in terms of a modified notion of redshift, the $y$-redshift"

$$y=\frac{z}{1+z}.$$

"All of our cosmographic expansions have also been expressed in terms of the $y$-redshift."

Going further

"One could in principle go to even higher order, the relevant formulae just become messy and tedious. We have also derived a quite general result for $z^{(n)}$ at lowest order in $z$."

What is left out

"Of course very much more could be said by introducing cosmo-dynamics, (that is, invoking the Einstein equations, or more specifically the Friedmann equations). This would first allow us to build specific explicit analytic models."

The observation

"potential observation of thi[s effect] ... [would discriminate a] universe exhibiting a nonzero decelerati[on]."

What the Paper Does

Classical point particles fall the same way in a gravitational field. Structured particles need not, because a quadrupole moment couples to the field gradient. The author argues that a quantum particle described by a wave packet has a probability quadrupole. If so, elementary particles violate the universality of free fall at some level. He proposes an experiment and states what either outcome would mean.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The background

"Classical point particles in Newtonian gravity obey, as they do in general relativity, the universality of free fall."

The exception

"However classical structured particles, (for instance with a mass quadrupole moment), need not obey the universality of free fall."

The quantum case

"Quantum mechanically, an elementary 'point' particle (in the particle physics sense) can be described by a localized wave-packet, for which we can define a probability quadrupole moment."

The claim

"This probability quadrupole can, under plausible hypotheses, affect the universality of free fall."

The significance

"point-like elementary particles, in the particle physics sense, can and indeed must nevertheless have structure in the general relativistic sense once wave-[packets are used]"

The no-lose argument

"While certainly challenging, experimentally probing the ideas developed in this essay is not entirely impossible or implausible. Better, it is almost a no lose proposition"

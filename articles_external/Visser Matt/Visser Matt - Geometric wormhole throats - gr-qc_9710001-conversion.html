What the Paper Does

This is a survey of how wormholes and black holes might interact, followed by an account of what can be said about throats in general. Three possible connections are listed: small geometric perturbations shifting the horizon by a large amount, Planck-scale wormholes letting information move along spacelike directions near a singularity, and vacuum polarisation punching through to another asymptotic region. The survey then covers energy condition violation at throats, the Morris–Thorne wormhole and its time-dependent generalisation, and Brans–Dicke wormholes as a case where the violation is hidden by a choice of definitions. It restates the minimal-area definition of a throat.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The traditional separation

"Wormholes and black holes have, apart from a few historical oddities, traditionally been treated a quite separate objects with relatively little overlap. The possibility of a connection arises in that wormholes, if they exist, might have profound influence on black holes, their event horizons, and their internal structure."

The first connection

"small wormhole-induced perturbations in the geometry can lead to massive non-perturbative shifts in the event horizon"

The second connection

"Planck-scale wormholes near any spacelike singularity might let information travel in effectively spacelike directions"

The third connection

"vacuum polarization effects near any singularity might conceivably lead to a 'punch through' into another asymptotically flat region—effectively transforming a black hole into a wormhole."

The survey content

"We discuss the violations of the energy conditions that typically occur at and near the throat of any traversable wormhole and emphasize the generic nature of this result."

The hiding of violations

"We discuss spherically symmetric Brans–Dicke wormholes as examples of how to hide the energy condition violations in an inappropriate choice of definitions."

The general conclusion

"In non-Einstein theories of gravity it is often possible to push the energy condition violations into the nonstandard parts of the stress-energy tensor and let the ordinary part of the stress-energy satisfy the energy conditions. The total stress-energy tensor, however, must still violate the energy conditions."

The definition and its limit

"We do not need to make any assumptions about the existence of any asymptotically flat region, nor do we need to assume that the manifold is topologically non-trivial. ... Generalization to the time dependent situation is clearly of interest. Unfortunately we have encountered many subtleties of definition, notation, and formalism in this endeavor."

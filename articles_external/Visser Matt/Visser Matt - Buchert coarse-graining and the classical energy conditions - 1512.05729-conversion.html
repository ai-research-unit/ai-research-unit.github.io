What the Paper Does

The paper argues that "Buchert averaging" is misnamed: it is a coarse-graining, in which small-scale structure is smeared out because the observer has limited resolution. Because the Einstein tensor is nonlinear, the Einstein tensor of the coarse-grained metric differs from the coarse-grained Einstein tensor, and the difference acts as an effective stress-energy. To keep the calculation controlled, the author works with cosmologies that are locally FLRW (CFLRW), for which the coarse-graining satisfies closed differential identities. The result is an explicit effective stress-energy that is not traceless, and the classical energy conditions are satisfied by it.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The terminology

"So-called 'Buchert averaging' is actually a coarse-graining procedure, where [structure] is 'smeared out' due to limited spatio-temporal resolution. For technical reasons (explained herein), 'averaging' is not really an appropriate term, and I shall describe the process as a 'coarse-graining'."

The mechanism

"Because Einstein gravity is nonlinear, the coarse-grained Einstein tensor is typically not equal to the Einstein tensor of the coarse-grained spacetime geometry. The discrepancy can be viewed as an 'effective' stress-energy."

The tractable model

"When studying Buchert coarse-graining, working with CFLRW spacetimes is a great technical help. Specifically, a FLRW background provides you with a very nicely controlled version of Buchert coarse-graining, and CFLRW coarse-graining satisfies some nice differential identities. Indeed CFLRW coarse-graining yields an explicit and tractable effective stress-energy."

The result

"The effective stress energy is not necessarily traceless. Furthermore for this form of coarse-graining the classical energy conditions are satisfied by the effective stress-energy."

The warning

"Finally I emphasize that Buchert coarse-graining $\neq$ ensemble average; several key properties are radically different."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. An effective source from nonlinearity

The idea. Coarse-graining produces a stress-energy that is not in the microscopic theory. It is a bookkeeping artefact of the mismatch between the two Einstein tensors.

What it would add. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md needs a rank-two source and cannot make one. This paper shows a mechanism that manufactures one from the nonlinearity of the field equations plus a resolution scale. If the framework had any nonlinearity in its metric sector, the same mechanism would apply; if it does not, the absence is diagnostic. Either way the article gains a named candidate and a test of whether it can work.

Where it lands. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md, in the classification of obstacles.

What stands in the way. The mechanism needs the Einstein equations and a coarse-graining scale, neither of which the framework has. Ranked first, because it is the only constructive source proposal in this batch.

2. Averages depend on resolution

The idea. An averaged quantity is not a property of the geometry alone; it depends on the scale at which the observer smooths.

3. Energy conditions survive

The idea. The effective stress-energy satisfies the classical energy conditions, which is not automatic for a coarse-grained object.

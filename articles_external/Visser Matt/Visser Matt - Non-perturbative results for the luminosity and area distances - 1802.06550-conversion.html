What the Paper Does

Luminosity distance is usually defined in FLRW or in perturbation theory. The authors define it in an arbitrary spacetime using a bundle of null geodesics. They write the distance in terms of the affine parameter separation and the van Vleck determinant. The affine parameter supplies the inverse square law; the determinant supplies the departure from it. They relate the van Vleck determinant to the Jacobi determinant and give explicit FLRW and conformally FLRW expressions. The result holds without perturbation theory, and they leave the near-FLRW perturbative case for later work.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The aim

"the abstract notion of luminosity distance is actually much more robust than this, and can be defined non-perturbatively in almost arbitrary spacetimes."

The tool

"We shall reformulate these results in terms of a suitably normalized null geodesic affine parameter and the van Vleck determinant, $\Delta_{\mathrm{vV}}$."

The split

"The contribution due to the null geodesic affine parameter is effectively the inverse square law for luminosity, and the van Vleck determinant can be viewed as providing a measure of deviations from the inverse square law."

The main formulae

$$d_L=(1+z)^{-1/2}(\lambda_o-\lambda_s)\Delta_{\mathrm{vV}}^{-1/2},\qquad d_{\mathrm{area}}=(\lambda_o-\lambda_s)\Delta_{\mathrm{vV}}^{-1/2} \qquad (paper\ 4.1)$$

The relation to Jacobi

"we have also shown that in all generality the Jacobi determinant is related to the van Vleck determinant by $|\det(J)|=(\lambda_o-\lambda_s)^{2}\Delta_{\mathrm{vV}}^{-1}$."

What the determinant separates

"the van Vleck determinant allows you to clearly separate the inverse square law contribution from the focussing/defocussing contribution; information which is obscured in the Jacobi determinant."

The scope

"In the current article we shall concentrate on non-perturbative results, leaving near-FLRW perturbative investigation for future work."

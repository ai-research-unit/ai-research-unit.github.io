What the Paper Does

The paper is a short account of variable-speed-of-light cosmologies, in which the speed of light changes because a scalar field $\chi$ sits away from the minimum of its potential. It sets out the geometric framework, notes several implementations, and checks that they are compatible with classical Einstein physics to zeroth order. The authors report that these models solve the kinematic puzzles as well as inflation does, but that in their purest form they do not solve the flatness problem, and that they are easy to unify with inflation rather than competing with it.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The subject

"Variable-Speed-of-Light (VSL) cosmologies are currently attracting much interest [as alternatives to] cosmological inflation. We discuss the fundamental geometrodynamic aspects of VSL [models and] several alternative implementations. These implementations provide a large class [of models that pass] the zeroth-order consistency tests of being compatible with both classical Einstein [gravity and laboratory] physics."

What they achieve

"While they solve the 'kinematic' puzzles as well as inflation does, VSL [models do not solve] the flatness problem since in their purest form no viola[tion of energy conservation is invoked]; these models are easy to unify with inflation."

The mechanism

The paper attributes the change in light speed to a scalar field displaced from its minimum: "the $\chi$ field is displaced from this minimum in the early universe: either trapped in a meta-stable state by high-temperature effects or displaced due to chaotic initial conditions. The transition to the global minimum may be either of first or second order and during it $\nabla_\alpha\chi\neq0$, so that $g_{\mathrm{em}}\neq g$. Once the true global minimum is achieved, $g_{\mathrm{em}}=g$ again."

Why laboratory physics is safe

"Since one can arrange $\chi$ today to have settled to the true global minimum, current laboratory experiments would automatically give $g_{\mathrm{em}}=g$."

How the horizon problem is solved

"the mechanism by which VSL models solve this cosmological puzzle relies on the fact that the (coordinate) size of the horizon at the time of last scattering $t_*$ is modified by the time dependence of the photon speed",

$$R_{hz}=\int_0^{t_*}\frac{c_{\mathrm{photon}}\,dt}{a(t)}.$$

Anomalous photon speeds are not new

"we stress that 'anomalous' (larger than $c_{\mathrm{gravity}}$) photon speeds have been calculated in relation with the propagation of light in the Casimir vacuum, as well as in gravitational fields."

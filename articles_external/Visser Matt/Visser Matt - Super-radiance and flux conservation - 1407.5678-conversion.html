What the Paper Does

Super-radiance in field theory is often confused with super-fluorescence in condensed matter. The authors trace part of the confusion to normalisation conventions, and part to mixing fluxes with probabilities. They argue that the real content is flux conservation, possibly with a controlled amount of dissipation. On that basis they write a single bookkeeping equation that covers both the normal and the super-radiant regime.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The source of confusion

"part of the confusion arises from traditional but sometimes awkward normalization conventions, and part is due to sometimes unnecessary confusion between fluxes and probabilities"

The key point

"We shall argue that the key point underlying the effect is flux conservation, (and, in the presence of dissipation, a controlled amount of flux non-conservation)"

The bookkeeping

$$F_{\text{transmitted}}+F_{\text{reflected}}+F_{\text{dissipated}}=1\qquad (paper\ 90)$$

The probability form

$$R+T+P_D=1\qquad (paper\ 98)$$

The generality

"This formula is now equally valid for both normal and super-radiant regimes, and for particles incoming from either [side]"

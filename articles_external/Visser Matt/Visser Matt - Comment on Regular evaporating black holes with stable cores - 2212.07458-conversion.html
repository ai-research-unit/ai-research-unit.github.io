What the Paper Does

This comment disputes a claim that Hawking evaporation stabilises regular black holes against mass inflation. The authors argue that the claim applies the linear perturbation model beyond the regime where it is valid. In that model the Misner–Sharp mass at the inner horizon grows exponentially, and the growth stops only when backreaction invalidates the linear treatment. Whether the later evolution is exponential or polynomial therefore cannot be used as evidence that inflation did not happen. The authors conclude that regular black holes of astrophysical interest remain unstable.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim under review

"Regular black holes are generically unstable [due to an effect known] by the name of 'mass inflation'. In [Phys. Rev. D 107 (2023) 024005] it is argued [that this instability can be cured, along with] some concerns a[bout] showing its existence in the first place."

The objection

"In a recent work [1], it is argued that the backreaction of Hawking evaporation is [a] relevant factor in the analysis of the stability properties of regular black holes. However in this comment we show that this claim is based on an extrapolation of the mathematical model used beyond its regime of validity."

What follows once a model is chosen

"Regardless of the model selected, there is an exponential mass inflation of the Misner–Sharp mass that continues until backreaction cannot be ignored, and thus the linear approximation breaks down (in complete parallelism with the Ori model). If one insists in using the (unphysical) linear approximation beyond this point, the Misner–Sharp mass is always divergent but different background geometries can display different degrees of divergence (ranging from exponential to polynomial)."

The core of the argument

"While from a mathematical perspective it is always possible to push the linear model to [its] ultimate consequences, such an exercise has clearly no physical relevance." And: "that the late-time behavior is not exponential does not mean that mass inflation has not taken place".

The quantitative statement

If the Misner–Sharp mass is linear in the asymptotic mass,

$$M(v,r)=g_1(r)\,m(v)+g_2(r),$$

then the mass at the inner horizon behaves as

$$M_+\sim\frac{e^{|\kappa_-|v}}{v^{\gamma+1}},$$

where $\kappa_-$ is the surface gravity of the inner horizon.

The conclusion

"by the end of the exponential phase the backreaction on the geometry is already very large, and the linear approximation at the core of the model can no longer be trusted." Hence "the backreaction of perturbations cannot be ignored, and the linear model cease[s] to be valid."

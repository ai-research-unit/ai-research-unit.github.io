What the Paper Does

The paper builds explicit, calculable models of coarse graining. It sets up examples in which a system with a specified fine-grained entropy is coarse-grained until its entropy reaches a maximum, and it tracks the entropy flow at every step. Classically it treats the Shannon entropy, with cases involving continuum-to-discrete transitions, a tunable smearing parameter, diffusion, density-density correlations, and correlations between two boxes. Quantum mechanically it treats the von Neumann entropy, coarse-grained by maximal mixing, by partial traces, and by decoherence. In every case it finds a controllable, monotonic entropy flow and a generic upper bound.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The problem

"The nature of coarse graining is intuitively 'obvious', but there are very few explicit and calculable models of the coarse graining process in the literature. What we would like to have at hand is [a procedure that] takes an arbitrary system, with specified initial entropy, [and coarse grains it until] the entropy [reaches] its maximum value."

What the paper supplies

For some purposes "it is better to deal with a gedanke[n] [model where] information is hiding in the fine-grain correlations", and the paper presents "several simple mathematically well-defined models of coarse graining".

The classical cases

"Classically, when considering the Shannon entropy, we have studied coarse-graining allowing the system [to] change (or not) between continuum and discretium character, and also differentiating situations where we introduce a tuneable smearing parameter to track the change from continuum to discretium. We [also] studied coarse-graining that involves diffusion, density-density correlations in a box, ... and correlation between the states of two boxes, obtaining in every [case a] controllable and monotonic flux of entropy."

The quantum cases

"we have considered the quantum von Neumann entropy, coarse-grained by maximal mixing or by partial traces, tuneable or not, asymmetric in the treatment and also combined with maximal mixing. That corresponds to blurring processes depending on a smearing parameter, and [to a] choice of separate subsystems that 'decide' not to look [at] each other. In all cases we have found a positivity of entropy flow and generic upper bound."

The connection

"We have also studied the coarse-graining induced by decoherence, either partial or complete", and the models are related to Hawking's super-scattering matrix.

The overall claim

"We see that we can model the coarse-graining procedure, in a quantifiable and controllable manner, by following different methods depending on what aspect of the system one is taking into account."

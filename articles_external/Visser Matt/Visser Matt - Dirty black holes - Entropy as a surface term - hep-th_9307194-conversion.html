What the Paper Does

The area law for black hole entropy is known to fail when the matter or the gravity action departs from the simplest case. Several authors had conjectured that the entropy of a "dirty" black hole is still given by a surface integral over the horizon, and Wald had proved this with Lorentzian methods. This note gives a different proof using Euclidean techniques, covering static spherically symmetric and stationary axisymmetric black holes. The result is that the total entropy is the area term plus a correction that is an integral of a local density over a slice of the horizon.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The problem

"It is by now clear that the naive rule for the entropy of a black hole, (entropy) = $1/4$ (area of event horizon), is violated in many interesting cases. Indeed, several authors have recently conjectured that in general the entropy of a dirty black hole might be given purely in terms of some surface integral over the event horizon of that black hole."

The relation to Wald

"A formal proof of this conjecture, using Lorentzian signature techniques, has recently been provided by Wald. This note performs two functions. Firstly, by extending a previous analysis due to the present author it is possible to provide a rather different proof of this result — a proof based on Euclidean signature techniques."

The scope of the proof

"The proof applies both to arbitrary static [spherically symmetric] black holes, and also to arbitrary stationary axisymmetric black holes."

The result

The total entropy is

$$S=\frac{kA_H}{4\ell_P^2}+\int_H S^{(2)}\sqrt{{}^{(2)}g}\,d^2x.\qquad (paper\ 66)$$

The first conclusion

"First: The naive area law for black hole entropies is in general false. $S\neq \frac{kA_H}{4\ell_P^2}$. The naive law certainly holds for Einstein–Hilbert gravity coupled to matter whose kinetic energy is quadratic. Once one moves beyond quadratic kinetic energies the naive law fails in general."

The second conclusion

"Second: For a general higher derivative Lagrangian the entropy of a black hole is given by an integral of some suitable density over (a fixed time spacelike cross-section of) the event horizon."

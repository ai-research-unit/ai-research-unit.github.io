What the Paper Does

Uses a Bose–Einstein condensate as an explicit, physically realisable model in which Lorentz invariance holds approximately at low momentum and fails at high momentum. The Gross–Pitaevskii equation, rewritten in Madelung form, is exactly inviscid irrotational hydrodynamics with a quantum potential; linearising and dropping that potential gives phonons on a curved Lorentzian metric. Keeping it gives the Bogoliubov dispersion relation, which is relativistic at low $k$ and Newtonian — with a rest-mass term — at high $k$. The paper's argument is that this settles a matter of principle: Lorentz breaking need not bring causality violations, provided quantisation is done in the frame in which the underlying equation is written.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why a concrete model is wanted

"it is standard lore that Lorentz symmetry breaking leads to severe causality problems. It is therefore useful to have at least one concrete physical model of Lorentz symmetry breaking at hand which is guaranteed to be physically consistent. Even if the symmetry breaking believed to arise from quantum gravity does not follow this particular pattern, exhibiting such a model unambiguously settles an important matter of principle."

The equivalence

"low-momentum phonon physics is completely equivalent to quantum field theory in curved spacetime. That is, everything that theorists have learned about curved space QFT can be carried over to this acoustic system, and conversely acoustic experiments can in principle be used to experimentally investigate curved space QFT."

The metric

With $\lambda\rho_0/m\equiv c_s^2$ and $\vec v_0=\nabla\theta_0$, the inverse metric is

$$g^{\mu\nu}(t,\vec x)\equiv\frac{\rho_0}{c_s}\begin{pmatrix}-1&\vdots&-\vec v_0\\ \cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\ -\vec v_0&\vdots&(c_s^2 I-\vec v_0\otimes\vec v_0)\end{pmatrix}, \qquad (paper\ 7)$$

so $ds^2=\frac{\rho_0}{c_s}\left[-c_s^2dt^2+\|d\vec x-\vec v_0dt\|^2\right]$ (paper 10).

The puzzle and its resolution

"We started with the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. That equation is parabolic, so we know that the characteristics move at infinite speed. How did we get a hyperbolic d'Alembertian equation with a finite propagation speed? The subtlety resides in neglecting the higher-derivative term $V_Q$."

The dispersion relation

$$\omega-\vec v_0\cdot\vec k=\pm\sqrt{c_s^2k^2+\left(\frac{\hbar}{2m}k^2\right)^2}, \qquad (paper\ 11)$$

"the curved-space generalization of the well-known Bogolubov dispersion relation." Both group and phase velocity have "the appropriate relativistic limit at low momentum, but then grow without bound at high momentum, leading to an infinite signal velocity and the recovery of the parabolic nature of the differential equation at high momentum" once $k\gg k_c=mc_s/\hbar$.

The high-momentum form

"at large momenta the dispersion relation again takes on Newtonian form, complete with 'rest mass' contribution $\omega(k)=\frac{\hbar k^2}{2m}+\vec v_0\cdot\vec k+\frac{mc_s^2}{\hbar}+O(k^{-2})$" (paper 15).

How causality survives

"The way to preserve causality in this system is clear — there is an intrinsic preferred frame in this model and one should quantize by applying equal time commutators in the inertial frame in which the original nonlinear Schrödinger equation is written down. In particular, field commutators need not generically vanish identically outside the lightcone. In the low-momentum limit, where the physics decouples from this preferred frame, the commutators are approximately zero outside the lightcone; this is good enough for an approximate Lorentz symmetry to arise."

The even-power observation

"In all of the condensed matter systems we are aware of, the modifications to the high-energy dispersion relation show up as even powers of energy and/or momentum. Ultimately this is due to the fact that the condensed matter systems considered so far explicitly conserve parity and time reversal invariance. In contrast some of the models based on quantum gravity hint at cubic deviations."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A preferred frame is not the same as a violation of causality

The idea. A theory can have a preferred frame, non-zero commutators outside the light cone at short distances, and still be causal, provided quantisation is done in that frame. The light cone is then an emergent, low-energy structure rather than a fundamental one.

What it would add. articles_physics/causality-and-the-light-cone-as-an-information-barrier-in-biquaternionic-form.md treats the light cone as a barrier. This paper shows the barrier can be approximate and still do its job, and states exactly what has to be given up: exact microcausality. That is a conditional the corpus's article never states, and it is the cleanest statement of it in the collection.

Where it lands. articles_physics/causality-and-the-light-cone-as-an-information-barrier-in-biquaternionic-form.md, as a stated assumption.

What stands in the way. The argument needs a second-quantised field theory with a heat bath of phonons. The corpus is algebraic. But the conditional — "if the cone is exact, then no preferred frame; the converse fails" — transfers.

2. Even powers from parity and time reversal

The idea. The leading correction to a relativistic dispersion relation is quadratic in momentum for any system that preserves parity and time reversal, and cubic only when one of them is broken.

What it would add. A structural explanation of a pattern. The corpus's articles_physics/the-empirical-status-of-the-biquaternion-framework.md asks what its framework predicts; here is a worked case where a symmetry assumption fixes the form of a correction without fixing its coefficient. That is a template the corpus could copy: predict the power, leave the scale free.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-empirical-status-of-the-biquaternion-framework.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing blocks the import; it is a symmetry argument. Ranked first, because it gives the corpus a falsifiable shape it currently lacks.

3. One equation, two regimes, one parameter

The idea. The Gross–Pitaevskii equation yields both the relativistic and the Newtonian regime, and the crossover is at $k_c=mc_s/\hbar$ — the acoustic Compton wavelength. No extra assumption separates the regimes; the same equation does both.

What it would add. The corpus's articles_physics/the-massless-regime.md treats the massless limit as a regime of a parameter, and this paper shows a case where the same equation covers both regimes and the boundary is computable. That is a stronger statement than a limit exists: the crossover scale is derived, not assumed.

What stands in the way. The derivation uses the quantum potential of a condensate. Ranked second.

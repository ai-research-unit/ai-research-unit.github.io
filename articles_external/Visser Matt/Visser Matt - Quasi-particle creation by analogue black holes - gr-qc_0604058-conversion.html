What the Paper Does

The authors ask what an analogue black hole must do to radiate like a black hole. They build simple acoustic models in one spatial dimension and compute the particle production. The surprise is that an ergoregion, meaning a supersonic flow, is not required. A flow that never forms a sonic region, or only reaches sound speed asymptotically, can still give stationary Planckian emission. They tabulate which flows have horizons and how much late-time radiation each produces.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"We discuss the issue of quasi-particle production by 'analogue black holes' with particular attention to the possibility of reproducing Hawking radiation in a laboratory."

The result

"By constructing simple geometric acoustic models, we obtain a somewhat unexpected result"

The surprise

"We show that in order to obtain a stationary and Planckian emission of quasi-particles, it is not necessary to create an ergoregion in the acoustic spacetime (corresponding to a supersonic regime in the flow)."

The alternative

"It is sufficient to set up a dynamically changing flow either eventually generating an arbitrarily small sonic region $v=c$, but without any ergoregion, or even just asym[ptotically approaching it]"

The method

"we have analyzed different dynamical black hole-like analogue geometries with regard to their properties in terms of quantum quasi-particle production"

The bookkeeping

"for each of them we (i) have calculated whether it possesses an event horizon or not, and if the answer is 'yes', (ii) have calculated the asymptotic redshift function"

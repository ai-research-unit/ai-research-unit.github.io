What the Paper Does

The paper argues that cosmologies conformally related to FLRW deserve separate study. A conformal factor that varies with position can produce large non-perturbative departures from a smooth FLRW geometry while leaving the cosmic microwave background almost untouched, because the conformal factor shifts the temperature by an observer-dependent amount rather than distorting the spectrum. Using a cosmographic analysis that avoids assuming the Einstein equations, the author constrains how much the conformal factor can vary between supernova host galaxies. The paper also insists on keeping three things apart: the known smoothness of the CMB, the hypothetical smoothness of the geometry, and the known lumpiness of the matter.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The motivation

"In a universe where, according to the standard cos[mology] 97% of the total mass-energy is still 'missing in action' i[t seems worth] [a] little effort critically assessing and exploring radical al[ternatives (if] we say plausible), nonstandard but superficially viable mod[els]."

The special role of conformal FLRW

"Conformally [deformed Friedmann–Lemaître–]Robertson–Walker [CFLRW] models play a very special role — these models have the uni[que property] of permitting large non-perturbative geometric deviations f[rom] Robertson–Walker cosmology without unacceptably d[amaging the microwave] background."

The method

"Performing a 'cosmographic' analysis, [one can avoid assuming] the Einstein equations, since the question of wh[ether they are] valid on galactic and cosmological scales is esse[ntially the question of] [whether] or not dark matter/dark energy actually exist."

What can and cannot be constrained

"Even without any] information about galactic structure, one can nev[ertheless place] observational constraints on any possible conform[al deformation], [leaving an] extremely rich range of phenomenological possibil[ities for] physicists to explore."

The three distinctions

"It is particularly important to carefully distinguish three different issues: The (known) smoothness of the CMB, the (hypothetical) smoothness of the geometry, and the (known lack of) smoothness of the matter distribution within the geometry."

The result

"Supernova observations, and the concomitant luminosity distance estimates, can then be used to constrain the variability of the conformal factor between the supernova-bearing regions of the relevant population of host galaxies in a manner that is largely independent of detailed cosmological assumptions."

What the Paper Does

Is the Hawking flux thermal? The author shows the question has no single answer until several unstated assumptions are fixed. He separates the Planck-shaped spectrum from thermodynamic thermality. He examines what a static observer measures, what reaches infinity, and what Wick rotation gives. He also argues that much of the information puzzle depends on using an event horizon rather than a trapping horizon.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"Is the Hawking flux 'thermal'? Unfortunately, the answer to this seemingly innocent question depends on a number of often unstated, but quite crucial, technical assumptions built into modern (mis-)interpretations of the word 'thermal'."

The history

"The original 1850's notions of thermality — based on classical thermodynamic reasoning applied to idealized 'black bodies' or 'lamp black surfaces' — when supplemented by specific basic quantum ideas from the early 1900's, immediately led to the notion of the black-body spectrum"

The distinction

"the Planck-shaped spectrum ... but without any [thermodynamic content]"

The sections

"Spectrum of the Hawking flux", "Schwarzschild black holes", "Connecting future and past null infinities", "Effective temperature", "Wick rotation and the Hawking flux"

The consequence

"the so-called 'information puzzle' ... is intimately reliant on the assumed existence of an event horizon, and much of the force of the information puzzle simply goes away once one uses apparent horizons or trapping horizons"

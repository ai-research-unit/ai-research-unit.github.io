What the Paper Does

The long paper performs the cosmographic programme in detail. It keeps the FLRW geometry as a working hypothesis but does not use the Einstein equations, so questions about the equation of state are deferred. It fits several inter-related cosmographic expansions to supernova datasets, with explicit treatment of statistical and systematic uncertainties and of the redshift range covered. The conclusion is that the case for acceleration is suggestive but not established, and that the fitted cosmological parameters depend strongly on which distance variable is used in the least-squares fit.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The definition

"Cosmography (cosmokinetics) is the part of cosmo[logy that can be developed with] minimal dynamic assumptions. One keeps the geometry [of] spacetime, at least as a working hypothesis, but doe[s not use the field] equations (Einstein equations), unless and until abs[olutely necessary]. [Thus] it is possible to defer questions about the equation [of state] and concentrate more directly on the observational s[ide]."

The method

"the 'big picture' is best brought into focus by performing a [cosmographic fit] to the Hubble relation, from the current ep[och outwards]."

What is done

"We perform a number of inter-related co[smographic fits to several] supernova datasets. We pay particular atten[tion to statistical] and systematic uncertainties, and also to t[he] scale and manner of representing the redshi[ft]."

The main conclusion

"While the 'preponderance of evidence' certa[inly suggests acceleration], we would argue that (based on the supernova [data alone this is not] supported 'beyond reasonable doubt'."

The dependence on the distance variable

"The modelling uncertainties we have encountered depend on the distance variable one chooses to do the least squares fit ($d_L$, $d_F$, $d_P$, $d_Q$, $d_A$). There is no good physics reason for preferring any one of these distance variables over the others."

The warning

"regardless of one's views on how to combine formal estimates of uncertainty, the very fact that different distance scales yield data-fits with such widely discrepant values strongly suggests the need for extreme caution in interpreting the supernova data."

Why the problem is not fixed by more terms

"One can always minimize the modelling uncertainties by going to a higher-order polynomial — unfortunately at the price of unacceptably increasing the statistical uncertainties — and we have checked that this makes the overall situation worse."

What the Paper Does

The authors build a formalism for scale-invariant differential equations. They rewrite the model in equidimensional and autonomous forms, find the fixed points, and get power-law background solutions. Linearising about a fixed point gives a second set of power laws describing deviations. They then generalise Frobenius series so that solutions can be built near singular points. The worked application is Newtonian and Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff stars near criticality.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What is presented

"We present a self-contained formalism for calculating the background solution, the linearized solutions, and a class of generalized Frobenius solutions to a system of scale invariant differential equations."

The steps

"We first cast the scale invariant model into its equidimensional and autonomous forms, find its fixed points, and then obtain power-law background solutions."

The second solution

"After linearizing about these fixed points, we find a second linearized solution, which provides a distinct collection of power laws characterizing the deviations from the fixed point."

The application

"Applications to Newtonian and TOV stars near criticality"

The interpretation of self-similarity

"Discrete self-similarity and discrete power laws are not as peculiar as one might at first imagine; instead they are simply the next most [general] thing (after simple power-law behaviour) that can happen in scale invariant systems."

The consequence

"the occurrence of discrete self-similarity in Choptuik's critical solution should not at all be considered [peculiar]"

What the Paper Does

The paper examines a dispute about the Casimir energy of a dielectric. Schwinger's dynamical Casimir calculation gives a change in zero-point energy proportional to the volume of the dielectric, plus finite-volume corrections. Other authors argued that the volume term should be discarded and that the leading term is a surface area. The paper redoes the calculation carefully, states the physical question precisely, and concludes that the leading term is indeed a bulk volume contribution, in agreement with Schwinger. The result bears on models of sonoluminescence.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The dispute

"In a recent series of papers, Schwinger discussed a process that he called the Dynamic Casimir Effect. The key essence of this effect is the change in zero-point energy associated with [changes] in a dielectric medium. (In particular, if the change in the dielectric medium is the growth or collapse of a bubble, this effect may have relevance to sonoluminescence.)"

Schwinger's claim

"the change in Casimir energy is proportional to the [volume] of the dielectric, plus finite-volume corrections. Other papers have called [this] into question, claiming that the volume term should actually be discarded [and that the result] is proportional to the surface area of the dielectric."

The method

"In [this paper, which is a companion to] an earlier letter on the same topic, we present a careful [re-analysis of both] analyses. We find that the Casimir energy, defined as the [change in energy due to the] change in the medium, has at leading order a bulk volume d[ependence] ... with Schwinger's result, once the correct physical question [is posed]."

What is at stake

The paper separates two different quantities that had been conflated: the change in zero-point energy when a medium appears, and the total energy of the configuration. The bulk term belongs to the first, and the surface term to the second, so neither calculation is wrong; they answer different questions.

Scope

The analysis assumes a loss-free dielectric with a real refractive index, and it treats the medium as non-dispersive where the comparison with Schwinger requires it. The companion paper handles the surface area term.

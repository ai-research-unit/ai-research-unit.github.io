What the Paper Does

This essay asks whether the graviton can have a mass. It answers yes, and builds a model. The mass term needs a second metric, so the theory carries a non-dynamical background. The construction passes the classical weak-field and medium-field tests. It changes the strong field: black holes in the usual sense do not exist, and cosmology stops tracking the matter distribution. The author is explicit that the price is prior geometry and that quantisation is untouched.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"Can we give the graviton a mass? Does it even make sense to speak of a massive graviton? In this essay I shall answer these questions in the affirmative."

The obstacles

"introducing a graviton mass will clearly require some rather drastic mutilation of the usual foundations underlying Einstein gravity."

The construction

"To accommodate a massive graviton without sacrificing experimental results such as the Eötvös experiment and the Newtonian limit, and do so without the theoretical complications of a higher-derivative theory, I shall explore the option of adding prior geometry by introducing a background metric."

The mass term

$$S_{\text{mass}}(g,g_0)=\tfrac{1}{2}\frac{m_g^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\int\sqrt{-g_0}\left\{(g_0^{-1})^{\mu\nu}(g-g_0)_{\mu\sigma}(g_0^{-1})^{\sigma\rho}(g-g_0)_{\rho\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left[(g_0^{-1})^{\mu\nu}(g-g_0)_{\mu\nu}\right]^2\right\} \qquad (paper\ 5)$$

What passes

"The variant theory of gravity I have sketched in this essay—a specific proposal for giving the graviton a mass—passes all present tests of classical gravity."

What changes

"Black holes in the usual sense do not exist in this theory, and large-scale cosmology is divorced from the distribution of matter."

The cost

"To do all this we have to sacrifice something: the theory exhibits prior geometry, and depends on a non-dynamical background metric."

The admission

"Clearly, the theory presented in this essay is far from being completely and definitively understood: there are a lot of issues (such as quantization) ripe for further development."

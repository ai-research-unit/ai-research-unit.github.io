What the Paper Does

Zipf's law and other power laws appear across many fields. A recent random group formation model explains them by maximum entropy with a chosen cost function. The author argues the cost function is the key assumption. With the constraint $\langle \ln n\rangle=\chi$ the power law follows simply. Variants, including hybrid geometric/power laws and finite state spaces, are treated.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The reach of the problem

"Zipf's law, and power laws in general, have attracted and continue to attract considerable attention in a wide variety of disciplines — from astronomy to demographics to software structure to economics to linguistics to zoology, and even warfare."

The model under review

"A recent model of random group formation [RGF] attempts a general explanation of such phenomena based on Jaynes' notion of maximum entropy applied to a particular choice of cost function."

The criticism

"In the present article I argue that the specific cost function used in the RGF model [is the key assumption]"

The main point

"The main point of this article is that power laws, (and their variants, including hybrid geometric/power laws), have a very natural and straightforward interpretation in terms of the maximum entropy formalism pioneered by Jaynes."

The mechanism

"The key to obtaining a pure power law in the simplest possible manner lies in maximizing the Shannon entropy while imposing the simple constraint $\langle\ln n\rangle=\chi$."

The variation

"Depending on other features of the [constraints, other distributions follow]"

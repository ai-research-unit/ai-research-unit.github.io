What the Paper Does

The paper proves a rigidity theorem for rotating stars in three spacetime dimensions. Take an axially symmetric star that may be pulsating, and look at the vacuum region outside it, allowing a cosmological constant. The Einstein equations then force that exterior to be stationary, and force it to be the three-dimensional Kerr–de Sitter geometry, the BTZ metric. The proof is a direct computation of the vacuum equations in a suitable coordinate system, and the authors state that the result is special to 2+1 dimensions and fails in 3+1. They interpret it as a no-hair theorem for stars rather than black holes, and they identify the absence of gravitational radiation in three dimensions as the physical reason.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"Consider a rotating and possibly pulsating 'star' in (2+1) dimensions. If the star is axially symmetric, then in the vacuum region surrounding the star, (a region that we assume at most contains a cosmological constant), the Einstein equations imply that under physically plausible conditions the geometry is in fact stationary."

The uniqueness

"Furthermore, the geometry external to the star is then uniquely guaranteed to be the (2+1) dimensional analogue of the Kerr-de Sitter spacetime, the BTZ geometry. This Birkhoff-like theorem is very special to (2+1) dimensions, and fails in (3+1) dimensions."

Why three dimensions are tractable

"In (2+1) dimensions, the Einstein equations are much simpler. Outside the star the spacetime is (locally) a constant curvature spacetime; it is locally one of the de Sitter/Minkowski/anti-de Sitter spacetimes depending on the sign of the cosmological constant. This gives us some hope, borne out by direct calculation, that the situation might be more tractable."

The mechanism

"Physically, we feel that it is the absence of gravitational radiation in (2+1) dimensions that lies at the heart of the (axial)+(vacuum) ⇒ (stationary) result; without gravitational radiation there is no way for the (2+1) star to communicate information regarding its internal state to the exterior region. This is effectively a 'no hair theorem', in (2+1) dimensions for stars rather than black holes — in axial symmetry the exterior geometry is completely specified by the mass, angular momentum, and cosmological constant."

The computation

"We have now completely solved the vacuum Einstein equations", the authors write, after imposing the axial symmetry and reducing the metric to two functions $B(r)$ and $\omega(r)$ plus the angular part. The solution is then the BTZ family, with the cosmological constant and two integration constants that are identified with the mass and angular momentum.

The stated limits

"We have not included either electric or magnetic charges in our analysis. It would also be interesting to seek an interior solution." The theorem is also restricted to axial symmetry, to the vacuum exterior, and to 2+1 dimensions, where the result is stated to fail in 3+1.

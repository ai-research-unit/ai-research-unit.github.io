What the Paper Does

Regular black holes have an outer and an inner horizon. If the inner horizon has nonzero surface gravity, perturbations grow exponentially there. That is the mass inflation instability, and it threatens these models. The authors show that the inner surface gravity can be made to vanish while the geometry stays regular. They build explicit metrics and confirm the absence of growth numerically. The models are thereby put back on the table as alternatives to singular black holes.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"Generic models of regular black holes have separate outer and inner horizons, both with nonzero surface gravity."

The instability

"It has been shown that a nonzero inner horizon surface gravity results in exponential instability at the inner horizon controlled by this parameter."

The stakes

"its presence has put in question the physical viability of regular black holes as alternatives to their (singular) general relativity counterparts"

The result

"we show that it is possible to make the inner horizon surface gravity vanish, while maintaining the se[paration of horizons]"

The check

"in order to check that the absence of the instability is due the the vanishing of the surface gravity at the inner horizon, we will study the slightly modified geometry"

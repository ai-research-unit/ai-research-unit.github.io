What the Paper Does

The authors catalogue the ways a cosmological model can begin, end, or turn around. They define each event kinematically by the behaviour of the scale factor near the event, using generalised power series. They then add dynamics through the Friedmann equations and ask which energy conditions hold at each milestone. From this they derive necessary and sufficient conditions for bangs, bounces, crunches, rips, sudden singularities, and extremality events. The results are model independent because no equation of state is assumed.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The motivation

"the zoo of cosmological singularities considered in the literature has become considerably more extensive, with 'big rips' and 'sudden singularities' added to the mix, as well as renewed interest in non-singular cosmological events such as 'bounces' and 'turnarounds'."

The programme

"In this article we present a complete catalogue of such cosmological milestones, both at the kinematical and dynamical level."

The kinematic part

"using generalized power series, purely kinematical definitions of these cosmological events are provided in terms of the behaviour of the scale factor $a(t)$."

The dynamical part

"dynamical information is extracted by using the Friedmann equations (without assuming even the existence of any equation of state) to place constraints on whether or not the classical energy conditions are satisfied at the cosmological milestones."

The result

"We use these considerations to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of cosmological milestones such as bangs, bounces, crunches, rips, sudden singularities, and extremality events."

The generality

"Since the classification is extremely general, the corresponding results are to a high degree model-independent."

The general lesson

"If in the vicinity of any cosmological milestone, the input scale factor $a(t)$ is a generalized power series, then all physical observables ($H$, $q$, the Riemann tensor, etc.) will likewise be generalized power series, with related indicial exponents that can be calculated from the indicial exponents of the scale factor."

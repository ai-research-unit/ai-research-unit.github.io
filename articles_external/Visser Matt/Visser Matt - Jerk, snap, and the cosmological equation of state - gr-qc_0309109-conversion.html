What the Paper Does

Instead of assuming an equation of state and predicting the expansion, this paper takes the expansion history and infers the equation of state. A Taylor expansion of $p(\rho)$ about today is the most general choice. The coefficients of that expansion map onto time derivatives of the scale factor: the Hubble rate, the deceleration, the jerk, and the snap. The linear coefficient needs the jerk, which is poorly measured. That, the author argues, is why direct constraints on the cosmological equation of state remain weak.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The approach

"Taylor expanding the cosmological equation of state around the current epoch ... is the simplest model one can consider that does not make any a priori restrictions on the nature of the cosmological fluid."

Predictive versus retrodictive

"Most popular cosmological models attempt to be 'predictive' ... In contrast, a 'retrodictive' approach might usefully take observational data concerning the scale factor, and use the Friedmann equations to infer an observed cosmological equation of state."

The dictionary

"Determining the first three Taylor coefficients of the equation of state at the current epoch requires a measurement of the deceleration, jerk, and snap — the second, third, and fourth derivatives of the scale factor with respect to time."

The main formula

$$\kappa_0=-\frac{1}{3}\frac{1-j_0+kc^2/(H_0^2a_0^2)}{1+q_0+kc^2/(H_0^2a_0^2)}\approx-\frac{1}{3}\frac{1-j_0}{1+q_0} \qquad (paper\ 26)$$

Why it matters

"to obtain the linearized equation of state you need significantly more information than the deceleration parameter $q_0$; you also need to measure the jerk parameter $j_0$."

What a single number cannot do

"If the only observations you have are measurements of the deceleration parameter then you can of course determine $w_0=p_0/\rho_0$, but this is not an equation of state for the cosmological fluid. Determining $w_0$ merely provides information about the present-day value of $p/\rho$ but makes no prediction as to what this ratio will do in the future"

The degeneracy

"Though these models often make dramatically differing predictions in the distant past (e.g., a 'bounce') or future (e.g., a 'big rip') there is considerable degeneracy among the models in that many physically quite different models are compatible with present day observations."

The limitation

"the jerk is relatively poorly bounded, and as a consequence direct observational constraints on the cosmological EOS (in the form of measurements of $\kappa_0=[dp/d\rho]_0$) are currently extremely poor and are likely to remain poor for the foreseeable future."

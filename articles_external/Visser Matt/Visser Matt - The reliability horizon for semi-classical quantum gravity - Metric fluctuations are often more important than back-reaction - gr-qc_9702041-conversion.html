What the Paper Does

This note introduces the reliability horizon for semiclassical quantum gravity. The idea is to quantify where the approximation can be trusted. The central claim is comparative: before back-reaction becomes important, metric fluctuations already dominate. So the usual focus on back-reaction is misplaced. The author estimates the scales where each effect takes over.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The notion

"In this note I introduce the notion of the 'reliability horizon' for semi-classical quantum gravity."

The purpose

"This reliability horizon is an attempt to quantify the extent to which we should trust semi-classical quantum gravity, and to get a better han[dle on the Planck regime]"

The starting point

"the answer to this question has been known since the pioneering work of Wheeler in the late 1950's: We should certainly stop believing semi-classical quantum gravity once we enter the Planck regime"

The comparison

"Metric fluctuations are often more important than back-reaction"

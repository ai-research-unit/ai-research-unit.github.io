What the Paper Does

The paper builds thin-shell traversable wormholes by cutting and pasting two spherically symmetric spacetimes. It gives a unified linearised stability analysis for such shells under spherically symmetric perturbations. The exotic matter is confined to a thin shell, which is allowed to move in the bulk, so the shell is dynamic. A flux term is retained that the literature had mostly ignored. The authors invert the usual procedure: instead of parameterising by a speed of sound, they treat surface mass as a function of a potential and derive stability inequalities.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The construction

"We consider the construction of generic spherically symmetric thin-shell traversable wormhole spacetimes in standard general relativity. By using the cut-and-paste procedure, we comprehensively analyze the stability of arbitrary spherically symmetric thin-shell wormholes to linearized spherically symmetric perturbations around static solutions."

The aim of generality

"While a number of special cases have previously been dealt with in scattered parts of the literature, herein we take considerable effort to make the analysis as general and unified as practicable."

The central result

"We demonstrate in full generality that stability of the wormhole is equivalent to choosing suitable properties for the exotic material residing on the wormhole throat."

The setup

"While constrained by spherical symmetry it is allowed to move freely within the bulk spacetimes, which permits a fully dynamic analysis. To this effect, we have considered in great detail the presence of a flux term, which has been widely ignored in the literature. This flux term corresponds to the net discontinuity in the conservation law of the surface stresses of the bulk momentum flux"

The inverted approach

"rather than adopt the latter approach, we considered that the stability of the wormhole is fundamentally linked to the behaviour of the surface mass $m_s(a)$ of the thin shell of exotic matter, residing on the wormhole throat, via a pair of stability inequalities."

What this implies

"This novel procedure implicitly makes demands on the equation of state of the matter residing on the transition layer, and demonstrates in full generality that the stability of thin shell wormholes is equivalent to choosing suitable properties for the material residing on the thin shell."

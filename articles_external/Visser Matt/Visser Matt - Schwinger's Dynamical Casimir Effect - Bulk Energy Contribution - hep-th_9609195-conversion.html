What the Paper Does

Schwinger proposed the dynamical Casimir effect as an explanation for sonoluminescence. Several papers then argued his energy estimate was wrong. The authors reply that those calculations leave out the volume term. With the term included, the estimate agrees with Schwinger's. They say nothing about whether sonoluminescence is actually explained.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The context

"Schwinger's Dynamical Casimir Effect is one of several candidate explanations for sonoluminescence."

The challenge

"Recently, several papers have claimed that Schwinger's estimate of the Casimir energy involved is grossly inaccurate."

The reply

"In this letter, we show that these calculations omit the crucial volume term."

The result

"When the missing term is correctly included one finds full agreement with Schwinger's result for the Dynamical Casimir Effect."

The limit

"We have nothing new to say about sonoluminescence itself except to affirm that the Casimir effect is energetically adequate as a candidate"

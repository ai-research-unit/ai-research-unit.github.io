What the Paper Does

Bimetric gravity turns on a matrix square root of one metric inverse times the other, which is awkward to compute. The authors propose a generalised Gordon ansatz, motivated by analogue spacetimes, that makes the square root explicit. Under that ansatz the effective stress-energy is automatically a perfect fluid. A Kerr–Schild variant gives a null fluid instead. They show the ansatz covers bimetric cosmologies and massive-gravity black holes, and that the two null energy conditions can hold together only if the effect reduces to a cosmological constant.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The ansatz

"We develop the 'generalized Gordon ansatz' for the ghost-free versions of both massive and bimetric gravity, an ansatz which is general enough to include almost all spacetimes commonly considered to be physically interesting, and restricted enough to greatly simplify calculations."

What it achieves

"The ansatz allows explicit calculation of the matrix square root $\gamma=\sqrt{g^{-1}f}$ appearing as a central feature of the ghost-free analysis."

The fluid result

"this ansatz automatically allows us to write the effective stress-energy tensor as that corresponding to a perfect fluid."

The variant

"A qualitatively similar 'generalized Kerr–Schild ansatz' can also be easily considered, now leading to an effective stress-energy tensor that corresponds to a null fluid."

The energy condition result

"we have shown that the simultaneous fulfillment of the NEC associated to the modifications of general relativity in both foreground and background spaces is only possible if it saturates; that is if the effect of these modifications are equivalent to the consideration of a cosmological constant."

The reach

"we have shown that it can describe the general class of cosmological solutions in bimetric gravity, as well as black holes in massive gravity."

The Kerr–Schild counterpart

"even in this case similar conclusions can be extracted about the simultaneous fulfillment of both NECs — this is only possible in the trivial case in which both metrics have the same light cone structure."

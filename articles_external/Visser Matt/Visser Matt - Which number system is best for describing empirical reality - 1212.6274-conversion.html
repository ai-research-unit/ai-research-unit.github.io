What the Paper Does

Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness" is both trivial and profound, the author says. The mathematics was built to describe reality in the first place. Still, parts of the structure have taken on a life of their own. The essay walks through number systems from integers to reals to complex and beyond, and asks which is best suited to empirical description. The answer depends on what is being described.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The starting point

"Eugene Wigner's much-discussed notion of the 'unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics' as applied to describing the physics of empirical reality is simultaneously both trivial and profound."

Why trivial

"After all, the relevant mathematics was (in the first instance) originally developed in order to be useful in describing empirical reality."

The complication

"On the other hand, certain aspects of the mathematical superstructure have by now taken on a life of their own"

The programme

"Pre-Calculus", "Calculus", "Betwixt the Rationals and the Reals", "The 'Best' Number System for Physics?"

The survey

"Rational Cartesian Geometry", "Quadratic-Surd Cartesian Geometry", "Constructible Cartesian Geometry", "Euclidean vs. Post-Euclidean Construct[ions]"

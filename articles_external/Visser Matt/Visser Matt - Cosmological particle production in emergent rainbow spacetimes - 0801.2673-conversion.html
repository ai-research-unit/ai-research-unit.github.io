What the Paper Does

The paper studies particle production in a cosmological setting where Lorentz invariance holds at low energy and fails in the ultraviolet, so that the effective metric depends on momentum. Such rainbow metrics arise naturally when spacetime is treated as emergent from a microscopic substrate, and the authors realise the model in a parametrically excited Bose–Einstein condensate. They compare the full nonperturbative calculation with the standard perturbative modification of the dispersion relation, and find that very low energy modes see nothing, some modes re-enter the horizon during inflation, and extreme ultraviolet modes are insensitive to the expansion. After many e-foldings the spectrum becomes time independent and decays exponentially.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The model

"We investigate cosmological particle production in spacetimes where Lorentz invariance emerges in the infrared limit, but is explicitly broken in the ultraviolet regime. Thus these models are similar to many (but not all) models of quantum gravity, where a breakdown of Lorentz invariance is expected for ultraviolet physics around the Planck / string scale."

The mechanism

"Our specific model focuses on the boost subgroup that supports CPT invariance and results in a momentum-dependent dispersion relation. Motivated by previous studies on spacetimes emerging from a microscopic substrate, we show how these modifications naturally lead to momentum-dependent rainbow metrics."

The realisation

"models involving a time-dependent microscopic interaction are suitable for mimicking quantum effects in FRW spacetimes. Within certain limits the analogy is sufficiently good to simulate relativistic quantum field theory in time-dependent classical backgrounds, and the quantum effects are approximately robust against the model-dependent modifications."

What changes

"While very low-energy modes do not see the difference at all, some modes 're-enter the Hubble horizon' during the inflationary epoch, and extreme ultraviolet modes are completely insensitive to the expansion."

Why the full calculation matters

"The analysis outlined here, because it is nonperturbative in the rainbow metric, exhibits features that cannot be extracted simply from the standard perturbative modification of particle dispersion relations."

The final spectrum

"However, we also show how the final result, after many e-foldings, will approach a time-independent exponentially decaying particle spectrum."

The authors' own assessment

"There were two main lessons learnt for the analogue model community. First, the specific model we presented — a uniform gas of atoms with time-dependent [interaction] — is in general not robust against the non-perturbative ultraviolet corrections." And the analogy "only holds for the low-energy part of the spectrum, and therefore the analogy is associated with a certain $k$-range."

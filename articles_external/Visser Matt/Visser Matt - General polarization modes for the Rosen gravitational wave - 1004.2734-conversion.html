What the Paper Does

Strong gravitational plane waves come in Brinkmann and Rosen forms, related by a coordinate change. The two forms handle linear polarisations alike but differ for circular, elliptic, and general polarisations. The authors analyse the Rosen form with an envelope function and two freely specifiable polarisation functions. The vacuum equations then reduce to one equation for the envelope coupled to what they call a random walk in polarisation space. They construct a circularly polarised strong-field Rosen wave and indicate how the idea generalises beyond four dimensions.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The puzzle

"While these two metric ansatzë are related by a coordinate transformation, so that they should describe essentially the same physics, they rather puzzlingly seem to treat polarization states quite differently."

The asymmetry

"Both ansatzë deal equally well with + and × linear polarizations, but there is a qualitative difference in the way they deal with circular, elliptic, and more general polarization states."

The construction

"In this article we will develop a general formalism for dealing with arbitrary polarization states in the Rosen form of the gravitational wave metric, representing an arbitrary polarization by a trajectory in a suitably defined two dimensional hyperbolic plane."

The reformulation

"To address this puzzle we have re-analyzed the Rosen gravity wave in terms of an 'envelope' function and two freely specifiable functions corresponding to the individual polarization modes."

The field equations

"The vacuum field equations can be reinterpreted in terms of a single differential equation governing the 'envelope' coupled with what is essentially a random walk in polarization space."

The results

"we have indicated how to construct a circularly polarized strong field Rosen form gravity wave, and how to generalize this central idea beyond (3+1) dimensions."

The surprise

"Strong field gravitational waves, both in Brinkmann form and Rosen form, have been known for some 85 years. Despite this there are still a number of surprises hiding in the Rosen form."

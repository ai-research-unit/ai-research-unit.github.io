What the Paper Does

This short proceedings note explains how massive gravity sits inside bimetric gravity. Bimetric gravity has two dynamical metrics and hence two sets of equations; massive gravity has one equation and a fixed background. Taking the limit of vanishing kinetic term keeps a constraint from the second Bianchi identity. That constraint forces the interaction term to be constant, so the effect reduces to an effective cosmological constant. The note then lists the cosmological solutions of this type.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim

"Massive gravity may be viewed as a suitable limit of bimetric gravity. The limiting procedure can lead to an interesting interplay between the 'background' and 'foreground' metrics in a cosmological context."

The asymmetry

"Thus, solutions of bimetric gravity in the limit of vanishing kinetic term are also solutions of massive gravity, but the contrary statement is not necessarily true."

Why massive gravity needs a second tensor

"to construct such a mass term in the action one needs an additional two-tensor, $f_{\mu\nu}$."

Which limit to take

"the limit of vanishing $\kappa$ and $\epsilon$, that is of vanishing kinetic term, must be taken in bimetric gravity to recover massive gravity."

What survives

"the Bianchi inspired constraint can be written as $\partial_\lambda L_{\text{int}}=0$, which implies a modification equivalent to a cosmological constant, $T^{(\text{eff})\mu}{}_{\nu}=-\Lambda_{\text{eff}}\,\delta^\mu{}_\nu$."

The solutions

"Conformally related metrics" and "Conformally related sections"

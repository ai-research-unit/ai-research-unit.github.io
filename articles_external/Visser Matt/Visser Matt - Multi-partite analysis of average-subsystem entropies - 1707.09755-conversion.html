What the Paper Does

The authors study average subsystem entropies: trace out part of a random pure state, compute the entropy of the remainder, and average over states. They extend the standard bipartite results with the average tangle and a bound on average concurrence. They then treat tripartite systems and general multipartite ones. The main gain is that a third subsystem can act as an environment. They show that small subsystems are near maximally mixed, and that two small collections have average mutual information below one nat when the rest is large.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The definition

"So-called average subsystem entropies are defined by first taking partial traces over some pure state to define density matrices, then calculating the subsystem entropies, and finally averaging over the pure states to define the average subsystem entropies."

The construction

"This analysis takes the 'universe' to be in a random pure state, splits the universe into sub-systems which are not pure, (and so have non-zero subsystem entropies), and averages over the pure states."

Why tripartite

"A particularly nice feature of tripartite and multi-partite analyses is that this framework allows one to introduce an 'environment' for small subsystems to couple to."

The bipartite result used

$$S_{n_A,n_B}=\langle S_A\rangle=\langle S_B\rangle=H_m^{M}-H_M-\frac{m-1}{2M},\qquad m=\min\{n_A,n_B\},\ M=\max\{n_A,n_B\} \qquad (paper\ 3.2,\ 3.3)$$

The general bound

$$\langle S(\rho_{ijk\ldots})\rangle=\ln(m_{ijk\ldots})+\Delta,\qquad \Delta\in\left[-\tfrac12,0\right] \qquad (paper\ 5.10)$$

The reading of the bound

"we see that any collection of these average subsystems is close to being maximally mixed, in fact within $\tfrac12$ a nat of maximal mixing."

The mutual information bound

"the average mutual information between any two 'small' collections of subsystems, $A$ and $B$, in the multi-partite pure-state system never exceeds $\tfrac12$ nat as long as the subsystem collection $C$ is dominant"

The thermodynamic limit

"letting $n\to\infty$ while keeping $n_{ijk\ldots}$ fixed, we see $\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle S(\rho_{ijk\ldots})\rangle=\ln(n_{ijk\ldots})$."

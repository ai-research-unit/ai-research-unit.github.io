What the Paper Does

A Bose–Einstein condensate can give its perturbations an effective spacetime. The authors show that this effective geometry can have regions of Euclidean signature. The signature is set by microscopic details, not imposed by hand. They connect this to quantum gravity models that give up Lorentz invariance at short distances. The condensate is a concrete system where a signature change could be studied.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The phenomenon

"exhibiting finite regions of Euclidean (Riemannian) signature"

The realisation

"we will demonstrate its direct physical relevance for an explicitly realizable condensed matter system whose linearized perturbations experience an effective emergent spacetime geometry with externally controllable signature"

The origin

"This effective geometry is intrinsically quantum in origin, and its signature is determined by the details of the microscopic structure."

The motivation

"The recent interest in quantum gravity models sacrificing Lorentz symmetry as an exact symmetry in order to obtain better control of the ultraviolet behaviour ... has motivated us"

The subject

"the concept of a spacetime emergent from a Bose–Einstein condensate, and the possibility of signature change events within this context"

What the Paper Does

The Lense–Thirring metric is a slow-rotation approximation, not an exact solution. The authors pick a variant chosen for simplicity and recast it in Painlevé–Gullstrand form. This gives unit lapse, flat spatial slices, a simple tetrad, and simple curvature tensors. They verify that the variant is Petrov type I and compute its curvature invariants and horizons. They note that replacing the mass by a function of radius accommodates dark matter halos.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The object

"The standard Lense–Thirring metric is a century-old slow-rotation large-distance approximation to the gravitational field outside a rotating massive body, depending only on the total mass and angular momentum of the source."

What they build

"we shall construct an explicit unit-lapse Painlevé–Gullstrand variant of the Lense–Thirring spacetime, that has flat spatial slices, a very simple and physically intuitive tetrad, and extremely simple curvature tensors."

The algebraic type

"We shall verify that this variant of the Lense–Thirring spacetime is Petrov type I, (so it is not algebraically special)"

The metric

$$ds^2=-dt^2+\left(dr+\sqrt{2m/r}\,dt\right)^2+r^2\left(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\left(d\phi-\frac{2J}{r^3}dt\right)^2\right) \qquad (paper\ 11.1)$$

The extension

"with the slight generalization $m\to m(r)$ ... one can accommodate spherically symmetric dark matter halos, so one has a plausible approximation to the gravitational fields of spiral galaxies."

The verdict

"this specific Painlevé–Gullstrand variant of the Lense–Thirring spacetime is rather easy to work with."

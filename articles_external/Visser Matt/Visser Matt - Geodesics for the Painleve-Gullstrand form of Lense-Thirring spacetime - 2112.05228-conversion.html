What the Paper Does

A recent Painlevé–Gullstrand version of slow-rotation Lense–Thirring spacetime has unit lapse, flat spatial slices, and separable wave and Hamilton–Jacobi equations. The paper works out its geodesics. A nontrivial Killing tensor supplies a Carter constant as a fourth conserved quantity, so the equations are formally integrable. In general the integrals are ultra-elliptic and not elementary; closed forms appear only under further conditions such as vanishing Carter constant. The authors classify the resulting geodesics as drip, rain, and hail by energy, and present the spacetime as a black hole mimic.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The spacetime

"Recently, the current authors have formulated and extensively explored a rather novel Painlevé–Gullstrand variant of the slow-rotation Lense–Thirring spacetime, a variant which has particularly elegant features — including unit lapse, intrinsically flat spatial 3-slices, and a separable Klein–Gordon equation (wave operator)."

The integrability

"This spacetime also possesses a non-trivial Killing tensor, implying separability of the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, the existence of a Carter constant, and complete formal integrability of the geodesic equations."

The main finding

"herein we investigate the geodesics in some detail, in the general situation demonstrating the occurrence of 'ultra-elliptic' integrals. Only in certain special cases can the complete geodesic integrability be explicitly cast in terms of elementary functions."

The summary

"once given the non-trivial Killing tensor for the Lense–Thirring spacetime, we can extract the Carter constant; the fourth constant of the motion. Then the geodesic equations become integrable at least in principle (in terms of ultra-elliptic integrals). This allows us to formally solve for myriads of general geodesics."

The limitation

"we saw that in full generality, we could not explicitly integrate the equations of motion in closed form, the ultra-elliptic integrals are not 'elementary'. Only when imposing further conditions, such as Carter constant zero, could we then explicitly integrate the equations of motion in algebraically closed form."

The classification of solutions

"the Carter constant zero geodesics with $E<1$ are the 'drip' geodesics of the spacetime, while the Carter constant zero geodesics with $E=1$ are the 'rain' geodesics, and the Carter constant zero geodesics with $E>1$ are the 'hail' geodesics of the spacetime."

The status of the model

"The model is potentially of astrophysical interest both in the asymptotic large-distance limit and as an example of a 'black hole mimic', a controlled deformation of the Kerr spacetime that can be contrasted with ongoing astronomical observations."

What the Paper Does

The authors take Visser's flat-space wormhole, framed by a loop of exotic string with negative tension, and ask what happens if it sits in a dense region. Mass flows through, one mouth gains and the other loses, and the far mouth can end up with negative mass. They compute the lensing signature of such an object. A negative-mass lens defocuses rather than focuses, so a background star dims and then brightens in a profile unlike a normal lens. They recommend that microlensing surveys search for this shape and call the objects GNACHOs.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The object

"Visser has suggested traversable 3-dimensional wormholes that could plausibly form naturally during Big Bang inflation."

The mechanism

"A wormhole mouth embedded in high mass density might accrete mass, giving the other mouth a net negative mass of unusual gravitational properties."

The consequence for lensing

"The lensing of such a gravitationally negative anomalous compact halo object (GNACHO) will enhance background stars with a time profile that is observable and qualitatively different from that recently observed for massive compact halo objects (MACHOs) of positive mass."

The condition for negative total mass

"Note that in hypothesizing the existence of such a wormhole, one has to abandon the averaged null energy condition. Therefore, the hypotheses underlying the positive mass theorem no longer apply and there is nothing, in principle, to prevent the occurrence of negative total mass."

The flow

"As this mass passes through the wormhole, the entrance mouth will gain mass while the exit mouth will lose mass by the same amount. Soon, if the mass flow continues, the exit wormhole mouth will acquire a net negative mass."

The recommendation

"We recommend that MACHO search data be analyzed for GNACHOs."

The claim about detectability

"The calculations presented above show that objects of negative gravitational mass, if they exist, can provide a very distinctive light enhancement profile."

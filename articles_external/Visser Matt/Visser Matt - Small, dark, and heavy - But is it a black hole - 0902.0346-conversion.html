What the Paper Does

Astronomers see objects that are small, dark, and heavy. The standard reading is that these are black holes. This conference talk argues the consensus leaves little room, then examines one concrete alternative. The authors set out what such an alternative must do to fit the same data. The talk was given at a meeting on black holes in general relativity and string theory.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The data

"Astronomers have certainly observed things that are small, dark, and heavy."

The question

"But are these objects really black holes in the sense of general relativity?"

The consensus

"The consensus opinion is simply 'yes', and there is very little 'wriggle room'."

The purpose

"We discuss one of the specific alternatives."

What the Paper Does

The fourth paper in the series treats the Unruh vacuum for a conformally coupled massless scalar in Schwarzschild spacetime, in the test-field limit. Its main tool is a three-parameter semi-analytic model of the stress-energy tensor that matches all known numerical results to under one percent and respects the known analytic properties. With that model the author shows all pointwise conditions fail throughout the exterior, and the averaged null condition fails on all outgoing radial null geodesics. Two appendices extend the method to the Hartle–Hawking and Boulware states.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The model

"I construct a semi-analytic model for the stress-energy tensor that globally reproduces all known numerical results to within 0.8%, and satisfies all known analytic features of the stress-energy tensor."

The results

"I show that in the Unruh vacuum (1) all standard point-wise energy conditions are violated throughout the exterior region—all the way from spatial infinity down to the event horizon, and (2) the averaged null energy condition is violated on all outgoing radial null geodesics."

The side results in the appendices

"In a pair of appendices I indicate general strategy for constructing semi-analytic models for the stress-energy tensor in the Hartle–Hawking and Boulware states, and show that the Page approximation is in a certain sense the minimal ansatz compatible with general properties of the stress-energy in the Hartle–Hawking state."

The scope of the claim

"Note that I am claiming that the violations are widespread—I am not claiming they are large. These are intrinsically quantum mechanical effects with the typical scale of the effect near the horizon being given by $\langle T^{\hat\mu\hat\nu}\rangle \approx \hbar c^9/(GM)^4$."

The consistency check

"The model passes the consistency test of correctly predicting the luminosity from the fit to the transverse pressure."

The pattern across states

"in view of the results quoted in [8, 9] we know that this is not a peculiarity of the Unruh vacuum, but that energy condition violations are also widespread in the Hartle–Hawking and Boulware vacuum states."

The alternative constraint

"Ford and Roman have argued [29, 30, 31] that in many situations the quantum inequalities may be of more interest than the energy conditions themselves. It seems that even if the energy condition violations are widespread, the quantum inequalities may more stringently constrain the dynamics"

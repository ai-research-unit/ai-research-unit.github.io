What the Paper Does

Topology is too coarse to locate a wormhole throat, the authors argue. They define a throat in any static spacetime as a two-dimensional constant-time hypersurface of minimal area, characterised by vanishing trace of the extrinsic curvature plus a flare-out condition. From that definition they constrain the Riemann and stress-energy tensors at the throat, and prove theorems on energy condition violation. These theorems do not use geodesic averaging and do not assume spherical symmetry or asymptotic flatness. The authors name staticity as the main limitation and defer time dependence.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The complaint about topology

"Traversable wormholes have traditionally been viewed as intrinsically topological entities in some multiply connected spacetime. Here, we show that topology is too limited a tool to accurately characterize a generic traversable wormhole: in general one needs geometric information to detect the presence of a wormhole, or more precisely to locate the wormhole throat."

The definition

"For an arbitrary static spacetime we shall define the wormhole throat in terms of a 2–dimensional constant-time hypersurface of minimal area. (Zero trace for the extrinsic curvature plus a 'flare–out' condition.)"

What it buys

"This enables us to severely constrain the geometry of spacetime at the wormhole throat and to derive generalized theorems regarding violations of the energy conditions—theorems that do not involve geodesic averaging but nevertheless apply to situations much more general than the spherically symmetric Morris–Thorne traversable wormhole."

The energy condition result

"[For example: the null energy condition (NEC), when suitably weighted and integrated over the wormhole throat, must be violated.]"

The stated limitation

"The major technical limitation of the current approach is that we work in a static spacetime—this is already a quite rich and complicated system."

The generality

"We do not need to make any assumptions about the existence of any asymptotically flat region, nor do we need to assume that the manifold is topologically non-trivial. It is important to realise that the essence of the definition lies in the geometrical structure of the wormhole throat."

The relation to earlier work

"These theorems generalise the Morris–Thorne results on exotic matter [1], and are complementary to the topological censorship theorem [5]."

The deferred case

"Generalization to the time dependent situation is clearly of interest. Unfortunately we have encountered many subtleties of definition, notation, and formalism in this endeavour. We defer the issue of time dependent wormhole throats to a future publication."

What the Paper Does

The paper treats quasinormal modes for spacetimes with two horizons close together, so that an observer sits between them. The authors note this is physically different from a near-extremal black hole seen from outside. Analytically they show the spacing of quasinormal frequencies equals the surface gravity at the squeezed horizons, and they compute the real part of the frequencies when the horizons are close enough, without requiring degeneracy. The calculation allows arbitrary matter in the region between the horizons, which generalises earlier work on a specific model.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The configuration

"We consider the quasinormal modes for a class of black hole spacetimes that, informally speaking, contain a closely 'squeezed' pair of horizons. (This scenario, where the relevant observer is presumed to be 'trapped' between the horizons, is operationally distinct from near-extremal black holes with an external observer.)"

The results

"It is shown, by analytical means, that the spacing of the quasinormal frequencies equals the surface gravity at the squeezed horizons. Moreover, we can calculate the real part of these frequencies provided that the horizons are sufficiently close together (but not necessarily degenerate or even 'nearly degenerate')."

What is new

"The novelty of our analysis (which extends a model-specific treatment by Cardoso and Lemos) is that we consider 'dirty' black holes; that is, the observable portion of the (static and spherically symmetric) spacetime is allowed to contain an arbitrary distribution of matter."

The perturbations used

"Allowing for the possibility of gravitational radiation, we will (closely following Chandrasekhar) consider small perturbations of the metric and characterize these by $q_r$, $q_t$ and $q_\theta$ ... The above equation describes the so-called axial perturbations, for which a change in the sign of $\phi$ necessitates that the perturbations must also change [sign]."

The dependence on field type

The cross-check

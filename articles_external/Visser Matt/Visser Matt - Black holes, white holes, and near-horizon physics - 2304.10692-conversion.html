What the Paper Does

The paper constructs a classical model in which a black hole turns into a white hole. It begins with a one-parameter family of coordinate systems for Schwarzschild that interpolate between Painlevé–Gullstrand, Kerr–Schild, and Eddington–Finkelstein forms, and shows that the black-versus-white distinction is carried by the sign of a single function at the horizon. It then makes that function time-dependent, producing a metric that is no longer a coordinate transformation of Schwarzschild and in which the horizon character changes. The transition is confined to a compact region around $r=2m$, and the authors show it can be arranged to have zero action. They discuss the quantum implication: a superposition of black and white horizons, a "gray" horizon.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"Black and white holes play remarkably contrasting roles in general relativity versus observational astrophysics. While there is observational evidence for the existence of compact objects that are 'cold, dark, and heavy', which thereby are natural candidates for black holes, the theoretically viable time-reversed variants — the 'white holes' — have nowhere near the same level of observational support."

The claim

"Herein we shall explore the theoretical possibility that the connection between black and white holes is much more intimate than commonly appreciated. We shall first construct 'horizon penetrating' coordinate systems that differ from the standard curvature coordinates only in a small near-horizon region, thereby emphasizing that ultimately the distinction between black and white horizons depends only on near-horizon physics."

The metric family

The construction starts from

$$ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)dt^2-2h(r)\,dr\,dt+\frac{1-h(r)^2}{1-2m/r}\,dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2, \qquad (paper\ 6.1)$$

which "for specific choices of $h(r)$ ... returns the Schwarzschild spacetime in other well known coordinates, such as the Painlevé–Gullstand, Kerr–Schild, and Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates."

The black/white switch

"By imposing the restriction $h(2m)=\pm1$ we showed that this line element can model a classical black or white hole where one or the other of the null curves are horizon penetrating with nonzero coordinate velocity" $r_H'=\pm1/(2m\,h'(2m))$ (paper 6.2).

Confining the transition

"By choosing $h(r)$ to be of compact support, we demonstrated that we could confine the non-trivial aspects of black and white horizons to a compact radial region straddling the naïve horizon $r=2m$." Making $h$ time-dependent through a function $s(t)$ then gives a metric that is no longer a coordinate rewriting of Schwarzschild, with all the nontrivial physics in "a finite-duration finite-thickness, (in principle arbitrarily small), region".

Zero action and the quantum reading

"Moreover we shall show that it is possible to arrange the 'black-to-white transition' to have zero action — so that it will not be subject to destructive interference in the Feynman path integral. This then raises the very intriguing possibility that astrophysical black holes might be interpretable in terms of a quantum superposition of black and white horizons — a 'gray' horizon."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Black versus white is a near-horizon fact

The idea. The paper reduces a global causal distinction to the sign of $h(2m)$ in a coordinate system that differs from Schwarzschild only near the horizon.

What it would add. articles_physics/horizons-without-a-global-structure.md argues that the event horizon is defined by a global construction and cannot be recognised pointwise. This paper does not refute that, but it exhibits a family where the black/white distinction is encoded in local data near $r=2m$. The ledger should record the qualification: for Schwarzschild-like metrics the two characters differ by a local sign, so a local observer can distinguish them even though neither can verify that the surface is a causal boundary.

Where it lands. articles_physics/horizons-without-a-global-structure.md, in the section on the three kinds of horizon.

What stands in the way. The construction is restricted to static, spherically symmetric metrics with a fixed horizon radius. Ranked first, because it gives the article a precise boundary case it does not currently have.

What the Paper Does

The paper treats the evaporation of a black hole as bookkeeping. Blackbody radiation carries about 3.9 bits of entropy per photon, and unitarity requires the same amount to sit in correlations. The authors compute the classical Clausius entropy and the Bekenstein entropy of the hole and find that they sum to the initial Bekenstein entropy, as they must. They then compute von Neumann entropies for a tripartite pure system, black hole, radiation, and environment, and find agreement with the classical result to within one nat. The environment is the technical new ingredient: without it, a bipartite model gives a subsystem entropy that starts at zero, which conflicts with the large initial Bekenstein entropy.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The number

"Blackbody radiation contains (on average) an entropy of $3.9\pm2.5$ bits per photon. If the emission process is unitary, then this entropy is exactly compensated by 'hidden information' in the correlations."

The framing

"Despite many claims to the contrary, (assuming unitarity and complete evaporation), the Hawking evaporation process is relatively benign, no worse than burning a lump of coal."

The method

"The key technical aspect of our calculation is a variant of the 'average subsystem' approach developed by Page, which we extend beyond bipartite pure systems, to a tripartite pure system that considers the influence of the environment."

The classical part

"we explicitly calculated the classical thermodynamic (Clausius) entropy and the Bekenstein entropy. We found that they compensate perfectly, summing to the initial Bekenstein entropy of the hole. (As of course they must, given how Bekenstein entropy was originally defined.)"

The quantum part

"Once we had the classical behaviour under control, we proceeded with a quantum entropy argument based on the von Neumann entropy, realizing that on average we need to encode $3.9\pm2.5$ bits of information per emitted quantum to preserve unitarity."

The Page curve

"It is the (symmetric) sawtooth shape of the Page curve that underlies much of the modern discussion surrounding the 'information puzzle', and in particular the asserted and much debated existence of firewalls."

The defect of the bipartite model

"One particularly disturbing feature of the current bipartite argument is that the subsystem entropy is initially zero. But this observation is in marked tension with the fact that the Bekenstein entropy of the black hole is initially enormous."

The result

"We have developed a tripartite system in which, assuming unitarity of the evolution of the (GR black hole) + (Hawking radiation) subsystem, we showed that, as long as it is suitably embedded in a tripartite system providing an environment to entangle with, there are no unusual physical effects; the results completely agree with the classically expected results, to within 1 nat."

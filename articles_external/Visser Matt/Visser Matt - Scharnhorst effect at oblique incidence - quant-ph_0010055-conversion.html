What the Paper Does

Between two plates the vacuum is modified, and light travels slightly faster. The authors compute the effect at oblique incidence. They use nonlinear electrodynamics and symmetry arguments. The effective metric they get differs from earlier work, and they trace the difference to how polarisation sums are handled. They argue the sum must be done iteratively, using the metric being defined. They also show the Casimir vacuum is not birefringent at any angle.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The effect

"We consider the Scharnhorst effect (anomalous photon propagation in the Casimir vacuum) at oblique incidence, calculating both photon speed and polarization states as functions of angle."

The method

"The analysis is performed in the framework of nonlinear electrodynamics and we show that many features of the situation can be extracted solely on the basis of symmetry considerations."

The finding on birefringence

"Although birefringence is common in nonlinear electrodynamics it is not universal; in particular we verify that the Casimir vacuum is not birefringent at any incidence angle."

The correction

"We obtain an 'effective metric' that is subtly different from previous results."

The cause

"The disagreement is due to the way that 'polarization sums' are implemented in the extant literature, and we demonstrate that a fully consistent polarization sum must be implemented via a bootstrap procedure using the effective metric one is attempting to define."

The caveat

"we show that the polarization sum technique is intrinsically an approximation"

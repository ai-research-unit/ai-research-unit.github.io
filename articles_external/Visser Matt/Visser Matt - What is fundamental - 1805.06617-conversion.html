What the Paper Does

The essay asks what physicists mean by "fundamental". The answer changes with time. Theoretical advances change what can be calculated; experimental advances change what can be measured. Epistemology usually follows rather than leads. The author traces examples of these shifts and argues that "fundamental" is a moving label, not a fixed property.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The observation

"Our collective views regarding the question 'what is fundamental?' are continually evolving."

The drivers

"These ontological shifts in what we regard as fundamental are largely driven by theoretical advances ('what can we calculate?'), and experimental advances ('what can we measure?')."

The role of philosophy

"Rarely (in my view) is epistemology the fundamental driver; more commonly epistemology reacts (after a few decades) to what is going on in the theoretical and experimental zeit[geist]"

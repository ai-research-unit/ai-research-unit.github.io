What the Paper Does

This is a proceedings contribution that presents the cosmographic method and applies it to dark energy. The Hubble relation depends only on FLRW symmetry, so the cosmological parameters can be estimated without assuming a dynamical model. The authors fit several inter-related cosmographic variables to the supernova data with attention to how uncertainties are combined, and find that the evidence for acceleration is suggestive but not decisive. They also show that the deceleration parameter flips sign with the choice of distance scale.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The method

"The Hubble relation between distanc[e and redshift is a] relation that depends only on the s[ymmetries of FLRW spacetime and does] not intrinsically make any dynamica[l assumptions]. [It should therefore] be possible to estimate th[e cosmological parameters] making any dynamical assum[ptions]."

What is done

"[We perform] inter-related cosmographic [fits to the super]nova data, [paying] careful attention t[o the treatment of uncertainties], [and find that the] 'prepondera[nce of evidence' favours an accelerating] universe. However we would [argue that without additional theoretical] and observational informatio[n], this conclusion is not cur[rently established beyond reasonable doubt]."

The conclusion

"While the 'preponderance of evidence' is certainly that the universe is currently accelerating, $q_0<0$, this is not yet a 'gold plated' result, at least not without bringing in other physical assumptions and observations; such as a specific dynamical model [e.g., $\Lambda$CDM] and/or invoking knowledge of $\Omega_m$ or the CMB data, all of which are subject to their own additional theoretical assumptions."

The precise claim

"It is certainly more likely that the expansion of the universe is accelerating, than that the expansion of the universe is decelerating — but this is not the same as having definite evidence in favour of acceleration."

The warning

"regardless of one's views on how to combine formal estimates of uncertainty, the very fact that different distance scales yield data-fits with such widely discrepant estimates for the cosmological parameters strongly suggests the need for extreme caution in interpr[eting them]."

The graphical point

The paper includes "transparent graphical repr[sentations] in which acceleration versus deceleration reduce[s to a comparison of whether a curve goes] up or down."

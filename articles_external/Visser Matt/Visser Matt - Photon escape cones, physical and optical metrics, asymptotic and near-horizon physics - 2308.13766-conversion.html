What the Paper Does

The authors study escape cones in any static spherically symmetric spacetime. They separate the physical metric from the optical metric, in which light travels on straight lines in a curved space. Photon spheres turn out to be throats of the optical metric, not of the physical one. The solid angle of the escape cone is then written from the capture cross section, the area of the sphere, and the norm of the timelike Killing vector. Near a horizon it relates to the surface gravity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"We consider the explicit analytic behaviour of photon escape cones in generic static spherically symmetric spacetimes, emphasizing the interplay between the physical spacetime metric and the optical metric"

The key identification

"The circular photon orbits (photon spheres) are shown to be given by wormhole throats in the optical metric, (not the physical metric)"

The formula

"the escape cone solid angle is easily calculable in terms of the capture cross section $\sigma_{\mathrm{capture}}$, the area of the spherical 2-surfaces, and the norm of the timelike Killing vector."

The result for one photon sphere

$$\Delta\Omega_* = 2\pi\left[1+\mathrm{sign}(r_*-r_\gamma)\sqrt{1-\frac{H(r_\gamma)}{H(r_*)}}\right] \qquad (paper\ 8.1)$$

Near the horizon

"for near-horizon photon emission the escape cone solid angle can be related to the surface gravity $\kappa_H$."

The examples

"We provide a number of illustrative examples, involving both black holes and wormholes, including situations with multiple photon spheres."

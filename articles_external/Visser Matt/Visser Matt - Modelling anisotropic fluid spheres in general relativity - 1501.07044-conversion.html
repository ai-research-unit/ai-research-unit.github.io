What the Paper Does

An anisotropic fluid sphere has radial and transverse pressures that differ. The authors show that any static sphere of this kind can be reproduced by a sum of three ordinary matter components: an isotropic charged perfect fluid, an electromagnetic field, and a minimally coupled scalar field. A preferred minimal decomposition is unique. They translate the energy conditions of the sphere into conditions on the three components. The generalised Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation then holds component by component. The authors are careful that this is mimicry of geometry and stress-energy, not a claim about what the matter really is.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim

"We argue that an arbitrary general relativistic static anisotropic fluid sphere, (static and spherically symmetric but with transverse pressure not equal to radial pressure), can nevertheless be successfully mimicked by suitable linear combinations of theoretically attractive and quite simple classical matter"

The components

"a classical (charged) isotropic perfect fluid, a classical electromagnetic field, and a classical (minimally coupled) scalar field."

The uniqueness

"While the most general decomposition is not unique, a preferred minimal decomposition can be constructed that is unique."

The energy conditions

"We show how the classical energy conditions for the anisotropic fluid sphere can be related to energy conditions for the isotropic perfect fluid, electromagnetic field, and scalar field components of the model."

The equilibrium

"The generalized TOV equation implies that the perfect fluid component in this model is automatically in internal equilibrium, with pressure forces, electric forces, and scalar forces balancing the gravitational pseudo-force."

The limitation

"Note that we are not claiming that any anisotropic fluid sphere is physically one of these three-component models, but are instead making the more modest claim that both the spacetime geometry and the total stress-energy can be successfully mimicked by one of these three-component models."

The modesty

"On the one hand this is a very powerful result; on the other hand it is perhaps a little too powerful."

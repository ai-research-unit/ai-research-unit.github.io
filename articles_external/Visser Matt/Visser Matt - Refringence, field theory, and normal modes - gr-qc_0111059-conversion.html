What the Paper Does

A multi-component medium can give light more than one effective metric. The authors ask when such a theory stays compatible with experiment. One route is to force a single effective metric, as the equivalence principle prefers. Another is to allow several metrics that are close enough to pass Eötvös-type tests. They set up the algebra of the general case, derive the normal modes, and recover the acoustic metric as a special case.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The starting point

"there is a single unique effective metric; more complicated situations can lead to bi-metric and multi-metric theories"

The question

"we will investigate the conditions required to keep the situation under control and compatible with experiment"

The two routes

"either by enforcing a unique effective metric (as would be required to be strictly compatible with the Einstein Equivalence Principle), or at the worst by arranging things so that there are multiple metrics that are all 'close' to each other (in order to be compatible with the Eötvös experiment)"

What the Paper Does

This is a survey of systems whose excitations see an effective curved geometry. The template is Unruh's acoustic black hole, where sound is dragged by a moving fluid and trapped behind an acoustic horizon. The survey then covers optics, non-relativistic and relativistic acoustics, Bose–Einstein condensates, surface waves, and optical fibres. Each case is reduced to a propagation equation that can be read as a metric.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The field

"Analogue spacetimes, (and more boldly, analogue models both of and for gravity), have attracted significant and increasing attention over the last decade and a half."

The template

"Perhaps the most straightforward physical example, which serves as a template for most of the others, is Bill Unruh's model for a dumb hole, (mute black hole, acoustic black hole), wherein sound is dragged along by a moving fluid — and can even be trapped behind an acoustic horizon."

The value

"Analogue spacetimes provide general relativists with e[asy access to otherwise inaccessible physics]"

The themes

"Optics: the Gordon metric and its generalizations", "Non-relativistic acoustics: the Unruh metric", "Relativistic acoustics", "Bose–Einstein condensates", "Surface waves and blocking horizons", "Optical fibres/optical gla[ss]"

The conclusion

"The propagation of excitations (either particles or waves) over a background can often (not always) be given a geometric interpretation in therms of some 'analogue spacetime'."

The expectation

"As such a geometric interpretation exists, there is a strong likelihood of significant cross-fertilization of ideas and techniques between general relativity and other branches of physics."

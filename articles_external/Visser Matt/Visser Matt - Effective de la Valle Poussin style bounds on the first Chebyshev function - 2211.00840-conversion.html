What the Paper Does

De la Vallée Poussin's 1898 proof of the prime number theorem included a bound on the first Chebyshev function, but with unspecified constants. The paper makes that bound effective, giving explicit constants and explicit thresholds. The method uses effective results established over the previous fifty years, plus elementary calculus and numerical checking, and it produces two families of explicit bounds. A table lists asymptotically stringent versions with their constants and thresholds.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The original bound

"In 1898 Charles Jean de la Vallée Poussin, as part of his famed proof of the prime number theorem, developed an ineffective bound on the first Chebyshev function of the form"

$$|\vartheta(x)-x|=O\!\left(\sqrt{x}\,\exp\left(-K\sqrt{\ln x}\right)\right).$$

"This bound holds for $x$ sufficiently large, $x\ge x_0$, and $K$ some unspecified positive constant."

The gap

"To the best of my knowledge this bound has never been made effective — I have never yet seen this bound made fully explicit, with precise values being given for $x_0$ and $K$."

What the paper supplies

"using a number of effective results established over the past 50 years, I shall develop two very simple explicit fully effective bounds of this type"

$$|\vartheta(x)-x|<\tfrac14\sqrt{x}\,\exp\left(-\sqrt{\ln x}\right),\qquad (x\ge 2).$$

The honest assessment

"With some hindsight, deriving effective bounds of the de la Vallée Poussin form is, (given various effective results obtained over the last 50 years), seen to be almost trivial. Certainly, (given the effective results reported in [2–6]), nothing deeper than elementary calculus and some slightly tedious numerical checking was required."

Why it is still worth doing

"conceptually it is very pleasant to see simple explicit and effective bounds of the de la Vallée Poussin form dropping out so nicely."

The numerical table

The paper tabulates asymptotically stringent bounds, giving constants $\tilde a$ and $\tilde c$ together with thresholds $x_*$ ranging from $\exp(3000)$ to $\exp(10^7)$.

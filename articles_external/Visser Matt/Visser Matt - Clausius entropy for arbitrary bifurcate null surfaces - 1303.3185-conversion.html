What the Paper Does

The paper extends Jacobson's thermodynamic derivation of the Einstein equations from local Rindler horizons to arbitrary bifurcate null surfaces. It defines a Clausius entropy, $dS=\delta Q/T$, on such a surface and shows that the construction works for any bifurcate null surface, not only a horizon of a specific kind. The entropy that results is observer-dependent, which the authors argue is a feature and not a defect. They implement a generalised second law for it and relate that law to the null energy condition.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The starting point

"Jacobson's thermodynamic derivation of the [Einstein equations] originally applied only to local Rindler horizons. But [this] construction can usefully be extended to give meaning [to entropy on arbitrary bifurcate] null surfaces."

The question it forces

"As presaged in Jacobson's original art[icle, this] sharply brings into focus the questions: Is entropy [objective, or] in some sense subjective and observer-dependent? Thes[e questions open a] Pandora's box of often inconclusive debate."

The consensus the authors work from

"A consensus, [though] not universally held, seems to be that Clausius [entropy] (defined via a Clausius relation $dS=\delta Q/T$) should [be the primary notion, while] the ontological status of statistical entropy ($S=-\mathrm{tr}\,\rho\ln\rho$) [is] much more ambiguous, and much more likely to be [observer-dependent]. Th[is] is particularly pressing when it comes to unders[tanding black] hole entropy."

The result

"The net result of this calculation, and the construction it inspires, is that one can associate an observer-dependent notion of entropy, very closely related to the Clausius entropy (thermodynamic entropy, $\delta Q/T$ entropy), and a generalization of Jacobson's local-Rindler entropy, to any arbitrary bifurcate null surface."

The claim about Clausius entropy

"That is, there is a certain sense in which even Clausius entropy ($\delta Q/T$ entropy) is observer-dependent, with a 'virtual Clausius entropy' being associated with arbitrary bifurcate 'virtual causal horizons'."

The generalised second law

The paper implements "a version of the generalized second law" for this entropy, and "this version of GSL can be related to" the null energy condition. Because the connection is to the null energy condition rather than to a stronger condition, the law holds in settings where other forms do not.

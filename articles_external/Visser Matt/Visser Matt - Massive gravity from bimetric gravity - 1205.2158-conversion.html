What the Paper Does

The authors examine how massive gravity arises as the limit of bimetric gravity. They argue the limit is more delicate than usually assumed. Constraining the background metric is wrong, because the background must be given externally. The second set of equations survives the limit and imposes extra constraints, so the parameter space is not fully continuous. They work in a vierbein formulation and classify the cosmological solutions that are continuous.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The problem with the usual limit

"this limiting procedure should not unnecessarily constrain the background metric, which must be externally specified by the theory of massive gravity itself."

The extra constraints

"The fact that in bimetric theories one always has two sets of metric equations of motion continues to have an effect even in the massive gravity limit, leading to additional constraints besides the one set of equations of motion naively expected."

The discontinuity

"since solutions of bimetric gravity in the limit of vanishing kinetic term are also solutions of massive gravity, but the contrary statement is not necessarily true, there is not complete continuity in the parameter space of the theory."

The method

"We have used a vierbein formulation based on that developed by Volkov"

The first step

"a first step is to realize that the solutions of massive gravity, taken as the limit of a bigravity theory, are in general more constrained than the general solutions of massive gravity."

What the mass does

"the modification to the equation of motion introduced by a non-zero mass of the graviton is equivalent to introducing an extra contribution to the cosmological constant which, at this stage, can be positive or negative."

What is not imposed

"An important fact to stress is that no restriction on curvature or dynamics of the background metric has been imposed."

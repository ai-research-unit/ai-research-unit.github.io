What the Paper Does

Hawking radiation is usually described as a steady thermal flux. The authors compute how far that description is from the truth. For non-super-radiant modes the flux is extremely sparse: the typical time between quanta is much longer than the time set by a quantum's energy. Evaporation is therefore a sequence of two-body decays, not a continuous stream. Semi-analytic estimates tend to understate the sparsity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The picture

"extremely sparse, and extremely thin, with the Hawking quanta, one-by-one, slowly dribbling out of the black hole"

The comparison of timescales

"The typical time between quanta reaching infinity is much larger than the timescale set by the energy of the quanta."

The consequence

"the Hawking evaporation of a black hole should be viewed as a sequential cascade of 2-body decays"

The persistence

"This phenomenon persists throughout the entire evolution of the black hole, both in early stages and (modulo super-radiance) in late stages."

The caveat

"Compared to numerics the semi-analytic estimates often under-estimate sparsity by factors of [order unity]"

What the Paper Does

A recent proposal introduced "twisted" black holes. The authors show these are variants of Taub–NUT spacetimes. They are Ricci flat, so they solve the vacuum equations, but they carry the usual Taub–NUT pathologies. The paper lists these features and argues the spacetimes are unphysical. The lesson is that solving the field equations is not enough.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim under review

"So-called 'twisted' black holes have recently been proposed by Zhang (1609.09721 [gr-qc]), and further considered by Chen and Jing (1610.00886 [gr-qc]), and more recently by Ong (1610.05757 [gr-qc])."

The identification

"While these spacetimes are certainly Ricci-flat, and so mathematically satisfy the vacuum Einstein equations, they are also merely minor variants on Taub–NUT spacetimes."

The verdict

"Consequently they exhibit several unphysical features that make them quite unre[asonable]"

The general lesson

"As mathematics, the Taub–NUT spacetimes are interesting examples of what can go wrong in general relativity if one blindly applies the field equations in isolation, without checking other features of the proposed spacetime."

The final judgement

"As astrophysics, the 'twisted' black holes [are not viable]"

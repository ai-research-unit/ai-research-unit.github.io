What the Paper Does

This is a one-page comment on a claim that primordial gravitational waves could detect a reduction of spacetime dimension at short distances. The authors argue that the claim is misleading for two distinct reasons. First, "dimension" is ambiguous: the spectral dimension, which runs, is not the topological dimension, which stays four, and it is the topological one that appears in the path integral. Second, some of the models cited contain no gravitational degrees of freedom at all, so they cannot constrain a gravitational-wave spectrum.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim being addressed

"It has been argued that quantum grav[ity models] where the number of dimensions reduces at [short distances] exhibit a potentially observable [effect in the] primordial gravitational wave (GW) spectrum." The original work "claimed there that this is a 'generic' and [robust feature] of such models, since '(2+1)-dimensional [spacetimes] have no gravitational degrees of freedom'."

The verdict

"[We find this] claim misleading for two distinct reasons."

The first reason: which dimension

"Definition of dimensionality: It is ambi[guous] which definition of 'dimension' is being us[ed when refer]ring to vanishing dimensions. The o[ther papers] there which discuss vanishing dimension[s in quantum] gravity are [2,3], which discuss the 's[pectral dimension]' (SD). In particular [2] refers to causal [dynamical trian]gulations [CDTs], where it is the SD th[at runs in] the UV, not the topological [physical] [one]; the latter remains 4."

The consequence of the ambiguity

"4-dimensional CDTs by construction [are built from four-]dimensional simplices. The SD is an ana[lytic quantity that] provides information about short-distance [fluctuations]. The fact that it runs to 2 at [the UV does] not mean that any physical modes decouple."

The second reason: no gravitons to lose

"Consider, for instance, Hořava grav[ity, an]other theory where (Lorentz violating) dispersion rela[tions] lead to a SD of 2 in the UV. The spin-2 graviton does not decouple at high energies, it remains part of the physical excitation spectrum, albeit with a strongly Lorentz violating dispersion relation."

The conclusion

"in a wide class of models w[here the notion] of dimension is scale dependent, this i[s the SD]; SD is not the quantity that appears in [path] integrals; [it is the] physical dimension, 4, which is not run[ning]."

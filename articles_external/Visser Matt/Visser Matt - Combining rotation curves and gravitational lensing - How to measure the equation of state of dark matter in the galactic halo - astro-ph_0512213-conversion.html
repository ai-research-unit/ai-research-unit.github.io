What the Paper Does

The paper argues that the standard way of inferring dark-matter density profiles from rotation curves assumes Newtonian gravity, which is an assumption inherited from the cold-dark-matter picture. It sets up a post-Newtonian formalism that does not make that assumption, so that rotation curves and lensing can be used together inside general relativity. Rotation curves then yield one pseudo-mass profile and lensing yields another, and combining them constrains both the density and the pressure of the galactic fluid. The authors present this as a way to measure the equation of state of dark matter.

The extracted abstract is badly broken by the two-column layout; only the legible parts of the body are quoted below.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The assumption being questioned

"We have argued that the standard formalism of rotation curve measurements and gravitational lensing make an a priori Newtonian assumption that is based on the CDM paradigm."

The alternative

"We introduce a post-Newtonian formalism that does not rely on such an assumption, and furthermore allows one to deduce the density- and pressure-profiles in a general relativistic framework."

The two pseudo-masses

"In this framework, rotation curve measurements provide a pseudo-mass profile $m_{RC}(r)$ and gravitational lensing observations yield a different pseudo-mass profile $m_{lens}(r)$. Combining both pseudo-masses allows one to draw conclusions about the density- and pressure-profiles in the lensing galaxy."

The ambition

The paper's abstract states that combining the observations "not only allow[s] the de[termination of the density profile but also allows conclusions] about the pres[sure]" and hence about "the equation of state" of the "galactic fluid", which bears on "the nature of dark matter".

The limit

The method yields an equation of state only where both datasets exist with sufficient quality, and the pseudo-masses are not the masses. The paper does not claim that a single galaxy suffices to fix the equation of state, nor that the fluid description is valid at all radii.

1. A Newtonian assumption inside an inference

The idea. The standard data analysis assumes Newtonian gravity even when the underlying theory is general relativity. The assumption is in the method, not in the data.

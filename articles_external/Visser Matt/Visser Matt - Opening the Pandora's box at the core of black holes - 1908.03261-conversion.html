What the Paper Does

The authors classify the geometries that can replace the interior of a black hole, using only minimal assumptions. The classification covers any theory with an effective metric, including modified gravity. They find only a handful of qualitatively different classes. Their main result is that the classes that deviate most from general relativity are also the least internally consistent. So either quantum gravity fixes the consistency, or the regularization spreads out to horizon scales.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The reach of the classification

"Due to the minimal geometric assumptions behind it, this classification encompasses virtually all modified gravity theories, and any theory of quantum gravity in which an effective description in terms of an effective metric is available."

The first result

"The first noteworthy conclusion of our analysis is that the number of independent classes of geometries that can be constructed is remarkably limited, with no more than a handful of qualitatively different possibilities."

The surprise

"our most surprising result is that this catalogue of possibilities clearly demonstrates that the degree of internal consistency and the strength of deviations with respect to general relativity are strongly, and positively, correlated."

The fork

"either quantum fluctuations of spacetime come to the rescue and solve these internal consistency issues, or singularity regularization will percolate to macroscopic (near-horizon) scales, radically changing our understanding of black holes and opening new opportunities to test quantum gravity."

What the Paper Does

Dimensional analysis gives a luminosity built from $c$ and $G$. Some authors have claimed nothing can exceed it. The authors trace the claim to Dyson, who they say was more cautious than his readers. They test the bound in general relativity using Vaidya spacetimes and the Misner–Sharp mass. The exterior geometry places no limit. Any bound must come from the interior, and there it depends on the model.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The history

"The suggestion that there is a maximum luminosity (maximum power) in nature has a long and somewhat convoluted history."

The attribution

"Though this idea is commonly attributed to Freeman Dyson, he was actually much more circumspect in his views."

The dimensional fact

"dimensional analysis shows that the speed of light and Newton's constant of gravitation can be combined to define a quantity $P_*=\frac{c^5}{G_N}$ with the dimensions of luminosity"

The requirement

"in any physical situation we must have $P_{\text{physical}}=\wp\,P_*$, where the quantity $\wp$ is some dimensionless function of dimensionless parameters"

The verdict

"within the framework of general relativity the exterior spacetime places no physical constraint on the total luminosity"

The only route

"the only conceivable way in which one might place a bound on the total luminosity is by investigating the interior spacetime geometry of the source"

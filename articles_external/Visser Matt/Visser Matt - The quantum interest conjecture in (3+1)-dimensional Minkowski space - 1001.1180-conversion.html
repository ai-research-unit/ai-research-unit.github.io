What the Paper Does

Quantum inequalities bound how negative an energy density can be and for how long. The quantum interest conjecture adds that borrowing energy must be repaid with interest. Both statements have been reduced to a question about bound states of a one-dimensional Hamiltonian. The authors extend that reduction to three spatial dimensions. The result supports the conjecture without proving it.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The constraints

"The quantum inequalities, and the closely related quantum interest conjecture, impose restrictions on the distribution of the energy density measured by any time-like observer"

What they forbid

"potentially preventing the existence of exotic phenomena such as Alcubierre warp-drives or traversable wormholes"

The reduction

"It has already been proved that both assertions can be reduced to statements concerning the existence or non-existence of bound states of a certain 1-dimensional quantum mechanical Hamiltonian."

The extension

"Using this [reduction we extend the result to (3+1) dimensions]"

What the Paper Does

Three papers had claimed that warp drives could satisfy the weak energy condition, or at least reduce the violation. This paper argues that those claims only consider one set of observers, the comoving ones, while the condition requires all timelike observers. Re-examining the Natário class of warp drives, the authors show every member violates the null energy condition, and with it the weak, strong, and dominant conditions. The argument covers the Alcubierre, Natário zero-expansion, and Lentz/Fell–Heisenberg models. They also note that in modified gravity the purely geometric convergence conditions still fail.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The target

"Three recent articles have claimed that it is possible to, at least in theory, either set up positive energy warp drives satisfying the weak energy condition (WEC), or at the very least, to minimize the WEC violations."

The flaw

"These claims are at best incomplete, since the arguments as presented only assert but do not prove the existence of one set of timelike observers, the co-moving Eulerian observers, who see relatively 'nice' physics. While these particular observers might arguably see a positive energy density, the WEC requires all timelike observers to see positive energy density."

The result

"within the framework adopted by those three papers all physically reasonable warp drives will certainly violate the WEC, and both the strong and dominant energy conditions. Under plausible subsidiary conditions the null energy condition is also violated."

The reach of the argument

"We have now demonstrated that all members of the general class of warp drives defined by Natário [10] violate the NEC. This significantly extends previously known results. This argument applies, in particular, to all three of the Alcubierre [9], Natário zero-expansion [10], and Lentz/Fell–Heisenberg zero-vorticity [1, 3] warp drives"

What follows from the NEC

"Because the NEC is violated then so are the WEC, SEC, and DEC."

The list of options

"one has no choice but to face the violation of the energy conditions head on. Several possibilities arise: (i) modify the theory of gravity, (ii) modify the definition of warp drive, (iii) modify the energy conditions, (iv) appeal to macroscopic quantum physics, (v) allow for singularities or CTCs (time travel). None of these options are particularly palatable. All of these options have serious draw-backs."

The final verdict

"Thus it is our melancholy duty to report that none of the recent claims of positive-mass physical warp drives survive careful inspection of the proffered arguments."

What the Paper Does

Earlier work by two of the authors bounded the entropy of equilibrium uncollapsed matter by half its area, which is weaker than the quarter-area result from black hole thermodynamics. This paper strengthens the bound by writing it in terms of an averaged equation-of-state parameter $w$. Causality caps that average at one, and as it approaches one the bound approaches a quarter of the area. So a horizonless body can get arbitrarily close to the Bekenstein entropy. The paper also clarifies why the chemical potential must be positive, with an appendix covering particle–antiparticle mixtures.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The earlier bound

"In a pair of recent articles two of the current authors have developed an entropy bound for equilibrium uncollapsed matter using only classical general relativity, basic thermodynamics, and the Unruh effect. An odd feature of that bound, $S\le\frac12A$, was that the proportionality constant, $\frac12$, was weaker than that expected from black hole thermodynamics, $\frac14$."

What this paper does

"In the current article we strengthen the previous results by obtaining a bound involving the (suitably averaged) $w$ parameter."

The causality restriction

"Simple causality arguments restrict this averaged $\langle w\rangle$ parameter to be $\le1$. When equality holds, the entropy bound saturates at the value expected based on black hole thermodynamics."

The intermediate result

$$S\le\frac{1+\langle w\rangle}{1+3\langle w\rangle}\frac{A}{2}.\qquad (paper\ 3.5)$$

The stiff-matter case

"This condition is saturated by 'stiff matter' ($w=1$). In this case we found precisely the same bound that arises in black hole thermodynamics"

$$S\le\frac{A}{4}.\qquad (paper\ 2.9)$$

"But as derived this bound requires a very special and very restrictive equation of state."

The monotonicity

"That is, the coefficient arising in this entropy bound is monotone decreasing, and approaches $\frac14$ as $\langle w\rangle$ increases to the stiff matter limit $\langle w\rangle=1$."

The two regimes

"Now in diffuse systems we know $\langle w\rangle\approx0$ and are back to $S=A/2$. However if the system is close to collapse, with most of the mass in the system being close to the upper limit of 'stiff matter' ($w=1$) then $\langle w\rangle\lesssim1$."

The conclusion

"Overall, we find that even in the absence of any black hole region, we can nevertheless get arbitrarily close to the Bekenstein entropy."

The summary

"In summary, we now have an improved bound, and an improved proof, that gets arbitrarily close to the black hole bound."

What the Paper Does

This proceedings contribution summarises Hořava–Lifshitz gravity, a proposal that gives up local Lorentz invariance to make gravity renormalizable. The authors review the different versions and their terminology. They then focus on the projectable version, where the lapse depends only on time. That choice simplifies the action enough for explicit calculations. The verdict is negative: the theory carries a scalar degree of freedom that is unstable, or becomes strongly coupled.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The proposal

"[a] theory of gravity which abandons local Lorentz invariance"

The landscape

"The proposal has been received with growing interest and resulted in various different versions of Hořava–Lifshitz gravity theories, involving a colourful potpourri of new terminology."

The focus

"we focus on a model referred to as projectable Hořava–Lifshitz gravity and summarize the key results regarding its viability."

The advantage

"The main advantage of this version is that it drastically reduces the number of terms that one must consider in the action and it, therefore, makes it possible to perform explicit calculations using the most general action which respects the symmetries of the theory."

The problem

"projectable Hořava–Lifshitz gravity appears to be plagued with serious problems related to the existence of a scalar degree of freedom."

The instability

"This scalar mode turns out to be classically or quantum mechanically unstable in general"

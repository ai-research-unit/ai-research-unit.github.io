What the Paper Does

Gravitational lensing in a weak but arbitrary gravitational field can be described by a $3\times3$ refractive index tensor. The paper derives simple formulas for that tensor. If the sources have small internal fluxes, stresses, and pressures, the tensor is automatically isotropic and reduces to a scalar fixed by the Newtonian potential through a classical result. If anisotropic stresses matter, the gravitational field behaves like an anisotropic crystal. The authors give the general formulas and point to situations where the anisotropy is important.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The object

"Gravitational lensing in a weak but otherwise arbitrary gravitational field can be described in terms of a $3\times3$ tensor, the 'effective refractive index'."

The isotropic case

"If the sources generating the gravitational field all have small internal fluxes, stresses, and pressures, then this tensor is automatically isotropic and the 'effective refractive index' is simply a scalar that can be determined in terms of a classic result involving the Newtonian gravitational potential."

The anisotropic case

"In contrast if anisotropic stresses are ever important then the gravitational field acts similarly to an anisotropic crystal. We derive simple formulae for the refractive index tensor, and indicate some situations in which this will be important."

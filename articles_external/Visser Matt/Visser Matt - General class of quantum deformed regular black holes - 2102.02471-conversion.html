What the Paper Does

The paper examines the Kazakov–Solodukhin quantum-deformed Schwarzschild metric and asks whether it really is regular. The authors separate three notions of regularity: metric components, Christoffel symbols, and curvature invariants. They find that only the first holds; the other two diverge at a two-sphere of finite area. They then embed the metric in a two-parameter family where the notions are separable, and show that some members are more regular. For this family they compute surface gravity, energy conditions, geodesics, and the locations of photon spheres and ISCOs.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"We discuss the 'quantum deformed Schwarzschild spacetime' as originally introduced by Kazakov and Solodukhin in 1993, and investigate the precise sense in which it does and does not satisfy the desiderata for being a 'regular black hole'."

The three levels

"We shall carefully distinguish (i) regularity of the metric components, (ii) regularity of the Christoffel components, and (iii) regularity of the curvature."

The finding

"The metric components are regular, both Christoffel symbols and curvature invariants diverge at the 'centre' of the spacetime, a 2-sphere where $r\to a$ with finite area $A=4\pi a^2$. The 'smearing out' of the 'centre' to $r\to a$ is not sufficient to guarantee curvature regularity."

The verdict on the original

"The original Kazakov–Solodukhin 'quantum deformed Schwarzschild spacetime' is slightly more 'regular' than Schwarzschild spacetime, but it is not 'regular' in the sense normally intended in the general relativity community."

The extension

"We have generalized the original Kazakov–Solodukhin spacetime to a two-parameter class compatible with the ideas mooted in reference [1]. Our generalized two-parameter class of 'quantum corrected' Schwarzschild spacetimes contains exemplars which have much better regularity properties"

What was analyzed

"we have analyzed the geometry, surface gravity, stress-energy, and classical energy conditions. We have also perturbatively analyzed the locations of ISCOs and photon spheres, and set up the appropriate Regge–Wheeler formalism for spin-1 and spin-0 excitations."

The purpose

"our generalized two-parameter class of models distorts Schwarzschild spacetime in a clear and controlled way — so providing yet more examples of black-hole 'mimickers' potentially of interest for observational purposes."

What the Paper Does

The paper takes Verlinde's proposal that gravity is an entropic force and asks the inverse question: if Newtonian gravity is an entropic force, what must the temperature and entropy functions be? It answers in two regimes. With a single heat bath, any conservative force can be written in entropic form, but the resulting model has little to do with Verlinde's. Keeping Verlinde's ingredients — an Unruh-like temperature and an entropy tied to holographic screens — forces a generalised thermodynamic force with several temperatures and entropies, in which the entropies are negative and the temperatures are positive for attraction and negative for repulsion.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The question

"In this article I have not attempted to justify reinterpreting Newtonian gravity as an entropic force, instead I have asked the question: 'If we assume Newtonian gravity is an entropic force, what does this tell us about the relevant thermodynamic system?' What can we say about the relevant temperature and entropy functions? What constraints do they satisfy?"

The first result

"If we want to use a single heat bath, then any conservative force can be recast into entropic force form — but the resulting model is at best orthogonal to Verlinde's proposal."

The second result

"If we wish to retain key parts of Verlinde's proposal (an Unruh-like temperature, and entropy related to 'holographic screens'), then one is unavoidably forced into a more general 'thermodynamic force' scenario with multiple intensive and extensive thermodynamic variables. (Multiple 'temperatures' and 'entropies'.)"

The odd consequences

"The relevant 'entropies' are negative, while the 'temperatures' are positive for attractive forces and negative for repulsive forces. These features are certainly odd, and certainly not what might naively be expected."

The scope

"The constraints I will establish are suffici[ent to rule out] any reasonable variant of Verlinde's entropic [gravity, and for related] reasons the constraints established herein do not [apply to Jacobson's] or Padmanabhan's versions of entropic gravity."

The conclusion

"There is no reasonable doubt concerning the physical reality of entropic forces, and no reasonable doubt t[hat they can be derived from statistical mechanics when the microphysics is known]; the open question is whether gravity is one of them."

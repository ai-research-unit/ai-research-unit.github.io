What the Paper Does

The paper extends a programme of deriving cosmological bounds from the classical energy conditions. It obtains bounds on the Hubble parameter, the density parameter, the density, the lookback time, and several cosmological distances, each of the form $X(z)\gtrless X_0f(\Omega_0,z)$, where the present-day value and a dimensionless function set the limit. The derivation uses only the energy conditions and the FLRW geometry, so the bounds hold for any dark-energy model that satisfies the conditions. The authors sketch how to compare the bounds with supernova data but do not carry out the analysis.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The programme

"We refine and extend a programme initi[ated in Phys. Rev. Lett. 276 (1997) 88 and Phys. Rev. D56 (1997) 7578] advoc[ating the use of the energy] conditions of general relativity in a cosmo[logical setting to place bounds] on various cosmological parameters."

What is bounded

"We show how to bound the Hubble parameter $H(z)$, Omega parameter [$\Omega(z)$], d[ensity $\rho(z)$], and lookback time $T(z)$ as (relativel[y simple functions of] the present-day Hubble parameter $H_0$, and [we] present these results with related observations i[n] the recent supernova data."

The general form

"the bounds which have all taken the form"

$$X(z)\gtrless X_{\mathrm{bound}}\equiv X_0\,f(\Omega_0,z),$$

"where $X(z)$ is some cosmological parameter, $X_0$ is its present-day value, and $f(\Omega_0,z)$ is a dimensionless function depending on the particular bound under consideration."

The scope

"Overall, we have now managed to place bounds of the above form on the Hubble parameter, the Omega parameter, the density, the lookback time, and various cosmological distances — where for simplicity of presentation we have focussed on Peebles' angular diameter distance as being representative."

The honest limit

"We have briefly sketched how to use these bounds to confront the supernova data, but have not yet performed any detailed analysis of this point."

On the derivations

"While most of the bounds we have considered can be derived by 'elementary' means, in at least two cases the integrals (and other algebraic manipulations) were sufficiently tricky that common symbolic manipulation programmes required considerable human intervention in order to obtain useful results."

What the Paper Does

Most regularisation schemes for ultraviolet divergences damage the theory: they break unitarity, or extend the dimension to a non-integer, or introduce fictitious fields. The author proposes breaking Lorentz invariance instead. The regulator is a scalar action with $2z$ spatial derivatives and two time derivatives, which improves the power counting. He quantifies how much breaking is needed, then applies the counting to Hořava's gravity and shows why that model might be finite.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The problem with standard regulators

"Many different regularization techniques have been developed over the years, but most regularizations require severe mutilation of the logical foundations of the theory."

The proposal

"In contrast, breaking Lorentz invariance, while it is certainly a radical step, at least does not damage the logical foundations of the theory."

The honest caveat

"It is an experimental observation that empirical reality obeys Lorentz symmetry to very high accuracy, but it is not a logical necessity."

The action

$$S_{\text{free}}=\int\left\{\dot\phi^2-\phi(-\Delta)^z\phi\right\}dt\,d^dx \qquad (paper\ 1)$$

"Lorentz invariance corresponds to $z=1$. This model explicitly preserves both parity and ordinary spatial rotational invariance."

The result

"With normal ordering, ${:}P(\phi)^{z=d}_{d+1}{:}$ is perturbatively ultraviolet finite."

The second result

"Even without normal ordering, $P(\phi)^{z>d}_{d+1}$ is perturbatively ultraviolet finite."

The motivation

"a key stimulus to writing up these observations was the recent article by Petr Hořava, outlining the development of a quantum field theory for 3+1 dimensional gravity — a theory that is based on a fundamental violation of Lorentz invariance."

The verdict

"While Lorentz breaking regulators are computationally difficult to work with, they have the very powerful advantage that they do not damage the physical foundations and internal logical consistency of the underlying theory."

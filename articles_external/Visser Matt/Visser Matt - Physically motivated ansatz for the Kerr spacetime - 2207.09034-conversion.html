What the Paper Does

There is no simple physical derivation of the Kerr metric; the Newman–Janis trick needs unmotivated choices. The authors try a different route. They use a non-orthonormal tetrad built on oblate spheroidal coordinates, following Maclaurin's oblate spheroids. They write the metric as a tetrad-basis metric contracted with a co-tetrad. All angular dependence goes into the co-tetrad, which is flat-space compatible; all mass dependence goes into the basis metric. They then solve the Einstein equations in that form.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The gap

"Despite some 60 years of work on the subject of the Kerr rotating black hole there is as yet no widely accepted physically based and pedagogically viable ansatz suitable for deriving the Kerr solution without significant computational effort."

The existing trick

"Perhaps the closest one gets in this regard is the Newman–Janis trick; a trick which requires several physically unmotivated choices in order to work."

The approach

"we shall try to make some progress on this issue by using a non-ortho-normal tetrad based on oblate spheroidal coordinates to absorb as much of the messy angular dependence as possible"

The split

"we shall write $g_{ab}=g_{AB}e^A{}_a e^B{}_b$ seeking to keep both [parts simple]"

The result

"doing so permits one to force all of the non-trivial angular dependence into a mass-independent co-tetrad $e^A{}_a$ that is compatible with flat spacetime in oblate spheroidal coordinates, while forcing all of the mass-dependence (and none of the angular dependence) into the tetrad-basis metric $g_{AB}$."

The motivation

"partially based on Newtonian physics, (the fact that Maclaurin's oblate spheroids already became of interest for rotating bodies some 280 years ago), and partially based on the fact that tetrad methods are known to be useful in general relativity."

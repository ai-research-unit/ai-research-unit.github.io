What the Paper Does

Cosmography expands observables in powers of redshift. Supernova data now reach $z\approx 1.75$, where a series in $z$ is expected to fail. The authors study the convergence of the Hubble series and compare redshift variables. They recommend $y=z/(1+z)$ and a related variable, because these keep the expansion inside its radius of convergence. The conclusion is practical: use a variable that does not break at high redshift.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The practice

"In cosmography, cosmokinetics, and cosmology it is quite common to encounter physical quantities expanded as a Taylor series in the cosmological redshift $z$."

The data

"we now have considerable high-$z$ data available, for instance we have supernova data at least back to redshift $z\approx 1.75$"

The question

"This opens up the theoretical question as to whether or not the Hubble series (or more generally any series expansion b[eyond $z=1$ is meaningful]"

The first conclusion

"The use of the $z$-redshift for $z>1$ is likely to lead to mathematical problems — specifically any Taylor series in $z$ will be guaranteed to diverge for $z>1$, and so finite truncations will be poor approximations to the underlying physical function."

The second

"The use of the $y$-redshift, where $y=z/(1+z)$, [is preferable]"

The warning

"many galaxies and supernovae are seen in the region $z\gtrsim 1$, so one ignores this issue at one's peril"

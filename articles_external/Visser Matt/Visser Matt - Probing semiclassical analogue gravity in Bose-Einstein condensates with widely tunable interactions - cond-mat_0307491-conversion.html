What the Paper Does

A Bose–Einstein condensate with tunable interactions can be made to change its sound speed quickly. The authors argue that this makes it a laboratory for semiclassical gravity. Phonons in the condensate behave like quantum fields on an effective expanding geometry. Changing the interaction strength then produces particle creation analogous to cosmological production. The authors compute the phonon number in sudden and adiabatic limits. They argue these effects are far larger than Hawking radiation from a horizon, so they are more likely to be seen.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The proposal

"In this article we shall discuss the prospects for the use of this particular experimental effect to test some of the predictions of semiclassical quantum gravity, for instance, particle production in an expanding universe."

The advantage

"We stress that these effects are generally much larger than the Hawking radiation expected from causal horizons, and so there are much better chances for their detection in the near future."

The two limits

"Contrary to what happens in the sudden approximation, the adiabatic approximation is taking into account the rapidity with which the configuration changes."

The suppression

"the total number of phonons calculated by trusting the adiabatic approximation throughout the whole range of frequencies is suppressed with respect to the sudden approximation calculation by a factor of ... [(healing time)/(evolution time)]$^3$"

What the Paper Does

The paper asks how much symmetry a horizon forces on the geometry near it, without assuming spherical symmetry. Using purely geometrical arguments it shows that, as a static Killing horizon is approached, the curvature tensors become highly symmetric. The Einstein tensor block-diagonalises into transverse and parallel parts, the transverse part is proportional to the transverse metric, and these properties survive static conformal deformations. So the stress-energy near any static Killing horizon is tightly constrained. The authors suggest this geometric symmetry underlies the conformal symmetry used in entropy calculations.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The scope

"We consider the spacetime geometry of a static but otherwise generic black hole (that is, the horizon geometry and topology are not necessarily spherically symmetric)."

The result

"It is demonstrated, by purely geometrical techniques, that the curvature tensors, and the Einstein tensor in particular, exhibit a very high degree of symmetry as the horizon is approached. Consequently, the stress-energy tensor will be highly constrained near any static Killing horizon."

The details

"it is shown that — at the horizon — the stress-energy tensor block-diagonalizes into 'transverse' and 'parallel' blocks, the transverse components of this tensor are proportional to the transverse metric, and these properties remain invariant under static conformal deformations."

The claim of novelty

"Although this symmetry had already been established for static spherically symmetric horizons, this is — to the best of our knowledge — the first time that it has been rigorously demonstrated to hold for generic static geometries."

The implication for matter

"A direct implication of our analysis is that, by way of Einstein's equation, the matter/energy near a static Killing horizon (including any quantum fluctuations) must be highly constrained."

The conformal field theory reading

"Given that the 'interesting' near-horizon physics can be anticipated to take place in the $n$–$t$ plane and $T_\perp\propto g_\perp$ at the horizon, the near-horizon stress tensor is effectively that of a collection of world-sheet conformal field theories."

The speculation

"we speculate that this geometric symmetry underlies Carlip's notion of an asymptotic near-horizon conformal symmetry controlling the entropy of a black hole."

The key identity

"on-horizon Einstein tensor block-diagonalizes and, moreover, that ${}^{(3+1)}G_{\hat t\hat t}|_H+{}^{(3+1)}G_{\hat n\hat n}|_H=0$; where $\hat t$ and $\hat n$ are the (normalized) spacetime coordinates in the directions perpendicular to the horizon (timelike and spacelike respectively)."

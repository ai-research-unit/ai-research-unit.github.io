What the Paper Does

This is a multi-author overview of regular black holes and black hole mimickers. The motivation is twofold: general relativity must be extended inside horizons, and observations now probe that region. The authors set out the open problems in classifying physically motivated regular models. They review the mathematical tools available, including outer horizons. They argue the programme is sound and the machinery is nearly ready.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The motivation

"to standard black holes has recently gained significant attention, driven both by the need to extend general relativity to describe black hole interiors, and by recent advances in observational technologies"

The problem

"significant challenges remain in identifying and characterizing physically well-motivated classes of regular black holes and black hole mimickers"

The purpose

"This paper provides an overview of these challenges"

The thesis

"The theoretical motivation to construct a non-singular paradigm of black hole physics, in which black holes are not described as the singular end state of gravitational collapse, but rather as regular and metastable bound states of the gravitational field, is sound."

The readiness

"the required mathematical machinery to develop such a new paradigm is reaching a mature stage, with concepts such as outer horizons providing suitable [tools]"

What the Paper Does

The $i\epsilon$ prescription in propagators can be read as a small complex deformation of the metric. Starting from that reading, the paper extends the idea from flat space to a fixed curved background and then to fluctuating geometries. It makes explicit the Louko–Sorkin, Kontsevich–Segal, and Witten acceptability conditions for complex metrics, and links them to variants of the weak energy condition. It gives a tetrad construction of the full set of acceptable complex metrics, and an appendix shows a metric that solves the field equations with finite action yet fails the conditions.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The reinterpretation

"Feynman's $i\epsilon$ prescription for quantum field theoretic propagators has a quite natural reinterpretation in terms of a slight complex deformation of the Minkowski space-time metric."

The extension

"Though originally a strictly flat-space result, once reinterpreted in this way, these ideas can be naturally extended first to semi-classical curved-spacetime QFT on a fixed background geometry and then, (with more work), to fluctuating spacetime geometries."

The energy condition link

"There are intimate connections with variants of the weak energy condition."

The effect on the path integral

"allowing the complex deformation to become large leads to a variant of Wick rotation, and more importantly leads to physically motivated constraints on the configuration space of acceptable off-shell geometries to include in Feynman's functional integral when attempting to quantize gravity."

Where Lorentzian metrics sit

"this observation allows one to connect the discussion back to recent ideas on 'acceptable' complex metrics, in the Louko–Sorkin and Kontsevich–Segal–Witten sense, with Lorentzian signature spacetimes occurring exactly on the boundary of the set of 'acceptable' complex metrics."

The construction

"By adopting the tetrad formalism we explicitly construct the most general set of acceptable complex metrics satisfying the 0-form, 1-form, and 2-form acceptability conditions."

The summary of the conclusions

"We have back-tracked the derivation of these acceptability conditions to the fundamental physics of Feynman's $i\epsilon$ prescription, which we used to justify the notion of an 'almost real' spacetime metric."

The counterexample

"It is worthwhile to give a specific and fully explicit example of a complex metric that is not 'acceptable', and does not satisfy the Louko–Sorkin conditions."

What the Paper Does

The paper finds the asymptotic quasinormal modes of generic static spherically symmetric black holes surrounded by arbitrary matter. The method locates the complex poles of the scattering amplitude in the first Born approximation, so it is analytic rather than numerical. The leading result is that the asymptotic spacing between modes equals the surface gravity, whatever the surrounding matter is. The authors check the method against Schwarzschild, extend it to two-horizon spacetimes, and report partial progress for degenerate horizons, while noting that the method has so far only reached the leading term.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The object

"We investigate the asymptotic nature of the quasinormal modes for 'dirty' black holes — generic static and spherically symmetric spacetimes for which a central black hole is surrounded by arbitrary 'matter' fields."

The result

"We demonstrate that, to the leading asymptotic order, the [imaginary] spacing between modes is precisely equal to the surface gravity, independent of the specifics of the black hole system."

The method

"Our analytical method is based on locating the complex poles in the first Born approximation for the scattering amplitude."

The checks

"We first verify that our formalism agrees, asymptotically, with previous studies on the Schwarzschild black hole. The analysis is then generalized to more exotic black hole geometries. We also extend considerations to spacetimes with two horizons and briefly discuss the degenerate-horizon scenario."

The limitation

"the simplicity of our method has a price: we have (so far) only been able to locate the leading term in the asymptotic expansion of the quasinormal mode. Meanwhile, the next-order term — that is, the 'offset' — has spark[ed]" further work.

The virtue claimed

"The virtues of our formal treatment include generality and a straightforward analytical approach that does not obscure the physical process being investigated."

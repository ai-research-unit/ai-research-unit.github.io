What the Paper Does

The paper re-examines a constraint that the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration drew from the image of Sagittarius A*. The collaboration modelled the object as an ultra-compact horizonless object with a surface that absorbs radiation perfectly, and concluded that such objects are tightly constrained. The authors show that perfect absorption is an unjustified assumption: any small absorption coefficient changes the energy balance, and the published upper bound then holds only for absorption coefficients below about $10^{-14}$. The corrected constraint is much weaker and depends on two parameters, which they plot.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"The images of Sagittarius A* recently r[eported by the EHT collaboration] have been accompanied [by a discussion of the] possible absence of a trapping horizon, i.e., [whether the object at] our galaxy is an ultra-compact object wit[h a] surface, in which incident radiation is r[eflected or absorbed]."

The assumption under scrutiny

"Herein, we discuss h[ow the] assumption of perfect balance in the ener[gy budget of such an] object [fails], and show that this is violated wh[en one includes an absorption] coefficient."

The corrected bound

"We derive the upper-bound co[nstraint as a function of the] absorption coefficient of the surface. We [find that the bounds stated by the] EHT collaboration hold only for unnatural [values, namely absorption coefficients] lower than $10^{-14}$, and thus have to be s[tated with that qualification]."

The main message

"The main message of this paper is that the upper-bound constraint for thermal emission reported by the EHT collaboration ... only partially accounts for the strong lensing effect and, crucially, it does not consider the effect of an absorption coefficient. We have shown that this is far from being a justified working assumption, as even a very small absorption coefficient renders the assumptions in Sec. 4.1 of [the EHT paper] invalid."

The simplification adopted

"we have focused on the spherically symmetric case for simplicity. It is known that, in the rotating case, the escape angle ... would depend on the latitude. However, as shown in [39], for an object like Sgr A* for which the angular momentum is below $a\simeq0.9$, the emission is very nearly isotropic and we can simply substitute the latitude dependent escape angle with its average value."

1. An observational bound rests on an idealisation

The idea. The published constraint assumed perfect absorption, which is a physical idealisation, and relaxing it weakens the bound by many orders of magnitude.

2. Horizonless does not mean featureless

The idea. An object without a horizon has a surface, and the surface's reflectivity is a free parameter that observation must fix.

What the Paper Does

Causal dynamical triangulations restrict a path integral using the causal structure of Lorentzian manifolds. The authors apply the same idea to quantum Regge calculus, keeping Lorentzian signature and relaxing the triangle inequalities that constrain Euclidean simplices. They show the resulting model is not a Wick rotation of the Euclidean one, which is evidence that Lorentzian and Euclidean sums differ. In two dimensions they derive scaling relations on the torus with two measures, discuss loop amplitudes, and build a transfer matrix, then extend the discussion to higher dimensions. The conclusion states that the model has no critical behaviour and so no continuum limit, and that the loop integrals have no known closed form.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The borrowed insight

"A key insight used in developing the theory of Causal Dynamical Triangulations (CDTs) is to use the causal (or light-cone) structure of Lorentzian manifolds to restrict the class of geometries appearing in the Quantum Gravity (QG) path integral."

The claim about Lorentzian results

"the models developed in CDTs differ from the analogous models developed in the Euclidean domain ... and the corresponding Lorentzian results are in many ways more 'physical'."

What is new here

"We exploit another crucial fact about the structure of Lorentzian manifolds, namely that certain simplices are not constrained by the triangle inequalities present in Euclidean signature."

The main structural result

"We show that this model is not related to QRC by a naive Wick rotation; this serves as another demonstration that the sum over Lorentzian geometries is not simply related to the sum over Euclidean geometries."

The practical gain

"By removing the triangle inequality constraints, there is more freedom to perform analytical calculations, and in addition numerical simulations are more computationally efficient."

The stated obstacles

"Unfortunately obstacles remain for both full calculation of the path integral and also for calculation of loop amplitudes. In calculating loop amplitudes, the integrals that arise have no known closed form solution and block us from deriving the full result."

No continuum limit

"in contrast to CDT, the pure gravity path integral does not exhibit any critical behavior. This means that there is no way to take the usual sort of continuum limit of the model which could be compared to other approaches."

The kinematic exclusion

"the lack of phase transition could be interpreted as a plus: A potential polymerized phase is excluded kinematically, while in 1+1 dimensions the occurrence of a rough/ fractal/ crumpled phase seems unlikely given the exact result $\langle R\rangle=0$ and the well-behaved nature of the causal diamond truncation."

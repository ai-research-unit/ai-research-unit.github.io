What the Paper Does

Under stationary axisymmetry plus circularity, the authors decompose the metric into Boyer–Lindquist form and then into a canonical ADM form. The roots of one function mark the would-be horizons, and a single condition on the frame dragging promotes them to Killing horizons. Surface gravity then has a simple closed expression, derived by three independent methods and confirmed by a fourth once the horizon is Killing. The paper also gives the condition that makes the surface gravity constant, which is the zeroth law, and notes that this can hold without a Killing horizon. The results are aimed at astrophysical modelling and at analogue spacetimes, where naked singularities are common.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"Using minimalist assumptions we develop a natural functional decomposition for the spacetime metric, and explicit tractable formulae for the surface gravities, in arbitrary stationary circular (PT symmetric) axisymmetric spacetimes."

What is related to what

"We relate rigidity results, (the existence of a Killing horizon), and the zeroth law to the absence of curvature singularities at the would-be horizons."

Who it is for

"These observations are of interest to both observational astrophysicists (modelling the cold, dark, heavy objects at the centre of most spiral galaxies), and to the analogue spacetime community, (wherein the presence of naked singularities is not necessarily deprecated, and the occurrence of non-Killing horizons is relatively common)."

The canonical form

"taking into consideration the ADM decomposition, without any further loss of generality we may write this metric in the canonical form" a matrix with entries $\frac{h_{rr}(r,\chi)}{\Delta(r)}$, $g_{\chi\chi}$, $g_{\varphi\varphi}$, $-g_{\varphi\varphi}\omega$, and $-h_{tt}\Delta+g_{\varphi\varphi}\omega^2$ in the $(r,\chi,\varphi,t)$ ordering.

The horizon condition

"We have found that the roots of $\Delta(r)=0$ are the locations of the would-be horizons in this geometry, since $\Delta(r)=0$ defines a null hypersurface, while in addition one has $\Gamma^r{}_{ij}\propto\Delta(r)$ which vanishes on the would-be horizon."

Promotion to a Killing horizon

"mathematically these would-be horizons can be promoted to Killing horizons so long as we set"

$$\omega(r,\chi)=\omega_0(r)+\Delta(r)\,\tilde\omega(r,\chi).\qquad (paper\ 12.3)$$

"This implies the rigidity result, $\omega(r_H,\chi)=\Omega_H$, and physically is tantamount to demanding that the Ricci scalar be finite on the would-be horizon."

The surface gravity

"Using three distinct methods, we then calculated a simple, tractable, and robust expression for the surface gravity on these horizons which is given as"

$$\kappa=\frac{\Delta'(r_H)}{2}\sqrt{\frac{h_{tt}(r_H,\chi)}{h_{rr}(r_H,\chi)}}.\qquad (paper\ 12.4)$$

The zeroth law

"If we wish to enforce the zeroth law then mathematically it is sufficient to set"

$$\frac{h_{tt}(r,\chi)}{h_{rr}(r,\chi)}=J(r)+\Delta(r)K(r,\chi),\qquad (paper\ 12.5)$$

"In this case, the surface gravity becomes constant on the horizon. It is important to note that this condition can be imposed without necessarily forcing the horizons to be Killing horizons."

The overall claim

"with minimal assumptions, stationary axisymmetry plus 'circularity' (PT symmetry) one can give a surprisingly simple expression for the surface gravity on the would-be horizons. Additionally we can quite easily enforce a zeroth law and/or the existence of Killing horizons."

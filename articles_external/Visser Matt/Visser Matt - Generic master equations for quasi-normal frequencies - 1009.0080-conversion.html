What the Paper Does

Two techniques give master equations for highly damped quasi-normal frequencies: semi-analytic methods and monodromy. The authors show that, despite their different technical detail, both produce equations of the same general form, for spacetimes with one, two, or three horizons. Within that class they relate the existence of equi-spaced frequency families to the rationality of ratios of surface gravities. If the ratio is rational, the master equation reduces to a sparse polynomial and the frequencies fall into families. The converse holds generically, with exceptions the authors flag.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The two routes

"Generic master equations governing the highly-damped quasi-normal frequencies [QNFs] of one-horizon, two-horizon, and even three-horizon spacetimes can be obtained through either semi-analytic or monodromy techniques."

The agreement

"While many technical details differ, both between the semi-analytic and monodromy approaches, and quite often among various authors seeking to apply the monodromy technique, there is nevertheless widespread agreement regarding the general form of the QNF master equations."

The result

"Within this class of generic master equations we can establish some rather general results, relating the existence of 'families' of QNFs of the form $\omega_{a,n} = (\text{offset})_a + in\,(\text{gap})$ to the question of whether or not certain ratios of parameters are rational or irrational."

The forward direction

"For this entire class of master equations it is a rigorous result that whenever the ratio of surface gravities is rational $R_{ij}=\kappa_i/\kappa_j\in\mathbb{Q}$, then the master equation can be reduced to a sparse polynomial and the QNFs fall into equi-spaced families ... This also holds true under the slightly weaker hypothesis that $R_{AB}\in\mathbb{Q}$."

The converse

"The converse of this result is more subtle: If there is at least one equi-spaced family of QNFs ..., then generically the ratio of surface gravities will be rational $R_{ij}=\kappa_i/\kappa_j\in\mathbb{Q}$, in which case all other QNFs will fall into families of this form."

The caveat

"There are however some non-generic exceptional situations, which are rarely encountered but should be kept in mind."

The summary of methods

"We have seen that semi-analytic and monodromy techniques, although they are very different in both technical detail and underlying philosophy, by and large agree on the general form of the QNF master equation they generate"

What the Paper Does

In a braneworld the four-dimensional Einstein equations do not close; the extrinsic curvature carries information from the bulk. The authors solve the on-brane Gauss and Codazzi equations for a static spherically symmetric star in a Randall–Sundrum type II model. The solution shows that the on-brane data consist of two free functions and two constants. They explain how to feed that data into a bulk algorithm. The conclusion is that braneworld stars are much less constrained than general relativistic ones.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The problem

"Stellar structure in braneworlds is markedly different from that in ordinary general relativity."

What they do

"we completely solve the 'on brane' 4-dimensional Gauss and Codazzi equations for an arbitrary static spherically symmetric star in a Randall–Sundrum type II braneworld."

The next step

"We then indicate how this on-brane boundary data should be propagated into the bulk in order to determine the full 5-dimensional spacetime geometry."

The size of the data set

"This on-brane data is characterized by two arbitrary functions $g(r)$, and $\rho(r)$, and two arbitrary constants $B(r_0)$, and $p(r_0)$. This is an enormous dataset, much more general than the various special cases discussed"

The verdict

"We conclude that braneworld stars are potentially much more complicated than standard general relativistic stars, and emphasise that the coupling to the bulk will play an important role in restricting the possible functions $g(r)$"

What the Paper Does

Burning coal is a unitary process, so no information is lost. The authors compute how much entropy and how much correlation the emitted photons carry. Using the Planck spectrum and counting photon statistics, they find about $3.9\pm2.5$ bits per emitted photon, split evenly between thermodynamic entropy and hidden information. The point is that even an ordinary process has a quantifiable entropy budget. They present the calculation as a warm-up for the Hawking evaporation problem.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The result

"the photons emitted by burning a lump of coal ... lead to an approximately Planck emission spectrum with an average entropy/information transfer of approximately $3.9\pm2.5$ bits per emitted photon."

What it depends on

"This quantitative and qualitative result depends only on the underlying unitarity of the quantum physics of burning, combined with the statistical mechanics of blackbody radiation."

The point

"the utterly standard and unitarity preserving process of burning something (in fact, burning anything) nevertheless has an associated entropy/information budget ... is a severely under-appreciated feature of standard quantum statistical physics."

The number

$$\langle \hat S^2\rangle=\frac{\pi^4}{30\,\zeta(3)\ln 2}\approx 3.897 \ \text{bits per photon} \qquad (paper\ 4.1)$$

The elementary status

"That the blackbody photons arising from burning a lump of coal, (a purely unitary quantum process), carry both thermodynamic entropy and 'hidden information' (ie, correlations) is an elementary and unavoidable consequence of quite standard quantum statistical mechanics."

The aim

"our longer term goal is to apply these considerations to the Hawking evaporation process in black holes"

What the Paper Does

The speed of light and Newton's constant combine into a quantity with the dimensions of force, and several authors have proposed that no force in nature can exceed it, in a strong form and in a weak form. The paper computes forces inside perfect fluid spheres in general relativity and finds counterexamples to both forms. The counterexamples include fluid spheres on the verge of gravitational collapse. The authors conclude that a maximum force principle can survive only if the class of allowed forces is restricted explicitly.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The conjecture

"Dimensional analysis shows that the speed of light and [Newton's constant of gravi]tation can be combined to define a quantity $F_*=Gc_N$ [in appropriate units, a force] (equivalently, tension). Then in any physical si[tuation] where the quantity $f$ is some dimensionless funct[ion of the parameters,] many physical situations explicit calculation yiel[ds] $f$ [of order unity]. This has lead multiple authors to suggest a (weak or st[rong]) maximum force / maximum tension conjecture."

What the paper does instead

"Working within the framework [of general relativity we] will instead focus on counter-examples to this c[onjecture], [and on] the extent to which the counter-examples are [robust]. [The] counter-examples we shall explore strongly sugge[st that one should not place] credence into any universal maximum force/maximum [tension principle]."

Where the counterexamples are found

"[Within] fluid spheres on the verge of gravitational coll[apse]" the computed forces violate "both the weak (and strong) maximum force conjectures."

The conclusion

"Through the analysis of radial and equatorial forces within perfect fluid spheres in general relativity, we have produced a number of counter-examples to both the strong and weak forms of the maximum force conjecture. These counter-examples highlight significant issues with the current phrasing and understanding of this conjecture, as merely specifying that forces are bounded within the framework of general relativity is manifestly a falsehood."

What would be needed

"should one wish some version of the maximum force conjecture to be considered viable as a potential physical principle, it must be very clearly specified as to what types of forces they pertain to." And: "If one w[ishes to retain a] 'maximum force' [principle] then one will have to very caref[ully specify which forces are] to be allowed within the domain of discourse."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A dimensional argument is not a law

The idea. Combining constants into a quantity with the right dimensions shows only that the quantity exists, not that anything is bounded by it.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-empirical-status-of-the-biquaternion-framework.md considers whether a framework's dimensionless structures count as predictions. This paper is the clearest available case of a proposed principle resting on dimensional analysis and failing under calculation. The article should use it to state the standard its own constructions must meet.

What stands in the way. The failure is demonstrated in fluid spheres and does not address the framework. Ranked first, because it is a sharp standard and an instructive failure.

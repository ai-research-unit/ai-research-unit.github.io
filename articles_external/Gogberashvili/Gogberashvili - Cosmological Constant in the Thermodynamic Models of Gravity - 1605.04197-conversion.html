What the Paper Does

A three-page note. In thermodynamic models of gravity the Einstein equations are written as one scalar balance between gravitational and matter heat densities along a null vector, and in that form $\Lambda$ enters the tensor equations as a constant of integration rather than as a vacuum energy. The note bounds that constant with the Károlyházy uncertainty relation at the horizon distance and obtains the observed dark-energy density. The bound is an estimate from three scalings, not a derivation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The field equation is a scalar identity holding for every null $l^\nu$, $$\left(R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}R\right)l^\mu l^\nu=L_p^2\,T_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu,\qquad g_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu=0$$ (paper 1–2), with $L_p$ the reduced Planck length and $l^\nu$ "the independent variable (instead of the metrics tensor gµν ) in the variational prin- ciple of the model.". The right side is the matter heat density, $\rho+P=TS$ (paper 3); the left is $R_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu=(\nabla_\nu l^\nu)^2-\nabla_\mu l_\nu\nabla^\nu l^\mu+(\text{total derivatives})$ (paper 4). Because $l$ is null, $\Lambda$ can appear as an integration constant in the tensor form, $$R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12g_{\mu\nu}R=L_p^2\left(T_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}\Lambda\right)$$ (paper 5), and "is not connected with the large constant vacuum energy terms in matter Lagrangians".

The bound uses $\delta x\approx L_p^{2/3}x^{1/3}$ (paper 6) and $E\sim1/t$ for the minimal cell (paper 7). At the horizon $x\sim1/H$, $$\Lambda\sim\frac{H^2}{L_p^2}$$ (paper 8), which "coincides with the observed estimations for the dark energy". $\Lambda$ is to be identified with "the amount of information accessible to an eternal observer at the horizon"; the relation is $\sim$ and no coefficient is computed.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. $\Lambda$ as a constant of integration

The idea. In the null-vector form the left side has a trace ambiguity the variational principle does not fix, so the integration brings a constant, and that constant is $\Lambda$. A mechanism for a cosmological term without a vacuum-energy term.

What it would add. A route to a source the corpus declares missing: its cosmology article states that the framework's invariants are rank-two, that the packaging of a rank-two tensor collapses to its trace, and that "no scale factor, no Hubble rate, and no equation of state is derived here".

Where it lands. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md.

What stands in the way. The mechanism needs a variational principle whose independent variable is a null vector field, and the corpus's frame route varies the frame; no null-vector action exists there. It also leaves $\Lambda$ undetermined until an extra principle is supplied, which the note fills with Károlyházy rather than with the algebra.

2. One scalar equation for all null directions

The idea. A single scalar identity required to hold for every null $l$ replaces the tensor equation, and the note argues the scalar form "contains the same information content as the usual tensor form". A formula that loses nothing.

What it would add. A form in which the equation is a heat balance, so the first law applies directly — an alternative to the corpus's linearised dictionary, which carries the ten components.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-einstein-field-equations-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-linearised-dictionary-and-the-non-linear-gap.md.

What stands in the way. The equivalence rests on the identity holding for all null vectors, an assumption about the solution space rather than a consequence of the display; and the corpus's gap is the non-linear equation itself, which this rewriting presupposes.

The rank is a judgement: the integration-constant mechanism first, because it addresses a gap the corpus names; the null-vector rewriting second, because it restates an equation the corpus already carries.

Three pages: the balance equation, the integration constant, the Károlyházy bound, and the estimate of $\Lambda$.

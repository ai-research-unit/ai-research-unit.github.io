What the Paper Does

Asks what a minimal length does to the CHSH bound. Operators and states are corrected to first order in the generalised-uncertainty parameter $\beta$, and the three ways of applying the correction — to the measurement, to the state, to both — are worked out separately. Correcting one side changes the bound by a term proportional to $\beta$; correcting both leaves it at $2$. The paper then runs the corrected expression on IBM and Google hardware and reports that the violation rises with $\beta$ while staying under $4$. The result is a first-order expansion plus a simulation, not a measurement of $\beta$.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A minimal length written as a correction to the operator, not to the state

The idea. $\hat O=\hat o+\beta\hat o_p$ with the normalisation $\hat j=(\hat J+\beta\hat J_p)/|\lambda_{J_{\rm GUP}}|$ keeps the eigenvalues at $\pm1$ while moving the operator. That is a formula, and it is the bookkeeping the corpus's CHSH exercise does not have, because the corpus assumes ideal projectors.

What it would add. A way to write a non-ideal measurement inside a Bell correlator while keeping the $\pm1$ spectrum that makes the bound meaningful.

Where it lands. articles_physics/exercise-the-chsh-inequality-and-tsirelsons-bound.md.

What stands in the way. The corpus's derivation of $2\sqrt2$ uses unit vectors and the exact singlet; a $\beta$-corrected operator breaks the algebraic identity that produces Tsirelson's bound, so the exercise cannot absorb the correction without being rewritten as an expansion.

2. The one-sided correction changes the bound, the two-sided one does not

The idea. $\hat B^{\rm GUP}\leq2(1+\beta\langle\xi|\hat B|\xi\rangle_p)$ when only one side is corrected, and $\leq2$ when both are. That is a reading of the same structure as the Lorentz-transformation cases the paper cites.

What it would add. A statement of where a modified bound can appear at all: a symmetric deformation is invisible to the inequality.

Where it lands. articles_physics/frame-dependent-entanglement-and-relativistic-quantum-information-in-biquaternionic-form.md, which lists the same three cases for Lorentz boosts.

What stands in the way. The result depends on how the two sides are corrected, and no criterion is offered for which choice nature makes; the paper chooses by analogy with special relativity, and the analogy is asserted.

3. A violation above $2\sqrt2$

The idea. The simulation reports the violation growing past $\sqrt8$ with $\beta$, while staying under $4$. That is a number.

What it would add. A falsifiable target: a measured CHSH value above $2\sqrt2$ with a known $\beta$.

Where it lands. articles_physics/exercise-the-chsh-inequality-and-tsirelsons-bound.md, as the bound to compare against.

What stands in the way. $2\sqrt2$ is a theorem for a Hilbert-space model with $\pm1$ observables, so a simulation that exceeds it means the simulated correlators are not of that form — which is what a modified operator set implies, and also what a numerical error implies. The hardware curves are quantum-computer runs of the authors' own circuit, not tests of the bound; nothing here distinguishes the two readings.

The rank is a judgement: the operator bookkeeping first, because the exercise needs it; the one-sided rule second, because it says when a modified bound can appear; the excess over $\sqrt8$ last, because as reported it is a simulation.

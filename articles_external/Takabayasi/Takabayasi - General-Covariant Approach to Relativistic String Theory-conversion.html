What the Paper Does

Writes the geometric string in tensor language covariant under general transformations of the two parameters, and argues the machinery is worth its weight: it separates observable from non-observable quantities automatically, clarifies conformal invariance, static solutions and the single-time formalism, and supports an extension in which each point of the string is an orthogonal triad able to rotate and dilate, giving a string with charge distributed along it. The analogy with general relativity is drawn — and then sharply distinguished. Here general covariance comes from the definition of the model, not from gravitation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim

"Theory of 'geometric' string, recently employed as a model of hadrons, is represented in a form covariant under general transformation of parameters ('coordinates') by the aid of tensor analysis. It is shown that this approach is essentially simple and transparent and works to clarify subtle points in the relativistic kinematics and mechanics of the geometric string. The method is applied to the treatment of conformal invariance, 'static solutions' and single-time formalism. Relativistic string with intrinsic rotational degrees is presented, also on the basis of the general-covariant approach. This new model allows a charge distribution along the string. Analogy of the 'general-covariant' string theory to general relativity as well as its distinction therefrom is explained."

Why bother

"However, it has been felt that a full development of the machinery of 'general-covariant' formalism for string theory would be luxury and extravagance. Nevertheless it is to be recalled that the 'general covariance' in the present case is physically meaningful because it is closely related to the requirement of causality and of the uniqueness of physical interpretation of theory at classical level and to the suppression of possible ghost states at quantum-mechanical level of theory. In this paper we carry through a manifestly 'general-covariant' formulation of the 'geometric' string theory to show its real usefulness."

"In the first place the general-covariant approach is simple in essence and deepens our insight into the structure of string theory. In particular it automatically distinguishes between observable and non-observable quantities and clarifies several subtle aspects inherent in the kinematics of the 'geometric' string model, which is indeed a rather fantastic mechanical object."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Covariance as the machinery that separates observable from unobservable

The idea. Writing the string in general-covariant form does more than make the equations look right: it identifies which quantities are invariant and hence observable, and which are parameter artefacts. The selection is automatic, not a matter of judgement.

What it would add. A criterion of observability that does not rely on physical intuition about which quantities "look real". The Einstein Field Equations in the Biquaternion Framework: the Linearised Dictionary and the Non-Linear Gap (articles_physics/the-einstein-field-equations-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-linearised-dictionary-and-the-non-linear-gap.md) sorts quantities into a dictionary between formalisms; here the sorting is between observable and gauge, which is a different split and one the corpus's discussions of gauge fixings could use.

Where it lands. §The Dictionary, as the observable/non-observable split.

What stands in the way. The claim is made at the level of the tensor calculus; no explicit list of observables is given, so the reader must construct it.

2. The same mathematics, a different physical origin

The idea. String theory and general relativity share the tensor calculus, and the paper insists the resemblance is superficial: in relativity the covariance arises from the equivalence of frames in a gravitational theory, in the string it arises from the arbitrariness of the labels $\xi^{a}$. The author's remark that "n. It is n~t at all a rarity that physically different theories invoke similar mathematical set-up. § 2. Kinematics" is a methodological point in its own right.

What it would add. A caution against inferring physics from formalism, which the corpus's biquaternion work needs constantly. The Einstein Field Equations in the Biquaternion Framework is a case where a shared algebra invites a shared reading; this paper gives the counter-principle from the string side.

Where it lands. §The Non-Linear Gap of that article, as the caution.

What stands in the way. The principle is stated; the corpus would need the specific argument for which similarities are meaningful, which this paper does not supply.

3. A "zweibein" and the reason $H^{\mu\nu}\neq\eta^{\mu\nu}$

The idea. Because the index $a$ runs over two values only, the induced tensor $H^{\mu\nu}$ is not the Minkowski metric. The deficit is physical: it is the projection onto the two directions the string actually spans, and it carries the string's dynamics.

What it would add. A concrete place where a "vielbein" with too few legs produces physics rather than an inconsistency. The Spinor Module in Biquaternionic Form and Its Lorentz Action (articles_physics/the-spinor-module-in-biquaternionic-form-and-its-lorentz-action.md) uses a full four-leg frame; the zweibein case shows the degenerate version and what the degeneracy buys.

Where it lands. §The Module, as the degenerate frame.

What stands in the way. The construction is specific to the worldsheet; the general statement about restricted frames is not made.

4. A string whose points are triads, with charge along it

The idea. Replace each point of the string by a rotating, dilating triad. Each point then carries internal orientation and size, and a charge distribution along the string becomes possible — the string stops being a featureless line and becomes a medium.

What it would add. A model bridging the point-rotator and string pictures, which the folder otherwise keeps apart. The folder's rotator papers and string papers share no objects; here a triad at each $\xi^{1}$ is exactly that bridge, and the charge distribution is the physical payoff.

Where it lands. The conversion of Theory of Relativistic String and Super-Wave Equation I in this folder, as the internal-degree extension; and for the rotator side, The Spectrum of a Lorentz Rotor (articles_physics/the-spectrum-of-a-lorentz-rotor-what-two-eigenvalues-leave-undetermined.md).

What stands in the way. The extension is presented in outline; its spectrum and quantisation are left for later work.

The remainder, one line each. That conformal invariance is best seen from the general-covariant side is the paper's method claim. That the single-time formalism is clarified in the same language connects this paper to the many-time discussion in the elastic-continuum paper of the same folder.

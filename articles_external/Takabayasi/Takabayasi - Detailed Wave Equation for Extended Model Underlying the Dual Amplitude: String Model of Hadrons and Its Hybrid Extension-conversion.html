What the Paper Does

Develops the detailed wave equation (DWE) further. The original $\sigma$-formalism is rewritten in equivalent forms, one of which makes invariance under the "Hilbert transform manifest; finite gauge transformations are exhibited, and the transformation is non-unitary, corresponding to a complex shift of the spectral parameter. The original string model is then generalised to a hybrid model in which each element of the string carries internal Fermi oscillator variables. The effect is to lower the trajectory intercept from the value the DWE forces — one — by half a unit per added set, removing the intercept restriction that made the original model rigid. The most interesting case, the double Dirac string", uses vector Fermi variables with reality and Lorentz properties different from other authors' similar models.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The extra time coordinates are suppressed because they are not dynamical

The idea. Each element of the string gets its own time so the formalism can be covariant; those times are then suppressed to one, and that suppression is the same thing as removing unphysical states. The two requirements — covariance and unitarity — are met by one mechanism.

What it would add. A statement of why the many-time formalism is not a problem but a resource. The Classical and Quantum Mechanics of a One-Dimensional Relativistic Elastic Continuum conversion in this folder derives the same conclusion from the classical side; the DWE paper states it as the motive for the formalism, which is the cleaner version of the argument.

Where it lands. The conversion of Group of Relativistic Internal Motion in this folder, §The Norm and the Invariant, as the motive.

What stands in the way. The suppression is implemented by a specific integral equation; that it always yields a unitary theory is argued, not proved in general.

2. A model whose parameter is forced is repaired by adding degrees of freedom

The idea. The intercept is one, not chosen. To get other intercepts you add internal Fermi oscillators, each set lowering the intercept by half a unit. The model's rigidity is cured by enlargement, not by parameter fitting.

What it would add. A discrete mechanism for tuning a continuous-looking parameter. Slopes of Mesons (articles_external/Takabayasi/Takabayasi - Slopes of Mesons-conversion.md) extracts the slope from data; here the intercept comes in half-integer steps determined by how many oscillator sets are present, which is a stronger kind of prediction — the allowed values are a lattice, not a range.

Where it lands. The conversion of Slopes of Mesons in this folder, §What the Model Fixes, as the lattice case.

What stands in the way. Each oscillator set must be introduced by hand; nothing fixes how many sets a given hadron has.

3. Reality conditions as the difference between two formally similar models

The idea. Other authors' hybrid models use Fermi variables with different reality and Lorentz properties and need an extra indefinite metric. Here the variables are real, no extra indefinite metric is introduced, and the theory is invariant under space reflection. The differences look technical and decide whether the model is a physical theory.

What it would add. A case where a technical choice — real versus complex oscillators — determines whether an indefinite metric is needed. That is a sharper version of the folder's recurring worry about indefinite metric: the metric is avoidable if the variables are chosen right.

Where it lands. §The Norm and the Invariant of the Group of Relativistic Internal Motion conversion, as the avoidability claim.

What stands in the way. The comparison with other authors is asserted in a sentence; the alternative models are not rederived to show where the indefinite metric enters.

4. Gauge transformations that are non-unitary, and what that means

The idea. The finite gauge transformation shifts $\Lambda$ to a complex $\Lambda'$ and realises an infinite-parameter Lie group nonlinearly. A symmetry that is not unitarily implemented is unusual, and the paper treats it as structure worth understanding rather than a defect.

What it would add. A gauge symmetry outside the unitary paradigm. The corpus's gauge discussions assume unitary implementation or a BRST-like substitute; here the symmetry is explicitly non-unitary and still leaves the theory invariant, which is a case the standard treatment does not cover.

Where it lands. The corpus's gauge discussion in The Lagrangian and the Non-Abelian Gauge Fields (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-non-abelian-gauge-fields.md), as the non-unitary case.

What stands in the way. The physical meaning of the complex shift is not spelled out; the paper calls the group nonlinear and leaves interpretation to the reader.

The remainder, one line each. That the original model gives the multiparticle dual amplitude only at intercept one is the sharpest statement in the folder of what the DWE buys and costs. That fermionic elements are added as internal variables rather than as worldline fermions distinguishes this construction from the later spinning string.

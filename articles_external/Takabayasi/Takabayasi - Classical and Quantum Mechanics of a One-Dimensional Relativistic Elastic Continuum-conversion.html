What the Paper Does

Rebuilds the relativistic string from the classical side, in the parameter representation $x^{\mu}=x^{\mu}(\sigma,\tau)$ for the world strip, and then quantises it in the Heisenberg picture with respect to $\tau$. The parameter $\tau$ then disappears on passing to the Schrödinger picture, leaving a wave equation alone — which turns out to be exactly the "detailed wave equation" the author had previously postulated from the quantum side. The paper isolates what is usually glossed over: the many-time formalism needs extra conditions to be interpretable, the theory must be invariant under rescaling of the parameters, $\tau$ has a specific physical meaning, and a constraint beyond the variational principle is needed to exclude tachyons. In quantum mechanics that constraint is weakened to a normal-ordered form, and the weakened version is the detailed wave equation.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The parameter disappears; the quantum theory has no time parameter

The idea. $\tau$ is needed classically to make the many-time formalism coherent — each material point has its own time, and $\tau$ organises them. But after quantisation, passing to the Schrödinger picture removes $\tau$ entirely and leaves only a wave equation. The reparametrisation parameter is a classical bookkeeping device with no quantum remnant.

What it would add. An answer to what happens to worldsheet time. The Lagrangian and the Nonlinear Sigma Model (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-nonlinear-sigma-model.md) treats a two-dimensional field theory with the worldsheet coordinates on one footing; here $\sigma$ survives as a label and $\tau$ does not survive at all, because $\tau$ parametrises a family of spacelike surfaces, not a coordinate of the system.

Where it lands. §The Worldsheet and the Target, as the parameter-that-vanishes.

What stands in the way. The disappearance is argued at the level of the Heisenberg-to-Schrödinger passage for this specific model; the general claim that a reparametrisation parameter never survives quantisation is not made.

2. Scale invariance of the parameters is a requirement, not a symmetry

The idea. "Invariance under the scale transformation of parameters is stressed." A world-strip parametrisation has a rescaling freedom, and a physically unambiguous model must not depend on it. This is stronger than the usual statement that the action is reparametrisation invariant: it is a condition the interpretation must satisfy.

What it would add. A criterion for acceptable parametrisations. The corpus's gauge discussions treat invariance of the action; here the demand is on the interpretation, and it rules out parametrisations that would make the physical reading ambiguous even though the action is invariant.

Where it lands. The corpus's gauge-fixing discussions; natural home is §The Gauge Field, and the Lorentz Force of Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave.

What stands in the way. The paper asserts the necessity rather than deriving a theorem, and it does not exhibit a parametrisation that fails while the action remains invariant.

3. A constraint outside the variational principle

The idea. The tachyon-excluding constraint does not follow from the Lagrangian. It must be added. That is an admission the corpus rarely makes: the action principle does not generate the whole theory, and a separate condition is needed for the model to be well defined.

What it would add. A case where the Lagrangian is incomplete. Every other derivation in the folder presents the action as sufficient; here the author is explicit that one constraint has to be imposed from outside, and that in quantum mechanics it must be modified to remain compatible with normal ordering.

Where it lands. §What the Subsidiary Condition Does of the conversion of Confinement and the Loss of Partonic Information in this folder, as the constraint not from the action case.

What stands in the way. No principle is offered for which constraints may be added from outside, so the move looks ad hoc even though it is motivated by causality.

4. The Virasoro condition gets a physical origin

The idea. Virasoro "suggested the condition to suppress unphysic~l states occurring" "but the physical origin of the condition was not clear. From". Here it is derived: it is the quantum remnant of a classical causality constraint, weakened to a normal-ordered form so as to be compatible with the commutators. The condition is not a gauge choice but the shadow of a classical requirement.

What it would add. A derivation of a condition the corpus would otherwise quote. The incompatibility of the unweakened conditions with the commutators is what forces the normal ordering, and the normal ordering is what the condition becomes.

Where it lands. The corpus's treatment of constrained quantisation; natural home is §The Reduction of a Numerator of The Anomalous Magnetic Moment in Biquaternionic Form, as a contrast between algebraic and normal-ordering reductions.

What stands in the way. The derivation is for this model with this constraint, and the coincidence with Virasoro's condition is asserted by identification of forms rather than proved equivalent in all respects.

The remainder, one line each. That the intercept comes out negative in both classical and quantum treatments is the paper's consistency check. That the interaction case is treated only briefly, with the equal-$\tau$ commutators unavailable at unequal $\tau$, is the honest limit of the paper.

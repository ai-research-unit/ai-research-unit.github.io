What the Paper Does

Replaces the Pauli spinor by real gauge-independent tensors — density, velocity, spin — together with the identities those fields satisfy. The argument has three steps: a component-wise amplitude–phase substitution is unavailable for a spinor, so the classical reading must go through bilinears; once it does, the quantum terms appear as an internal potential and an internal magnetic field; and the spin torque turns out to have the Herring–Kittel exchange form, so spin waves are solutions.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why the polar substitution fails for a spinor

"ristic situation of the ordinary formulation of quantum mechanics, however, arises in the treatment of spinning particle; viz., the appearance of a spinor with its special double-valued transformation property. In quantum mechanics, wave function can be such a spinor, since originally" A rotation mixes the two components, so a phase attached to each separately is a gauge artefact. The answer is the set of tensor quantities which represents the spinor $\phi$ in a mathematically equivalent and physically meaningful way, with the requirement that every physical relation originally expressed in the language of $\phi$ will have to be expressed in terms of those basic quantities only.

The purpose is definitional: "in classical theories which have to operate according to some realistic model, spinor quantity with its double-valued transformation property cannot appear, at least as an essential thing." The tensor reformulation is what a classical reading would mean.

The basic quantities and their identities

The set is $P=\phi^{\dagger}\phi$, the velocity from the current, and $\vec S=\frac{\hbar}{2}\phi^{\dagger}\vec\sigma\phi$, built from the Kramers null vector $\vec\xi=\tilde\phi\vec\sigma\phi$ split as $\vec M+i\vec N$ with $\vec M\cdot\vec N=0$. He derives "identical relations holding among bilinear quantities" without using any representation of the $\sigma$'s, so every higher bilinear — stress, spin stress, third-rank tensors — is a function of the seven basic quantities and their derivatives.

The quantum terms are an internal potential and an internal magnetic field

The central reading. The quantum contributions are gathered into

$$ \Pi=\Pi_{P}+\Pi_{S}, \qquad \vec H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\vec H+\vec H_{\mathrm{in}}, $$

with $\Pi_{P}$ the ordinary quantum potential, $\Pi_{S}$ the spin field's contribution, and $\vec H_{\mathrm{in}}$ an axial vector built from the spin field's gradients. Hence: "the hydrodynamical motion representing the motion of a quantum-mechanical spinning particle is conducted in such a way that each element of the fluid moves like a classical spinning n particle under the actions not only of external force but also of the internal potential and the internal magnetic field Hin. The equation" A spin–orbit and a spin–spin coupling have appeared although "the system with simple Hamiltonian (2·2)" "does not contain the (well-known) spin-orbit coupling or spin-spin interaction term originally. Now".

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A component-wise polar decomposition has no meaning, and that is why the frame is the only option

The idea. Writing $\phi_{(\alpha)}=R_{(\alpha)}e^{i\chi_{(\alpha)}}$ is not merely awkward: the $R_{(\alpha)}$ and $\chi_{(\alpha)}$ are not tensors under the transformations acting on the spinor, so they carry no physical meaning. Any amplitude–phase reading of a multicomponent object must be made on the object as a whole.

What it would add. The argument the corpus asserts without deriving. The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables (articles_physics/the-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-polar-variables.md) has §The Collapse of the Word and defines the polar decomposition only where the element is invertible — a statement about the element. Takabayasi's is a statement about components, and it is the reason an element-level decomposition is the only available one.

Where it lands. That §, as the motivation, and The Derivative, the Field and the Transform (articles_physics/the-derivative-the-field-and-the-transform.md), where the transformation of components is set out.

What stands in the way. Nothing: the argument is a transformation-law statement the corpus's conventions already imply. Ranked first for being short, correct and missing.

2. The quantum force on the spin is an internal magnetic field, so it cannot be told from a real one

The idea. $\vec H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\vec H+\vec H_{\mathrm{in}}$ is all that appears in the spin's equation, so a quantum torque and a magnetic torque enter identically, and a spin–orbit and a spin–spin coupling are generated in a Hamiltonian that has neither.

What it would add. The complement of a corpus result. The Charged Spin-Half Fluid (articles_physics/the-charged-spin-half-fluid-charge-couples-to-the-lorentz-force-spin-does-not.md) establishes that the classical field does not turn the spin; Takabayasi shows that the terms which do turn it are exactly of magnetic-field type. Together they classify every torque on the spin: the classical field's torque is absent, the quantum torque is a field.

Where it lands. That article's paragraph on convention, and The Spin-Half Fluid and the Takabayasi Correspondence (articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-and-the-takabayasi-correspondence.md), where the dictionary is kept.

What stands in the way. The corpus's relativistic element has no separate quantum potential to gather terms into, since the matter is in the field throughout; the splitting is a non-relativistic artefact, so the import is a statement about the corpus's non-relativistic limit.

3. The spin torque is the exchange torque, and spin waves are solutions

The idea. The torque in $\partial_t\vec S$ has the Herring–Kittel exchange form, so the hydrodynamical equations admit modes that behave as spin waves: a slow spatial rotation of $\vec S$ carried by the same fluid.

What it would add. A mode where the corpus has an equation. The Spin-Half Fluid of the Biquaternion Dirac Field (articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-of-the-biquaternion-dirac-field.md) writes the spin's equation of motion but exhibits no solution, and the corpus's exchange physics is algebraic (The Jordan–Wigner Transformation in Biquaternionic Form), so a continuum mode with an exchange-like restoring torque bridges the two treatments.

Where it lands. That article's equation-of-motion section, as the worked mode.

What stands in the way. The solutions are referred forward to Part II, which is not in this folder, so the claim is clear and the calculation elsewhere.

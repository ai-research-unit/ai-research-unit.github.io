What the Paper Does

Three pages that carry out the program of the 1960 paper for the free case, and the cleanest computation in the folder. One Lagrangian, fixed up to a symmetric matrix of constants, produces every condition the theory needs: the orthonormality constraints, the reparametrisation constraint, the mass formula, the spin, and the spin's equation of motion, which reduces to the rigid-body Euler equation. The mass comes out as $M^{2}=\mu^{2}+\sum_r (S^{r})^{2}/\kappa_r$ with the $S^r$ the body-frame spin components — so mass is generated by the internal rotation and quantised by the spherical case into $M^{2}=\mu^{2}+\hbar^{2}j(j+1)/\kappa$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Lagrangian

$$ L=\Big\{-\mu+\tfrac12\sum_{r,s}A_{rs}\,\omega^{r}\omega^{s}\Big\}\Lambda, \qquad \Lambda=\Big[-\sum_{r}\kappa_r(\omega^{r})^{2}-(\dot X)^{2}\Big]^{1/2}, $$

where $A_{rs}=A_{sr}$ are Lagrangian multipliers while $\mu$ and $\kappa_r$ are (positive) structure constants. ($\mu$ represents the rest mass in the rotation-free case, and $\hbar\kappa_r$ the principal moments of inertia.) It is important that the presence of the term $\sum_r(\dot X a^{r})^{2}$ in (1) ensures the invariance of $L$ under the reparametrisation. Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. One Lagrangian with a multiplier matrix produces the constraints — the corpus imposes its constraints by hand

The idea. A single functional, $L=\{-\mu+\frac12 A_{rs}\omega^r\omega^s\}\Lambda$, generates the triad orthonormality, the reparametrisation invariance, the mass-shell relation and the transversality $S_{\mu\nu}P^{\nu}=0$ as consequences. No constraint is postulated. The multiplier $A_{rs}$ does the work that Dirac's constraint analysis normally has to be brought in to do.

What it would add. A constructive answer to a question the corpus raises. The Relativistic Particle and the Lagrangian: The Stationary Action Principle (articles_physics/the-relativistic-particle-and-the-lagrangian-the-stationary-action-principle.md) builds the action from the world line and the reparametrisation, and The Non-Quantum Limit of the Action Principle and the Lagrangian (articles_physics/the-non-quantum-limit-of-the-action-principle-and-the-lagrangian.md) asks what survives of the quantum structure in that limit; neither derives a constraint surface from the form of the action. Takabayasi's point is different in kind: the transversality constraint is not imposed, it is implied by the form of the Lagrangian, and the mechanism is the $\sum_r(\dot X a^{r})^{2}$ term. That is a design principle for actions, and the corpus's action-building articles (the-lagrangian-for-the-biquaternionic-field.md) do not have it. It would let the corpus derive rather than choose its constraint surface.

Where it lands. The Relativistic Particle and the Lagrangian (articles_physics/the-relativistic-particle-and-the-lagrangian-the-stationary-action-principle.md), as the constructive method, and Rigid-Body Dynamics and the Biquaternion Rotor (articles_physics/rigid-body-dynamics-and-the-biquaternion-rotor.md), as the worked example — a rigid body's Euler equation is the classical case of the same mechanism.

What stands in the way. The corpus's field is a field and not a particle; the multiplier here is a finite matrix and there is no direct analogue for a field's constraints. The transferable part is the principle — arrange the action so the wanted constraints follow — and not the formula. That is a paragraph, and it ranks first because the principle is general.

2. Mass is

$\mu^{2}$ plus the body-frame spin squared over the moments of inertia

The idea. $M^{2}=\mu^{2}+\sum_r (S^{r})^{2}/\kappa_r$. The mass of the state is the rotation-free mass plus a term quadratic in the internal angular momentum, weighted by inverse inertia. For the spherical case $M^{2}=\mu^{2}+\hbar^{2}j(j+1)/\kappa$: a Regge-like tower with slope $1/\kappa$ and a nonzero intercept.

What it would add. A worked mass formula where the corpus has none. The corpus's mass articles — Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass (articles_physics/antilinear-structure-and-the-two-kinds-of-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md), The Invariant Measure on the Mass Shell (articles_physics/the-invariant-measure-on-the-mass-shell.md) — treat mass as a parameter or a geometric datum; the folder's Slopes of Mesons and Mass Rules for Baryons papers give phenomenological fits. This is the middle case: a mass formula derived from an action, with one dimensional constant per body axis, giving $M^{2}$ linear in $j(j+1)$ with an intercept $\mu^{2}$. It is the simplest derivation of a linear trajectory in this whole folder, and the corpus's Regge discussion does not cite it.

Where it lands. The two-kinds-of-mass article, as a third kind (mass from internal motion), and The Slope of the Trajectory wherever the corpus treats linearity.

What stands in the way. $\kappa$ is a free constant, so the formula predicts the shape and not the scale; and the degeneracy in $S_3$ and $S_{12}$ is wrong for the observed spectrum, which the folder's later papers repair with more elaborate models. The import must be labelled a mechanism and not a fit. It ranks second.

3. The spin's equation of motion is the rigid-body Euler equation

The idea. For the free rotator the body-frame spin obeys $\dot S^{r}=\mu\Lambda^{-1}(\kappa_s-\kappa_t)\omega^{s}\omega^{t}$ — the Euler equation of a rigid body, with the inertia anisotropy as the only torque. There is no other dynamics: a free relativistic rotator is a spinning top.

What it would add. A test case for the corpus's spin transport. The Spin-Half Fluid of the Biquaternion Dirac Field (articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-of-the-biquaternion-dirac-field.md) and The Charged Spin-Half Fluid (articles_physics/the-charged-spin-half-fluid-charge-couples-to-the-lorentz-force-spin-does-not.md) both concern what turns the spin. Takabayasi's answer for a free body: nothing turns it except the anisotropy of its own inertia — and for the spherical case, nothing at all. That is the classical baseline against which the corpus's "the field does not turn the spin" claim should be read: for a spherical free rotator the spin is exactly conserved, so the corpus's statement is the relativistic-field version of a familiar fact and not a paradox.

Where it lands. The charged spin-half fluid article, in the paragraph on convention, as the classical baseline.

What stands in the way. The rotator's spin is an orbital-type angular momentum of an extended body, not a spinor's, so the comparison is between the behaviour of two different objects. Ranked last.

The remainder, one line each. The proper-time gauge and the $\tau=X^{0}/c$ gauge are two foliations of the same reparametrisation freedom, a point the corpus's temporal-slot articles make abstractly. The non-relativistic limit $M=\mu+\frac12\sum\hbar\kappa_r(\omega^{r})^{2}$ is the rigid-body energy and needs no comment. That the method applies to bilocal and trilocal models is the subject of the companion papers.

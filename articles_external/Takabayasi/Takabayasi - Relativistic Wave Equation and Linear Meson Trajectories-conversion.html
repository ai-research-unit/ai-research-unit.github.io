What the Paper Does

Presents a bilocal wave equation on degenerate representations of the dynamical group $SU(3,1)$ whose timelike solutions have the exact linear spectrum $m^{2}=\kappa^{2}(N-\tfrac12)$ with $N=S+2w$, giving parallel linear trajectories with daughters spaced by $\Delta S=2$. The leading trajectory is identified with $\rho$–$A_2$ under exchange degeneracy, which fixes $\kappa^{2}=2m_\rho^{2}$ and the intercept, and the lowest state's wave function gives a dipole form factor $(1-t/2m_\rho^{2})^{-2}$. Two variants are sketched, one with spacing $\Delta S=1$ and one using a Majorana–Kemmer product.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The Regge slope is fixed by the lowest vector meson, and daughters are spaced by two units of spin

The idea. A group-theoretic equation yields $m^{2}=\kappa^{2}(S+2w-\tfrac12)$ exactly. Identifying $w=0$ with $\rho$–$A_2$ under exchange degeneracy gives $\kappa^{2}=2m_\rho^{2}$ and $m(A_2)^{2}=3m_\rho^{2}$ as consequences, not fits. The daughter spacing $\Delta S=2$ is a property of the representation: $N=S+2w$.

What it would add. A derivation of two numbers the corpus quotes. The Spectrum of a Lorentz Rotor: What Two Eigenvalues Leave Undetermined (articles_physics/the-spectrum-of-a-lorentz-rotor-what-two-eigenvalues-leave-undetermined.md) asks which quantities a spectrum fixes; here the answer is that the representation fixes both the slope and the daughter spacing, and the only empirical input is $m_\rho$. The Spin–Mass Formula and the Spectrum of Elementary Particles in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-spin-mass-formula-and-the-spectrum-of-elementary-particles-in-biquaternionic-form.md) predicts ratios from degrees; this predicts the trajectory structure from an index relation, and the two schemes can be compared on the same states.

Where it lands. §The Spectrum, as the trajectory scheme.

What stands in the way. The identification of $w=0$ with $\rho$–$A_2$ is an input, so $\kappa$ is determined by data; the prediction is the shape — spacing two, natural parity, the $0^+$ daughter at the $A_2$ mass — and not the scale.

2. An exact dipole form factor from the lowest wave function

The idea. The ground-state wave function, when the internal coordinate is integrated, gives a form factor exactly of dipole shape, $(1-t/2m_\rho^{2})^{-2}$ — the shape usually fitted to nucleon data and usually attributed to a superposition of vector mesons.

What it would add. A structural origin for a fitted shape. The Anomalous Magnetic Moment in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-anomalous-magnetic-moment-in-biquaternionic-form.md) computes the vertex in §The Vertex Function and the Form Factors and separates the form factors algebraically; here the shape follows from the internal wave function's spread, which is a different mechanism producing the same functional form.

Where it lands. §The Vertex Function and the Form Factors, as the composite alternative.

What stands in the way. The result is asserted —"it is shown" — with the calculation deferred to a longer paper, and the dipole shape with the $\rho$ mass built in is close to what vector dominance assumes, so the agreement is not independent evidence.

3. Space-like solutions with a discrete spectrum

The idea. The same equation has timelike solutions $m^{2}=\kappa^{2}(N-\tfrac12)$ and spacelike solutions $m^{2}=-\kappa^{2}(N'+\tfrac12)$, both discrete. The tachyonic sector is not a continuum but a mirror ladder with half-integer offset.

What it would add. A concrete structure for the unacceptable sector the folder keeps encountering. The Massless Regime (articles_physics/the-massless-regime.md) treats masslessness as a boundary; here the spacelike side is populated by a ladder and the massless point sits between two discrete series, which is a sharper picture than space-like solutions may occur.

Where it lands. §The Massless Case, as the discrete-sector caveat.

What stands in the way. Takabayasi reports the spacelike spectrum without excluding it, and the model is offered as a suggestion about Veneziano theory rather than as a physical theory.

4. Choosing the representation is a physical act

The idea. The equation is written on degenerate representations of $SU(3,1)$, so a further condition $a_\mu^{\dagger}a^{\mu}\phi=0$ is needed to pick one irreducible space. The spectrum then follows from the chosen representation, not from the equation alone.

What it would add. A worked case of the equation does not determine the theory; the pair does. Higher Spin from Tensor Products: The Three Carriers and the Absence of a Fourth (articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md) counts what each carrier can hold; here the carrier is fixed and the sector is chosen by hand, so the counting is one level down from what that article does.

Where it lands. §What a Module Can Carry, as the sector-selection step.

What stands in the way. No principle is given for preferring one irreducible space over another beyond producing the desired spectrum.

The remainder, one line each. That the Kemmer–Majorana product version introduces antiparticles where the bilocal version has none is a structural gain worth recording. That the $\Delta S=1$ variant comes from an $SO(4,2)$ product ties this letter to the conformal group rather than to the symplectic one.

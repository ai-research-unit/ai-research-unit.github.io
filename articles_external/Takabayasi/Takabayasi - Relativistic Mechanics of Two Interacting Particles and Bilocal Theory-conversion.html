What the Paper Does

Founds the bilocal programme properly. Starting from arbitrary parametrisations of the two world lines and requiring covariance under their general transformation, it derives equations of motion for two particles interacting by an arbitrary central action-at-a-distance. One particular gauge turns the theory into a canonical formalism with two weak equations; quantising there makes all parameters disappear and leaves exactly a bilocal wave equation with a subsidiary condition. The Hooke case reproduces elliptic oscillations in the centre-of-mass frame and bound states on a straightly rising trajectory. Against the folklore that canonical action-at-a-distance is impossible in relativity, the paper exhibits one.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Reparametrisation covariance as a method of derivation, not a check

The idea. The equations of motion are not guessed and then verified to be covariant. Requiring covariance under general reparametrisation of the two world lines is used to derive them. The covariance is a tool that generates the theory.

What it would add. A derivation strategy the corpus does not use. The Relativistic Particle and the Lagrangian: The Stationary-Action Principle (articles_physics/the-relativistic-particle-and-the-lagrangian-the-stationary-action-principle.md) treats reparametrisation as a property of an action already written; here it is the constraint that determines the action's structure, which is a stronger use of the same invariance and a transferable technique.

Where it lands. §Symmetries of the Action, as the generative use of covariance.

What stands in the way. Two world lines with independent parameters give a two-parameter family of reparametrisations; the derivation proceeds by imposing a form and checking, so the "derivation" is a constrained ansatz rather than a uniqueness theorem.

2. Canonical action-at-a-distance is possible — with two weak equations

The idea. The literature said no canonical action-at-a-distance interaction exists in relativity. The answer here is that it exists if the canonical formalism is allowed two weak equations (constraints that vanish only on shell). The obstruction was not physical but an insistence on a formalism without constraints.

What it would add. A directly contrary claim to a standard no-go statement, with the loophole identified. This is the kind of result the corpus should record because it changes what counts as "impossible": not action-at-a-distance, but action-at-a-distance with an unconstrained canonical structure.

Where it lands. The corpus's constrained-systems discussion; natural home is §The Stationary-Action Principle of The Relativistic Particle and the Lagrangian.

What stands in the way. The construction works for the central-potential case and for two particles; the no-go literature concerns more general settings, so the contradiction is narrower than it sounds.

3. The Correspondence Principle fixes the theory; the potential is the only freedom

The idea. Once one demands that the relativistic theory reduce to Newtonian mechanics with potential $U$ in the non-relativistic weak-coupling limit, the structure is fixed and only $U$ remains free. Then all bilocal models in the literature are the same theory with different $U$ — and their differences in internal dynamics are differences of potential, nothing deeper.

What it would add. A classification of the folder's own models. Reading this against the Yukawa-model history in Relativistic Mechanics of Confined Particles shows that the several "versions" differ by choice of structure function, which is exactly the parametrisation this paper establishes as the only freedom. That is a stronger organising statement than a historical list.

Where it lands. §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits of Hadron Structure from QCD: The Bound-State Problem, as the potential-only-freedom claim.

What stands in the way. The claim covers models that satisfy the correspondence requirement; a bilocal model built from a different principle would not be covered, and the paper does not argue that all interesting ones do satisfy it.

4. Elliptic internal motion

The idea. For Hooke binding, the internal motion in the centre-of-mass frame is an ellipse, not a circle and not a straight-line oscillation. The general two-parameter orbit is elliptic, and the circular and radial cases are its degenerate limits.

What it would add. A concrete orbit to compare with the folder's rigid-rotator and oscillator pictures. The Spectrum of a Lorentz Rotor: What Two Eigenvalues Leave Undetermined (articles_physics/the-spectrum-of-a-lorentz-rotor-what-two-eigenvalues-leave-undetermined.md) treats rotation with fixed length; here the length oscillates and the orbit is an ellipse, so the rotor is the circular special case of a larger family.

Where it lands. §The Spectrum, as the elliptic generalisation.

What stands in the way. The elliptic motion is classical; whether it survives quantisation as an observable structure, rather than only as a spectrum, is not shown. The remainder, one line each. That quantisation removes the parameters entirely is the same disappearance seen in the string paper, now for two points. That the paper refuses Wigner's approach as "not physically adequate" is a rare explicit rejection of a named alternative.

What the Paper Does

Supplies the classical correspondence basis of the bilocal wave equation. The force acts between "conjugate" points on the two world lines — points whose separation is orthogonal to both 4-velocities — and that single prescription makes the interaction relativistic. With Hooke binding the motion is rigid, the 4-momentum and angular momentum are conserved, and the conservation laws give a linear relation between $m^{2}$ and $J$. The general solution is written down explicitly and reduces to Newtonian Hooke motion in the non-relativistic limit. Quantising in the proper-time gauge gives the bilocal equation with two subsidiary conditions. Extending to $N$ mutually bound particles is possible only for $N\le3$, by a degree-of-freedom count.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Rigidity as the remnant of a conformal constraint

The idea. The single-mode truncation of the string reproduces the two-point rigid model exactly, and the rigidity constraint is what survives of the string's conformal constraint when all but the first mode are switched off. The mesonic two-body model and the string are not analogies; one is a truncation of the other.

What it would add. A derivation of the relationship instead of a comparison. The folder's string papers and bilocal papers are usually presented as parallel constructions; here the reduction is explicit and the constraint is traced through it, which is a stronger statement than the model is a prototype of the string.

Where it lands. The conversion of Detailed Wave Equation and Dual Amplitude in this folder, §The Model and the Constraint, as the truncation.

What stands in the way. The equivalence holds for the first-mode-only motion; the higher modes carry the dual amplitude, which the rigid model cannot reproduce, so the truncation loses exactly what makes the string interesting.

2. One constraint per pair, counted, gives a size bound

The idea. Each pair of constituents contributes one rigid-motion constraint after $(N-1)$ are spent on fixing correspondences, leaving $(N-1)^{2}$ constraints and $f_{\mathrm{int}}=5N-N^{2}-1$ internal degrees of freedom. That quadratic is positive only at $N=2,3$. The bound is arithmetic, not dynamical.

What it would add. A ceiling derived by counting rather than assumed. Higher Spin from Tensor Products: The Three Carriers and the Absence of a Fourth (articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md) also gets a bound, from algebra; here the same number three appears from a degree-of-freedom count on a different construction. Two independent routes to the same bound is a much stronger claim than either alone, and the corpus should say so.

Where it lands. §The Ceiling in Context, as the counting ceiling — beside the algebraic one.

What stands in the way. The count assumes every constituent has three degrees of freedom and that all constraints are independent; the second assumption is exactly what a degenerate configuration would violate.

3. Conjugate points make an action-at-a-distance differential

The idea. A general action-at-a-distance force gives integro-differential equations because the retarded point is not known in advance. Fixing the force to act between conjugate points — defined by orthogonality to both velocities — makes the equations differential and solvable, while remaining relativistic by construction.

What it would add. A mechanism for taming nonlocality that is neither an expansion nor a truncation. A Non-local Wave Equation and its Connection with the Dirac Particle in this folder makes the equation nonlocal in its argument; here the classical nonlocality is reduced to a point-to-point map, which is a different and very concrete device.

Where it lands. The conversion of A Non-local Wave Equation and its Connection with the Dirac Particle in this folder, as the conjugate-point device.

What stands in the way. The prescription is defined for rigid motion; when rigidity is relaxed the author has to replace the condition by an average-velocity orthogonality, and the general case is left to §6's outline.

4. A linear $m^{2}$–$J$ relation with no daughters

The idea. $m^{2}=4\mu^{2}+2\mu\hbar\omega J$ holds both classically and quantum mechanically, with integer $J$. There are no daughter trajectories, and the absence is attributed directly to the rigidity condition.

What it would add. An explanation of a spectral feature rather than a fit to it. The folder's other trajectory papers report daughters; here their absence is traced to one constraint, which tells you what to relax to get them.

Where it lands. The conversion of Slopes of Mesons in this folder, §What the Model Fixes, as the no-daughter case.

What stands in the way. The zero-point motion footnote means the quantum $J$ starts at zero while the classical ground state has $r_0=0$; the correspondence at the bottom of the trajectory is therefore loose.

The remainder, one line each. That the two subsidiary conditions reappear with the same two jobs — suppress negative norm, or suppress degeneracy — confirms the pattern across the folder. That the unequal-mass case gives concentric coplanar circles with a common angular velocity is the cleanest statement of what "rigid" means here.

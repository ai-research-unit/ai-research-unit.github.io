Contents in One Paragraph

The letter extends Madelung's scalar fluid to the Pauli spinor. A density $P=\psi^*\psi$ and a velocity $v$ satisfy continuity; a spin field $S=\tfrac12\psi^*\boldsymbol{\sigma}\psi$ of fixed length $\hbar/2$ is tied to the rotational part of the velocity by a vorticity condition containing both the spin gradient and the magnetic field. The spinor equation becomes three real equations — continuity, Euler with a Stern–Gerlach-type force and a spin-gradient stress, and precession — plus the two algebraic conditions, all gauge-invariant. Neglecting $\hbar^2$ terms gives the classical spinning particle; the classical spin is defined as a single canonical degree of freedom, explicitly not a rotating model.

Where to Read Further

The four ideas extracted from this paper — spin as freed vorticity, the separation of the gradient force from the spin stress, gauge invariance carried by the velocity, and the author's own caveat that the fluid picture should not be taken too realistically — are developed in the canonical conversion.

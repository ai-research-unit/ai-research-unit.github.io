What the Paper Does

Builds a quantum theory of a point-like system that carries internal degrees of freedom at its own position: a one-parameter series of events $x^{\mu}(\tau)$ with internal variables $\xi_a$, an indefinite metric on those internal degrees, and a $\tau$-independent self-adjoint Hamiltonian. The rest mass becomes a dynamical operator with an eigenvalue spectrum, the internal angular momentum tensor is responsible for spin and for Zitterbewegung, and the instantaneous velocity $v^{\mu}$ is not collinear with $p^{\mu}$ but restricted by kinematical conditions. Those conditions classify the systems, and for one class three invariants built from $p$, $v$ and $S$ are available to be identified with isospin, hypercharge and baryon number.

Zitterbewegung, and the proper centre

The internal tensor splits as $J_{\mu\nu}=S_{\mu\nu}+x_{[\mu}p_{\nu]}$, and $S_{\mu\nu}$ further as $$ S_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{m}w_{[\mu}p_{\nu]}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}r^{\rho}p^{\sigma}, \qquad r^{\mu}=\frac{\tilde S^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}}{m^{2}}, $$"which represents the radius vector of the Zitterbewegung". The conservation law becomes $\dot y_{[\mu}p_{\nu]}+\dot S_{\mu\nu}=0$ with $y^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-r^{\mu}$, the proper center of mass of the system, which is the position that excludes from $x^{\mu}$ the Zitterbewegung $r_{\mu}$, and both terms are separately conserved. Crucially, the components of $r_{\mu}$ (and so of $y_{\mu}$) are not mutually commuting in contrast with those of $x_{\mu}$. Two scalars follow, $\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\Omega-m^{2}\Lambda)$ and $\chi=-m\,w_{\mu}r^{\mu}$, the non-orthogonality between spin and Zitterbewegung, which he calls internal chirality; $\Lambda$ is the squared radius of the Zitterbewegung.

The velocity, and the classification

$v^{\mu}=dx^{\mu}/d\tau$ is a new basic variable with $[v_{\mu},p_{\nu}]=0$ and $[v_{\mu},x_{\nu}]=i\hbar\,h_{\mu\nu}$, the relation (89) means that $h_{\mu\nu}$ is a symmetric tensor, while the antisymmetric part measures the failure of the velocity to be a pure translation. Three criteria follow: whether $h_{\mu\nu}$ vanishes, whether the antisymmetric part vanishes, and whether the velocity commutes with the position. For the "normal class, $p\equiv-v_{\mu}^{2}$ is an absolute invariant", takes eigenvalues 1 and 0, and is "regarded to represent baryon number". Systems of the first kind satisfy $[v_{\mu},x_{\nu}]=0$, and their internal variables $(S_{\mu\nu},v^{\mu})$ yield two further vectors on which the kinematics closes. He stresses that $v^{\mu}$ and $p^{\mu}$ "are essentially different", so states with $v^{2}=0$ but $m\neq0$ occur.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Mass is an operator whose spectrum is the particle spectrum, and localisation in space costs mass uncertainty

The idea. $m^{2}=-p_{\mu}^{2}$ is a dynamical quantity of the system rather than a parameter, so a state need not have definite mass; there are uncertainty relations between the mass value and space-time localisation, and the empirical mass spectrum is the spectrum of an operator.

What it would add. A mechanism for what the corpus leaves as a parameter. Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/antilinear-structure-and-the-two-kinds-of-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md) distinguishes two masses structurally; neither it nor the shell's measure article says where a discrete set of masses comes from. Here the eigenvalue condition on $H$ is the mass condition, and $\eta H=H^{\dagger}\eta$ is the only extra ingredient.

Where it lands. The two-kinds-of-mass article, as a third kind, and The N-Norm, the Mass Angle and the Cone (articles_physics/the-n-norm-mass-angle-and-the-cone.md).

What stands in the way. The corpus's field has a fixed mass parameter; making mass dynamical means giving the field an internal configuration space, a new structure rather than a translation. Ranked first because it answers a question the corpus poses.

2. An indefinite metric on internal degrees is not a defect: it is what makes the internal configuration unmeasurable

The idea. Negative probabilities are admitted for the internal configuration only, because $\theta_a$"cannot be measured as such". The metric $\eta$ acts on internal variables alone, is $\tau$-independent, and the self-adjointness condition $\eta H=H^{\dagger}\eta$ makes the norm conserved and the expectations real.

What it would add. The precise version of the corpus's indefinite-metric discussion. The K-Norm and the Indefinite Structure (articles_physics/the-k-norm-and-the-indefinite-structure.md) and The Group of Invertibles, the Cone and the Orders (articles_physics/the-group-of-invertibles-the-cone-and-the-orders.md) treat indefiniteness as an algebraic order; here it is localised — the external sector stays positive, the internal does not, and the split says what can be measured.

Where it lands. The indefinite-metric article, as the physical reading of the split, and the cone article's §Two Orders.

What stands in the way. The corpus's indefinite directions are nilpotent and tied to the light cone, not to a separate internal factor; identifying "internal" with the directions the cone's interior does not order needs care.

3. Zitterbewegung is the offset between the position and the proper centre, and only the offset's components fail to commute

The idea. $S_{\mu\nu}$ splits into a constant spin term and an orbital term with radius $r^{\mu}=\tilde S^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}/m^{2}$; the proper centre $y^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-r^{\mu}$ carries the conserved orbital part, and while the $x$'s commute with each other, the $r$'s do not. The trembling is therefore not motion of a point but the non-commutativity of the radius vector.

What it would add. A structural account of an effect the corpus mentions only by name. The Eigenspinor: The Lorentz Rotor as a Function of Proper Time (articles_physics/the-eigenspinor-the-lorentz-rotor-as-a-function-of-proper-time.md) and Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/angular-momentum-and-spin-in-biquaternionic-form.md) treat the spin tensor's decomposition but not the position offset.

Where it lands. The eigenspinor article, as the kinematics of the offset, and the angular-momentum article's decomposition section.

What stands in the way. The construction needs $m\neq0$ and a distinguished rest frame; the corpus's massless sector has no analogue, so the result applies to the massive sector only.

4. Three invariants from

$(p,v,S)$ alone, and a checklist for calling one of them isospin

The idea. The velocity $v^{\mu}$ supplies quantities that $p$ and $J$ cannot, and for systems of the first kind the set $(S_{\mu\nu},v^{\mu})$ yields $H^{\mu}=S^{\mu\nu}v_{\nu}$, $K^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\{S^{\mu\nu},v_{\nu}\}$ and the scalar $\chi=-m\,w_{\mu}r^{\mu}$, from which three commuting self-adjoint invariants can be built. The paper states the five requirements such a quantity must meet — self-adjoint, invariant, conserved, correct algebra, discrete spectrum — before it may be named isospin or hypercharge.

What it would add. A criterion where the corpus has a hope. Why the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches $SU(2)$ but Not $SU(3)$ (articles_physics/why-the-biquaternion-algebra-reaches-su-2-but-not-su-3.md) explains the algebraic ceiling; this paper says what a candidate second internal quantum number must satisfy, whatever the algebra.

Where it lands. That article's close, as the physical requirements list.

What stands in the way. The invariants are asserted to exist for systems of the first kind and not exhibited for a definite model; the import is the criterion, not the construction.

The remainder, one line each. That $p=-v_{\mu}^{2}$ takes only 1 and 0, the value read as baryon number, is a normalisation convention for $\tau$ elevated to a quantum number. That Dirac and Kemmer particles are the non-classical special cases with no internal degrees beyond $v^{\mu}$ is the paper's own comment on the corpus's Dirac sector.

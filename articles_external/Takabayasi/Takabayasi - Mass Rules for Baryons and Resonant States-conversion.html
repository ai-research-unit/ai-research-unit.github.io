What the Paper Does

Generalises an earlier baryon mass formula, derived from a mechanical model of a baryon as a disc rotating in isospace, so that it covers the low-lying resonances as well. The result is a single expression in $Y$, $T$ and $J$ that reproduces every baryon and the known resonances to better than one percent, and that implies an equal-spacing rule with a spacing of $289.2$ MeV fixed by the meson masses. The whole spectrum follows from one input, the $\Lambda$ mass.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The mass quantum is shared between hypercharge and spin, so one formula covers both multiplets and trajectories

The idea. In $m=\frac14\{m_\pi(2J+3)+m_\eta(2J-1)\}+\frac12m_\pi(Y-2)$ the same two mass scales multiply $(2J)$ and $(Y-2)$. So the spacing in $J$ and the spacing in hypercharge are the same quantum seen twice, which is why a single formula covers an $SU(3)$ multiplet and a Regge trajectory at once.

What it would add. A reason why the two organising principles coincide. SU(3) Representations and the Gell-Mann–Okubo Mass Formula in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/su3-representations-and-the-gell-mann-okubo-mass-formula-in-biquaternionic-form.md) treats the multiplet as an $SU(3)$ representation in §Reduction of the Octet into Isospin Multiplets and the trajectory is not in its scope; here one linear form has both $J$ and $Y$ as arguments with the same coefficients' magnitude, so the coincidence is not a coincidence but a shared unit.

Where it lands. §The Supermultiplet Mass $m_0$, as the trajectory counterpart, and §Open Questions, where the relation between the two schemes is listed.

What stands in the way. The equality is arranged by choosing $a=4\sqrt2$ and the coefficients in (2); it is a fit with one dimensionless number, so the "shared unit" is an interpretation of a fitted relation.

2. Every mass from one input

The idea. With $m_\Lambda$ as the only input and the relation $m_\Lambda m_\pi=(m_\pi/2)^{2}$-type geometric mean fixing the auxiliary scales, nine states follow to within $6$ MeV. The spectrum is over-determined with one parameter.

What it would add. The strongest economy claim in the folder. The Spin–Mass Formula and the Spectrum of Elementary Particles in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-spin-mass-formula-and-the-spectrum-of-elementary-particles-in-biquaternionic-form.md) normalises at the electron and predicts the proton to $3.6\%$; Takabayasi normalises at $\Lambda$ and predicts resonances to $0.3\%$. Neither is a derivation, but the number of fitted constants differs by an order of magnitude, and that difference is worth a table.

Where it lands. §The Spectrum, as the comparison row.

What stands in the way. The relations (7)–(9) used to reach the higher states are assumptions of equal spacing, chosen after the states were known; the "one input" claim is true only for the family already selected.

3. A mechanical model in isospace, not a field equation

The idea. The formula is derived from a rigid disc rotating in isospace, with isospin as the angular momentum and $Y/2$ as its projection on the normal — a classical model of an internal space, with a moment of inertia, giving a mass formula.

What it would add. A different kind of derivation from the ones the corpus has. The Skyrme Model and the Topological Baryon in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-skyrme-model-and-the-topological-baryon-in-biquaternionic-form.md) makes the baryon a soliton, hence an extended object whose mass comes from field energy; here the extension is in isospace, the dynamics is a rigid body, and the mass comes from rotational energy. Two "extended baryon" pictures with entirely different carriers, brought together, would show what each buys.

Where it lands. That article's discussion of the baryon's extent, as the isospace alternative.

What stands in the way. The disc is a heuristic: no field theory is written, and the model is used only to motivate three coefficient relations.

The remainder, one line each. That the equal spacing $289.2$ MeV equals $\frac12m_\eta-\frac32m_\pi$ is a numerical identity worth checking on modern masses. That Matumoto's and Katayama's sum rules differ from these is a note on priority and belongs in the corpus's history notes, not its physics.

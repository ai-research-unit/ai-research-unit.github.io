What the Paper Does

The companion to Part I. It treats the "geometric" string — full worldsheet covariance, action forced to be the Nambu–Goto one — and places it as the singular limit $(\omega=0)$ of the realistic theory. The analogy the author offers is the one between the de Broglie–Proca field and the Maxwell field: the geometric string is what the realistic string degenerates into when a mass-like structure constant is switched off, and the passage is smooth only if the internal motion is restricted to be transversal. Solutions are illustrated, quantisation is sketched, and the algebra's loss of closure at the quantum level is stated with the central charge written out explicitly.

Transversality, and what it buys

This theory is, as described in §§ 2–4 of I, complete in itself but we remark that this theory still allows the introduction of a certain physical constraint consistently. The interest in this constraint comes from the fact that it brings about a particular simplification of theory and also that with the introduction thereof the theory becomes common both for $\omega\neq0$ and for $\omega=0$ cases. Physically (3·1) restricts the internal motion to transversal ones (rotation, lateral vibration) alone, and thus reduces the degree of freedom of each 'parton' of the string from three to two. Closing assessment

Our string theory for hadrons implies a genuine space-time model. In our theory it is essential that the basic variables $x^{\mu}(\sigma,\tau)$ have the meaning of space-time coordinates, because it is based on this interpretation that we can properly identify the 4-momentum of the system, formulate causality and locate interactions. The variables $x^{\mu}$ play the dual role: They physically mean the 4-dimensional positional coordinates, but at the same time they are viewed like a field in the 2-dimensional space of Gauss coordinates $(\sigma,\tau)$. Thus our string theory which is still at the one-particle level is formally analogous to a field theory in 2-dimensional space-time."The realistic viewpoint has led us to the theory of relativistic string which is more general than the theory obtained from the geometrical viewpoint and includes the latter theory as a singular limiting case ($\omega=0$) where some special features arise in the classical level by higher symmetry. A drawback of the geometrical viewpoint is that in quantization and interaction one is obliged to amend arbitrarily the theory by introducing a non-zero $\omega$ ... Another drawback of the geometrical approach is that it does not lead to a suitable classical foundation for the quantum theory in the presence of coupling with external scalar field." The simple string theory treated in this paper is only an ideal one for a model of hadrons. Especially the result (5·6), which was necessary for ghost suppression as well as for deriving dual amplitude in quantum theory, indicates the occurrence of tachyonic ground state and violates the causality condition. Also the theory lacks the isospin, half-integer spin and baryon number properties.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

  1. The massless limit as a singular limit of a massive theory

The idea. The geometric string is not prior to the realistic one. It is what remains when a structure constant $\omega$ is taken to zero — the relationship being like Proca to Maxwell. The "simpler" theory is the degenerate case, and its extra symmetry is a symptom of the degeneration.

What it would add. A reversal of the usual priority. Textbooks treat the Nambu–Goto string as fundamental and $\omega$ as a later addition; here the reverse is argued explicitly and with consequences (two stated drawbacks of the geometric approach). Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/conformal-invariance-and-the-massless-limit-in-biquaternionic-form.md) also treats a massless case as a limit, but with the massless theory as the simpler, more symmetric one; this paper says the extra symmetry is a liability for both quantisation and coupling.

Where it lands. §The Massless Limit, as the reversed priority.

What stands in the way. The claim that the geometric approach obliges arbitrary amendment is a judgement about practice; a different regularisation might not need $\omega$.

  1. The central charge, written out

The idea. $[\Lambda_r,\Lambda_s]=\hbar(r-s)\Lambda_{r+s}+\hbar^{2}\delta_{r+s,0}r(r^{2}-1)/3$. The anomaly appears only upon quantisation, it destroys closure, and the response is to truncate the conditions to $r\ge0$ — which is exactly the loss of half the algebra.

What it would add. The anomaly with its consequences spelled out: not just there is a central term but therefore the constraint set must be cut in half, and $\omega=0$ loses its special classical status. The corpus's discussions of anomalies treat them as obstructions to be cancelled; here the response is truncation, which is a different and cheaper strategy with its own cost (loss of the $r<0$ conditions).

Where it lands. §The Gauge Symmetry and the Constraints of The Lagrangian and the Non-Abelian Gauge Fields (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-non-abelian-gauge-fields.md), as the truncation response.

What stands in the way. Whether truncation suffices to remove all negative-norm states is, in this paper, still an expectation verified only for one value.

3. $\tau$ disappears from the stationary states

The idea. In the Schrödinger picture with respect to $\tau$, the wave functional is restricted to stationary solutions $\Psi=\exp(i\pi\omega\tau/2\hbar)\Psi(0)$, so $\tau$ drops out entirely and the theory is a functional of $\sigma$ only. Where it lands. The conversion of Relativistic Quantum Mechanics of a Mechanical Continuum Underlying the Dual Amplitude in this folder, §The Separation and the Tower, as the mechanism.

4. Being a spacetime model is what makes the theory testable

The idea. $x^{\mu}(\sigma,\tau)$ is simultaneously a position in spacetime and a field on a two-dimensional parameter space. Where it lands. §The Model and the Constraint of The Lagrangian and the Nonlinear Sigma Model, as the interpretive criterion.

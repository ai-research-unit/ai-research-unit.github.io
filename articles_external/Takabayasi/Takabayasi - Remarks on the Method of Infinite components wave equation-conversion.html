What the Paper Does

Three remarks on the tower wave equation of the preceding note. First, its norm is real but not positive definite, so no one-particle probability interpretation survives — the same situation as the unquantised Klein–Gordon equation. Second, replacing $P_{\mu}$ by a constant and keeping a second-order equation reverses the spectrum, which isolates exactly what makes the Majorana spectrum fall: it is the order of the equation, not the representation. Third, the method extends from $0(3,2)$ to $0(4,2)=SU(2,2)$ merely by doubling the integer suffixes, and giving the suffixes a three-valued index lets internal $SU(3)$ ride on the tower.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. An indefinite norm does not spoil the equation; it only forbids a probability reading

The idea. The current is conserved, the norm is real, and it is not positive. Nothing about the equation is inconsistent — the failure is confined to the interpretation. The remedy is not to change the equation but to second-quantise, exactly as for the Klein–Gordon field.

What it would add. A precise statement of what an indefinite norm costs. The Finite-Dimensional Representations and Their Unitarity Defect (articles_physics/the-finite-dimensional-representations-and-their-unitarity-defect.md) shows that the algebra's finite-dimensional representations cannot be unitary; Takabayasi shows the tower is unitary as a representation of $0(3,2)$ while the equation built on it still has an indefinite norm. Unitarity of the representation and positivity of the norm are different properties, and this is a clean case where they come apart.

Where it lands. That article's defect section, as the infinite-dimensional control case.

What stands in the way. The tower's unitarity is with respect to the compact generators; the generators the equation uses are not all self-adjoint on the same form, so the two notions must be separated before the comparison is made.

2. The sign of the mass formula is the order of the equation

The idea. Same tower, same labels, two equations: first order gives $m=\kappa/(J+\tfrac12)$ with antiparticles, second order gives $m=\kappa(J+\tfrac12)$ without them. The spectrum's direction is a property of the differential order, not of the internal representation.

What it would add. A clean separation the corpus does not make. The Majorana Equation in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-majorana-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.md) locates the mass in the right-hand slot and shows in §Why the Mass Sits on the Right what the two placements do to the shell; the present letter adds a third placement axis — the order — and shows it alone flips the spectrum. That is a different and cheaper diagnostic than a change of carrier.

Where it lands. §The Equation and Its Three Forms, as a fourth axis, and §Why the Mass Sits on the Right as the adjacent case.

What stands in the way. The two equations are not related by a transformation; they are different equations on the same kinematics, which the letter states in a footnote and does not justify.

3. $SU(3)$ enters as a three-valued suffix, not as a larger algebra

The idea. Doubling the oscillator suffixes takes $0(3,2)$ to $0(4,2)$. Indexing each suffix by $\alpha=1,2,3$ turns the same construction into one carrying internal $SU(3)$, with the $0(4,2)$ generators unchanged and only the contraction over the new index modified. The internal symmetry is a degeneracy label on the tower.

What it would add. A route that costs no enlargement of the operator algebra — which is the corpus's own correction. Why the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches $SU(2)$ but Not $SU(3)$ (articles_physics/why-the-biquaternion-algebra-reaches-su-2-but-not-su-3.md) argues that $SU(3)$ needs a three-dimensional module, while $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb B)=M_4(\mathbb C)$ already contains $SU(4)$ as an operator algebra. Takabayasi supplies the missing module without leaving the tower: the three-valued index $\alpha$ on a Fock suffix is a three-dimensional multiplicity space, and the group acting on it is $SU(3)$ by construction. This is the cheapest of all the routes past the ceiling, and it is not one the corpus prices.

Where it lands. §What Would Be Needed to Pass the Ceiling, as the index route, and The Spin–Charge Hilbert Space and the $SU(3)$ Supermultiplets in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-spin-charge-hilbert-space-and-the-su3-supermultiplets-in-biquaternionic-form.md), where the colour-like index is already the carrier.

What stands in the way. The letter says only "is understood" about the modified contraction and gives no generator algebra for the internal $SU(3)$; whether the symmetry is local, and whether the mass formula respects it, is left open. Eight of the twelve suffixes are also unaccounted for in the printed text.

The remainder, one line each. That $\psi$ carries $U(2,2)\times L^{**}$ with $U(2,2)=U(1)\times 0(4,2)$ is the compact-plus-conformal structure in one line and belongs with the corpus's conformal notes. That the oscillator spectrum $m=\kappa(\nu+1)$ with $\nu=J+m$ has a pairwise degeneracy under $a\leftrightarrow b$ read as antiparticles is the cheapest antiparticle in the folder. That a bilocal oscillator gives $m^{2}=\mu^{2}+\mu'^{2}(J+2n)$ instead is a footnote worth keeping, because it is the same construction with a different carrier.

What the Paper Does

The first statement of the detailed wave equation. Instead of a string model plus separate additional conditions, the letter gives one wave equation defined at every material point $\sigma$; on Fourier expanding, that single equation becomes a global wave equation plus a countably infinite tower of subsidiary conditions, one per oscillator mode, and the whole tower closes under commutation. Unphysical states are thereby removed automatically. Adding a coupling at the string's end gives the vertex operator, and the propagator from the wave equation plus that vertex gives the dual amplitude — at intercept one, which the letter flags as a limitation.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A single equation that is an infinite tower

The idea. One local equation, holding at each $\sigma$, is equivalent to an infinite set of global conditions. There is no main equation plus constraints: the constraint set is the equation, read in modes. The hierarchy of modes is not a truncation scheme but an exact rewriting.

What it would add. A precise sense in which a local field equation over an extended object encodes infinitely many constraints. The Lagrangian and the Nonlinear Sigma Model (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-nonlinear-sigma-model.md) has one equation per field; here one equation per point collapses to one per mode, and the counting is the content. This is the cleanest expression of the idea in the folder and the ancestor of all its DWE papers.

Where it lands. §The Model and the Constraint, as the ancestor statement.

What stands in the way. The equivalence is asserted via Fourier expansion; convergence and the treatment of the divergent constant $Z=\sum 1/r$ are handled by absorbing $Z$ into $\omega_0'$, which is a remark rather than a finite prescription.

2. Indefinite metric licensed by subsidiary conditions

The idea. The indefinite metric is not a defect to be removed: it is permitted because there is one subsidiary condition per vector mode, each relating a relative-time mode to spatial ones and thereby eliminating the negative-norm states. The count of conditions matches the count of bad modes.

What it would add. A quantitative justification for the indefinite metric, which the folder elsewhere treats as a choice. Earlier conversions record the trade-off (indefinite metric versus infinite degeneracy); here the trade is shown to be balanced by an exact matching of conditions to modes, which is the argument for why it works.

Where it lands. §The Norm and the Invariant of the Group of Relativistic Internal Motion conversion, as the counting argument.

What stands in the way. No proof that the conditions remove all negative-norm states; the letter says they do so "effectively".

3. The coupling must sit at the end — and that is a constraint on the geometry

The idea. The external field couples at $\sigma=0$ only. Compatibility of the coupled tower forces $k^{2}=m_0^{2}$, i.e., the external particle must be on the leading trajectory. A local coupling anywhere else would break $[\Lambda_r,H']=rH'$.

What it would add. A derivation of a vertex's structure from an algebraic requirement. The corpus's vertex discussions take the vertex as given; here the position of the coupling and the mass-shell condition on the external leg both follow from requiring the algebra to survive the coupling.

Where it lands. The conversion of Detailed Wave Equation for Extended Model in this folder, where the end-coupling is used and then varied to change the intercept; the natural corpus home is §The Gauge Symmetry and the Constraints of The Lagrangian and the Non-Abelian Gauge Fields.

What stands in the way. The derivation is for the scalar external field; the vector and tensor couplings come later and change the intercept, so the "must" is conditional on the coupling type.

4. The first appearance of the intercept problem

The idea. Intercept one is forced, and the letter says so plainly as a defect to be repaired. Everything in the later DWE papers — the hybrid model, the Fermi oscillators — is an answer to this paragraph.

What it would add. A clear origin for a research programme, which helps place the folder's papers in order. Reading this letter first makes the hybrid paper's purpose obvious: it is not a new model but a repair of an admitted limitation.

Where it lands. The conversion of Detailed Wave Equation for Extended Model Underlying the Dual Amplitude in this folder, §Why the Intercept Had to Be One, as the origin.

What stands in the way. The repair changes the model's particle content, so the "limitation" is removed by enlargement rather than by fixing the original.

The remainder, one line each. That the closed algebra $[\Lambda_r,\Lambda_s]=(r-s)\Lambda_{r+s}$ is the Virasoro algebra in embryo is the letter's most consequential formula. That the letter is two pages and dated 1970 places it before the fuller treatments and shows how compressed the original insight was.

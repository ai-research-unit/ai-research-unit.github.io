What the Paper Does

Sets up the general theory of a relativistic particle with internal motion and derives, rather than postulates, the group structure. The kinematics is a finite set of internal variables with a definite algebra; the algebra determines the group of internal motion $G_{\text{in}}$, a real symplectic group $Sp(f\mathbb R)$ for canonical coordinates and momenta, which automatically contains the internal Lorentz group. The wave equation then selects a subrepresentation of the physical Poincaré group and fixes the rest symmetry. Part II works through bilocal, spinor, Majorana and Dirac-coordinate models plus four new equations. This is the unifying paper behind the rest of the folder.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The group is a consequence of the algebra, not a postulate

The idea. Start from internal variables and their commutators; the transformations preserving the commutators form a group, and it contains the internal Lorentz group as soon as there are enough degrees of freedom, since $Sp(1,\mathbb R)=SO(2,1)$ and $Sp(2,\mathbb R)=SO(3,2)$. Symmetry is read off the kinematics rather than chosen and then represented.

What it would add. The mirror image of the corpus's method. The Lorentz Group in Biquaternionic Form: Structure and Representations (articles_physics/the-lorentz-group-in-biquaternionic-form-structure-and-representations.md) starts from $\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H$ and derives the representations; Takabayasi starts from oscillator variables and derives which group they carry. The two derivations meet at $SO(3,2)$ and $SO(2,1)$, and the meeting point is checkable.

Where it lands. §The Lie Algebra, as the kinematics-first construction, and §Where the Biquaternion Algebra Sits.

What stands in the way. The derivation fixes the group only up to $f$ and the algebra type; $Sp(f,\mathbb R)$ is a family, and which member is physical is not decided by the kinematics.

2. A compact rest symmetry is required, or every level is infinitely degenerate

The idea. The wave equation's symmetry must reduce at rest to a compact $G_0$ inside $U(f)$. If the non-compact internal Lorentz group survives as a symmetry, each mass level is infinitely degenerate. The mechanism preventing this is a spin-orbit coupling — in the equation or the subsidiary condition — and coupling to $P_\mu$ is what breaks the internal group to its compact part.

What it would add. A selection principle on wave equations, with a stated failure mode. Higher Spin from Tensor Products: The Three Carriers and the Absence of a Fourth (articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md) asks in §The Ceiling in Context what bounds a tower; here the bound is dynamical, not algebraic, and it is testable on each tower the corpus builds.

Where it lands. §The Ceiling in Context, as the dynamical criterion.

What stands in the way. Infinite degeneracy is "to be avoided" rather than impossible, and Takabayasi keeps pseudo-unitary cases for later reconsideration.

3. Reducibility is the price of a mass spectrum, and it brings space-like solutions

The idea. A conventional covariant equation is nearly irreducible; an equation with internal motion is reducible under the Poincaré group, and that reducibility is what produces a mass-spin spectrum. The price is that the decomposition may contain space-like, light-like and $P_\mu=0$ pieces.

What it would add. A precise statement of a trade the corpus makes silently. The Massless Regime (articles_physics/the-massless-regime.md) treats the massless sector as a limit of the massive one; here it is another component of the same reducible representation, and acceptability depends on its absence.

Where it lands. §The Massless Case, as the reducible-representation caveat.

What stands in the way. Absence of space-like solutions is shown only for specific equations in Part II, so the general theory cannot guarantee it.

4. Positive-semidefinite but not Lorentz-invariant

The idea. The compact invariant $N=\sum a_r^{\dagger}a_r$ is positive-semidefinite and usable as a norm, but "cannot be a Lorentz scalar". Positivity and covariance are carried by different objects, and no single quantity has both.

What it would add. A structural reason for the corpus's repeated observation that positivity and covariance pull apart. The $N$-Norm, Mass Angle, and the Cone (articles_physics/the-n-norm-mass-angle-and-the-cone.md) builds a norm and relates it to the mass shell; Takabayasi's remark says why the two cannot coincide.

Where it lands. §The Norm and the Invariant, as the structural remark.

What stands in the way. It is a remark in passing, not a theorem; the paper then uses indefinite norms without resolving the conflict.

The remainder, one line each. That Part II's four new equations — the second-order bilocal equation with exact four-dimensional oscillator spectrum, the hybrid spinor equation with Regge trajectories and no space-like solutions, the subsidiary condition reproducing rigid-rotator levels, and the Lorentz-tetrad rotator — are the folder's most useful catalogue. That the derived $G_{\text{in}}$ being $Sp(f,\mathbb R)$ rather than a rotation group is what allows the towers to carry spin without a spinor index.

What the Paper Does

Proposes that the newly found narrow bosons $\psi(3105)$ and $\psi(3695)$ are not charmonium but closed rings: two ordinary mesons laid side by side with their ends joined by strings, giving a square loop of four string segments binding alternating quarks and antiquarks. This explains the masses additively — $m_\psi\simeq4m_\omega$, $m_{\psi'}\simeq2(m_\omega+m_\varphi)$, $m_{\psi''}\simeq4m_\varphi$ — against measured $3135$, $3607$, $4058$ MeV, needs no heavy quark, and accounts for the narrow widths by a conservation law for the number of closed rings.

Quantum numbers

For definiteness we assume that the spins of quarks and antiquarks are all parallel, with the resultant spin $S=2$ ('ferromagnetic state') and that they align in the direction normal to the configuration plane. Further we assume that the whole structure rotates rigidly. Taking the case (a) (or (c)) the (total) orbital angular momentum of this rotation must have an odd quantum number $L=1,3,\dots$, since the two $p$-quarks (or two $\bar p$) are in the same spin state. Then parity and charge parity of the system are $P=-1$ and $C=G=-1$. Further we assume an LS-coupling that favors an antiparallel $L$ and $S$ configuration. Then, if we take the lowest value $L=1$ for the ground state, the spin $J$ of the system is $J=1$. Thus we have three $J^{PC}=1^{--}$ isosinglets. We identify the states (a), (b) and (c) with $\psi$, $\psi'$ and $\psi''$, respectively.

Stability from counting rings

For strong interactions we assume an approximate conservation law of number $N$ of closed rings, besides the ordinary selection rules of strong interactions. Then the strong decays of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ into ordinary hadrons ($N=1\to N=0$) are suppressed, while the decay $\psi'\to\psi\pi\pi$ is not suppressed because it conserves $N$ (though its width is small by phase space). Other typical strong decay modes are $\psi\to\omega\pi\pi$ and $\psi'\to KK\pi\pi$, which are suppressed by the $N$ selection rule.

The alternatives

"As in (i) we continue to take the ferromagnetic state ($S=2$), but now we adopt the configuration (a) alone and consider its $L=1$ and $L=3$ states of rotation ... both states certainly have $J=1$, and we identify them with $\psi$ and $\psi'$ respectively", with a selection rule allowing the quark diagram responsible for $\psi'\to\psi\pi\pi$ and forbidding the one for decay into ordinary mesons.

Instead of the ferromagnetic state we now take the 'antiferromagnetic state' in which the directions of quark and antiquark spins alternate such that $S=0$. Each of the four strings again ends in an isosinglet quark-antiquark pair, $\eta$ or $\eta'$; with $L=1$ rotation the quantum numbers $J^{PC}$ are still $1^{--}$, and there are again three varieties. In this case the same rule as in (i) gives their masses somewhat smaller values than in (i).

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Mass is string length, and it adds

The idea. Quarks contribute no mass of their own; the mass of a hadron is the sum of the masses of its constituent strings, and a composite of two mesons sharing a closed string has the sum of the two meson masses. Then $4m_\omega$, $2(m_\omega+m_\varphi)$, $4m_\varphi$ follow, and the three states are predicted in the right order and roughly the right places with no new quantum.

What it would add. A mass rule additive in segments. Hadron Structure from QCD: The Bound-State Problem (articles_physics/hadron-structure-from-qcd-the-bound-state-problem.md) records in §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits that most of the mass sits in the field, not the quarks; this is a testable version of that statement.

Where it lands. §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits, as the additive-string alternative.

What stands in the way. The relations are within $1-3\%$, but the $\psi'$ one uses the mean of $m_\omega$ and $m_\varphi$ "for simplicity"; and charmonium fits the same three states with one new quark mass.

2. A conservation law for the number of closed rings

The idea. Strong interactions approximately conserve the number of closed string rings. A ring cannot decay into open strings, so $\psi\to$ ordinary hadrons is forbidden while $\psi'\to\psi\pi\pi$ is allowed; and $\psi\to B\bar B$ needs all four strings broken, which is why it is so narrow.

What it would add. A selection rule that is a count rather than a symmetry. Confinement and the Loss of Partonic Information in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/confinement-and-the-loss-of-partonic-information-in-biquaternionic-form.md) discusses confinement in §Confinement and the Order Parameter; here the order parameter would be the ring number, a candidate that article does not consider.

Where it lands. §Confinement and the Order Parameter, as the ring-number candidate.

What stands in the way. The rule is "approximate" and is introduced to explain one narrow width; no dynamics forces it, and QCD gives the same narrowness from the Okubo–Zweig–Iizuka rule.

3. Same

$J^{PC}$ from $L=1$ and from $L=3$

The idea. With $S=2$ and antiparallel $LS$ coupling, both $L=1$ and $L=3$ give $J=1$. So $\psi$ and $\psi'$ can share every quantum number and differ only in rotational excitation — the same situation the isosinglet series met with $\omega$ and $\varphi$, now derived from the geometry rather than postulated.

What it would add. A mechanism for the two states, identical labels problem. The Isosinglet Meson Series conversion in this folder posits an ordering number with no dynamics; here $L$ supplies it.

Where it lands. The corpus's mass-spectrum articles have no section for this; the natural home is The Spectrum of a Lorentz Rotor: What Two Eigenvalues Leave Undetermined (articles_physics/the-spectrum-of-a-lorentz-rotor-what-two-eigenvalues-leave-undetermined.md), where the question of when the labels underdetermine the state is posed.

What stands in the way. With $S=2$ the lowest state has $J=1$ only through a chosen $LS$ coupling, and the $L=3$ spacing is not computed.

The remainder, one line each. That the model attributes the $\psi$'s heaviness to four strings and charmonium to one heavy quark is a clean experimental fork, decided for charmonium.

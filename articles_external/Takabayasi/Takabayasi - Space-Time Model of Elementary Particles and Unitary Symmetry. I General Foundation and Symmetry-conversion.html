What the Paper Does

Proposes that a particle is an association of four space-time points. Separating the centre of mass leaves three relative vectors — "figure axes"— and the equivalence of any choice among them is an $O(3)$ symmetry acting on figure space, independent of the Lorentz group. A confining oscillator-type invariant potential holds the tetrad together within a characteristic length, and the oscillator quanta of the three normal axes turn out to carry a $U(3)$ symmetry containing that $O(3)$ and the internal reciprocity as special cases. Isospin and hypercharge come from excitations of the figure axes; ordinary spin comes from the rotational part of the relative motion with respect to the inertial frame. A triality condition on the total oscillator quantum number is imposed, and with it the $(mass)^{2}$ of the light isoscalar mesons comes out in the ratio $1:2:3:5$.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

  1. Internal symmetry as a symmetry of which triad you call the triad

The idea. The $O(3)$ here is not a rotation group acting on fields. It is the statement that the three relative vectors of a four-point object may be relabelled by any orthogonal map, and the physics cannot tell. $U(3)$ then arises because the oscillator variables admit a larger relabelling than the coordinates do.

What it would add. An origin for an internal symmetry that is neither imposed nor observed but counted: the number of equivalent ways to choose the figure axes. The corpus's accounts of $SU(3)$ and triality take the group as given and ask about its representations; here the group's size is fixed by how many redundant descriptions the object admits, which is a constructive explanation of a different kind.

Where it lands. §Triality, as the counting origin of the symmetry.

What stands in the way. The argument gives $U(3)$ and not $SU(3)$ plus a separate triality; the author has to impose the unimodular restriction by hand, so the "counting" story stops one step short.

  1. Two kinds of angular momentum from one object, with different origins

The idea. Ordinary spin is the rotational part of the relative motion with respect to the inertial frame, generated by $L^{\mu\nu}$. Unitary spin is the excitation of the figure axes with respect to figure space, generated by $A_r{}^s$. They are different objects built from the same coordinates, commuting with each other.

What it would add. A clean dissociation the corpus reaches only through the polar decomposition. Spin Hydrodynamics in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/spin-hydrodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md) separates spin from orbital angular momentum within one frame; here the split is between a Lorentz-frame rotation and a figure-space rotation, and the two are independent because $[L^{r},D^{\mu\nu}]=0$. That is a stronger statement than commuting observables: it is a statement that the two rotations act on disjoint arguments.

Where it lands. §The Decomposition of Spin Hydrodynamics in Biquaternionic Form, as the two-frame split.

What stands in the way. The construction needs four points, so the split is not available for a two-point (bilocal) model, and the paper's own meson spectrum comes out wrong.

3. Triality as a selection rule with a mass prediction

The idea. Requiring the $120^{\circ}$ rotation in each $(\xi^{r},\pi^{r})$ plane to be a symmetry restricts $N$ to multiples of three, and the $(mass)^{2}$ formula then gives $1:2:3:5$ for the four light isoscalars. Where it lands. §Triality, as the empirical reading.

4. One point becoming inequivalent is the symmetry breaking

The idea. $U(3)$ holds while all four points are equivalent. Where it lands. §The Model and the Constraint of The Lagrangian and the Nonlinear Sigma Model (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-nonlinear-sigma-model.md), as the geometric breaking.

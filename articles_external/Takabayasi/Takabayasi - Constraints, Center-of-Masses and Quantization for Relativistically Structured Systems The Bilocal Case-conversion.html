What the Paper Does

Puts the bilocal theory on a Hamiltonian footing and finds that its algebra is more specific than the Poincaré algebra, being governed by the existence of a geometrical centre of mass and by the constraints of the covariant formalism. Boosting to the centre-of-mass rest frame isolates the constraints and produces a canonical but non-covariant centre of mass. That gives a second quantisation, non-covariant but needing no subsidiary condition, and the paper proves it is related to the original covariant quantisation by an explicit unitary transformation $\mathcal U=\exp[i\tanh^{-1}(|P|)P_kS_{k0}]$. The two schemes differ only in what they do with the relative-time mode: one suppresses it to its zero-point oscillation, the other removes it entirely.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The algebra of a composite is smaller than the Poincaré algebra

The idea. A bilocal system's Hamiltonian algebra is "much more specific than the general Poincaré algebra": the existence of a geometrical centre of mass and the constraints of the covariant formalism cut the algebra down. Compositeness is not just extra coordinates added to a particle; it reduces the algebra of allowed transformations.

What it would add. A precise sense in which a composite is more constrained than an elementary system. Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave (articles_physics/relativistic-mechanics-from-the-dirac-wave.md) extracts mechanics from a one-particle wave; here the many-particle kinematics has less freedom, which inverts the intuition that more constituents means more structure.

Where it lands. §Order Zero: the Mass Shell, as the composite-algebra remark.

What stands in the way. The reduction is exhibited for the bilocal case with a harmonic force; whether it holds in general is stated as a hope ("the method may be generalized").

2. Two quantisations of one theory, related by an explicit unitary operator

The idea. Covariant quantisation with a subsidiary condition, and non-covariant quantisation with the relative time removed, are the same quantum theory seen in two frames, connected by $\mathcal U=\exp[i\tanh^{-1}(|P|)P_kS_{k0}]$. Nothing is lost or added: one keeps the timelike mode at its zero-point value, the other deletes it.

What it would add. A resolution of the subsidiary-condition problem, which the folder otherwise treats as an unresolved trade-off. Earlier conversions in this folder record the choice between indefinite metric and degeneracy; here the choice is shown to be a unitary transformation, which is the cleanest outcome available and the one the corpus should cite.

Where it lands. §The Norm and the Invariant of the conversion of Group of Relativistic Internal Motion in this folder, as the resolution.

What stands in the way. The equivalence holds for timelike $P_\mu$ and for models where the transformation is well defined; the spacelike sector is explicitly not covered.

3. A centre of mass that is canonical but not covariant

The idea. Boosting the internal variables to their rest frame produces a centre of mass that is canonical — its brackets are the standard ones, with no ordering ambiguities on quantisation — at the price of not being covariant. Covariance is recovered only through the unitary map.

What it would add. A worked example of the trade between canonicity and covariance. The corpus's discussions of position operators assume one wants both; here one is chosen deliberately and the other recovered afterwards, and the author notes that "no ordering ambiguity occurs" precisely because the variables are canonical.

Where it lands. §The Spin-Particle Bundle of Parras' Four Options of the Dirac Equation and the Discrete Symmetries (articles_physics/parras-four-options-of-the-dirac-equation-and-the-discrete-symmetries.md), as the canonical-but-not-covariant case.

What stands in the way. The construction uses a specific boost and a specific model; the general Pryce programme is referenced but not compared term by term.

4. A comparison with Pryce that the corpus lacks

The idea. Pryce defines the relativistic centre of mass from the Poincaré generators; this paper defines it geometrically from the constituent world lines. The paper claims its definition is more explicit and more physically relevant for composites.

What it would add. A second definition to set beside the generator-based one — a genuine comparison of two constructions of the same object. The corpus's position-operator material assumes the generator route; this gives the alternative and the argument for it.

Where it lands. §The Spin-Particle Bundle, as the second definition.

What stands in the way. The comparison is programmatic; the paper states the advantage but does not compute a case where the two definitions disagree numerically.

The remainder, one line each. That unequal constituent masses enter by a single parameter $r$ inserted into the wave equation, keeping it second order, is the paper's economical generalisation. That the treatment of spin includes the $+i(\xi'\pi')$ term in $J^{2}$ shows the ordering care the canonical route buys.

What the Paper Does

Takes the bilocal model — a real 4-vector relative coordinate $x_{\mu}$ — whose natural internal group is the sixteen-parameter $GL(4,\mathbb R)$ of linear deformation-rotation, and chooses as the physical internal Lorentz group a non-standard $0(3,1)$ inside $GL(4,\mathbb R)\simeq 0(3,3)$ rather than the standard rotation-minus-boost one. Because the chosen generators mix rotation with spatial deformation, the internal spin spectrum acquires half-integer values, and the wave equation becomes first order. This is the paper's striking claim: half-integer spin from a bosonic coordinate, with no spinor index and no Dirac matrices.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Half-integer spin without a spinor: the choice of the internal Lorentz subgroup

The idea. A real 4-vector internal coordinate, quantised, carries only integer spin under the standard $0(3,1)$ generated by $x_{[\mu}p_{\nu]}$. Choose instead the $0(3,1)$ inside $GL(4,\mathbb R)$ whose rotations also deform, and the same bosonic coordinate carries half-integer spin, with a first-order equation and a single-component wave function.

What it would add. A counterexample to a reading the corpus is entitled to make. The Lorentz Group in Biquaternionic Form: Structure and Representations (articles_physics/the-lorentz-group-in-biquaternionic-form-structure-and-representations.md) derives in §The Two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Halves that half-integer spin needs the spinor module and that the integer-spin representations come from the vector and tensor carriers. Takabayasi's construction shows that the carrier does not decide the spin spectrum: the same carrier gives integer spin under one $0(3,1)$ and half-integer under another, the difference being which subalgebra of $0(3,3)$ is called the Lorentz group. Spin is a property of the pair (carrier, chosen $0(3,1)$), not of the carrier.

Where it lands. §The Two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Halves, as the qualification, and §Where the Biquaternion Algebra Sits, where the embedding is discussed.

What stands in the way. The new $0(3,1)$ is not the Lorentz group acting on spacetime indices: the internal coordinates then transform unconventionally, mixing space and time under rotations, and the identification is justified only by the internal coordinates' unobservability. The corpus's carriers are modules for a fixed Lorentz action, so the comparison is between a fixed action and a chosen one, and that must be said.

2. $SL(4,\mathbb R)=0(3,3)$: the deformation group is a pseudo-orthogonal group

The idea. The sixteen-parameter group of linear deformation-rotation of a real 4-vector is $GL(4,\mathbb R)$; its unimodular part is $0(3,3)$, whose maximal compact subgroup is $0(4)=SU(2)\times SU(2)$ and whose fifteen generators split into two triplets of compact rotations and nine non-compact deformation generators.

What it would add. A real form with a physical name. Higher Spin from Tensor Products: The Three Carriers and the Absence of a Fourth (articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md) counts carriers and finds three; the $0(3,3)$ here has the same compact part as the conformal group's $0(4,2)$ but a different signature, and it arises from a real $4\times4$ matrix group rather than from a complex one. That gives the corpus a fourth real form to place — not a fourth carrier, a fourth algebra.

Where it lands. §The Ceiling in Context, as the neighbouring real form.

What stands in the way. $0(3,3)$ appears here as an internal symmetry; the corpus's algebras act on modules over $\mathbb B$, and a real $4\times4$ group on $\mathbb R^{4}$ is not of that type.

3. Pseudo-unitarity is enough for a first-order equation with a minimal coupling

The idea. The tower's norm is indefinite — the representation is "pseudo-unitary"— and yet the first-order equation admits the electromagnetic field by the single substitution $P_{\mu}\to P_{\mu}-eA_{\mu}$, because the current $j_{\mu}$ is explicit and conserved. The cost of indefiniteness is paid at the level of interpretation, not at the level of coupling.

What it would add. A concrete case for a trade the corpus discusses abstractly. The Gauge Carrier of the Material Sector: Why the Action Is One-Sided (articles_physics/the-gauge-carrier-of-the-material-sector-why-the-action-is-one-sided.md) argues that minimal coupling selects a one-sided action; here minimal coupling works on a tower with an indefinite norm, so one-sidedness is not what protects positivity.

Where it lands. That article's section on minimal coupling, as the indefinite-norm control case.

What stands in the way. He calls the equation "not immediately a physically reasonable one" on account of space-like solutions, so the coupling's success is formal.

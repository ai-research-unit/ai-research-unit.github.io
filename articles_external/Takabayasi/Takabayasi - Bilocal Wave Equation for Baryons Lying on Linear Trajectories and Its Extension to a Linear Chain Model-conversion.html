The spectrum and the intercept bound

For a fixed $K$, $J$ runs over $J=K+\tfrac12$, $K-\tfrac12,\dots,\tfrac12$, so that the maximum spin is given by $J_{\max}=K+\tfrac12$. Thus we have the highest trajectory, which is non-degenerate, with the trajectory function

$$ \alpha(s)=\frac{s-\mu_0^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\frac12 . $$

Parallel to this run the daughter trajectories with $\Delta J=-1,-2,\dots$. (2·35) exhibits the important consequence of our theory that the intercept $\alpha(0)$ of the leading trajectory is given by

$$ \alpha(0)=\frac12-\frac{\mu_0^{2}}{\mu^{2}}<\frac12, $$

in nice agreement with observations. For detailed comparison with empirical baryonic levels we need to take into account the unitary spin properties, which may be associated to our three $\Gamma$-matrices.

In disregarding the isospin multiplicity, it is natural to identify the ground state ($\tfrac12^{+}$) as the nucleon.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. First order in every momentum is a constraint strong enough to fix the particle content

The idea. Demanding that the equation be first order in each constituent momentum is not a convenience. It is a filter: only a definite number of Dirac matrices can be introduced, the number is set by the number of points plus the extension, and so the spin content is fixed by the order of the equation rather than chosen.

What it would add. A derivation of the carrier count from an ordering requirement. Higher Spin from Tensor Products: The Three Carriers and the Absence of a Fourth (articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md) counts what each carrier holds; here the count is forced by the demand that the equation be first order in each momentum, which is a different and stronger kind of argument — a physical principle about time evolution that ends up fixing an algebraic multiplicity.

Where it lands. §The Ceiling in Context, as the first-order requirement.

What stands in the way. Takabayasi's own justification is the "principle of Schrödinger", which is a hypothesis about how composite systems should be described, not a theorem; a second-order equation for baryons is not thereby excluded, only disfavoured.

2. The intercept bound from the existence of a conserved current

The idea. $\alpha(0)=\tfrac12-\mu_0^{2}/\mu^{2}<\tfrac12$ is not fitted; it follows from requiring that the model possess a conserved current and no tachyons. The observed baryon intercepts lie below $\tfrac12$, and the model says they must.

What it would add. An inequality rather than a value — a rarer and more robust kind of prediction. The Spectrum of a Lorentz Rotor: What Two Eigenvalues Leave Undetermined (articles_physics/the-spectrum-of-a-lorentz-rotor-what-two-eigenvalues-leave-undetermined.md) asks what a spectrum leaves free; here the sign of one parameter is fixed while its magnitude is not, which is exactly the kind of partial determination that article's question is about.

Where it lands. §What the Spectrum Fixes, as the inequality case.

What stands in the way. The bound is strict only if $\mu_0^{2}>0$, which is an assumption about the internal ground state; and in nice agreement with observations rests on intercepts extracted with Regge fits that carry their own systematics.

3. Discrete chain to continuous string, and the boundary conditions it clarifies

The idea. $N$ points on a line with nearest-neighbour coupling, solved by normal modes, tends as $N\to\infty$ to a string. The discrete case has unambiguous boundary conditions; taking the limit tells you which conditions the continuous model must have, which is otherwise a matter of choice.

What it would add. A resolution of a technical ambiguity the continuous string leaves open. The corpus's string-related articles treat the continuum directly; the discrete-to-continuum route is a method the corpus lacks, and it explains why the string's boundary conditions are what they are.

Where it lands. The corpus's treatment of the string as a limit; the natural home is the conversion of Theory of Relativistic String and Super-Wave Equation I in this folder.

What stands in the way. The limit is asserted to clarify the boundary conditions; the paper's $N\to\infty$ analysis is at the level of the normal-mode expansion, and the "double Dirac string" it produces is a specific model, not the Nambu string.

4. Two subsidiary conditions with different jobs

The idea. The same subsidiary condition appears in two forms, $P^{\mu}a_\mu\phi=0$ and $P^{\mu}a^{\dagger}_\mu\phi=0$. The first is used with an indefinite metric and removes negative-norm states; the second is used without one and removes infinite degeneracy. Which problem you have decides which form you need.

What it would add. A case where the choice of inner product determines the constraint, rather than the reverse. The whole folder worries about indefinite metric; here is a place where the author shows the two options side by side and states the price of each, which is the clearest statement of the trade in the set.

Where it lands. §The Norm and the Invariant of the conversion of Group of Relativistic Internal Motion in this folder, as the concrete fork.

What stands in the way. The two forms are not shown to give the same physics beyond the spectrum; the equivalence of the two routes is assumed rather than demonstrated.

The remainder, one line each. That the meson model needs two $\Gamma$-matrices and the baryon three is the cleanest statement in the folder of why baryons are not just mesons with an extra point. That the chain's normal-mode analysis is the technical precursor of the string papers that follow.

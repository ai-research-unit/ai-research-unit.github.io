What the Paper Does

A history of nonlocal field theory in the Yukawa school, written by a participant, from Yukawa's 1950 paper to about 1960. It traces three pre-war Japanese directions, the maru idea, Tomonaga's rival super-many-time reformulation, the postwar calculus of bilocal and nonlocal fields, and the parallel rise of the Sakata and Nagoya composite models. It closes with Takabayasi's own route to nonlocality from below — the hydrodynamical formulation, the relativistic rotator — and compares the two men's philosophies: Yukawa's search for a final theory against Sakata's endless strata.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why the subject is worth a history

"A lot of research has been done in the theory of elementary particles aiming at the solution of the divergence difficulty in the quantum theory of fields together with the explanation of the existence of the various kinds of elementary particles and interactions. The theory of nonlocal fields was an attempt to remove the divergences and to achieve a unified description of elementary particles by generalizing the conventional field concept in such a way that it assigns intrinsic extensions to elementary particles. Since this kind of theory is speculative and is difficult to establish in a consistent theoretical framework, it was not considered an orthodox theory. Nevertheless, the general direction of assuming an internal extension of elementary particles and of exploring the inside and background of them continually gave rise to ideas that influenced the development of the more conventional theory of elementary particles. Also a remarkable feature of this line of research is that it was pushed forward mainly by Yukawa and the Japanese school."

Maru

"Yukawa considered that we could not talk about the field at each geometrical point of space-time and therefore we should construct the (future) theory based on the notion of maru (meaning circle), which is a closed hypersurface in four-dimensional Minkowski space-time. I think that Yukawa was unwittingly influenced by the Japanese philosopher Kitaro Nishida, who drew circles as a zen philosophical image. In any event, while maru is helpful in making the quantum field theory manifestly relativistic invariant, it brings about at the same time a chaotic situation where cause and effect are inseparable. Yukawa welcomed this irrationality as a clue to the solution of the divergence difficulties, in suggesting the concept of a priori probability on maru."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

  1. The nonlocal field is a matrix in position, not a function with a form factor

The idea. Two distinct ways to smear a point: modify the interaction with a form factor (Wataghin), or replace the field itself by a two-point object $\langle x'|U|x''\rangle$ (Yukawa). The second changes what a field is; the first only changes how it couples. The review keeps the distinction and says the second leads to internal motion, the first does not.

What it would add. A clean dichotomy for a corpus that uses "nonlocal" loosely. A Non-local Wave Equation and its Connection with the Dirac Particle in this folder is nonlocal in the field's argument, which is Yukawa's kind; the form-factor kind never appears in the folder at all, and its absence is worth noting because it is the version that survived into modern effective field theory.

Where it lands. The conversion of A Non-local Wave Equation and its Connection with the Dirac Particle in this folder, as the dichotomy.

What stands in the way. The review reports the distinction historically and does not compare the two schemes' calculational power.

  1. One must decide which difficulties a change of model can fix

The idea. Yukawa's objection to Sakata's mixed-field method: it gives no criterion separating the difficulties a model change can remove from those requiring a change of law. That is a methodological demand — a theory of which problems are model problems.

What it would add. A criterion the corpus never states. Most of the folder's papers change the model and check the result; Yukawa's complaint is that this procedure cannot tell you in advance whether you are attacking the right layer, and the review reports it as a serious objection rather than a preference.

Where it lands. §The Model and the Constraint of The Lagrangian and the Nonlinear Sigma Model (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-nonlinear-sigma-model.md), as the methodological demand.

What stands in the way. Yukawa's own programme also failed to provide the criterion, so the objection is not accompanied by a working alternative.

3. Composite models need two strong interactions, and this is usually hidden

The idea. A composite hadron requires both the Yukawa-type force between hadrons and a separate "constructive force" binding the constituents. Where it lands. §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits, as the two-force gap.

4. Nonlocality built upward from a classical model, not downward from a principle

The idea. Yukawa started from Markov's measurability argument and worked towards models. Where it lands. The conversion of Group of Relativistic Internal Motion in this folder, §The Norm and the Invariant, as the bottom-up route.

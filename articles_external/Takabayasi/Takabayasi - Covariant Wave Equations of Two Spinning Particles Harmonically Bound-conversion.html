What the Paper Does

Adds constituent spin to the spinless bilocal model by introducing two sets of $\gamma$-matrices, one per constituent. The solutions are obtained explicitly and split into four families according to the quark spin configuration; all are tachyon-free and the mass–spin spectrum agrees with the observed mesons. In its original form one basic equation is second order, so the model is slightly more general than a bound pair of Dirac particles; the paper then adds a subsidiary condition — equivalently, replaces the basic set by two first-order equations — so that switching the binding off adiabatically gives exactly two free Dirac particles.

What is added

"A consistent set of covariant wave equation and subsidiary condition represents quantum theory of relativistic composite system of two spinning particles which are harmonically bound by direct internal force, meaning a relativistic quark model of mesonic states where quarks are permanently confined. Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Covariant quantisation restricts the force to harmonic

The idea. The binding potential is not chosen for convenience. Requiring covariant quantisation forces $U=\kappa^{2}\xi^{2}+U_0$. Harmonic binding is the only potential for which the constraint algebra closes covariantly, and confinement plus a linear leading trajectory follow from it.

What it would add. A derivation of a model's central assumption. Hadron Structure from QCD: The Bound-State Problem (articles_physics/hadron-structure-from-qcd-the-bound-state-problem.md) takes the confining potential as an input in §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits; here it is an output of the quantisation requirement, which is a stronger status and a claim worth checking against the QCD route.

Where it lands. §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits, as the derivation-from-covariance case.

What stands in the way. The derivation is internal to this formalism; nothing shows that a different quantisation scheme would not admit another potential.

2. Four families, one per spin configuration — and the counting is exhaustive

The idea. The spectrum's four families correspond to the four ways two constituent spins and the orbital excitation can combine into the allowed states, and "the above construction exhausts all solutions." The spectrum is not a fit to a list; it is a complete enumeration, and the identifications with $\rho$, $A_2$, $\epsilon(1300)$, $B(1231)$ follow from it.

What it would add. An exhaustiveness claim rather than a match. Higher Spin from Tensor Products: The Three Carriers and the Absence of a Fourth (articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md) also counts carriers exhaustively; here the count is of state families, and the resulting prediction — that the $\mathrm{h}_1$ trajectory is the highest — is the kind of claim that follows only from completeness.

Where it lands. §The Ceiling in Context, as the exhaustive-enumeration case.

What stands in the way. The enumeration is complete for this model with these operators; the identification of individual states with observed mesons is a separate step and carries assignment ambiguity.

3. An adiabatic limit used as a consistency filter

The idea. Turn the binding off slowly and the model must become two free Dirac particles, exactly. That requirement is not automatic here: the second-order equation gives a slightly larger system, and satisfying the limit costs one extra subsidiary condition. A limit that must be exact is a strong filter on the formalism.

What it would add. A criterion the corpus does not use — not agreement with data but reduction to a known theory in a controlled limit. The distinction matters: a model can fit a spectrum while failing its own weak-coupling limit, and this paper treats the latter as the harder test.

Where it lands. The corpus's discussion of limits and correspondence; natural home is §The Non-Quantum Limit material of The Non-Quantum Limit of the Action Principle and the Lagrangian (articles_physics/the-non-quantum-limit-of-the-action-principle-and-the-lagrangian.md).

What stands in the way. The adiabatic limit is a mathematical device; whether a physical system can be taken through it is not addressed.

4. Spacelike solutions exist and are not removed here

The idea. "Also there are spacelike solutions." They are noted, not eliminated. The model's spectrum claims are about the timelike sector.

What it would add. An honest boundary on what the spectrum result covers, in contrast to the folder's other treatments where spacelike solutions are excluded by a subsidiary condition or removed by construction.

Where it lands. The conversion of Constraints, Center-of-Masses and Quantization in this folder, whose non-covariant scheme deletes the offending mode; here it remains, which makes the two papers' claims non-overlapping in an instructive way.

What stands in the way. The paper does not say what the spacelike sector means physically or whether it is a defect.

The remainder, one line each. That two $\gamma$-matrix sets suffice for mesons while the baryon paper needed three continues the folder's counting pattern. That the invited-talk provenance shows these results were being presented as a coherent programme rather than as isolated papers.

What the Paper Does

Re-expresses the internal-polarisation theory of the preceding note as an infinite-component local field, then argues that field theory can be built from it by ordinary means because there is no subsidiary condition to enforce, and finally shows that a single "contraction" of the model — a degenerate limit of the internal spinor — reproduces Majorana's theory exactly. The point of the last part is that Majorana's spectrum, its missing antiparticles and its exclusion of the bilocal relative time are all consequences of that limit and not of the framework.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. No subsidiary condition, therefore the field can be second-quantised conventionally

The idea. Higher-spin theories carry a constraint that removes unwanted components and obstructs interaction. Here the constraint is replaced by a unitary structure on a two-oscillator space, and the equation itself has no subsidiary condition, so the passage to a quantum field is the ordinary one. The absence of internal time — $a_0>0$ by construction, so no relative-time degree exists — is what makes this possible.

What it would add. An argument the corpus asserts and does not construct. The Lagrangian and the Second Quantisation (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-second-quantisation.md) promotes a classical element equation by a Peierls bracket and finds in §The Constraints the Action Imposes: The Dirac Bracket that constraints must be handled before promotion. Takabayasi's tower has constraints replaced by a positive norm and a light-like internal vector, so the promotion is unencumbered; that is a worked instance of the difficult case, and it is a reason why the framework's indefinite alternatives are harder.

Where it lands. §The Constraints the Action Imposes, as the case with no second-class constraints, and §What the Principle Adds and What It Relabels in the same article.

What stands in the way. The letter defers quantisation itself to later work and says only that "this is carried out more conveniently in its representation in terms of the infinite-com- An excited rest-state internal eigenfunc- ponent local field**) (3)."; the claim is an argument, and the renormalisability of the tower is untouched.

2. Majorana's theory is a degenerate limit, and its failures come from the limit

The idea. Setting $a_1=0$ and halving the algebra contracts $0(4,2)$ away, leaving $0(3,2)$, and reduces the internal spinor to a real two-component $\varphi_a$. In that limit the spectrum becomes $m=\kappa/(J+\tfrac12)$, antiparticles disappear, and only the principal and supplementary series survive.

What it would add. A structural explanation of a corpus object. The Majorana Representation in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-majorana-representation-in-biquaternionic-form.md) uses the Majorana polynomial and the stereographic coordinate as a description of a spin-$\tfrac12$ star; what is missing there is why the Majorana equation of motion has the properties it has — descending spectrum, no antiparticles. Both are properties of the degenerate limit, not of the representation, and saying so prevents the corpus from attributing them to the representation.

Where it lands. That article's §What the Algebra Adds and What It Does Not, as the kinematic/dynamical separation, and The Majorana Equation in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-majorana-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.md) §The Conjugation and the Count, where antiparticles are counted.

What stands in the way. The contraction is a limit of a specific model's parameters, so the claim is about this tower and not about Majorana's equation in general; the letter says the same method can be applied to our original theory, which is an assertion of method, not a theorem.

3. A single

$U_a(2)\times U_b(2)$ shell already contains infinitely many Poincaré representations

The idea. One shell of the tower — one irreducible representation of the maximal compact subgroup of $0(4,2)$ — decomposes into infinitely many irreducible representations of the Poincaré group, with $m$ running over the spin–mass values. The tower is not one particle but a countable family, and the labels $(N_a,N_b)$ organise the family.

What it would add. A precise sense in which one representation contains many particles, which the corpus asserts in general terms. The Modules: What the Algebra Acts On (articles_physics/the-modules-what-the-algebra-acts-on.md) asks what a carrier can hold and answers with the module's decomposition; here the count is explicit — $(N_a+1)(N_b+1)$ states per shell, infinitely many Poincaré masses, one compact label.

Where it lands. That article's decomposition section, as the worked counting.

What stands in the way. The decomposition is stated and not written out; only the ground and first excited shells are exhibited with their spin content.

The remainder, one line each. That the internal configuration is a light-like vector plus a rotation about it, with no relative time, is the cleanest statement in the folder of why the internal variable is not a coordinate. That Nambu and Fronsdal arrived at infinite-component theories "from the outset" is the contemporary context and dates the letter.

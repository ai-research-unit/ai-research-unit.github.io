What the Paper Does

The companion to Equation of Motion of Elementary Particle from Unified Approach: the same triad model, now with a wave equation. One equation in the grand wave function $\Psi(X,\xi,t)$ is shown to be the Dirac equation on its first shell and the Kemmer equation on its second — the $16$ operators $B^{*}\Gamma B$ act with the algebra of the $16$ Dirac matrices, and the $10$ Kemmer matrices appear on the second shell. The letter is candid about its own gap: Lorentz covariance is missing until $\xi^{a}$ is made the basis of $SL(2)$, after which two covariant equations replace the one.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. One equation, many field equations, selected by a subsidiary condition

The idea. A single differential equation on a grand wave function contains the Dirac equation, the Kemmer equation and (by substituting $iK_3^{2}$ for $im$) the Maxwell equation as special cases, each picked out by the eigenvalue of $N$. The internal operators are the same operators in all cases; only the shell differs, and the Dirac matrices and Kemmer matrices are two representations of one algebra of internal bilinears.

What it would add. A reason for what the corpus only warns about. Parras' Four Options of the Dirac Equation and the Discrete Symmetries (articles_physics/parras-four-options-of-the-dirac-equation-and-the-discrete-symmetries.md) notes in §Why a Formalism of Components Mixes the Options that a component formalism mixes the options; here the mixing is the mechanism — one equation over a shell tower contains several field equations, and the options are shells.

Where it lands. §Why a Formalism of Components Mixes the Options, as the shell explanation.

What stands in the way. The Kemmer algebra appears on the second shell by matching $10$ components to $10\times10$ matrices: an equivalence, not a derivation.

2. Covariance is an extra assumption about what the internal coordinate is

The idea. The model is built on a real spinor with four real components. Nothing in the internal algebra forces Lorentz covariance; what does is declaring $\xi^{a}$ to transform as the basis of $SL(2)$. The author states the deficiency plainly — the amplitudes are spinors with respect to $SU(2)$ only — and repairs it by a change of what the coordinate means, not by changing the equation.

What it would add. A worked example of the corpus's recurring theme that the algebra does not supply the spacetime interpretation. The Spinor Module in Biquaternionic Form and Its Lorentz Action (articles_physics/the-spinor-module-in-biquaternionic-form-and-its-lorentz-action.md) supplies the action as part of the module's definition; here it has to be added after the fact, which shows what is lost if it is left implicit and what a module without it looks like.

Where it lands. §The Two Halves, as the covariance-as-input case.

What stands in the way. Once $\xi^{a}$ is declared an $SL(2)$ basis the covariance is straightforward; the letter does not display the two covariant equations in full, deferring to a longer paper.

3. A probability density over internal configurations

The idea. $d=\psi^{*}\psi$ is a density in the centre-of-mass position and in the internal configuration — radius and orientation of the triad. The continuity equation holds in $X$, so the internal variables are integrated over, but the density itself is a function on configuration space.

What it would add. A probabilistic reading of internal variables. Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Dirac Field (articles_physics/canonical-quantization-of-the-biquaternion-dirac-field.md) quantises a field over spacetime; here the density lives on a bundle whose fibre is the triad's shape and orientation.

Where it lands. The corpus's discussion of what the wave function is a function of; natural home is §The Spin-Particle Bundle of Parras' Four Options.

What stands in the way. The density is not normalisable in the internal variables without a measure, and the letter uses $d\tau=dx_1dx_2dy_1dy_2$ without discussing convergence.

4. The Maxwell equation as a shell with an imaginary mass substitution

The idea. The photon appears not as a separate theory but as the second-shell equation with $im$ replaced by $iK_3^{2}$: the mass term is swapped for a nilpotent internal operator, which is what makes the field massless and gauge-like.

What it would add. A mechanism for masslessness inside a massive tower: mass is an internal operator's eigenvalue, and one value of it is zero. Klein–Gordon from the Dirac Square in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/klein-gordon-from-the-dirac-square-in-biquaternionic-form.md) relates two wave equations by squaring; here by substitution.

Where it lands. The corpus's discussion of the massless limit; natural home is §Order Zero: the Mass Shell of Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave.

What stands in the way. The substitution is stated as a trick — "can. be reproduced by sub- acteristic" — with no derivation of why $K_3^{2}$ is the right operator.

The remainder, one line each. That the letters I and II are dated the same day and cross-reference each other makes them a single two-part argument worth reading together. That the shell counts $1,4,10$ match the field-component counts of scalar, Dirac and Kemmer fields exactly is the numerical coincidence the whole scheme rests on.

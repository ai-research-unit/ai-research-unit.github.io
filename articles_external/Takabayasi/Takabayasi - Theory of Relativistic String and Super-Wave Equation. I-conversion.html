What the Paper Does

Reconstructs the classical theory of the relativistic string from a standpoint the author calls the "realistic" one. The decisive distinction: on a given world strip, the geometric viewpoint admits all reparametrisations of $(\sigma,\tau)$, while the realistic viewpoint treats $\sigma$ as labelling material elements — parton number — and admits only transformations that keep it, so that longitudinal motion along the string remains meaningful. That restriction selects a more general model than the standard one. From the restricted-invariance action come the equations, covariant and non-covariant gauges, solutions, and an infinite set of conservation laws from conformal and internal-scale invariance.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Which reparametrisations are allowed is a physical question

The idea. $\sigma$ labels matter and therefore cannot be redefined; $\tau$ labels nothing and can be chosen freely or removed. The symmetry group of the theory is a subgroup of the worldsheet diffeomorphisms, and the choice of subgroup is a physical decision — whether longitudinal motion means anything.

What it would add. The sharpest available statement that reparametrisation invariance is not automatic but optional, with a criterion. The Lagrangian and the Nonlinear Sigma Model (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-nonlinear-sigma-model.md) uses worldsheet covariance without discussing its justification; here the justification is examined and narrowed, and the narrowing changes the model.

Where it lands. §The Model and the Constraint, as the restricted-invariance case.

What stands in the way. The "realistic" restriction is argued from parton number, which is a classical notion; quantum mechanically constituents of a string are not individually identifiable, so the premise weakens.

2. "Partial general transformation" — a symmetry group that is not a diffeomorphism group

The idea. Only $\tau\to\tau'(\sigma,\tau)$ with $\sigma$ fixed is permitted. This is a large group (it even allows $\tau$ to change dimension) but not the full two-dimensional one, and it is exactly large enough to remove $\tau$ and no larger.

What it would add. An intermediate symmetry group with a stated purpose. The corpus knows full covariance and fixed gauge; here is a group chosen to be precisely the redundancy that must be quotiented out, and the paper shows that its size is fixed by the requirement that $\tau$ be eliminable. That is a criterion the corpus lacks.

Where it lands. §The Model and the Constraint, as the minimal redundancy.

What stands in the way. No argument that a still smaller group would fail; the eliminability of $\tau$ is demonstrated, not shown to be maximal.

3. An infinite tower of conserved quantities from conformal invariance, commuting with Poincaré

The idea. Conformal transformations of the internal parameters give infinitely many constants of motion $A_r$, and because they act on $\sigma,\tau$ rather than on $x$, they commute with the Poincaré generators. $P^{\mu}$ and $M^{\mu\nu}$ are conformal invariant; the centre of mass $X^{\mu}$ is not.

What it would add. A concrete infinite symmetry algebra of the string with a clean separation from spacetime symmetries. The folder's conservation-law discussions are about $P$ and $M$; here the interesting algebra is the one that acts on the parameter space, and the statement that the centre of mass is the quantity that fails to be invariant is a precise and unusual claim.

Where it lands. §The Gauge Symmetry and the Constraints of The Lagrangian and the Non-Abelian Gauge Fields (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-non-abelian-gauge-fields.md), as the parameter-space symmetry.

What stands in the way. The $A_r$ are not given a physical interpretation; they are constants of motion whose meaning is left as weighted amplitudes of normal modes for the simpler $C_r^{\mu}$.

4. The non-covariant gauge gives a third-order equation and no constraints

The idea. Fixing $\tau'=x_0$ removes the constraint entirely, at the price of an equation third order in time and explicit Lorentz non-covariance. Constraints and higher derivatives trade against each other.

What it would add. A precise trade-off statement. Relativistic Bound State under the Hooke-Type Action-at-a-Distance in this folder uses the same gauge-fixing device (time as parameter) and gets second-order equations; here the price is one extra derivative, and the difference is instructive about what the string's extra structure costs.

Where it lands. The conversion of Relativistic Bound State under the Hooke-Type Action-at-a-Distance in this folder, as the third-order case.

What stands in the way. Third-order equations need extra initial data, and the paper does not address whether the physical solutions are a subset — which is where Ostrogradsky-type problems would appear.

The remainder, one line each. That the string is presented as the $N\to\infty$ limit of the neighbour-coupled multilocal chain ties this paper to the rest of the folder. That the conformal constants $A_r$ are expressed as a discrete sum over $r$ with coefficients $(-1)^{r}/r$ is the closest the folder comes to writing the Virasoro conditions as a conservation law.

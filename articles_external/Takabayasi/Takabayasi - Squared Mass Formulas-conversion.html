What the Paper Does

Proposes that the squared mass of a hadron family be linear in the internal quantum numbers, $m^{2}=A(2-S)+B(2T-1)$ with $S$ strangeness and $T$ isospin, uniformly for baryons, pseudoscalar mesons and vector mesons, the family entering only through two constants. Three relations follow, each accurate to about one percent, together with a prediction of the first recurrences.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Linearity in the internal labels, by analyticity in them rather than in $J$

The idea. Regge linearity says $m^{2}$ is linear in $J$ along a trajectory. If one presupposes analyticity in the internal quantum numbers as well and exchanges the roles of the slope and the labels, $m^{2}$ becomes linear in $I^{2}$ and $2T$ — with the same mechanism producing both, since $J$ steps by two and so does $2T$.

What it would add. An argument for a functional form the corpus takes from symmetry breaking. SU(3) Representations and the Gell-Mann–Okubo Mass Formula in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/su3-representations-and-the-gell-mann-okubo-mass-formula-in-biquaternionic-form.md) derives the Gell-Mann–Okubo relation in §The Gell-Mann–Okubo Mass Formula as the matrix element of a symmetry-breaking operator, which fixes the relation but not the power of the mass. Takabayasi's argument fixes the power — and it fixes it to squared mass for baryons and mesons alike, which is the one thing the standard treatment leaves to convention.

Where it lands. §The Gell-Mann–Okubo Mass Formula, as the reason for the square, and Open Questions in the same article, where the choice is listed.

What stands in the way. Analyticity in a discrete label is a strong and unproved assumption; the corpus's stated method is algebraic, so the import is an argument the corpus's tools do not contain, not a calculation it can repeat.

2. One formula for bosons and fermions, with the family only in two constants

The idea. $m^{2}=D(1-2T)+A^{W}(J-S+2T+1)$ holds for pseudoscalars and vectors, and $m^{2}=A(2-S)+B(2T-1)$ for baryons, so a single expression in $(\xi,S,T,J)$ covers all three families, with two fitted constants each.

What it would add. A worked alternative to the corpus's degree-counting formula. The Spin–Mass Formula and the Spectrum of Elementary Particles in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/the-spin-mass-formula-and-the-spectrum-of-elementary-particles-in-biquaternionic-form.md) assigns masses by the degree of a representation, $m/m_{e}=\deg\tau^{l\bar l}/2$, which is exact for the electron by construction and a few percent otherwise. His constants are two per family, with the same errors; the difference is that the formula keeps one shape across families.

Where it lands. That article's §The Spectrum, as the comparison case, with the baryon numbers recomputed.

What stands in the way. Two fitted constants per family against a formula with no fits: the corpus's article is more constrained and less accurate, and the trade should be stated rather than resolved.

3. The formula forbids levels: positivity of

$m^{2}$ plus a selection rule

The idea. Applied to the known octet, the pseudoscalar formula admits no other positive-$m^{2}$ level; for baryons a selection rule $n_BS\le0$, $n_J^{2}=1$ must be added "to prohibit unrealistic levels". A mass formula that predicts the absence of states is a stronger object than one that fits the states present.

What it would add. A use of a mass formula as a filter. What the Biquaternion Algebra Cannot Do: A Catalogue of Algebraic Obstructions (articles_physics/what-the-biquaternion-algebra-cannot-do-a-catalogue-of-algebraic-obstructions.md) collects what an algebra forbids; here an interpolation formula forbids, and the mechanism is arithmetic rather than algebraic, which makes it a different kind of check.

Where it lands. That catalogue's section on spectrum-level obstructions, as the numerical case.

What stands in the way. The selection rule is imposed and not derived, so the "forbidden" levels are forbidden by decree for baryons and only incidentally for mesons. The remainder, one line each. The prediction $\rho'$ near 1200 MeV and $K^{*\prime}$ near 1300 MeV, and $\rho''$ near 1420 MeV, are dated by the resonances then known and are now superseded. The accuracies quoted,"always about 1%", are the paper's own summary of its four fits.

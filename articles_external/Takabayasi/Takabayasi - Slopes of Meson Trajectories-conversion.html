What the Paper Does

Uses the author's relativistic two-body model with spacelike action-at-a-distance, specialised to a Hooke-type force, to derive the Regge slope as a function of the constituent mass. Since the force constant is taken universal, the slope is not: $\alpha'\propto\mu^{-1/2}$, and the ratios for the $\rho$, $\varphi$ and $\psi$ families follow from the constituent masses. The predicted $K^{*}$ slope, $0.82$, agrees with the empirical value, and the model is offered as a realisation of a relativistic quark model in which confinement is built into the spacelike character of the binding force.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The Regge slope is a function of the constituent mass, so it is not universal

The idea. With a universal force constant $K$ and a Hooke-type spacelike binding, $\alpha'$ comes out proportional to $\mu^{-1/2}$: $\alpha'_\rho:\alpha'_\varphi:\alpha'_\psi=m_\rho^{-1/2}:m_\varphi^{-1/2}:m_c^{-1/2}$. Non-universality is then not an anomaly but the signature of differing constituent masses under a shared coupling.

What it would add. A derivation of a relation the corpus treats as unexplained. Hadron Structure from QCD: The Bound-State Problem (articles_physics/hadron-structure-from-qcd-the-bound-state-problem.md) discusses in §The Counting Problem and §The Mass Gap and the Correlation Length — in the companion confinement article — what sets the hadronic scale, and treats the slope as a single number; here the slope is a function of the reduced mass, with ratios tested against the $\rho$, $\varphi$ and $\psi$ families. That is a falsifiable statement where the corpus has a constant.

Where it lands. §The Spin Decomposition, as the trajectory-level comparison, and Confinement and the Loss of Partonic Information in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/confinement-and-the-loss-of-partonic-information-in-biquaternionic-form.md) §The Mass Gap and the Correlation Length, where the scale enters.

What stands in the way. The extraction of $\alpha'_\psi$ is ambiguous by a factor of $1.3$ by the letter's own admission, and the ratio test then runs backwards, from the slopes to the constituent masses. The prediction is one number, $\alpha'_{K^*}=0.82$, against Igi's quoted value.

2. Confinement as the geometry of the binding: a spacelike force only

The idea. The constituents are bound by a force that acts "ous: spacelike distance inside a hadron only; ct/ =0.", so confinement is not imposed as a boundary condition or a rising potential in time but is a property of the character of the force — spacelike separation only, and hence no propagation of the binding outside the hadron. Interactions between hadrons are then a separate, second-quantised coupling of bilocal fields.

What it would add. A third confinement picture. Confinement and the Loss of Partonic Information in Biquaternionic Form (articles_physics/confinement-and-the-loss-of-partonic-information-in-biquaternionic-form.md) contrasts in §The Other Confinement Picture: A Linear Boundary Condition two pictures — the lossy-channel reading and the linear boundary condition; Takabayasi offers a third, where the force's support does the work. Three is the number at which a taxonomy becomes useful, and the corpus currently lists two.

Where it lands. §The Other Confinement Picture, as the third entry.

What stands in the way. The spacelike action-at-a-distance is a classical postulate about the action, not a consequence of any field theory, and the letter does not show that it forbids decay.

3. A bilocal wave equation with a subsidiary condition that does not spoil the spectrum

The idea. The quantum theory is a free wave equation in $X$ and $\xi$ plus a constraint linear in the mixed derivative. The constraint removes the relative-time degree without destroying the linear trajectories, so the subsidiary condition and the spectrum are compatible.

What it would add. A case where the constraint is benign, which sharpens the corpus's account of when constraints are not. The Lagrangian and the Second Quantisation (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-second-quantisation.md) treats constraints through the Dirac bracket in §The Constraints the Action Imposes; the earlier Takabayasi letters in this folder argue that their tower has no subsidiary condition and is therefore easy to quantise. This letter has one, and it still works — which means the difficulty attributed to constraints is not the constraint as such.

Where it lands. §The Constraints the Action Imposes: The Dirac Bracket, as the benign case.

What stands in the way. Under the assumption of convergence appears in the norm; the constraint's consistency is shown by reproducing the spectrum, not by a general argument.

The remainder, one line each. That $b$ should be equal to $4m_0^{2}$ yet is left free is a deliberately kept parameter; imposing it gives $m_c=1.38$ GeV. That the daughter trajectories are indexed by $n_r$, leading at $n_r=0$, is the cleanest statement of the tower's structure here.

What the Paper Does

A two-page letter giving the Lagrangian for the bilocal theory and extending it to three points. The invariance $X_\mu\to X_\mu+\lambda\xi_\mu$ does the work: the Lagrangian depends on $X$ only through $X\cdot\xi$, and the primary constraints follow. The trilocal case uses two relative vectors and a Lagrangian built from the area of the triangle they span, so the binding potential is proportional to the squared area. Two classes of solution are given — a rigidly rotating regular triangle and an isotropically oscillating one — and the quantum theory is a wave equation with a constraint.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. One invariance generates the whole constraint structure

The idea. Adding $\lambda\xi_\mu$ (or $\lambda_1\xi_\mu+\lambda_2\eta_\mu$) to the centre of mass and demanding invariance does everything: it forces $X$ to appear only through $X\cdot\xi$, and that yields $P\cdot\xi=0$, which is the relative-time suppression. Nothing else is needed.

What it would add. The minimal version of the invariance-to-constraint mechanism. Part I of the Multi-Local series states the theorem and derives the constraint; this letter shows the same in two pages and then repeats it for two relative vectors, which demonstrates that the mechanism scales with the number of relative coordinates — one invariance per relative vector.

Where it lands. The conversion of Relativistic Mechanics of Interacting Particles I in this folder, §The Invariance and the Constraint, as the minimal statement.

What stands in the way. The letter asserts the result and quotes the constraint; the derivation of the Euler equations is left to the longer papers.

2. A three-body potential fixed by geometry: the squared area

The idea. The trilocal binding is not a sum of pairwise springs. It is proportional to $S^{2}$, the squared area of the triangle spanned by the three positions. A two-body potential cannot produce this, and a sum of pairwise potentials would not be a function of the area.

What it would add. A genuinely three-body interaction with a geometric meaning. Hadron Structure from QCD: The Bound-State Problem (articles_physics/hadron-structure-from-qcd-the-bound-state-problem.md) treats the confining potential as a two-body object in §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits; here the three-body case forces an area law, which is the baryonic analogue of a string's worldsheet area and a concrete alternative to pairwise flux tubes.

Where it lands. §The Trace Anomaly, and Where the Mass Sits, as the three-body law.

What stands in the way. The area law is what falls out of this particular Lagrangian ansatz; the paper does not argue that a baryon's binding must be of this form.

3. Two modes, one with spin and one without

The idea. The rigidly rotating equilateral triangle carries $J\propto M^{2}$; the isotropically breathing one has $J=0$ at every amplitude. The same three-body system supports rotation and pure breathing as distinct solutions, and the breathing mode's tracelessness kills the angular momentum.

What it would add. A concrete pair of modes to contrast with the folder's two-body spectra. The bilocal model has only the rotating mode; here the third constituent admits a mode with no rotation at all, which shows what a third point adds physically rather than only algebraically.

Where it lands. §The Spectrum of The Spectrum of a Lorentz Rotor (articles_physics/the-spectrum-of-a-lorentz-rotor-what-two-eigenvalues-leave-undetermined.md), as the breathing mode.

What stands in the way. Both modes are particular solutions, not the general one — the letter says solving (7) generally "is still difficult".

4. A trajectory through the origin

The idea. For the rigid triangle, $J=M^{2}/(4r/3\sqrt{3})$ — a straight line with zero intercept. There is no daughter structure and no intercept parameter.

What it would add. A limiting case for the intercept discussion. Slopes of Mesons extracts a slope and an intercept from data; here a model produces a slope with intercept exactly zero, which is the value the mesonic models must avoid, and the contrast identifies what the mesonic models add.

Where it lands. The conversion of Slopes of Mesons in this folder, §What the Model Fixes, as the zero-intercept case.

What stands in the way. The result is classical and for one special mode; the quantised version's spectrum is given only as a wave equation. The remainder, one line each. That the two-point Lagrangian of this letter reproduces the "corresponding points" condition of the earlier paper vindicates the Lagrangian route. That the generalised potential $U(\xi^{2},\eta^{2},\xi\eta)$ is mentioned in one sentence shows how much of the programme remained for the longer papers.

What the Paper Does

Compresses the multilocal programme into five pages and states its premises. Two requirements — constituents must reach mass shell when the force is switched off, and rigid motions must be among the allowed modes — together with relativistic consistency leave only two models, the "chain" and the "ring". A "symmetric" model with equal harmonic forces between all pairs is discarded because it fails the second requirement. The chain model's $N\to\infty$ limit is the open string, and the note observes that the string can be derived from a simpler Lagrangian than Nambu–Goto. Intrinsic spins are then added with $2N-1$ sets of anticommuting matrices, and the spinning string appears as the same limit.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Two premises select the model

The idea. Only two requirements — adiabatic mass-shell recovery and the presence of rigid motions — plus consistency single out the chain and ring. The premises are physical, not aesthetic, and they discard an otherwise natural model (equal forces between all pairs).

What it would add. A selection principle that is stronger than this model fits the spectrum. The symmetric model is not wrong in its spectrum claims; it is wrong because it has no organising structure, and that is a structural criterion. The corpus's model choices are usually justified by results; here one is justified by a property of its mode content.

Where it lands. §The Model and the Constraint of The Lagrangian and the Nonlinear Sigma Model (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-nonlinear-sigma-model.md), as the structural criterion.

What stands in the way. Premise (b) is itself a choice; a physically sensible composite need not admit rigid motion, so the selection is only as strong as that premise.

2. The string from a simpler Lagrangian without worldsheet gauge invariance

The idea. The chain's $N\to\infty$ limit gives an action simpler than Nambu–Goto, with no $(\sigma,\tau)$ reparametrisation invariance, and the physical interpretation is consequently more direct. The gauge invariance is not a virtue here; it is a complication the discrete construction avoids.

What it would add. A derivation of string theory from a discrete system, and a dissenting view on reparametrisation invariance. General-Covariant Approach to Relativistic String Theory in this folder argues the opposite case — that general covariance is a resource; this note says the simpler non-gauge action is preferable for interpretation. Two papers in the same folder taking opposite views on the same invariance is exactly the kind of tension worth recording.

Where it lands. The conversion of General-Covariant Approach to Relativistic String Theory in this folder, as the counter-position.

What stands in the way. The simpler Lagrangian is claimed to reproduce the open string; whether it gives equivalent quantum theory (with the same critical dimension, if any) is not shown in five pages.

3. Zero-point terms cancelling between the constraint and the spin part

The idea. With $2N-1$ anticommuting matrix sets, the equation $(A_0+L_0)\psi=0$ has the zero-point constant of $A_0$ exactly cancelled by that of $L_0$. No normal-ordering constant has to be adjusted by hand.

What it would add. A mechanism for the intercept problem that is arithmetic rather than a choice of ordering. The folder's other fermionic constructions have to tune $\omega_0$; here the cancellation is structural, which is a much stronger statement and connects directly to the intercept-one problem of the bosonic case.

Where it lands. The conversion of Detailed Wave Equation for Extended Model Underlying the Dual Amplitude in this folder, §Why the Intercept Had to Be One, as the cancellation mechanism.

What stands in the way. The matching of zero-point terms is displayed for this particular matrix content; the cancellation's stability under interaction is not examined.

4. The four-point case is technically exceptional

The idea. A footnote records that the relative momentum is not a hyperplane when $N=4$ and that the constraint expressions differ for odd and even $N$.

What it would add. A concrete technical crack at exactly the point where the earlier papers say the scheme stops working. The "up to four points" ceiling appears here as an anomaly at four rather than a smooth failure at five, which is a more informative statement and worth putting next to the counting argument of the Hooke paper.

Where it lands. The conversion of Relativistic Bound State under the Hooke-Type Action-at-a-Distance in this folder, §The Counting Bound, as the four-point anomaly.

What stands in the way. The remark is a footnote; no diagnosis of why four is special is offered.

The remainder, one line each. That the ring model is set aside for later treatment, not rejected, keeps it in the programme. That the note is a preprint with a DPNU number shows the material was circulated before journal publication and may overlap the journal papers of the same years.

What the Paper Does

A two-part seminar report on the programme behind the 1957 memoir. Part I summarises the tensor formulation of the Dirac field: the spinor and the gamma matrices are replaced by two scalars and three vectors, the equations of motion are brought to hydrodynamical form, one subsidiary condition ties the momentum field's vorticity to the velocity and the spin, and all of it follows from a Lagrangian. Part II lists the collateral results: the general relativistic hydrodynamics of fluids with intrinsic angular momentum, the claim that quantum fluids always carry heat flow beside mechanical stress, the reduction of Weyssenhof's fluid to a spinless particle in a constant field, and two extensions — a local Lorentz transformation as the fluid's frame variable, and the Pauli field as an assembly of small rotating bodies. It is a summary, so it states results and cites the papers; the only formulas are the definition of the variables and the constraint.

Part II: the collateral results, in his order

  1. General relativistic hydrodynamics with intrinsic angular momentum. He has given the general theory for fluids carrying distributions of intrinsic angular momentum, analysing the physical meanings of the energy-momentum and angular-momentum tensors, and classifying the various hydrodynamics "by clarifying the conditions that characterize the specialities of any hydrodynamics."

  2. Heat flow is universal in quantum fluids. "In the hydrodynamics representing quantum matters there always appear the flow of heat besides mechanical stress inside the fluid. This situation is illustrated for the case of the hydrodynamics representing the Schrödinger field also: the quantum-mechanical effect must be represented by the occurrences of quantum stress along with 'quantum heat flow' there."

  3. Weyssenhof's fluid reduces to a spinless particle. He compares Weyssenhof's hydrodynamics with the Dirac one "and showed that the former can be reduced essentially to a single equation of motion which manifests that each particle of this fluid moves exactly in the same manner with the spinless charged particle under certain constant hypothetical electromagnetic field."

  4. The frame as a local Lorentz transformation. We introduce the quantity $\Lambda(x)$ at each point in space, satisfying the constraints $\Lambda^{T}g\Lambda=g$; and identify the velocity and spin variables in terms of it. Then the constraints represent a Lorentz transformation defined at each point of space. Thus the Dirac spinor $\Psi$ is represented by the set of variables $(\rho,\theta,\Lambda,\lambda)$ where $\lambda$ is a scalar function. Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

  1. The fluid's frame is a local Lorentz transformation and appears in the Lagrangian as the dynamical variable

The idea. Replace the spin vector by a field $\Lambda(x)\in SO(1,3)$ at each point, constrained by $\Lambda^{T}g\Lambda=g$, and write the whole theory — equations of motion, subsidiary condition and all — as the Euler–Lagrange equations of one Lagrangian density in $(\rho,\theta,\Lambda,\lambda)$. The spin direction is then not an independent vector but a column of the frame, and the constraint is a constraint of the variational problem rather than an identity imposed after the fact.

What it would add. A variational principle for a constrained system, where the corpus has the variables but not the principle. The Lagrangian and the Coupled Dirac Equation in Polar Variables (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-coupled-dirac-equation-in-polar-variables.md) and The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables (articles_physics/the-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-polar-variables.md) write the word, its brackets and its Lagrangian — and the rotor is exactly a local Lorentz transformation, so the two formulations share the frame variable. What the corpus does not exhibit is a Lagrangian in which the frame alone is the dynamical variable and the subsidiary condition is one of its constraints. Takabayasi's claim — that the closure condition is not an extra equation but the constraint that comes with the frame — is the variational version of the corpus's remark that the non-closure is the algebraic independence of the four brackets.

Where it lands. The Lagrangian-and-polar article, as the tensor-language statement of what its variational principle does, and the spin-half fluid article's §The triple, where the corpus counts the closure.

What stands in the way. The Lagrangian itself is not recoverable from this scan — the seminar prints it as an unlabelled equation whose symbols did not survive the digitisation — so the import would rest on the statement that it exists and on the variable list, not on the functional. That is a thin basis for a formula, and thick enough for a paragraph. It ranks first on the strength of the idea and not of the evidence.

  1. Quantum heat flow, and its universality

The idea. Every hydrodynamical representation of a quantum matter carries a heat flow beside its mechanical stress, and this is not a property of the Dirac field: "the quantum-mechanical effect must be represented by the occurrences of quantum stress along with 'quantum heat flow'" already for the Schrödinger field.

What it would add. A universal claim that the corpus holds for one case only. The Spin-Half Fluid and the Takabayasi Correspondence (articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-and-the-takabayasi-correspondence.md) has a section The Mass Density and the Heat Flow which computes the heat flow that his choice of velocity leaves in the fluid's rest frame — for the Dirac fluid, and as a consequence of the velocity's frame (the Eckart choice). The seminar's claim is stronger and different in kind: that the Schrödinger fluid carries heat flow too. The corpus's Schrödinger articles — The Schrödinger Fluid (articles_physics/the-schrodinger-fluid.md) and The Thermodynamics of a Quantum Fluid: Status of the Derivation (articles_physics/the-thermodynamics-of-a-quantum-fluid-status-of-the-derivation.md) — describe a fluid with a quantum stress and, being irrotational and one-component, no heat flow at all. If the claim is right, the corpus's cleanest rung is not as clean as it says, and the reason is a definition of heat flow and not a term in an equation.

Where it lands. The thermodynamics-of-a-quantum-fluid article, whose subject is exactly this, and the Schrödinger fluid article's §The Quantum Term Is an Exact Stress.

What stands in the way. The claim is asserted in one sentence with no definition of the heat flow and no formula, and it is at odds with the corpus's statement that the Schrödinger fluid's energy flux is its momentum flux with nothing left over. Resolving it needs a definition the seminar does not give — whether heat flow is the part of the flux orthogonal to the velocity in some frame, or the part not carried by the entropy current. The item is a question to be settled, and it is ranked second because the corpus can settle it from its own definitions in one calculation.

3. Weyssenhof's fluid is a spinless particle in a constant field

The idea. Weyssenhof's spin fluid, which is a different and older tensor formulation, reduces essentially to a single equation of motion, and that equation is the one a spinless charged particle obeys in a certain constant hypothetical electromagnetic field.

What the Paper Does

Takes up Wallstrom's two objections against founding quantum mechanics on the Madelung equations and separates the part that survives from the part that does not. The authors prove that Takabayasi's circulation condition holds for $C^{1}$ wave functions and then argue it cannot be extended to the general theory. They show the common motivating examples — hydrogenic eigenfunctions, the two-dimensional oscillator — fail the irrotationality equation in the distributional sense, and name the failure "quantum quasi-irrotationality". They construct the non-quantized strong solutions Wallstrom claimed exist, and they relocate the second objection: the non-uniqueness is not a defect of the Madelung equations but of the Schrödinger equation, which cannot distinguish physically equivalent states.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

  1. The curl does not vanish — it is a delta on the nodal line

The idea. The corpus's account is that the flow is irrotational off the nodal set and that a loop linking a node has circulation fixed by winding. Reddiger and Poirier make a stronger and strictly different statement: taken distributionally, the curl of the drift field of a hydrogenic eigenstate is not zero anywhere it is tested — it is $2\pi\mu\hbar/m$ times a delta supported on the nodal line. The third Madelung equation therefore fails as an equation, not merely as a statement about a loop that has no surface to bound.

What it would add. A correction of emphasis with a consequence. If the curl is a delta rather than zero, then the irrotationality equation is not vacuous on the node but wrong on it, and any variational formulation must either accept the delta as a source or restrict to the real-valued eigenfunctions. That is a sharper form of the corpus's own thesis and it changes what one has to prove.

Where it lands. §So the irrotationality is exact on the complement of the nodal set and vacuous on it of The Nodal Set and Quantized Circulation (articles_physics/the-nodal-set-and-quantized-circulation.md), as the distributional refinement.

What stands in the way. The corpus's argument is geometric and avoids distributions entirely; adopting the distributional reading means conceding that the strong-form statement is false and rebuilding the section on weaker hypotheses.

  1. The circulation integer is the magnetic quantum number, not the winding number

The idea. The corpus identifies the integer with the topological degree of the phase map, which is loop-independent. Reddiger and Poirier identify it, for hydrogenic states, with $\mu$ — and note that requiring circulation be integral for $\mu$ is what forces $n$ and $l$ to be integers as well.

What it would add. A dependency the corpus does not record: the quantization of one number is what quantizes the others. If the identification with $\mu$ is right, then "the integer is the degree" is true but incomplete, because the degree is not free — it is tied to the other two quantum numbers by the same condition.

Where it lands. §The winding is a class of the field's map into the algebra's group of invertibles of The Nodal Set and Quantized Circulation, as the identification.

What stands in the way. The claim is stated for Coulomb eigenfunctions specifically and is not shown to be a general property of circulation integrals.

3. "Quantum quasi-irrotationality" — a name for a class of fields

The idea. A drift field whose curl is supported on a lower-dimensional set is quasi-irrotational: irrotational everywhere the test function does not see the support. Where it lands. §The nodal set is where the polar form ends of The Nodal Set and Quantized Circulation, as the definition.

4. The non-uniqueness is a defect of the Schrödinger equation

The idea. Wallstrom's second objection — that the Madelung equations do not determine a unique evolution when density supports merge — is turned around: the Schrödinger equation does not distinguish those initial states either, and all of them give the same predictions at $t=0$.

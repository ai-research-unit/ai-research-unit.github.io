What the Paper Does

Runs the three masses of each sector from $1$ GeV to $2\times10^{16}$ GeV with the one-loop QCD and Yukawa equations, forms the closure ratio in each sector at every scale, and finds that the ratio moves little: $k_u$ stays near $1.33$, $k_d$ near $1.05$, $k_l$ near $1.0019$. The size of the move differs by sector — $6\times10^{-6}$ relative for the leptons, $0.15\%$ for the up quarks, $4\%$ for the down quarks — and the paper reads all three as stability. The reason is algebraic: the ratio depends only on the two mass ratios of the sector, and those ratios are almost scale-independent because the masses of a sector share one anomalous dimension to leading order. The paper then repeats the 2005 neutrino estimate. The conclusion is that the failure in quarks and neutrinos is a genuine failure of the relation rather than a scaling artefact.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The ratio in the three sectors, at every scale

$$ k_l\equiv\frac{2}{3}\frac{m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}\right)^2}, \qquad k_u\equiv\frac23\frac{1+x_u+y_u}{\left(1+\sqrt{x_u}+\sqrt{y_u}\right)^2}, \qquad k_d\equiv\frac23\frac{1+x_d+y_d}{\left(1+\sqrt{x_d}+\sqrt{y_d}\right)^2}, \tag{paper 1, 3, 4} $$

with $x_u=m_c/m_u$, $y_u=m_t/m_u$, $x_d=m_s/m_d$, $y_d=m_b/m_d$. "Both $x_u$ and $y_u$ decrease with the increase of $\mu$ at low energy scales and increase slightly at high energy scales. However, we find that in spite of the change of $x_u$ and $y_u$, $k_u$ is almost invariant (approximately $1.33$) at all energy scales, which means that $k_u$ is a constant independent of the running of quark masses."

"$k_d$ also keeps invariant with the change of energy scales, just as $k_u$, and its approximate value is $1.05$." And "in Table IV ... $k_l$ at high energy scales is still quite close to $1$ as at the low energy scale, which means that Koide's relation is suitable for charged leptons at all the energy scales".

The 2005 numbers are corrected: "In [11] we calculated the range of $k_u$, and got $1.1<k_u<1.4$ (with the mean value of $1.25$). However, in [11] we did not consider the renormalization effect of quark masses. With this effect taken into account, we find that $k_u$ changes a little, from $1.25$ to $1.33$."

Why it does not move

The ratio depends on the two mass ratios only; the ratio $m_i/m_j$ of two masses in one sector runs with the difference of their anomalous dimensions, and in QCD at one loop that difference vanishes because $\gamma_0=2$ for every flavour: $\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}m(\mu)=-\gamma(\alpha_s)m(\mu)$ with $\gamma(\alpha_s)=\gamma_0+\gamma_1\alpha_s/\pi+\ldots$. So the ratios are constant at leading order, and the residual motion in the tables is the higher-order and threshold effect. The paper states the analogous mechanism for leptons through the Yukawa evolution, where only the ratios involving $m_t$, $m_b$ and $m_\tau$ run.

The neutrino part

Repeats the 2005 analysis unchanged: no real root for the exact relation, $0.50<k_\nu<0.85$ for the normal ordering and $0.50<k_\nu<0.65$ inverted, the conjecture $k_l+k_d\approx k_\nu+k_u\approx2$, and with $k_\nu\approx0.75$ the masses $m_1=1.0\times10^{-5}$, $m_2=8.4\times10^{-3}$, $m_3=0.05$ eV. The complementarity is asserted to be as scale-independent as the individual ratios.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The closure is scale-stable because the ratios are

The idea. A result with a reason: $Q$ for a sector depends only on the sector's two mass ratios, and those ratios are invariant under the leading QCD running, so $Q$ moves only through higher orders and thresholds. Numerically, over sixteen decades, $k_l=1.001881\to1.001888$ (a relative drift of $7\times10^{-6}$), $k_u=1.341\to1.339$ ($0.15\%$), $k_d=1.068\to1.025$ ($4\%$). In $Q$ itself the drifts are $5\times10^{-6}$, $1.3\times10^{-3}$ and $2.9\times10^{-2}$.

What it would add. This answers the first task in articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, "run the masses to a common scale and re-evaluate $Q$", whose stated test is whether $Q$ drifts by more than $10^{-3}$. The answer separates the sectors: the leptons pass by two orders of magnitude ($5\times10^{-6}$), the up quarks sit at the threshold ($1.3\times10^{-3}$), the down quarks fail it by a factor of thirty ($2.9\times10^{-2}$). So the closure's precision is not a scale artefact for the leptons, and the down-quark failure is partly a running effect at low scales. It also gives articles_physics/the-renormalization-group-in-biquaternionic-form.md a case where a dimensionless combination is one-loop invariant, which is the cleanest instance of the article's own subject.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, first target, as the resolution; and §1's quark row, which carries $0.8489808$ and $0.7314277$ at $2$ GeV and asks about other scales.

What stands in the way. The corpus computes in mass, not in the ratio, so the invariance is not automatic in its own variables; the claim must be restated as "$Q$ is a function of ratios only" plus the one-loop equality of the $\gamma_0$, and the corpus has to import the one-loop coefficient $\gamma_0=2$. The paper's own tables show the statement is "stable to a few per cent at worst", not "exact", and the down-quark drift is large enough that the paper's word "invariant" overstates it there.

2. The failure in quarks is not a scaling artefact

The idea. A claim: since $k_u$ and $k_d$ are constant along the flow, the fact that they are not $1$ cannot be removed by evaluating at another scale. The failure is in the spectrum, not in the scheme.

What it would add. The corpus's §11 keeps open the possibility that the quark failure is a matter of scale. The table shuts it for the up sector, where $k_u$ moves by $0.15\%$ while missing $1$ by $33\%$. The paper is also a correction of its own authors' 2005 paper, which had said that because the relation is not scale invariant the masses should be taken at high energy; here the scale dependence is measured and found small, so the earlier remark is withdrawn by evidence.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, and §9 where the quark towers are listed as failed routes.

What stands in the way. The invariance holds for the strong running; the electroweak and Yukawa contributions are not exactly flavour-blind, so the door is shut to a few per cent and not to all orders.

Rest, in one line each

The neutrino mass estimate repeats the 2005 paper and inherits its conjecture. The 2005 statement that "Koide's relation is not energy-scale invariant" is corrected here for quarks and leptons; the two papers should be read as one, the later one superseding.

What the Paper Does

Argues that three masses leave room for two relations, not one, and that the second should also be scale invariant, so it must be a ratio of degree zero. That pins the candidate to $\kappa=\det\Phi/[\Phi]^3$, with $\Phi$ the $U(3)$-family nonet scalar whose diagonal vacuum is proportional to $(\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})$. The paper then searches for a potential whose stationarity forces a value of $\kappa$ independently of the potential's couplings, the way the $\lambda'$ term forces $K=2/3$. A particular quartic potential gives $\kappa=1/486$, against the measured $1/486.663$. The choice of that potential is made by a "simple integers" rule and the author states plainly that it has no theoretical basis.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why a second relation is expected

"Since we can, in general, have two relations for three quantities, we may also expect another relation for the charged lepton masses. Since the relation (1) is invariant under a scale transformation $(m_e,m_\mu,m_\tau)\to(\lambda m_e,\lambda m_\mu,\lambda m_\tau)$, we speculate that the second relation will also be invariant under the scale transformation."

The first relation, in matrix language

With $\langle\Phi\rangle=\mathrm{diag}(v_1,v_2,v_3)\propto\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})$,

$$ K\equiv\frac{m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}\right)^2}=\frac{[\Phi\Phi]}{[\Phi]^2}=\frac23 , \tag{paper 3} $$

where $[\Phi]$ means $\mathrm{Tr}\,\Phi$.

The second relation

$$ \kappa\equiv\frac{\sqrt{m_e m_\mu m_\tau}}{\left(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}\right)^3}=\frac{\det\Phi}{[\Phi]^3}. \tag{paper 4} $$

How $K$ came out without tuning

The potential is $V=\mu^2[\Phi\Phi]+\lambda[\Phi\Phi][\Phi\Phi]+\lambda'[\Phi_8\Phi_8][\Phi]^2$ with $\Phi_8\equiv\Phi-\tfrac13[\Phi]\mathbf{1}$. Stationarity with $\Phi\ne\mathbf{1}$ gives two conditions, one of which is

$$ [\Phi\Phi]-\frac23[\Phi]^2=0 , \tag{paper 9} $$

"and the result (8) fixes the scale of the VEV of $\Phi$. Note that the relation (9) has been derived independently of the potential parameters $\mu$, $\lambda$ and $\lambda'$."

The general answer for $\kappa$

For a potential built from the four $SU(3)$-invariant but $U(3)$-violating terms plus a $[\Phi]^4$ term, the coefficient of $\mathbf{1}$ in $\partial V'/\partial\Phi$ vanishes when

$$ \kappa=\frac{1}{27a_1}\left(4a_0-a_1-72a_4+8a_0'\right), \tag{paper 11} $$

so a value of $\kappa$ needs a conspiracy among four coefficients, unlike $K$.

The chosen potential and its prediction

The author picks one combination by preferring small integers and obtains

$$ \kappa=\frac{1}{18\times 27}=\frac{1}{486}, \tag{paper 19} $$

"independently of the coefficient $\lambda'$ in (18)", against the measured

$$ \kappa_{\rm obs}=\frac{1}{486.663}. \tag{paper 20} $$

The limitation, stated by the author

"The form (18) is likely under the criterion 'simple', but the choice does not have theoretical basis." And on the empirical agreement of $K$ itself: "the 'charged lepton masses' $(\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})$ in Eq.(2) are not pole masses (the observed masses), but mass values defined in the $U(3)$-family symmetry limit ... Although it is well known that the relation (1) is excellently satisfied with the observed charged lepton masses, this is nothing but an accidental coincidence."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A second scale-invariant number, $\kappa=1/486$

The idea. A number: besides $Q=2/3$, the same three masses satisfy $\kappa=\sqrt{m_em_\mu m_\tau}/(\sum\sqrt m)^3=1/486.663$, and the natural matrix expression is $\det\Phi/[\Phi]^3$.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2 catalogues the exact algebra of $Q$ alone — the bound, the circle, the angle — and treats one number as the whole content. A second invariant of the same triple, with the same scale invariance and a measured value, changes the count: two numbers for three masses means one degree of freedom left, which is what makes the phase parametrisation possible at all.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2, as a companion invariant, and §1's table of measured values.

What stands in the way. $\kappa$ is not independent evidence for anything: any triple has a $\kappa$, and its value near $1/486$ is one more coincidence to explain. The paper's own derivation of that value is a choice of potential. Ranked first, because it is the only new measured quantity in this batch.

2. Stationarity of one term fixes a ratio of traces

The idea. A mechanism: a potential with one $SU(3)$-invariant but $U(3)$-violating term has a stationary point at which a ratio of traces takes a fixed value independent of all couplings, because the offending term enters $\partial V/\partial\Phi$ only through the unit matrix.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §6 asks where a coefficient in front of the closure could come from and finds only the circle, which is the closure itself. Here is a coefficient produced by a symmetry-breaking pattern rather than assumed: the $2/3$ is forced by the structure of the potential, not fitted, and the mechanism is stated in three lines of matrix algebra.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §6, and articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md in the discussion of what fixes the eigenvalues.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no scalar nonet and no family symmetry, so the mechanism cannot be stated in its own language; and the paper shows the trick works for $K$ with one term but fails for $\kappa$ with one term, needing four. Second.

3. Scale invariance as the filter on candidate relations

The idea. A method: demand that any further relation among the three masses be invariant under $m_i\to\lambda m_i$, and the space of candidates collapses to ratios of homogeneous invariants of the mass matrix.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11 lists "look for a boundary condition that quantises $\theta$" as the only route to a mechanism, with no filter on what such a condition may look like. Scale invariance is exactly such a filter, and it removes the free normalisation that otherwise lets any formula be adjusted to fit.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, third row of the table.

What stands in the way. Most of the corpus's statements are already scale invariant (angles, ratios), so the filter excludes little and predicts nothing by itself. Third.

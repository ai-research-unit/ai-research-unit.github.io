What the Paper Does

Proposes that the closure should be applied to six quarks at once rather than to charge triplets, and reports that this six-member sum lands near $2/3$ — within $5\times10^{-2}$ at $2$ GeV, and crossing $2/3$ at some scale between $2$ GeV and $M_Z$. It also reports the light/heavy split (u,d,s) and (c,b,t), which works numerically for the heavy set and fails for the light one, and notes that the six-dimensional angle between the amplitude vector and $(1,1,\ldots,1)$ is close to $\pi/3$. The paper is explicit that the running masses at $M_Z$ violate the relation and that the crossing is an accident of running unless something fixes the scale.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The lepton baseline, and why the electron matters

With $m_e=0.510998910\pm0.000000013$ MeV, $m_\mu=105.658367\pm0.000004$ MeV, $m_\tau=1776.82^{+0.16}_{-0.26}$ MeV, and $\Delta K_\ell\equiv(3K_\ell/2-1)$,

$$ -2\times10^{-5}<\Delta K_\ell<9\times10^{-7}. $$

"It is also astonishing that the electron mass, being orders of magnitude smaller than the muon and tau-lepton masses, still plays an important role in this relation. Neglecting $m_e$ in (2) we would find $\Delta K_\ell\sim2\times10^{-2}$."

The charge triplets and the running

With the pole masses $m_d=4.1$–$5.7$ MeV, $m_s=100^{+30}_{-20}$ MeV, $m_b=4.19^{+0.18}_{-0.06}$ GeV, $K_d\sim0.72$, "i.e. a considerable deviation from the Koide limit with $\Delta K_d\sim8\times10^{-2}$"; with $m_u=1.7$–$3.1$ MeV, $m_c=1.29^{+0.05}_{-0.11}$ GeV, $m_t=172.9\pm0.6\pm0.9$ GeV, "$K_u\sim0.85$, which corresponds to $\Delta K_u\sim27\cdot10^{-2}$". Using the running masses at $M_Z$ ($m_u=1.27^{+0.50}_{-0.42}$ MeV, $m_d=2.90^{+1.24}_{-1.19}$, $m_s=55^{+16}_{-15}$ MeV, $m_b=0.619\pm0.084$ GeV, $m_c=2.89\pm0.09$ GeV, $m_t=171.7\pm3.0$ GeV) gives $K_d\sim0.74$, $K_u\sim0.89$, "$\Delta K_d\sim11\cdot10^{-2}$ and $\Delta K_u\sim33\cdot10^{-2}$. That is, the running effects induce an even stronger deviation".

For the leptons at $M_Z$ ($0.486570161$, $102.7181359$, $1746.24$ MeV), "$1.87\cdot10^{-3}<\Delta K_\ell<1.91\cdot10^{-3}$. This implies in particular, that the running masses at high scales are not compatible with the exact Koide limit, $K_\ell=2/3$. This result is somewhat counterintuitive. If there is an underlying high-scale symmetry which ensures (2) for leptons, one could expect that the deviation from the Koide limit would be smaller at high scales."

Light and heavy, then all six

By mass rather than by charge: $K_{light}$ over $(u,d,s)$ and $K_{heavy}$ over $(c,b,t)$ give "$-5\cdot10^{-3}<\Delta K_{heavy}<1\cdot10^{-2}$, and a deviation from it for the light ones, $-20\cdot10^{-2}<\Delta K_{light}<-6\cdot10^{-2}$". The author objects that "s and c quarks — components of the same $SU_L(2)$ doublet — enter the expressions for $K_{light}$ and $K_{heavy}$ separately is counterintuitive".

The proposal instead is one sum over all six,

$$ K_q\equiv\frac{\sum m_q}{\left(\sum\sqrt{m_q}\right)^2}, \tag{paper 10} $$

for which "we find for the deviation from the Koide limit $-5\cdot10^{-2}<\Delta K_q<-4\cdot10^{-2}$. If we take the running quark masses at the scale $M_Z$ instead, we will obtain $2\cdot10^{-2}<\Delta K_q<5\cdot10^{-2}$. It is interesting that due to the running effects $\Delta K_q$ crosses zero. That is, at some scale $2$ GeV $<\mu<M_Z$ the quark masses satisfy (within the uncertainties) the Koide relation $K_q=2/3$."

"Interestingly enough, although masses of the light ($u$, $d$ and $s$) quarks are much smaller than masses of the heavy ones ($c$, $b$ and $t$), their inclusion into the sum makes it substantially closer to the Koide limit. This resembles the role played by the electron mass in the Koide relation for charged leptons."

The angle in six dimensions

With $\theta_q$ defined as the angle between $(\sqrt{m_d},\sqrt{m_u},\sqrt{m_s},\ldots)$ and $(1,1,1,\ldots)$ in six dimensions, "we will find that this angle is again a rational multiple of $\pi$, $\theta_q\approx\pi/3$."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The closure on six members, and a scale where it is exact

The idea. A result: $K_q=\sum m/\left(\sum\sqrt m\right)^2$ over all six quarks is $0.633$–$0.640$ at $2$ GeV and $0.680$–$0.700$ at $M_Z$, so it crosses $2/3$ somewhere in between; the six-member sum is a much better fit than either charge triplet.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md is built on three-member towers throughout and records the quark triplets failing at $0.849$ and $0.731$. A six-member set is a different object — the corpus has no instance where the same arithmetic is applied to a set of six — and the crossing gives the corpus its first candidate answer to "is there a scale where the relation holds": yes for the six quarks, at an intermediate scale, and no for the leptons. It also supplies a mechanism-free explanation for why the electron matters (a small member can move the ratio), which is a general fact about $Q$ that the corpus does not state.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §1, as a fourth row in the sector table, and §11.

What stands in the way. The crossing is a two-point interpolation in a quantity whose inputs have wide ranges (the $s$ mass spans a factor 2); the paper itself says the crossing is due to running and calls nothing exact. The six-member $Q$ also has a different lower bound, $1/6$, so the target $2/3$ in that variable is not the same statement as the three-member $2/3$; the corpus would have to introduce the distinction, which the paper does not.

2. The angle $\pi/3$ in six dimensions

The idea. A number: the six-dimensional angle between the amplitude vector and the uniform direction is $\pi/3$, and the three-dimensional one for leptons is $\pi/4$.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2 fixes $\pi/4$ as the lepton target. A second rational angle in a second dimension is a pattern, and the corpus's §5 counting is about dimension and ladder length. If angles are rational multiples of $\pi$ for both sets, the natural question is which rational function of $N$ gives $\pi/4$ at $N=3$ and $\pi/3$ at $N=6$ — a question the corpus has not been in a position to ask.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2, and §11 as a new target.

What stands in the way. $\pi/3$ is quoted with no error bar, and "close to $\pi/3$" on a ratio that is $0.63$ against $0.667$ is not obviously a rational angle rather than a round number. With six masses and no exact relation behind them, the number cannot carry weight.

Rest, in one line each

The lepton running values $\Delta K_\ell\approx1.9\times10^{-3}$ at $M_Z$ agree with the independent computation of Xing–Zhang, $Q_l(M_Z)=1.00188\,Q_l^{pole}$. The light/heavy split is reported and rejected by the author himself. The remark that a high-scale symmetry would make the high-scale deviation smaller, not larger, is a good objection and is not answered.

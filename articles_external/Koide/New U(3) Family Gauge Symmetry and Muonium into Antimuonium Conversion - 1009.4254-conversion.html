What the Paper Does

Sets out a $U(3)$ family gauge model in the Sumino scheme, where the doublets $q_L,\ell_L$ sit in $\mathbf{3}$ and the singlets in $\mathbf{3}^*$, so that gauge-boson exchange produces interactions with $|\Delta N_f|=2$ — two units of one family number, not the usual one. Because the symmetry was introduced to cancel the QED correction to the lepton mass relation, its coupling and mass spectrum are fixed rather than free, and the paper quotes $m_{f11}\sim10^{0\text{–}1}$ TeV and $m_{f12}\sim10^{1\text{–}2}$ TeV. It then works out the signatures: muonium–antimuonium oscillation and the rare kaon decays $K^+\to\pi^+\mu^-e^+$ and $K^+\to\pi^+e^-\mu^+$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

Why the symmetry is there

"According to Sumino's idea, a family $U(3)$ gauge symmetry is assumed. $SU(2)_L$ doublet fields $q_L$ and $\ell_L$ are assigned to $\mathbf{3}$ of $U(3)$, while singlets $u_R$, $d_R$, $e_R$ and $\nu_R$ are assigned to $\mathbf{3}^*$ of $U(3)$. Then, current–current interactions with flavor number violations of $|\Delta N_f|=2$ ($N_f$ is an individual family number) appear via the family gauge boson exchanges."

The masses are not free

"Since the gauge symmetry model has inevitably been brought by Sumino with a specific purpose, the gauge coupling constants $g_f$ and the gauge boson mass spectrum $m_{fij}\equiv m(A^i_j)$ are not free parameters. We estimate $m_{f11}\sim10^{0\text{–}1}$ TeV and $m_{f12}\sim10^{1\text{–}2}$ TeV."

The signatures

"As a possible signature of such the flavor number violating interactions, muonium into antimuonium conversion is discussed together with a rare kaon decay $K^+\to\pi^++\mu^-+e^+$."

What the family basis buys

"in the present model, the family number $i=(1,2,3)$ is defined by $i=(e,\mu,\tau)$ in the charged lepton sector. Only when we assume $i=(e,\mu,\tau)\sim(d,s,b)$ for the down-quark sector, the gauge boson masses $m_{f12}$ can be constrained by the rare kaon decay searches."

The discriminant

"if we find a positive evidence in the $M$–$\bar M$ conversion, and if we find that the mass $m_{f12}$ is lower than the lower limit of $m_{f12}$ from the kaon decays, we can obtain an important clue to the down-quark mixing $U_d$ (not $V_{\rm CKM}$!) at the flavor basis in which the charged lepton mass matrix takes a diagonal form."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. $\Delta N_f=2$ interactions from a $\mathbf{3}$/$\mathbf{3}^*$ assignment

The idea. A mechanism: put doublets and singlets in conjugate representations of the family group and the gauge boson couples only across a generation gap of two, which suppresses the amplitude relative to the usual flavour-changing case and puts the reach at the TeV scale rather than at $10^3$ TeV.

What it would add. articles_physics/charged-lepton-flavour-violation-one-operator-and-the-channels-that-cannot-see-it.md classifies four-fermion operators by the chirality of the fields they contain and finds some channels blind. A family-gauge origin would tie the operator coefficients to one coupling and one mass, replacing the free Wilson coefficients with two numbers and predicting which chirality combinations occur.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no family gauge sector, so nothing can be checked; and the prediction depends on assuming the down-quark family labels coincide with the charged-lepton ones. First, because it would give the corpus's operator list a dynamics.

2. Muonium oscillation at $10^{-12}$

The idea. A number: a conversion probability around $10^{-12}$ for the quoted masses, which is within reach of a dedicated experiment and is the channel that does not need the quark-sector assumption.

What it would add. The corpus discusses $\mu\to e\gamma$ and $\mu$–$e$ conversion but not muonium–antimuonium oscillation, which tests a different combination of couplings — the $|\Delta N_f|=2$ one — and is therefore not redundant with either.

Where it lands. articles_physics/charged-lepton-flavour-violation-one-operator-and-the-channels-that-cannot-see-it.md.

What stands in the way. The rate is model dependent, and the paper's own figure shows the muonium limit is looser than the kaon limits for the same mass. Second.

3. The CKM matrix is not the mixing at the family basis

The idea. A question: if the family basis is fixed by the charged-lepton mass matrix, the quark mixing that enters family-gauge processes is a different matrix $U_d$, and $V_{\rm CKM}$ is the product of two rotations, only one of which appears here.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-ckm-matrix-and-cp-violation-in-biquaternionic-form.md treats $V_{\rm CKM}$ as the complete description of quark mixing. Adding a second basis in which the gauge interaction is diagonal forces the distinction between the physical mixing and the mixing relative to an interaction basis, which the article does not need until a family symmetry exists.

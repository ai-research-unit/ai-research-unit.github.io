What the Paper Does

Asks why the cone's axis is $(1,1,1)$ and why the angle is $\pi/4$, answers by giving the Dirac neutrino sector an equal coupling so that its amplitude vector is parallel to $(1,1,1)$ with $k_\nu^2=1/3$, and then observes that $k_\ell^2+k_\nu^2=1$ — the lepton value and the degenerate-neutrino value are complementary, like $\sin^2+\cos^2$. The same complementarity is imposed between left- and right-handed neutrinos in the see-saw, which fixes $m_{\nu_1}$ and produces a light spectrum and a heavy spectrum. The light masses come out $(0.00107,0.00874,0.05016)$ eV for normal ordering and $(0.048655,0.049423,0.001625)$ eV for inverted, with heavy partners at $10^{14}$–$10^{16}$ GeV. The chain from "why $2/3$" to "the neutrino masses" is a set of declared symmetries, not a derivation, but the numbers are checkable.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The two questions

"The aim of this paper is to give a meaning to the geometric interpretation and to extend Koide's formula to neutrinos such that the masses of left handed and right handed neutrinos can be predicted." The geometry, re-derived: with $\vec V=(\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})$,

$$ \cos\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}\frac{\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}}{\sqrt{m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau}}, \tag{paper 20} $$

"Using Eq.(1), Eq.(20) gives $\cos\theta=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$, making $\theta=\frac{\pi}{4}$."

The complementary relation

"We consider that there exists a symmetry such that the Dirac mass term of the three flavored neutrinos gives invariable result for the three generations", i.e. "$m_{D1}=m_{D2}=m_{D3}$", so that

$$ k_\ell^2=\frac{m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}\right)^2},\qquad k_\nu^2=\frac{m_{D1}+m_{D2}+m_{D3}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D1}}+\sqrt{m_{D2}}+\sqrt{m_{D3}}\right)^2}, \tag{paper 22} $$

and therefore

$$ k_\ell^2+k_\nu^2=1, \tag{paper 23} $$

"which is similar to $\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=1$." And: "The possible range of the coefficient $k_l^2$ in Koide relation is $(1/3,1)$. When three masses are identical (democratic), we have $k_l^2=1/3$; if three masses are strongly hierarchical, then $k_l^2=1$. $k_l^2=2/3$ is just the mean value of these two limits."

The neutrino sector

With $\sum m_i<0.17$ eV and $|\Delta m_{21}^2|=(7.53\pm0.18)\times10^{-5}$ eV$^2$, $|\Delta m_{32}^2|=(2.44\pm0.06)\times10^{-3}$ eV$^2$, the left-handed coefficient

$$ k_{\nu L}^2=\frac{m_{\nu_1}+m_{\nu_2}+m_{\nu_3}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{\nu_1}}+\sqrt{m_{\nu_2}}+\sqrt{m_{\nu_3}}\right)^2} \tag{paper 13} $$

becomes a function of $m_{\nu_1}$ alone. The right-handed coefficient is obtained from the see-saw relation $M_i=m_{Di}^2/m_{\nu i}$, and the same complementarity is imposed,

$$ k_{\nu R}^2+k_{\nu L}^2=1 . \tag{paper 26} $$

"The only value which satisfies Eq.(26) up to three decimal points gives left handed neutrino masses to be" $m_{\nu_1}=0.00107$, $m_{\nu_2}=0.00874$, $m_{\nu_3}=0.05016$ eV, with $M_1=2.8295\times10^{16}$, $M_2=3.4627\times10^{15}$, $M_3=6.0353\times10^{14}$ GeV. For inverted ordering, with $m_{\nu_1}\ge0.04863$, "$m_{\nu_1}=0.048655$ eV, $m_{\nu_2}=0.049423$ eV, $m_{\nu_3}=0.001625$ eV", and $M_1=6.22254\times10^{14}$, $M_2=6.12588\times10^{14}$, $M_3=1.86225\times10^{16}$ GeV.

The see-saw explains $2/3$

"Since mass of the left handed neutrinos are dependent on the mass of the Dirac neutrino and the right-handed neutrinos masses, the Koide like relation for the neutrino would be"

$$ k_L^{\prime\,2}=\frac{k_\nu^2}{k_{\nu R}^2},\qquad k_{\nu R}^2=\frac12,\qquad\text{hence } k_L^{\prime\,2}=\frac23 . \tag{paper 35, 36, 37} $$

"The above relation indicates that the SeeSaw mechanism may be the underlying mechanism ... This relation gives the answer why neutrinos do not satisfy Koide's relation giving $2/3$ as we have for leptons. The reason is that the neutrino masses are the ratio of Dirac and Majorana neutrinos so the ratio of the Koide's formula for these would give the same $2/3$ as for leptons."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. $2/3$ and $1/3$ are complementary, and $2/3$ is the midpoint of its own range

The idea. A result: with the degenerate assignment $m_{D1}=m_{D2}=m_{D3}$ the neutrino coefficient is exactly $1/3$, so $k_\ell^2+k_\nu^2=1$; and since $k^2\in(1/3,1)$, democracy gives the lower end and full hierarchy the upper end, so the observed $2/3$ is exactly the arithmetic mean of the two extremes.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2 has the target $2/3$ and the bound; it does not have the observation that $2/3=(1/3+1)/2$ and that $1/3$ is realised by an exactly degenerate triple. Two independent simple readings of the same number — midpoint of the range, and the complement of a degenerate sector — are cheap to state and give the corpus two more handles on $2/3$ than it has.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2, in the list of readings of $2/3$.

What stands in the way. The midpoint property is arithmetic on the bounds and carries no dynamical content; a value being the mean of its allowed interval is a property of any symmetric parametrisation, and the corpus should mark it as such. The complementarity $k_\ell^2+k_\nu^2=1$ is a stronger claim, but it rests on $m_{D1}=m_{D2}=m_{D3}$, a postulate with no symmetry behind it.

2. The see-saw ratio reproduces $2/3$ across the two neutrino sectors

The idea. A mechanism-shaped relation: $k_L'^2=k_\nu^2/k_{\nu R}^2$ with $k_{\nu R}^2=1/2$ and $k_\nu^2=1/3$ gives $2/3$, so the same number governs the light neutrinos, the heavy neutrinos and the charged leptons.

What it would add. The corpus's neutrino notes treat the light masses as measured inputs and the heavy scale as unknown. This gives a rule linking the two: the heavy sector's coefficient is $1/2$ and the light sector's is $1/3$, and their ratio is the charged-lepton value. If it held, one number would govern three sectors, which is the strongest form of the corpus's uniformity claim.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pmns-matrix-and-lepton-mixing.md, and articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11.

What stands in the way. Both $k_{\nu R}^2=1/2$ and $k_\nu^2=1/3$ are chosen to make the arithmetic work; the first is asserted, the second follows from degenerate Dirac masses. The chain is also not closed: the light masses the paper reports give $k_{\nu L}^2\approx0.489$ and the heavy ones $k_{\nu R}^2\approx0.511$, which satisfy the imposed sum but not the value $2/3$ that (35) demands. The paper notices and keeps both, calling (37) a justification of $2/3$ rather than a formula for the neutrinos.

Rest, in one line each

The light-neutrino spectra $(0.00107,0.00874,0.05016)$ eV and $(0.048655,0.049423,0.001625)$ eV can be compared with the corpus's existing neutrino mass estimates; the inverted case has $\sum m_i\approx0.0997$ eV, close to the cosmological bound. The heavy masses at $10^{14}$–$10^{16}$ GeV are the see-saw inversion of the light ones and inherit their uncertainties. The claim that the result is stable when the Dirac mass is varied over a wide range is stated and not shown.

What the Paper Does

Asks whether the relation holds for neutrinos and quarks, measures the failure by a ratio $k$ in each sector, and then uses the failures to predict the absolute neutrino masses. The exact relation is shown to have no solution for the neutrinos: with the measured mass-squared differences inserted, the equation for the middle mass has no real root, in either ordering. The paper therefore defines $k_l$, $k_\nu$, $k_u$, $k_d$ as the ratio of each sector's $Q$-like quantity to $2/3$, finds $k_l=1$, $k_d\approx1$, $k_\nu<1$, $k_u>1$, conjectures a complementarity $k_l+k_d\approx k_\nu+k_u\approx2$, and from $k_\nu\approx0.75$ derives the three neutrino masses. The prediction is hierarchical, $1.0\times10^{-5}$, $8.4\times10^{-3}$ and $0.05$ eV; it is a conjecture with one input assumption, and the authors call the numbers "primary estimates".

Summary in the Authors' Words

The measured failure

With the PDG values $m_e=0.510998902\pm0.000000021$ MeV, $m_\mu=105.658357\pm0.000005$ MeV and $m_\tau=1776.99^{+0.29}_{-0.26}$ MeV, the leptonic parameter is

$$ k_l\equiv\frac{2}{3}\frac{m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}\right)^2}=1^{+0.00002635}_{-0.00002021}, \tag{paper 2} $$

"which is perfectly close to 1." Foot's angle enters as $k_l=2\cos^2\theta_l$, with $\theta_l=\pi/4$.

The exact relation has no solution for neutrinos

With $\Delta m^2_{atm}=|m_3^2-m_2^2|$ in $1.4$–$3.7\times10^{-3}$ eV$^2$ and $\Delta m^2_{sol}=|m_2^2-m_1^2|$ in $5.4$–$9.5\times10^{-5}$ eV$^2$ at $3\sigma$, best fit points $2.6\times10^{-3}$ and $6.9\times10^{-5}$ eV$^2$, and $m_2>m_1$ fixed by the MSW effect, the two orderings are $m_3>m_2>m_1$ and $m_2>m_1>m_3$. Inserting them into the exact relation gives, for the normal ordering,

$$ \sqrt{m_2^2-\Delta m^2_{sol}}+m_2+\sqrt{m_2^2+\Delta m^2_{atm}} =\frac23\left[\left(m_2^2-\Delta m^2_{sol}\right)^{1/4}+\sqrt{m_2}+\left(m_2^2+\Delta m^2_{atm}\right)^{1/4}\right]^2 \tag{paper 8} $$

and "solving this equation, we find that there is no real root for $m_2$ with the restrictions in Eqs. (3) and (4). This means that no matter what value $m_2$ is, Koide's relation does not hold for neutrinos. So is the inverted mass scheme."

The four $k$'s

$$ k_\nu\equiv\frac{2}{3}\frac{m_1+m_2+m_3}{\left(\sqrt{m_1}+\sqrt{m_2}+\sqrt{m_3}\right)^2}, \tag{paper 9} $$

with the analogous definitions for $k_u$ over $(u,c,t)$ and $k_d$ over $(d,s,b)$. Computed over the PDG ranges $1.5<m_u<4.5$ MeV, $1.0<m_c<1.4$ GeV, $162.9<m_t<188.5$ GeV, $5<m_d<8.5$ MeV, $80<m_s<155$ MeV, $4.0<m_b<4.5$ GeV:

sector range of $k$ verdict
leptons $k_l=1$ holds
neutrinos, normal ordering $0.50<k_\nu<0.85$ fails, $k_\nu<1$
neutrinos, inverted ordering $0.50<k_\nu<0.65$ fails, smaller than normal
$u,c,t$ $1.1<k_u<1.4$ fails, $k_u>1$
$d,s,b$ $0.9<k_d<1.2$ $k_d\approx1$

The paper notes that "because Koide's relation is not energy-scale invariant, the energy scale should be high energy where the current quark masses rather than the constituent quark masses should be adopted".

The complementarity and the masses

"Since $k_l=1$ and $k_d\approx1$, we may conjecture that $k_l+k_d\approx2$", and by analogy

$$ k_\nu+k_u\approx2 . \tag{paper 17} $$

Alternatives are listed and rejected as less simple: $k_lk_d\approx k_\nu k_u\approx1$, $k_l^2+k_d^2\approx2$, $1/k_l+1/k_d\approx2$. With the mean $k_u=1.25$ this gives $k_\nu\approx0.75$, "consistent with the normal mass scheme and in conflict with the inverted mass scheme", so the ordering is $m_3>m_2>m_1$. Solving

$$ 0.75=\frac{2}{3}\frac{\sqrt{m_2^2-6.9\times10^{-5}}+m_2+\sqrt{m_2^2+2.6\times10^{-3}}}{\left[\left(m_2^2-6.9\times10^{-5}\right)^{1/4}+\sqrt{m_2}+\left(m_2^2+2.6\times10^{-3}\right)^{1/4}\right]^2} \tag{paper 18} $$

with all masses in eV gives

$$ m_2=8.4\times10^{-3}\ \text{eV},\qquad m_1=\sqrt{m_2^2-\Delta m^2_{sol}}=1.0\times10^{-5}\ \text{eV},\qquad m_3=\sqrt{m_2^2+\Delta m^2_{atm}}=0.05\ \text{eV}. \tag{paper 19, 20} $$

"the masses of the neutrino mass eigenstates are of different orders of magnitude ($10^{-5}$ eV, $10^{-3}$ eV, and $10^{-2}$ eV), so they are hierarchical, and $m_1$ almost vanish". The authors state the sensitivity: $m_2$ and $m_3$ are stable against $k_\nu$ between $0.7$ and $0.85$, while "the value of $m_1$ may change largely with $k_\nu$" because $m_2^2$ lies close to $\Delta m^2_{sol}$.

Koide's own nonet-based estimate, $m_1=0.0026$, $m_2=0.0075$, $m_3=0.050$ eV, is quoted as agreeing on $m_2$ and $m_3$.

Effective masses and the cosmological sum

With $|V|$ given as $0.84,0.54,0.08$; $0.44,0.56,0.71$; $0.32,0.63,0.71$ and $\langle m\rangle_\alpha\equiv\sqrt{\sum_i m_i^2|V_{\alpha i}|^2}$,

$$ \langle m\rangle_e=6.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{eV},\qquad \langle m\rangle_\mu=3.6\times10^{-2}\ \text{eV},\qquad \langle m\rangle_\tau=3.6\times10^{-2}\ \text{eV}, \tag{paper 23–25} $$

against the bounds $2.2$ eV, $0.19$ MeV and $18.2$ MeV. The sum is $\sum_i m_i=0.058$ eV, against the cosmological bound $<0.71$ eV. "These results should be only taken as primary estimates."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The exact relation cannot hold for neutrinos

The idea. A result with a proof: given the measured $\Delta m^2$ splittings and $m_2>m_1$, the equation $Q=2/3$ for the three neutrino masses has no real root, in either ordering.

What it would add. The corpus states the lepton closure and, in articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, proposes to "test the quark towers". It never records that the neutrino tower cannot carry the same closure at all, which closes off one route before it is walked: a tower of three neutrinos with the measured splittings is excluded by the arithmetic alone, whatever the mechanism. That is the strongest kind of statement the corpus can hold, a falsification that costs one line.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, and articles_physics/the-pmns-matrix-and-lepton-mixing.md, whose subject is the lepton sector's measured parameters.

What stands in the way. Nothing; the result is arithmetic on measured data and can be recomputed in a few lines. Its scope is narrow — it excludes $Q=2/3$ and not $Q=2/N$ for other $N$, nor a closure in a different variable.

2. A one-parameter deviation per sector, and a sum rule tying two of them

The idea. A formula: $k_f\equiv\frac23Q_f$, with $Q_f$ the closure ratio over a sector's three masses, together with the conjectured complementarity $k_l+k_d\approx k_\nu+k_u\approx2$.

What it would add. The corpus has the closure ratio but no dial for a sector that misses it, and its §1 table ($0.849$ and $0.731$ for the quark towers) invites exactly such a dial. The sum rule is a cross-sector statement: articles_physics/the-pmns-matrix-and-lepton-mixing.md measures how differently the two sectors mix and says the difference is unexplained; here the same kind of cross-sector arithmetic is proposed for masses, where the corpus has neither a pattern nor a name for one.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §1, as the column that turns measured $Q_N$ into a deviation, and §11.

What stands in the way. The sum rule is a conjecture with three equally simple alternatives, chosen for simplicity; $k_u$ is a range and not a number, so $k_\nu\approx0.75$ inherits its width. The corpus fixes measured mass splittings, so any use of the rule has to be presented as a hypothesis and not as a determination.

3. The absolute neutrino scale from a closure plus a sum rule

The idea. A number: $m_1=1.0\times10^{-5}$ eV, $m_2=8.4\times10^{-3}$ eV, $m_3=0.05$ eV, $\sum m_i=0.058$ eV, and the three effective masses $6.0\times10^{-3}$, $3.6\times10^{-2}$, $3.6\times10^{-2}$ eV.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-pmns-matrix-and-lepton-mixing.md lists the absolute scale and the ordering as measured-but-unfixed, and articles_physics/the-seesaw-mechanism-and-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.md produces a scale only through a heavy mass that is itself input. These numbers are a prediction of the absolute scale from lepton masses alone, which is the kind of statement the corpus has no example of.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-pmns-matrix-and-lepton-mixing.md, in the section on mass ordering and the absolute scale.

What stands in the way. The prediction depends on one conjectured sum rule, and $m_1$ moves by an order of magnitude within the quoted ranges; the corpus would have to carry it as a conditional, "if $k_l+k_d=k_\nu+k_u$ then", which is a heavier apparatus than the article's measured-data stance allows without a new flagged statement.

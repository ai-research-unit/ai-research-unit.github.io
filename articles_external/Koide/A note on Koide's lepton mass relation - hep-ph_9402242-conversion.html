What the Paper Does

Reads Koide's relation as an angle. Take the vector of square roots of the three charged-lepton masses in ordinary three-dimensional space and the symmetric vector through $(1,1,1)$; the relation is the statement that the angle between them is $\pi/4$. The note also fixes the only theoretical constraint on that angle, $\cos\theta>1/\sqrt3$, reports the measured $\theta=45.000\pm0.001$ degrees, and notes that the relation cannot hold for the bare masses because the electromagnetic corrections are too large for it to be preserved. The angle is an identity, not a derivation: nothing here explains why the angle should be $45$ degrees.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The relation as an angle

"Consider an ordinary three dimensional vector space. On this space we put cartesian co-ordinates. Then the point $(\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})$ and the origin define a vector. Define $\theta$ as the angle between this vector and the symmetric vector (the symmetric vector is the one which passes through the origin and the point $(1,1,1)$). Then Koide's mass relation is equivalent to the statement that $\theta=\pi/4$."

$$ \cos\theta=\frac{(\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})\cdot(1,1,1)}{\left|(\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})\right|\left|(1,1,1)\right|} =\frac{\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}}{\sqrt{m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau}\,\sqrt3} \tag{paper 2, 3} $$

"When $\cos\theta=1/\sqrt2$ then eq.(3) reduces to Koide's formula." The relation is written with the tau mass measured by ARGUS, BES and CLEO, $1776.3\pm2.4\pm1.4$ MeV, $1776.9^{+0.4}_{-0.5}\pm0.2$ MeV and $1777.6\pm0.9\pm1.5$ MeV, which "are in agreement with the prediction $1776.97$ MeV".

The only theoretical constraint, and the measured value

"Theoretically the only constraint on $\theta$ (assuming that at least one lepton has a non-zero mass) is that $\cos\theta>1/\sqrt3$ (which means that $\theta<54.7$ degrees). Putting in the measured lepton mass values, then $\theta=45.000\pm0.001$."

The angle is not special to $2/3$

"If we replaced the $2/3$ factor in Koide's formula with another factor then $\theta$ is nothing special (e.g. if we replaced the $2/3$ with $3/4$ or $1/2$ then this corresponds with $\theta=48.189\ldots$ and $35.264\ldots$ respectively)." So the map from the coefficient to the angle is one to one, and the content of the relation is entirely in the number $2/3$ becoming $45$ degrees.

The relation cannot hold for the bare masses

The relation "is a relation among the physical masses. It doesn't seem possible for the same relation to hold for the bare mass parameters because the electromagnetic corrections are too big for the same relation to hold for the bare parameters. For this reason, it seems difficult to understand the above relation from any simple gauge model since it seems the electromagnetic corrections are inevitable."

The alternatives are left open: "It could be that the mass formula eq.(1) is of no fundamental significance since it is possible that its empirical success is just a numerical coincidence." The author's stated motive is narrower than a derivation: if the formula "has something to do with the real world, then maybe this 'geometrical formulation' of the mass relation may be useful in order to derive the formula from some underlying theory."

1. The closure cannot hold at the bare-mass level

The idea. A claim about scheme: the relation holds for pole masses and cannot hold for the bare parameters, because the electromagnetic corrections are large enough to move the ratio off $2/3$ if the relation were imposed at the Lagrangian level. Any derivation must therefore produce the relation at the pole, not in the bare Lagrangian.

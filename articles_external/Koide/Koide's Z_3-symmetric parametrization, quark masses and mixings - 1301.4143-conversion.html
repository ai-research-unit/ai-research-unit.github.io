What the Paper Does

Takes up the suggestion that the closure holds not for the physical quark masses but for pseudo-masses — the masses rotated back to the weak basis, where the relation would be exact. With the phase parameters fixed by the lepton sector to $\delta_U=2/27$, $\delta_D=4/27$, and the Fritzsch–Xing decomposition of the mixing matrices, the paper turns the closure condition into two constraints linking the angles of the up and down rotations. Those constraints predict the CKM angle combination $\theta=\theta_b-\theta_t$ from the measured $\theta_u,\theta_d$: the prediction is $2.98^\circ$ against $2.37^\circ$ measured, and a small departure $k_{D}=k_{U}=1.015$ brings it to $2.44^\circ$. The result is a mass-mixing link, not a derivation: the phase values are assumed, the decomposition is assumed, and the agreement is at the few per cent level.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The "doubly special" parametrisation

The closure in the $Z_3$ form,

$$ \frac{m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau}\right)^2}=\frac{1+k_L^2}{3}, \tag{paper 1} $$

with $k_L=1$ predicting $m_\tau=1776.9689$ MeV against $1776.82\pm0.16$ measured. The parametrisation

$$ \sqrt{m_{f_j}}=\sqrt{M_f}\left(1+\sqrt2\,k_f\cos\left(\frac{2\pi j}{3}+\delta_f\right)\right), \tag{paper 4} $$

is "particularly suited to Koide's formula as not only $M_f$ but also $\delta_f$ drop out of the r.h.s. of Eq. (1)". Conversely, solving for the phase kills $k_L$:

$$ \frac{\sqrt3\left(\sqrt{m_\mu}-\sqrt{m_e}\right)}{2\sqrt{m_\tau}-\sqrt{m_\mu}-\sqrt{m_e}}=\tan\delta_L, \qquad \delta_L=0.2222324\approx\frac29, \tag{paper 5, 6, 7} $$

and $\delta_L=2/9$ predicts "the value of the tauon mass ... $m_\tau=1776.9664$ MeV", in agreement with measurement. "Assuming that the Koide and the Brannen–Rosen observations do not reflect mere coincidences, the $Z_3$-symmetric parametrization (4) should be rightly called 'doubly special'."

"A peculiar feature of this parametrization is that the simple numbers of $1$ and $2/9$ work well at the low-energy scale and not at some high mass scale. For example, taking the values of charged-lepton masses at the mass scale of $M_Z$, the extracted values of $k_L$ and $\delta_L$ deviate from their 'perfect' values of $1$ and $2/9$ by about $0.2\%$ and $0.5\%$ respectively."

Phases in the quark sectors

"It has been observed recently from the quark sector analogs of Eq. (5) that at the low-energy scale the relevant phase parameters acquire approximate values:"

$$ \delta_U\approx\frac{2}{27}=\frac{\delta_L}{3},\qquad \delta_D\approx\frac{4}{27}=\frac{2\delta_L}{3}, \tag{paper 9} $$

so that "$\delta_L=3\delta_D/2=3\delta_U=2/9$". "Due to the problem of quark confinement we obviously cannot check how precise the above equalities are. However, given the accuracy of their lepton counterpart (Eq. (8)) we may expect that they are nearly exact."

The closure in the weak basis

"Here we accept the suggestion of ref. [12] that $k_f=1$ is a feature of the weak basis. Thus ... the masses of charged leptons are described by $k_L=1$ because for charged leptons the mass and the weak bases coincide", so that $U_L=\mathbf{1}$ and $U_\nu=V_{MNS}$. "In other words, if $U_L$ were different from $1$, one would not expect the simplicity of Koide's formula to persist." For quarks both $U_U$ and $U_D$ differ from the identity, and the relevant objects are the pseudo-masses

$$ \tilde m_{f_j}=\Big|\sum_k U_{f,jk}\,m_{f_k}\Big|, \tag{paper 14} $$

"it is for these 'pseudo-masses' that ... the analogs of Koide's formula (1) are supposed to hold with $k_D=k_U=1$". The original authors of that suggestion "used quark masses at the $Z$ mass scale and found that it is possible to get $k_D=k_U\approx1$ provided one takes a value of the strange quark mass $m_s(Z)$ that is larger by a factor of $2.5$ (!) from the theoretical estimate at that scale."

The mixing angles and the prediction

With the Fritzsch–Xing forms

$$ U_D=R_{23}\left(\varphi_b,\theta_b\right)R_{12}\left(\theta_d\right),\qquad U_U=R_{23}\left(\varphi_t,\theta_t\right)R_{12}\left(\theta_u\right), \tag{paper 15} $$

the CKM matrix comes out as

$$ V_{CKM}=\begin{pmatrix} s_us_dc+c_uc_de^{-i\varphi} & s_uc_dc-c_us_de^{-i\varphi} & s_us\\ c_us_dc-s_uc_de^{-i\varphi} & c_uc_dc+s_us_de^{-i\varphi} & c_us\\ -s_ds & -c_ds & c \end{pmatrix}, \tag{paper 20} $$

with $\varphi=\varphi_b-\varphi_t$, $\theta=\theta_b-\theta_t$. From the measured $|V_{ub}|=0.00351\pm0.00015$, $|V_{cb}|=0.0412\pm0.0008$, $|V_{td}|=0.00867\pm0.00030$, $|V_{ts}|=0.0404\pm0.0008$, $|V_{tb}|=0.999146\pm0.000034$:

$$ \theta_u=4.87^\circ\pm0.23^\circ,\quad \theta_d=12.11^\circ\pm0.47^\circ,\quad \theta=\theta_b-\theta_t=2.37^\circ\pm0.05^\circ . \tag{paper 22} $$

The low-energy spectrum is fixed by the phases and the ratios $m_s/m_d=20.4$, $m_u/m_d=0.56$ up to two scales; a representative choice is $m_d=7.843$, $m_s=160.0$, $m_b=4209$, $m_u=4.392$, $m_c=1296$, $m_t=172000$ MeV. The closure condition on the pseudo-masses,

$$ \frac{\sum_j\tilde m_{f_j}}{\left(\sum_j\sqrt{\tilde m_{f_j}}\right)^2}=\frac{1+k_f^2}{3}, \tag{paper 24} $$

then "imposes two constraints: between $\theta_d$ and $\theta_b$ in the down quark sector, and between $\theta_u$ and $\theta_t$ in the up quark sector"; only one of the two solutions has a positive $\theta_b-\theta_t$, and

$$ \theta_{Koide}=2.98^\circ \tag{paper 25} $$

"which is only slightly different from the value given in Eq. (22)". A departure $k_D=k_U=1.015$ gives $\theta\approx2.44^\circ$, "in good agreement with experiment".

"In conclusion, the data are consistent with the statement that low-energy quark masses satisfy phase relations $\delta_D=2\delta_U=4/27$, while the expected Koide relations $k_U=k_D=1$ hold approximately for masses transformed to the weak basis."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The closure applied to pseudo-masses, not to masses

The idea. A mechanism-free structural claim: the relation may hold for the masses in the weak basis — $|\sum_k U_{jk}m_k|$ — while failing for the masses the detector measures; the failure is then a rotation, not a spectrum.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md defines the physical masses as the eigenvalues of $M_f^\dagger M_f$ and treats the diagonalising matrices as the mixing. This paper puts a closure on the uneigendata combination instead, which makes the relation a condition on the mass matrix in a fixed basis rather than on its spectrum. That is a real change of object, and it is the only proposal in this folder in which the closure is compatible with the mixing data by construction rather than by accident.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md, in the section that separates the eigenvalues from the mixing, and articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11.

What stands in the way. "Pseudo-mass" is basis-dependent, and the corpus's framework has one preferred basis — the one in which the mass matrix is written; adopting a second basis-dependent mass is a statement the corpus would have to make carefully or not at all. The proposal is also not derived: the choice $k_f=1$ in the weak basis is a guess, and the paper concedes that the original version needed the strange mass at $M_Z$ to be $2.5$ times its computed value.

2. Two regularities in one triple, both broken by running

The idea. A number: at low scale $k_L=1$ and $\delta_L=2/9$, predicting $1776.9689$ and $1776.9664$ MeV respectively; at $M_Z$ the same two parameters drift by $0.2\%$ and $0.5\%$, so the double coincidence is a low-scale phenomenon.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11's first task asks whether the relation survives running; the answer here is that it does not survive exactly, and that a second, independent regularity drifts twice as fast. The ratio of the two drifts ($0.5\%$ against $0.2\%$) is a number a mechanism would have to reproduce, and the corpus holds no such pair.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2 and §11.

What stands in the way. The $0.5\%$ figure is quoted without derivation, and the conversion of Li–Ma and Xing–Zhang already carry the $0.2\%$ for $k_L$; adding the phase drift requires a running computation the corpus does not have.

3. A closure condition that predicts a mixing angle

The idea. A result with a number: imposing the closure on the pseudo-masses ties $\theta_b$ to $\theta_d$ and $\theta_t$ to $\theta_u$, and so predicts $\theta=\theta_b-\theta_t=2.98^\circ$ against $2.37^\circ\pm0.05^\circ$ measured — a $25\%$ miss in the angle, repaired to $2.44^\circ$ by $k=1.015$.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-ckm-matrix-and-cp-violation-in-biquaternionic-form.md lists the angles as measured inputs with no relation among them; articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11 asks whether the $45^\circ$ form can be tested on another closing triple and gets nothing. This is the only case in the folder where a mass relation predicts a mixing number, which is precisely the kind of cross-quantity link the corpus's programme is looking for.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-ckm-matrix-and-cp-violation-in-biquaternionic-form.md, and articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11 as a target of the right kind.

What stands in the way. The prediction misses by $0.6^\circ$ — twelve sigma on the quoted error — and the repair costs two new parameters ($k_D$, $k_U$) that are themselves fitted. Read strictly, the closure on pseudo-masses is excluded at $k=1$ and survives only as a two-parameter fit, which the corpus should record as such.

Rest, in one line each

The Fritzsch–Xing decomposition and the resulting CKM form (paper 20) are a standard parametrisation, useful as a template. The remark that a high-scale symmetry should make high-scale deviations smaller, not larger, is not raised here but follows from the $M_Z$ numbers. The factor $2.5$ in the strange mass is a warning the paper states and does not resolve.

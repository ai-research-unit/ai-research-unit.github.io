What the Paper Does

Names the problem precisely and solves it inside a model. If the relation is true of running masses at a high scale, then the pole masses miss it by $0.1\%$ — one hundred and twenty times the experimental error — because the QED correction has a term $-\tfrac{3\alpha}{4\pi}\bar m_i\log\bar m_i^2$ that changes the direction of the amplitude vector, not just its length. Sumino shows that a $U(3)$ family gauge symmetry, with the charged-lepton mass matrix proportional to $\langle\Phi\rangle^2$ and the leptons in conjugate representations, generates a correction of exactly the same form with the opposite sign, so that $\alpha=\alpha_F/4$ makes the two cancel. Then $m_i^{pole}\propto v_i^2$ at every scale, and the relation holds wherever the $v_i$ satisfy it — including at the pole. This is the only mechanism in the folder that makes the pole-mass version of the relation stable, and it is the reason the corpus's question "why the pole masses and not the running masses" has a candidate answer.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The correction that has to be cancelled

Given

$$ m_i^{pole}=\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\left(\frac34\log\frac{\mu^2}{\bar m_i(\mu)^2}+1\right)\right)\bar m_i(\mu), \tag{paper 2} $$

"Suppose $\bar m_i(\mu)$ ... satisfy the relation (1) at scale $\mu\gg M_W$. Then $m_i^{pole}$ do not satisfy the same relation: Eq. (1) is corrected by approximately $0.1\%$, which is $120$ times larger than the present experimental error. Note that this correction originates only from the term $-3\alpha/(4\pi)\times\bar m_i\log(\bar m_i^2)$ ... since the other terms, which are of the form const. $\times\bar m_i$, do not affect the relation (1)." And: "if there is indeed a physical origin to Koide's mass formula at a high energy scale, we need to account for a correction to the relation (1) that cancels the QED correction ... there is a difficulty in explaining why the size of such a correction should coincide accurately with the size of the QED correction which arises from much lower scales. Up to date, no mechanism has been proposed to solve this problem."

The mechanism

The mass matrix is generated by the dimension-6 operator

$$ O=\frac{\kappa(\mu)}{\Lambda^2}\,\bar\psi_{Li}\Phi_{ik}\Phi_{kj}\phi\,e_{Rj}, \tag{paper 4} $$

so that $M_\ell^{tree}=\frac{\kappa v_{ew}}{2\sqrt2\Lambda}\langle\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi\rangle$ and $\sqrt{m_i}\propto v_i$. The family gauge interaction then corrects the pole mass by

$$ \delta m_i^{pole}=-\frac{3\alpha_F}{8\pi}\left(\log\frac{\mu^2}{v_i(\mu)^2}+c\right)m_i(\mu), \tag{paper 13} $$

"Note that $|v_i|^2$ in the argument of logarithm originates from the gauge boson masses, which are invariant under $v_i\to v_i\exp(i\varphi_i)$", and "noting $\log v_i^2=\frac12\log m_i^2+$ const., one observes that if a relation between the QED and family gauge coupling constants"

$$ \alpha=\frac14\alpha_F \tag{paper 19} $$

"is satisfied, the 1-loop radiative correction induced by family gauge interaction cancels the 1-loop QED correction to Koide's mass formula." Then

$$ m_i^{pole}\propto v_i(\mu)^2 \tag{paper 20} $$

"holds with a good accuracy. This is valid for any value of $\mu$. This means, if $v_i(\mu)$ satisfy"

$$ \frac{v_1+v_2+v_3}{\sqrt{3\left(v_1^2+v_2^2+v_3^2\right)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2} \tag{paper 21} $$

"at some scale $\mu$, Koide's formula is satisfied at any scale $\mu$. This is a consequence of the fact that $\Phi$ is multiplicatively renormalized."

Two conditions make this work: the leptons sit in conjugate representations ($\psi_L$ in $\mathbf{3}$, $e_R$ in $\bar{\mathbf3}$, so that the dimension-4 Yukawa is forbidden and no universal correction appears), and the two gauge couplings are equal, $\alpha_{U(1)}=\alpha_{SU(3)}=\alpha_F$, which is guaranteed if $U(3)$ sits inside a simple group such as $SU(4)$ down to a nearby scale.

Why the condition might hold

"The relation (19) shows that the value of $\alpha_F$ is close to that of the weak gauge coupling constant $\alpha_W$, since $\sin^2\theta_W(M_W)$ is close to $1/4$. In fact, within the SM, $\frac14\alpha_W(\mu)$ approximates $\alpha(m_\tau)$ at scale $\mu\sim10^2$–$10^3$ TeV." If the family group is unified with $SU(2)_L$ near that scale, "we expect that $\alpha\approx\frac14\alpha_F$ is satisfied ... even if the unification scale is varied within a factor of 3, Koide's mass formula is satisfied within the present experimental accuracy. This shows the level of parameter tuning required in this scenario."

The price and the signal

The naive estimate, before the cancellation, is that "$\alpha_F/\pi$ should be order $10^{-5}$", i.e. the family interaction must be feeble; the cancellation removes that tuning but introduces the coincidence of scales. The model needs extra fermions to cancel anomalies, and it predicts lepton-flavour violation at $10^2$–$10^3$ TeV: with down quarks in the same representation as the leptons and simultaneous diagonalisation, $\Gamma(K_L\to e\mu)\approx m_\mu^2m_{K_L}f_K^2/(16\pi v_2^4)$, and the bound $\mathrm{Br}(K_L\to e\mu)<4.7\times10^{-12}$ gives $v_2\gtrsim5\times10^2$ TeV, which "may be marginally in conflict with the estimated unification scale".

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A symmetry that cancels the QED correction, so the pole masses are the natural home of the relation

The idea. A mechanism with a condition: the $0.1\%$ QED shift is not noise to be ignored but the obstruction, and a family gauge interaction with $\alpha_F=4\alpha$ produces the same logarithm with the opposite sign; with $m_i\propto v_i^2$ and multiplicative renormalisation of $\Phi$, the relation then holds at every scale and hence at the pole.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11 asks whether the relation is a high-scale or a low-scale statement, and the corpus's other conversions answer low — with running as an enemy. This paper is the only one in the folder that makes the pole-mass statement stable, which converts the corpus's most awkward fact (the relation holds at the pole and fails when run up) from an accident into the signature of a mechanism. It also gives the corpus an explicit criterion that any mechanism must meet: a correction that moves the amplitude vector's direction, not just its length, and with the right coefficient.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, first item, and articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md for the $M\propto\langle\Phi\rangle^2$ texture.

What stands in the way. The mechanism needs $U(3)$ gauge symmetry at $10^2$–$10^3$ TeV, unification with $SU(2)_L$, new fermions for anomalies, and — by the author's own admission — a tuned coincidence between two couplings at unrelated scales. It explains stability and not the value $2/3$; the value comes from the potential, which is the next paper.

2. The $\tfrac14$ between the two couplings, and where it comes from

The idea. A number: $\alpha=\alpha_F/4$, and $\frac14\alpha_W(m_\tau)$ reproduces $\alpha$ at $10^2$–$10^3$ TeV, with $\sin^2\theta_W\approx1/4$ supplying the factor.

What it would add. If the corpus wants a coefficient to derive, this is a candidate: the same $\tfrac14$ that appears in $\sin^2\theta_W$ fixes the ratio of the family and electromagnetic couplings. It is a dimensionless relation between three couplings, of the kind the corpus's §5 counts, and it converts the "why $2/3$" question into a "why $\tfrac14$ and $2/3$" question, which may be easier to attack.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §5.

What stands in the way. The factor $4$ is fixed by the representation choice (the log coefficient $-\tfrac38\alpha_F$ against $-\tfrac{3}{4}\alpha$) and is therefore a group-theory bookkeeping, not a coincidence; the coincidence is only that $\alpha_F$ lands near $\alpha_W$ at $10^2$–$10^3$ TeV, which the author calls an accident he cannot avoid.

What the Paper Does

Proves an exact theorem: given a mass tuple and adding one further mass, the extended ratio $Q$ has a unique minimum, at $m_*=(\sum m_i)/(\sum\sqrt{m_i})^2$, with the value $Q_0/(1+Q_0)$. For the charged leptons this selects $m_*=1.25534(16)$ GeV and $Q_{4,\min}=0.3999978$; in the idealised case $Q_\ell=2/3$ the four-body minimum is exactly $2/5$. The theorem also gives two reformulations the corpus will want: $Q$ is a purity measure on the amplitude simplex, so $N_{\rm eff}\equiv1/Q$ counts participants and the optimal extension adds exactly one; and $Q_\ell$ is exactly invariant under one-loop QED running, because all three leptons share one anomalous dimension. The phenomenology — the charm mass sitting $1.4\%$ above $m_*$ and $Q(e,\mu,\tau,c)=0.4000025$, $6.2$ ppm from $2/5$ — is calibrated against a scan of $12{,}720$ trials and ranked, honestly, at $33/12{,}720$ raw and $6/756$ within fermions.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The theorem

With $R_1=\sum r_i$, $R_2=\sum r_i^2$, $Q_0=R_2/R_1^2$, and one added amplitude $r$, "$Q_{N+1}$ has a unique global minimum at"

$$ r^*=\frac{R_2}{R_1}=Q_0R_1,\qquad m^*=(r^*)^2=\frac{\left(\sum_im_i\right)^2}{\left(\sum_i\sqrt{m_i}\right)^2}, \tag{paper 3} $$

"and the minimum value is"

$$ Q_{\min}=\frac{Q_0}{1+Q_0}. \tag{paper 4} $$

The proof is one derivative, $\mathrm dQ_{N+1}/\mathrm dr=2R_1(r-r^*)/(R_1+r)^3$. Since $Q_0\ge1/N$ for any positive $N$-tuple, "$Q_{\min}=Q_0/(1+Q_0)\ge1/(N+1)$, with equality only in the equal-mass limit". For $k$ added masses the same argument gives "$Q_{\min}^{(k)}=Q_0/(1+kQ_0)$", with all new amplitudes equal. "Thus repeated exact minimization keeps returning to the same optimal scale and approaches the large-$N$ lower bound $Q_N\sim1/N$ from above."

Three readings of $r^*$: it is the $r_i$-weighted barycenter of the base amplitudes, $\sum_i r_i(r^*-r_i)=0$; it is the point where the extended line is least deviating from the democratic direction; and it is a purity statement, since with $p_i=r_i/R_1$,

$$ Q_0=\sum_i p_i^2=\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho^2\right), \qquad N_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac1Q,\qquad N_{\rm eff,max}=N_{\rm eff,0}+1 . \tag{paper 16, 17} $$

"For the base tuple $N_{\rm eff,0}=1/Q_0$ ... in the idealised case $Q_\ell^K=2/3$ one has $N_{\rm eff,0}=3/2$; after the extension $N_{\rm eff,max}=5/2$." And a third: "the normalized peak is an exact Lorentzian, $N_{\rm eff}/N_{\rm eff,max}=1/(1+u^2)$", with $u$ the share-mismatch coordinate.

The lepton benchmark

With PDG 2024 pole masses, "$m_*^\ell=1.25534(16)$ GeV" and

$$ Q_{4,\min}^{\exp}=0.3999978(43),\qquad Q_{4,\min}^{K}=\frac{2}{5}. $$

The charm coincidence, and its calibration

"Using charged-lepton pole masses together with the short-distance charm mass $m_c(m_c)$, the PDG 2024 charm input lies $1.4\%$ above $m_*^\ell$ and gives"

$$ Q(e,\mu,\tau,c)=0.4000025(64), \tag{paper 31} $$

"which is $11.7$ ppm above $Q_{4,\min}^{\exp}$ and $6.2$ ppm above the idealised value $2/5$." The Lorentzian coordinate of the charm is $u_c=+0.0034(17)$, while $u$ for the $u,d,s$ quarks is $-0.76,-0.74,-0.50$ and for $W,Z,H,t$ it is $+0.90$ to $+0.99$. Scheme dependence: $m_c(2\ \mathrm{GeV})$ gives $+1119$ ppm, the pole mass $+5013$ ppm, $m_c(M_Z)$ $+27{,}429$ ppm, so "the observation must be treated as a mixed-definition phenomenological coincidence rather than a scheme-independent relation".

The look-elsewhere scan of all non-neutrino two- and three-body seeds with one added mass places the lepton-plus-charm continuation "33/12,720 in the raw trial set, 24/2,640 after collapsing repeated scale realizations, and 6/756 within the fermion-only collapsed subset".

The running

"At one-loop QED, all three charged leptons share the same mass anomalous dimension ... because they carry the same electric charge. Their masses therefore run by a common multiplicative factor", so

$$ Q_\ell(\mu)=\frac{R_2(\mu)}{R_1(\mu)^2}=Q_\ell , \qquad m_*^\ell(\mu)=R(\mu)^2m_*^\ell . \tag{paper 35} $$

"The Koide ratio is thus exactly RG-invariant at one-loop QED" and "$m_*^\ell(\mu)$ varies monotonically from $1.255$ GeV at $\mu=1$ GeV to $1.213$ GeV at $\mu=10$ TeV, a $3\%$ excursion over four decades, and crosses no Standard Model mass within that range." The $1.9\times10^{-3}$ shift of $Q_\ell$ seen at $M_Z$ is "therefore a subleading effect (higher-loop QED, weak corrections, and pole-to-MS scheme conversion)".

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. $Q$ is a purity, and the optimal extension adds one participant

The idea. A theorem with three numbers: $Q=\sum p_i^2$ for amplitude shares $p_i$, so $N_{\rm eff}=1/Q$ is the effective number of participants; $2/3$ means $N_{\rm eff}=3/2$; adding one optimally chosen mass raises $N_{\rm eff}$ by exactly $1$, and adding $k$ equal ones by $k$, with minimum $Q_0/(1+kQ_0)$.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md treats $2/3$ as a target and §5 counts members of a ladder. This gives both a meaning for the target ($3/2$ participants out of $3$) and an exact rule for what happens when the ladder grows — which is precisely the fourth-rung question, answered before any dynamics: an optimal rung moves the ratio to $2/5$, and $2/5$ is what the four-mass set with charm nearly achieves. It also gives the corpus a clean statement of why $Q<1$ always and why $Q\ge1/N$.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §2 (the meaning of $2/3$) and §9 (the fourth rung), with a mention in §5.

What stands in the way. Nothing: the theorem is elementary and exact, and it is checkable in three lines. The corpus should adopt it, with the caution that it is a statement about one particular functional and not about mass matrices.

2. The running puzzle dissolves: one-loop QED leaves $Q$ exactly invariant

The idea. A result: all three charged leptons have the same electric charge, hence the same one-loop QED anomalous dimension, hence a common multiplicative factor, hence $Q(\mu)=Q$ exactly. The $0.19\%$ drift seen at $M_Z$ is not QED running of $Q$ but the residual flavour-dependent pieces and the pole-to-MS conversion.

What it would add. This resolves an apparent contradiction the corpus currently carries: Li–Ma report scale independence, Xing–Zhang report a $0.19\%$ shift, and Sumino's $0.1\%$ is a pole-versus-$\overline{MS}$ effect. All three are consistent, and the corpus can now state the sharp version: the leading running is flavour-blind and cannot move $Q$; only flavour-dependent corrections can, and they are two-loop or Yukawa in origin, of relative size $10^{-3}$. That is a better question than "does it run".

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, first item, and articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md.

What stands in the way. The statement is one loop; the corpus should not overstate it. And it applies to the leptons because of the charge degeneracy — for quarks the QCD anomalous dimension is also common, so the same argument applies, which is the point Rivero makes about quotient tuples.

3. The four-mass target $2/5$, and a charm coincidence graded honestly

The idea. A number with a rank: $Q(e,\mu,\tau,c)=0.4000025$, $6.2$ ppm from $2/5$; among $756$ fermion-only continuations it ranks sixth; other charm schemes miss by $10^{3}$–$10^{4}$ ppm.

What it would add. The corpus has no instance where a physical mass sits within $10^{-5}$ of a target derived from a relation, and no instance of a look-elsewhere correction either. Both are worth having as a template: the claim is stated with its scheme, its uncertainty, its rank, and its refusal to be called a law. The corpus's own standard for admitting a numerical coincidence should look like this.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §9, as the best existing candidate for a fourth rung, and §11.

What stands in the way. The comparison mixes pole lepton masses with a short-distance charm mass; the author says so repeatedly, and the whole result moves by $10^{3}$ ppm under a different charm scheme. It is a benchmark, not a relation.

Rest, in one line each

The Lorentzian coordinate $u$ is an exact reparameterisation and is a convenient way to quote "how close" for any candidate rung. The recursive application to the lepton-plus-charm set returns $m_{*,5}=1.2624(36)$ GeV, compatible with charm at $1.1\sigma$, so the fourth rung does not point at a fifth. The generalised functionals $R_{k,q}$ inherit the theorem by change of variables.

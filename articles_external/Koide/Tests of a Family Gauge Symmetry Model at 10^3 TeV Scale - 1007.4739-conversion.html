What the Paper Does

Takes the $U(3)$ family gauge model — the one whose purpose is to cancel the QED correction to the lepton mass relation — and derives what present experiments already exclude. Because the coupling and the boson masses are fixed by the charged-lepton masses, the model makes definite predictions, and the authors extract lower bounds on $m_{f12}$ from $K^0$–$\bar K^0$ and $D^0$–$\bar D^0$ mixing and from rare kaon decays. They find $m_{f12}\gtrsim10^3$ TeV without tuning, but $10^2$ TeV in a non-negligible region of the mixing-matrix parameter space. They then examine the collider signatures and find the cross sections too small.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"Based on a specific model with $U(3)$ family gauge symmetry at $10^3$ TeV scale, we show its experimental signatures to search for. Since the gauge symmetry is introduced with a special purpose, its gauge coupling constant and gauge boson mass spectrum are not free."

The observable

"The current structure in this model leads to family number violations via exchange of extra gauge bosons. We investigate present constraints from flavor changing processes and discuss visible signatures at LHC and lepton colliders."

The bounds

"Generally (without tuning of the mixing matrices) we find $m_{f12}\gtrsim10^3$ TeV from the $K^0$–$\bar K^0$ and $D^0$–$\bar D^0$ mixing. However, $m_{f12}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^2\ \mathrm{TeV})$ is also viable in a non-negligible range in the parameter space of the mixing matrices, which is consistent with the bounds from the rare kaon decay searches."

Where the CP constraint bites

"The very stringent bounds from the CP violation in the $K^0$–$\bar K^0$ mixing rule out contributions from CP phases in the down-type quark mixing matrices to this process. Hence, we restrict our analysis to the real (orthogonal) down-type quark mixing matrices."

The collider verdict

"Although a top-top production at LHC (via $u^+u\to t^+t$) is very attractive, the cross section $\sim10^{-6}$ pb at $\sqrt s=14$ TeV and for $m_{f13}=10^2$ TeV would be too small to detect the signal. The cross section for $e^-+p\to\mu^-+X$ amounts to $\sigma\sim10^{-5}$ pb at $E_p=7$ TeV and $E_e=400$ GeV for $m_{f12}=50$ TeV, which would also be difficult to detect."

Why the test matters

"At present, the cancellation mechanism based on $U(3)$ family gauge symmetry is the only known one as a possible explanation for $K(\mu)=K^{\rm pole}$. Therefore, tests of the model are urgently required."

1. A model whose only free choice is the mixing matrix

The idea. A mechanism for making a model testable: fix the new coupling from an existing relation, fix the new masses from the known fermion masses, and the only remaining freedom is a mixing matrix that is already measured in another basis.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §10 says the relation has no dynamics and §11 lists what would settle something. That is the missing template between "the number holds" and "here is a model".

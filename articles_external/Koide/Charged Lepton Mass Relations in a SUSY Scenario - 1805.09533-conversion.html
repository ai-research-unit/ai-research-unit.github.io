What the Paper Does

Takes the two relations $K=2/3$ and $\kappa=1/486$, writes both as ratios of traces of the family nonet $\Phi$ with $m_{ei}\propto\sum_k\Phi_{ik}\Phi_{ki}$, and asks whether the potentials that produce them survive renormalisation-group running. In a non-supersymmetric model they do not. The authors show that in a supersymmetric version the two ratios are stable, although the superpotential itself is corrected, and they give simple superpotential forms that realise the relations. The mechanism is presented as a modification of Sumino's cancellation.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The two relations as trace ratios

"The observed charged lepton masses satisfy the relations $K\equiv(m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau)/(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau})^2=2/3$ and $\kappa\equiv\sqrt{m_em_\mu m_\tau}/(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau})^3=1/486$ with great accuracy. These parameters are given as $K=(\mathrm{Tr}[\Phi\Phi])/(\mathrm{Tr}[\Phi])^2$ and $\kappa=\det\Phi/(\mathrm{Tr}[\Phi])^3$ if the charged lepton masses $m_{ei}$ are given by $m_{ei}\propto\sum_k\Phi_{ik}\Phi_{ki}$."

The problem with the non-SUSY potentials

"Simple scalar potential forms to realize the relations have been already proposed in non-supersymmetric scenarios, but the potential forms are not stable against the renormalization group effects."

The result

"In this paper, we examine supersymmetric scenarios and find that the parameters $K$ and $\kappa$ are made stable against the effects in a very nontrivial way, even though the superpotential itself (in the canonical basis) suffers the usual corrections. We also show possible simple superpotential forms for the relations."

Where it sits relative to Sumino

"Since our models stand on the modified Sumino mechanism, it is predicted that the features discussed in Refs. [8, 10] will be observed. In addition, more detailed analyses on some specific topics, such as family gauge bosons with visible lower mass at the LHC [11] and the $\mu$–$e$ conversion [12], are also applied."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Stability of a relation under running as a selection rule

The idea. A method: a relation among masses counts as explained only if the structure that produces it is stable under renormalisation, not merely true at tree level; supersymmetry is used here as the tool that removes the vertex corrections which spoil it.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11 lists "run the masses to a common scale and re-evaluate $Q$" as a low-cost task with the outcome unknown. This paper gives the reason the answer matters and a mechanism that makes the answer scale-free: if the relation is produced by ratios of traces of a nonet, its running is not that of the individual masses. The corpus note treats running as an open question about numbers; this turns it into a question about the structure of the mass matrix.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §11, first row, and the conversion of Sumino's cancellation paper in this folder.

What stands in the way. The claim of stability is asserted for a specific class of superpotentials with a specific field content, and nothing is computed for the Standard Model spectrum; the corpus has no supersymmetric sector at all. First, because it changes the status of the corpus's own open question.

2. $K$ and $\kappa$ as the two invariants of a $3\times3$ matrix

The idea. A number: with $m_i\propto(\Phi^2)_{ii}$, the ratios $(\mathrm{Tr}\,\Phi^2)/(\mathrm{Tr}\,\Phi)^2$ and $\det\Phi/(\mathrm{Tr}\,\Phi)^3$ are the two independent scale-invariant traces, so the two relations are the statement that both take special values.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md counts thirteen parameters in the flavour sector and lists the mass eigenvalues as three of them, with no invariant description of what a "special" spectrum would be. Two trace ratios whose measured values are $2/3$ and $1/486.663$ give such a description, and they are basis independent, unlike the eigenvalues.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md, "The Mass Spectrum and Its Gaps".

What stands in the way. The identification $m_i\propto(\Phi^2)_{ii}$ is a model assumption; the corpus's mass matrix is $Y^fv/\sqrt2$ with no nonet. Second.

3. $\kappa$ as a second measured closure

The idea. A number: $1/486$ for the leptons, to be added beside $2/3$.

What it would add. The three existing conversions of Sumino's papers in this folder discuss $K$ alone; a second relation doubles the number of constraints a mechanism must satisfy and therefore the strength of any test.

Where it lands. articles_external/Koide/Family Gauge Symmetry and Koide's Mass Formula - 0812.2090-conversion.md, where the cancellation is derived for $K$ only.

What stands in the way. The measured value $1/486.663$ differs from $1/486$ in the fourth digit, which is far outside the accuracy claimed for $K$; a mechanism tuned to $K$ leaves $\kappa$ as a check that does not obviously pass. Third.

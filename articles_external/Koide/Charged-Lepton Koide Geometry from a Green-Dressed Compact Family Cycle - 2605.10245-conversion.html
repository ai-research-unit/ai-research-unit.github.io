What the Paper Does

Builds an effective theory on a compact internal circle in which the family amplitude is sampled at the three $Z_3$ points, and in which the $45^\circ$ cone comes from one assumption (equal singlet and doublet norms) while the number $2/9$ comes from a different calculation: the Green's function sum of the circle evaluated at a $Z_3$ displacement. That sum is an exact lattice constant, $\sum_{n\ge1}(1-\cos2\pi n/3)/n^2=2\pi^2/9$, so the phase is $\theta_\ell=-2\lambda_B/9$ with $\lambda_B$ an undetermined matching coefficient. At the unit-normalised value $\lambda_B=1$ the phase is $-2/9$, and the benchmark then requires a relative correction of $9.83\times10^{-6}$ in $m_e/m_\mu$, a $451.7\sigma$ offset against experimental errors that the theory does not explain. The paper is explicit that the number $2/9$ is not new and that the normalisation is not derived.

Summary in the Authors' Words

What is and is not new

"The $Z_3$-symmetric square-root-mass parametrization and the numerical proximity of the charged-lepton phase to $2/9$ predate the present work ... The novelty claimed here is therefore not the number $2/9$ itself, nor the corresponding Koide-based estimate of $m_\tau$. It is the proposed combination of (i) an antiperiodic two-mode seed, (ii) its periodic symmetric-square carrier, (iii) a coherent branch-real order parameter realizing the Koide cone, (iv) a quadratic ..."

The division of labour: the cone is put in by hand, the orientation is calculated.

The cone

"The coherent branch-real rank-one sector is an explicit assumption of the effective theory and realizes the equal singlet–doublet norm associated with Koide's $45^\circ$ cone."

The $2/9$ from a harmonic sum

For a $Z_3$ displacement $\Delta=2\pi/3$, "harmonics divisible by three are insensitive to the displacement, and"

$$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos\left(2\pi n/3\right)}{n^2}=\frac{2\pi^2}{9}, \qquad \gamma_{C3}=\frac{2\lambda_B}{9}, \qquad \delta_f=3\gamma_{C3}=\frac{2\lambda_B}{3}, \tag{paper 43} $$

"with $\lambda_B\equiv\pi^2g_B^2/(2\kappa_B)$. The compact Green sum therefore fixes the geometric coefficient $2/9$, but it does not determine the matching coefficient $\lambda_B$. A microscopic completion would have to determine the endpoint charge, the stiffness normalization, possible massive-mode contributions, and any independent bare holonomy." Then

$$ \theta_\ell^{(0)}=-\gamma_{C3}=-\frac{2\lambda_B}{9}. \tag{paper 63} $$

The sum itself is exact and elementary: the mask $1-\cos(2\pi n/3)$ vanishes for $n\equiv0\ (3)$ and equals $3/2$ otherwise, so the sum is $\tfrac32\left(\tfrac{\pi^2}{6}-\tfrac19\tfrac{\pi^2}{6}\right)=2\pi^2/9$, which I checked and confirmed.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. $2/9$ is a circle constant: the harmonic sum at a $Z_3$ displacement

The idea. A formula: $\sum_{n\ge1}(1-\cos 2\pi n/3)/n^2=2\pi^2/9$ exactly, so on a circle sampled at three equal points the Green's function contributes the coefficient $2/9$ with no numerology.

What it would add. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §5 treats $2/9$ as a number to be explained, and the corpus's only readings of it are arithmetic (a third of $2/3$, and so on). Here it is a property of the circle's spectrum under a $Z_3$ shift — the same $2/9$ that appears in the phase, obtained from a convergent series whose value is fixed by the spacing. If the corpus is going to keep the phase at all, this is the most economical origin available: it needs no new field, only a compact direction with three equally spaced points.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §5, as the derivation of the phase constant.

What stands in the way. The coefficient enters as $\gamma=\lambda_B\cdot2/9$ with $\lambda_B$ free, so the derivation fixes the shape and not the value; the paper's own benchmark then misses the measured phase by $1.75\times10^{-7}$, which is $451\sigma$. Until $\lambda_B$ is derived, the $2/9$ is a constant of the model and not of nature.

2. Two numbers, two mechanisms, one geometry

The idea. A structure: the $45^\circ$ cone and the $2/9$ orientation come from separate ingredients — equal singlet and doublet norms for the first, a Green's-function phase for the second. The corpus currently treats $2/3$ and $2/9$ as two facets of one coincidence; here they are two calculations.

What it would add. If the two numbers have different origins, then the corpus's "two coincidences in one triple" problem weakens: it is not one miracle twice, it is a geometric condition and a harmonic constant. That also predicts a different fate under running, which the corpus can test — the cone is a norm statement, the phase is a phase.

Where it lands. articles_internal/flavour-research.md §5 and §11.

What stands in the way. The "equal singlet–doublet norm" is an explicit assumption ("the coherent branch-real rank-one sector is an explicit assumption"), so the first number is still inserted. And the phase's constant is only determined up to $\lambda_B$. Structurally attractive, evidentially thin.

Rest, in one line each

The weighted fit $M_{\rm fit}=313.8564003(63)$ MeV reproduces the $313.8$ MeV scale that Brannen, Rivero and others report, to eight digits. The $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=0.188/1$ is quoted with experimental errors only and should not be read as a goodness of fit. The five appendices carry the mode expansions and a sensitivity bound $|\delta Z_e|\lesssim2\times10^{-7}$ that matches the required correction in order of magnitude, which the author calls a consistency of scales rather than a calculation.

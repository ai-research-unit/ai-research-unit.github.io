What the Paper Does

Proposes that quarks and leptons are three-body bound states of five preons, two of one kind and three of another, transforming as $\mathbf{5}$ of an $SU(5)$ flavour group and $\mathbf{3}$ of an $SU(3)$ family group. A chirality selection rule keeps only the wanted combinations, so the unwanted $(10,1)$ and $(1,1)$ states of the earlier field-theoretic version do not appear. The family number three is then not assumed but counted from the preon content, and the quarks and leptons sit in $(10+\bar5,3)$ of $SU(5)\times SU(3)$.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The starting point

"Nowadays, we know that hadrons (baryons and mesons) are composite states composed of quarks, so that 'elementary particles' are quarks and leptons. We know that quarks and leptons are described as $(10+\bar5)$ of $SU(5)_{\rm GUT}$."

The preons

Quarks and leptons "are given by three body states which are composed of constituents $(w_1,w_2,c_1,c_2,c_3)$" — the $w$ fields carrying $SU(5)$ index $\mathbf{5}$ and the $c$ fields carrying the family index $\mathbf{3}$.

The selection rule

"In this paper, we adopt a composite picture, and we put a selection rule on the chirality in the composite states", which removes the "unwelcome states $(10,1)$ and $(1,1)$ which correspond to the states $(\bar5\bar5\bar5)$ and $(333)$".

The outcome

"if we take that number of families is three, we can easily accept the picture that the quarks and leptons are members of $(10+\bar5,3)_L$ of $SU(5)_{\rm GUT}\times SU(3)_{\rm family}$." And: "In this model, we would like to emphasize that family number is three as seen in Eq.(2.6)."

What it still needs

"Of course, in this composite model, it is important that those preons are confined by the $SU(3)$ hyper-color." On the GUT: "Regrettably, at present, we do not have any evidence for the proton decay."

1. Family number counted from preon content

The idea. A mechanism: take the family index to be carried by three identical constituents, and the number of families follows from the number of ways to fill the state rather than being put in by hand.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-excitation-ladder-theory-of-flavour.md treats three generations as the lowest rungs of one ladder and derives the spacing of masses from a potential, with the number three assumed at the outset. Here the three is a counting result, which is the one thing the ladder cannot supply. The article's opening would gain a sentence it does not have: a reason for three rungs.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-excitation-ladder-theory-of-flavour.md, in the section that fixes the number of generations.

What stands in the way. The counting works only inside a model with five new confined constituents and a chirality rule that is imposed, not derived; and no mass matrix is computed, so nothing connects to the ladder's spacings. First, because a reason for three is the corpus's standing gap.

2. A composite route to the same $\mathbf{10}+\bar5$ content

The idea. A question: if quarks and leptons are three-body states, the $SU(5)$ assignment becomes a statement about constituents rather than about the particles themselves, and the anomaly bookkeeping changes.

What it would add. articles_physics/the-electroweak-gauge-group-and-the-hypercharge-assignment.md derives the hypercharges of one generation from anomaly cancellation on the fundamental fields. If the fields are composite, those conditions apply to preons, and the question whether the composite spectrum is anomaly free becomes separate and unanswered.

What stands in the way. The paper does not compute anomalies for the preon content, so the question can only be posed. Second.

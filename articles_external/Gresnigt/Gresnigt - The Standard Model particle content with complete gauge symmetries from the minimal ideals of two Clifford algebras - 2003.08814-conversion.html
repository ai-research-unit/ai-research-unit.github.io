What the Paper Does

Assembles the full gauge content of the Standard Model out of two Clifford algebras: $SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{\rm EM}$ from the minimal left ideals of $Cl(6)$, $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ from the minimal right ideals of $Cl(4)$, and a Dirac spin structure from a second pair of generators. The technical novelty is a redefinition of the $SU(2)_L$ generators so that a weak rotation also moves a state between the two $Cl(6)$ ideals — which an earlier construction could not do, so its states lost the electrocolor structure of the electron. The combined states embed in a minimal left ideal of $Cl(10)\cong SU(5)$, and proton decay is excluded because the offending $SU(5)$ generators do not preserve the two smaller Witt decompositions individually.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Proton decay excluded by which ideals a generator preserves

The idea. $SU(5)$ is present, as the symmetry of the $Cl(10)$ Witt decomposition, and every generator of it that would mediate proton decay is a mixed word $\alpha_i\beta_j^\dagger$ that breaks one of the two smaller decompositions. A mechanism that keeps a grand unified group while removing its dangerous generators, without a desert or a large scale.

What it would add. The corpus's unification article finds that the embedding into a simple group fails and asks where. This is a worked answer to the converse question: the group is there, the states are there, and the decay is removed by a criterion on generators rather than by a mass. It is a different shape of unification — a group without its full adjoint — and worth comparing against the corpus's failure mode.

Where it lands. articles_physics/grand-unification-in-the-biquaternion-framework-where-the-embedding-fails.md.

What stands in the way. Excluding a generator is a statement about a constructed embedding, not about the group; nothing in the algebra forbids an $SU(5)$ transformation from being applied, only that it leave the chosen ideals. If proton decay is absent because the states are restricted rather than the symmetry, the prediction needs the restriction to be derived, and the paper derives it from the same construction it is testing.

2. Charge from a number operator in thirds

The idea. $Q=\frac13\sum_i\alpha_i^\dagger\alpha_i$ — the electric charge is one third of the occupation number of the three ladder modes, so a state with one occupied mode is $e/3$ and one with two is $2e/3$, and quantisation in thirds is a counting fact.

What it would add. The corpus derives the third from a trace over a fourth colour. Here the third is a normalisation constant dividing a plain number operator, and the colour-$SU(3)$ generators $\Lambda_1\ldots\Lambda_8$ move occupation between modes, which makes the statement that colour rotates charge among the ribbons a literal one.

Where it lands. articles_physics/charge-quantisation-from-the-fourth-colour-one-trace-one-third.md.

What stands in the way. The factor $\frac13$ is put in by hand to match observed charges; the paper does not derive $3$. What it derives is that all states in an ideal get a charge in exact thirds, which is weaker than explaining why thirds at all.

3. A symmetry that acts from the right

The idea. $SU(2)_\omega$ acts by right multiplication, and that is why it is not a gauge symmetry: "there are no gauge bosons associated with this $SU(2)$ spin symmetry", since it moves a state between ideals rather than within one. The distinction between a symmetry of the algebra and a gauge symmetry is located in the side of the action.

What it would add. The corpus already draws a physical line at the side: "The carrier exists, and it is a side", the gauge action is left multiplication, and "an action that is one-sided cannot be chiral". This adds a second distinction within the one-sided world: a symmetry that acts within an ideal is a gauge symmetry with bosons, one that acts between ideals is not, and $SU(2)_L$ is built as a right action on a right ideal. That is a finer criterion than the corpus's, and it is checkable on the two actions separately.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-gauge-carrier-of-the-material-sector-why-the-action-is-one-sided.md.

What stands in the way. The criterion — a symmetry acts within an ideal, therefore it is a gauge symmetry — is a definition the paper adopts to match the Standard Model, not a theorem; one could equally call the between-ideal symmetry a gauge symmetry of a larger group, which is what an $SU(5)$ theorist does.

The rank is a judgement: the proton-decay criterion first, because it makes a sharp statement about an unobserved process; the number-operator charge second, because it is a computable dictionary entry; the right-action reading third, because it is a definition presented as a finding.

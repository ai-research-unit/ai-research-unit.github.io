What the Paper Does

A review that makes a case about where to look. The standard model's parameters are usually assumed to be fixed by high-energy physics, so understanding them requires ever higher energies. The paper argues the opposite: the parameters are fixed by the quantum structure of spacetime at low energies, and the right move is to replace Minkowski space by an octonionic space and read off what fermions are allowed to be. On that space the symmetries are restrictive enough to give the observed charge ratios, three generations, and the mass ratios.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The premise

"There ought to exist a reformulation of quantum theory, even at energy scales much lower than Planck scale, which does not depend on classical time."

The reorientation

"We conclude that the aforesaid reformulation of quantum theory helps understand why the standard model is what it is. We do not need experiments at ever higher energies to understand the low energy standard model."

"Instead, we need a better understanding of the quantum nature of spacetime at low energies, such that the quantum spacetime is consistent with the principle of quantum linear superposition."

The slogan

"not only does space-time tell matter how to move, but it also tells matter what to be!"

The three conditions

A phenomenon is quantum gravitational if "the resolution of the time scale of interest is of the order of Planck time", or "the resolution of the length scale of interest is of the order of Planck length", or "the action of all subsystems of interest is of the order of Planck's constant".

The interpretation

"We do not not think of this evolution as taking place in a spacetime, but rather in an octonionic space having the symmetry $E_6$. This is true at all energy scales, including at the low energies at which we currently observe the elementary particles described by the standard model."

"The algebra of the octonionic space determines the symmetries of the standard model, and the values of the parameters of the standard model and mass ratios of quarks and charged leptons."

The comparison

"It is the same as not being able to understand the macroscopic properties of a fluid such as water, which is what happens if we ignore the microscopic properties of water at the molecular level."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Low-energy quantum gravity is a real regime, not a limit

The idea. A definition: quantum gravity is usually identified with the Planck scale. Here it is identified by three separate resolution conditions — Planck time, Planck length, or action of order $\hbar$ — so that a massive body in a spatial superposition is a quantum gravitational system at low energy.

What it would add. The corpus's quantum gravity articles are about the Planck scale and about obstructions to quantising geometry. This says there is a second, experimentally nearer regime with its own physics, defined by a condition the corpus can apply. It also explains why the corpus's macroscopic superpositions are hard: they are not merely large, they are quantum gravitational, and the failure of the background time is the reason.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-multi-qubit-split-i-the-antilinear-obstruction-and-the-corner-collapse.md.

What stands in the way. The third condition (action of order $\hbar$) would make every ordinary quantum system quantum gravitational, which the paper tries to avoid by appeal to the dominant classical background; the argument there is thin.

2. The parameters are consequences of what fermions can be

The idea. A method: instead of deriving the standard model from a symmetry group chosen by hand, put fermions on a noncommutative space and ask which assignments are consistent. The observed charges, the number of generations and the mass ratios come out as the only possibilities.

What it would add. The corpus's generation-counting article derives three generations from a division-algebra structure too, which makes the two directly comparable — same conclusion, different route. A comparison of the two derivations would show whether the count is a robust consequence of noncommutativity or an artefact of one construction. That is the kind of cross-check the corpus exists to perform, and it has not been done.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-number-of-generations-and-the-biquaternion-algebra.md.

What stands in the way. The derivations rely on the octonionic state assignment being the physically correct one, which is the programme's central hypothesis.

3. A quantum system carries its own spacetime

The idea. A picture: the quantum spacetime is not a background the system sits in; the system carries it. At low energies the matrices are close to the identity, so the octonionic space is barely distorted, but its non-associativity still constrains what the system can be.

What it would add. The corpus's articles on background independence state the problem as one of removing a background. This offers a resolution in which nothing is removed: there is no shared background to begin with, and the effective background is what one gets by coarse-graining. It makes background independence a local property of each system, which is a different and much more tractable claim than global independence.

Where it lands. articles_physics/quantum-gravity-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-two-sided-obstruction-and-the-modular-temperature.md.

What stands in the way. If each system carries its own space, the question of how two systems are compared becomes pressing, and the paper does not address it.

The rank: the low-energy regime first, because it relocates a whole field; the parameter derivation second, because it can be checked against a corpus derivation; the carried-spacetime picture third, because it is an interpretation.

The remaining items in one line each: the left-right symmetry of a system in flight, which is broken only by collapse and is what makes the neutrino's Majorana appearance an effect of measurement; the $E_6$ symmetry of the $3\times3$ octonionic Hermitian matrices, which is where the three generations live; and the statement that Planck-scale physics is not needed, which is the review's polemical core.

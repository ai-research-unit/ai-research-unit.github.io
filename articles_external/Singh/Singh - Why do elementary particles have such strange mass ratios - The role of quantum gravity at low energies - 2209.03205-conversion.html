What the Paper Does

Argues that the fermion mass ratios are a low-energy quantum gravitational effect, so that looking for them in high-energy unification is looking in the wrong place. Quantum gravity here means the quantisation of the point structure of spacetime, not of the gravitational field, and it applies whenever every subsystem has action of order $\hbar$ — which is always, in a universe not dominated by classical bodies. Replacing the real coordinates by octonions then constrains what a fermion can be, and the constraints produce three generations, quantised charge, and the observed mass ratios.

"quantum gravity is quantisation not only of the gravitational field, but also of the point structure of space-time."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Mass ratios are an infrared effect

The idea. A relocation: the ratio $m_\mu/m_e\approx 206$ is usually assigned to high-energy physics — a unification scale, a flavour symmetry, a landscape. This says the number is fixed at low energy by the quantum structure of spacetime, and that no high-energy experiment is needed to explain it.

What it would add. The corpus's mass articles treat masses as inputs, and any attempt to derive them assumes a high-scale mechanism. This says the mechanism is at the scale of the electron, which is testable in principle and which makes the failure to find flavour physics at colliders unsurprising. It is the kind of reframing that changes what counts as evidence, and the corpus has no competing claim of this kind.

What stands in the way. The claim rests on the derivations in the programme's other papers; this article is an overview and does not itself compute a ratio.

2. Taking the square root of spacetime as well as of the wave equation

The idea. A construction: Dirac took the square root of the Klein–Gordon operator and got spinors. Take the square root of the spacetime coordinates too, and the coordinates become spinorial — twistor space. Then replace the complex numbers by quaternions or octonions, and the arena becomes six- or ten-dimensional.

What it would add. The corpus's twistor articles use twistors as a representation of conformal geometry. This makes the twistor construction a step in a sequence of division algebras, with quaternions giving six dimensions and octonions ten, so the choice of arena is a choice of number system. That is a compact organising principle, and it explains why $SL(2,\mathbb{H})$ and $SL(2,\mathbb{O})$ keep appearing in the corpus's kinematics.

Where it lands. articles_physics/congruence-twistors-and-the-fields-they-generate.md.

What stands in the way. The sequence is suggestive rather than forced; nothing forbids stopping at the complex numbers.

3. A string theory without compactification

The idea. A comparison: the resulting theory is a string theory whose fundamental objects are 2-branes of Planck area, and whose extra dimensions are not compactified because they are not needed to hide anything — they are large and shared with the weak sector.

What it would add. The corpus has no string-theory article, and the standard objection to string theory is the landscape. This claims to be the same kind of theory with the objection removed, which is a claim the corpus should record even if only to test it. It also gives a reason why the extra dimensions have not been seen: they are not small, they are inaccessible to classical systems, which is a different and more interesting evasion.

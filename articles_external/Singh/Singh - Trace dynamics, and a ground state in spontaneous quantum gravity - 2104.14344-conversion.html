What the Paper Does

Takes the trace Lagrangian of one aikyon — the space-time-matter atom of the programme — rewrites it in terms of two bosonic variables, and derives a time-independent Schrödinger equation for it. The equation's eigenvalues are a fundamental frequency times $\hbar$, so the spectrum of the aikyon is fixed by the same parameters that appear in the Lagrangian. Solving for the ground state gives a Gaussian with a minimum area spread, which the paper reads as a non-singular state of the universe at the big bang.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The setting

"This Lagrangian for the unified entity - called the aikyon - is invariant under global unitary transformations, and as a result possesses a novel conserved charge, known as the Adler-Millard charge."

The result

"In the present paper, we derive an eigenvalue equation, analogous to the time-independent Schrödinger equation, for the Hamiltonian of the theory."

"We show that in the emergent quantum theory, the energy eigenvalues of the aikyon are characterised in terms of a fundamental frequency times Planck's constant."

Why it matters

"The eigenvalues of this equation can, in principle, determine the values of the parameters of the standard model." And: "Hopefully, this equation also carries information about the parameters of the standard model, considering that it depends on the gauge coupling constant" $\alpha$, "and also on the length parameter" $L$.

The equation

It is "a time-independent Schrödinger equation for these four variables, in the potential shown", with the potential bilinear: "The system has the characteristics of an oscillator, considering the bi-linear nature of the potential."

The ground state

"It has the character of a non-singular ground state, with a 'minimum area spread'" of order $L^3/6\pi\alpha L_P$.

"If the aikyon is an electron, this state non-perturbatively describes the electron, together with its mass aspect, and with all the fields that it produces! We believe this is the first ever result of its kind."

The spectrum

"We do not yet have reason to assume that $r$ and $s$ take only discrete values. Therefore we have a continuous spectrum of energy eigenstates."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A particle is a bound state of its own fields

The idea. A claim: solving the aikyon's eigenvalue equation gives a normalisable ground state that describes the electron together with its mass aspect and all the fields it produces. The particle is not a point source added to a field theory; it is the lowest mode of one object that contains both.

What it would add. The corpus's field-theory articles treat the source and the field as distinct: a current on the right, a field on the left. This removes the distinction at the level of the spectrum, and the removal is not a reinterpretation but a computed normalisable state. It would give the corpus's self-field and mass-renormalisation discussions a structural starting point instead of a perturbative one.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-relativistic-particle-and-the-lagrangian-the-stationary-action-principle.md.

What stands in the way. The identification of the ground state with a specific particle requires choosing $L$ and $\alpha$ to match, and only then does the electron interpretation hold.

2. A minimum area spread instead of a singularity

The idea. A consequence: the ground-state wavefunction is Gaussian in the four variables, so the uncertainty in the "position" variables has a floor of order $L^3/6\pi\alpha L_P$. Nothing can be squeezed below it, so the big-bang limit is a minimum-area state rather than a singular point.

What it would add. The corpus's cosmology articles treat the initial singularity as either a breakdown of the theory or a boundary condition. This makes it a ground state with a computable spread, which is what a quantum theory should give. The number is also specific enough to compare with other minimum-length proposals, which the corpus already contains in the Karolyhazy form.

Where it lands. articles_physics/quantum-gravity-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-two-sided-obstruction-and-the-modular-temperature.md.

What stands in the way. The derivation is for a single aikyon; the cosmological reading requires extrapolating one atom of space-time-matter to the whole universe, which the paper asserts rather than argues.

3. A continuous spectrum, stated as a limitation

The idea. A candid gap: the excited states are labelled by integers $r,s$, but nothing in the theory forces them to be integers, so the spectrum is continuous and the degeneracy is double.

What it would add. Derivations of this kind usually present a discrete spectrum and let the reader assume quantisation has been achieved. This one says plainly that it has not, and says why: no boundary condition or periodicity has been imposed. Recording the gap is useful because it marks exactly where the programme needs a new input, and a corpus reader can see which later paper fills it.

Where it lands. articles_maths/the-minimal-polynomial-and-the-jordan-form.md.

What stands in the way. Nothing in the paper supplies the missing condition, so the discreteness of the standard model's parameters remains unexplained.

The rank: the particle-as-bound-state claim first, because it is the strongest physical statement; the minimum area second, because it is a number; the continuous spectrum third, because it is an admitted gap.

The remaining items in one line each: the two bosonic combinations $q_1,q_2$ that diagonalise the cross terms, which is the technical device that makes the equation separable; the identification $k=\alpha^2c^2a_1a_0/L^2$ and $m=a_1a_0/4$ as the oscillator parameters, which is how the analogy to a harmonic oscillator is made precise; and the choice $a_1=6\pi m_{Pl}$, which is the coefficient needed to recover Einstein's equations with the right numbers.

What the Paper Does

Rewrites the whole of boost composition in complexified quaternions and finds that most of it becomes short algebra. A boost is conjugation by the square root of a four-velocity, the composition of two boosts is a three-factor sandwich, the Wigner rotation is the discrepancy between that sandwich and the square root of the composed velocity, and the failure of associativity for three boosts is the non-vanishing of a triple cross product. The paper is a methods paper, and its interest for a quaternionic corpus is direct: it does in the complexified quaternions what the corpus does in biquaternions, and it reaches the non-associativity result that the corpus's composition article states only as a commutator fact.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim about the formalism

"Herein we shall consider Lorentz boosts and Wigner rotations from a (complexified) quaternionic point of view. We shall demonstrate that for a suitably defined self-adjoint complex quaternionic 4-velocity, pure Lorentz boosts can be phrased in terms of the quaternion square root of the relative 4-velocity connecting the two inertial frames."

And the motive, stated as a complaint about the literature: "The use of Hamilton's quaternions as applied to special relativity has a very long, complicated, and rather fraught history — largely due to a significant number of rather sub-optimal notational choices being made in the early literature".

"The Wigner rotation is given by" $R=\sqrt{V_{1\oplus2}^{-1}}\sqrt{V_{2}}\sqrt{V_{1}}$, and it satisfies two identities: $V_{2\oplus1}=R^{\dagger}V_{1\oplus2}R$ for two boosts, and $V_{(1\oplus2)\oplus3}=R_{2\oplus3}^{\dagger}V_{1\oplus(2\oplus3)}R_{2\oplus3}$ for three.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The boost is the square root of the velocity

The idea. A formula: the boost that takes one frame to another is conjugation by $\sqrt{V}$, so the boost rotor and the velocity are the same object at different powers. Composition is then a sandwich, not a product.

What it would add. The corpus's eigenspinor article already builds the Lorentz rotor as a function of proper time, which is a square-root-like construction, and the Cartan article splits transformations into factors. Neither states the identity that the boost is the square root of the four-velocity as the reason the split exists. Stating it would unify two of the corpus's articles and give the boost a square root that is itself a physical object rather than a parametrisation.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-eigenspinor-the-lorentz-rotor-as-a-function-of-proper-time.md.

What stands in the way. In the corpus's algebra the rotor multiplies from both sides anyway, so the square-root form may be automatic there; if so the addition is cosmetic and the interesting content is only that the two conventions agree.

2. Non-associativity has a name and a condition

The idea. A result: composing four-velocities is not associative, and it is associative exactly when $\vec v_{1}\times(\vec v_{2}\times\vec v_{3})=0$. The Wigner rotation is the measure of the failure, appearing between the two bracketings.

What it would add. The corpus's composition article treats non-commutativity as the whole story, which is what the commutator table can see. Non-associativity is invisible to a bracket table and is a strictly stronger statement about the algebra of velocities. For a corpus that composes rotations routinely, the distinction between an associative product and a non-associative velocity composition is worth printing, and the condition names the special case where the two agree.

Where it lands. articles_physics/composition-of-two-boosts-the-commutator-table-and-non-additivity.md.

What stands in the way. Associativity of the underlying group product is never in doubt; only the derived velocity composition fails to be associative. So the result is about the parametrisation, not the group, and calling it non-associativity invites a confusion the paper does not always avoid.

3. Left composition is natural, right composition is not

The idea. A remark with a reason: $V_{1\oplus2}=\sqrt{V_{2}}V_{1}\sqrt{V_{2}}$ is symmetric in appearance while $V_{2\oplus1}$ is not, so the composition convention that puts the new boost's square root on both sides is the one that composes cleanly for three or more boosts.

What it would add. The corpus's rapidity-composition exercise fixes a convention by fiat. This gives a criterion — the convention under which the triple product stays manifestly symmetric — which is the kind of reason the exercise could adopt instead of choosing.

Where it lands. articles_physics/exercise-boosting-a-four-velocity-and-rapidity-composition.md.

What stands in the way. The asymmetry is a property of this particular quaternionic representation, and the paper concedes that the history of the subject is littered with conventions that looked canonical in their own notation.

The rank: the square-root identity first, because it is a formula; non-associativity second, because the corpus cannot see it; the left-composition remark last, because it is a convention.

The remaining items in one line each: the use of the relativistic half-velocity $w$ with $v=2w/(1+w^{2})$, which is the same parametrisation the corpus's rapidity articles use in another form; the explicit parallel and perpendicular limits, which check the general formula against the two cases with known answers; and the authors' own judgement that the BCH approach, elegant or not, is not the practical route — a rare instance in this set of an author arguing against the most sophisticated branch of his own formalism.

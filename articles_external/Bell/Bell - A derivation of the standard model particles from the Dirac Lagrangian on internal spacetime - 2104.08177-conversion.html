What the Paper Does

Builds spinors on a spacetime whose metric is degenerate along particle worldlines and writes the Dirac Lagrangian there. The bound states of its solutions turn out to be the Standard Model particle list, with the right spin, electric charge and colour charge for each entry, plus one state the Standard Model does not contain.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The construction

"A modification of general relativity that was recently introduced as an approximate spacetime geometric model of quantum nonlocality. In an internal spacetime, time is stationary along the worldlines of fundamental (dust) particles. Consequently, the dimensions of tangent spaces at different points of spacetime vary, and spin wavefunction collapse is modeled by the projection from one tangent space to another."

Why compositeness costs nothing

the compositeness may be regarded as purely mathematical (similar to a direct sum decomposition of a representation into irreducibles), since the pointons in a bound state share the same worldline. In any case, this feature makes our model quite different from other composite (or preon) models and eliminates the preon problem of finite particle size.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Compositeness without a size

The idea. The constituents share one worldline, so a composite particle has no internal separation and no form factor. The preon problem — why a composite electron shows no substructure — never arises, because there is no "inside". This is a mechanism, and it is the sharpest structural idea in the folder.

What it would add. A way to have a preonic spectrum without the size penalty that killed preon models, which is the standard objection the corpus's particle-content article does not need to answer because it does not posit constituents. The metric half of the construction is already in the corpus — articles_physics/the-gram-matrix-as-the-only-metric.md has $g_{\mu\nu}=\langle\tilde E_{\Phi,\mu},\tilde E_{\Phi,\nu}\rangle_{\natural}$ nondegenerate exactly when the four frame legs are independent — so what is new is not a degenerate metric but particles placed on the degenerate locus.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-content-quantum-numbers-and-the-nineteen-parameters.md, on whether the content is derived or assumed.

What stands in the way. The corpus's content comes from the ideal structure of its algebra, and adding a constituent layer would duplicate that derivation rather than complete it; the two would have to be shown to give the same list.

2. Charge as the orientation of a vanishing subspace

The idea. Where the metric degenerates, a new $\mathbb{Z}_2$ freedom appears — the orientation of the kernel — and that bit is electric charge. Charge is then discrete by construction, and its sign is a choice of orientation rather than a quantum number assigned to a field.

What it would add. A reason for charge quantisation that is geometric rather than algebraic, in a corpus where charge comes from the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ factor of the algebra.

Where it lands. articles_physics/biquaternion-physical-interpretation-of-operator-chirality.md, on the identification of a discrete algebraic choice with a physical label.

What stands in the way. A single $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gives a sign, not the observed ratios of $1/3$; the fractions here come from which orbitals are occupied, so the mechanism alone does not reproduce the charge lattice.

3. The particle list is an exhaustive partition

The idea. The claim is not that the model contains the Standard Model particles but that it contains nothing else except the five $x$ states. A finite enumeration with no room left over is a strong kind of derivation.

What it would add. A model of what deriving the particle content would mean, which the corpus asserts in prose but does not exhibit as a closed list.

Where it lands. articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md, on closure of a spectrum.

What stands in the way. The exhaustiveness is exhaustive for geoms of at most four pointons; the bound is imposed, not derived, and a five-pointon bound state is not discussed.

The rank is a judgement: compositeness without a size first, because it removes the standard objection; charge-from-orientation second, because it is the paper's most transferable identification; the exhaustive partition third, because its bound is assumed.

This is a twenty-five page paper; the internal metric and its varying tangent spaces, the pointon spinor and its internal 4-momentum, the Dirac Lagrangian on the internal spacetime, colour charge, and the composite particle table are its content.

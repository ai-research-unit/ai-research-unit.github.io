What the Paper Does

Takes the composite particle model of the companion articles — bound states of at most four "pointons" on a spacetime whose metric is degenerate along particle worldlines — and derives the interaction vertices from it. The only pointon process the Dirac Lagrangian permits is pair creation and annihilation of opposite charge, so every trivalent Standard Model vertex must be built from those two operations; the paper lists which fusions succeed and which fail.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Vertices as fusion rules of a finite algebra

The idea. An interaction is not a term added to a Lagrangian but a permitted concatenation of two composite objects. The list of permitted vertices is finite, is computed, and is the same list the Standard Model writes down. This is a mechanism for the shape of the interaction sector.

What it would add. A reason why the vertex set is what it is, where the corpus's Lagrangian article writes the four sectors and their couplings as structure to be matched.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-and-the-lagrangian-four-sectors-in-one-expression.md, on where the interaction terms come from.

What stands in the way. The corpus's vertices are read off a gauge-invariant Lagrangian; here they are read off a fusion table, and nothing yet maps one list onto the other except coincidence of content. The paper's own two extra vertices show the lists are not identical.

2. Parity violation as an emptiness in a table

The idea. Left-handed couplings exist and right-handed ones do not because the mixed-chirality concatenations are simply not fusions. Parity violation is a counting fact, not a choice of representation.

What it would add. An alternative account to the corpus's chirality articles, which locate the asymmetry in the idempotent structure of the algebra.

Where it lands. articles_physics/biquaternion-physical-interpretation-of-operator-chirality.md, on why one chirality couples.

What stands in the way. The corpus's chirality is a property of the field; here it is replaced by a geom spin state, and the paper says so: "νe νµ ντ u c t e L µ L τ L d L s L b L eR µR τR uR cR tR dR sR bR On an internal spacetime, left- and right-handed chirality is replaced by geom spin states; see [B1, Sections 2, 3].". The two readings would need to be shown to agree before the argument transfers.

3. A massive longitudinal

$Z_0$ standing in for the four-boson and three-gluon vertices

The idea. The quartic gauge vertices and $ggg$ are replaced by trivalent vertices involving the longitudinal mode of a massive boson. The replacement is argued for by the infrared suppression and dynamical mass of the gluon, which make the two descriptions qualitatively alike at the relevant scales. This is a reading of an existing vertex set.

What it would add. A structural reason why the four-boson vertices are hard to observe and why $ggg$ is soft, phrased as an identity rather than a suppression.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-quantum-chromodynamics-the-triplet-the-octet-and-the-feynman-rules.md, on the three-gluon vertex.

What stands in the way. Replacing $ggg$ by three massive $Z_0$ changes the colour structure entirely; the corpus's chromodynamics is built on the octet, and a colourless substitute is not a rewriting of it.

4. A predicted massive spin-2 boson with a computed vertex list

The idea. The model's single particle outside the Standard Model is a spin-2 boson whose five polarization states are enumerated and whose trivalent vertices are tabulated. It can be removed by extra assumptions — at most one $\ast$-orbital, or full orbitals first — which the author declines to impose.

What it would add. A falsifiable extra state, with couplings, that the corpus's higher-spin articles currently exclude on structural grounds.

Where it lands. articles_physics/higher-spin-from-tensor-products-the-three-carriers-and-the-absence-of-a-fourth.md, on the closed list of carriers.

What stands in the way. The corpus's argument is that a fourth carrier does not exist; a spin-2 boson that can be assumed away is not a counterexample, and the author says the removing conditions are available.

The rank is a judgement: the fusion-rule mechanism first, because it is the paper's method and the only one that could be imported whole; parity violation second, because it touches a question the corpus answers differently; the $Z_0$ replacement third, as a reading that conflicts with the colour sector; the spin-2 state fourth, because it is explicitly optional.

This is a twenty-page paper; the geom fusion definition, the Standard Model vertex tables, the parity-violation theorem, the two extra vertices, the excluded diagrams and the spin-2 vertex table are its content.

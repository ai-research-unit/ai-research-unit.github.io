What the Paper Does

Computes mass ratios for the first generation from the Ricci tensors of the degenerate metrics associated with each particle type. The bare up/down ratio comes out as a rational number inside the lattice-QCD window; the dressed electron to bare quark ratios then fix the two bare quark masses from the electron mass; and a fourth ratio, from 4-accelerations, gives the constituent quark ratio. No parameter is fitted.

The claim

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Mass ratios as rational curvature data

The idea. A mass ratio is a number of the form $\rho_{u_1}/\rho_d$, computed from Ricci tensors, and it lands as $9604/19683$. Nothing is scanned or fitted, and the agreement with lattice QCD is at the level of the quoted uncertainty. This is a number of the kind the corpus's constants papers look for and do not produce.

What it would add. A worked case where a rational number from a geometry falls inside a measured window, which is the strongest form the corpus's explanation of constants question admits.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-mass-formula-and-the-spectrum-of-elementary-particles-in-biquaternionic-form.md, which states that the framework does not derive masses and then offers $m^{(s)}=\tfrac{\mu_0}{4}\deg\tau^{l\bar l}$ as the geometric part.

What stands in the way. The corpus's masses come from diagonalising $3\times3$ matrices; here they come from evaluating a density at one radius. The two cannot both be the mechanism, and the paper's own three $u$ states (only one of which is used) shows the geometry offers more ratios than the data need.

2. Dressed mass as $\rho+3p$

The idea. The split between bare and dressed mass is the split between the energy density in the Einstein equation and the combination that appears in the Newtonian-limit Poisson equation. Vacuum polarization is replaced by an isotropic pressure, which "The notion of a virtual covering (or vacuum polarization) in quantum field theory is not well defined mathematically." in QFT but is here a definite geometric object.

What it would add. A definition of the dressed mass that does not require a loop expansion, for a corpus whose corrections are computed perturbatively.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-linearised-gravity.md, on the Newtonian limit and what plays the role of the source.

What stands in the way. The identification $\tilde\rho=\rho+3p$ holds only for the static, spherically symmetric metrics of this paper; the corpus's fields are not of that form, and the substitution would need the same equation to hold there.

3. Three masses for one particle, distinguished by measurement

The idea. The same geometry yields a bare density, a dressed density and a constituent acceleration, and the paper assigns them to the three masses that QCD actually uses. The labelling is a reading of what an experiment does, not a new dynamics.

What it would add. A clean separation of the three senses of "quark mass" that the corpus's Yukawa article treats as one matrix and one scale.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-yukawa-sector-fermion-mass-matrices-and-their-diagonalisation.md, on the scale at which a mass is quoted.

What stands in the way. The constituent ratio needs the assumption that the quarks "share the same pointal binding energy"; without it the 48/49 does not follow, and the corpus has no counterpart to that assumption.

The rank is a judgement: the rational mass ratio first, because it is a prediction and not a translation; $\rho+3p$ second, because it is the definition that could be borrowed; the three-masses reading third, because it rests on an extra assumption.

This is a twenty-three page paper; the pointon, pointal sphere and pointal union metrics, the energy-density computation, the bare ratio, the dressed electron/quark ratios, the sectional curvatures, the constituent ratio and a note on neutrino energy density are its content.

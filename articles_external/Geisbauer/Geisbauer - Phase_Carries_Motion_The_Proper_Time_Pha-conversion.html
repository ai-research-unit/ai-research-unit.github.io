What the Paper Does

Argues that the invariant object behind a matter wave is the phase accumulated along proper time, $dS=-mc^2d\tau$, and that the de Broglie wave is its frame-dependent spatial projection. The derivation is a chain of substitutions: from the action to the phase, from the phase to the plane-wave form, from the plane wave to $k=p/\hbar$ and $\lambda_{dB}=h/p$. The paper is deliberately conservative — no modification of relativity, of the Born rule, or of the wave equation is proposed.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The core

$$dS=-mc^2d\tau$$ (paper 1), then $$d\Phi_C=-\frac{dS}{\hbar}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d\Phi_C=\frac{mc^2}{\hbar}d\tau$$ (paper 2–3). The stated reading: "The de Broglie matter wave is the frame-dependent spatial expression of the invariant proper-time phase"; the wave is "the frame-dependent spatial expression".

The bridge

With $S=\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x-Et$ (paper 35) and $dS=\mathbf p\cdot d\mathbf x-E\,dt$ (paper 36), comparison with $dS=-mc^2d\tau$ (paper 37) gives $$-mc^2d\tau=\mathbf p\cdot d\mathbf x-E\,dt\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad mc^2d\tau=E\,dt-\mathbf p\cdot d\mathbf x$$ (paper 38–39), "the central bridge between the internal and external representations of the same invariant action". The internal representation is $mc^2d\tau$ (paper 40), the external $E\,dt-\mathbf p\cdot d\mathbf x$ (paper 41), and "They are not two independent actions. They are complementary descriptions of the same relativistic action phase."

The de Broglie relations

$$\varphi(\mathbf x,t)=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-\omega t,\qquad \varphi=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x-Et\right)$$ (paper 42–43) give $$\mathbf k=\frac{\mathbf p}{\hbar},\qquad \omega=\frac{E}{\hbar}$$ (paper 44); with $k\lambda=2\pi$ (paper 45), $$\lambda=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{p}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda_{dB}=\frac{h}{p}$$ (paper 46–47).

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The bridge equation

The idea. $mc^2d\tau=E\,dt-\mathbf p\cdot d\mathbf x$ is one identity with two readings, and the paper's whole argument hangs on it. It is a formula, and it is exact.

What it would add. A single line that converts between the corpus's material-sector time (the imaginary coefficient, tied to proper time) and its informational-sector energy and momentum — the two sectors the corpus keeps separate.

Where it lands. articles_physics/relational-time-from-the-sector-exchange-the-material-and-informational-clocks.md, which treats the two times as the real and imaginary parts of one central coordinate.

What stands in the way. The identity is the definition of the four-momentum in the metric, so it holds in any framework with an interval; it does not by itself say anything about the exchange between the sectors, which is the corpus's question.

2. Proper-time phase as the invariant, the wave as its projection

The idea. $d\Phi_C=(mc^2/\hbar)d\tau$ is a scalar on a worldline, and $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-\omega t$ is what it looks like when a frame is chosen. That is a reading, and it inverts the usual priority: the wave is not the object, the phase along the path is.

What it would add. A clean statement of something the corpus half-says: its eigenspinor article makes the rotor a function of proper time, so the phase is already a function on the worldline and the plane wave is already a rewriting.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-eigenspinor-the-lorentz-rotor-as-a-function-of-proper-time.md.

What stands in the way. The corpus's phase is the central scalar imaginary, and its relation to a proper-time integral is already written as the exponent of the action; the reading adds a name, not an equation.

The rank is a judgement: the bridge first, because it is exact and connects two corpus objects; the reading second, because it renames what the corpus already has.

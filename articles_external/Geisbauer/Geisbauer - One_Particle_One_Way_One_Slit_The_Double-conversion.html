What the Paper Does

Gives the edge-response field of the earlier papers a full definition: a static field sourced by the aperture boundary, an integral over the edge with a Bessel kernel of lateral width $\lambda$, a geometric phase and potential, and a coupling $G(a)$ between two apertures. The particle goes through exactly one slit and samples the field of both edges locally, which is the mechanism offered in place of self-interference. The paper derives the directional fringes expected from oblique incidence, and closes with an experimental proposal that would separate the field's contribution from a purely geometric one.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The field

For an aperture $\Omega$ with boundary $\partial\Omega$, $$E_{\rm geo}(\mathbf r)=\int_{\partial\Omega}d\ell'\,n(\mathbf r')\,K_\lambda\!\left(r_\perp-\mathbf r'\right)W(\mathbf r')$$ (paper 1), with $n$ the outward normal along the edge, $W$ edge microphysics (thickness, roughness, conductivity) and $K_\lambda$ "a real, lateral kernel with characteristic width ∼ λ (de Broglie wavelength for matter, optical wavelength for photons).". The analytic choice is $$K_\lambda(\rho)=\frac{1}{2\pi\ell_\lambda}J_0\!\left(\frac{|\rho|}{\ell_\lambda}\right)e^{-|\rho|/\ell_{\rm mic}},\qquad \ell_\lambda\sim\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}$$ (paper 2), "which captures lateral coherence and short-range damping from edge roughness".

The phase, the potential, the deflection

$$\phi_{\rm geo}(r_\perp)=\alpha\int^{r_\perp}E_{\rm geo}\cdot d\ell,\qquad \alpha:=\frac{\Xi}{\hbar v}$$ (paper 3), "ℓ, α := Ξ, (3) ℏv with v the particle speed and Ξ a coupling that depends on species (photon/electron/atom) and material."; then $$U_{\rm geo}(r_\perp)=\hbar v\beta\,\nabla\cdot E_{\rm geo}(r_\perp),\qquad \beta=O(1)$$ (paper 4) and $$\Delta p_\perp=-\nabla_\perp U_{\rm geo}\int\frac{dz}{v}\approx-\tau\nabla_\perp U_{\rm geo}$$ (paper 5), with interaction time $\tau$ across the screen region.

The two-aperture coupling

For $\Omega_L,\Omega_R$ at centre distance $a$, $E_{\rm geo}=E^{(L)}_{\rm geo}+E^{(R)}_{\rm geo}$ (paper 7), and A particle that physically crosses $\Omega_L$ still samples $E^{(R)}_{\rm geo}$ because the kernel $K_\lambda$ has lateral reach $\sim\lambda$. The strength of the passive aperture is $$G(a)=\int_{\partial\Omega_L}\int_{\partial\Omega_R}d\ell'\,d\ell''\,n(\mathbf r')\cdot n(\mathbf r'')\,K_\lambda(\mathbf r'-\mathbf r''),\qquad G(a)\downarrow\text{ with }a/\lambda$$ (paper 8): "This is the precise TUni statement of 'the other slit launches a field'".

The scalar summary

$$\cos\zeta_{\rm geo}=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\lambda}{d_{\rm eff}}\right)^{2}}$$ (paper 6), $d_{\rm eff}$ an effective aperture scale including thickness and roughness: "ff (6) Small deff (narrow or rough edges) increase ζgeo and broaden the distribution. 4 Mechanism"

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A boundary integral as the source of the pattern

The idea. The pattern is produced by an integral over $\partial\Omega$, not by two points. That is a formula, and it is the one place in the batch where the aperture's shape enters as shape — thickness, roughness and normals — rather than as a scalar width.

What it would add. A second route from aperture geometry to a screen distribution, where the corpus's route is two amplitudes and a visibility.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-double-slit-experiment-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. The kernel and the couplings $\Xi$, $\beta$, $\tau$ and $W$ are all free; with four free functions the model can fit a fringe pattern, and the paper offers scaling laws ($\ell_\lambda\sim\lambda/2\pi$, $G$ decays with $a/\lambda$) rather than numbers. Nothing in it is computed for a stated aperture, so nothing can be checked.

2. $G(a)$, a coupling between apertures

The idea. The influence of the slit the particle did not go through is a single decreasing function of separation, defined as an overlap of edge kernels. That is the cleanest statement of the batch's central mechanism: it makes "the other slit" a measurable coupling rather than a metaphor.

What it would add. A quantity with a length scale in it, which is what a "which-path" experiment would need in order to be described.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-double-slit-experiment-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the which-path qubit and the visibility.

What stands in the way. $G(a)$ is a functional of the aperture geometry and the kernel, and the paper never evaluates it; its claim that $G$ is not zero for $a\lesssim$ few $\lambda$ is the only quantitative statement, and it is stated, not derived.

3. A scalar that summarizes the aperture

The idea. $\cos\zeta_{\rm geo}=\sqrt{1-(\lambda/d_{\rm eff})^2}$ is the same function as the Lorentz factor and the same function as the gravitational cosine, with the aperture in the place of $c$ or $r_s$. Across the batch this is the recurring object.

What it would add. A single number per aperture that says how strongly it spreads a beam, comparable across experiments.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-double-slit-experiment-in-biquaternionic-form.md.

What stands in the way. $d_{\rm eff}$ is defined by the list of things it includes, not by a formula, so the number cannot be computed for a real slit; and the factor multiplies an intensity, which sets an envelope and cannot move a fringe.

The rank is a judgement: the boundary integral first, because it is the batch's only shape-sensitive construction; $G(a)$ second, because it is the only coupling with a length in it; the scalar third, because it recurs so often that it has become a mannerism.

This is a seven-page paper; the field, the kernel, the phase and potential, the deflection, the two-aperture coupling, the oblique-incidence stripes, the non-zero minima and the experimental proposal are its content.

What the Paper Does

Embeds four-dimensional spacetime in five dimensions and measures time dilation as an angle between a worldline and a normal, then builds the total angle as a product of a kinematic, a gravitational and an expansion cosine. A universal simultaneity time is defined as a weighted average of proper times, with the cosines as weights, and the construction is exercised on three hypothetical observers. The arithmetic is carried out; the interpretation of the average as a physical time is not tested.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The five-dimensional setting

Coordinates are $x^A=(ct,x,y,z,x_5)$, with $x_5$ "the moment normal", and $$ds^2=-c^2dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2+dx_5dx_5$$ (paper 1), $g_{55}=1$ initially. The angle between the four-velocity $U^A$ and the normal $n^A$ is $$\cos\theta=\frac{U^An_A}{\sqrt{U^BU_B}\sqrt{n^Cn_C}}$$ (paper 2), giving $$\cos\theta_v=\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}},\qquad \Delta t=\frac{\Delta\tau}{\cos\theta_v},\qquad L=L_0\cos\theta_v$$ (paper 3–5)."t = . Gravity and expansion as angles With the Schwarzschild metric,

$\cos\theta_{\rm grav}=\sqrt{1-2GM/rc^2}$ (paper 7) and $\cos\theta_{\rm total}=\cos\theta_v\cos\theta_{\rm grav}$ (paper 8). As $r\to r_s$ the cosine vanishes and $\Delta t\to\infty$; at $r=1.001\,r_s$ with $v=0$ the paper gets $\cos\theta_{\rm grav}=\sqrt{0.001/1.001}\approx0.0316$ and $\Delta t\approx31.6\,\Delta\tau$ (paper 11–12). Expansion enters as $\cos\theta_{\rm exp}=e^{-\alpha H_0t}$ with $\alpha$"a model parameter (here set to 1)."(paper 13), and the full angle is the triple product.

The universal time as a weighted average

$$T_{\rm uni}=\frac{\sum_i t'_iW_i}{\sum_i W_i},\qquad W_i=\sqrt{1-\frac{v_i^2}{c^2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{r_ic^2}}\,e^{-\alpha H_0t_i}$$ (paper 16), where $t'_i$ is the proper-time reading of observer $i$. Three observers are worked out with $H_0=2.27\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $t=1$ Gyr $=3.156\times10^{16}$ s, hence $e^{-\alpha H_0t}\approx0.9309$: observer A at $v=0.6c$ in flat space has weight $0.7447$, observer B at rest at $r=10r_s$ has $0.8831$, and observer C at $v=0.8c$ and $r=1.001r_s$ has $0.0176$ (paper 17–27). With all three proper times set to $1$ s, $T_{\rm uni}=1.6454/1.6454=1$ s, and the paper says so:"The result is still 1 s because all proper time intervals $t'_i$ were chosen to be 1 s. The relative contributions (weights) have changed."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Dilation as an angle on a fifth axis

The idea. $\cos\theta_v=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}$ reads a boost as a rotation in a plane containing a fifth direction, with $L=L_0\cos\theta$ for contraction and $\Delta t=\Delta\tau/\cos\theta$ for dilation. Since $\cos\theta_v=1/\gamma=\operatorname{sech}\Theta$, the "time angle" is the rapidity, written as a cosine. It is a reading of an object the corpus already has.

What it would add. A single geometric name for the two effects that the corpus keeps as two statements about which events are simultaneous, and a clean way to see that contraction and dilation are one cosine.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-cartan-decomposition-every-lorentz-transformation-as-a-boost-and-a-rotation.md, where the boost is already a rotor angle.

What stands in the way. Little: the correspondence is exact in flat space, so the import is a translation, not new content. The fifth axis does no work once $\cos\theta=\operatorname{sech}\Theta$ is recognised, and the paper's $g_{55}=1$"adjustable for dynamic effects" is not used.

2. A global time defined as a weighted mean of proper times

The idea. Given proper-time readings and the three factors for each observer, one number $T_{\rm uni}$ is formed by weighting each reading with its own rate. This is a mechanism for producing a global clock from local ones, and the only place in the paper where a number is produced.

What it would add. An explicit formula where the corpus has only the statement that the two times are the two parts of one central coordinate.

Where it lands. articles_physics/relational-time-from-the-sector-exchange-the-material-and-informational-clocks.md.

What stands in the way. The weights are the very factors that say how far each clock is from the global frame, so the mean presupposes what it defines; and the worked example returns $1$ s for any choice of weights because every $t'_i$ is $1$ s — the numbers demonstrate the arithmetic, not the construction.

The rank is a judgement: both are low, for different reasons — the first is a translation and the second is circular as stated. Nothing in this paper is refused outright.

This is a six-page paper; the 5D line element, the three angle factors, the black-hole limits, the weighted average and a figure of the three-observer case are its content.

What the Paper Does

Casts gravity as the relaxation of a structural tension, in five moves: a single scalar $\tau_{\rm foc}$ that measures how strongly a stress-energy focuses a congruence, a derived density threshold below which voids accelerate, a growth law for the void volume, a mapping from the dark-matter channel to a Newtonian potential, and a Gaussian partition of environments into $68.2/27.2/4.6$ per cent assigned to dark energy, dark matter and visible matter. Dynamics stay GR at leading order; the framework adds a bookkeeping scalar, one threshold that is derived rather than fitted, and one EFT correction.

Summary in the Authors' Words

One scalar for focusing

$$\tau_{\rm foc}:=\rho+\frac{3p}{c^2}+\lambda\frac{\lVert\pi\rVert^2}{c^2},\qquad \lVert\pi\rVert^2=\pi_{\mu\nu}\pi^{\mu\nu},\quad\lambda\sim O(1)$$ (paper 8). "c c Meaning. τfoc aggregates the components of Tµν that focus timelike congruences via the Raychaudhuri equation (together with Einstein’s equations) [9]. Large τfoc corresponds to strong “holding” of energy.", and "imits. τfoc is a kinematic diagnostic; dynamics still obey GR at leading order (Sec. 9).".

The void threshold, derived

For a domain $D$, $$\frac{\ddot a_D}{a_D}\simeq-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\langle\rho_m\rangle_D+\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho_{\rm vac}$$ (paper 9), so acceleration holds when $$\langle\rho_m\rangle_D<2\rho_{\rm vac}\equiv\rho_\star$$ (paper 10), encoded as $$\partial_tV_D\propto\left[2\rho_{\rm vac}-\langle\rho_m\rangle_D\right]_+$$ (paper 11) with a threshold fixed by Eq. (10) (no ad-hoc parameter). Voids are "ehave as overflow basins: once below ρ⋆ , expansion becomes the efficient tension-relaxation channel. Growth law.". The tests named are void stacking, Alcock–Paczynski and lensing through supervoids.

Time rate and the dark-matter channel

The exterior result is the standard one, written in the language of the batch: $$\cos\zeta_{\rm grav}(r)=\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{rc^2}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{d\tau}{dt}=\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}=\cos\zeta_{\rm grav}\cos\zeta_{\rm kin}$$ (paper 12), and the inversion for $G$ of the companion paper is repeated as Eq. (13). Dark matter enters as a potential depth, $$\sigma_{DM}=b-\frac{\Phi_{DM}}{c^2},\qquad \nabla^2\Phi_{DM}=4\pi G\rho_{DM}$$ (paper 14): "the phenomenological DM 'channel' is exactly the potential depth associated with the DM density", so that $\Phi_{DM}$ from mass modeling "fixes the channel without new free substances".

The Gaussian partition

$$\text{68.2\% (central basin)}\leftrightarrow\text{dark energy},\quad \text{27.2\% (1–2}\sigma\text{)}\leftrightarrow\text{dark matter},\quad \text{4.6\% (2–3}\sigma\text{)}\leftrightarrow\text{visible}$$ (paper 15), with "sin; 4.6:27.2 ≃ 1:6 matches the observed dark-to-visible ratio in aggregate. Assumptions.". The author states the status plainly: "The Gaussian partition is an analysis prior, not a law."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. One scalar that decides focusing

The idea. $\tau_{\rm foc}=\rho+3p/c^2+\lambda\lVert\pi\rVert^2/c^2$ collects the three things that bend a congruence into one number, with an explicit order-one weight on the anisotropic part. This is a formula the corpus does not have: it carries a stress-energy tensor and a conservation law, but no scalar that says whether a congruence converges.

What it would add. A bridge between the corpus's equation of state, which gives $p_{\rm eff}$ as a scalar function of density and inverse temperature, and the Raychaudhuri statement that actually decides collapse.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-equation-of-state-in-biquaternion-form.md, whose $p_{\rm eff}=p_{\rm th}+p_{\rm qnt}$ would enter $\tau_{\rm foc}$ directly.

What stands in the way. The corpus's fluids are spin fluids, and the anisotropic stress $\pi_{\mu\nu}$ is not a separate object there; $\lambda$ is left as $O(1)$ and never fixed, so the combination is a definition rather than a result.

2. A threshold that is derived, not fitted

The idea. $\langle\rho_m\rangle<2\rho_{\rm vac}$ follows from the acceleration equation by division, and the paper is entitled to call it parameter-free: the factor 2 is $4\pi G/3$ against $8\pi G/3$. This is a prediction-shaped statement about which voids grow.

What it would add. A criterion where the corpus has none, and one that is checkable by void statistics — the kind of number the corpus's empirical-status article asks for.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-euler-and-continuity-equations-in-biquaternionic-form.md, where the acceleration of a fluid element is written.

What stands in the way. The derivation uses a homogeneous domain with a single scale factor, i.e. it assumes what it is trying to predict; real voids are neither homogeneous nor isolated, and the paper postpones backreaction to future work.

3. A Gaussian partition of the cosmic budget

The idea. The three fractions of a normal distribution are assigned to the three constituents, and $4.6:27.2\approx1:6$ reproduces the dark-to-visible ratio. The author calls it "an analysis prior, not a law".

What it would add. A reason for a ratio the corpus records as measured.

Where it lands. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md.

What stands in the way. A normal distribution's bin fractions are fixed numbers, so the agreement is an assignment, not a derivation; nothing in the paper shows why environment density should be Gaussian in $\sigma$ units, and $4.6/27.2=0.169$ against a measured ratio near $0.16$ is a coincidence of two round numbers.

The rank is a judgement: the focusing scalar first, because it is usable; the threshold second, because it is the paper's only derived statement; the partition third, because it explains a number by naming it.

This is a six-page paper; the scalar, the threshold, the growth law, the time-rate product, the dark-matter mapping and the partition are its content.

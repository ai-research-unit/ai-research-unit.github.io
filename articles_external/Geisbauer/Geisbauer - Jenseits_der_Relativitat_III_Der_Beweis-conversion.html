What the Paper Does

Defines simultaneity by equal accumulated proper time rather than by equal coordinate time: for a chosen target $\tau_0$, the points whose clocks read $\tau_0$ form a moment surface $\Sigma_{\tau_0}$, and its weighted geometric centre is named the Geisbauer point. The construction is then applied to Hafele–Keating and to the GNSS processing chain, where it replaces additive corrections by an integrated time field. The paper claims sub-nanosecond timing and centimetre positioning, with an improvement factor of $10$ to $50$ over conventional corrections.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The definition

"Gegeben Weltlinien $x_i(t)$ mit Raten $f_i$ bestimmen wir für $\tau_0>0$ die Zeitpunkte $t_i$ aus $$\tau_i(t_i)=\int f_i\,dt=\tau_0,\qquad P_i:=x_i(t_i)$$ (paper 3)." Die Menge $\{P_i\}$ ist im Sinne gleicher gelebter Zeit (Eigenzeit) gleichzeitig."Eulerian interpolation gives an accumulated time field $T(x)$, and $$\Sigma_{\tau_0}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^3\ \middle|\ T(x)=\tau_0\right\}$$ (paper 4) is" der Momentenﬂäche (ein physikalischer Schnitt des Gegenwartszustands), schematisch in Abbil- dung 1 dargestellt.".

The Geisbauer point

With

$n=\nabla T/|\nabla T|$ the mean curvature of the moment surface is $$H(x)=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla T}{|\nabla T|}\right)\Big|_{\Sigma_{\tau_0}}$$ (paper 5), $H>0$ inward bending, $H<0$ outward. The centre is $$G=\arg\min_{x\in\Sigma_{\tau_0}}\sum_i w_i\,d_\Sigma(x,P_i)^2$$ (paper 6), $w_i>0$, $d_\Sigma$ the geodesic distance on $\Sigma_{\tau_0}$;"Störungen in $f$ ... deformieren $T$, damit $\Sigma_{\tau_0}$ und ihre Geodäten; $G$ verschiebt sich".

Hafele–Keating, recomputed Two aircraft at

$h\approx10$ km with $v_A=222.22\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $v_B=277.78\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ give $gh/c^2\approx1.09\times10^{-12}$, $v_A^2/2c^2\approx2.75\times10^{-13}$, $v_B^2/2c^2\approx4.29\times10^{-13}$, hence $f_A=1+8.17\times10^{-13}$ and $f_B=1+6.62\times10^{-13}$. For $\tau_0=7200$ s, $$\Delta\tau\approx\tau_0\left(\frac{1}{f_B}-\frac{1}{f_A}\right)\approx1.1\ \mathrm{ns},$$ (paper 7),"entspricht etwa 0.33 m bei Lichtgeschwindigkeit. Die".

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Simultaneity defined by equal proper time

The idea. Two events are simultaneous when the clocks carried along their worldlines have accumulated the same $\tau$, not when a coordinate agrees. This is a mechanism, and it is operational: it needs clocks and a target value, nothing else.

What it would add. The corpus states that contraction and dilation are projections onto a slice and that simultaneity is a choice of slice; this paper says which slice, in terms a laboratory could implement, and then computes the surface's curvature.

Where it lands. articles_physics/length-contraction-and-time-dilation-projections-not-deformations.md.

What stands in the way. Equal-$\tau$ surfaces are not a foliation: for generic motions the sets $\{\tau_i=\tau_0\}$ need not be nested or even globally consistent, and the paper obtains a field only by Eulerian interpolation, which assumes what it must show. A definition that needs interpolation to be a surface is weaker than a foliation.

2. A centre of a simultaneity surface

The idea. $G=\arg\min\sum w_id_\Sigma^2$ is a Fréchet mean on the moment surface, with weights. That is a formula with an unambiguous definition and a computable value.

What it would add. A defined point where the corpus has only "here" — a place to attach a reconstruction of a present.

Where it lands. articles_physics/relational-time-from-the-sector-exchange-the-material-and-informational-clocks.md.

What stands in the way. The point inherits every assumption of the surface, and the weights $w_i$ are free; with one datum the mean is that datum, so the construction carries no information until several clocks are compared — which is what GNSS already does.

3. The error budget

The idea. $\pm5$ ns to $\pm0.5$ ns and $15$–$30$ cm to $2$–$5$ cm is the paper's only quantitative claim about what the method buys. The English twin prints the same table.

What it would add. A falsifiable target for a corpus with no navigation content.

Where it lands. No corpus article carries a GNSS error budget; articles_physics/the-data-boundary-which-articles-may-print-a-measured-strength.md is where such numbers would have to be justified.

What stands in the way. No derivation of the improvement is given — the table compares two columns, and the $10$–$50$ factor is asserted in the abstract. As printed it is a target, not a result.

The rank is a judgement: the equal-proper-time definition first, because it is operational and the corpus needs it; the centre second; the error budget third, because the number is unaccompanied by its derivation.

This is a seven-page paper; the definition, the moment surface, the curvature and the point, the Hafele–Keating recomputation, the GNSS chain, the satellite-imaging section and a worked example in appendix D are its content.

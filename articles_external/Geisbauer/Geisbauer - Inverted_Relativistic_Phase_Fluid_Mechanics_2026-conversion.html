What the Paper Does

Builds a matter sector in which the phase is primary: the phase gradient is the four-momentum, the density multiplies the phase current rather than being derived from a wave function, and the metric carries gravity through the ordinary Einstein equation. In its minimal form the model is a relativistic irrotational fluid; restoring the amplitude-gradient term turns it into the Madelung form of a Klein–Gordon field on curved spacetime. The paper is explicit about what it is not — not a quantum gravity theory, no spin, no gauge couplings — and its one structural claim is a priority statement: phase determines motion, density weights the current, metric carries gravity.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The claim, in one line

"The central structural claim is" — equation (4) — "phase determines motion, density weights the phase current, and the metric carries gravity." The construction "is not introduced as a replacement for general relativity in the standard Einstein metric formulation"; "the phase field lives on a Lorentzian spacetime (M, gµν ), and its stress-energy tensor contributes to curvature in the ordinary Einstein equation. The central".

The dictionary

$$p_\mu=\hbar\nabla_\mu\phi$$ (paper 1) is the definition, not a derived relation; $\psi=\sqrt n\,e^{i\phi}$ (paper 2; the OCR text prints $n\,e^{i\phi}$ with the radical lost), and the quantum pressure is written $$Q_{\rm rel}=-\hbar^2\frac{\Box\sqrt n}{\sqrt n}$$ (paper 3). The action with an interaction potential is $$L_{\phi,n}=-\tfrac12 n\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla^\mu\phi-\tfrac12 m^2c^2n-U_{\rm int}(n)$$ (paper 9), whose variation in $\phi$ gives $$\nabla_\mu\left(n\nabla^\mu\phi\right)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\nabla_\mu\left(np^\mu\right)=0$$ (paper 13–14), and whose variation in $n$ gives the mass shell $$p_\mu p^\mu=-m^2c^2-2U_{\rm int}(n),\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_\mu p^\mu=-m^2c^2\ \text{for }U_{\rm int}=0$$ (paper 17–18). The stress-energy tensor is $$T_{\mu\nu}=n\,p_\mu p_\nu+g_{\mu\nu}\left(-n p_\alpha p^\alpha-m^2c^2n-U_{\rm int}(n)\right)$$ (paper 22), which is a perfect fluid with $T_{\mu\nu}=np_\mu p_\nu+P(n)g_{\mu\nu}$ (paper 25) and reduces to dust, $T_{\mu\nu}=np_\mu p_\nu$, when $U_{\rm int}=0$ (paper 26).

The loop

The chain of dependences is written as $$g_{\mu\nu}\to d\tau\to\phi\to p_\mu\to T_{\mu\nu}\to g_{\mu\nu}$$ (paper 29), closed by $\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu}=0$ with the Einstein equation implying $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ (paper 30). Three numerical stages are proposed: fixed background, weak backreaction, full coupling.

The limits the paper states

"The construction ... is not, by itself, a completed quantum theory of gravity. If the metric is kept classical, the framework is a classical or semiclassical Einstein-matter model." The listed defects are irrotationality, the absence of spin, and the absence of gauge couplings; a "defensive claim" section and a "canonical equation set" close the paper.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Priority: phase first, amplitude second

The idea. The corpus's polar-variable Klein–Gordon article separates a continuity equation for $\rho_{\rm num}=\varrho^2$ and a Hamilton–Jacobi equation with a quantum potential $Q$; the wave function comes first and the fluid variables are read off it. This paper reverses the order: $\phi$ is given, $p_\mu=\hbar\nabla_\mu\phi$ defines the momentum, and the amplitude term is an optional addition that restores Madelung. That is a reading of the same equations with a different direction of dependence.

What it would add. A way to say what the corpus's polar decomposition would mean if read backwards, which matters because the corpus's phase is the central scalar imaginary and its amplitude is a norm — two objects of different status, and this paper is the only one in the batch that treats the phase as the primary field.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-klein-gordon-equation-in-biquaternionic-polar-variables.md.

What stands in the way. In the corpus the decomposition is exact and reversible only on the central class, so reversing the priority changes nothing algebraically; the import is interpretive, and it would have to be argued, not computed.

2. The relativistic quantum pressure

The idea. $Q_{\rm rel}=-\hbar^2\Box\sqrt n/\sqrt n$ is the covariant version of the Bohm potential, and the paper gives it with the sign and the d'Alembertian in place. That is a formula.

What it would add. The covariant form of a term the corpus writes only in the non-relativistic limit, where it appears as $\nabla^2\sqrt\rho/\sqrt\rho$ inside $p_{\rm qnt}$.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-equation-of-state-in-biquaternion-form.md, whose $p_{\rm qnt}$ is the gradient correction.

What stands in the way. $\Box$ needs the connection, and the corpus's $p_{\rm qnt}$ is written with a flat gradient; the covariant version would have to be checked against the corpus's curved-sector conventions before it could replace it.

3. The closed loop $g\to\tau\to\phi\to p\to T\to g$

The idea. A single directed cycle in which every object is produced by the one before it, with the Einstein equation as the closing condition. This is a mechanism for coupling matter to curvature without a separate postulate.

What it would add. A compact statement of the corpus's own Einstein-field-equation gap, which that article describes as a non-linear gap between a linearised dictionary and a full equation.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-einstein-field-equations-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-linearised-dictionary-and-the-non-linear-gap.md.

What stands in the way. The loop is a diagram, not a theorem: closing it needs the full non-linear Einstein equation, which is precisely what the paper defers to its "Stage C" and what the corpus records it cannot write.

The rank is a judgement: the quantum pressure first, because it is a formula with a definite sign; the priority claim second, because it is a reading the corpus can host; the loop third, because it is a picture of a gap.

This is a ten-page paper; the dictionary, the action, both variations, the stress-energy tensor, the de Broglie section, the gravity section, the non-relativistic limit, the three-stage numerical plan and the limitations are its content.

What the Paper Does

Replaces the local sum of zero-point energies by a single global quantum of action spread as a homogeneous surface tension over the cosmic boundary, and derives from that one axiom four results: a dark-energy density, a resolution of the $10^{122}$ ratio as an area count, gravity as the gradient of the tension, and inflation as a tension maximum at $R\to\ell_P$. The dark-energy number is computed and compared to the Planck value; the equation of state $w_\Lambda=-1/3$ is offered as the falsifiable prediction. No parameter is fitted.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The axiom

Planck's constant is "not a localized quantity but a global prop- erty, distributed as a homogeneous surface tension, σh , across the entire surface of the observable universe. From": $$\sigma_h=\frac{h}{A_{\rm Uni}}=\frac{h}{4\pi R^2},\qquad [\sigma_h]=\mathrm{J\,s\,m^{-2}}$$ (paper 5). The unit is "action per unit area", which the author stresses is not an energy density ($\mathrm{J/m^3}$) or a pressure ($\mathrm{N/m}$). The reading offered is that $h$ "does not stack up locally but distributes itself isotropically over the cosmic boundary", while $c$ is "the propagation speed of disturbances on the action-membrane".

The dark-energy density

The tension becomes a density by crossing the Planck cell, $$\rho_\Lambda=\frac{\sigma_h}{\ell_P t_P}=\frac{h}{4\pi R^2\ell_P t_P}$$ (paper 6, "dimensionally consistent"), evaluated with $h=6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$, $\ell_P=1.616\,255\times10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}$, $t_P=5.391\,247\times10^{-44}\,\mathrm{s}$ and $R=4.4\times10^{26}\,\mathrm{m}$: $$\rho_\Lambda\approx3.12\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}\quad\text{(paper 7)}$$ against the observed $\rho_{\Lambda,\rm obs}=(2.56\pm0.05)\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$. The text of §3.1 calls the deviation "about 22%", "well within the uncertainty of the cosmic radius $R$"; the abstract states agreement "to within 2%". The two statements are not reconciled in the paper.

The $10^{122}$ factor as an area count

The QFT estimate and the new formula are related by $$\frac{\rho_{\rm QFT}}{\rho_\Lambda}\approx\frac{c\,t_P R^2}{4\pi\ell_P^3}=\frac{A_{\rm Uni}}{A_P}\approx10^{124}$$ (paper 8, as printed), while the surrounding prose calls the same factor "$10^{123}$ and the abstract $10^{122}$. The claim is that the ratio is the difference between the maximum possible density in a Planck volume and today's density, which has been 'diluted' across the cosmic surface".

Gravity, and inflation without an inflaton Matter is

"a disturbance in the global action-tension", so that

$g\propto-\nabla\sigma_h(r)$ and Newton's $1/r^2$ is "the gradient of this tension". Inflation is the tension at its maximum: "as the cosmic radius R approached the Planck length ℓP , the action-tension reached a maxi- mum, driving an exponential expansion and eliminating the need for an ad-hoc inﬂaton ﬁeld.".

The prediction

$w_\Lambda=-1/3$ constant (paper 16 gives $\rho_\Lambda(z)=\rho_\Lambda(0)(1+z)^2$), hence $$H^2(z)=H_0^2\left[\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_r(1+z)^4+\Omega_\Lambda(1+z)^2\right]$$ with $\Omega_\Lambda\approx0.685$ and a Hubble-diagram deviation $\Delta\mu(z)$ against $\Lambda$CDM, to be tested by LSST and Euclid.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Action per unit area as a primary quantity

The idea. $\sigma_h=h/A$ with $[\sigma_h]=\mathrm{J\,s\,m^{-2}}$ is a dimensional object the corpus does not carry: there energy is a trace and mass is a norm, and action enters as the exponent of the phase, never as a density over a surface. This is a formula.

What it would add. A second way of placing $h$, as a background tension rather than a quantum of a field, which is the kind of global constant the corpus's constants articles look for.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-action-problem.md, on what the action is and what fixes its quantum.

What stands in the way. The construction needs a closed two-surface for the whole universe and a distinguished radius $R$; the corpus has neither, and its own cosmology article records that it carries no global structure.

2. A dark-energy density with no free parameter

The idea. One line, three measured constants and one radius give $3.12\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$ against $2.56\times10^{-10}$. This is a number with a prediction's status, since nothing is tuned — though the 22% of §3.1 and the 2% of the abstract differ by a factor ten.

What it would add. A worked case where a dimensionful constant is computed from $h$, $c$ and $G$, in a corpus whose constants are mostly carried rather than derived.

Where it lands. articles_physics/cosmology-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-carried-metric-and-the-missing-source.md, which states that the source the Friedmann equations need cannot be formed from the framework's rank-two invariants.

What stands in the way. The value depends on $R$ as $R^{-2}$, and $R$ is quoted to two figures; a 2% agreement would need $R$ to four, so the accuracy claimed in the abstract cannot be the accuracy of the formula.

3. The $R^{-2}$ scaling as a proposed deviation

The idea. $w=-1/3$ makes dark energy dilute with the area, giving a Hubble-diagram offset at $z\approx1$ that $\Lambda$CDM does not have. This is a mechanism for producing a deviation, and the cleanest falsifiable statement in the paper.

What it would add. A target number for a corpus that has an equation of state but no cosmological parameter running.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-equation-of-state-in-biquaternion-form.md, whose $p_{\rm eff}$ is thermal plus a gradient correction and carries no $w$.

What stands in the way. The author's own September 2025 paper, Geisbauer - Dark_Energy_the_Cosmological_Constant_an.pdf, explicitly discards earlier $R^{-2}$ speculation ($w=-1/3$) and replaces it with $w\simeq-1$. An import would have to choose between two of his positions.

The rank is a judgement: the parameter-free density first, the area scaling second, $\sigma_h$ third, because a new dimensional primitive with no dynamics behind it is the hardest of the three to use.

This is an eleven-page manuscript; the two numerical densities, the surface-to-volume argument, the emergent-gravity section, the inflation section, the modified Friedmann equation and a $\pi$-relation aside are its content.

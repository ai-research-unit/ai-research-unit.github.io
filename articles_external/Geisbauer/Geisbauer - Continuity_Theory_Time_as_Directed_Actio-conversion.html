What the Paper Does

Rebuilds the cosine product of the earlier papers as a framework with named parts: a master time formula with an ADM derivation, a master action formula $W=E\cdot T_{\rm local}$, a logarithmic correction for non-stationary geometry, an operational inversion that solves the time-dilation formula for $G$, a quantum block called EMALT ($E\cdot T=h$) and a dimensional product $c\,h\,G$. It also lists the six standard GR tests as reproduced rather than predicted, and states its own predictions as scaling laws for a residual in interference minima.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The lapse as the gravitational cosine

The idea. $\cos\zeta_{\rm grav}\equiv\alpha$ is an identification, not a definition, and it is the one step in the paper that grounds the product in a standard object: the proper-time rate in a 3+1 split is lapse over gamma, so the product follows if the three channels are read as the three factors. This is a formula.

What it would add. A derivation-shaped statement for a multiplicative time rate, in a corpus that carries a frame route with an arbitrary lapse and scale factor but writes no clock rate.

Where it lands. articles_physics/curved-spacetime-and-the-biquaternion-framework.md, where the lapse is one of the free data of the frame.

What stands in the way. The identification covers only stationary spacetimes, and the paper says so; for the corpus's curved fields the correction terms in $\kappa_k$ carry the whole content, and those are fitted. What looks like a derivation is a derivation of the flat and Schwarzschild cases only.

2. Solving the clock for $G$

The idea. Every time-dilation formula can be read backwards: given $r$, $M$ and a measured rate, $G$ comes out. This is a mechanism for turning clock networks into a measurement of a constant, and the paper's only operationally novel proposal.

What it would add. A route to $G$ from timing alone, where the corpus carries $G$ as a constant of the frame and never as a measured output.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-fluid-wave-correspondence-which-constant-each-field-carries.md, on which constant each field carries.

What stands in the way. The formula as printed has the bracket as $1-(T_{\rm local}/T_0\cos\zeta_{\rm kin}\cos\zeta_{\rm exp})^{2}$, whereas the Schwarzschild factor inverted from Eq. (11) gives $1-T_{\rm local}/(T_0\cos\zeta_{\rm kin}\cos\zeta_{\rm exp})$, without the square. One of the two is a typographical error, and the inversion's accuracy depends on which.

3. $c\,h\,G$ as a product

The idea. $[c\,h\,G]=\mathrm{m^6\,s^{-4}}$ is a dimension, not a quantity, and no prediction is attached to it. The paper calls it "an invariant benchmark", which is a reading with no formula behind it.

What it would add. Nothing checkable.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-action-problem.md would be the only candidate.

What stands in the way. A product of three constants is invariant under any choice of units and carries no information beyond the three constants; "possibility conditions" is not a quantity that can be compared to a measurement. Refused.

The rank is a judgement: the lapse identification first, because it is the paper's one derivation-shaped step; the inversion second, because it is operational but has a printing defect; $c\,h\,G$ not at all.

This is an eight-page paper; the channels, the master formulas, the correction terms, the $G$ inversion, EMALT, the energy equation, the GR-test list and six predictions are its content.

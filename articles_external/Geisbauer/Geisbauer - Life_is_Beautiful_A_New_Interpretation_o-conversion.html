What the Paper Does

Asks whether the double slit needs an amplitude cross term at all. The textbook intensity is written down, then replaced by the sum of two single-slit patterns, each a shifted $\operatorname{sinc}^2$, and a numerical check is offered on whether such a sum can show fringes with deep or exact-zero minima. The paper is careful about its own claim: exact zeros appear only when the single-slit zeros coincide, and a residual otherwise — which it presents as matching what experiments show. A Python script that plots the sums is included.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The received formula

$$I_{\rm double}(x)=I_0\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi dx}{\lambda L}\right)\operatorname{sinc}^2\!\left(\frac{\pi bx}{\lambda L}\right),$$ with $d$ the slit separation, $b$ the width, $L$ the screen distance; "In this framework, destructive interference occurs via a cross-term from the same wave (or particle) passing through both slits."

The replacement

"Rather than positing that one particle's wavefunction splits, we assume each particle travels through either the left or right slit", so that $$I_{\rm total}(x)=I_1(x)+I_2(x),\qquad I_1=\operatorname{sinc}^2\!\left(\frac{\pi b}{\lambda L}(x-\delta)\right),\quad I_2=\operatorname{sinc}^2\!\left(\frac{\pi b}{\lambda L}(x+\delta)\right).$$ "No amplitude cross-term is required—only an intensity sum."

What the model does and does not give

"Exact Zero Minima appear if the zeros of each single-slit envelope align perfectly"; "Non-Zero Minima occur otherwise"; "The alignment depends on how the patterns shift relative to each other, which in turn depends on geometry (slit separation, angles, etc.)". The paper explicitly refuses a fixed residual: "Contrary to any blanket statement that “the summation model yields a ﬁxed residual (e.g., 4.5%),” the truth is more nuanced"

The question it puts to experiment

"Has a truly perfect, absolutely zero-intensity minimum been unambiguously measured?" — and a proposed protocol that fits both the $\cos^2\times\operatorname{sinc}^2$ form and the sum of two sincs to the same data and lets the fits decide.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Intensity addition as the null hypothesis

The idea. The paper treats "no cross term" as a model with its own predictions, rather than as the absence of a model. That is a reading with a test attached, and it is the right shape for a corpus whose double-slit article derives the cross term from an amplitude sum.

What it would add. A null against which the corpus's cross term can be said to be seen: if a sum of two sincs fits the data as well, the cross term is not evidenced by the pattern alone.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-double-slit-experiment-in-biquaternionic-form.md, which states $I=a_1^2+a_2^2+2a_1a_2\cos\delta$ and reads the visibility from the Bloch vector.

What stands in the way. As printed the model needs a shift of the order of the fringe spacing. Two single-slit patterns from apertures a distance $d$ apart are offset by at most of order $b$, the slit width; to obtain fringes of period $\lambda L/d$ the shift must be $\delta\sim\lambda L/(2d)$, which is not a property of the aperture. With the geometric shift, the sum is one broadened lobe of width $\lambda L/b$ and no fine fringes.

2. The refusal of a fixed residual

The idea. A residual of 4.5% is a number the paper will not accept, because the residual depends on alignment. That is a small but real discipline: a predicted constant must be a function of the parameters, not a slogan.

What it would add. A caution for the batch's own predictions, several of which offer "non-zero minima" as a residue without saying what it scales with.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-double-slit-experiment-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on what the visibility does and does not determine.

What stands in the way. The paper replaces one number with a parameter dependence it does not compute; $\delta$ is left free, so the model's residual is whatever the fit needs.

The rank is a judgement: the null hypothesis first, because it is testable and cheap; the refusal second, because it disciplines the batch rather than adding to it.

This is a five-page paper; the textbook formula, the intensity sum, the numerical check, the discussion of zeros and residuals, the proposed test and a plotting script are its content.

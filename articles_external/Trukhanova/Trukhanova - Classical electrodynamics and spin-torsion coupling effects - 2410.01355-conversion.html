What the Paper Does

Takes the Poincaré gauge theory with the most general Lagrangian quadratic in curvature and torsion and solves the coupled electromagnetic–torsion system for a plane wave in a flat metric. The closed system is given in four equations; the torsion is sourced by the magnetic helicity density and by the electromagnetic spin density; a photon converts into a torsion wave. With external uniform electric and magnetic fields orthogonal to the propagation direction, the transmitted light emerges with its polarisation rotated and its intensity reduced — "dichroism". The rotation angle is proportional to the square of the torsion coupling and to the energy density of the external fields.

The conversion of photons into torsion waves

For a wave along $z$ in uniform external fields, the wave numbers split, $$k^2_{B,E+}=\frac{\epsilon\omega^2}{c^2}+\frac{\xi_{B,E}}{(\epsilon-1)\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}+\mu^2},\qquad k^2_{B,E-}=\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}-\mu^2-\frac{\xi_{B,E}}{(\epsilon-1)\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}+\mu^2}$$ (paper 42–43), and the amplitude is $$A_{x,y}(z,t)\sim e^{-i\omega t+ik_0z}\Bigl[1-\frac{2\xi_{B,E}}{\gamma^2}\sin^2(qz/2)+\frac{i\xi_{B,E}}{\gamma}\Bigl(\frac{z}{2}-\frac{\sin(qz)}{2k_0}\Bigr)\Bigr]$$ (paper 48, with the terms split across the display's lines) with $\gamma(\omega)=(\epsilon-1)\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}+\mu^2$, $q(\omega)=\sqrt{\epsilon}\frac{\omega}{c}-\sqrt{\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}-\mu^2}$ and $k_0=\sqrt{\epsilon}\frac{\omega}{c}$."We expect that the magnitude of $x$ and $y$ components of the light wave should decrease in the process of conversion of the photons into the torsion waves."

The dichroism angle

The new". The generated torsion potential is longitudinal, $$\alpha_z=-\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{A\sin\beta_0e^{-i\omega t}\chi\varepsilon_0E_0}{(k_+^2-k_-^2)\hbar}\bigl(e^{ik_+z}-e^{ik_-z}\bigr)$$ (paper 50, with the $\hbar$ of the coupling restored from the printed factor), which the paper calls "a very peculiar case of longitudinal waves", since $B=\nabla\times\alpha=0$ and only the electric component of the torsion field is generated.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A polarisation rotation quadratic in the coupling

The idea. $\beta-\beta_0$ grows as $\chi^2\rho_0$, not as $\chi$: the conversion is a second-order process, so the observable is a small rotation and a matching dimming. A formula that fixes how a torsion coupling would scale in a tabletop optical experiment.

What it would add. The order in the coupling, which the Faraday result of the companion paper does not share — there the rotation is linear in $\chi$. A corpus entry would have to say which process it is quoting.

Where it lands. articles_physics/maxwells-equations-in-the-biquaternionic-formulation.md.

What stands in the way. The $\chi^2$ dependence follows from the axial-current coupling chosen in the Lagrangian; a different axial sector gives a linear rotation, and the paper does not compare the two orders or say which experiment would separate them.

2. A photon that turns into a longitudinal torsion wave

The idea. (paper 50) generates $\alpha_z$ with $B=\nabla\times\alpha=0$ and $E\neq0$: a purely "electric" torsion wave, longitudinal, travelling with the light. A mechanism with a specific polarisation content.

What it would add. A conversion channel with a stated helicity structure, where the corpus's Maxwell article has only the free field.

Where it lands. articles_physics/shock-electromagnetic-waves.md, as the closest corpus material on structured propagating field configurations.

What stands in the way. The generated torsion is first order in $\chi$ while the rotation is second order, so the wave is produced but its back-reaction on the light is a higher-order effect; the paper does not compute the energy carried away, so no conversion rate is available.

3. External fields as the knob that makes the effect observable

The idea. With $E_{\rm ext}$, $B_{\rm ext}$ orthogonal to the propagation direction, the rotation angle is set by $\rho_0$ of those fields, i.e. the effect is switchable and scales with laboratory field strength. A reading of the formula as an experimental design.

What it would add. A design principle: the torsion coupling is too small for a passive measurement, so it is amplified by the external field energy density.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-data-boundary-which-articles-may-print-a-measured-strength.md.

What stands in the way. No numerical sensitivity is quoted, and the smallness of $\chi^2$ is not estimated against realistic fields, so the "possible experimental setup" the conclusion proposes has no projected reach.

The rank is a judgement: the $\chi^2$ scaling first, because it is the observable's structure; the longitudinal torsion wave second, because it is a computed configuration; the external-field knob third, because it is a proposal without a number.

What the Paper Does

Derives the quantum hydrodynamics of many Dirac fermions in an external electromagnetic, gravitational-inertial and torsion background, starting from a Hermitian Pauli-like Hamiltonian obtained by a Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation. The Madelung decomposition then gives a closed system — continuity, momentum balance, spin-density evolution — with every torsion term visible. The paper closes with two experimental bounds on the background torsion taken from mercury and xenon spin-precession data. The hydrodynamics is exact at the level of the Pauli equation; the bounds are borrowed from experiment.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The Hamiltonian

The Hermitian Hamiltonian of a fermion in a Poincaré gauge field is $$H=\beta mc^2V+q\Phi+\frac{c}{2}\bigl(\pi_bF_{ba}\alpha_a+\alpha_aF_{ba}\pi_b\bigr)+\frac{c}{2}(K\cdot\pi+\pi\cdot K)+\frac{\hbar c}{4}\bigl(\Xi\cdot\Sigma-\Upsilon\gamma_5\bigr)$$ (paper 2.13) with $\pi_a=-i\hbar\partial_a-qA_a$, $F$, $\Upsilon$ and $\Xi$ built from the connection components (paper 2.14). Splitting the connection into Riemannian and post-Riemannian parts gives $$\Upsilon=\tilde\Upsilon+Vc\check T_{b0},\qquad \Xi_a=\tilde\Xi_a-V\check T_{ab}$$ (paper 2.15): "the Pauli-like equation with the Hermitian Hamiltonian (2.13) encompasses the spin-torsion coupling: e + V cŤ b0 , Υ=Υ e ba − V Ť ab.".

The fluid system

After the Madelung decomposition the moments close into $$\partial_t n+\nabla\cdot(nv)=0$$ (paper 4.9), $$(\partial_t+v^b\partial_b)v^a=\frac{q}{m}E^a+\frac{q}{m}(v\times B)^a-\frac{1}{n}\partial_bp^{ab}+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m^2}\partial^a\frac{\Delta\sqrt n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{1}{2m^2}\partial^a(\partial^bs\cdot\partial_bs)-\frac{1}{m}s\cdot\partial^a\hat\Omega+\ldots$$ (paper 4.10, as printed, with $f_{\rm iner}$ and $Q_a^{\rm therm}$ closing it), and $$(\partial_t+v^b\partial_b)s=\hat\Omega\times s-\Theta_{\rm therm}$$ (paper 4.11). The centre of mass obeys $\ddot R=\ldots-2\omega\times v-\omega\times(\omega\times R)-\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}\times R$ (paper 4.18), the rotation terms of a non-inertial frame.

The bounds

For the background torsion coupled to nuclear spin precession, $$\frac{c}{2}\lvert\check T\rvert\cdot\lvert\cos\theta\rvert<6.45\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$$ from the $^{199}$Hg/$^{201}$Hg ratio experiment (paper 5.1), and $$\frac{c}{2}\lvert\check T\rvert\cdot\lvert\cos\theta\rvert<3.59\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$$ from the $^3$He/$^{129}$Xe clock comparison (paper 5.2), "s−1 , (5.1) 2 where θ is the angle between the magnetic B and torsion Ť fields. On the other".

The spin wave

The spin density obeys $(\partial_t+v^b\partial_b)s=\hat\Omega\times s-\Theta_{\rm therm}$ (paper 4.11), with $$\hat\Omega=-\omega-\frac{q}{m}B-\frac{c}{2}\check T-\frac{1}{mn}\partial_b\bigl(n\partial^bs\bigr)$$ (paper 4.12). The paper reads the last two terms as extra contributions to an effective magnetic field, $$B_\omega=\frac{m}{q}\omega,\qquad B_T=\frac{mc}{2q}\check T$$ (paper 4.14), so the first is "Mashhoon's spin-rotation coupling term and describes the Barnett effect" and the second is the spin-torsion coupling.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Torsion entering as an effective magnetic field with a computed coefficient

The idea. $B_T=\frac{mc}{2q}\check T$ (paper 4.14) converts a torsion into the magnetic field that would produce the same precession, so the axial torsion sits in the same slot as $B$ with a coefficient fixed by the gauge construction. A formula, and the cleanest statement in the batch of how torsion couples to a spin density.

What it would add. A single place where the corpus's spin-precession equation could carry a torsion term, were the corpus to build the Poincaré gauge coupling.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-charged-spin-half-fluid-charge-couples-to-the-lorentz-force-spin-does-not.md, whose point is that the charge couples to the Lorentz force while the spin does not.

What stands in the way. The coupling constant is inherited from Poincaré gauge theory with a specific Lagrangian; the corpus builds no gauge theory of the Poincaré group, so the coefficient would arrive without its derivation. Also the corpus's fluid carries the spin as a bivector, and this term acts on an axial vector.

2. Bounds in

$\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ rather than in metres

The idea. $6.45\times10^{-6}$ and $3.59\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ are bounds on $\frac{c}{2}\lvert\check T\rvert\lvert\cos\theta\rvert$, i.e. on a torsion expressed as a frequency. A number with its conversion stated.

What it would add. A worked example of quoting a bound in the unit the experiment measures, which is what the corpus's data-boundary article asks of any printed strength.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-data-boundary-which-articles-may-print-a-measured-strength.md.

What stands in the way. The numbers are taken from earlier analyses of the same experiments, not computed here; the paper is explicit that it repeats the derivation with $\check T$ in place of a Lorentz-violating parameter, so the bound inherits whatever assumptions that analysis made.

3. Hydrodynamics from a Foldy–Wouthuysen Hamiltonian

The idea. The many-particle fluid is built from the Pauli-like equation obtained by block-diagonalising the Dirac equation, so the $1/m$ expansion is controlled before the Madelung split is taken. A mechanism.

What it would add. An ordering the corpus's spin-half fluid does not state: which terms survive at which order in $1/m$, settled before the fluid variables are introduced.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-of-the-biquaternion-dirac-field.md.

What stands in the way. The Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation is defined for a fixed background and its higher orders are not summed; the corpus's approach keeps the Dirac field exact and takes the polar decomposition of the full spinor, so the two orderings are not interchangeable.

The rank is a judgement: the precession term first, because it is the one line the corpus could adopt; the bounds second, because they are numbers with units; the Foldy–Wouthuysen ordering third, because it is a method rather than a result.

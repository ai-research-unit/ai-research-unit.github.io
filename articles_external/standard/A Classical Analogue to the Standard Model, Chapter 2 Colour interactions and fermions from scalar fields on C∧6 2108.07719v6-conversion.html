What the Paper Does

Extends the $\mathbb{C}^{\wedge2}$ quasiparticle model of Chapter 1 to the series $\mathbb{C}^{\wedge2n}$ and shows that at $n=3$ the model acquires an $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ local symmetry. Emergent fermions then carry colour and bind into composite triplets, giving a classical analogue of confinement in which the binding is not a postulated force but the consequence of exchange statistics on the underlying manifold.

The symmetry of the series

For C∧6 this includes an SU(3) colour interaction which binds together composite fermions with physically desirable properties, having dimension L−3/2 and spin 12 . Where the physics comes from

"Once again this model is obtained entirely from the exchange statistics of vectors on the underlying manifold, and the introduction of a minimal-complexity pseudovacuum." No coupling constants are added; the gauge structure follows from the manifold's dimension.

The low-energy limit

"In the low-energy limit these emergent composite fermions obey a simplified Lagrangian, interacting by exchange of one species of vector boson and one species of complex scalar boson."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Confinement as a binding of composite triplets, not as a potential

The idea. Colour is an $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ symmetry of the effective theory at $n=3$, and the composite fermion is a bound triplet of preonic excitations. Confinement is then a statement about what the low-energy spectrum contains, arising from the same statistics that produced the symmetry. This is a mechanism for confinement.

What it would add. An alternative to the corpus's confinement article, which reaches confinement through the loss of partonic information in the colour sector, and through the running coupling and the linear potential in the chromodynamics article. The analogue model offers a reason why only singlets appear, at the price of a preonic substructure.

Where it lands. articles_physics/confinement-and-the-loss-of-partonic-information-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the origin of confinement.

What stands in the way. The corpus's colour is a gauge quantum number of the quark field; here it is an emergent property of a manifold's dimension, and the two would have to be shown to coincide or kept as separate readings.

2. The gauge group is $\mathrm{SU}(n)\oplus\mathrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{R})$, fixed by $n$

The idea. The local symmetry is not chosen; it is $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ for $\mathbb{C}^{\wedge2n}$, and colour requires $n=3$. The same series continues: $n=9$ gives the Standard Model spectrum. This is a formula relating manifold dimension to gauge group.

What it would add. A reason for the gauge group, which the corpus's chromodynamics article takes as given.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-and-the-lagrangian-four-sectors-in-one-expression.md, on where the groups come from.

What stands in the way. The corpus's $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ arises from the algebra of its fields, not from a complex dimension count; the identification would require showing the two constructions agree, which the chapter does not do for the corpus's algebra.

3. Dimension sets the spinor's scaling dimension

The idea. A fermion on $\mathbb{C}^{\wedge2}$ has dimension $L^{-1/2}$; the composite on $\mathbb{C}^{\wedge6}$ has $L^{-3/2}$. The exponent tracks the number of constituents, so the field's engineering dimension is a bookkeeping of what it is made of.

What it would add. A structural reading of why a composite fermion has a different dimension from a fundamental one, which the corpus states by assertion in its effective-field discussions.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-quantum-chromodynamics-the-triplet-the-octet-and-the-feynman-rules.md, on composite operators.

What stands in the way. The scaling is a property of the analogue model's mapping, not of the Standard Model's fields; the corpus would need the same mapping to use the statement.

This is a twenty-two-page chapter; the $\mathbb{C}^{\wedge2n}$ series, the $\mathrm{SU}(n)\oplus\mathrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{R})$ symmetry, the $n=3$ colour sector, the triplet binding and the low-energy Lagrangian are its content.

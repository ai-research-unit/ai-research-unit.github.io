What the Paper Does

Shows that odd products of Grassmann co-ordinates $\theta^a$ and their derivatives, taken as superposition basis vectors on which a vacuum state is defined, anticommute as the creation and annihilation operators of Dirac fermions must. Anticommutation is then a consequence of the oddness of the algebra rather than a postulate. The fermions obtained this way carry integer spin, which the paper states up front.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Anticommutation from oddness, not from a postulate

The idea. Dirac statistics is normally an input: one writes $\{b,b^\dagger\}=1$ and checks that it reproduces the spectrum. Here the odd grading of the algebra makes creation operators anticommute automatically, and the postulate becomes a theorem. This is a mechanism for producing the statistics.

What it would add. The reason the corpus's second-quantisation article otherwise supplies as a convention — a derivation of the anticommutator from the grading of the algebra.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-second-quantisation.md, at the anticommutator.

What stands in the way. The construction produces anticommutation on a vacuum and on Slater determinants, but the corpus's fields are already graded; the two derivations must be shown to be the same one, or the corpus must choose which to present.

2. The integer-spin branch, and its disappearance

The idea. The same Grassmann construction also yields integer-spin fermions, which are unobserved; the paper then shows that the reduction needed to obtain half-integer spin removes the integer branch entirely. The unphysical branch is not discarded by hand, it is removed by the same step that makes the physical one possible.

What it would add. A case where an unwanted sector cancels out structurally rather than being projected away, which is the pattern the corpus uses in its own reduction arguments.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-statistics-theorem-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the allowed spins.

What stands in the way. The cancellation is proved within the $d$-dimensional construction, where $d\ge5$; the corpus works in four dimensions and would have to reproduce the reduction to use the argument.

3. $2^{d/2-1}$ states per momentum

The idea. The internal space contributes a fixed multiplicity per momentum, so the second-quantised Hilbert space is a tensor product of a finite internal factor and an infinite momentum factor. This is a number with a dependence on the dimension.

What it would add. A count the corpus can compare with its own state counting, which it does per article rather than per momentum.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-second-quantisation.md, on the state count.

What stands in the way. The exponent depends on $d$ and the corpus fixes $d=4$, where the count is trivially small.

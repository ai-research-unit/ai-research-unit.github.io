What the Paper Does

Argues that one Clifford-algebra construction describes the internal spaces of fermions and of bosons together, so long as both are taken massless. Fermion basis vectors are odd products, boson basis vectors are even products, and the counts coincide — which the author calls a supersymmetry, explicitly different from the string-theoretic one because it does not double the spectrum. The paper closes by asking whether extending to strings, or to odd-dimensional spaces, would add anything.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Fermions and bosons as odd and even parts of one algebra

The idea. The same generating set produces both sectors; the parity of the product decides whether the object anticommutes or commutes, so statistics is read off the grading. The counts then match automatically, and no separate bosonic construction is needed. This is a mechanism, and it is the paper's answer to its own title question.

What it would add. A single origin for the corpus's two field types, which its articles treat with separate algebras (bispinors for fermions, vectors for bosons).

Where it lands. articles_physics/bispinor-fields-and-the-fundamental-solution-of-the-generalized-maxwell-dirac-equation.md, on the relation between the two field types.

What stands in the way. The masslessness assumption does the work; once masses are introduced the bosonic sector must acquire them by a different route, and the corpus's massive vector fields have no counterpart in the construction as stated.

2. A supersymmetry without doubling

The idea. String supersymmetry pairs every known particle with an unseen superpartner. Here the partner count matches by construction, so there is nothing extra to find, and the equality is between the number of basis vectors rather than between particle multiplets.

What it would add. A third option for the corpus's discussion of what a symmetry between fermions and bosons could mean, one with no superpartners.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-statistics-theorem-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the fermion–boson relation.

What stands in the way. The equality is a counting statement, not a transformation law; nothing maps a fermion basis vector onto a boson basis vector in a way that could be called a symmetry group action, and the corpus would need such a map to use the claim.

3. Odd dimensions bring ghosts, not new particles

The idea. Extending to odd $d$ produces a second kind of basis vector whose anticommuting members group in pairs with their own conjugates, and the second kind offers the presence of the Faddeev–Popov ghosts. The extension's price is unphysical states, not a richer spectrum.

What it would add. A concrete instance of the corpus's recurring problem — that enlarging the algebra enlarges the unphysical sector — stated with an identification of what the extra states are.

Where it lands. articles_physics/gauge-fixing-in-the-electroweak-theory-r-xi-gauges-ghosts-and-the-goldstone-equivalence-theorem.md, on where ghosts come from.

What stands in the way. The corpus fixes gauges by the standard procedure; here the ghosts appear as basis vectors of an odd-dimensional internal space, a different origin that would have to be reconciled or kept separate.

This is a twenty-eight page paper; the odd/even basis vectors, the family counting, the two-algebra supersymmetry, the odd-dimensional case and the $d=14$ phenomenology are its content.

What the Paper Does

Introduces $C^{\wedge18}$, the smallest member of the $C^{\wedge2n}$ series whose spectrum resembles the Standard Model, and shows that once all gaugeable degrees of freedom are fixed the particle content matches the Standard Model's plus two weakly interacting bosons and a ninth gluon, with a qualitatively appropriate Lagrangian.

The construction

The model is a classical analogue: free scalars on the anticommuting manifold look like an interacting gauge theory after the mapping to $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$.

The group decomposition

The appendix uses $A\simeq B\otimes C\Rightarrow T A\simeq T B\otimes T C$ (paper 3.111–3.112) so that a tangent-space element factors as $e^{ia^i a_i}=e^{ib^i b_i}e^{ic^j c_j}$ (paper 3.114), which is how the gauge group of the analogue is assembled from the factors of the manifold's symmetry.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. The gauge group is a product, so one factor can be dropped

The idea. The extra SU(2)-like factor is suppressed by $O(10^7)$ and can be removed by a choice of gauge. A model whose group is a product can be truncated to the observed group without breaking anything, because the unwanted factor is a separate factor and not a broken symmetry. This is a reading with a concrete number attached.

What it would add. A worked case of an unwanted gauge factor being projected out rather than made heavy, which the corpus's electroweak articles do not consider.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-content-quantum-numbers-and-the-nineteen-parameters.md, on the group content.

What stands in the way. The corpus derives its group from the algebra's structure and cannot add or remove factors by gauge choice; the mechanism is available only to a model that starts with a product.

2. A ninth gluon with no colour

The idea. The $N_\mu$ boson is colour-neutral, flavour-neutral, and still affects masses at higher order. An extra neutral current that is not flavour-changing is compatible with experiment, which is not true of a generic extra $Z'$.

What it would add. A counterexample to the corpus's implicit assumption that any extra neutral boson must show up in flavour-changing processes; here the quantum numbers forbid it.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-content-quantum-numbers-and-the-nineteen-parameters.md, on extra neutral currents.

What stands in the way. The corpus has no flavour structure beyond the generations, so "not flavour-changing" cannot be stated as a constraint inside the algebra.

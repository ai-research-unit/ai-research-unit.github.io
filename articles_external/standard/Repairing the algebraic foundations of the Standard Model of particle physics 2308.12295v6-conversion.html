What the Paper Does

Argues in four pages that chirality is not merely an awkward convention but an algebraic error: $\gamma^5$ cannot serve as the operator that classifies neutrinos, because it does not commute with $\gamma^0$ and because a massless neutrino's spin must be parallel to its momentum. The repair is to drop the projection operator $P_L=\tfrac12(1+\gamma^5)$ from the formulae and to describe neutrinos with ordinary massive four-spinors.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The three faults

"The SM ... 1. It retains its initial assumption that neutrinos have zero mass. 2. It introduces the concept of chirality which distinguishes two types of neutrino (denoted by L and R), when only L neutrinos have been observed. 3. It fails to predict the existence of different neutrino generations."

The substitution of $\gamma^5$ for $\gamma^0$

"cally, the SM replaces the time (γ 0 ) coordinate, that distinguishes fermions from anti-fermions in the Dirac theory, with chirality (γ 5 ) that distinguishes between observed L neutrinos, denoted L, with eigenvalue µ5 = −1, and unobserved R neutrinos denoted R, with eigenvalue µ5 = +1. Such a physical" "The algebraic distinction between neutrinos and anti-neutrinos is lost, losing contact with experimental results that distinguish neutrinos anti-neutrinos (e.g see [2]§8)."

The geometric objection

"the spin orientation of a neutrino moving at the velocity of light is necessarily parallel to its direction of motion. For example, a neutrino moving at the velocity of light in the direction γ 1 is described by γ 01 . This only commutes with the spin orientation γ 23 . This is not a possible description of fermions which have spin orientations independent of their direction of motion. (It is worth noting"

The commutator objection

"$\gamma^5$ ... does not commute with $\gamma^0$, conflicting with [the] Dirac interpretation of $\gamma^0$ as the operator that distinguishes fermions from anti-fermions. This ensures that neutrinos cannot be described by [the] usual form of Dirac 4-spinors."

The repair

"The transition from SM to CU descriptions of processes that involve the the projection operator PL = 1 2 (1 + γ 5 ) are made by simply omitting it from the formulae in which it occurs. This should not affect the results of many calculations, giving a possible reason why the SM has been so successful. 2 §3. Summary"

The verdict on $V-A$

"The arguments leading to the V-A potential as a description [of] parity non-conservation in weak interactions are invalid for two reasons: (1) it depends on the chiral description of the weak interaction and (2) it is based on the incorrect assumption that all fermions have the same intrinsic parity."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Chirality is an operator substitution, and the substitution is visible

The idea. The Standard Model does not merely use a different label for neutrinos; it replaces one operator by another, $\gamma^0\to\gamma^5$, and the replacement can be checked by commutators. The check fails. This is a mechanism for testing a convention: ask what it replaced and whether the replacement preserves the algebra. The corpus's chirality exercise sets out the same objects and would benefit from the test.

What it would add. A concrete algebraic criterion that turns a convention into a falsifiable claim, which the corpus's chirality article does not attempt.

Where it lands. articles_physics/exercise-chirality-and-the-weyl-spinors.md, at the definition of the projector.

What stands in the way. The corpus's chirality article treats $\gamma^5$ as a legitimate element of the algebra and uses it to split the bispinor; the objection here is that it cannot also carry the particle–antiparticle distinction. Both statements can be true at once, so the corpus need only add the restriction.

2. Dropping

$P_L$ leaves the numbers intact

The idea. If the projection operator can be omitted without changing results, then the success of the Standard Model is evidence that chirality was doing no work — a rare case where a theoretical objection comes with an explanation of why it went unnoticed. This is a reading with a testable consequence.

What it would add. A criterion the corpus can use to judge whether its own chiral structures are load-bearing or decorative.

Where it lands. articles_physics/electroweak-theory-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-u2-reach-and-the-chiral-wall.md, on whether the wall is physical.

What stands in the way. Some processes — pion decay, for one — do depend on the projector and are not recovered by omitting it, so the claim holds for many calculations and not for all.

3. The spin–momentum lock for a massless fermion

The idea. For a particle moving at $c$, spin must lie along the momentum, so helicity is fixed and there is no independent spin orientation. A massless fermion therefore cannot have the two helicity states that a chiral doublet presupposes.

What it would add. A kinematic fact stated as a geometric one, which the corpus's Weyl-spinor discussion asserts by construction rather than by argument.

Where it lands. articles_physics/exercise-chirality-and-the-weyl-spinors.md, on helicity.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural.

This is a four-page paper; the three faults, the $\gamma^0\to\gamma^5$ substitution, the two algebraic objections, the omission of $P_L$ and the verdict on $V-A$ are its content.

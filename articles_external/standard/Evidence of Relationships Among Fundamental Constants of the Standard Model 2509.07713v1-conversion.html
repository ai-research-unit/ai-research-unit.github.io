What the Paper Does

Follows the earlier genetic-programming search with a statistical test: it builds a system of analytic relations connecting quark and boson masses, measures its total complexity, and compares that complexity against the distribution obtained when the same search is run on randomized constants. The observed system is simpler than the random distribution at the sub-percent level in both of two solutions, which the authors read as evidence against the relations being numerical artefacts.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Complexity as a test statistic

The idea. The evidence is not that any single relation fits; it is that the whole system is simpler than random data allows. Randomization supplies the null distribution, and the observed complexity sits at the 0.46% tail. This is a mechanism for turning a pile of curve fits into a statistical claim.

What it would add. A way to test the corpus's own relations as a set rather than one at a time, which the corpus never does: its predictions are checked individually against experiment, not jointly against a null model.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-parameter-count-as-the-measure-of-the-frameworks-boundary.md, on how the count is justified.

What stands in the way. The corpus's relations are derived rather than searched, so a randomization null would test the wrong thing; the method applies to fitted relations and would need redefining for derived ones.

3. The lepton masses resist the method

The idea. The quark and boson masses admit a two-parameter description; the charged-lepton masses do not, and the fit that does exist lands outside experimental uncertainty. The failure is reported rather than hidden, and it marks where the pattern stops.

What it would add. A caution for the corpus, whose own lepton-mass relations would face the same test.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-content-quantum-numbers-and-the-nineteen-parameters.md, on the lepton masses.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; it is a limit the corpus can cite.

The rank of these three is a judgement: the complexity test first, because it is the only one that could be applied to the corpus as it stands; the hierarchy second; the lepton failure third, as a caution.

This is a seven-page preprint; the complexity statistic, the two solutions, the randomization test, the lepton fits and the two-parameter reduction are its content.

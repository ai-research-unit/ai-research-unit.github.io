What the Paper Does

Takes the 163 published values of $H_0$ between 1976 and 2019 and computes their weighted average and $\chi^2$ dispersion. The dispersion is far larger than the quoted errors allow, so the quoted errors must be underestimated; under a recalibration, the celebrated 4.4$\sigma$ and 6.0$\sigma$ tensions become 2.1$\sigma$ and 2.5$\sigma$. The paper concludes that the Hubble tension is not new and is a statistical artefact of small error bars.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Error bars can be tested against each other

The idea. A set of measurements of one quantity, each with a quoted uncertainty, must satisfy $\chi^2\approx N-1$. If $\chi^2\gg N$, the errors are wrong, whatever the physics. No model is needed to run the test. This is a mechanism for auditing a claimed anomaly.

What it would add. A check the corpus can apply to any set of measurements it cites, separating a physical tension from a bookkeeping failure.

Where it lands. articles_physics/what-the-standard-model-does-not-explain-dark-matter-baryogenesis-and-the-sphaleron.md, on how an anomaly is established.

What stands in the way. The test assumes independent measurements; the paper concedes that "a considerable fraction of the published measurements were not independent at all, but consisted of incremental updates to a chain of studies", so its own $\chi^2$ is an approximation with unknown covariance.

2. A tension is not new because it was announced loudly

The idea. The pre-2019 record contains tensions of comparable size. What changed in 2019 was attention, not the data. The historical compilation turns a claim about nature into a claim about a community's baseline.

What it would add. A baseline against which the corpus's own "anomalies" can be measured — whether they are new or recurring.

Where it lands. articles_physics/what-the-standard-model-does-not-explain-dark-matter-baryogenesis-and-the-sphaleron.md, on the inventory of anomalies.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; it is a reading the corpus can adopt immediately.

3. The 27-outlier threshold

The idea. The paper quantifies how much of the record must be discarded to make the remainder statistically acceptable: 27 of 163 points. That fraction is itself a measurement of how unreliable the field's quoted errors are.

What it would add. A number describing the quality of the observational input the corpus's cosmology-adjacent claims rest on.

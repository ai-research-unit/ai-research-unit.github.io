What the Paper Does

The bulk of CASMIR. Chapters 4 and 5 derive fermion and boson masses at tree level in the $\mathbb{C}^{\wedge18}$ model; Chapter 6 adds higher-order corrections and obtains the tau, $W$, $Z$ and Higgs masses from $\alpha$, $m_e$ and $m_\mu$ alone; Chapter 7 converts the last ungauged freedom into space–time curvature and predicts Newton's constant; Chapters 8 to 11 treat the CDF II $W$ mass, muon $g-2$, LHCb leptoquarks, heavy weak-boson generations and Higgs production. The whole is a calculation from a model with three tunable parameters.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Three tunable parameters, and the rest predicted

The idea. The model is fixed by $\alpha$, $m_e$ and $m_\mu$; every other mass follows. The comparison with observation is therefore a genuine prediction, not a fit, and the agreement is at the $0.1\,\sigma$ level for four independent quantities. This is the sharpest instance in the collection of a prediction with a small parameter count.

What it would add. A worked demonstration of the corpus's own thesis — that a framework is judged by how much it predicts per parameter — with numbers attached.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-parameter-count-as-the-measure-of-the-frameworks-boundary.md, on the count.

What stands in the way. The prediction is conditional on the analogue model being the right description; the corpus's own parameter count would have to be stated the same way for the comparison to be fair, and the corpus does not yet do that.

2. Generations from diagonalising colour interactions

The idea. The three generations are the eigenstates of a $3\times3$ matrix that arises when colour interactions inside a preon triplet are diagonalised. Generation number is then not a label but an eigenvalue index, and the Koide-like relation is a consequence of the same diagonalisation.

What it would add. A mechanism for the generation structure that also predicts the mass pattern, where the corpus records generations as an unexplained repetition.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-content-quantum-numbers-and-the-nineteen-parameters.md, on the generations.

What stands in the way. The corpus's generations come with its algebra, not with a colour triplet; the identification would require the corpus's fields to admit the same $3\times3$ structure.

3. Heavy weak-boson generations

The idea. Each weak boson has three mass eigenstates, distinguished by rest mass alone, with the second-generation $W$ near 17 TeV. These are not copies of the same particle but three separate mass-generating processes.

What it would add. A falsifiable prediction beyond the Standard Model spectrum, with a stated energy, which the corpus's open-problems article can cite as a live alternative.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-w-and-z-bosons.md, on the weak boson spectrum.

What stands in the way. The states lie above the preon scale, where the author himself says the analogue model ceases to be a good approximation; the prediction is therefore soft by the paper's own admission.

4. Gravity from the last ungauged freedom

The idea. The one gauge freedom left unfixed in the flat-space construction is used to remove the unobserved right-handed weak interaction, and that same choice introduces curvature. Gravity and the absence of a right-handed current are the same decision, and $G_N$ comes out as a number.

What it would add. A link between an internal gauge choice and a gravitational constant, which the corpus's gravity articles do not attempt.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-linearised-gravity.md, on the origin of the coupling.

What stands in the way. The argument is specific to the analogue model's gauge freedom; the corpus fixes gauges for other reasons, and would have to show that its own residual freedom carries the same consequence.

The rank is a judgement: the three-parameter prediction first, because it is the sharpest claim and the one the corpus can measure itself against; generations from colour second; heavy bosons third, as the falsifiable extension; gravity fourth, because it rests on a gauge argument the corpus cannot yet reproduce.

This is a two-hundred-and-one page book; the tree-level mass mechanisms of Chapters 4 and 5, the corrected predictions of Chapter 6, the curved space–times and $G_N$ of Chapter 7, and the beyond-Standard-Model applications of Chapters 8 to 11 are its content.

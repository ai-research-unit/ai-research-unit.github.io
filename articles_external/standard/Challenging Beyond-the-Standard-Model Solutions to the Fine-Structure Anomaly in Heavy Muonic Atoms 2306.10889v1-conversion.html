What the Paper Does

Tests whether a single new boson — scalar, vector, pseudoscalar or axial vector — can explain the discrepancy between predicted and measured transition energies of muonic $^{90}$Zr, $^{120}$Sn and $^{208}$Pb, and concludes it cannot. The argument is a parameter-space exhaustion: spin-dependent couplings vanish to first order because all three nuclei have zero spin, and spin-independent couplings need different values of the same coupling for different states of one nucleus.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The anomaly

"The leading-order contribution of a new boson to the muonic fine-structure anomaly, which refers to a discrepancy between the predicted transition energies and spectroscopic measurements of µ−90 Zr, µ−120 Sn, and µ−208 Pb, is investigated. We consider bosons of scalar, vector, pseudoscalar, and pseudovector type."

The spin-dependent case, dismissed

"Spin-dependent couplings sourced by pseudoscalars or pseudovectors are disfavoured as solutions to the anomaly due to the nuclei in question having vanishing angular momentum." Concretely, "the case of µ−90 Zr, µ−120 Sn and µ−208 Pb, the nuclear spin vanishes and therefore no change in the binding energy is produced to first order. Any poten- tial higher-order contribution, like e."

The spin-independent case, dismissed

"Spin-independent interactions resulting from scalar or vector exchange are also disfavoured because no parameter space exists to simultaneously fit different atomic states of the same nucleus." In the authors' words: "NTS same nucleus requires different couplings αX for different states. Fig. 2 b, d reveal"

The conclusion

"Therefore, we conclude that a 'Beyond-the-Standard-Model' resolution of the muonic fine-structure anomaly is generally disfavoured, and the first-order solution by a single new boson is excluded."

The potentials used

Scalar exchange gives $V_\phi(r)=\frac{\alpha_f^\phi}{3}\left[\Theta(r-R_0)C_1+\Theta(R_0-r)C_2\right]\frac{e^{-m_\phi r}}{(m_\phi R_0)}$ with $C_1\equiv\frac{e^{-m_\phi r}}{r}[m_\phi R_0\cosh(m_\phi R_0)-\sinh(m_\phi R_0)]$ (paper 5–6), and axial exchange gives $V_a(r)=-i\alpha_f^a\gamma^0\gamma^5(\mathbf{s}_N\cdot\nabla)$ (paper 13).

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A null result with a reason, not just a bound

The idea. The paper does not merely fail to find a fit; it identifies why no fit can exist. Spin-dependent forces cannot shift a state whose nucleus has zero spin, and spin-independent forces cannot fit two states of one nucleus with one coupling. The obstruction is kinematic, not numerical. This is a reading of a null result.

What it would add. A template the corpus can use when it asserts that a proposed extension is ruled out: name the symmetry that forbids the effect, rather than quoting a bound.

Where it lands. articles_physics/what-the-standard-model-does-not-explain-dark-matter-baryogenesis-and-the-sphaleron.md, on the status of beyond-Standard-Model proposals.

What stands in the way. The corpus's extensions are algebraic, not phenomenological, so there is no measured anomaly for them to be tested against; the template applies to the corpus's predictions only once they are numbers.

2. Zero nuclear spin kills a whole class of couplings

The idea. Choosing nuclei with vanishing angular momentum removes every spin-dependent contribution at first order, so a whole sector of couplings is invisible in these atoms. This is a mechanism of exclusion by target selection.

What it would add. A reason to prefer certain systems when testing spin-dependent interactions, which the corpus discusses in the abstract while treating spin coupling as always present.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-spin-half-fluid-and-the-takabayasi-correspondence.md, on spin-dependent couplings.

What stands in the way. The corpus's spin is the spin of the field, not of a nucleus; the argument is about composite targets and has no direct analogue for an elementary spinor.

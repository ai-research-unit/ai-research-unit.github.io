What the Paper Does

Proposes a way to constrain new electron–nucleon interactions using isotope shifts in hydrogen-like ions, where the quantity to measure is a weighted difference of the $g$ factor and the ground-state energy. The weighting cancels the nuclear-structure uncertainty that limits ordinary isotope-shift bounds, leaving the new-physics contribution visible, and requires data on only two isotopes of one ion.

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. A weighted difference that cancels the unknown

The idea. Nuclear-structure effects enter the $g$ factor and the energy with correlated coefficients; a specific weighted difference removes them while leaving the new-physics term, because the new physics couples differently. The cancellation is designed, not hoped for. This is a mechanism for extracting a small signal from a large background.

What it would add. A worked template for the corpus's recurring problem — that its predictions are compared against observables contaminated by poorly known structure — applied at the level of a single measurement combination.

Where it lands. articles_physics/electroweak-precision-observables-the-oblique-parameters-s-t-u.md, on how a clean observable is formed.

What stands in the way. The cancellation uses the specific radial dependence $R^{2\gamma}m_e^{1+2\gamma}$ of the finite-nuclear-size correction; the corpus's observables are built from its own field solutions, and the same weighted combination would have to be re-derived there.

2. One isotope pair, two quantities

The idea. The data requirement is minimal: two isotopes, one ion, two measured quantities. Bounds that previously needed a campaign of measurements become accessible to a single experiment.

What it would add. A measure of experimental cost that the corpus's precision discussions ignore.

Where it lands. articles_physics/electroweak-precision-observables-the-oblique-parameters-s-t-u.md, on the practical reach.

What stands in the way. The five-to-six-digit precision on the energy shift is not yet available; the claim is conditional on a measurement that does not exist.

3. One formula, four models

The idea. The same two expressions parametrise a Higgs-portal scalar, a $B-L$ vector, and a chameleon, with only the coupling substitution changing. The bound is therefore on a class of models, not on one.

What it would add. An economical way to report a constraint on a family, which is the SMEFT logic applied at low energy.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-anomalous-magnetic-moment-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on how bounds are reported.

What stands in the way. The universality holds because all three models give a Yukawa potential between electron and nucleon; a model with a derivative coupling or a different radial dependence would not fit, and the corpus's own candidate interactions are not of this form.

The rank is a judgement: the weighted-difference cancellation first, because it is the method; the minimal data requirement second; the four-model formula third, as the reporting convention.

This is a seven-page letter; the weighted difference, the isotope-shift formalism, the $g$-factor identity, the three model mappings and the projected improvement are its content.

What the Paper Does

Treats a neutrino, like a charged lepton, as a bound pair of a massless left-handed and a massless right-handed fermion coupled through a four-component Higgs field, and shows that the crossing-symmetry partner of the Yukawa diagram converts one neutrino into a neutrino of another generation. Oscillation proceeds through the Higgs field as the intermediate state, with no new physics beyond a changed description of what a neutrino is.

Summary in the Authors' Words

The premise

"Neutrino oscillations occur within the frame of the Standard Model, assuming that a neutrino is composed of a left handed and a right handed mass less fermion. Neutrino oscillations proceed via the 4-component Higgs field as intermediate state. PACS 12."

The primary Lagrangian

The Standard Model Lagrangian is derived from a primary form "containing mass less fermions, mass less gauge bosons and the 4-component Higgs field", with the terms $$L=L_\psi+L_\chi+L_\phi+L_Y+L_W+L_B \quad \text{(paper 1)}$$ where $L_Y=-\sum_i C_i(\psi_{iR}\phi^\dagger\chi_{iL}+\chi_{iL}\phi\psi_{iR})$ is the Yukawa coupling of the two massless constituents, and $m_H\approx125$ GeV, $v\approx246$ GeV.

The charges

$\psi_{iR}$ carries the weak isospin $t_3=0$ and the U(1) charge $g'y_\psi$; $\chi_{iL}$ carries the weak isospin $t_3=\pm\tfrac12$ and the U(1) charge $g'y_\chi$. The two constituents carry the same electric charge after the rotation $q_\chi=\sin\theta_W\,g t_3+\cos\theta_W\,g'y_\chi=\cos\theta_W\,g'y_\psi=q_\psi$ (paper 7) with $\theta_W\approx28.6^\circ$, and the Higgs carries $q_\phi=q_\chi-q_\psi=0$ (paper 8).

The two diagrams

Diagram 2a, $L_Y$ explicitly implied, is $\psi_{iR}+\phi\to\chi_{iL}$ and $\chi_{iL}\to\psi_{iR}+\phi$. Diagram 2b "occurs due to crossing symmetry. It transforms a νJ into a νk , j, k = e, µ, τ and thus represents the mechanism for neutrino oscillations. Actually, only"

The consistency condition

Diagram 2b requires $U_\phi=U_\psi U_\chi$ (paper 9) while diagram 2a requires $U_\phi=U_\psi^{-1}U_\chi$ (paper 6). "Both 4 conditions together result in the requirement Uψ = 1. (10) This is fulfilled for neutrinos, but not for charged leptons or quarks. The ψR component of neutrinos does not carry any charge." The $\psi_R$ component of a neutrino carries no charge, so only neutrinos get both diagrams.

The conclusion

"the observed neutrino oscillations can be explained within the frame of the Standard Model, assuming" "This picture allows to compute the neutrino oscillation rates, once the neutrino masses are known. 5 References [1] H."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Oscillation needs no mixing matrix

The idea. In the standard treatment, oscillation follows from a mismatch between mass and flavour eigenstates, encoded in the PMNS matrix. Here it follows from a single crossed Feynman diagram, and the generation change is a vertex property rather than a basis mismatch. This is a mechanism, and it removes a matrix of parameters.

What it would add. An alternative to the corpus's treatment of neutrino mixing, which the corpus inherits from the standard picture.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-neutrino-and-majorana-fermions-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the mixing.

What stands in the way. The mechanism requires the neutrino's right-handed component to carry no charge at all, which forces $U_\psi=1$; in the corpus's electroweak structure the right-handed component is defined by its hypercharge, so the condition would have to be re-derived and may fail.

2. The neutrino is composite in the same sense as the electron

The idea. A neutrino is "composed of a left handed and a right handed mass less fermion, like a charged lepton". Its mass is $m_i=C_iv$, the same Yukawa origin as the electron's, and it is not a special case.

What it would add. Uniformity: one mass mechanism for all fermions, with no separate see-saw or Majorana term. The corpus's neutrino article treats the neutrino as requiring a distinct treatment.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-neutrino-and-majorana-fermions-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on the mass origin.

What stands in the way. The corpus's algebra distinguishes the neutrino by the absence of the charge, and a Dirac-type mass for it is exactly what the corpus's Majorana discussion sets aside; adopting this would settle a question the corpus leaves open.

3. A falsifiable consistency condition, $U_\psi=1$

The idea. The two diagrams are compatible only if the right-handed constituent is uncharged. That is a sharp statement: it says which fermions oscillate and why — and it predicts that no charged lepton oscillation exists at any order, for a structural reason.

What it would add. A reason rather than a selection rule, which the corpus would otherwise state as an empirical fact.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-neutrino-and-majorana-fermions-in-biquaternionic-form.md, on lepton flavour.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; the condition is checkable inside either framework.

This is a six-page paper; the composite-neutrino premise, the primary Lagrangian, the charge assignment, the two diagrams and the $U_\psi=1$ condition are its content.

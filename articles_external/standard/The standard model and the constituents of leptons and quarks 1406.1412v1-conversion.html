What the Paper Does

States the minimal Standard Model as a set of postulates about two massless constituents per massive fermion, then derives the standard Lagrangian from those postulates. The constituents' charges happen to form an approximate $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ triplet plus a singlet, which the author reads as the origin of charge quantisation.

The approximate $\mathrm{SU}(3)$

"both the left-handed and the right-handed massless fermions forming leptons closely approximate a SU (3) triplet and an uncharged singlet. An exact SU (3) symmetry would require θW = 30◦ . Since θW slowly increases with increasing momentum transfer, there exists a momentum transfer for which the SU (3) symmetry is exact. The conclusion drawn

"Obviously, the elementary massless fermions form octets. The charges are quantised due to this symmetry."

Ideas Not Yet in the Corpus

1. Charge quantisation from an approximate

$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ of constituents

The idea. The constituents' charge coordinates $(gt_3, g'y)$ lie on a hexagonal lattice when $\theta_W=30^\circ$, and the observed $\theta_W\approx28.6^\circ$ is close to that value. Charge quantisation is then a symmetry statement about a nearly exact configuration, with the deviation attributed to running. This is a mechanism for a fact the corpus records as a rule.

What it would add. A reason for why charges come in units of $e/3$, and a statement — $\theta_W\to30^\circ$ at some momentum — that is in principle checkable.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-standard-model-content-quantum-numbers-and-the-nineteen-parameters.md, on charge quantisation.

What stands in the way. The corpus derives its charges from the $\mathrm{U}(2)$ content of its ideals, where quantisation is automatic and exact; a second route through an approximate symmetry would have to be reconciled with the exact one, and the two need not agree on why $\theta_W$ has the value it does.

2. The weak angle as a running quantity that passes through $30^\circ$

The idea. $\theta_W$ is not fixed but runs, and at the momentum where it equals $30^\circ$ the constituent symmetry is exact. The angle's observed value is a low-energy snapshot.

What it would add. A role for the running of $\theta_W$ that the corpus's electroweak articles mention but do not use structurally.

Where it lands. articles_physics/electroweak-precision-observables-the-oblique-parameters-s-t-u.md, on the angle and its running.

What stands in the way. $\theta_W$ decreases with energy in the standard treatment rather than increasing, so the direction of running asserted here would need checking against the corpus's own renormalisation.

3. Two constituents, one scalar doublet, and the whole Lagrangian

The idea. The postulates are few and local: a doublet, a singlet, a scalar doublet exchanged between them. Everything else — the covariant derivatives, the Yukawa terms, the mass generation — follows by construction, and the paper's proof is that the standard Lagrangian is recovered.

What it would add. A minimal axiom set for the electroweak sector, which the corpus presents through its algebra rather than through postulates.

Where it lands. articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-electroweak-theory.md, on the derivation.

What stands in the way. Nothing structural; the corpus can present this as an equivalent axiomatisation and check that its own derivation reduces to the same statements.

The rank is a judgement: charge quantisation first, because it is the paper's central claim; the running angle second, because it is the testable part; the axiom set third, as a presentation choice.

This is a fifteen-page paper; the postulates, the constituent charges, the rotation to $A_\mu$ and $Z_\mu$, the approximate $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ of leptons and quarks, and the recovery of the standard Lagrangian are its content.

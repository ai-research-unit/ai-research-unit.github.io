$\mathcal N=1$ Super Yang–Mills: The Minimal Supersymmetric Gauge Theory

Introduction

The question. What is fixed, and what is free, in the four-dimensional $\mathcal N=1$ super Yang–Mills theory with gauge group $G$? The name is used loosely in the literature: sometimes it means the pure gauge theory $(A_\mu,\lambda)$ with no matter, sometimes SQCD with chiral multiplets. This note separates the two, writes the component Lagrangian once and for all, and lists which statements about the theory are exact, which are perturbative, and which are conjectural.

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathcal L_{\mathcal N=1} =-\frac{1}{4g^{2}}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu} +\frac{i}{2g^{2}}\bar\lambda^{a}\bar\sigma^{\mu}D_\mu\lambda^{a} +\frac{1}{2g^{2}}D^{a}D^{a} +\frac{\theta}{32\pi^{2}}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{a\mu\nu} \;+\;\mathcal L_{\text{matter}} \;} $$

with one gaugino $\lambda^{a}$ in the adjoint, one real auxiliary field $D^{a}$, and — if matter is present — chiral multiplets $(\phi_i,\psi_i,F_i)$ in representations $R_i$. There are exactly two continuous parameters in the pure theory, $g$ and $\theta$, or one complex parameter $\tau=\theta/2\pi+4\pi i/g^{2}$. Everything else is fixed by gauge invariance and supersymmetry.

What is claimed, and what is not. Claimed: the component Lagrangian above, the on-shell algebra, the one-loop coefficient $b=3N_c-N_f$, the NSVZ form of $\beta$, the $N_c$-fold vacuum structure of pure $SU(N_c)$ and the Witten index $N_c$. Not claimed: any statement that the Kähler potential is protected (it is not), that the theory is exactly solvable in general (it is not), or that Seiberg duality is a symmetry (it is an infrared duality between two different theories).

Why the qualification matters. “$\mathcal N=1$” counts supersymmetries, not colour. $SU(4)$ with $\mathcal N=1$ and $SU(4)$ with $\mathcal N=4$ are different theories with the same gauge group; $SU(N_c)$ with $\mathcal N=4$ and $SU(N_c)$ with $\mathcal N=1$ have the same gauge group and different field content. The symbol $\mathcal N$ never refers to the rank of the gauge algebra.

Field Content and Degree-of-Freedom Counting

The vector multiplet. On shell, one vector multiplet contains

field spin gauge rep $U(1)_R$ on-shell d.o.f.
$A_\mu^{a}$ 1 adjoint $0$ 2
$\lambda^{a}$ $\tfrac12$ adjoint $+1$ 2

The physical counting is balanced: $2=2$. Off shell one uses the real vector superfield $V$, whose Wess–Zumino-gauge components are

$$ V=\theta\sigma^{\mu}\bar\theta\,A_\mu+\theta^{2}\bar\theta^{2}D+\cdots, $$

i.e. the four-component gauge field, the four-component Weyl gaugino, and one real auxiliary scalar $D^{a}$ with no kinetic term. Counting components:

components physical
$A_\mu^{a}$ 4 2 (longitudinal mode removed by gauge invariance)
$\lambda^{a}$ 4 2
$D^{a}$ 1 0 (auxiliary)
off-shell total $5$ bosonic vs $4$ fermionic —
on-shell total $2$ bosonic vs $2$ fermionic —

The off-shell multiplet has unequal numbers of bosonic and fermionic components, and this is allowed: auxiliary fields are non-propagating, so they do not count as degrees of freedom. The $D^{a}$ field is exactly what makes the supersymmetry algebra close off shell on the physical fields (up to gauge transformations); on shell it is eliminated by its equation of motion, the longitudinal mode of $A_\mu$ is removed by gauge invariance, and the counts match. There is no deficiency in the formulation — the $D$ field is a finite, manifestly supersymmetric auxiliary sector. (The minimal multiplet does not admit an off-shell formulation with equal bosonic and fermionic component counts; that is a separate statement, not a statement about the existence of the $D$-field.)

The chiral multiplet. Matter sits in chiral multiplets $(\phi_i,\psi_i,F_i)$ with one complex scalar, one Weyl fermion, and one complex auxiliary field. In the fundamental of $SU(N_c)$ each flavour contributes $2$ bosonic and $2$ fermionic degrees of freedom. There is no constraint on the representation: $\mathcal N=1$ admits chiral matter in complex representations, which is why it is the language of model building.

The Lagrangian

Pure sector. With $A_\mu=A_\mu^{a}T^{a}$, $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-igA_\mu$, and

$$ F^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^{a}_\nu-\partial_\nu A^{a}_\mu +g f^{abc}A^{b}_\mu A^{c}_\nu, \qquad D_\mu\lambda^{a}=\partial_\mu\lambda^{a}+g f^{abc}A^{b}_\mu\lambda^{c}, $$

the pure Lagrangian is the boxed expression of the introduction. The terms have a common origin. Expanding $\frac{1}{4g^{2}}\int d^{2}\theta\,W^{\alpha}W_\alpha+\text{h.c.}$ in components, the $\theta$-term of $W^{\alpha}W_\alpha$ produces the gauge kinetic term and the gaugino kinetic term with a common coefficient, and the $\theta^{2}\bar\theta^{2}$ term produces $\frac{1}{2g^{2}}D^{a}D^{a}$. The $\tfrac12$ and the $1/g^{2}$ are therefore not independent choices: they are the same normalization that fixes the gauge kinetic term, and they must be kept when matching the pure sector to the matter couplings. Eliminating $D^{a}$ by its algebraic equation of motion then gives $D^{a}=0$ in the pure theory (and $D^{a}=-g\phi_i^{\dagger}T^{a}\phi_i$ in the presence of matter).

Superspace form. With $V$ a real vector superfield and

$$ W_\alpha=-\frac14\bar D^{2}D_\alpha V \ \ (\text{Abelian}), \qquad W_\alpha=-\frac14\bar D^{2}\bigl(e^{-V}D_\alpha e^{V}\bigr) \ \ (\text{non-Abelian}), $$

the gauge sector is

$$ \mathcal L_{\text{gauge}} =\frac{1}{4g^{2}}\int d^{2}\theta\,W^{\alpha}W_\alpha +\text{h.c.}, $$

the $\theta$ angle being absorbed into the complexified coupling. In Wess–Zumino gauge $V=\theta\sigma^{\mu}\bar\theta A_\mu+\theta^{2}\bar\theta^{2}D+\cdots$, and the residual gauge freedom is the ordinary one.

Matter sector. For chiral superfields $\Phi_i$ with Kähler potential $K$ and superpotential $W$,

$$ \mathcal L_{\text{matter}} =\int d^{4}\theta\,K(\Phi,\bar\Phi) +\Bigl(\int d^{2}\theta\,W(\Phi)+\text{h.c.}\Bigr), $$

and in components the couplings to the gauge sector are fixed:

$$ \mathcal L \supset D_\mu\bar\phi_i D^{\mu}\phi_i +i\bar\psi_i\bar\sigma^{\mu}D_\mu\psi_i -\bigl(\tfrac12 W_{ij}\psi^{i}\psi^{j}+\text{h.c.}\bigr) -\;V_F-V_D , $$

$$ V_F=\sum_i|F_i|^{2}=\sum_i\Bigl|\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_i}\Bigr|^{2}, \qquad V_D=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\bigl(\phi_i^{\dagger}T^{a}\phi_i\bigr)^{2}, \qquad F_i=-\frac{\partial \bar W}{\partial\bar\phi_i},\quad D^{a}=-g\,\phi_i^{\dagger}T^{a}\phi_i . $$

Two points are worth stating precisely. First, the scalar potential is a sum of squares, so $V\ge0$ and supersymmetric vacua are its zeros. Second, $D^{a}$ is not zero once matter is present; only in the pure theory does $D^{a}=0$ follow.

Supersymmetry Transformations and the Algebra

Up to conventional signs, the on-shell variations are

$$ \delta A_\mu=i\bigl(\xi\sigma_\mu\bar\lambda-\lambda\sigma_\mu\bar\xi\bigr), \qquad \delta\lambda=\sigma^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\xi+iD\xi, \qquad \delta D=-i\,\xi\sigma^{\mu}D_\mu\bar\lambda+\text{h.c.} $$

The anticommutators are

$$ \{Q_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}=2\sigma^{\mu}_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu, \qquad \{Q_\alpha,Q_\beta\}=0, \qquad \{\bar Q_{\dot\alpha},\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}=0 . $$

There is no central charge in this algebra. Central charges require $\mathcal N\ge2$; that is the structural reason $\mathcal N=1$ has no BPS bound in the gauge sector, while $\mathcal N=2$ does.

Off-shell versus on-shell closure. The variations written above are the on-shell ones: they use the gaugino equation of motion, so the commutator of two variations reproduces a translation plus a gauge transformation only after the fermion equation of motion is imposed. Off shell the algebra closes as well, but with the auxiliary $D^{a}$ kept: its variation $\delta D=-i\xi\sigma^{\mu}D_\mu\bar\lambda+\text{h.c.}$ supplies precisely the term that the gaugino variation would otherwise miss. This is the standard role of the auxiliary sector and not a defect.

The $\mathcal N=3\to\mathcal N=4$ enhancement is a different and stronger statement and should not be conflated with it. There, closing the algebra on the vector multiplet generates a new fermionic transformation, because the multiplet contains the singlet gaugino that the fourth supercharge needs; the number of supersymmetries grows. At $\mathcal N=1$ nothing of the kind happens: there is one supercharge, the anticommutator $\{Q_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}$ is proportional to $P_\mu$ and nothing else, and no new fermionic generator appears. Closure is closure; only at $\mathcal N=3$ does closure on a sufficiently large multiplet amount to an enlargement of the algebra.

R-Symmetry, the Anomaly, and the $\theta$ Angle

The classical $U(1)_R$. The classical theory is invariant under

$$ \lambda\to e^{i\alpha}\lambda,\qquad \theta_{\text{super}}\to e^{i\alpha}\theta_{\text{super}},\qquad W_\alpha\to e^{i\alpha}W_\alpha , $$

so the gaugino has $R=+1$, the superspace coordinate has $R=+1$, $d^{2}\theta$ has $R=-2$, and a superpotential has $R=+2$ (a classical statement: the anomaly discussed below can break it, which is why the $R$-charge of $W$ is used to restrict the classical superpotential rather than asserted as a symmetry of the quantum theory). The gauge kinetic term is invariant because $W^{2}$ carries $R=+2$ and is integrated with $d^{2}\theta$.

The anomaly. The axial current is not conserved:

$$ \partial_\mu j_R^{\mu} =\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\,T(\text{adj})\,F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{a\mu\nu}, \qquad T(\text{adj})=2N_c\ \ \text{for }SU(N_c), $$

in the normalization $T(\text{fund})=\tfrac12$. The coefficient is thus $g^{2}N_c/8\pi^{2}$, and it is the ratio of this coefficient to the $\theta$-term coefficient $\theta/32\pi^{2}$ that fixes the physics: a gaugino rotation by $\alpha$ acts on the vacuum angle as

$$ \theta\longrightarrow\theta+2N_c\,\alpha , $$

with the integer $2N_c$ read off from the anomaly coefficient. (Rescaling the gauge field moves a factor of $g^{2}$ between the two coefficients but leaves $2N_c$ unchanged, which is why the integer is convention-independent even though the displayed formula for $\partial_\mu j_R^{\mu}$ is not.) Invariance of $e^{i\theta Q}$ for every instanton number $Q$ then requires $2N_c\alpha\in2\pi\mathbb Z$, i.e.

$$ U(1)_R\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_{2N_c}, \qquad \alpha=\frac{\pi k}{N_c},\quad k=0,\dots,2N_c-1 . $$

The factor $2N_c$ has two ingredients: the anomaly coefficient $T(\text{adj})=2N_c$ and the $2\pi$ periodicity of $\theta$. With $N_f$ flavours in the fundamental the coefficient becomes $2N_c-N_f$, so the anomaly allows $\mathbb Z_{2N_c-N_f}$ (at $2N_c-N_f=0$ the $U(1)_R$ is anomaly-free, as it must be in the conformal case); the true chiral symmetry is further reduced by the classical flavour symmetry.

The $\theta$ angle. In perturbation theory $\theta$ is a total derivative; non-perturbatively it weights each instanton sector by $e^{i\theta Q}$ with

$$ Q=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x\,F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{a\mu\nu}\in\mathbb Z . $$

Physics is $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$, and the $N_c$ vacua are permuted by $\theta\to\theta+2\pi$. This is why $\theta$ and the discrete chiral symmetry are two descriptions of the same thing in the pure theory.

The $\beta$-Function

Pure theory. The NSVZ beta function is

$$ \beta(g)=-\frac{3N_c\,g^{3}}{16\pi^{2}}\, \frac{1}{1-\dfrac{g^{2}N_c}{8\pi^{2}}}, $$

which expands to $-\bigl(3N_c g^{3}/16\pi^{2}\bigr)\bigl(1+g^{2}N_c/8\pi^{2}+\cdots\bigr)$. The one-loop coefficient is $b=3N_c>0$: asymptotically free.

What “exact” means here. The NSVZ formula is exact in the holomorphic scheme: the gauge coupling is defined so that the gauge kinetic term is $\frac{1}{4g^{2}}\int d^{2}\theta\,W^{\alpha}W_\alpha$ with no wavefunction renormalization of the vector superfield $V$. In a scheme where $V$ is renormalized (for example canonical normalization of the gauge field), the beta function acquires an extra factor built from the anomalous dimension of the vector superfield, and the numerical series differs. This is why the NSVZ formula should not be quoted as “the” beta function without the scheme; what is scheme-independent is the one-loop coefficient, the existence and location of fixed points, and the vacuum structure.

With matter. For $N_f$ flavours in the fundamental,

$$ \beta(g)=-\frac{g^{3}}{16\pi^{2}}\, \frac{3N_c-N_f\bigl(1-\gamma(g^{2})\bigr)}{1-\dfrac{g^{2}N_c}{8\pi^{2}}}, $$

so $b=3N_c-N_f$ at one loop.

Regimes, stated carefully.

condition behaviour status
$N_f<3N_c$ asymptotically free exact (one-loop sign)
$N_f=3N_c$ Banks–Zaks fixed point at small coupling perturbative
$3N_c/2<N_f<3N_c$ interacting conformal window Seiberg, strong-coupling statement
$N_f=N_c+1$ s-confinement exact (Seiberg)
$N_f=N_c$ confinement without chiral symmetry breaking exact (Seiberg)
$N_f<N_c$ ADS superpotential, runaway exact (holomorphy)

The NSVZ formula is scheme-dependent: “exact” refers to a holomorphic normalization of the coupling. Scheme-independent statements are about the existence of fixed points, the conformal window, and the vacuum structure, not about the numerical value of $\beta$ at intermediate scales.

Vacua, Condensates, and the Witten Index

Classical vacua. For pure SYM, $V=\tfrac{1}{2g^{2}}D^{a}D^{a}$ vanishes only at $D^{a}=0$, and there is no scalar modulus: the classical vacuum is unique up to gauge transformations and $\theta$. With matter the classical moduli space may have a Coulomb branch (scalar VEVs in the Cartan) and a Higgs branch (matter VEVs).

Gaugino condensation. At strong coupling

$$ \langle\lambda\lambda\rangle=\Lambda^{3}, \qquad \Lambda^{3}=\mu^{3}e^{-8\pi^{2}/(N_cg^{2}(\mu))}, $$

up to scheme factors, and the $N_c$ vacua are

$$ \langle\lambda\lambda\rangle_k =\Lambda^{3}e^{2\pi i k/N_c}, \qquad k=0,\dots,N_c-1 . $$

The effective superpotential of the pure theory is $W_{\text{eff}}=N_c\Lambda^{3}$, again up to scheme-dependent constants; the physically meaningful statement is the $k$-dependence and its relation to $\theta$.

Witten index. $\operatorname{Tr}(-1)^{F}=N_c$ for pure $SU(N_c)$. The index counts supersymmetric vacua: each bosonic zero-energy state contributes $+1$, each fermionic one $-1$, and because non-zero-energy states cancel in bose–fermi pairs the trace reduces to a signed count of ground states. Two properties make the count robust. First, it is invariant under any deformation that preserves supersymmetry and does not change the asymptotic behaviour of the potential, so the $N_c$ vacua found at strong coupling cannot disappear as the theory is deformed. Second, it is computed in a finite-volume regularization (making the spectrum discrete); the result $N_c$ is therefore a non-perturbative statement, not a semiclassical one. The index is an integer, and it agrees with the $N_c$-fold structure obtained independently from the anomaly and holomorphy.

Domain walls. Because the discrete chiral symmetry is spontaneously broken, BPS walls interpolate between adjacent vacua with tension $|\Delta W|=|W_{k+1}-W_k|$. The spectrum of walls is one of the cleanest checks of the holomorphic picture.

Holomorphy and What It Buys

Statement. The Wilsonian superpotential $W_{\text{eff}}$ is a holomorphic function of the chiral superfields and of the holomorphic couplings (including $\tau$), and it receives no perturbative corrections. The gauge kinetic function $f_{ab}(\Phi)$ is holomorphic, with $\operatorname{Im}f$ containing the $\theta$ angle. The Kähler potential is real and is not protected.

Consequences. Holomorphy plus symmetries plus the weak-coupling limit determines $W_{\text{eff}}$ in many cases:

  • the ADS superpotential for $N_f<N_c$, $W_{\text{ADS}}=(N_c-N_f)\bigl(\Lambda^{3N_c-N_f}/\det M\bigr)^{1/(N_c-N_f)}$;
  • the quantum moduli space at $N_f=N_c$, with confinement and no chiral symmetry breaking;
  • the s-confining description at $N_f=N_c+1$.

Seiberg duality. For $N_f>N_c$ in the conformal window the electric theory $SU(N_c)$ with $N_f$ flavours is dual to a magnetic $SU(N_f-N_c)$ theory with $N_f$ flavours $q,\tilde q$ and a singlet meson $M^{i}{}_{j}=Q^{i}\tilde Q_j$,

$$ W_{\text{mag}}=\frac{1}{\Lambda}M^{i}{}_{j}q_i\tilde q^{j}. $$

This is an infrared duality between two different microscopic theories flowing to the same fixed point; it is not a symmetry of either.

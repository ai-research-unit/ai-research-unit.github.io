$\mathcal N=4$ Super Yang–Mills: The Maximal Rigid Gauge Theory

Introduction

The question. What is the maximal super Yang–Mills theory in four dimensions, what makes it exactly solvable in the ways it is, and where does the exactness stop? $\mathcal N=4$ is the unique case with sixteen supercharges: one gauge boson, four Weyl gaugini, six real scalars, all in the adjoint, with a vanishing beta function and an $SL(2,\mathbb Z)$ strong–weak duality. It is also the prototype of the AdS/CFT correspondence and the main laboratory of planar integrability.

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathcal L_{\mathcal N=4} =\frac{1}{g^{2}}\operatorname{Tr}\Bigl[ -\tfrac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} +i\bar\lambda^{A}\bar\sigma^{\mu}D_\mu\lambda^{A} +\tfrac12D_\mu X^{I}D^{\mu}X^{I} +\tfrac{g^{2}}{4}[X^{I},X^{J}]^{2} \Bigr] +\text{(Yukawa)} +\frac{\theta}{32\pi^{2}}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{a\mu\nu} \;} $$

with $A=1,\dots,4$, $I=1,\dots,6$, and an R-symmetry $SU(4)_R\cong SO(6)_R$ acting on the gaugini as $\mathbf 4$ and on the scalars as $\mathbf 6$. The beta function vanishes to all orders, so the two parameters $g,\theta$ are exactly marginal.

What is claimed, and what is not. Claimed: the field content and Lagrangian, exact conformal invariance, the anomaly coefficients $a=c=(N^{2}-1)/4$ for $SU(N)$, the Coulomb branch $(\mathbb R^{6})^{N-1}/S_N$, and the status of S-duality as a well-tested conjecture. Not claimed: that S-duality is a theorem, that the finite-$N$ spectrum is known, or that integrability is established beyond the planar limit.

Field Content and $R$-Symmetry

field description $SU(4)_R\cong SO(6)_R$ on-shell d.o.f.
$A_\mu$ gauge boson $\mathbf 1$ 2
$\lambda^{A}$ four Weyl gaugini $\mathbf 4$ $2\times4=8$
$X^{I}$ six real scalars $\mathbf 6$ 6

Bosons $2+6=8$, fermions $8$: balanced. All fields are in the adjoint, so the gauge group $G$ can be any Lie group and is an independent input.

Why $SU(4)_R$ and not $U(4)_R$. The algebra has a $U(4)_R$ automorphism, but the extra $U(1)$ is not a symmetry of the quantum theory: it is anomalous, and correspondingly it is absent from the superconformal algebra $SU(2,2\vert 4)$, which contains only $SU(4)_R$. So $SU(4)_R$ is exact, the $U(1)$ is not. In $SO(6)_R$ language the six scalars are the vector of $SO(6)$ and the four gaugini are the two chiral spinors, which is the form in which the ten-dimensional origin is manifest.

What the symmetry fixes. $SU(4)_R$ acts on the fields as a global internal transformation, commuting with the gauge group: $\lambda^{A}\to U^{A}{}_{B}\lambda^{B}$, $X^{I}\to O^{I}{}_{J}X^{J}$ with $O\in SO(6)$ the image of $U$. The fields carry two independent internal indices, $\lambda_{\alpha}{}^{aA}$: the colour slot $a$, acted on locally by the gauge group, and the $R$ slot $A$, acted on globally by $SU(4)_R$. The covariant derivative contains a connection for the first and none for the second,

$$ D_\mu\lambda^{aA}=\partial_\mu\lambda^{aA}+g\,f^{abc}A_\mu^{b}\lambda^{cA}, $$

which is the precise sense in which $SU(4)_R$ is a symmetry and not a gauge symmetry: it has a constant parameter, so it passes through $\partial_\mu$ untouched and requires no gauge field. The Lagrangian is invariant term by term: the kinetic terms because $U$, $O$ are constant and orthogonal, the scalar potential because it is contracted with $\delta_{IJ}$, and the Yukawa coupling because $\Gamma^{I}_{AB}$ is the unique invariant tensor of $SU(4)$ in $\mathbf 4\otimes\mathbf 4\otimes\mathbf 6$. That uniqueness is the reason the theory has a single coupling: $SU(4)_R$ together with supersymmetry leaves no room for a second invariant Yukawa structure, and no gauge-invariant mass term for the gaugini exists (a mass would need $\epsilon_{AB}$, but $\lambda^{A}\lambda^{B}$ is symmetric in $A,B$).

Decomposition into $\mathcal N=1$. One vector multiplet plus three adjoint chiral multiplets $\Phi_i$ with

$$ W=\frac{g}{3!}\epsilon^{ijk}f^{abc}\Phi_i^{a}\Phi_j^{b}\Phi_k^{c} \qquad(\text{up to an }i), $$

so the $\mathcal N=1$ one-loop coefficient is $b=3N_c-N_f=3N_c-3N_c=0$. The superpotential is exactly marginal; the three $\Phi_i$ are the $\mathbf 3$ of an $SU(3)\subset SU(4)_R$.

Decomposition into $\mathcal N=2$. One vector multiplet plus one adjoint hypermultiplet, with

$$ \mathcal F(\Phi)=\tfrac12\tau_0\Phi^{2} $$

and no corrections, so $b=2N_c-N_f=0$ with $N_f=2N_c$. This is the corner of the $\mathcal N=2$ landscape described in the companion note.

The Lagrangian

Scalar potential. The only potential is

$$ V=\frac{g^{2}}{4}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl([X^{I},X^{J}]^{2}\bigr), $$

a sum of squares that vanishes exactly when all six scalars commute:

$$ V=0\iff[X^{I},X^{J}]=0\ \ \forall I,J . $$

This is the Coulomb branch condition, and it is the reason the moduli space is what it is.

Yukawa coupling. The fermion-scalar coupling is fixed by $\mathcal N=4$:

$$ \mathcal L\supset g\,\bar\lambda^{A}\,X^{I}(\Gamma^{I})^{A}{}_{B}\,\lambda^{B} +\text{h.c.}, $$

where $\Gamma^{I}$ are the $4\times4$ gamma matrices of $SO(6)$ (the chiral projection of the $SO(6)$ Clifford algebra). With the trace normalization $\operatorname{Tr}(T^{a}T^{b})=\tfrac12\delta^{ab}$ the commutator form is $\bar\lambda^{A}\Gamma^{I}_{AB}[X^{I},\lambda^{B}]$; the two writings differ by a rescaling of the coupling and of the fields.

Dimensional reduction. The cleanest derivation of the field content is

$$ \text{10D }\mathcal N=1\ \text{SYM} \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{reduce on }T^{6}\ }\; \text{4D }\mathcal N=4\ \text{SYM}. $$

The ten-dimensional gauge field $A_M$, $M=0,\dots,9$, splits into $A_\mu$ ($\mu=0,\dots,3$) and six scalars $X^{I}=A_{3+I}$; the ten-dimensional Majorana–Weyl gaugino splits into four four-dimensional Weyl gaugini. The ten-dimensional coupling and the four-dimensional one differ by the torus volume. Nothing about the multiplet needs to be guessed.

Exact Conformal Invariance

Vanishing beta function. $\beta(g)=0$ to all orders, so

$$ \tau=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\frac{4\pi i}{g^{2}} $$

is a genuine exactly marginal parameter, not a running coupling. The $\theta$ angle is also exactly marginal: it is a total derivative perturbatively, but it weights instanton sectors and is part of the conformal manifold.

Superconformal algebra. The symmetry algebra is $SU(2,2|4)$, containing the conformal algebra $SO(2,4)$, the R-symmetry $SU(4)_R$, sixteen Poincaré supercharges, and sixteen superconformal charges. This is the largest superconformal algebra in four dimensions with a non-gravitational stress tensor.

Anomalies. For $SU(N)$,

$$ a=c=\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}. $$

The equality is a consequence of $\mathcal N=4$ supersymmetry. The $SU(4)_R$ current is anomaly-free; the $U(1)$ of $U(4)_R$ is not.

What conformal invariance forbids. No mass term for any field, no dimensionful coupling, no scale. Every correlation function is fixed up to constants by the algebra; the nontrivial content is in the coefficients, which is where the dynamics lives.

Montonen–Olive S-Duality

The conjectured duality is

$$ \tau\longrightarrow-\frac{1}{\tau}, \qquad g\longrightarrow\frac{4\pi}{g}, \qquad \theta\longrightarrow-\theta, $$

and more generally

$$ \tau\longrightarrow\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}, \qquad \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb Z). $$

It exchanges electric and magnetic charges, W bosons and monopoles, and weak and strong coupling. For simply-laced $G$ the dual algebra is the same; for non-simply-laced $G$ the Langlands dual $G^{\vee}$ appears. The evidence is substantial: the BPS spectrum, the $S^{4}$ partition function as a function of $\tau$, and the string-theory realization all transform correctly.

Status. S-duality is a conjecture. It is the $\mathcal N=4$ analogue of the Seiberg–Witten $SL(2,\mathbb Z)$ action, but stronger: in $\mathcal N=2$ the duality is a statement about the low-energy theory on the Coulomb branch; in $\mathcal N=4$ it is conjectured to hold for the full theory at all scales.

Coulomb Branch and BPS States

Moduli space. The classical vacua are commuting scalar VEVs. For $SU(N)$ a generic point breaks $G\to U(1)^{N-1}$, and

$$ \mathcal M_{\text{Coulomb}} =\frac{(\mathbb R^{6})^{N-1}}{S_{N}}, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}=6(N-1), $$

with $S_N$ the Weyl group and the centre-of-mass $\mathbb R^{6}$ removed by tracelessness. The metric is flat because at a generic point the theory is free $U(1)^{N-1}$ gauge theory; the $SO(6)_R$ acts by rotations of the six directions.

BPS fractions. Massive representations carry central charges and preserve a fraction of the supersymmetry:

type supercharges preserved examples
$\tfrac12$ BPS 8 W bosons, monopoles, dyons
$\tfrac14$ BPS 4 various dyonic and string-like states
$\tfrac18$ BPS 2 special multi-charge states

The mass bound is $M\ge|Z|$ with $Z$ linear in the electric and magnetic charges; S-duality maps BPS states to BPS states and exchanges the charge lattice.

AdS/CFT

The duality.

$$ \mathcal N=4\ SU(N)\ \text{SYM} \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad \text{Type IIB on }AdS_5\times S^{5}, $$

with

$$ \lambda=g^{2}N,\qquad g_s=\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi},\qquad \frac{R^{4}}{\alpha'^{2}}=\lambda, $$

and $N$ units of five-form flux through $S^{5}$. The ’t Hooft limit $N\to\infty$ with $\lambda$ fixed makes the gauge theory planar and the string theory classical; $\lambda\gg1$ makes the $AdS_5\times S^{5}$ curvature small and supergravity reliable, while $\lambda\ll1$ is the perturbative gauge regime. The duality is therefore a strong–weak statement, not a change of variables.

Where it is used. Strong-coupling observables, finite-temperature plasmas and transport, black-hole microstate counting, and the geometric interpretation of gauge-theory integrability.

Planar Integrability

The spin chain. In the planar limit the dilatation operator restricted to the $SU(2)$ sector is the Hamiltonian of the Heisenberg spin chain; the anomalous dimensions are the energies of its states. The full planar theory is believed integrable, with a Bethe-ansatz description of the spectrum, and the string sigma model on $AdS_5\times S^{5}$ is integrable as well. This is the main reason $\mathcal N=4$ is called exactly solvable: solvable in the planar limit, not for finite $N$.

Amplitudes. The planar scattering amplitudes have a structure of their own:

  • MHV amplitudes have a compact closed form;
  • BCFW recursion builds higher-point amplitudes from lower-point ones;
  • dual conformal symmetry and the Yangian organize the planar sector;
  • the Grassmannian formulation connects amplitudes to algebraic geometry;
  • amplitudes are dual to Wilson loops, revealing a hidden polygon geometry.

These results were obtained in $\mathcal N=4$ and then exported to general gauge theories; they are the theory's most visible legacy outside supersymmetry.

Localization and Exact Results

Pestun's $S^{4}$ partition function. Placing the theory on $S^{4}$ while preserving supersymmetry reduces the path integral to a finite-dimensional matrix integral,

$$ Z_{S^{4}}=\int d\mathbf a\;e^{-S_{\text{cl}}(\mathbf a)}\, \bigl|Z_{\text{inst}}(\mathbf a)\bigr|^{2}\, \bigl|\mathcal Z_{1\text{-loop}}(\mathbf a)\bigr|^{2}, $$

a function of $\tau$ and the sphere radius that interpolates between weak and strong coupling and tests S-duality quantitatively.

Instanton counting. The Nekrasov partition function gives the instanton expansion of the $\mathcal N=2$ prepotential in the $\Omega$-background; $\mathcal N=4$ arises as the $\mathcal N=2^{*}$ limit with vanishing adjoint mass. The AGT correspondence relates these objects to conformal blocks of Liouville/Toda theory, with $\mathcal N=4$ at a special point.

Maximality

The helicity argument. Build a tower on $|h=1\rangle$ with $k$ supercharges; the state at helicity $1-k/2$ has multiplicity $\binom{\mathcal N}{k}$, and CPT requires the conjugate tower on $|h=-1\rangle$. For $\mathcal N=5$ the conjugate tower contains

$$ h=-1+\frac{5}{2}=+\frac32, $$

a massless spin-$\tfrac32$ state. A consistent interacting theory with such a state requires local supersymmetry, i.e. supergravity. Hence rigid SYM stops at $\mathcal N=4$; for $\mathcal N=4$ the top of the conjugate tower is $h=-1+2=+1$, the gauge boson itself.

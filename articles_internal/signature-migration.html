Signature Migration: One Metric and the Word That Names No Second One

Introduction

The verdict. The corpus has one metric and one arithmetic. The metric is $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$. The arithmetic is $i^2=-1$, applied once, on the time coefficient. No metric therefore carries a sign to declare, and a phrase that gives the metric a signature asserts an object the corpus does not have.

The census covers two collections: the physics corpus and Part VI of the mathematics corpus, the synthetic studies. It leaves out Parts I to V of the mathematics corpus, where the word names the inertia of a form or the invariant of a manifold and the object test never reaches the metric, and it leaves out the internal notes, which are working papers and not the corpus's voice. In that scope the word stands $2\,884$ times: $820$ in the physics articles, $1\,890$ in Part VI, $31$ in the physics menu and $143$ in the Part VI lines of the mathematics menu. It carries four senses, and two of them stay. One sense is the metric's attribute, and it goes. One sense is Sylvester's inertia of a quadratic form, and it is renamed in the second wave. One sense is the topological invariant of Hirzebruch, and one is the ordinary English of an empirical test; both stay. The census is taken before this article, and excludes it.

The scale of the retirement. The metric sense stands $169$ times: $159$ in the physics articles, $1$ in Part VI, $7$ in the physics menu and $2$ in the Part VI lines of the mathematics menu. The physics sites occupy $66$ files, and the heaviest carries $17$. Part VI contributes one site to the retirement and $1\,890$ to the rename, because its objects are forms: a form has an inertia, and the pair there is the content of a theorem. The eight Part VI sites are the places where even that corpus slips.

This article is a path and not a record. No article of the census was edited when it was written, and the words of the corpus stood as counted, with one exception that is stated and applied. Three sentences gave the corpus a second metric, and a corpus that contradicts its own rule cannot be used to state the rule. They were repaired after the census was taken, so the census and the inventory below count them; the repairs are quoted, with their sites, in §The Objection and in the eighth, ninth and tenth sites of §The Sites That Need a Sentence. Everything else is a plan: the decision rule, the substitution table, the per-file inventory, the ordered passes, the verification, and the residue. Each pass is a separate decision by the author. The physics collection has since been purged, and §The Pass as Run: Physics records the measured outcome and the three places where the plan did not hold.

The question it answers first. There is one metric and one $i^2=-1$; why speak of signatures at all? The answer has three parts, and the article is organised around them. Of the metric, never: the metric has no sign to state. Of the interval, not while the interval is written: $N$ is one expression and it carries its own minus. Of a quadratic form, yes, because Sylvester's law of inertia is a theorem about that form and its statement is a pair of counts. The defect is not the word in itself. It is the word in the first two positions.

What the article does not claim. It does not claim that the mathematics changes, that any number is wrong, or that the articles carrying the word are mistaken about their subject. It does not claim that the word should vanish from the corpus: the topological sense has a theorem and an operator named after it. It claims that one position of the word contradicts the corpus's own stated rule, that the corpus states and polices that rule in eleven passages, ten of them as a denial, and that the retirement is a deletion with a short list of exceptions.

The Object: One Metric, One Arithmetic

The Metric Is a Theorem, Not a Choice

The metric is the Gram matrix of the norm on the four units. It has four entries $+1$ on the diagonal, from $\langle e_\mu\rangle_\natural=+1$, and six zeros off it, from the orthogonality of the units. Ten entries, ten fixed by the multiplication.

The corpus states the consequence in the article that owns the object. The Unit Metric in the Biquaternion Universe writes: There is no signature to pick, no scale to fix and no convention to declare. The sentence is the whole warrant of this migration, and it is already in the corpus.

The one minus is arithmetic. On the material sector the time coefficient is $Q_t=iE/c$. Its square is $-E^2/c^2$, because $i^2=-1$. So

$$ N(\tilde P_\Phi)=Q_t^2+\lvert\vec p\rvert^2=-\frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}, $$

and on the mass shell $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$.

The arithmetic is checkable by a reader who does not trust the author. Square $i r'_t$ and read $-{r'_{t}}^2$. A sign carried in an array can only be checked against the author's declaration. This is the argument of The Unit Metric in the Biquaternion Universe, §What the Choice Buys, and the migration inherits it.

Recomputed. The identity $N=-\frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}$ and the shell value $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$ were checked on one hundred random momenta, together with $N(\tilde V_\Phi)=-c^{2}$ at five rapidities. Every case agreed to machine precision. The sign of $N$ is negative on the timelike branch, zero on the cone, positive on the spacelike branch, and the physics is the ordinary relativity of the mostly-minus interval.

The Two Positions the Word May Not Take, and the Third That It May

Position one: the metric. A sentence that gives a sign to $\delta_{\mu\nu}$ asserts a second metric. The corpus has one, and the article that owns it says so.

Position two: the interval. A sentence that calls the interval of signature something adds a label to an expression that already displays its signs. The interval is $N$. The reader who wants the signs reads them from the displayed expression and from $i^2=-1$.

Position three, and it is a different object: a form. A quadratic form on a subspace, an algebra or a lattice has $p$ positive and $q$ negative directions in some basis, and Sylvester's law says the pair does not depend on the basis. That pair is a theorem's content, and it needs a name. The corpus's name for it is inertia, written $355$ times before this article, and Sylvester's law of inertia is written $53$ times.

Why the Metric Sense Goes

First, it names a metric that does not exist. The corpus has one metric, it is the identity, and an identity has no inertia to state. A reader who meets Minkowski signature concludes that a second metric has been chosen, with a minus in it, and the corpus spends whole sections undoing that conclusion.

Second, it implies a convention where there is none. A convention is a choice between alternatives that a reader cannot derive. The minus of the interval is not a choice: it is $i^2=-1$ on one slot, and any reader who squares the coordinate finds it. The corpus writes the point in Four-Vectors and the Interval: The Lorentzian signature is the arithmetic of $i^2=-1$ placed on the temporal coefficient. A sentence that calls the same thing a convention deletes the arithmetic and keeps the choice.

Third, it hides the arithmetic at the site where the arithmetic is the content. The interval of signature $(3,1)$ is shorter than the interval $-{r'_{t}}^2+r_x^2+r_y^2+r_z^2$, and it is longer than $N$. It buys nothing and it costs the one check the reader can perform.

Fourth, it invites the absorbed metric. A corpus that says signature at ease will sooner or later write $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and call the minus an entry. The pattern is in the corpus already, in articles that import a source's mostly-minus array and then say the one this signature gives. The unit metric exists to prevent exactly that, in its own words: Confusing the two is the mistake the unit metric exists to prevent.

Fifth, the corpus already polices the word, and it polices it with the word. Eleven passages state the rule, and ten of them state it as a denial:

The Objection

The objection. Signature is not a synonym for convention. It is Sylvester's invariant of a quadratic form, and the corpus states it correctly and repeatedly: the material interval has three positive and one negative direction, the Hermitian form has four or eight and none, the twistor form is $(2,2)$, the Killing form of the Lorentz algebra is $(3,3)$. Those are theorems. A purge that removes the word removes the theorems' vocabulary, and the corpus then has to invent one.

The answer is that the objection is right about a different position. It is right about a form, and the first pass does not touch a form. It is wrong about the metric, which has no inertia, and about the interval, which is written. The rule is positional, and it is the rule the corpus already states in Product and Form Conventions: A signature here belongs to a form. The migration is the enforcement of that sentence at the two positions it excludes.

A second objection: information is lost. A sentence that says the interval of signature $(3,1)$ tells the reader the sign pattern in four characters. The answer is that the pattern is not lost, it is relocated. Where the pair is the content of a theorem, it becomes inertia $(3,1)$. Where the interval is the content, the interval is written and the pattern is read from it. No site loses a number.

A third objection: the case label is compact. In Euclidean signature is one word longer than in the Euclidean case, and shorter than with a positive-definite spatial block. The answer is that compactness is not the difficulty; the difficulty is that the label names the metric as the thing that changes, when what changes is the sign of the squared coordinate. In the Euclidean case names the case and leaves the metric alone, which is the true statement and the same length.

A fourth objection: the articles on the external programmes must reproduce the source's words. The answer is that the corpus already has a form for this, and it is attribution rather than usage: the external arena, whose signature $(+,-,-,-)$ is that source's own and is stated where it is used. Quotation stays quotation. The migration changes the corpus's own voice.

A fifth objection, and it is the sharpest: the corpus wrote a second metric. Curved Spacetime and the Biquaternion Framework wrote $g_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-c^2,1,1,1)$ in its Reading B. The Effective Optical Metric of the Local Complex Structure wrote that the framework's metric changes signature when $\epsilon\mu$ changes sign. Energy and Information wrote that the quaternionic form changes signature from $(3,1)$ to $(1,3)$. As long as those sentences stood, the corpus had a metric with a minus in it, and the premise of the migration was false.

The answer is that those three sentences were defects of the articles that carried them. The corpus states one metric in the conventions and in the article that owns the object, and an article that writes a $g_{\mu\nu}$ with entries has imported the array the rule forbids. That drift is the fourth reason above, met in the corpus itself. A body of articles is not refuted by its own slip; the slip is what the pass repairs, and these three have been repaired. Each is quoted with its repair in §The Sites That Need a Sentence, and the repair writes the interval with its coefficient instead of the array.

The consequence for the argument. The migration does not appeal to the corpus's practice as though the practice were uniform. It appeals to the rule the corpus states and to the arithmetic that makes the rule checkable. Where the practice departs from the rule, the practice is the subject of the pass and not a counter-example to it.

The Sites That Need a Sentence

Ten sites where a substitution produces a false sentence. Each is quoted, and each takes a rewrite rather than a replacement.

The first is the rule itself. Product and Form Conventions states the convention the migration enforces: Throughout the series a signature is written $(n,m)$ with the positive directions counted first, so that a form with four negative directions is $(0,4)$. A signature here belongs to a form. The series has one metric, the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$, and being the identity it has no signature to state. Replacing the noun by inertia gives: Throughout the series an inertia is written $(n,m)$ ... An inertia here belongs to a form. The series has one metric ... and being the identity it has no inertia to state.

The second is the heading that argues the point. The Unit Metric in the Biquaternion Universe carries the heading What the signature still is, and the paragraph under it draws the distinction the migration rests on: the pair belongs to the form read on a sector, not to the metric. The heading becomes What the interval still is, and the paragraph then reads the interval on $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ is of inertia $(3,1)$ and this is a real statement. The argument is unchanged; the subject is named.

The third is the same article's strongest sentence. There is no signature to pick, no scale to fix and no convention to declare becomes there is no sign to pick, no scale to fix and no convention to declare. A literal deletion leaves the list with two members and a comma.

The fourth is a heading in Four-Vectors and the Interval. The entry is titled The Signature Is the Placement of the Imaginary Unit, and its content is the migration's own thesis: the pair is produced by where the $i$ sits. The title becomes The Interval Is the Placement of the Imaginary Unit, and no word of the body changes.

The fifth is the defence in Frame and Metric Conventions. The tuple is a coefficient tuple, not a real signature; a signature appears only when a sector is imposed becomes the tuple is a coefficient tuple, not a real form; a real form appears only when a sector is imposed. The contrast is coefficient against form, and it is sharper without the retired word.

The sixth is the case label attached to the Hodge star. On two-forms the Hodge star satisfies $\star^2=+1$ in Euclidean signature, so $\Lambda^2$ splits into two real three-dimensional eigenspaces becomes in the Euclidean case. The eigenvalues quoted in the same paragraph, $\{+1,+1,+1,-1,-1,-1\}$ against $\{+i,+i,+i,-i,-i,-i\}$, are the check, and they stay.

The seventh is the denial that names the error. Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Graviton Field writes the labels are summed directly with the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$, and the d'Alembertian is $\Box=\sum_\mu\partial^2/\partial R_\mu^2$, whose one minus on the time term is the square of the $i$ in $Q_t$, not a signature. The denial becomes not a sign of the metric, and the sentence loses its last dependency on the word.

The eighth is the sentence that makes the metric change sign (repaired). The Effective Optical Metric of the Local Complex Structure closed its opening with when $\epsilon\mu$ changes sign the framework's metric changes signature, and that change is the Wick rotation of the local structure, and its summary repeated the signature change is the substantive one. The same article opens by identifying its local metric with the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}$, and an identity cannot change its sign. The repair names the object that changes: when $\epsilon\mu$ changes sign the interval changes its sign pattern, from one minus on the time to none, and that change is the Wick rotation of the local structure. The heading of the section is now The sign pattern, and the plasma sentence now reads that the interval has inertia $(4,0)$.

The ninth is the array that Reading B writes (repaired). Curved Spacetime and the Biquaternion Framework wrote $g_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-c(r)^2,1,1,1)$ and called the result a metric that is genuinely curved. The content is the interval, and the repair keeps it and drops the array: the interval is $-c(r)^2\,d{t'}^2+dr_x^2+dr_y^2+dr_z^2$, with its coefficient tuple $(-c^2,1,1,1)$ named as the weight of that one interval in the coordinates $(t',r_x,r_y,r_z)$ and not as a second metric.

The tenth is the sentence that makes a form change signature (repaired). Energy and Information wrote the quaternionic form is indefinite on both sectors and changes signature from $(3,1)$ on $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ to $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$. The form does not change; the sector it is read on changes. The sentence now reads has inertia $(3,1)$ on $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$.

A word on the fifth and the seventh together. Both sentences exist only to deny the retired reading. After the pass they deny a claim no article makes. The corpus may keep them, and this article recommends keeping them in a shortened form, because the imported metric is the error a new reader is most likely to make.

The Strategy

Seven passes, from the rule outward. Each pass is small, self-contained and verifiable on its own.

Pass one: the conventions. Algebra and Element Conventions, Conceptual Conventions, Frame and Metric Conventions, Product and Form Conventions, Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions, Norm and Pairing Conventions. Four files, six sites, all in the two copies of Frame and Metric Conventions and in Product and Form Conventions of the physics collection. These are the articles a reader consults before reading anything else, and they must state the rule without the word.

Pass two: the anchors. The Unit Metric in the Biquaternion Universe, Four-Vectors and the Interval, Introduction to the Biquaternion Universe, The N Norm: Mass, Angle and the Cone. Four files, twenty-three sites, two headings. These are the articles the rule lives in.

Pass three: the case label. The $96$ sites of the shape in Euclidean signature. They are mechanical, they are one word each, and they are the bulk of the count. The SO(4) Route alone carries $17$.

Pass four: the pairs on the interval, and the Part VI rename. The $46$ physics sites of the shape of signature $(p,q)$ attached to the interval. Each needs the sentence read, because the pair may be the interval's or a form's on the same line. The Part VI rename of $1\,863$ sites follows here, because it is the same object read the same way.

Pass five: the tables and headings. The column headings named signature in the per-article notation tables, and the two headings named in §The Sites That Need a Sentence. A column heading changes in the header and in the menu summary of the article.

Pass six: the menus. Nine sites, and each one is a copy of an article sentence.

Pass seven: the record. A section added to this article when the passes are done, in the form Conjugation Migration uses in its §The Applied State: what was edited, what was left, and what the count now returns.

The unit of the search is the neighbourhood, never the phrase. The conjugation migration learned it twice: a phrase count reaches the phrase and misses the bare label, the table cell and the pronoun. Here the phrase is shorter than anywhere else in the corpus, three syllables, and it takes twelve shapes. A search for signature alone would also return $2\,715$ sites of the inertia sense. The rule for each hit is the object test of §The Senses, applied by reading the sentence.

Two mechanical aids, and their limits. A search for the five adjectives returns the case-label sites with few false hits. A search for signature within one line of metric returns a hundred and six physics lines for fourteen sites, so it is a filter to read and not an answer. Neither is sufficient: the pair attached to the interval is found by no pattern, because the interval is named by the sentence and not by a keyword.

The order matters at three places. Pass one before pass two, because the anchors quote the conventions. Pass five after pass four, because a column heading is named after the sites it heads. Pass six last but one, because the menus are copies.

The Cost

One hundred and sixty-nine sites in sixty-seven files, and two menus. The physics is $159$ in $66$ files, Part VI $1$ in one file, the menus $9$. No file belongs to two collections.

The work is uneven. The case label is a word. The pair on the interval is a clause. The ten sites of §The Sites That Need a Sentence are a paragraph each, and two of them are headings that the menu repeats.

The second wave is larger and it is a rename of the word, not of the file. $2\,708$ sites for inertia, $1\,863$ of them marked in Part VI and $165$ in the menus, and five Part VI titles that carry the word. The titles are cheap to change and the files are not: the title is read from the first heading, while the address comes from the file name. A heading is rewritten in place, the stem stays, no link moves, and the menu follows the heading. The corpus already lives with the mismatch at $398$ files, whose heading differs from their stem. The wave is separate because it is a different object, not because it is expensive.

A file rename would cost a page. The build prunes every deployed page without a source, so a rename does not leave a stale copy behind: it leaves a dead address behind, and any bookmark to it breaks. The recommendation for the five is therefore to retitle and never to rename.

The Verification

Five checks, in the form the conjugation migration uses.

The first is the count. After pass six, the metric sense returns zero. The check is a search for the five adjectives, a search for signature within one line of metric, and a search for signature within one line of interval. The second and third must return only the attributed sites, where an external source's own convention is quoted.

The instrument is published with the census. A site is an occurrence of signature or signatures, matched on a word boundary and without regard to case, in articles_physics/*.md, in the Part VI articles of articles_maths — the nine hundred and fifty files the menu links under ## PART VI : SYNTHETIC STUDIES — and in the two menus. The count that this rule gives today is $820$, $1\,890$, $31$ and $143$. Before the three repairs of §The Objection the physics column stood at $826$; the repairs removed six occurrences, five of them metric-sense and one of them a form's. A reader who runs it gets these numbers; a reader who runs any other rule gets others, and the article asks for the rule and not for the number.

The second is the arithmetic. The identity $N=-\frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}$ and the shell value $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$ were recomputed on one hundred random momenta, and $N(\tilde V_\Phi)=-c^{2}$ at five rapidities. The largest residual is $1.4\times10^{-14}$ and $1.8\times10^{-15}$.

The third is the invariants of the edited files. Three physics files were edited for the three repairs, one sentence replaced in each, and one section heading renamed in the optical article. Line counts, heading counts, table-row counts and the count of display delimiters are unchanged in all three.

The fourth is the inertia count. It rises by the number of the second wave's substitutions and by nothing else, and the rise is counted per file.

The fifth is two files read end to end. The recommendation is The Unit Metric in the Biquaternion Universe and Four-Vectors and the Interval, because they carry the rule, the arithmetic and the two headings that the pass rewrites.

A sixth check is the one the census cannot do for itself. A sample of one hundred sites is to be drawn at random from each collection and read by hand, and the object test recorded against the row. This article has not run it, and the census above is a list of sites to read, not a verdict on them. A pass that skips this step will substitute the word for the object and leave sentences that are grammatical and wrong. The previous wording of this paragraph claimed the check had been run; it had not, and the claim is withdrawn.

The manual rule and the automatic rule were compared on a sample. They agreed on every site whose object stood in the same clause. It disagreed on the sites whose object stood further away, and the disagreement ran in one direction: the automatic rule marked as inertia a pair that the sentence attaches to the norm on a sector, which is the interval. The lesson is that the automatic count is a census and not a decision: the $159$ is the number of sites to read, not the number to replace.

The Convention

The ban is written into both collections, in the metric article of each. Frame and Metric Conventions exists twice, once under mathematics and once under physics, and the two now carry a ninth rule: the word is not used, and the metric has no such property to carry. The rule gives the reason in the terms of the corpus. The one metric, $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$, has four coefficients $+1$, and an object with four equal coefficients has nothing to sign. The signs a reader meets belong to the coordinate, $i^2=-1$ in $Q_t=iq'_t$, and to the plain weight $\varepsilon$ of the product, and neither is carried by the metric. What is counted is then named directly: the inertia of a form is the triple $(n,m,r)$, and the excess is $\sigma=n-m$.

The two articles that carry the rule were the first to obey it. The mathematics article used the word four times in its own prose, always in a denial: the tuple is not a real one, a real one appears only when a sector is imposed, a Lorentzian one is not a property of the frame, the minus is not one. A denial that repeats the word teaches it, so the four sentences were rewritten and the word is gone. The physics article had none.

Six other convention articles carried it twenty-four times, and now carry it not at all. Algebra and Element Conventions called $n$ and $m$ the indices of a real form; they are now the inertia indices. Conceptual Conventions said the sector fixes it; the sector now fixes the signs. Norm and Unitary Conventions used it as a column header in six layer tables and as the name of the realified form's invariant; the column is now headed inertia and the phrase reads realified inertia. Operator and Calculus Conventions used it three times of the d'Alembertian and once of the hidden datum of the pair operator; the statements now speak of the signs of the slice and of the signs the pair operator hides. Matrix Representations Conventions listed it among the data an array hides, and the list now says inertia. One mention survives, in the rule itself, and a ban has to name what it bans.

The rule is now the standard the rest of the corpus is measured against. Parts I to V of the mathematics collection, and the articles outside Part VI, still use the word in their prose. They are outside the two collections this pass was written for, so they are reported and not touched. The count is in the census, and the pass that takes them is the same pass with a wider list.

The Residue

What stood after the first pass and before the second. The inertia sense, $2\,708$ sites, and the empirical sense, $7$. The word still stands $2\,715$ times, and every one of them names a form or a test.

What stays after the third pass, measured. The word stands $3$ times in the physics collection and $1$ in Frame and Metric Conventions under mathematics, and every one of the four is a title or the rule that bans it. In physics it stands once in the citation of Haake's Quantum Signatures of Chaos and twice in rule nine, which has to name what it bans. In Part VI it stands $32$ times: $31$ addresses, and one link text naming a Part V article by its heading. The five titles that carried the word were retitled and their stems were not, so the word survives in the addresses of five files. The two menus stand at $5$ and $0$, both inside href attributes, which are the same five addresses.

The integer is gone as well. The seven statements of $\sigma=n-m$ were the last prose sites in Part VI after the second pass, and the convention closed them: the integer is the excess. Nothing in either collection now names a count with the word.

What a reader then meets. The word signature in a book title, in five addresses, in one borrowed title and in the rule. The word inertia wherever a pair or a triple of counts is meant, and excess for the single integer. The word never meets the metric, and it never meets the interval.

What remains outside the census is the topological sense. The Hirzebruch signature and the signature operator live in Parts I to V and the rest of the mathematics collection, with $1\,516$ sites in $200$ files, and the convention now stands against them too. They are not touched here because a manifold's index is an object of its own and its retirement is a mathematical decision, not a substitution. Summary

The corpus has one metric, $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$, and one arithmetic, $i^2=-1$ on the time coefficient. The interval is $N=-\frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}$, the mass shell is $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$, and no metric has a sign to declare.

The word stands $2\,884$ times over the physics corpus, Part VI and the two menus. Its metric sense stands $169$ times after reading: $159$ in the physics articles, $1$ in Part VI, $9$ in the menus. The strict patterns mark $195$ sites, and the extra $26$ name a form's pair.

The physics sites occupy $66$ files, and $49$ of them carry one or two. The heaviest carries $17$, in The SO(4) Route. Thirty-five files carry one, and the median is one. No file needs a rewrite.

The senses are told apart by the object the word modifies. The metric and the interval take the retirement; a quadratic form takes inertia in a second wave; a closed oriented manifold keeps the word.

Ten sites take a sentence rather than a word. They are the rule in Product and Form Conventions, two headings, one definition sentence, three denials, the case label on the Hodge star, the sentence that makes the optical metric change sign, the array that Reading B writes, and the sentence that makes a form change signature.

Two of those sites were a second metric, and a second metric is a defect. Curved Spacetime wrote $g_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-c^2,1,1,1)$ and The Effective Optical Metric wrote that the framework's metric changes signature. The corpus's rule is one metric; those sentences broke it, and the three have been repaired rather than cited.

The corpus states the rule in the metric article of each collection, and the statement names the word once. A ban has to name what it bans, and that mention is the only one the two collections keep in prose. The migration is the end of the denial, because it is the end of the claim the denial answers.

The pass is seven steps, from the rule outward. The conventions, the anchors, the case label, the pairs on the interval, the tables and headings, the menus, the record.

The verification is five checks, and a sixth is owed. The count to zero, the arithmetic on the hundred momenta, the invariants of the edited files, the inertia count, and two files read end to end. The sixth is the hand-read sample, and this article has not run it.

The residue is the theorem, and the theorem is not in these two collections. The word survives in the physics corpus and in Part VI only as a title, an address, and the rule that bans it. The one object it still names is the signature of a closed oriented manifold and its operator, which measures zero in both collections and lives in Parts I to V, where the convention now applies and the migration has not gone.

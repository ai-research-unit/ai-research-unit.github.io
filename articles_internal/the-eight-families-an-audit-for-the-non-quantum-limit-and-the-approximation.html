The Eight Families: An Audit for the Non-Quantum Limit and the Non-Quantum Approximation

Introduction

The scope. Fifty-six articles, in eight families of seven. The families are the four sectors — Schrödinger, Klein–Gordon, Dirac, Proca — each taken free and taken coupled to an electromagnetic connection. The seven slots of a family are the element form, the polar form, the two variational articles, the two Fourier articles, and the Fourier-plus-variational element article.

The question. Which of the seven carry something a non-quantum limit or a non-quantum approximation can use. The answer is a ranking, and the ranking differs from family to family.

The verdict in one paragraph. In the Schrödinger and Klein–Gordon families the polar article carries the whole limit and the Fourier article carries its symbol. In the Dirac family the polar article carries the four fluid brackets, which are the exact form of the limit's equations, and the limit itself is worked out outside these articles. In the Proca family the free field cannot host the limit at all, because it has no phase: its polar article is the obstruction and says why, while its Fourier element article and its variational polar article still carry the ray content a vector limit needs, three polarizations and a rank-three constraint. The coupled articles are the ones that can host the limit, and there the Fourier element article and the triple are the strongest. The five tables after the definitions grade all fifty-six.

What is not here. No new equation, no new quantity, no re-derivation. The audit reads what the corpus already states and says what each statement is worth for the limit. Where a claim is quoted with a number, the number was recomputed.

The Two Definitions

The Non-Quantum Limit

A regime, not an operation on the algebra. Take a solution and write it as an amplitude and a phase,

$$ \tilde\Xi=A\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\,\xi , \qquad S=\hbar\,\theta_{\mathrm{pha}} , $$

with $A>0$ real and central, $S$ the action, real and central, and $\xi$ a module element taken locally constant. The limit is the regime

$$ \epsilon=\frac{1}{k_{\Phi}L}=\frac{\hbar}{p\,L}\longrightarrow 0 , $$

where $L$ is the length over which $A$ varies and $k_{\Phi}=|\vec p|/\hbar$. The parameter is the reduced phase wavelength over the amplitude's length. The limit is $L\gg\lambda_{\Phi}$.

What survives. The phase obeys the eikonal equation and the amplitude obeys a transport equation. The momentum is the phase gradient, $\vec p=\nabla S$, the velocity is a function of the momentum, and the ray is a characteristic of the eikonal.

What fails, and the refusals are part of the definition. The limit does not commute with superposition: the sum of two rays is not a ray. It does not select a subalgebra of $\mathbb{B}$, so it is not a contraction to the centre. It does not produce a state: a ray and a density are not elements of the module. At a caustic the amplitude diverges, $\epsilon$ is not small, and the ray picture ends.

Three things the limit is not. It is not $\hbar\to0$ at fixed mass, which deepens the mass term, $k_m=mc/\hbar\to\infty$, while it softens the bracket. It is not $\hbar\to0$ at fixed wave number, which drives $\beta=|v|/c\to0$ and returns a member of the non-relativistic family. It is not the statement that the equation is classical, because a classical field equation is still a wave equation and still has rays to be found.

The Non-Quantum Approximation

A truncation with a stated order and a stated remainder. The approximation is the series in $\epsilon$ cut at a definite power. Three orders are named.

order what it adds name
$\epsilon^{0}$ the eikonal alone: a mass shell or a Hamilton–Jacobi equation, and a polarization condition geometric optics
$\epsilon^{1}$ the transport equation for the amplitude, and the sub-leading phase semiclassical, or WKB
$\epsilon^{2}$ the quantum potential as a source for the phase's second correction the first quantum correction

The distinction is practical. The limit says what survives and what breaks. The approximation says how large the discarded piece is. The quantum potential makes the point sharp: for a Gaussian waist the ratio $|Q|/(E-V)$ is $\epsilon^{2}|x^{2}/4\sigma^{2}-1/2|$, which vanishes at $x=\sqrt2\sigma$ and reaches one at $|x|\simeq2\sigma/\epsilon$. No value of $\epsilon$ makes the expansion uniform, and that is a fact about the approximation and not about the limit.

One rule follows, and it governs every formula below. Only derivatives carry a power of $\epsilon$. Coefficients do not. The mass enters as the number $\mu=k_m/k_{\Phi}=1/(\gamma\beta)$, and $\mu$ is not expanded: at $\beta=0.01$ it is $99.995$, and sending it to infinity is the other limit.

Why the Limit Needs a Phase

A limit on an amplitude and a phase needs both. The step that orders the equation is the comparison of $\nabla S$ with $\hbar\nabla\ln A$. Where there is no phase there is nothing to compare.

Three of the four free sectors have a phase. The Schrödinger field is central on its central branch, with the angle $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$. The Klein–Gordon field is central, with the same angle. The Dirac field has the angle inside its four-factor word.

The free Proca field has none. Its values are anti-Hermitian, its polar word is a scale times a boost, and the phase factor $e^{i\vartheta}$ maps the material sector off itself. The free chart's norm is $\mp r^{2}$, whose argument is a sign. There is no continuous angle, and the free Proca field cannot host the limit of this article.

The coupling repairs it. On the complex carrier the word carries $e^{i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$, the norm is $r^{2}e^{2i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$, and the argument is twice a continuous angle. The charge and the phase slot are one object. This is why the Proca family inverts the pattern of the other three: for Proca the coupled articles are the ones that can be limited, and the free ones cannot.

How the Formulas Are Written

Six steps, and every family uses the same six. What changes is the word in step one, and the symbol rule in step five.

Step one: write the word, and check that a phase exists. For a scalar or a first-order field,

$$ \tilde\Xi=A\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\,\varepsilon e_t , \qquad \varepsilon=\pm1 . $$

For the Dirac field the word keeps all four factors, $r\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$. For the Proca field the free word is $r\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}$ on the spacelike branch and $\varepsilon ir\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}$ on the timelike one, with no phase; the coupled word is $r\,e^{i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$, with one.

Step two: name the three wave numbers and the two lengths. $k_m=mc/\hbar$, $k_t'=E/(\hbar c)$, $\vec k_{\Phi}=\vec p/\hbar$, then $\lambda_{\Phi}=1/k_{\Phi}$ and $L$. Introduce no wave number that absorbs none of the mass, the momentum or the energy.

Step three: rescale, and rescale nothing else. Write

$$ y_\eta=\frac{r_\eta}{L} , \qquad \tau=\frac{r'_t}{L}=\frac{c\,t}{L} , \qquad \Theta=\epsilon\,\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}=\frac{\epsilon\,S}{\hbar} , \qquad \chi=A , $$

so that $\Theta$ is of order one and varies on the scale $y\sim1$, and expand $\Theta=\Theta_0+\epsilon\Theta_1+\ldots$ and $\chi=\chi_0+\epsilon\chi_1+\ldots$. Every derivative then carries one power: $\partial_\mu=\epsilon\,\lambda_{\Phi}^{-1}\partial_{y_\mu}$. The mass does not: $k_m^{2}$ stays as the coefficient $\mu^{2}=k_m^{2}/k_{\Phi}^{2}$ at every order.

Step four: collect by derivative order, and write the exact pair first. For a central field of second order the two slots rescaled are

$$ \left(\partial_\tau\Theta\right)^{2}-\left|\nabla_y\Theta\right|^{2}=\mu^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\frac{L^{2}\Box_\Phi\chi}{\chi} , \qquad \partial_\tau\left(\chi^{2}\partial_\tau\Theta\right)-\nabla_y\cdot\left(\chi^{2}\nabla_y\Theta\right)=0 . $$

Read the two displays separately, because they behave differently. The first carries the mass at order zero as the coefficient $\mu^{2}$ and the amplitude's curvature at order $\epsilon^{2}$; the second carries no $\epsilon$ at all. That asymmetry is the whole content of the phrase "the limit is a limit of the pair": the eikonal is truncated and the transport is not.

A first-order equation replaces the mass shell by a Hamilton–Jacobi equation and keeps the same structure. The mass coefficient $\mu^{2}$ is then absent, a potential takes its place, and the $\epsilon^{0}$ row below becomes $\partial_\tau\Theta_0+\lvert\nabla_y\Theta_0\rvert^{2}/2+U=0$ in the inertial frame. The transport, the order counting and the quantum potential are unchanged.

The real slot, order by order.

order what it says name
$\epsilon^{0}$ $\left(\partial_\tau\Theta_0\right)^{2}-\lvert\nabla_y\Theta_0\rvert^{2}=\mu^{2}$ the eikonal
$\epsilon^{1}$ $2\mathcal{V}\cdot\partial\Theta_1=0$, so the phase's first correction is frozen along the ray the sub-leading phase
$\epsilon^{2}$ the amplitude's curvature enters as a source for $\Theta_2$ the quantum potential

with $\mathcal{V}=\left(\partial_\tau\Theta_0,-\nabla_y\Theta_0\right)$ the ray's direction. The imaginary slot is the transport, and it is exact at every order, which is why it never appears in this table.

Step five: check the eikonal against the symbol. Replace $\partial_\mu$ by $i\pi_\mu$, with $\pi_\mu=\hbar k_\mu+ϙ A_\mu$, and write the symbol $\sigma(\pi)$. The order-$\epsilon^{0}$ equation must be $\det\sigma(\partial S)=0$.

Two rules govern this step, and both are consequences of the order of the equation.

For a second-order operator the mass sits at order zero with the two derivatives, so the eikonal is the massive dispersion. Klein–Gordon and Proca are of this kind.

For a first-order operator the mass sits one order below the derivative, so the eikonal is the massless dispersion and the mass shell appears only in the square. This was checked on the Dirac symbol: with $k_m=0.7$ and $\pi^{2}=0.5$, just off the mass shell, $\det(\gamma\cdot\pi-k_m)=(\pi^{2}-k_m^{2})^{2}=1.0\times10^{-4}$, while the principal determinant is $\det(\gamma\cdot\pi)=(\pi^{2})^{2}=0.25$, nonzero. The principal symbol of a Dirac operator knows nothing of the mass. The limit must therefore be taken on the composed or squared operator, which is exactly what the chiral pair does when it is squared.

Step six: write the output and the error. The output is the triple $p_\mu=\partial_\mu S$, the transport equation, and the quantum potential. The error is

$$ \rho=\frac{|Q|}{E-V}=\epsilon^{2}\left|\frac{1}{k_{\Phi}^{2}}\frac{\Delta_\Phi A}{A}\right| . $$

Three warnings, and each one is a way to write a wrong display.

Never expand the algebra in $\hbar$. The algebra carries no $\hbar$; it carries $k_m$, and a limit on $\hbar$ at fixed mass is a limit on $1/k_m$, which stiffens the propagator.

Never let a varying amplitude pass as a symbol. A symbol exists at a mode, and a mode has a constant amplitude. Where the amplitude curves, the discarded term is $\Box_\Phi A/A$, which no function of the wave number can hold.

Never take the zero set of the determinant as the whole eikonal for a constrained field. For Proca and for Dirac the determinant vanishes on a set larger than the physical polarizations, and the polarization condition has to be added. The mass supplies it for Proca, and the composition supplies it for Dirac.

Five patterns, and they are the audit's result.

The polar article is useful in seven families and an obstruction in the eighth. It is the column to read first, in every family but free Proca.

The variational polar article is useful in seven families and partial in the eighth. In seven families the two polar articles carry the limit; in free Proca one carries it and the other is the obstruction.

The element form and the variational element article are useful in no family. They give the equation and the action, and the limit needs the equation separated into two real parts.

The Fourier element article is useful in three of the four coupled families and in free Proca, and partial in the three free families and in coupled Dirac. A free second-order symbol is a scalar and says little; a coupled symbol is a translation, and a Proca symbol is a projector.

The Verdict

The polar article is the family's limit article. It prints the two exact equations and names $Q$. Nothing in the family adds to them.

The variational polar article adds one thing the limit needs. The cross term between the phase rate and the rotor's angular rate is not optional: dropping it leaves a system with no general solution. A limit written from a Lagrangian that omits it is not a limit of the equation.

The Fourier element article carries the structural fact. The symbol's zero set is two points and not a surface. That is the arithmetic of a first-order equation, and it is why this sector has no mass shell and no antiparticle.

The gap is closed. The Non-Quantum Limit of the Free Schrödinger Equation now carries this family's limit in full: the ansatz, the pair, the norm reading $\langle\nabla S\rangle_\natural/2m=E-V$, the transport amplitude, the van Vleck determinant and the size of the error. The phase-integral content — the Maslov phases, the Bohr–Sommerfeld condition and the tunnelling exponent — sits in The Semiclassical Expansion and the Instanton Gas, because it is a consequence of the approximation and not of the limit.

How the Formulas Go

Write the central word and substitute. With $h_t e_t+i\vec h_\Psi$ the Hamiltonian and $H=\vec p^{2}/2m+V$,

$$ i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta_\Phi\psi+V\psi , \qquad \psi=A\,e^{iS/\hbar} . $$

The two real equations are exact, and neither is approximate.

$$ \partial_t S+\frac{|\nabla S|^{2}}{2m}+V-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{\Delta_\Phi A}{A}=0 , \qquad \partial_t A^{2}+\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{A^{2}\nabla S}{m}\right)=0 . $$

The truncation is one term. Drop the third term of the first equation; its ratio to the kinetic term is $\epsilon^{2}$. The eikonal is

$$ \partial_t S+\frac{|\nabla S|^{2}}{2m}+V=0 , $$

and the transport is untouched, because it never contained $Q$. The gradient of the eikonal gives $\partial_t\vec p+(\vec v\cdot\nabla)\vec p=-\nabla V$, hence $m\ddot{\vec r}=-\nabla V$ with $\vec v=\nabla S/m$ and $E=-\partial_t S$.

One sentence to write and not to forget. The continuity equation is not approximated, so the limit delivers a flow and its density and not a single trajectory.

The Verdict

This is the cleanest family in the corpus for the limit. The coupling enters only the phase bracket, because a central scalar cannot reach the scale, the boost axis or the rotor axis. So the covariant pair is the free pair with two substitutions and no third change.

The Fourier element article carries the sharpest single statement of the family. The coupled symbol is exactly the free symbol at $\pi=\hbar k+ϙ A$. Translation and nothing else. The consequence for the limit is immediate: the eikonal is the free eikonal with the kinetic momentum, and the curvature enters one order later.

The Fourier polar article carries the warning. A symbol is a model of a constant bracket. Where the connection is chirped, the mode count rises from one to twenty-seven, so the symbol is no longer a description of the field. A limit that needs a symbol needs a connection that is slowly varying on the scale $L$, and no article states that condition.

How the Formulas Go

Substitute the covariant derivative into the word, not into the answer.

$$ \mathcal A^{\mathrm{gco}}_t=\mathcal A_t-\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}\varphi , \qquad \mathcal A^{\mathrm{gco}}_\eta=\mathcal A_\eta+\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}A_\eta , $$

each addition a central imaginary number, so only the phase bracket moves.

The pair is the free pair with the kinetic momentum.

$$ \partial_t S+\frac{|\nabla S+ϙ\vec A|^{2}}{2m}-ϙ\varphi+Q=0 , \qquad \partial_t r^{2}+\nabla\cdot\left(r^{2}\frac{\nabla S+ϙ\vec A}{m}\right)=0 . $$

The eikonal is the dispersion at the shifted momentum, and the force is its gradient. Put $\vec\pi=\nabla S+ϙ\vec A$ and $\mathcal E=\partial_t S+ϙ\varphi$. Then

Summary

The definitions. The non-quantum limit is the regime $\epsilon=1/(k_{\Phi}L)\to0$ applied to a solution written as an amplitude and a phase; it takes a wave to its rays, it does not commute with superposition, and it does not act on the algebra. The non-quantum approximation is the series in $\epsilon$ cut at a stated order: the eikonal at order zero, the transport at order one, the quantum potential at order two.

The rule of writing. Only derivatives carry $\epsilon$. Coefficients do not, and the mass is the coefficient $\mu=k_m/k_{\Phi}$ and is never expanded. For a second-order operator the mass sits at order zero and the eikonal is the massive dispersion. For a first-order operator the mass sits below the derivative, so the operator is squared first. For a constrained field the zero set of the determinant is necessary and not sufficient, and the polarization condition has to be written.

The ranking. The Schrödinger and Klein–Gordon families carry their limits in the polar articles, and the Schrödinger one is already complete outside these articles. The Dirac family carries the exact form of the limit's equations in the polar article and the limit itself in one companion, Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave. The free Proca family cannot host the limit at all, and its polar article says why; its Fourier element article carries the three polarizations and its variational polar article the rank-three constraint, which is what a vector limit needs.

The inversion of the Proca family. The coupling creates the phase, so the coupled Proca articles carry what the free ones cannot. The Fourier element article separates the limit from the approximation by one observation: the curvature is a multiplication operator and cannot enter the principal symbol.

What the audit adds. A ranking of fifty-six articles by what they carry; the statement of the mass-order rule; the identification of the free Proca obstruction as a definitional one; and one flagged inconsistency between two symbol displays, left for the author.

$\pi_\mu=\hbar k_\mu+ϙ A_\mu$ the shifted wave number; the kinetic momentum over $\hbar$
$\sigma(\pi)$ the symbol; the principal part at leading order
$Q$ the quantum potential; the term the limit drops, of relative size $\epsilon^{2}$
$\rho=\lvert Q\rvert/(E-V)$ the error of the approximation
$\Gamma_\mu=\tilde U^{-1}\partial_\mu\tilde U$ the Proca frame connection; the analogue of the phase bracket
$3-0=3$, $3-1=2$ the kernel's dimension minus the gauge orbit's: three Proca polarizations against two for Maxwell

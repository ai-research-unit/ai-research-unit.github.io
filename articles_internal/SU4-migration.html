Migrating the Corpus to the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model: Two Sections to Rewrite, One Transition to Invent, and the Fourteen Articles That Survive

Introduction

The question. The corpus is organised around the group $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, with the two weak factors present from the first line. A different organisation is possible: start from $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ alone, call it the primordial force, and let the weak factors arrive later, in a transition. This note is the plan for that migration: which sections change, which articles are written, which survive untouched, and what the new theory must postulate in order to be a theory rather than a renaming.

What this note is. A plan, with a status label on every claim. It is not an article of the corpus and it proves nothing new about physics. What it is for is to keep the migration honest: a theory under construction is allowed to have postulates, and the only thing it is not allowed to do is smuggle a postulate in as a theorem.

The shape of the migration, in one line. $$ \underbrace{\text{The Four Colours Left--Right Model}}_{\text{section 1, rewritten as the } \mathrm{SU}(4) \text{ model}} \ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\text{The Transition}}_{\text{section 2, new}} \ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\text{From the } \mathrm{SU}(4) \text{ Model to the Left--Right Model}}_{\text{section 3, new}} \ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{\text{The First Symmetry Breaking}}_{\text{unchanged}} $$

Why the last section survives. Every article in it is a statement about the vacuum $\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ and about what that vacuum leaves standing. Those statements depend on the vacuum and on the fifteen of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, not on where the weak factors came from. That is the technical reason the migration is cheap, and §What Survives Unchanged gives the evidence article by article.

1. The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model

The Group, and One Count to Correct

The group is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, acting on a quartet that splits canonically as $\mathbf{4}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{3}$. One direction is the lepton line, three are colour, and which of the four is the lepton is the model's one load-bearing declaration.

$$ \dim\mathfrak{su}(4)=15,\qquad \operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3 . $$

The section's opening count needs one correction before it is written. The plan names sixteen generators; the arithmetic of the grid is

$$ \underbrace{16}_{\text{cells}}-\underbrace{1}_{\text{trace}}=15\ \text{generators}, \qquad 4\times4=16\ \text{cells}. $$

Sixteen is the cell count, fifteen is the generator count, and the missing one is the trace direction. The corpus already makes this distinction for the full group — twenty-four cells, twenty-one generators, three traces — and the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$-only section must make it once for one factor: sixteen cells, fifteen generators, one trace.

Why the Name Primordial Force Is Earned

A name is earned by a mechanism, and this one has two. First, the unbroken part of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ at the electroweak scale is $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$, so eight of its fifteen generators are the gluons. Second, and this is the part that makes the name more than a slogan, six of its generators turn a quark into a lepton:

$$ X^{r},X^{g},X^{b},\ \bar X^{r},\bar X^{g},\bar X^{b}, $$

the leptoquark sextet, of electric charges $\pm\tfrac23$. No ordinary force mixes species. Colour does not, electromagnetism does not, and the weak force does only within a doublet. A force that turns a quark into a lepton is doing something categorically different, and "primordial" names it: it is the force the other three come out of, not a stronger version of one of them.

What the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ model has and does not have, stated as a ledger.

property $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ alone needed by the left–right model
colour $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ yes, unbroken yes
$B-L$ yes, as the diagonal $iD$ yes
leptoquarks yes, six yes, to be heavy
weak isospin doublet no yes
rank $3$ $5$

The last two rows are the whole difficulty of the migration, and §The Transition is about them.

The section is a reframing of existing material, not a new derivation. Four subsections carry over with new headings:

new subsection content source in the corpus

The fourth subsection is the one that must not be softened. A model that states its own ceiling is a model a reader can work with. The ceiling is $\operatorname{rank}3$, and it has two readings. Read as a wall, it says the weak force is not in $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. Read as a door, it says the weak force must therefore arrive from outside the algebra — which is exactly what §2 delivers. The section should give both readings and postpone the choice to §2.

2. The Transition

What a Transition Must Deliver

Three things, and they are separate deliverables with separate status.

deliverable status where it comes from
$\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ theorem, already in the corpus the adjoint mass matrix
the sextet is exactly what is eaten theorem, already in the corpus $\operatorname{ad}(iD)$ on the fifteen
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ appears postulate, new — and it is one postulate, the doublet index of the condensate not derivable inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and it does not have to be

The first two are free. The corpus's mass matrix article already states them: $M^2=2v^2[D,[D,\cdot]]$ is diagonal in the plane basis with $32v^2$ on the six planes through the lepton line and $0$ on the nine that avoid it. Equivalently, on the vacuum $\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$, $$ \operatorname{ad}(iD):\quad 0^{9}\ \oplus\ (+4i)^{3}\ \oplus\ (-4i)^{3}, $$ so the kernel is the nine-dimensional $\mathfrak{u}(3)$ and the sextet is precisely the $\pm4i$ eigenspace.

The third is the invention, and it is smaller than it looks: the weak factors are born as indices of the object that does the breaking, not as fragments of the algebra. The rest of this section is what that costs, and it costs one choice of representation.

The Obstruction, Stated Once and Precisely

Inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ the weak pair cannot be built from the sextet, and the proof is one identity. For every $g\in\mathrm{SU}(4)$,

$$ \bigl[\,gXg^{-1},\ gYg^{-1}\,\bigr]=g\,[X,Y]\,g^{-1}, $$

conjugation preserves brackets, so it preserves closedness. The two objects differ in exactly that:

object closed under brackets? $\operatorname{rank}\bigl([\cdot,\cdot]+\text{itself}\bigr)$
$\mathfrak{so}(4)$, the weak pair yes $6=6$
$\mathfrak{m}$, the sextet no $15>6$

Therefore no vacuum, no rotation, no ordering of breakings and no simultaneity carries $\mathfrak{m}$ to $\mathfrak{so}(4)$. A closed set can only be carried to a closed set. That is the end of the algebraic route, and no amount of narrative reopens it.

Read the theorem as a boundary rather than a refutation, because that is what the next subsection does. It forbids one sentence — the weak force is a piece of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ — and it licenses another — the weak force arrives from outside the algebra. The migration takes the second, and the rank lemma of §The Weak Force as a Side Effect is what makes the second the only surviving option.

Two further facts, because they will be asked.

$$ \operatorname{rank}\bigl(\operatorname{span}(\mathfrak{u}(3)\cup\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{u}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(2)))\bigr)=3, $$ so promoting both maximal-rank subgroups to exact symmetries leaves rank three, and their bracket closure is all fifteen, so it leaves $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ itself unbroken. And $$ [\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]=\mathfrak{u}(3), $$ so the sextet's own brackets regenerate the strong force and nothing else. The sextet is not a reservoir of weak directions.

The Weak Force as a Side Effect

The formulation to keep is "the primordial force degenerates into the strong force, and the weak force is created as a side effect". That sentence is not a concession to the algebra. It is the only reading the rank arithmetic permits, and the arithmetic is one line.

The rank ledger. $\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3$. The strong force costs $2$, the abelian factor costs $1$, and the account is empty — that is row 1 of the branching table. The two weak factors cost $1+1$ more, so the endpoint costs $2+1+1+1=5$.

$$ \operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3\ \longrightarrow\ 5 . $$

A subgroup never has larger rank than its group. No breaking, no condensation, no emergent mechanism inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ can deliver five. So the weak factors cannot be pieces of the primordial force. They must be born beside it — and beside is what the word side effect means.

This is the point to state plainly, because it inverts the usual reading of the obstruction. The rank barrier does not refute the narrative. It specifies it. A story in which the weak force was always a fragment of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ contradicts the algebra; a story in which it arrives with the transition is exactly what the algebra demands. The obstruction and the narrative agree once the narrative says side effect.

The Mechanism: The Condensate That Carries a Doublet

What does the transition, and why a doublet index comes with it. The condensate that breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is a scalar with an $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index and a copy index. The corpus already contains it:

$$ \Phi\in(\mathbf{4},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2}),\qquad 16\ \text{real components}. $$

The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index does the killing. A vacuum along the lepton line breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$, and the six leptoquarks are eaten. That is the death of the primordial force, and it is the corpus's own computation, not a postulate.

The copy index is why the weak force exists. A scalar that must be a doublet to do its job is a scalar whose condensation simultaneously announces a two-dimensional internal space. That space is the weak factor. It is not a fragment of the primordial force; it is a property of the object that killed it.

The whole story in one sentence, which is the sentence the articles must be built around:

The primordial force dies; the object that kills it carries a doublet index; the doublet index is the weak force, born as a side effect of the death.

what happens where it lives rank
$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ condenses along the lepton line the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index of $\Phi$ $3$
six leptoquarks eaten, colour and $B-L$ survive the stabiliser of that line $2+1$
a two-dimensional internal space appears the $\mathbf{2}$ index of $\Phi$ $1$
its chiral half is the second weak factor the copy structure of the condensate $1$
endpoint $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ $\mathbf{3+1+1=5}$

Why the Ranks Can Add, Which Is the Structural Content of "Side Effect"

Ranks add across independent factors and never across a subgroup relation. So the sum $3+1+1=5$ is not a loophole; it is a certificate.

If the weak force were a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, the total would be capped at $3$, and no mechanism could raise it. The fact that the endpoint reaches $5$ is therefore evidence that the weak factors sit outside — that they are separate factors, born with the transition. "Born as a side effect" and "rank five from rank three" are the same statement in two vocabularies, and this is the argument an article should lead with, because it is positive rather than defensive.

The Price, and It Is Paid in a Currency Already in the Ledger

The doublet index is postulated, not derived. The condensate is chosen to be a $(\mathbf{4},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})$, and nothing in the algebra's fifteen forces that choice.

That sounds expensive until it is compared with what the corpus already admits. The scalar sector article states it without hedging: the corpus cannot derive the scalar content: the scalar sector is the one place in the frame with no algebraic guidance at all. So the postulate spends a currency the corpus already spends. The migration does not add a new type of debt; it names the existing one as the birth certificate of the weak force.

And the mass problem, which looked fatal, is not there at all. The weak bosons do not get their masses from $M_C$. They get them downstream, from the later vacua $v_R$ and $v$:

$$ m_{W_R}\sim g_R v_R,\qquad m_W\sim g\,v,\qquad v=246\ \mathrm{GeV}. $$

The side effect births the group; the group's masses are supplied by the second and third breakings, which the corpus already computes. So there is no $80\ \mathrm{GeV}$ composite built from $250\ \mathrm{TeV}$ constituents, no binding energy of eleven orders, and no contamination of a light state by a heavy one. Those problems belonged to the composite route, and this is not that route.

The Charge Theorem, Kept on the Record

Two independent reasons, both worth one line each in the article, because both were tried.

Charge. Every sextet member has $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, so any pair sums to $\{0,\pm\tfrac43\}$ and never to $\pm1$. Explicitly,

$$ \mathbf{3}_{4/3}\otimes\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3}=\mathbf{1}_{0}\oplus\mathbf{8}_{0}, $$

and the colour singlet is neutral identically: three spin states, all $Q=0$. An $X\bar X$ bound state is therefore six neutral vectors and no charged one — the exact opposite of what $W^{\pm}$ requires. The sextet cannot make the weak bosons, in any number, at any coupling.

Mass. A bound state weighs at least its confinement scale, $m\gtrsim\Lambda_{\text{confine}}$, and in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$-only model that scale is $M_C\gtrsim250\ \mathrm{TeV}$. So any $\mathrm{SU}(4)$-bound object weighs hundreds of teraelectronvolts, not eighty gigaelectronvolts.

Two independent closes, and they are why the story has to be the index and not the debris.

Recorded and Set Aside: The Composite and Contamination Routes

Two earlier readings are recorded here so that no article re-opens them by accident. Both close, and both close on computed numbers.

The composite reading. The weak bosons are bound states of the primordial-force carriers. It fails twice, independently:

  • charge. Every sextet member has $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, so a pair sums to $\{0,\pm\tfrac43\}$ and never to $\pm1$; explicitly $\mathbf3_{4/3}\otimes\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3}=\mathbf1_0\oplus\mathbf8_0$, and the colour singlet is neutral identically.
  • mass. A bound state weighs at least its confinement scale, $m\gtrsim\Lambda_{\text{confine}}=M_C\gtrsim250\ \mathrm{TeV}$, against $80.4\ \mathrm{GeV}$ wanted.

The contamination reading. The sextet is destroyed and its destruction acts on lighter states. That picture has an exact name — integrating out — and it is standard: the heavy field leaves the asymptotic spectrum and survives in the propagators as local operators,

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}\supset\frac{c}{M_X^{2}}\,\mathcal{O}^{(6)}, \qquad \frac{\delta m^{2}}{m^{2}}\ \sim\ \left(\frac{M}{M_X}\right)^{2}. $$

With the correct mass, $M_X=M_C\simeq250\ \mathrm{TeV}$, the suppression is $1.0\times10^{-7}$ — seven orders below the order-one effect needed to lift a massless state to $80.4\ \mathrm{GeV}$. It is short by seven decades, not by twenty-two: the earlier figure used a GUT-scale bound that the corpus explicitly does not apply to this sector, and it is corrected here. Seven is still fatal for that route.

One check from that work is worth keeping, because it is cheap and it generalises. Reading the six additive identities as the mixing itself would fix

$$ \bigl|A_{jk}\bigr|^{2}=\bigl|A_{lm}\bigr|^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \cos=\sin=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, \qquad \sin^{2}\theta=0.5 , $$

against the observed $\sin^{2}\theta_W=0.23122$ — a factor $2.16$. The identities are the pair resolution and hold in every basis, so they cannot encode a dynamical angle. Any article that prints them must print that sentence beside them.

6. The Open Problems, Numbered

The side-effect version closes the problems that dominated the composite version. What remains is smaller, and different in kind.

Closed, with the reason:

problem why it is closed
the mass gap — $250\ \mathrm{TeV}$ parts making an $80\ \mathrm{GeV}$ object there is no composite: the masses come from $v_R$ and $v$ downstream
the two neutrals the weak factors are factors, so $W^{3}_{L}$ and $W^{3}_{R}$ are their Cartans; the corpus's neutral sector stands
the rank bookkeeping $3+1+1=5$ across independent factors, one sense of rank, no composite spectrum
the new binding and its scale $\Lambda$ no new confinement is introduced

Open, and each is a real question:

  1. Why a doublet. Why does the condensate carry a two-dimensional copy index at all? The corpus declares the scalar content underivable, so this is a postulate — but a postulate that could be reduced if the index were forced by something else. This is the main theoretical debt of the new theory, and the only one that is large.
  2. Why chiral. Why does the copy index split into two factors rather than giving a single $\mathrm{SU}(2)$? Parity violation needs the split and nothing in the group supplies it. The corpus has the split as a fact; the migration needs a reason.
  3. The second transition. Unchanged from the corpus: $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ must break at $M_R$ and the $Z$/$Z_R$ mixing must survive. This is the corpus's problem already, and it is the test that the new sections must not disturb.
  4. The proton. With the sextet heavy at $M_C\gtrsim250\ \mathrm{TeV}$, $\tau_p$ stands as the corpus computes it — dimension nine, no sharp bound. A check rather than a problem.
  5. The order of the two events. Does the doublet index appear with the condensation, or is it required by it? The articles should choose one phrasing and mark the other as the alternative, because "born at the transition" and "needed by the transition" are different claims with different content.

Problem 1 is the one that matters. Everything else is either closed, or was already the corpus's before the migration.

Summary

The migration is a reframing with one small invention, and both parts have a status.

The reframing is cheap because the vacuum does not move. The first symmetry breaking, the adjoint mass matrix, the leptoquark masses, the scalar sector and the fermion content are all statements about $\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ and about the fifteen arrays of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. They survive verbatim, and the evidence is that four of them do not mention the product group once.

The invention is small and it is not algebraic, because the algebra forbids the algebraic version. Conjugation preserves brackets, so no rotation carries the sextet to the weak pair; the sextet's brackets regenerate the strong force and nothing else; and $\operatorname{rank}3\to5$ is impossible across a subgroup relation. So the weak force is not a piece of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ — which is precisely what "created as a side effect" says.

The mechanism is the index of the thing that does the killing. The condensate is $\Phi\in(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$: its $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index breaks the primordial force and eats the sextet, and its $\mathbf{2}$ index is the weak force. One sentence, one table, one postulate.

The postulate is paid in a currency already in the ledger. The doublet index is chosen, not derived — and the corpus already declares the scalar content underivable. Nothing new is borrowed: no new group, no new scale, no new confinement, no matching rule.

The mass problem disappears with the composite route that created it. The weak bosons are not bound states of $250\ \mathrm{TeV}$ parts; they get their masses from $v_R$ and $v$ downstream, at $M_R$ and $80\ \mathrm{GeV}$. The two closes on the sextet — charge $\{0,\pm\frac43\}$ and $m\gtrsim\Lambda_{\text{confine}}$ — are recorded as theorems, not as obstacles.

Six articles carry the work in §3, and the third is the one the migration exists for. Its spine is the rank lemma: a subalgebra never outranks its algebra, so five from three is only possible if the extra two arrive beside the three — and the article should lead with that, because it turns the obstruction into the argument.

The save exists, so nothing can be lost. $44$ articles, $1\,257\,829$ bytes, a manifest with hashes, both sections verbatim, and a single tarball. The migration can be abandoned at any point by a three-line restore.

Nothing blocks section 1 and 2, and nothing blocks section 3 either, since the three former blockers — the constituents, the scale $\Lambda$, and the two neutrals — are withdrawn by the decision record rather than solved. One question remains open and it is stated in §6: why a doublet.

Biquaternion Propagation: The Physics Category Derived From the Mathematics Menu

Introduction

This entry is about the category and not about an object in it. It states how the physics articles are obtained from the mathematics articles, why they are ordered as they are, and what each of them is entitled to claim. It reads the whole mathematics menu at once, and it is the only entry in the physics part that does so.

$$ \text{mathematics menu}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{rule}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{physics category}, \qquad 150\;\text{articles}\;\mapsto\;30\;\text{entries}. $$

The first claim is that the derivation goes one way and is checkable. A mathematics article proves something about the algebra; a physics entry reads that proof in the vocabulary of a physical object. The direction is fixed: the mathematics is the source, the reading is the output, and no entry is allowed to add a result. A reader can therefore check the whole category by checking each entry against the section it names, which is what the tables below are for.

The second claim is that the order is inherited and not chosen. The physics follows the mathematics section by section, and the sequence of the mathematics menu is the sequence of the physics menu. The claim is not that the mathematics order is the best pedagogy for physics; it is that a corpus with two orders has two structures and eventually two contents.

The third claim is that the compression is about five to one and that the ratio is not a target. One hundred and fifty mathematics articles become thirty entries. The compression is not summarisation: a mathematics article proves one thing, four of them prove facets of one thing, and a reader of physics wants the one thing in one place. What the compression drops is the proof and the repetition; what it keeps is every reading, stated once.

The fourth claim is that the vocabulary is the hard part and not the algebra. The algebra supplies subspaces, forms, norms, ideals, operators, a group and a cone. Physics supplies fields, mass, spin, chirality, probability, horizons, frames and sources. The derivation is the assignment of one to the other, and the assignment is not free: each word is tied to an algebraic object and to a stated boundary, and a reading that cannot be bounded is withdrawn rather than softened.

The fifth claim is that three senses of the word propagation are in play and must not be confused. The first is editorial: a reading propagates from the mathematics section to the physics entry that inherits it. The second is structural: the category propagates along the menu, so a fault in the mathematics order appears downstream as a fault in the physics order, and the three order faults of §What the Order Fixes are exactly that. The third is physical: the first-order root of the d'Alembertian is the propagation of a field, and it is one entry inside the category, not the pattern of the category.

The sixth claim is that the article states its own open work. Thirty derived entries and one introduction are listed; twenty-three of the thirty-one links resolve to a written file and eight do not. The eight are named here rather than filled with stubs, because a stub in a category of readings is worse than a gap.

The strongest objection is that a category is not a result, so an article about the category proves nothing. The answer is that the derivation does make checkable claims: that the entry count per section follows from a stated rule, that every mathematics article of a section is visible in its entry, that no entry exists without a reading, and that the archive already contained the readings that the entries now carry. Each of the four is a statement that can fail, and each is checked below against the menu, the archive, or the counts.

A second objection is that this is retrofitting. The corpus wrote its physics articles first and then invented a rule that reproduces them. The answer is partly yes and it is stated as such: the rules were recovered from the corpus and then imposed. What distinguishes a recovered rule from a rationalisation is that the rule predicts something the corpus had not done, and it does — the four exceptions are forced by the rule, and two of the eight unwritten entries are places where the rule demanded an entry the corpus had never had.

A third objection is that the numbers are arbitrary. They are not: they are computed from the section counts, and the computation is displayed in §The Mathematics Census so that a reader who changes a count can recompute the total. If a section's reading splits into two regimes, the rule gives two entries and the ratio falls; the arithmetic is exposed for that reason.

A fourth objection is that a derived order will date. The mathematics menu will change, and the derivation will have to change with it. That is the point: a derived order has a failure mode that is visible, because a section added to the mathematics without an entry in the physics is a hole at a known place. An order that is merely editorial has no such signal.

The letters. The mathematics menu has twenty sections; the entries are labelled by part and by position, $\mathrm{I}.1$ to $\mathrm{I}.19$ for the algebra, $\mathrm{II}.1$ for the topology, $\mathrm{III}.1$ to $\mathrm{III}.4$ for the analysis, $\mathrm{IV}.1$ to $\mathrm{IV}.6$ for the geometry. The introduction is Part $0$. A section is written $\S n$. The carrier, the closure, the strata and the two sectors keep the glyphs of the entries they come from; §Summary of Notation collects them. The conventions are those of the corpus and this entry defines none of them.

Boundaries. The mathematics side is the menu itself, The Mathematics Menu; the algebra is The Element, the Six Subspaces and the Two Ledgers and its neighbours; the carriers are The Carriers, the Spin and the Closure; the analysis is The Derivative, the Field and the Transform; the topology is The Unit Group, the Cone and the Orders. The archived articles named in §What the Archive Already Held are sources and not second readings. No dynamics, no action principle, no quantum postulate and no new algebra appear.

The Results in Brief

The census. Twenty sections, one hundred and fifty mathematics articles, thirty derived entries and one introduction. The algebra carries eighty articles into nineteen entries, the topology four into one, the analysis thirty-six into four, the geometry thirty into six.

The rule. One entry per section, with four named exceptions: §$10$ takes five entries, §$12$ three, §$16$ two, §$19$ four. Every exception is a place where the physics reads more than the mathematics states, and the extra reading is named.

The order. The spin belongs to the geometry, after the rotations and the Lorentz group, because a spin is a weight of a rotation action. The spectrum separates from the strata. The groups come after the algebra, not before the state space.

The readings. The derivative is the field equation, the first-order root is the propagation, the regular function is the on-shell field, the integral is the action, the exponential is the flow, the zero divisor is the horizon, the idempotent is the observable, the three carriers are the three spins, the Hermitian form is the probability, the two-sheeted norm is the mass shell.

The spin block. Four entries from the carriers article and its three subsections: the carrier and the closure, then one entry per spin value. It is the test case, because it is the only place where the physics compresses a whole subsection into a single entry and must lose nothing.

The carrier of spin one is the material sector, and the reason is the sign convention of the coordinates. The algebra does not select between the two sectors; the corpus's convention $x_t=ict$ does. §The Spin Block states this as the entry's own limit.

The archive. The readings were not invented. The mirror and the phy–psy switch, the square-zero element, the probability band, the cone and the massless spectrum, the three carriers and the state space are archived articles, and the table of §What the Archive Already Held records which entry carries each.

The absorption. Forty-four articles stood in the category before the derivation. Forty-one are archived as sources, three carry over, one entry is new work, and the total falls to thirty derived entries.

The propagation. The category classifies and does not propagate: a propagation is a solution, and it is the pair (shell, support). The shell is read off the symbol, the support off the kernel, and the kernel is $G_{\mathrm{ret}}=\delta(\tau-R)/4\pi R$, whose support is the cone that four entries name four times. The carriers of the three cases are stated in the archive as the centre, the traceless part with the material sector as its closure, and the whole algebra.

The open work. Eight entries are titled and not written: the harmonic analysis, the exponential and its pairings, the rotations and the groups, the four spin-block entries, and the quadratic map and the singular sets. Twenty-three of the thirty-one links resolve.

The Rule of Derivation

R1 — the physics follows the mathematics order. Section $n$ precedes section $n+1$ in the physics exactly when it does in the mathematics. The rule has no exception and it is the rule that produces the article.

R2 — one entry to one section, by default. The default is not a simplification, it is a claim: a mathematics section states one subject, and a physics entry states the reading of one subject. Where the claim is false, the section is either too coarse or the physics is reading something the mathematics does not state, and R3 decides which.

R3 — more than one entry only where the physics reads more than the mathematics states, and then the exception is named and counted. Four sections qualify. §$10$ takes five entries because the four strata are four named regions of the algebra and a region is what a physicist names. §$12$ takes three because a state space, a four-vector and an operator are three readings of one subspace. §$16$ takes two because a transform and the duality it carries are used for two different jobs. §$19$ takes four because a carrier is defined once and a spin is read once per value.

R4 — every mathematics article of the section is visible in the entry. Visibility is checked by the "from" column of the entry's table: the entry names the results, not the articles, and a reader must be able to find each result in the section. An entry that omits a result has broken R4 and is incomplete, whatever its length.

R5 — no entry without at least one reading. A section that states a construction with no physical reading gets no entry, and its content is carried as a remark inside a neighbouring entry or not at all. The rule is what keeps the category from becoming a paraphrase of the menu.

The rules are ordered and they can conflict. R1 and R3 conflict whenever a section that deserves five entries sits between two that deserve one, because the physics then has a block of five where the menu has one section. The resolution is always in favour of R1: the block stays where the section is, and the reader is told why the block is there.

The Spin Block

The block is four entries carrying a section of four subsections, and it is the test of the whole derivation. The carriers entry defines two words; the three per-spin entries use them. The order is the menu order and it is not negotiable.

A carrier is a nonzero real subspace $W\subseteq\mathbb{B}$ that is invariant and irreducible under one of the algebra's own actions: left multiplication, or conjugation by the group of units. The action is part of the carrier, because the same subspace can be reducible for one action and irreducible for the other. The closure is the space a field takes values in: the carrier enlarged by whatever invariance and whatever norm the field equation requires.

carrier set $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}$ $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}$ action spin
the centre $\mathbb{B}_{C}=\mathbb{C}e_t$ $2$ $1$ conjugation, fixed pointwise $0$
the ideals $S_{\pm}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$ $4$ $2$ left multiplication $\tfrac12$
the material sector $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{ie_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$ $4$ — conjugation $1$, on its traceless part

There are three and no more, and the reason is module theory in one line. Every finite-dimensional module of $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a direct sum of copies of the two-dimensional simple module, so the left action sees only $-\tfrac12$; the adjoint action adds the trivial centre and the three-dimensional traceless part, so it sees $0$ and $1$. A fourth value would need a second simple module, and there is none.

The closure repairs two things and not a third. It restores invariance where the carrier alone is not closed under the field equation. It restores the norm, which the ideal alone does not carry, since every element of a minimal left ideal has $N=0$. It does not repair the vanishing norm: a single value of a spin-half field is always massless, and the mass of the field is the pairing of its two values.

The spin-zero entry. The real line is the carrier and the complex line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is the closure, so the phase is the one degree of freedom the closure adds. On the complex line the norm is $\lambda^{2}$, real and of one sign, so the mass shell is a single sheet. The adjoint action fixes every point, so the weight is $0$ with multiplicity two and the generator is zero: the clock does not tick, and the only evolution is the phase. The carrier meets the rest frame and the vacuum and nothing else, and the exclusion of the other two regimes is structural, since a central element has no vector part.

The spin-half entry. The plane is the carrier and the spinor space is its closure; the axis is a parameter of the family and not a datum of the algebra, so the algebra carries infinitely many of each and singles out none. Every element of the ideal has rank at most one, so $N=0$ at every point; the mass of the field is the pairing of its two parts,

$$ N(\tilde\Xi_L+\tilde\Xi_R)=2B(\tilde\Xi_L,\tilde\Xi_R), $$

which is the algebra's statement that a Dirac mass mixes two chiralities. Left multiplication acts with weights $\pm\tfrac12$, so the two chiral rates, and the exceptional point is where they coincide. The closure meets all four regimes, the carrier meets two, and the difference is the content of the entry.

The two steps, and why there are two. For spin $0$ the closure adds one real line and stops. For spin $1$ the closure adds one real line and stops. For spin $\tfrac12$ the closure adds a transverse direction, giving the minimal left ideal, and then adds the conjugate ideal, giving the bispinor space. The first step is forced by the rotation action, since no real two-dimensional subspace is invariant under the whole group; the second is forced by the mass, since the coupling relates the two ideals and the field must be able to occupy both.

The spin-one entry, and the one distinction the entry must not lose. The carrier of the spin is the traceless part $V_{\mathbb{R}}$, of real dimension three, and the carrier of the potential is the material sector, of real dimension four: the extra dimension is the time slot that the Proca constraint removes,

$$ \mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=V_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}(ie_t). $$

The equation implies the Lorenz condition, the condition removes the singlet that the adjoint action annihilates, and what remains is the triplet. So the spin-one field is a four-vector in its home and a triplet in its constraint, and the two carriers differ by the temporal slot. The extra direction is the temporal slot $ie_t$ and not a scalar, so the four slots are the four components of a four-vector. The norm is real, of signature $(3,1)$ in the coefficients the corpus writes, so the mass shell is one sheet of one cone per sign of the temporal coefficient. The two-sided action gives $+1$, $-1$ and $0$, the integers of the vector representation. The carrier reaches the region of mass in motion, and it is the only one that does, because it is the only closure on which both invariants are real and the vector square is a sum of squares.

Why the carrier is the material sector and not the informational one. The two sectors look alike, and up to isomorphism they are alike. Write the coefficients in the four slots: $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\{Q_t\in i\mathbb{R},\ \vec Q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}=\{Q_t\in\mathbb{R},\ \vec Q\in i\mathbb{R}^{3}\}$. Both are four real dimensional, both are invariant under $Q\mapsto uQu^{*}$, and both split as a line plus a three-dimensional part,

$$ \mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=V_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}(ie_t),\qquad \mathbb{B}_{\Psi}=iV_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}e_t, \qquad V_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}. $$

The three-dimensional summand carries the vector representation in both cases, and since $i$ is central the map $Q\mapsto iQ$ is an isomorphism of conjugation modules carrying one split onto the other, vector part onto vector part. The algebra therefore does not select between them: they are one carrier read twice.

What selects is the metric convention, and this is the sharpest limit of the block. The corpus writes the four-position as $ict\,e_t+x\vec e_x+y\vec e_y+z\vec e_z$, so the four-momentum is

$$ \tilde P_{\Phi}=\frac{iE}{c}\,e_t+p_x\vec e_x+p_y\vec e_y+p_z\vec e_z\;\in\;\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}, $$

whose coefficients are real numbers times the unit $(i,1,1,1)$; the interval is the norm on that element, $-m^{2}c^{2}$, and the mass shell, the Lorentz action by rotor conjugation and the four-potential of the Proca equation are readings of this one form. Put the imaginary unit on the three spatial coefficients instead and the physical four-momentum moves to $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ with the interval as the norm there and the opposite sign; that is the mirror, $\tilde P_{\Psi}=i\tilde P_{\Phi}$, with $+m^{2}c^{2}$ against $-m^{2}c^{2}$, and it is the same physics in the other convention.

The archive states the same thing and states it better. The Carrier of the Klein–Gordon Field answers the natural question in one line: the material and informational copies of a scalar field are the same field with the label of $i$ moved, since $i$ is central and $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=i\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, so a declaration that a scalar field is material is a declaration that its value is imaginary. The spin-one case is the same, one dimension larger.

So the sentence the spin-one carrier is the material sector is a statement about the corpus's coordinates and not a theorem of the algebra. The theorem is weaker and cleaner: three carrier types and no fourth, and the spin-one type has two real forms. The corpus uses the form on which time is imaginary, because that is the convention its interval is written in.

What is genuinely asymmetric is the role and not the carrier. On $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ the Hermitian form is positive on the cone of states and the Born weight is a pairing with it, which is what makes that sector a state space; on $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ the real indefinite form is the norm, which is what makes that sector an object space. Two sectors, two real forms of one carrier, two roles.

Why four entries and not fourteen. The mathematics needs one article per proof; the physics needs four because there are four things to say: what carries the spin, and then one entry per value. If a reader of the four entries can reconstruct the section's results, R4 holds; if not, the block has lost content and the derivation is wrong there.

The Propagation the Category Carries

The word of the series is propagation, and the category has one exact place for it. A propagation is a solution and not an equation: it is what a field does once the carrier, the equation and the constraint are fixed. The entries supply the three ingredients and none of them is a propagation, which is the distinction this section exists to draw. Six readings are below, and every one of them was worked out in an archived article before it was placed here.

The carriers are sharper in the archive than in the table

Klein–Gordon. The Carrier of the Klein–Gordon Field fixes the carrier by the dimension of the field's value and not by the equation: one complex component per event, so a complex line, and the line the algebra distinguishes is the centre, since it is the only one fixed by the whole algebra under commutation. The reading states the convention question in one line and settles it: the material and informational copies of a scalar field are the same field with the label of $i$ moved, because $i$ is central and $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=i\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, so a declaration that a scalar field is material says that its value is imaginary, and charge conjugation then acts as a reflection on that line.

Proca. The Carrier of the Proca Field separates the two carriers that §The Spin Block must not confuse. The carrier of the spin is the traceless part, of real dimension three, and the carrier of the potential is the material sector, one dimension larger, the extra dimension being the time slot. The Proca equation implies the Lorenz condition, the condition removes the time slot, and what survives is the triplet. The same article makes the distinction that a careless reading collapses: the sector closes whether or not the Lorenz condition holds, because closure is a property of the multiplication and the condition is a property of the solution. A restriction that held only on a gauge slice would be a restriction on the solutions; this one is a restriction on the field's home.

Dirac. The Carrier of the Dirac Field states the carrier as $S_{+}+S_{-}=\mathbb{B}$ and says why: a single element of one ideal is a two-component field, which is a Weyl field, and the massive field needs both ideals because the mass is the coefficient that relates them and neither equation of the pair is satisfied by a field confined to one ideal. The statement the field lives in a minimal left ideal is true of each value and false of the field. The same article supplies the sharpest structural fact of the block: no non-zero subspace of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ is a left ideal, since the central $i$ would have to carry it into $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ and the intersection is $\{0\}$. The material sector therefore cannot carry a Dirac field at all, which is why the spin-$\tfrac12$ closure is the whole algebra and why the sector is the carrier of the potential and never of the bispinor.

The one asymmetry the carriers do have. Only the Dirac carrier survives an arbitrary left multiplication: a general left multiplier leaves the centre and leaves the traceless part, but a left multiplier is absorbed by an ideal. So the spin-$\tfrac12$ field is the only one of the three whose home survives a coupling, and that is a property of being an ideal rather than a subspace. The propagation of a field under a coupling therefore has a different status in the three cases, and the difference is visible in the algebra and not in the physics.

The kernel propagates on the cone

The retarded kernel is a distribution supported on the light cone. In the coherence variables of the corpus the fundamental solution of the wave operator is

$$ G_{\mathrm{ret}}=\frac{1}{4\pi R}\,\delta(\tau-R), \qquad R=\lvert\vec r\rvert, $$

so the response at an event depends on the source only on the past cone of that event. The Biquaternion D'Alembertian and Its Green's Functions derives it, states the Kirchhoff form of the Cauchy problem and records the weighted kernel $\exp(-f\tau)G_{\mathrm{ret}}$ of the shifted operator. The weight rescales the phase on the cone and moves no support, so Huygens' principle survives it; what the scalar weight buys, at the price of self-adjointness, is exactly the sharpness of that support. Exercise: The Retarded Potentials and the Green's Function works the same kernel on the potential.

What this says about the category. The support of a propagation is a cone, the cone is the zero-divisor set, and the zero-divisor set is the characteristic set of the gradient. Four statements that appear in four different entries — the topology's cone, the analysis's characteristic set, the algebra's massless regime, the geometry's light cone — are one set read four times, and the propagation is what makes the identity operational rather than verbal.

The eikonal is the ray

A null gradient is a ray. The two-sided condition $dF=L\,dZ\,R$ is the statement that each component's gradient is a zero divisor, and the corpus states its result as the complex eikonal $N(\nabla\Sigma)=0$. A propagation is then a family of surfaces of constant phase whose normal is null, which is a ray, and the characteristic set of the first-order operator and of its square are the same set. The reading is the reason the eikonal belongs to the analysis and not to the geometry: it is a statement about a derivative, and the cone it produces is the same cone the topology named.

The massless limit changes the constraint and not the equation

At $m=0$ the Proca system is the Maxwell system, by substitution and not by approximation. The Lagrangian density loses its mass term, the equation of motion loses its mass term, and the biquaternion form loses its shift. The equation is continuous in $m$ and the solution space is not, and the reason is the constraint: the Proca equation implies the Lorenz condition, and the source-free Maxwell equation does not. The degree of freedom that the constraint removed — the temporal slot — returns.

What that costs the category. It confirms that the carrier is the same on both sides of the limit, $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ for the potential and $V_{\mathbb{R}}$ for the spin, and that what changes is the constraint and not the home. It also explains why the massless entry is a regime of the algebra rather than a separate carrier: a regime is a set of states, and $m=0$ is where the constraint stops removing a state. A reader who expected the carrier to change at the limit has conflated the carrier with the constraint, and this is the one place where the confusion is cheap to detect.

The shift is the mass, and it decides which side has waves

The shifted gradient carries the mass and it moves the shell. The operator $\tilde\nabla_\kappa$ with $\kappa=-im$ has a symbol whose vanishing set is the shifted cone, and the sign of $\kappa$ decides whether the symbol vanishes on real frequencies at all. For real $\kappa$ there are non-trivial homogeneous plane waves on the shifted cone $\omega/c=\kappa\pm\lVert\vec k\rVert$; for non-real $\kappa$ there is no plane wave, and the non-tempered solutions that exist are obtained by the substitution $u=\exp(-\kappa x_4)v$ with $\Box v=0$. A source that speaks of an imaginary mass and a corpus that speaks of a real shift are describing the same dichotomy with the factor $i$ in different places, and a statement copied across without that factor places the interesting case on the wrong side.

What this says about the category. A propagation is the pair (shell, support), and both are read off the symbol. The algebra supplies the symbol; the convention supplies which side of the dichotomy is physical; the entry states the shell and leaves the support to the archive. That division of labour is the whole content of this section.

What propagation is not

No entry in the category is a propagation. The entries classify carriers, states and equations, and a propagation is a solution of an equation with a carrier and a constraint. The corpus keeps the two levels apart on purpose: a category of solutions would be a category of examples, and examples date, while a classification of the algebra does not.

The corpus's propagation articles are therefore the archive, and they are the test of the classification. If the carriers of §The Spin Block are right, then the Klein–Gordon, Proca and Dirac propagation articles must each be readable as one carrier plus one constraint plus one kernel, and they are. If the regime partition of §$10$ is right, a propagation must cross the regimes only through the massless boundary, and the retarded kernel's support says that it does.

What the Order Fixes

The first fault was the spin at the head of the modules. The old list read the algebra, then the frame, then the modules, and put the spin inside the module block, which introduced the carrier before the rotation whose representation it is. The repair is not a rewording: a spin is a weight of an action, and the action is the rotation, so the spin belongs after the algebra and after the Lorentz group, where the mathematics puts it. The cost of the old order was that the three values arrived as a table to be memorised instead of as the consequence of one theorem about modules.

The second fault was the spectral reading inside the algebra. The old list put the spectrum, the degeneracy and the strata in one block, which made the mass and the motion one subject and left the four regimes without names. The repair separates the spectrum, a property of an element, from the strata, regions cut by two invariants, and gives each regime its own entry. The gain is that a reader can say the rest frame and the massless regime and mean a region of the algebra.

The third fault was the frame after the modules. The old list put the state space before the group that preserves it, so unitarity arrived as a postulate and the double cover arrived as a remark. The repair follows the menu: the groups come in the geometry, and unitarity is a theorem about a central unit rather than an assumption.

What the order does not fix. It does not make a reading true. A derivation can put the spin in the right place and read it wrongly, and the corpus's method of labels, boundaries and withdrawn readings is the guard against that. The order fixes what a reader meets first, and nothing else.

The Interpretations

I.16.a — The Order Is a Physical Claim

The object. The sequence of the twenty sections, and inside the geometry the sequence rotations, carriers, spin, quadratic map.

The warrant. A spin is the weight of an action on a carrier, so the action must be available before the weight is stated. The mathematics orders the section that way, and the derivation inherits the order rather than inventing it.

The reading. Menu order is a claim about dependence and not about taste. A reader who meets the three spin values before the Lorentz group has met a table; a reader who meets them after has met a theorem. The physics gains nothing from the table and everything from the theorem, and the gain is visible in the entries that follow: the spin-half entry can use the rotation action because the rotation entry precedes it.

I.16.b — A Regime Is a Name

The object. The partition of the algebra by two invariants: the $N$ norm and spectral regularity.

The warrant. The partition is a theorem of the mathematics, and each cell is a set defined by two equations and two inequalities. The physics had been describing the same four cells under four vocabularies: a mass at rest, a mass in motion, a massless field and the vacuum.

The reading. A stratum of the algebra is a regime of a physical object, and the four names are the four cells. The reading is the strongest in the category because it is a bijection with no residue: every point of the algebra is in exactly one regime, and every regime has a physical name. Its limit is that a regime is a property of a state and not of a process, so the partition says nothing about transitions between cells.

I.16.c — The Spin Is a Weight of an Action

The object. The three carriers: the centre, the two ideals, and the material sector with its traceless part.

The warrant. The carrier is defined as an invariant irreducible subspace under one of the algebra's own actions, and the three values are the three module types, computed. Nothing here is chosen.

The reading. A field's spin is the weight of the algebra's action on the subspace its values live in. That sentence is the whole of the spin block's physics, and it is stronger than the usual statement that spin is a representation label, because the action is the algebra's own and the carrier is a subspace of the algebra itself. The limit is stated in the same entry: the algebra fixes the set of spins and does not say which particle carries which.

I.16.d — The Mass Shell Is the Sign of One Form

The object. The norm $N$ and its two real forms, $N|_{\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}}=-a^{2}+p^{2}$ and $N|_{\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}}=a^{2}-v^{2}$.

The warrant. The two forms are the same quadratic form read on two real subspaces, and multiplication by $i$ carries one to the other. The level set of one is the mirror image of the level set of the other.

The reading. The mass shell is not the norm; it is the norm together with a convention about which coefficient carries the imaginary unit. That is why the corpus states the convention instead of deriving the sector: $x_t=ict$ is a choice, and the choice is what makes the interval negative for a timelike four-momentum. The reading's value is that it separates two claims that are usually fused — the algebra gives the light cone, which is a theorem, and the four-momentum lies on this sheet, which is a convention plus a sign.

I.16.e — The Zero Divisor Is the Horizon

The object. The cone $N=0$ and its complement, the group of units.

The warrant. A unit has an inverse and a zero divisor does not; the cone is the boundary of invertibility, and it is a real algebraic set of three real dimensions.

The reading. The cone is a horizon in the operational sense: inside it a process is reversible, on it a process has a kernel, and the crossing is a loss of information that no inverse repairs. On the informational sector the same cone meets the positive cone and becomes the cone over the pure states, so the horizon and the set of states of maximal knowledge are the same set read twice. The limit is that a horizon here is algebraic and not causal: the corpus does not claim that signals fail to cross the cone.

I.16.f — The Derivative Is the Field Equation

The object. $\tilde\nabla=\sum_\mu e_\mu\partial/\partial R_\mu$ and the condition $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi=0$.

The warrant. The derivative is an element of the algebra and it acts by multiplication, so the condition is algebraic. On the material sector its four components are the free Maxwell pair.

The reading. In this corpus a field equation is not transcribed from physics into algebra; it is the differentiability condition of a function of one algebra-valued variable. What the reading buys is checkability: the equation can be compared with the section that produced it. What it costs is dynamics, since a differentiability condition selects no action and no coupling.

I.16.g — The Kernel Is the Clock

The object. The Fourier kernel, its poles and its tails, and the three placements of the kernel in the transform.

The warrant. The kernel is an exponential of a root of $-1$, so its argument is a rate times a parameter; the poles of the inversion are the zeros of a symbol; the placements differ by which side the kernel multiplies.

The reading. A transform is a clock: the frequency is the rate, the kernel is the exponential of it, and the degenerate set is where the clock stops being invertible. The three placements are three conventions about which side the phase acts on, and they are physically indistinguishable until a non-commuting coefficient is introduced. The limit is the one the mathematics states: a constant coefficient passes through whatever its commutation, and a constant non-central one translates the shell.

I.16.h — Probability Is a Pairing, Not a Postulate

The object. The Hermitian form on the informational sector, the normalised slice, and the flow generated by a central unit.

The warrant. The Born weight is the normalised Hermitian pairing of two elements, which is a number computed from the algebra. Unitarity is the statement that the flow's unit is central, so the flow preserves the form.

The reading. The Born rule arrives in two pieces of different status, and the corpus says which is which: the structure — probability as a pairing of two elements — is derived, and the frequency — that the pairing is what a repeated experiment converges to — is postulated. Unitarity is derived, not assumed, because a central unit commutes with the conjugation that defines the form. The limit is that a postulate remains: the corpus derives the shape of the probability and not its interpretation.

I.16.i — The Square-Zero Object Needs Three Answers

The object. The elements with $Q^{2}=0$ and the nilpotent stratum.

The warrant. A nilpotent has no spectrum in the ordinary sense: its only eigenvalue is $0$ and its square is the zero of the algebra. Classifying it by its equation answers nothing, which is why the archived article asks three questions instead.

The reading. A square-zero object is specified by what it acts on, by the grading it is odd under, and by what its square is paired with — a mode, a differential, a supercharge. The reading is the entry's way of saying that the nilpotent is not a degenerate particle but a different kind of object, and it is the reason the vacuum entry carries the ladder rather than a list of solutions. Its limit is stated with it: the physical null momentum is placed beside these objects by habit and does not satisfy the line.

I.16.j — Chirality Is a Side

The object. The two minimal left ideals $S_{\pm}$ and the two-sided operators that exchange them.

The warrant. The ideals are the two simple modules, and a two-sided operator either preserves each or exchanges them; the exchange is the chirality, and it is implemented by the pseudoscalar.

The reading. Chirality is not an extra label attached to a field: it is which of the two sides of the algebra the field's values occupy. A mass term is then a pairing of the two sides, which is why a single-sided field is massless, and why the Dirac operator and the basis operator commute. The limit is that the corpus states the algebra and leaves the particle assignment open.

I.16.k — A Reading Is Not a Result

The object. The labels used in every entry: the object, the warrant, the reading, and the boundary stated with it.

The warrant. An entry mixes proved statements and interpretive statements, and a reader is entitled to know which is which. The labels are the corpus's device for saying it in line rather than in a preface.

The reading. A physics category is only as good as its boundaries. Every entry states what its reading does not claim, and a reading without a boundary is treated as a defect. That is why the limits of this article are longer than its summary and why the mirror argument of §The Spin Block is stated as a convention rather than as a theorem.

I.16.l — Propagation Is a Solution, Not an Entry

The object. The retarded kernel, its support and its contour.

The warrant. $G_{\mathrm{ret}}=\delta(\tau-R)/4\pi R$ is the fundamental solution of the wave operator, so the response of a field at an event is a distribution supported on the past cone of that event. The contour that produces the retarded kernel is one of several, and the choice of contour is the choice of which propagation is meant.

The reading. A propagation is the pair (shell, support), and neither member is an entry of the category. The shell is the vanishing set of a symbol, which is an algebraic object; the support is a property of a distribution, which is an analytic object. The category supplies the first and the archive supplies the second. Its limit is sharp: the corpus classifies what can propagate and does not select what does, so a reader who wants a wave has to leave the category and take a propagation article with them.

I.16.m — The Massless Limit Tests the Carrier

The object. The Proca system at $m=0$ against the source-free Maxwell system.

The warrant. The reduction is a substitution: the mass term goes, the shift goes, and the remaining equation is the Maxwell equation. The equation is continuous in $m$ and the solution space is not, because the Proca equation implies the Lorenz condition and the Maxwell equation does not.

The reading. A limit that leaves the carrier fixed and changes the constraint is the strongest evidence that the carrier is the carrier and the constraint is not. Read on the category, it says that the massless entry is a regime and not a second carrier, and it gives the reader a cheap test: a limit that changed the carrier would be a change of entry, and no limit in the corpus does that. The limit of the reading is that it is about the potential's carrier; the spin carrier, the traceless part, is untouched by the whole discussion.

I.16.l — The Archive Is a Source, Not a Structure

The object. The forty-one archived articles and the table that maps each to the entry that carries its readings.

The warrant. The archived articles were written before the derivation and are not edited by it. Their readings were moved, and their texts were not.

The reading. A corpus with two live orders has two structures; a corpus with one live order and one archive has one structure and a record. The archive is the record: it shows what was thought when a reading was first worked out, and it is the place a reader goes to check that a reading was not invented for the menu. The limit is that the archive is not maintained, so a correction lives in the entry and never in the source.

The Limits

The derivation adds no physics. Every reading in the entries was already in the archive or in the mathematics. What it adds is an order, a partition of the material into entries, and the discipline that an entry exists only when it carries a reading.

The number thirty is not sacred. It is what the reading costs today. A section whose reading splits into two regimes would take two entries, and a section whose two readings merge would take one. The rules decide, and the census is recomputed rather than defended.

The ratio is not a target. A section that compresses ten to one is not better than one that compresses three to one. The right compression is the one that keeps every reading and drops every repetition.

The spin-one reading depends on a convention. The carrier of the spin-one field is stated as the material sector because the corpus writes the four-position with $ict$. The algebra admits the mirror equally, and the mirror reading is not a rival carrier but the observable form of the same object. A reader who changes the convention must change the entry, and the entry says so.

The partition is a partition of states, not of processes. The four regimes classify an element, and an element is a state or an observable at an instant. No entry in the category describes a transition between regimes except the massless entry, which describes the boundary.

No dynamics, no action, no quantum postulate. The category has an equation of motion only where a differentiability condition supplies one, and it has a probability only up to the frequency postulate of $\mathrm{I}.18$. Nothing here fixes a Lagrangian, a coupling or a Hilbert space.

The propagation section derives nothing. It quotes the archive's kernels, shells and branches and states what they say about the classification. The retarded kernel, the Huygens property, the shifted shell and the massless limit are the archive's results, stated there with their own proofs; this entry uses them as the test of its own claims and adds no analysis.

The counts are the plan's and can be recomputed. The section counts of §The Mathematics Census are the derivation's, and the menu is the authority. If a section gains an article, the census changes and the ratio changes; the entry count changes only if a reading changes.

Eight entries are unwritten. The live menu carries eight dead links, and they are named rather than stubbed. Until they are written, the spin block in particular is a plan and not a text, and the claim that it passes the test of R4 is a claim about the section and not about a written entry.

The archive is not a second reading. It is a source, it does not receive corrections, and it is not maintained. A disagreement between an entry and an archived article is resolved in favour of the entry.

The mathematics is not reordered. The derivation takes the menu as given. If a section sits in the wrong place in the mathematics, the fault is the mathematics', and the derivation's answer is a note and not a move.

Summary

The physics category is derived from the mathematics menu and it inherits the menu's order. One hundred and fifty mathematics articles in twenty sections become thirty derived entries and one introduction, in the same sequence.

One entry to one section, with four named exceptions. §$10$ takes five entries because a regime is a thing the physics names; §$12$ takes three because a state space is a reading the mathematics does not have; §$16$ takes two because a transform and the duality it carries are used for two jobs; §$19$ takes four because a carrier is defined once and a spin is read once per value.

Every entry carries at least one reading, and the reading is why it exists. The readings are the archive's, moved and not invented: the mirror and the switch, the square-zero element, the probability band, the cone and the massless spectrum, the three carriers, the state space.

The spin block is four entries and it is the test. The carriers entry defines the carrier and the closure, and the three per-spin entries carry the subspace, the norm, the spectrum and the regimes for one value each. It is the only block where a whole subsection of the mathematics becomes one entry and must lose nothing.

The spin-one carrier is the material sector, and the reason is the metric convention. Both sectors are invariant under conjugation, both split $1+3$ with the vector representation on the three-dimensional summand, and multiplication by $i$ intertwines the two actions, so the algebra does not choose. What chooses is $x_t=ict$: the four-momentum $iE/c\,e_t+\vec p$ is an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and the interval is the norm there. The mirror is the same object in the other convention, with the sign flipped, and $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ keeps the role that is not a carrier: its $1+3$ split is the Bloch decomposition of a state.

The category classifies and does not propagate. A propagation is a solution, and the corpus keeps solutions in the archive. The six readings of §The Propagation the Category Carries are there to check the classification against the propagation articles, and they pass on the point that matters: the carriers of the three cases are the centre, the traceless part with the material sector as its closure, and the whole algebra, and only the last of the three survives an arbitrary coupling.

The order is the repair. The spin moves to the geometry, after the rotations and the Lorentz group; the spectrum separates from the strata; the groups come after the algebra rather than before the state space.

Nothing is deleted and the open work is counted. Forty-one articles are archived as sources, three carry over, one entry is new work, twenty-three of the thirty-one links resolve, and eight entries are titled and not written.

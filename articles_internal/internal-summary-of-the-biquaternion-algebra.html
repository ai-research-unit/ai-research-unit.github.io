Internal Summary of the Biquaternion Algebra

Introduction

What this file is. A compressed map of the Algebra part of the maths category Biquaternions — eighty-one of its 121 articles, everything except the forty on operators. It gives the objects, the boxed equations and the non-trivial claims. No proofs: an agent re-derives them. Every formula is copied from the source articles named at the end of the section.

What the algebra is. One object, four descriptions:

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C}),\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{B}=8,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}=4 . $$

$$ \tilde Q=Q_te_t+Q_x\vec e_x+Q_y\vec e_y+Q_z\vec e_z=\sum_{\mu}Q_\mu e_\mu,\qquad Q_\mu=q_\mu+iq'_\mu\in\mathbb{C}, $$

$$ e_t=1,\qquad \vec e_x^{2}=\vec e_y^{2}=\vec e_z^{2}=-e_t,\qquad \vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z,\quad \vec e_y\vec e_z=\vec e_x,\quad \vec e_z\vec e_x=\vec e_y . $$

The one-sentence version. Every structure in this category is one of four products read through one of three involutions, and everything else — ideals, modules, spectrum, polar decomposition, strata — is a consequence of that reading.

The three involutions, and the one identity that generates them.

$$ \tilde Q^{\natural}=Q_te_t-\vec Q,\qquad \bar{\tilde Q}=\bar Q_te_t+\bar{\vec Q},\qquad \tilde Q^{*}=\overline{\tilde Q^{\natural}}=\bar Q_te_t-\bar{\vec Q}, $$

$$ {}^{\natural}\circ{}^{\natural}=\mathrm{id},\qquad \bar{\cdot}\circ\bar{\cdot}=\mathrm{id},\qquad {}^{\natural}\circ\bar{\cdot}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}={}^{*},\qquad (\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)^{*}=\tilde Q_2^{*}\tilde Q_1^{*}. $$

Why this matters to an agent. Note what is absent: there is no $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$-positive-definite norm and no division. The involution group is $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2$, and each of the four elements gives a different bilinear form. The whole "which norm?" difficulty of the category is this one fact.

1. The Products, and the Four That Matter

The product, in the vector form.

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2=\bigl(Q_{1t}Q_{2t}-(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2)\bigr)e_t+Q_{1t}\vec Q_2+Q_{2t}\vec Q_1+\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2\;} $$

with $(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2)=\sum_kQ_{1k}Q_{2k}$ and $\times$ the complex cross product.

The four general products. Applying the involutions to one slot gives exactly four:

$$ \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2,\qquad \tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2,\qquad \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*},\qquad \tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}. $$

The non-trivial claim. These are not four conventions. They are the four elements of the involution group acting on one factor, and they are the only products that are $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear and equivariant for the structure. Every object of the category is one of them:

$$ \text{twelve multiplication tables }T(\star)_{\mu\nu}=e_\mu\star e_\nu\iff\text{four products}\times\text{three readings (plain, halved, sesquilinear)} . $$

The symmetric and antisymmetric halves.

$$ \tilde Q_1\bullet\tilde Q_2=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2+\tilde Q_2\tilde Q_1\bigr),\qquad \tilde Q_1\wedge\tilde Q_2=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2-\tilde Q_2\tilde Q_1\bigr)=\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The antisymmetric half of the plain product is only the cross product — the scalar and the two linear terms are symmetric. So the Lie structure of the plain product is $\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2$, i.e. $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ over $\mathbb{C}$, and nothing else. The four $\wedge$ products give four different Lie brackets, one per involution pair.

The Jordan side.

$$ (te_t+i\vec s)\bullet(ue_t+i\vec v)=(tu+\vec s\cdot\vec v)e_t+i(t\vec v+u\vec s) $$

on $\mathbb{B}_\Psi=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm=t\pm|\vec s|$ and

$$ 2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde Q=2t,\qquad \det\mathsf M(\tilde Q)=t^{2}-|\vec s|^{2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. On the Hermitian subspace the symmetric product is a JB-algebra with a Lorentzian cone, not a Euclidean one: the determinant is $t^2-|\vec s|^2$ and the cone is $C=\{\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q:\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}_\Psi\}$, which is the forward light cone. This is where the physics sector's signature comes from, and it comes from the algebra, not from a choice.

2. The Four Forms and the Four Norms

$$ P(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=Q_{1t}Q_{2t}-(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2), $$

$$ N(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2)=Q_{1t}Q_{2t}+(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2), $$

$$ H(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*})=Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}+(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2}), $$

$$ \mathrm{Kr}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*})=Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}-(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2}). $$

The non-trivial claim, and it is the load-bearing one of the category. Only $H$ is positive definite. $P$ and $N$ are complex-bilinear and symmetric; $\mathrm{Kr}$ is Hermitian with signature $(1,3)$. So the algebra supplies exactly one Hilbert norm ($H$), one indefinite but non-degenerate Hermitian form ($\mathrm{Kr}$), and two symmetric forms ($P$, $N$) — and every "norm" used later is one of these four, never a fifth.

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=|Q_t|^{2}+|Q_x|^{2}+|Q_y|^{2}+|Q_z|^{2}\ge0,\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=Q_t^{2}+Q_x^{2}+Q_y^{2}+Q_z^{2},\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=|Q_t|^{2}-|Q_x|^{2}-|Q_y|^{2}-|Q_z|^{2}. $$

The isometry groups. Each form has one, and they differ:

$$ \mathrm{Isom}(P)=O_4(\mathbb{C}),\qquad \mathrm{Isom}(N)=O_4(\mathbb{C}),\qquad \mathrm{Isom}(H)=\mathrm{U}(4),\qquad \mathrm{Isom}(\mathrm{Kr})=\mathrm{U}(1,3). $$

3. The Inverse, the Zero Divisors, the Square Roots

Invertibility is $N\neq0$, with a closed inverse.

$$ \mathbb{B}^{\times}=\{\tilde Q:\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0\},\qquad \tilde Q^{-1}=\frac{\tilde Q^{\natural}}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}},\qquad \langle\tilde Q_2\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}. $$

The zero divisors.

$$ \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2=0\iff Q_{1t}Q_{2t}=(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2)\ \text{and}\ Q_{1t}\vec Q_2+Q_{2t}\vec Q_1+\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2=0, $$

with $\mathrm{Ann}_r(\tilde Q_1)=\{\tilde Q_2:\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2=0\}$ and $\mathrm{Ann}_l$ the mirror.

The non-trivial claim. The zero-divisor set is the null cone $\mathcal N=\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, a quadric, and it is the union of two families of linear subspaces. Its link is

$$ L=\mathcal N\cap S^{7}_{E}\cong S^{2}\times S^{3}. $$

Every failure of the algebra in the rest of the corpus — the undefined polar decomposition, the singular states, the non-faithful representations — happens on this cone, and the corpus names it once rather than each time.

Square roots.

$$ \tilde Z^{2}=\bigl(Z_t^{2}-(\vec Z,\vec Z)\bigr)e_t+2Z_t\vec Z \ \Longrightarrow\ 2Z_t\vec Z=\vec Q,\qquad Z_t^{2}-(\vec Z,\vec Z)=Q_t . $$

The non-trivial claim. The square-root problem is two equations in four unknowns, so solutions come in families, and the scalar branch has a sign choice $\pm Z_t$ that is not a global sign. On the cone the two branches merge or vanish; that degeneration is the whole content of the "square roots of a general element" article.

4. Idempotents, Nilpotents, Peirce

$$ \tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\pm i\vec u\bigr),\qquad \vec u\cdot\vec u=1, $$

$$ \tilde\Pi_\pm^{2}=\tilde\Pi_\pm,\qquad \tilde\Pi_+\tilde\Pi_-=\tilde\Pi_-\tilde\Pi_+=0,\qquad \tilde\Pi_++\tilde\Pi_-=e_t . $$

The four corners.

$$ \mathbb{B}_{st}=\tilde\Pi_s\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_t,\qquad \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_{++}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{+-}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{-+}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{--},\qquad \dim\mathbb{B}_{ss}=1,\ \dim\mathbb{B}_{st}=1 . $$

The nilpotents, and the basis they give.

$$ \vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tfrac12(i\vec a+\vec b),\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{+-}=\tfrac12(i\vec a-\vec b),\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{-+}^{2}=\vec\Upsilon_{+-}^{2}=0,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{-+}\vec\Upsilon_{+-}=\tilde\Pi_-,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{+-}\vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tilde\Pi_+ , $$

$$ \tilde Q=\bigl(Q_t-iQ_z\bigr)\tilde\Pi_++\bigl(-iQ_x+Q_y\bigr)\vec\Upsilon_{-+}+\bigl(-iQ_x-Q_y\bigr)\vec\Upsilon_{+-}+\bigl(Q_t+iQ_z\bigr)\tilde\Pi_- . $$

The non-trivial claim. The Peirce basis is the matrix-unit basis in disguise:

$$ \tilde\Pi_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \tilde\Pi_-=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad \vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \vec\Upsilon_{+-}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}. $$

So "idempotent, nilpotent, Peirce corner" is not extra structure: it is the canonical form of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. An agent should never treat a Peirce decomposition as an assumption.

Minimal left ideals.

$$ S_\pm(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_\pm(\vec u)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\tilde\Pi_\pm,\ \vec\Upsilon_{\mp\pm}\}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}. $$

5. Modules

Four modules, four readings of the same left action.

$$ V_{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathbb{B},\qquad V_{\mathrm{pol}}=S_+\oplus S_-=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_+\oplus\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_-, $$

$$ \mathcal P=(\tilde\Pi_+,\vec\Upsilon_+,\vec\Upsilon_-,\tilde\Pi_-)\ \ (\text{the Pauli module}),\qquad V_\Pi=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\Pi=\tilde X\}\ \ (\text{unpolarized}). $$

The non-trivial claim. All left $\mathbb{B}$-modules of complex dimension two are isomorphic to a minimal left ideal, and the four names record four bases, not four modules. The category of modules is the category of $\mathbb{C}^2$ with the defining action; the corpus proves this once and then never needs it again.

The two matrix models used throughout.

$$ L_{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde Q)=\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&-Q_x&-Q_y&-Q_z\\Q_x&Q_t&-Q_z&Q_y\\Q_y&Q_z&Q_t&-Q_x\\Q_z&-Q_y&Q_x&Q_t\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathsf M_2(e_t)=I_2,\ \mathsf M_2(\vec e_x)=-i\sigma_1,\ \mathsf M_2(\vec e_y)=-i\sigma_2,\ \mathsf M_2(\vec e_z)=-i\sigma_3 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The axis-free model is real-linear and does not see $i$; the Pauli model is complex-linear and does. The two are related by complexification, and every confusion between "real dimension 8" and "complex dimension 4" is a confusion between these two models.

6. The Spectrum and the Strata

$$ B^{2}=Q_x^{2}+Q_y^{2}+Q_z^{2},\qquad \vec Q^{2}=-B^{2}e_t,\qquad p_{\tilde Q}(X)=(X-Q_t)^{2}+B^{2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The characteristic polynomial has degree two with coefficients in $\mathbb{C}$, and its discriminant is $-4B^2$. So "diagonalisable" is not generic: an element is spectrally degenerate exactly on

$$ \mathcal S=\{B^{2}=0\}=\Sigma_{10}\sqcup\Sigma_{00}, $$

and $\Sigma_{10}=\{N\neq0,\ B^2=0\}=\{Q_t(e_t+\vec\Upsilon):Q_t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times},\ \vec\Upsilon^{2}=0\}$ is a large set, not a measure-zero curiosity. This is the single most surprising quantitative fact of the category.

The strata. Two independent dichotomies cross:

$$ \text{spectral: regular }(B^2\neq0)\ \text{vs degenerate }(B^2=0),\qquad \text{norm: }N\neq0\ \text{vs }N=0, $$

giving four sectors: N-regular/spectrally regular, N-regular/spectrally degenerate ($\Sigma_{10}$), N-degenerate/spectrally regular, N-degenerate/spectrally degenerate ($\Sigma_{00}$). Each has its own article.

The semisimple–nilpotent split.

$$ \tilde Q=\tilde Q_s+\vec\Upsilon,\qquad \tilde Q_s=Q_te_t,\qquad \vec\Upsilon=\vec Q,\qquad \tilde Q_s\vec\Upsilon=\vec\Upsilon\tilde Q_s,\qquad (be_t+b\vec Q-be_t)^{2}=b^{2}\vec Q^{2}=0 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The split is always available and it is trivial to write: the semisimple part is the scalar part. The Jordan basis

$$ \mathcal J(\tilde Q)=(\vec\Upsilon_-,\tilde\Pi_-,\tilde\Pi_+,\vec\Upsilon_+) $$

exists only off $\mathcal S$; on $\mathcal S$ the two eigenvectors coalesce and one needs the nilpotent chain. An agent that assumes a diagonal form on $\Sigma_{10}$ is wrong.

7. Polar Decomposition, and the Two Norms It Needs

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde Q=\tilde Q_H\tilde Q_0,\qquad \tilde Q_H=\bigl(\tilde QQ^{*}\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad \tilde Q_0=\tilde Q_H^{+}\tilde Q\;} $$

$$ \tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}e_t+i\vec W,\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=|Q_t|^{2}+|\vec Q|^{2}\ge0 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The H-polar decomposition needs the positive norm, and it exists exactly off $H=0$, which never happens except at $\tilde Q=0$. The N-polar decomposition is the interesting one: it needs $N\neq0$, it is built from $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=N e_t$, and on the N-degenerate sector it must be replaced by a complete decomposition with a different normal form.

The complete decomposition, off the cone.

$$ \tilde Q=\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\,\tilde U_{\mathrm b}\,\tilde u_{\mathrm r}=\varrho\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}})\exp\Bigl(i\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}\Bigr)\tilde u_{\mathrm r}, $$

i.e. modulus, central phase, boost rotor, spatial rotor — four factors matching the four real dimensions of the "shape" space. The non-trivial claim. The four factors are the four exponentials of the four sectors of the algebra, so the decomposition is a statement about the algebra's subspace structure, not a general polar theorem.

The Kr strata.

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=|Q_t|^{2}-|\vec Q|^{2},\qquad \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}^{\natural*}_{+}\uplus\mathcal K\uplus\mathbb{B}^{\natural*}_{-},\qquad \partial\mathbb{B}^{\natural*}_{\pm}=\mathcal K . $$

The set $\mathcal K$ is the null cone of the Krein form; the two open sets are its inside and outside. The plus set is the one on which the Krein-unitary group acts transitively, and it is where the hyperbolic geometry of the Geometry section lives.

8. The Classification of the Structures

What the classification article does. It lists, for the one algebra, every structure that is "canonical up to the choice of one involution and one form": the real form, the complex form, the two Hermitian halves, the centre, the derived algebra, the automorphism group, the derivation space, the trace form.

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_\Phi\oplus\mathbb{B}_\Psi,\qquad \mathbb{B}_\Phi=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{ie_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\},\qquad \mathbb{B}_\Psi=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}, $$

$$ \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}^{\times}/\mathbb{C}^{\times},\qquad \mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}/\mathbb{B}_C,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})=3, \mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathfrak{so}(1,3)\ \text{as a real Lie algebra}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The two halves are not symmetric: $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ is the anti-Hermitian one and exponentiates to the compact group, $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$ is the Hermitian one and carries the Jordan structure. Every physical reading of the corpus ("material" vs "informational") is a naming of these two subspaces, and the algebra itself does not prefer either.

Summary

The category in six formulas.

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C}),\qquad \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2=\bigl(Q_{1t}Q_{2t}-(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2)\bigr)+Q_{1t}\vec Q_2+Q_{2t}\vec Q_1+\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2, $$

$$ \tilde Q^{-1}=\frac{\tilde Q^{\natural}}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}},\qquad p_{\tilde Q}(X)=(X-Q_t)^{2}+B^{2},\qquad \tilde\Pi_\pm=\tfrac12(e_t\pm i\vec u),\qquad \tilde Q=\tilde Q_H\tilde Q_0 . $$

Five results to keep. Four products, three involutions, one identity $(Q_1Q_2)^{*}=Q_2^{*}Q_1^{*}$. Only $H$ is positive definite; $\mathrm{Kr}$ has signature $(1,3)$; $P$ and $N$ are complex-symmetric. Invertibility is $N\neq0$ with the closed inverse. The Peirce basis is the matrix-unit basis. Spectral degeneracy is the quadric $B^2=0$, which is large.

Three refusals. The category does not give a division algebra: $\mathbb{B}$ has zero divisors and a cone of them. It does not give a canonical norm: four forms compete and the choice is the physics. It does not give a single polar decomposition: three exist and each fails on its own set.

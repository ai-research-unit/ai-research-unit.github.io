The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model Hypothesis: Fifteen Bosons, Two Borders, and the Six Obstacles

Introduction

The hypothesis, stated fairly. The Standard Model gauges twelve directions, $$ \underbrace{8}_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}+\underbrace{3}_{\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}}+\underbrace{1}_{\mathfrak u(1)}=12 . $$ The suggestion is that one chirality is missing, so that the honest count is $$ 8+3+3+1=15 , $$ with a second weak triple restored. And $15$ is exactly $\dim\mathfrak{su}(4)=4^{2}-1$. The further suggestion is geometric: pass from a $3\times3$ block to a $4\times4$ by adding one corner and two borders, and the two borders will carry the two weak triples. That last step is right in the generative sense and wrong in the assemblic sense: the borders do generate $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$, and they pay for it with three of the colour directions.

The verdict, in one line. The count is right, the model exists, and it is not $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. The fifteen-boson group is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}\times\mathrm U(1)_{B-L}$. It is a subgroup of the twenty-one of the Four Colours model, and it is not a subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$.

Why the two fifteens are different. $\dim\mathfrak{su}(4)=15$ and $\dim\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)\bigr)=15$. Equal dimension, different content, and the difference is not a matter of taste:

algebra content dimension rank
$\mathfrak{su}(4)$ $8+1+6$ $15$ $3$
left–right model $8+1+3+3$ $15$ $5$

The two rows agree in the middle column and in nothing else. The $6$ of the first row is the leptoquark sector; the $3+3$ of the second row is the two weak triples. The numerical coincidence is exactly $3+3=6$, and that is the whole of it.

What follows. §The Counting says where the coincidence comes from. §The Geometry You Saw confirms the block picture, which is correct. §The Six Obstacles gives the obstructions, each with an exact number. §What Is True gives the constructive statement, and §What Would Make an $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model lists the honest routes.

The Counting

Twelve is the Standard Model. Three algebras, three couplings, rank $$ 2+1+1=4 . $$

Fifteen is the left–right model. Restoring the second weak triple gives four algebras, four couplings, rank $$ 2+1+1+1=5 . $$ This is not a speculation: the group $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}\times\mathrm U(1)_{B-L}$ is a standard object, and the corpus treats its weak pair in $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$: Two Sides, Six Generators, One Group.

Fifteen is also $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. But $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is simple: one algebra, one coupling, rank $3$. Its fifteen generators split as $8+1+6$, and the six are the leptoquarks, which are gauge bosons and not optional.

So there are two fifteens and only one of them is simple. The hypothesis picks the number $15$ and then reads it as the simple one. The reading is what fails.

The Geometry You Saw

The block picture is correct, and it is worth printing exactly. Write a traceless anti-Hermitian $4\times4$ array in the block form of the quartet $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$: $$ M=\begin{pmatrix}i\alpha & \bar v^{\mathsf T}\\ -v & A\end{pmatrix},\qquad A^{\dagger}=-A,\ v\in\mathbb C^{3},\ \alpha\in\mathbb R . $$ The traceless condition fixes the corner, $i\alpha+\operatorname{tr}A=0$, so the corner is not an extra parameter. The count is then $$ \underbrace{9}_{A\ \text{and its trace}}+\underbrace{6}_{v}=15 . $$

The two blocks, named.

block what it is real dimension the corpus's names
$3\times3$ block $\mathfrak u(3)\subset\mathfrak{su}(4)$, the corner fixed by the trace $9$ the eight gluons and $iD$
border $v$ and $\bar v^{\mathsf T}$ the coset $\mathfrak{su}(4)/\mathfrak u(3)$ $6$ $A_{ta}$ and $iS_{ta}$

And $\mathfrak u(3)=\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$, with the $\mathfrak u(1)$ the corner direction $iD=i\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$, which is $B-L$ on the quartet. So $$ 15=\underbrace{8+1}_{9}+\underbrace{6}_{\text{border}} . $$ This is exactly the shape the hypothesis saw: a $3\times3$ block, one corner, two borders. The arithmetic of the picture is right.

The borders, counted once. The $(t,a)$ border is three complex entries, so six real numbers; the $(a,t)$ border is not independent, because anti-Hermiticity relates it to the first. The two borders together carry six real parameters, not twelve, and those six split as $$ \underbrace{3}_{\text{real antisymmetric }A_{ta}}+\underbrace{3}_{\text{imaginary symmetric }iS_{ta}} . $$ So the "two borders, $3+3$" reading is arithmetically available. It is the identification of that $3+3$ with the two weak triples that fails, and it fails in six separate ways, given next.

The Six Obstacles

Obstacle one: rank. This one is decisive on its own

The two numbers, and which group each belongs to. $\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3$, because $\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(n)=n-1$. The group this hypothesis wants is $$ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}\times\mathrm U(1)_{B-L},\qquad \operatorname{rank}=2+1+1+1=5 . $$ The rank five belongs to the product, not to $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. The model's full group is the twenty-one, $$ G_{4LR}=\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R},\qquad \operatorname{rank}=3+1+1=5, $$ and that product is rank five too, for the same reason: the two weak factors are put in as factors. Rank adds over a direct product. It does not add over a subgroup of one simple factor.

So the rank five is real, and it is not a property of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. The corpus states the rank of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ as three in three places — dimension fifteen, rank three, in Structural GUT Comparisons, in its table, and in its notation list — and the whole point of that note's rank ceiling is that $\mathfrak{u}(4)$ at rank four is the operator layer's maximum.

The obstruction, in its sharpest form. It is not "rank five against rank three" in the abstract. It is this: $$ \underbrace{\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(3)}_{2}+\underbrace{\operatorname{rank}\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}\bigr)}_{2}=4\ >\ 3=\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4). $$ Colour needs two commuting directions. The two weak triples need two more. Four directions in a Cartan that has three. That is the whole argument, and it is a sum.

Why the sum cannot be dodged. A Cartan subalgebra is a maximal abelian subalgebra, so any subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ has rank at most three. Now take the Standard Model's four commuting generators: $T_{3}$, $T_{8}$ from colour, $T_{3L}$ from the weak triple, and hypercharge. The first two are independent, the third is independent of them, and hypercharge is independent of all three. Four commuting directions, in a space whose maximum is three. Verified: the traceless imaginary diagonals are three-dimensional, and adjoining any fourth diagonal leaves the rank at $3$.

Two subgroups of maximal rank, and they are different. $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ has several maximal-rank subalgebras, and two of them matter here, because they are the two ways to spend a rank-three Cartan that a unified model cares about:

subalgebra dimension rank content fits the hypothesis?
$\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$ $9$ $3$ colour and $B-L$ colour: yes, weak: no
$\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$ $7$ $3$ a weak pair and a circle weak: yes, colour: no

Both are genuine subalgebras of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ and both are closed. Verified: the first is the colour option of the corpus, the eight gluons and $iD$; the second is $S(\mathrm U(2)\times\mathrm U(2))$ on a $2+2$ split, dimension seven. A third, $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$, spends the rank on one weak triple and two circles. The hypothesis wants the union of the first two rows, and that union has rank four.

The line that separates them. Where the first row spends its rank on colour and leaves one direction, the second spends it on the weak pair and leaves one. There is one rank-three Cartan and two claimants, and the hypothesis asks for both claimants at once.

Why $\mathfrak su(5)$ works and $\mathfrak su(4)$ does not. $\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(5)=4$, so the Standard Model's four commuting directions fit, and $24-12=12$ accommodates the surplus. The test is the same sum and it passes by one. For the left–right content the sum is five, and the standard simple group of rank five that holds it is $\mathfrak{so}(10)$, which contains $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}$; that is why $\mathfrak{so}(10)$ is the standard left–right unification and $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is not.

Obstacle two: the borders do generate the two $\mathfrak{su}(2)$'s

The reading is right, and the first thing to say is that it works. Take the three real border planes $A_{tx},A_{ty},A_{tz}$ and close them under brackets. They are not closed among themselves: $$ [A_{tx},A_{ty}]=-A_{xy}, $$ and that single bracket produces a colour–colour plane. Closing the triple gives a six-dimensional algebra, and it is exactly $$ \langle A_{tx},A_{ty},A_{tz}\rangle_{\text{Lie}} =\operatorname{span}\{A_{tx},A_{ty},A_{tz},A_{xy},A_{xz},A_{yz}\}=\mathfrak{so}(4) =\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}. $$ So the border does produce the two weak triples. Not as a linear span inside the border, which is impossible, but generatively: the border, closed, is the weak pair. The geometric intuition is correct, and the earlier phrasing of this section — that the border spans no $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ — was the wrong test, because the proposal was never about a span.

The imaginary triple does the same thing. Closing $\{iS_{tx},iS_{ty},iS_{tz}\}$ gives another six-dimensional algebra, and it contains the same three colour planes $A_{xy},A_{xz},A_{yz}$.

And closing both triples together gives everything: $$ \dim\langle\text{border}\rangle_{\text{Lie}}=15=\mathfrak{su}(4). $$ So the two borders generate the whole algebra, not two separate factors. That is the first structural fact, and it is favourable: the border is exactly the right size to be the source of the weak pair.

Obstacle three: the price is three colour planes, and there is only one copy

Where the two triples are built. The self-dual and anti-self-dual combinations are $$ 2T^{L}_{x}=-A_{tx}-A_{yz},\qquad 2T^{R}_{x}=-A_{tx}+A_{yz}, $$ one term in the border and one in the colour block. Every one of the six is half border, half block, and both triples draw on the same three planes $A_{xy},A_{xz},A_{yz}$.

The exact counts. $$ \dim\bigl(\text{weak six}\cap\text{border}\bigr)=3,\qquad \dim\bigl(\text{weak six}\cap\mathfrak u(3)\bigr)=3, $$ and, separating the two triples, $$ \dim\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\cap\mathfrak u(3)\bigr)=0,\qquad \dim\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}\cap\mathfrak u(3)\bigr)=0, $$ $$ \dim\bigl((\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R})\cap\mathfrak u(3)\bigr)=3 . $$ Each weak triple on its own avoids the colour block entirely. Together they meet it in three directions, and those three are $$ \mathfrak{so}(4)\cap\mathfrak u(3)=\operatorname{span}\{A_{xy},A_{xz},A_{yz}\}, $$ the three real off-diagonal planes of the colour block — the three gluons that turn one colour into another.

So the two weak triples and the colour octet compete for the same three directions. A direction can be in the weak pair or in the colour octet, not in both. That is the content of the corpus's "$42$ of $48$ brackets nonzero".

The span and the closure, which differ. $$ \dim\bigl(\text{weak six}+\mathfrak u(3)\bigr)=12,\qquad \dim\bigl\langle\text{weak six}+\mathfrak u(3)\bigr\rangle_{\text{Lie}}=15 . $$ So the fifteen generators $8+1$ plus the six weak ones are twelve-dimensional as a list and generate all fifteen. Closing them does not give a fifteen-dimensional product; it gives the simple algebra back.

And the rank closes the door. The group the hypothesis wants is $8+3+3+1$, rank $5$. The algebra that carries it has rank $3$. Adding the three leptoquarks to reach $8+3+3+1+3$ does not help: the sum is still generated in a rank-three algebra.

So the reading of the borders is right, the generation is right, and the assembly is what fails: $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and the two weak triples cannot be carried at once, because they want the same three planes.

Obstacle four: the fifteen quartet arrays do not close

Take the fifteen bosons the hypothesis wants, as arrays of the quartet: eight gluons, the corner $iD$, and the six weak arrays. Their rank is twelve, not fifteen, and they are not closed: $$ [\text{the fifteen}]\not\subset\text{the fifteen},\qquad\text{closure}=15=\mathfrak{su}(4). $$

The smallest witness. With $iD=i\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ the $B-L$ direction, the commutator with each weak generator is exactly the imaginary symmetric border array in the same direction: $$ [iD,T^{L}_{a}]=[iD,T^{R}_{a}]=2\,iS_{ta}\ \ne0,\qquad a=x,y,z . $$ Six nonzero commutators out of six. Verified in all six cases.

What that means. On the quartet there is no fifteen-dimensional subalgebra whose content is $8+1+3+3$. Closing the set up gives all of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, that is, the content changes to $8+1+6$ and the weak triples are gone. The fifteen exists as a group, but not as fifteen matrices on one carrier.

This is the corpus's copy theorem in one line. The weak factor acts on $\mathbb C^{2}_{L}$, a carrier with no colour rows. On that carrier the fifteen closes. On the quartet it cannot: colour and weak do not commute, $42$ of $48$ brackets are nonzero, and the Lie closure is all fifteen generators.

Obstacle five: the border is irreducible, so its six directions share one mass

This is the one that ends the concrete version of the plan — put the two weak triples in the two borders, and let the leptoquarks be heavy.

The module is irreducible. One border direction generates all six by bracketing: $$ \dim\bigl(\text{the module generated by }A_{tx}\text{ under }\mathfrak u(3)\bigr)=6 . $$ So the border carries no invariant subspace, and a gauge-invariant mass operator on it must commute with the action of $\mathfrak u(3)$.

The commutant is computed exactly. Solving $[M,\operatorname{ad}_{u}]=0$ for all $u\in\mathfrak u(3)$ on the six-dimensional border gives a two-dimensional commutant, spanned by the identity and the complex structure $J$ that sends $A_{ta}\mapsto iS_{ta}$: $$ \operatorname{Comm}(\text{border})=\operatorname{span}\{\mathrm I_{6},\,J\},\qquad J^{2}=-\mathrm I_{6}. $$ This is Schur's lemma for a module of complex type: the six real directions are one complex triplet, so its invariant endomorphisms are the complex scalars.

A mass squared must also be symmetric, because it comes from the trace form, and $J$ is antisymmetric. So the coefficient of $J$ vanishes and the mass operator is a scalar: $$ M^{2}_{\text{border}}=m^{2}\,\mathrm I_{6}. $$ All six border bosons have one mass. You cannot make three of them light at $100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and three heavy. A gauge vacuum cannot split an irreducible module, and the border is irreducible as a $\mathfrak u(3)$-module.

The physical consequence. In $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ the six border directions are the leptoquarks, and the corpus's bound is $M_X\gtrsim4.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{J}$ from $K_L\to\mu e$. Proton decay does not bite, because the leading operator is dimension nine. So the obstruction is not proton decay; it is that the six border directions are one object. If they are heavy, the $W$ is heavy too, and there is no weak interaction at $100\,\mathrm{GeV}$.

Obstacle six: one coupling against four

$\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is simple, so it has one gauge coupling. The fifteen-boson left–right model has four: $g_{3}$, $g_{2L}$, $g_{2R}$, $g_{B-L}$. A simple group cannot produce four independent couplings at any scale below its breaking, and above the breaking it forces relations among them that the observed spectrum does not satisfy.

This is the obstacle that makes $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ a desert hypothesis, and it is sharper here: $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ at least contains the Standard Model. $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ contains only $\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_{B-L}$, so it would force $g_{3}=g_{B-L}$ while saying nothing whatever about the weak couplings.

The corpus's own threshold statement is the same point in one line: $g_{4}=g_{L}=g_{R}$ at one scale, with its failure, is The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model. A single group forces a single coupling, and the data has four.

What Is True

The fifteen-boson model exists, and its place in the chain is exact. $$ \underbrace{12}_{\text{Standard Model}}\ \subset\ \underbrace{15}_{\text{left--right}}\ \subset\ \underbrace{21}_{\text{Four Colours}} . $$

The middle group. $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}\times\mathrm U(1)_{B-L}$, dimension $$ 8+3+3+1=15, $$ rank $5$, four couplings.

Where it sits. Its colour-times-$B-L$ part is inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$: $$ \mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_{B-L}\ \subset\ \mathfrak{su}(4),\qquad 9\ \subset\ 15, $$ and the two weak triples are external to $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. So $$ \mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R} \ \subset\ \mathfrak{su}(4)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}, $$ that is, the fifteen is inside the twenty-one, not inside the fifteen of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$.

The gap is exactly the six leptoquarks. $$ 21-6=15 . $$ So the hypothesis is correct about the ladder and correct about the number, and it locates the rung one group too low. The fifteen-boson theory is the Four Colours model with the six leptoquark directions deleted. Deleting them is not a group operation: $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is simple, so its six border directions are not an ideal, and there is no quotient of the twenty-one by them. The fifteen exists only as a different product, $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$, sitting inside the twenty-one.

The rank ladder confirms the placement.

rung group dimension rank couplings
Standard Model $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm U(1)$ $12$ $4$ $3$
left–right $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}\times\mathrm U(1)$ $15$ $5$ $4$
Four Colours $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}$ $21$ $5$ $3$

Between the second and third rungs the rank does not move and the dimension grows by six. The six added are the leptoquarks. Between the first and second the rank grows by one and the dimension by three: the restored chirality. Those are two different steps and the hypothesis merges them into one.

One caveat on the middle rung. It is a group-theoretic intermediate, and the minimal content of the Four Colours model does not stop there: one bidoublet $\Phi=(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ with $\Phi_0=v_R\,e_t\otimes e_{\uparrow}$ breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm U(1)_{B-L}$ at once, so the chain runs $21\to12\to9$ and skips $15$. Reaching the fifteen-boson rung needs a second field. That is The Breaking Chain's statement, and it is why the fifteen is a legitimate group but not an automatic stage.

What Would Make an $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model

Three honest routes, each with its price.

Route one: accept the leptoquarks and drop the weak sector. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ alone, fifteen bosons, content $8+1+6$, rank $3$, one coupling. This is the Pati–Salam colour group without the weak factors. It is a consistent gauge theory. It contains no $W$, no $Z$, no photon. The corpus's Unbroken Subgroup is the calculation: the nine directions that commute with the corner are the eight gluons and $B-L$, and nothing else survives.

Route two: accept the leptoquarks and put the weak factors outside. This is the Four Colours model, twenty-one bosons, rank $5$. It is the corpus's model, and it is the only one of the three that has a weak sector and a colour sector at once. The price is a declared copy plus a leptoquark mass at $10^{15}\,\mathrm{GeV}$.

Route three: enlarge the group until the rank fits. $\mathfrak{su}(5)$ has rank $4$ and contains the Standard Model; $\mathfrak{su}(5)$ or $\mathfrak{so}(10)$ are the standard routes. The price is a desert and proton decay, and the reward is that the counting is forced rather than chosen.

What does not work is a fifteen-dimensional simple group with content $8+1+3+3$. No such object exists: a simple group of dimension fifteen is $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, and its content is fixed at $8+1+6$ by its own root system.

Summary

The number is right and the group is not. $8+3+3+1=15$ is a true count, and it names $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}\times\mathrm U(1)_{B-L}$. It is not $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, whose content is $8+1+6$. The coincidence is $3+3=6$, and nothing more.

The block picture was read correctly and interpreted one step too far. The $3\times3$ block is $\mathfrak u(3)$, nine directions; the corner is fixed by the trace; the two borders carry six real parameters together. Every one of those statements is confirmed. What fails is that the border is a module: $[\text{border},\text{border}]\subset\mathfrak u(3)$, and the border contains no $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ at all.

The borders do generate the two weak triples, and that is the strong point of the proposal. Closing $\{A_{tx},A_{ty},A_{tz}\}$ gives $\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ exactly. What it costs is three colour directions: the closure contains $A_{xy},A_{xz},A_{yz}$, and the paired weak algebra meets $\mathfrak u(3)$ in exactly those three. The list of twelve spans twelve, and closing it gives fifteen: $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and the two weak triples compete for the same three planes.

The border is irreducible, so it cannot be split into a light half and a heavy half. Its invariant endomorphisms are the complex scalars, its mass operator is a multiple of the identity, and all six of its directions carry one mass. This is the obstruction that ends the concrete plan, and it is not proton decay.

Rank is the decisive obstruction, and it is three lines long. $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ has rank $3$; four commuting traceless imaginary diagonals are always dependent; the Standard Model needs four commuting generators and the left–right model five.

The positive result is the ladder, with the rung corrected. $12\subset15\subset21$ is real, and the fifteen-boson rung sits inside the twenty-one, not inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. The gap between the fifteen-boson rung and the twenty-one is exactly the six leptoquarks, and those six are what a fourth colour is.

Operator Migration: The Operators Subcategory under the Four Cells

Introduction

This article lists the articles of the §Operators subcategory of maths.md, and states for each one what it becomes. It is an internal article. It fixes no mathematics and it edits nothing. It records a decision and its consequences.

The decision. Three headings are renamed, one is added, one is dissolved, and five articles change place. The renamed headings are the one-side group, the two-side group, and the tied two-side group. The added one is the tied one-side cell, placed between the one-side group and the two-side group. The dissolved one is §Operator Chirality, whose article joins §The Four Readings and the Slots. The articles that change place are four that rise above the cells — §Operator Chirality, the SVD, and §Adjoint Pair of $L$ and $R$ Operators — and §The Hermitian Pair of Operators, which also rises. One article is created, and one is rewritten.

The menu edits, already applied. All the moves are in maths.md. §The Four Readings and the Slots now holds four articles in this order: Introduction to the Operators, Operator Chirality, the SVD, and Adjoint Pair of $L$ and $R$ Operators, at lines 6771 to 6780. The chirality heading is dissolved. §The Hermitian Pair of Operators stands after §The Two-Sided Operators, at line 6798, holding one article, and §Spin and the Rotation follows it. No title, no link and no comment is altered.

The reason. Two independent questions classify an operator of $\mathbb{B}$, and the present menu answers only one of them. The first question is how many multiplications each term of the operator carries: one, so that the term acts on one side, or two, so that it acts on two sides. The second is whether the parameters are free, or tied to one another by an involution. The menu answers the first and leaves the second implicit. The tied one-side cell therefore has no heading and no article, although it is the cell the physics corpus works in.

Why the cells must be contiguous. The four cells are two questions applied to one algebra, and a reader who meets them one at a time, with three unrelated articles in the middle, has to hold the grid in memory across a gap. The two articles that follow §Spin and the Rotation are consumers of the cells and not members of them: the spin is a tied two-side operator with a unitary parameter, and the Dirac spinor is the tied one-side pair. So the cells go as one block, and the consumers follow.

Why the chirality article rises. It is not a consumer of the cells. It is the article that defines the words the cells are named with: left slot, right slot, both slots for the scalars, and no single side for everything with two parameters. A reader who meets §Operators Acting on One Side without having read it has to take the word side on trust, and the four new headings would then be four words with no stated meaning. Read before the cells, it does the opposite: it says what a side is before any cell is named, and the four headings then follow from it. It also settles the naming hazard of §The Naming Hazard, because the reader learns from it that no single side and acting on two sides are two different questions about one operator.

Why the SVD and the adjoints travel with it. Four articles answer one question — what an operator is made of — at four levels. The readings give it a shape and an inserted sign. The side label gives it a slot. The SVD gives it a sum of at most four rank-one terms. The four adjoints give it a partner. All four are statements about a single operator, made before any family of operators is named, so all four stand ahead of the cells. The fourth also earns its place for a second reason: the two tied cells are named by involutions, and this is the article that lists the involutions before either cell is named.

What the reordering is, in total. §Operator Chirality, the SVD and §Adjoint Pair of $L$ and $R$ Operators leave their groups and follow Introduction to the Operators, and the chirality heading is dissolved. §The Hermitian Pair of Operators rises above §Spin and the Rotation. Nothing else moves, and the one insertion, the new cell B article, goes where the cell order places it.

What the order becomes. The ambient space; the readings; the side label; the SVD; the four adjoints; the one-side cell; the tied one-side cell; the two-side cell; the tied two-side cell; the spin and the rotation; the spinors. The space and its four preparatory articles come first, then $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ as one block, then the two consumers.

What follows. Three headings are renamed, one is added, one is dissolved, and five articles change position. Of the twelve entries listed below, two are untouched and stay, one changes nothing but its heading, three gain one section, one paragraph or one sentence in place, one is renamed and rewritten, one is created, and five move. The counts are in §What Changes, Counted.

The Four Cells

The first question: one multiplication or two. A term of an operator is a product of the argument with parameters drawn from the algebra. The one-sided operator $L_{\tilde Q}$ has one parameter and one multiplication. The two-sided operator $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}$ has two parameters and two multiplications. The distinction is a statement about the shape of a term. It is not a statement about a carrier, and it needs no matrix.

The second question: free or tied. In $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}$ the two parameters are independent. In $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}$ the second is the involute of the first, and no choice is left. The tied family is a subset of the untied one, cut out by one equation.

The four cells. Each is a set of operators on the one algebra $\mathbb{B}$.

cell multiplication per term tie cell name
A one free operators acting on one side
B one tied by an involution Hermitian pairs acting on one side
C two free operators acting on two sides
D two tied by an involution Hermitian pairs acting on two sides

The sizes separate the cells.

cell set dimension
A $\operatorname{End}_L(\mathbb{B})$, and $\operatorname{End}_R(\mathbb{B})$ complex space, complex dimension $4$
B $\{(L_{\tilde Q},L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}})\}$ complex space, complex dimension $4$
C $\operatorname{End}_{LR}(\mathbb{B})$ complex cone, complex dimension $7$
D $\operatorname{End}_{LR^{*}}(\mathbb{B})$ real cone, real dimension $7$

The cells with one multiplication are spaces. The cells with two multiplications are cones. That is the structural reason the four cells are four, and not two subdivided by taste.

The Two Ties

Each tied cell needs a named involution, and the two involutions are not the same. This is the point the migration must state once, in one place, or the two tied cells will be read as one.

Cell D is tied by ${}^{*}$. The Hermitian Pair of Operators Acting on Two Sides, §The Rigidity, proves that $L_{\tilde A}R_{\tilde B}$ maps $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ into itself exactly when $\tilde B=\lambda\tilde A^{*}$ with $\lambda$ real. So the tie of cell D is the Hermitian conjugation, and the real scalar $\lambda$ is part of the answer and not a nuisance. Verified by recomputation on $20$ random parameters: the tie with ${}^{*}$ and real $\lambda$ stabilises the sector $20$ times out of $20$; the tie with ${}^{\natural}$ stabilises it $0$ times; the tie with ${}^{*}$ and complex $\lambda$ stabilises it $0$ times.

Cell B is tied by ${}^{\natural}$. The one-side pair closes its own product when the second parameter is the natural conjugate. Verified by recomputation on $100$ random elements: $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}$ is central $100$ times out of $100$, and it equals the quaternionic norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$; the product commutes with the mirrored product, $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q$, $100$ times out of $100$. The tie with ${}^{*}$ gives a central product $0$ times out of $100$.

Why the two differ. ${}^{*}=\overline{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$ is conjugate linear, so $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$ is not central for a general element. ${}^{\natural}$ is complex linear, so $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}$ is. The tied one-side pair is tied by the complex-linear involution, and the tied two-side pair by the conjugate-linear one.

Why both cells may be called Hermitian. The two involutions agree on the real quaternion subspace, and the gradient lies in it, because its marks act on the units at fixed coordinate: $\tilde\nabla^{*}=\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$. Verified on real coefficients, where the two involutions give the same element. On the gradient the two tied cells are therefore the same pair, which is why the physics corpus writes the pair once and both readings agree with it.

The sign on the derivative. On an element the pair is $(L_{\tilde Q},L_{\tilde Q^{*}})$. On the gradient the transpose of the derivative contributes one minus, so the operator adjoint pair is $(L_{\tilde\nabla},-L_{\tilde\nabla^{\natural}})$. The pair of the derivative is skew-adjoint, and $i$ times it is Hermitian. This sign is already recorded in the physics corpus and must not be lost in the rewrite.

The Naming Hazard

Two phrases, one word. The new headings say that cells C and D are operators acting on two sides. Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra labels a composite $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}$ as holding no single side. Both sentences are true, and a reader who meets them without a warning reads them as a contradiction.

The reconciliation. The two phrases answer different questions. Acting on two sides counts the multiplications of a term: two parameters means two multiplications, one on each side. The side label names the one slot an operator occupies when it occupies exactly one: left for a pure left multiplier, right for a pure right multiplier, both for a scalar, none otherwise. A composite with two free parameters occupies neither slot alone, so it has no side label, and it still acts on two sides.

Where the sentence goes. It goes in The Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra, at the head of §The Four Cells of the Operator Space. It does not go in Operator Chirality, which stays untouched and stays the authority for the label.

The Migration, Article by Article

The entries follow the present order of the menu, and the new article takes the place that order gives it, between the one-side group and the two-side group.

Now. First article of §The Endomorphism Algebra. After. Same heading, same position.

What it becomes. The declaration of the ambient space on which all four cells are subsets: $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$, sixteen complex dimensions. 2. Introduction to the Operators of the Biquaternion Algebra

Body. Untouched. Title. Unchanged. Heading. Unchanged.

What it becomes. The article that licenses the four cells, read in its present position between the ambient space and the one-side cell. 4. The SVD Decomposition of Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra

Now. Second article of §The Endomorphism Algebra. After. Unchanged in body and title, moved out of §The Endomorphism Algebra into §The Four Readings and the Slots, as its fourth article.

Body. Untouched. Title. Unchanged.

What it becomes. Nothing new. It moves with §Operator Chirality, and §Adjoint Pair of $L$ and $R$ Operators follows it. The four answer the same kind of question — what an operator is made of — at four levels: the readings give it a shape and an inserted sign, the label gives it a slot, the SVD gives it a sum of at most four rank-one terms, and the adjoints give it a partner. Read in that order, the four prepare the cells.

What the restructure uses from it. One fact, quoted and not developed: every endomorphism of $\mathbb{B}$ is a finite sum of at most four rank-one terms, $F=\sum_{k\le 4}\rho_k\mathcal{P}_{\tilde U_k,\tilde V_k}$, with orthonormal families and decreasing weights. A rank-one term is a two-side operator with two free parameters. So the theorem says that cell C spans the ambient space and that four terms always suffice. That sentence is written once, in §The Four Cells of article 1, and Two-Sided Operators states the same thing from inside cell C as its generation theorem. The SVD is the view from above, and it sits above.

7. Hermitian Pairs of Operators Acting on One Side

Now. Does not exist. After. New heading §Hermitian Pairs of Operators Acting on One Side, holding one new article of the same name, inserted here.

Body. New. Title. New.

Why here and not elsewhere. The order of the subcategory is: a cell, then the tied cell of the same multiplication count. The two-side group already follows that pattern at the bottom, where the untied two-side cell is followed by the tied one. The one-side group must follow it too, so the tied one-side cell goes directly after The One-Sided Operators and directly before Two-Sided Operators. That is the only insertion the order allows, and it is the insertion the physics corpus needs.

What it must contain. Five sections, in this order.

  • The pair. $(L_{\tilde Q},L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}})$, read as an operator together with its adjoint. The set is a complex space of complex dimension four, because ${}^{\natural}$ is complex linear.
  • The closure. $L_{\tilde Q}L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}=L_{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}}$, with $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ central for every element. Verified $100$ out of $100$. This is the whole content of the cell: the product of the pair is multiplication by a scalar.
  • The square and the second order. The closure is the passage from the first order to the second. The squared pair is the second-order operator, and the sign of the constant term is the norm. Nothing here multiplies on the right and nothing here has two free parameters.
  • The gradient. On the gradient the two involutions agree, $\tilde\nabla^{*}=\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, so the pair is the physics pair. The operator adjoint adds the sign of the derivative, so the pair is skew-adjoint and $i$ times it is Hermitian.
  • What the cell does not do. It does not preserve the sectors. $L_{\tilde A}$ maps $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ into itself exactly when $\tilde A$ is a real scalar, verified $20$ out of $20$ for a real scalar and $0$ out of $20$ for a general element. Sector stability is the warrant of cell D and of no other cell. A reader who carries the word stable from one tied cell to the other will make a false statement, and this section prevents it.

Why the article must exist. The pair $(L_{\tilde Q},L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}})$ is written throughout the physics corpus and defined nowhere in the mathematics corpus. Its definition is in Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Two First-Order Equations and Their Symbol (the section was called The Pair Operator and Its Symbol before the glyph was retired), which is a conventions article. The mathematics subcategory has a hole exactly where the physics corpus does its first-order work, and the hole is filled by a conventions article that carries no proof.

Why the cell is not cell D written twice. Cell D is the same pair with the second member moved to the other side, by the reading $(L_{\tilde Q})^{N}=L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$ and $(L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}})^{\approx}=R_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$. The two cells are one pair in two placements, and the sentence that says so belongs at the end of this article, with the pointer forward to cell D. That sentence is the architecture, and it is the only place in the subcategory where the two tied cells are named together.

8. Operators Acting on Two Sides

Now. First article of §The Two-Sided Operators. After. Same article, heading renamed to §Operators Acting on Two Sides.

Body. Kept, with one paragraph added. Title. Unchanged.

What it becomes. Cell C. The cone, the operator $T_{\tilde Q,\tilde P}$, the scaling orbit, the composition law $T_{\tilde Q,\tilde P}T_{\tilde R,\tilde S}=T_{\tilde Q\tilde R,\tilde S\tilde P}$, the generation theorem and the sixteen basis operators, the one-sided boundaries, the plain adjoint, the isometries and the absence of a pin group.

The added paragraph. 9. The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the Biquaternion Algebra and the Bi-module Structure

Now. Second article of §The Two-Sided Operators. After. Unchanged, in place.

Body. Kept, with one sentence added. Title. Unchanged.

What it becomes. The algebra of cell C. The enveloping algebra $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ acts on $\mathbb{B}$ by $a\otimes b^{\mathrm{opp}}\mapsto L_aR_b$, and the isomorphism with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$ says that cell C is the image of the decomposable tensors. The two parameters are the two tensor factors. That is the cleanest available statement of what a tie is: a tie is an equation between the two factors.

The added sentence. After the bi-module structure is stated: the tie of cell D is the equation $b=\lambda a^{*}$ between the two factors, so the tied subcone is the fixed set of an involutive map on the tensor product. One sentence, and it gives the tied cells a home in the enveloping algebra without a new article.

What changes. Three things, and no more.

The biquaternion object is not that: it is the object whose second parameter is forced to be the involute of the first by the sector condition. A generic word for a generic construction hides a theorem. The name that carries the theorem is the name of the tie.

Summary

The tied two-side group rises above the spin group, so that the cells are read as a block and the two consumers after them.

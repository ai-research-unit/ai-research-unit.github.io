The Colour Wall, Restated: What a Larger Group Moves and What It Does Not

Introduction

The question. The corpus has two notes saying the biquaternion algebra reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ but not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and one saying the gluon is an octet outside the algebra. Does moving to $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ change either verdict?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\;\text{No. Colour is a stabiliser, not a subalgebra of the algebra's own action; the wall moves from }\mathfrak{su}(3)\text{ to }\mathfrak m,\text{ and }\mathfrak{su}(4)\cap\operatorname{Comm}L(\mathbb H)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\text{ is unchanged.}\;} $$

What this note is, in the migration. New article $6$ of §12.4, phase $2$, and the one article in the migration whose job is to say what the migration does not achieve. It is a companion to the-gluon-an-octet-outside-the-biquaternion-algebra and to why-the-biquaternion-algebra-reaches-su-2-but-not-su-3, and it supersedes neither.

What is claimed. That the three walls the corpus records are distinct and that only one of them moves; that the moving one is the dimension wall, and that it is replaced by a different obstruction of the same size; that the algebra's own action contains $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and cannot contain $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ acting on the same carrier; and that the frame's colour is a stabiliser acting on a copy, which is a weaker claim than containment.

What is not claimed. That the migration breaches the wall. It does not, and any article that says it does is wrong. Nor that the wall is fatal: a frame can be right about a group it cannot house, and §5 says what that costs.

1. The Three Walls, Distinguished

First, the test being applied, because the three walls answer three questions and not one. The corpus's implicit test of legitimacy has been that a gauge group must sit inside the algebra: its generators must be elements of $\mathbb B$. Run honestly, that test rejects every factor the corpus uses except the central $\mathrm{U}(1)$ — $L_{\vec e_a}$ and $R_{\vec e_a}$ are endomorphisms, not elements, so even the two-sided action fails it. The replacement is the corpus's own Conway grading: a generator is an element of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)$, a sum of Conway words $L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$, and legitimacy is its length $\ell=\operatorname{rank}c\le4$. Under that grading the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s are length $1$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is length $2$, and $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is at most $3$. The criterion is stated once in the published frame article, §Where Each Factor Comes From, and How Firmly; this section applies it.

The corpus's obstruction has been stated three ways, and they are three different statements.

wall statement kind
the dimension wall $\dim\mathfrak{su}(3)=8>\dim\mathfrak{u}(2)=4$, the largest compact subalgebra of $\mathbb B$ counting
the carrier wall the algebra acts on a four-dimensional complex space; colour needs a three-dimensional space that is not one of its invariant subspaces structural
the chiral wall the algebra's product is two-sided; a chiral gauge theory needs a one-sided action representation-theoretic

Only the first is about numbers, and only the first is touched by the frame. $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is fifteen-dimensional and has an eight-dimensional subalgebra, so the counting objection disappears the moment the group is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$.

The other two do not move, and §2 and §3 say why. A migration that claimed to have removed "the colour wall" would be conflating three objections with one, and the conflation is the most likely way for this migration to go wrong.

2. The Carrier Wall Does Not Move

The reason is the commutant theorem, and it is the sharpest statement in the migration.

2.1 The theorem. If colour acted on the algebra's own carrier, it would have to commute with the algebra's own action — because both would be acting on the same four dimensions, and the carrier is what the algebra acts on, not something beside it. Then

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)\cap\bigl\{X:[X,L_{\vec e_a}]=0,\ a=x,y,z\bigr\}=\operatorname{span}\{R_{\vec e_x},R_{\vec e_y},R_{\vec e_z}\}\cong\mathfrak{su}(2), \qquad\dim_{\mathbb R}=3 . $$

Verified: the right multiplications commute with the left ones, each $R_{\vec e_a}$ is anti-Hermitian and traceless, and $R_1$ is Hermitian and not traceless, so $iR_1$ is the only further anti-Hermitian element and it is excluded. The full commutant of $L(\mathbb H)$ in $M_4(\mathbb C)$ is $R(\mathbb B)$, eight real dimensions, of which exactly three lie in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$.

2.2 So the ceiling is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and it is lower than the old one, not the same. The corpus's old statement was that the maximal compact subalgebra of $\mathbb B$ is $\mathfrak{u}(2)$, of dimension four. The new statement is that the maximal subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ commuting with the algebra's action is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, of dimension three. Four and three are not the same number, and the earlier draft of this note said they were. The count falls for a reason worth naming: $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ contains the central phase, and the phase is $iL_{e_t}$. It commutes with the whole left action, because $e_t$ is central, but it is not traceless: $\operatorname{tr}(iL_{e_t})=4i$, verified. The phase is therefore in $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ and not in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, so the commutant inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and not $\mathfrak{u}(2)$. The frame therefore does not raise the ceiling; it records a tighter one.

2.3 What the frame does instead. It declares the colour group to act on a formal copy of the carrier's quartet:

$$ (\text{internal }\mathbf 4)\otimes(\text{spinor}), \qquad \mathbf 4_{\text{int}}\cong\mathbb C e_t\oplus B_V . $$

The copy inherits the split, because the split is canonical; it does not inherit the Lorentz action, because it must not. So colour is a stabiliser acting on the copy, and the carrier wall is not breached — it is routed around, which is a different thing, and the difference has to be visible.

2.4 The honest formulation, in one sentence. Colour is not in the algebra; the split that colour needs is. The migration's claim is about the split, and it must stay about the split.

2.5 What changes is the question, and the ceiling survives the change. Before the migration the obstruction was stated as $\mathfrak{su}(3)\not\subset\mathfrak{u}(2)$ — the algebra's own compact part is four-dimensional and colour needs eight. That is a statement about a subalgebra of $\mathbb B$, and it is the statement the migration retires. The surviving statement is the commutant of 2.1, and the point of stating it twice is that the two statements answer different questions and agree in verdict: the phase that $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ counts is $iL_{e_t}$, of trace $4i$, so it leaves the moment the trace condition is imposed. The ceiling is three, not four — and it does not rise when the group grows. $\mathfrak{u}(2)\subset\mathbb B$ was a ceiling that fifteen dimensions could break; a commutant is a ceiling that no enlargement of the group touches, because it is computed against the algebra's action and not against the group's size.

Colour is not that commutant, and the check is one line. Every colour generator fixes the canonical line, $Xe_t=0$ for $X\in\mathfrak{su}(3)$, verified on all eight; whereas $R_b(e_t)=b$, so $R_b$ fixes the line only when $b=0$. Hence

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)\cap R(\mathbb B)=0 . $$

So two obstacles are not one. The group-theoretic obstruction is gone: colour exists, inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, at length two or three in the Conway words. The module-theoretic obstruction stands, and it is now located exactly: no operator that commutes with the algebra's multiplication is colour, so colour never multiplies.

3. The Chiral Wall Does Not Move

The third wall is untouched, and the reason is that it was never about the dimension of the group.

3.1 What the wall says. The algebra's multiplication is two-sided: $L_x$ and $R_x$ both act, and every module is at least a bimodule. A chiral gauge theory needs an action that distinguishes left from right at the level of the gauge coupling. In the frame that distinction is put in by the representation assignment — $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ on $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ on $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ — and the assignment is a declaration.

3.2 Why a larger group cannot supply it. Chirality is a property of the module, not of the group. $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ for any $N$ has both chiral and vector-like representations, and which one occurs is a choice. Enlarging $4\to 15$ changes which representations are available; it does not force the chiral one.

3.3 The test that separates the two. A frame that had solved the chiral wall would produce the sixteen from an algebraic structure with no choice of representation. The migration does not: it writes the sixteen and declares the assignment. That is the diagnostic, and it is mechanical.

3.4 Consequence. Every article downstream quotes the wall. None solves it. The migration's largest structural gain is on the group side — the fourth colour, the tracelessness, the anomaly ledger — and its largest unchanged obstruction is on the chirality side. Saying both in one breath is what makes the migration honest.

4. What the Larger Group Does Move

One thing genuinely moves, and it is worth its own section because it is the migration's real gain on this front.

4.1 The colour group is now a stabiliser rather than a subalgebra. Previously, colour would have had to be inside the algebra, which is false. Now colour is $\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(\mathbb C e_t)$: it acts on a three-dimensional quotient, not as a subalgebra of the algebra's action. A stabiliser needs no containment, so the carrier wall of §2 is the only obstacle, and the group-theoretic existence question is settled.

4.2 The octet is now inside a group the frame owns. $\mathbf 8$ is the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ block of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, so the gluon is no longer an octet outside the frame's group — it is the octet of the stabiliser. The corpus's old title, The Gluon: An Octet Inside the Carrier's Group, becomes An Octet Inside the Carrier's Group — the rename of §12.6 — and the rename is accurate precisely because it says inside the carrier's group and not inside the algebra.

4.3 The difference between the two phrasings is the whole article. Inside the carrier's group is true. Inside the algebra is false and stays false. A reader who drops the phrase "carrier's group" has misunderstood the migration, and the rename list is written to make that phrase un-droppable.

5. What This Costs

Three costs, and the second is the one that matters.

5.1 The frame asserts a copy, and copies are cheap. A structure that transfers — one canonical line, one axis-free three-space, a traceless diagonal — can be found between many pairs of objects. What makes this copy more than an analogy is that the three properties Pati–Salam uses all transfer (the declaration note, §6); but a reader is entitled to ask for more than three properties, and the frame cannot give more.

5.2 The algebra's own multiplication remains unused by colour. The algebra multiplies; colour does not multiply. In the frame, colour is a symmetry of a vector space the algebra happens to be isomorphic to as a vector space, and the isomorphism uses no product. That is the residue of the wall, and it is not small: a group that acts on a space but not on its algebra is a group the algebra does not contain. Read against the criterion, the sentence has a second half that the first half obscures: the algebra does not contain the group, and the split still supplies the line that colour fixes. So the correct statement is not "the algebra does not know about colour" but "the algebra knows about colour only through one line". An address and a containment are not the same relation, and this paragraph is where the difference is paid for.

5.3 The wall will be quoted against the migration, and it should be. A critic will say: you have renamed the obstruction instead of removing it. The correct reply is not "no" but "we have removed one of three and we say so" — which is why this article exists and why it is placed in the migration rather than at its end.

6. What Would Actually Breach the Wall

Four candidate mechanisms, and the verdict on each, so that the wall is stated as a live problem rather than a prohibition.

6.1 A larger carrier. If the algebra acted on a space of dimension $\ge6$, a commuting $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ would fit. The corpus's carrier is four-dimensional because the algebra is; a larger carrier means a different algebra, and that is a different programme.

6.2 A one-sided module. The corpus's open problem: a module on which the algebra acts on one side, chiral, of dimension $\ge3$. If it exists, the chiral wall and part of the carrier wall fall together. internal-note-on-the-electroweak-interaction-in-the-corpus.md states the four levels of the attempt.

6.3 Colour as an inner symmetry of the algebra's automorphism group. $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)$ is six real dimensions and meets $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ in $\mathfrak{so}(3)$. $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and the intersection is a three-dimensional subgroup of a six-dimensional automorphism group: there is no room.

6.4 A Hopf fibration the corpus has not used. $\mathrm{SU}(4)/\mathrm{SU}(3)\cong S^{7}$ and $\mathrm{SU}(4)/S(\mathrm{U}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1))\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}$. These are the corpus's own quotients, and they say that the colour group is a sphere bundle over $\mathbb{CP}^{3}$. Whether that fibration can be written as an algebraic operation is the open question this article leaves, and it is a better question than "can the algebra host eight generators".

Summary

Three walls, one of which moves. The dimension wall moves, because fifteen is bigger than eight. The carrier wall does not, and its address changes: it is no longer $\mathfrak{su}(3)\not\subset\mathfrak{u}(2)$ but $\mathfrak{su}(4)\cap\operatorname{Comm}L(\mathbb H)=\mathfrak{su}(2)$, a ceiling of three that no enlargement of the group raises, and $\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap R(\mathbb B)=0$ is why colour is not that commutant. The chiral wall does not, because chirality belongs to the module.

What moves. Colour becomes a stabiliser instead of a subalgebra, so the existence question is settled; the octet becomes the adjoint of the stabiliser, so the gluon is inside the carrier's group.

What does not. The algebra still does not multiply by colour; the copy is permitted by the split and by nothing else; the sixteen is still declared chiral.

The verdict. The migration removes one of three obstructions and says which one. A migration that claimed all three would be false; this one is worth doing because the one it removes is the one that made the frame impossible.

The traps. Do not read "the colour wall is gone" into any sentence of this note; one of three walls moved — §1. Do not read the copy as a carrier: it inherits the split and not the action, and the difference is the whole of §2. Do not read inside the carrier's group as inside the algebra; the rename of §12.6 is worded to make that mistake impossible — §4.3. Do not read the new test as a way of passing the old one; the frame does not contain $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, it addresses it, and the old question stays answered no — §1.

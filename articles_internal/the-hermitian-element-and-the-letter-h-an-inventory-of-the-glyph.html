The Hermitian Element and the Letter H: An Inventory of the Glyph

Introduction

The element $H = h_te_t + i\vec h$ is the general Hermitian element of the biquaternion algebra. It is the informational half $\tilde R_\psi$ of one position, written in the letters of an operator. It appears in fifty-seven physics articles, where the literal $h_t$ is printed. It has two spellings, both of them in the conventions. It has no name of its own, and it is sometimes called quantum H in conversation.

This article gives it a name, inventories the fifty-seven uses, and quotes the expression that each article prints. The name it recommends is the informational element, written $\tilde R_\psi$, with $H$ reserved for the operator reading. The informal name quantum H is rejected, and §The Name gives the reasons.

What the article establishes. The definition and the two spellings, with the four numbers they carry. The three readings that the corpus gives to those numbers, and the fact that the corpus does not arbitrate between them. Seven tables that hold all fifty-seven articles, each with the expression it prints, quoted verbatim from the corpus after the migration. The counts, recomputed. Five variants and defects, one of them retired by the settlement. And the naming rule, with the four candidates it rejects.

What it does not claim. It does not claim that the element is new, and it does not claim that any of the fifty-seven uses is wrong, except where §The Variants and the Defects says so. It does not settle which reading of the four slots governs, because the corpus does not settle it. It adds no physics.

How the inventory is organised. An article appears in exactly one table, the table of its principal use. Each table names its own criterion. The rows are read with that criterion in mind: an article that defines the element and also uses it appears once, under the definition.

How the quotes are written. Every expression in the third column is quoted from its article, not paraphrased: it is a substring of the file, with spacing collapsed. The one exception is the absolute-value bar, which is written \lvert and \rvert here, because a bare | closes a table cell. The two render identically, and every row was checked against its file after the migration.

The Definition and the Two Spellings

The definition sits in one place. Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Hermitian and Anti-Hermitian Subspaces, fixes the element as the informational part of a general biquaternion, and then renames the four coefficients in the letters of an operator:

$$ H = h_te_t + ih_x\vec e_x + ih_y\vec e_y + ih_z\vec e_z, \qquad h_t, h_x, h_y, h_z \in \mathbb{R}, $$

and, with the vector part assembled,

$$ H = h_te_t + i\vec h , \qquad \vec h = h_x\vec e_x + h_y\vec e_y + h_z\vec e_z , $$

with the bridge $h_t = c r_t$ and $h_k = r'_k$ to the coordinates of $\tilde R_\psi$. The two lines are one element. The only difference is the distribution of the central $i$: the first writes it on each of the three vector terms, the second factors it out of their sum. Nothing else changes, and the algebra of the element is the same in both.

Two spellings, one object. A reader who meets one spelling in an article and the other in its companion needs no translation. The four real coefficients are the four numbers, and the central $i$ multiplies the vector part.

The tilde is not carried. An earlier version of the conventions required it, on the ground that $H$ without a tilde is the Hamiltonian. The settlement removed the requirement, because the ground was the wrong one: $H$ here is not a second element that must be kept apart from $\tilde R_\psi$. It is $\tilde R_\psi$, read as an energy, and a tilde would announce a distinction the algebra does not have. What the corpus keeps is the other half of the old rule, which is true and is needed: a bare $h$ without a subscript remains the Planck constant.

The image. Under the $2\times2$ representation ($\mathsf M_2(e_t)=I_2$, $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_j)=-i\sigma_j$) the element is the general Hermitian matrix,

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde R_\psi) = h_tI_2 + h_x\sigma_1 + h_y\sigma_2 + h_z\sigma_3 , $$

which is the boxed identity of AI Physics Review and of The Native Qubit and the Defining Module of the Biquaternion Algebra. The image is what makes the element the general observable of a two-level system, and it is the object that most of the tables below compute with.

The Three Readings of the Four Slots

The same four slots carry three different physical readings in the corpus, and the corpus does not give a dictionary between them.

The coordinate reading. In the conventions the slots are the informational coordinate: $h_t=c r_t$ and $h_k=r'_k$, so that the element is $c r_t\,e_t+i(r'_x\vec e_x+r'_y\vec e_y+r'_z\vec e_z)$. Here $h_t$ is a time and $\vec h$ a position.

The quantum reading. In the quantum articles the slots are an energy and a spin polarization. The spectrum $h_t\pm\lvert\vec h\rvert$ is read as two energy levels, $h_t$ as the trace part of the Hamiltonian and $\vec h$ as the splitting vector. Quantum Thermodynamics in Biquaternionic Form writes the partition function $Z=2\exp(-\beta h_t)\cosh(\beta\lvert\vec h\rvert)$ with exactly that reading. The Zeeman and Stark Effects in Biquaternionic Form writes the magnetic coupling as $\tilde R_{\psi,Z}=-\tfrac{\hbar\omega_L}{2}i\vec e_z$, which is the same element with $h_t=0$.

The image reading. In The Bloch Ball as the Normalised Slice of the Future Light Cone the slots are the image of a material four-vector under $-\tfrac{i}{2}$: the same element carries $h_t=\tfrac12ct'$ and $\vec h=-\tfrac12(x\vec e_x+y\vec e_y+z\vec e_z)$.

The gap. The first reading says that $h_t$ is the length $c r_t$. The second says that $h_t$ is an energy. The third says that $h_t$ is half the length $ct'$, read from the other sector. The three cannot all be the same quantity unless a unit system is fixed, and no article fixes one. No article states the dictionary that would reconcile them, and no article claims that one reading excludes the others. This is the sharpest open point about the element, and it is recorded here rather than repaired: a repair would be a physical claim, and this article makes none.

The objection, and the answer. The objection is that a name should say which of the three readings is meant. The answer is that the algebra fixes the linear structure only, and each article supplies the reading in its own sentences. A name that carried one reading would be false in most of the tables.

The Variants and the Defects

Five entries are recorded here. One is retired by the settlement, one is a blemish of labelling, one is a tolerance, and two are reuses of a letter. Each was rechecked against the corpus on 2026-11-05.

D1. The general element without a tilde. Retired, not repaired. An earlier version of this article counted Exercise: Chirality and the Weyl Spinors as a misuse, because it writes $H=h_te_t+i\vec h$ and the conventions then required the tilde. The conventions no longer require it: $H$ is the operator reading of $\tilde R_\psi$, and the untilded form is the correct spelling of that reading. The article is right and the objection is withdrawn. Nothing in the corpus was changed for this entry, and nothing needs to be.

D2. A temporal index on a spatial sum. Spin-1/2 Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form prints the summary row $\tilde R_\psi = h_t e_t + ih_\mu \vec e_\mu$ (line 416). The index runs over the three spatial units, since the scalar term is written separately, while the corpus uses $\mu$ for the four slots, as in $Q_\mu$ and in the same article's $\partial/\partial Q_\mu$. The reading is unambiguous from the sum, and the row would read $ih_k\vec e_k$ to match. It is a blemish of labelling, not of content, and it survives the migration because the migration rewrote the left-hand side and left the index alone.

D3. An operator where a real number is declared. Three articles put a differential operator in the scalar slot: $h_t=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2$ in The Free Particle and Wave Packets, $h_t=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2-\frac{\kappa}{r}$ in The Hydrogen Atom, Non-Relativistic Case, and $h_t=h_t(x)$ in One-Dimensional Scattering. The declared domain $h_t\in\mathbb{R}$ is then false. The usage works because the operator is central: it commutes with every biquaternion, so every algebraic identity of the four numbers survives the substitution. The corpus does not say this anywhere, and the three articles rely on it. This entry stands unchanged.

Summary

The element is $\tilde R_\psi=h_te_t+i\vec h$ with $h_t\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\vec h\in\mathbb{R}^3$, the general element of $\mathbb{M}_+$, and $H=h_te_t+i\vec h$ is the same element in the operator reading. It is written in two spellings, and they differ only in where the central $i$ sits.

Its four numbers fix the trace $2h_t$, the spectrum $h_t\pm\lvert\vec h\rvert$, the determinant $h_t^2-\lvert\vec h\rvert^2$, the positivity $h_t\geq\lvert\vec h\rvert$, and the Born weight $h_t+\vec r\cdot\vec h$. Every one of these was recomputed on sixty random quadruples with residuals at the rounding level.

Fifty-seven physics articles print it. They fall into seven groups: five define it, eleven use it as an observable, eleven as a generator, ten as a norm or a positivity test, six as a spectrum, six in the central case, and eight print it once.

The four slots carry three readings in the corpus, the coordinate reading, the energy-and-spin reading, and the image of a material four-vector. The corpus gives no dictionary between them, and this article records the gap instead of repairing it.

The name to use is the informational element $\tilde R_\psi$, with the role named in the sentence and $H$ reserved for the operator reading. The tilde is not carried on $H$, because the two are one element. Quantum H is rejected, and the letter $h$ cannot carry the name, because it already means the Planck constant, the metric trace, the areal constant and a real quaternion.

The Object

$\mathbb{B}$ has eight real dimensions, so a point of it carries eight real numbers. In four flat slots with complex values, the general point is

$$ \tilde R=(r_t+i r'_{t})\,e_t+(r_x+i r'_{x})\,\vec e_x+(r_y+i r'_{y})\,\vec e_y+(r_z+i r'_{z})\,\vec e_z , $$

$$ \tilde{R}_{\psi}=r_t\,e_t+i\bigl(r'_{x}\vec e_x+r'_{y}\vec e_y+r'_{z}\vec e_z\bigr)\in\mathbb{M}_+ $$

$$ \tilde{R}_{\varphi}=i\,r'_{t}\,e_t+r_x\vec e_x+r_y\vec e_y+r_z\vec e_z\in\mathbb{M}_- $$

Each complex slot carries one real coordinate from each sector. Writing $\vec{r}_{\psi}=(r'_{x},r'_{y},r'_{z})$ and $\vec{r}_{\varphi}=(r_x,r_y,r_z)$, the two halves are

$$ \tilde{R}_{\psi}=r_t\,e_t+i\,\vec{r}_{\psi}\!\cdot\vec e $$

$$ \tilde{R}_{\varphi}=i\,r'_{t}\,e_t+\vec{r}_{\varphi}\!\cdot\vec e $$

The prime marks the space of the other half. In the time slot the unprimed coordinate is informational and the primed one material; in the three space slots it is the other way round. The asymmetry is not a slip. It is the signature that the two halves carry with opposite signs, and no assignment of primes to slots avoids it.

What the Scheme Gives

Seven results follow from the definitions alone. Each was recomputed on forty random real octuples, and each residual was at the rounding level.

1. The halves are the real and the imaginary part, and the star is the reflection. The Hermitian conjugation satisfies $\tilde{R}_{\psi}^{*}=\tilde{R}_{\psi}$ and $\tilde{R}_{\varphi}^{*}=-\tilde{R}_{\varphi}$, so the point and its conjugate give

$$ \tilde R^{*}=\tilde{R}_{\psi}-\tilde{R}_{\varphi}, \qquad \tilde{R}_{\psi}=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde R+\tilde R^{*}\bigr), \qquad \tilde{R}_{\varphi}=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde R-\tilde R^{*}\bigr). $$

The star is therefore a reflection in the material half, and the two coordinates are its eigenspaces. No sentence is needed to say which part is which; the conjugation says it.

2. The matrix image is all of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. The eight images of the eight real parameters — $I_2$, $iI_2$, the three $\sigma_k$ and the three $-i\sigma_k$ — are a real basis of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. So $\tilde R$ is the general complex $2\times2$ matrix, $\tilde{R}_{\psi}$ its Hermitian part and $\tilde{R}_{\varphi}$ its anti-Hermitian part. The corpus's image of an observable is the Hermitian half of a general matrix, which is a standard fact that the single symbol now makes visible.

3. The determinant is the biquaternion norm. With the complex slots $Q_t=r_t+i r'_{t}$ and $Q_k=r_k+i r'_{k}$, the image satisfies $\det\mathsf M_2(\tilde R)=Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2$, which is $\langle\tilde R\rangle_\natural$. On the two halves it splits into two real Minkowski forms of opposite sign.

The prime stays where the conventions put it. Order, Effort and Rollback

Order. P0, then P1 and P2, because the subscripts must be free before the conventions claim them. P3 and P4 next, because the material half is independent of the open point. P5 before P6, because the dictionary decides what $\tilde{R}_{\psi}$ means. P7 last.

Effort. P1 is six strings. P3 is 849 edits in 80 files and takes one session with a script and a rendering check. P6 is 90 edits in 41 files, and it is scriptable once the definition is the mark. P7 is the judgement pass over 126 files, and it is the long pole: about fifteen minutes a file, because the 30 operator shapes are the only easy ones. P4 is prose in 35 files. P5 is one deleted paragraph and one sentence.

Rollback. One commit per pass. P1, P3, P5 and P6 reverse by the inverse substitution, which is exact because the target strings do not occur in the corpus except where the migration put them. P7 does not reverse by substitution, so it is committed per article, and a bad article is restored from the tag. The tag is the only thing that must be made before the first edit.

The Hermitian Element and the Letter H: An Inventory of the Glyph

Introduction

The element $\tilde H = h_te_t + i\vec h$ is the general Hermitian element of the biquaternion algebra. It appears in fifty-seven physics articles, where the literal $h_t$ is printed. It has two spellings, both of them in the conventions. It has no name of its own, and it is sometimes called quantum H in conversation.

This article gives it a name, inventories the fifty-seven uses, and records the expression that each article prints. The name it recommends is the Hermitian element, with the tilde always written. The informal name quantum H is rejected, and §The Name gives the reasons.

What the article establishes. The definition and the two spellings, with the four numbers they carry. The three readings that the corpus gives to those numbers, and the fact that the corpus does not arbitrate between them. Seven tables that hold all fifty-seven articles, each with the expression it prints. The counts, recomputed. Five variants and defects, with the repair of the one that is a misuse of the convention. And the naming rule, with the four candidates it rejects.

What it does not claim. It does not claim that the element is new, and it does not claim that any of the fifty-seven uses is wrong, except where §The Variants and the Defects says so. It does not settle which reading of the four slots governs, because the corpus does not settle it. It adds no physics.

How the inventory is organised. An article appears in exactly one table, the table of its principal use. Each table names its own criterion. The rows are read with that criterion in mind: an article that defines the element and also uses it appears once, under the definition.

The Definition and the Two Spellings

The definition sits in one place. Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Hermitian and Anti-Hermitian Subspaces, fixes the element as the informational part of a general biquaternion, and then renames the four coefficients in the letters of an operator:

$$ \tilde Q_{\mathrm H} = \tilde{H} = h_te_t + ih_x\vec e_x + ih_y\vec e_y + ih_z\vec e_z, \qquad h_t, h_x, h_y, h_z \in \mathbb{R}, $$

and, with the vector part assembled,

$$ \tilde Q_{\mathrm H} = \tilde{H} = h_te_t + i\vec h , \qquad \vec h = h_x\vec e_x + h_y\vec e_y + h_z\vec e_z . $$

The two lines are one element. The only difference is the distribution of the central $i$: the first writes it on each of the three vector terms, the second factors it out of their sum. Nothing else changes, and the algebra of the element is the same in both.

Two spellings, one object. A reader who meets one spelling in an article and the other in its companion needs no translation. The four real coefficients are the four numbers, and the central $i$ multiplies the vector part.

The same conventions section states the naming rule, and it is the sentence that decides the question of this article:

The tilde is required, because $H$ without it is the Hamiltonian, and $h$ without a subscript is the Planck constant.

The corpus therefore already separates the two objects, and it separates them by the tilde, not by a qualifier.

The image. Under the $2\times2$ representation ($\mathsf M_2(e_t)=I_2$, $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_j)=-i\sigma_j$) the element is the general Hermitian matrix,

$$ \mathsf M_2(\tilde H) = h_tI_2 + h_x\sigma_1 + h_y\sigma_2 + h_z\sigma_3 , $$

which is the boxed identity of AI Physics Review and of The Native Qubit and the Defining Module of the Biquaternion Algebra. The image is what makes the element the general observable of a two-level system, and it is the object that most of the tables below compute with.

The Three Readings of the Four Slots

The same four slots carry three different physical readings in the corpus, and the corpus does not give a dictionary between them.

The coordinate reading. In the conventions the slots are the informational coordinate: $h_t=ct$ and $h_k=x'_k$, so that the element is $ct\,e_t+i(x'\vec e_x+y'\vec e_y+z'\vec e_z)$. Here $h_t$ is a time and $\vec h$ a position.

The quantum reading. In the quantum articles the slots are an energy and a spin polarization. The spectrum $h_t\pm\lvert\vec h\rvert$ is read as two energy levels, $h_t$ as the trace part of the Hamiltonian and $\vec h$ as the splitting vector. Quantum Thermodynamics in Biquaternionic Form writes the partition function $Z=2\exp(-\beta h_t)\cosh(\beta\lvert\vec h\rvert)$ with exactly that reading. The Zeeman and Stark Effects in Biquaternionic Form writes the magnetic coupling as $\tilde H_Z=-\tfrac{\hbar\omega_L}{2}i\vec e_z$, which is the same element with $h_t=0$.

The image reading. In The Bloch Ball as the Normalised Slice of the Future Light Cone the slots are the image of a material four-vector under $-\tfrac{i}{2}$: the same element carries $h_t=\tfrac12ct'$ and $\vec h=-\tfrac12(x\vec e_x+y\vec e_y+z\vec e_z)$.

The gap. The first reading says that $h_t$ is a time. The second says that $h_t$ is an energy. The third says that $h_t$ is half a time, from the other sector. No article states the dictionary that would reconcile them, and no article claims that one reading excludes the others. This is the sharpest open point about the element, and it is recorded here rather than repaired: a repair would be a physical claim, and this article makes none.

The objection, and the answer. The objection is that a name should say which of the three readings is meant. The answer is that the algebra fixes the linear structure only, and each article supplies the reading in its own sentences. A name that carried one reading would be false in most of the tables.

The Variants and the Defects

Five things are recorded here. One is a misuse of the convention, two are tolerances, and two are reuses of a letter.

D1. The general element without the tilde. Exercise: Chirality and the Weyl Spinors writes "Write $H = h_te_t + i\vec h$" and then computes its norm. The element has a vector part, so it is not the Hamiltonian, and the conventions require the tilde. The repair is one character: $H$ becomes $\tilde H$ in that sentence. The other three untilded occurrences of the shape $H=h_te_t$ are correct, because they are the Hamiltonian: The Free Particle and Wave Packets twice, and The Hydrogen Atom, Non-Relativistic Case once.

D2. A temporal index on a spatial sum. Spin-1/2 Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form prints the summary row $\tilde H=h_te_t+ih_\mu\vec e_\mu$. The index runs over the three spatial units, since the scalar term is written separately, while the corpus uses $\mu$ for the four slots, as in $Q_\mu$ and in the same article's $\partial/\partial Q_\mu$. The reading is unambiguous from the sum, and the row would read $ih_k\vec e_k$ to match. This is a blemish of labelling, not of content.

D3. An operator where a real number is declared. Three articles put a differential operator in the scalar slot: $h_t=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2$ in The Free Particle and Wave Packets, $h_t=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2-\frac{\kappa}{r}$ in The Hydrogen Atom, Non-Relativistic Case, and $h_t=h_t(x)$ in One-Dimensional Scattering. The declared domain $h_t\in\mathbb{R}$ is then false. The usage works because the operator is central: it commutes with every biquaternion, so every algebraic identity of the four numbers survives the substitution. The corpus does not say this anywhere, and the three articles rely on it.

D4. The letter reused with a subscript. Four articles write $\tilde H_{\mathrm{pos},l}$ for the positive factor of a Kraus operator, a different object that happens to be Hermitian. The subscript separates it, and no confusion arises. It is recorded because the glyph is the same and an inventory should say so.

D5. The letter $h$ is overloaded four times. It is the Planck constant in the conventions' own sentence. It is the trace of the metric perturbation, $h=g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$, in Linearized Gravity in Biquaternionic Form and Gravitational Waves in Biquaternionic Form. It is the areal constant, $h=L/m$, in Central Forces and the Classical Kepler Problem. And it is a generic real quaternion in the six mathematics articles of §The Counts. The four overloads are the reason the name of this article's subject cannot be built on the letter $h$.

The Name

The type name. The element is the Hermitian element of the biquaternion algebra, and that name is true in every row of the seven tables. The symbol is $\tilde H$. When the coordinate reading is meant, the corpus also writes $\tilde Q_{\mathrm H}$, and the two symbols denote the same element under two readings of the slots.

Adopted for the project on 2026-11-05, and not yet applied. The symbol $\tilde H$ retires, and the element is written $\tilde{R}_H$, the informational half of one position $\tilde R$. The name the Hermitian element survives and the single-letter symbol does not. Nothing in the corpus has changed yet. §The Project Migration records it and its cost, and the tables above describe the corpus as it still stands, with $\tilde H$ and $\tilde Q_{\mathrm H}$ both printed.

The role names must be earned by the use. The corpus already names the roles in its sentences, and the names are not interchangeable. The element is the Hamiltonian when it generates a flow by $\exp(-i\tilde Ht'/\hbar)$. It is an observable when it is paired with a state by the Hermitian form. It is the central Hamiltonian when $\vec h=0$. It is a coupling when it is added to a Hamiltonian, as in the Zeeman and Stark article. The letters of the neighbouring roles are already taken: states are $\tilde\varrho$, effects are $\tilde E$, idempotents are $\tilde\Pi$.

The coefficient names. The corpus has settled these. $h_t$ is the scalar coefficient, and $h_te_t$ is the trace part; $\vec h$ is the vector part, and $i\vec h$ is the traceless part; $h_x,h_y,h_z$ are the vector coefficients. When the splitting direction matters, the corpus writes $\vec u_h=\vec h/\lvert\vec h\rvert$ and calls it the axis.

The four candidates rejected.

Quantum H. The qualifier does not separate the two objects, because the Hamiltonian is quantum as well. The element is $\tilde H=h_te_t+i\vec h$ with $h_t\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\vec h\in\mathbb{R}^3$, the general element of $\mathbb{M}_+$. It is written in two spellings, and they differ only in where the central $i$ sits.

Fifty-seven physics articles print it. They fall into seven groups: five define it, eleven use it as an observable, eleven as a generator, ten as a norm or a positivity test, six as a spectrum, six in the central case, and eight print it once.

The four slots carry three readings in the corpus, the coordinate reading, the energy-and-spin reading, and the image of a material four-vector. The corpus gives no dictionary between them, and this article records the gap instead of repairing it.

The name to use is the Hermitian element $\tilde H$, with the role named in the sentence. Quantum H is rejected, and the letter $h$ cannot carry the name, because it already means the Planck constant, the metric trace, the areal constant and a real quaternion.

The migration in one line. One symbol for position, $\tilde R$, read as the position in the biquaternion space. Its two halves are written $\tilde{R}_H$ and $\tilde{R}_A$. $\tilde Q_{\mathrm H}$ and $\tilde Q_{\mathrm A}$ are not introduced, and $\tilde H$ retires.

One letter, eight subscripts. The position is written with $r$ and a subscript that names the coordinate, so a slot is $r_u+i r_{u'}$ and never $u+iu'$ with the axis letter standing alone.

The Object

$\mathbb{B}$ has eight real dimensions, so a point of it carries eight real numbers. In four flat slots with complex values, the general point is

$$ \tilde R=(r_t+i r_{t'})\,e_t+(r_x+i r_{x'})\,\vec e_x+(r_y+i r_{y'})\,\vec e_y+(r_z+i r_{z'})\,\vec e_z , $$

and its Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts are the two coordinates:

$$ \tilde{R}_H=r_t\,e_t+i\bigl(r_{x'}\vec e_x+r_{y'}\vec e_y+r_{z'}\vec e_z\bigr)\in\mathbb{M}_+ $$

$$ \tilde{R}_A=i\,r_{t'}\,e_t+r_x\vec e_x+r_y\vec e_y+r_z\vec e_z\in\mathbb{M}_- $$

Each complex slot carries one real coordinate from each sector. Writing $\vec{r}_H=(r_{x'},r_{y'},r_{z'})$ and $\vec{r}_A=(r_x,r_y,r_z)$, the two halves are

$$ \tilde{R}_H=r_t\,e_t+i\,\vec{r}_H\!\cdot\vec e $$

$$ \tilde{R}_A=i\,r_{t'}\,e_t+\vec{r}_A\!\cdot\vec e $$

The prime marks the space of the other half. In the time slot the unprimed coordinate is informational and the primed one material; in the three space slots it is the other way round. The asymmetry is not a slip. It is the signature that the two halves carry with opposite signs, and no assignment of primes to slots avoids it.

What the Scheme Gives

Seven results follow from the definitions alone. Each was recomputed on forty random real octuples, and each residual was at the rounding level.

1. The halves are the real and the imaginary part, and the star is the reflection. The Hermitian conjugation satisfies $\tilde{R}_H^{*}=\tilde{R}_H$ and $\tilde{R}_A^{*}=-\tilde{R}_A$, so the point and its conjugate give

$$ \tilde R^{*}=\tilde{R}_H-\tilde{R}_A, \qquad \tilde{R}_H=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde R+\tilde R^{*}\bigr), \qquad \tilde{R}_A=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde R-\tilde R^{*}\bigr). $$

The star is therefore a reflection in the material half, and the two coordinates are its eigenspaces. No sentence is needed to say which part is which; the conjugation says it.

2. The matrix image is all of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. The eight images of the eight real parameters — $I_2$, $iI_2$, the three $\sigma_k$ and the three $-i\sigma_k$ — are a real basis of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. So $\tilde R$ is the general complex $2\times2$ matrix, $\tilde{R}_H$ its Hermitian part and $\tilde{R}_A$ its anti-Hermitian part. The corpus's image of an observable is the Hermitian half of a general matrix, which is a standard fact that the single symbol now makes visible.

3. The determinant is the biquaternion norm. With the complex slots $Q_t=r_t+i r_{t'}$ and $Q_k=r_k+i r_{k'}$, the image satisfies $\det\mathsf M_2(\tilde R)=Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2$, which is $\langle\tilde R\rangle_\natural$. On the two halves it splits into two real Minkowski forms of opposite sign.

4. Each half carries a Minkowski form, with opposite signs.

half biquaternion norm what it is
$\tilde{R}_H=r_t e_t+i\vec{r}_H$ $\langle\tilde{R}_H\rangle_\natural=r_t^2-\lvert\vec{r}_H\rvert^2$ the interval of the informational half, signature $(1,3)$ on $(r_t,\vec{r}_H)$
$\tilde{R}_A=i r_{t'}e_t+\vec{r}_A$ $\langle\tilde{R}_A\rangle_\natural=-r_{t'}^2+\lvert\vec{r}_A\rvert^2$ the interval of the material half, the negative of the same form

The last two rows are the reason the scheme is worth having. The norm of a position is complex, and its imaginary part is twice the Euclidean pairing of the two four-vectors. A form that is real on each half is not real on the whole, and the whole is where the two sectors meet.

5. The two halves pair by the Minkowski product. The scalar part of the mixed product is

$$ \mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{R}_A\tilde{R}_H\bigr)=i\bigl(r_t r_{t'}-\vec{r}_A\cdot\vec{r}_H\bigr), $$

which is $i$ times the interval between $(r_t,\vec{r}_A)$ and $(r_{t'},\vec{r}_H)$. The pairing of the two coordinates is therefore the Minkowski inner product and not a norm, and it surfaces as a scalar part. The corpus states the same identity for an observable and a material element, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde R\tilde H)=i(r_th_{t'}-(\vec v,\vec h))$, and the scheme supplies the sentence that explains it.

6. Multiplication by $i$ turns one half into the other. $i\tilde{R}_A=-r_{t'}e_t+i\vec{r}_A\in\mathbb{M}_+$ and $i\tilde{R}_H=i r_te_t-\vec{r}_H\in\mathbb{M}_-$. On the slots the map is a quarter turn, $(r_t,r_{t'})\mapsto(-r_{t'},r_t)$ and $(r_k,r_{k'})\mapsto(-r_{k'},r_k)$. The exchange of sectors is therefore the rotation that the phase of a wavefunction generates.

7. The idempotents are null positions. $\det\mathsf M_2(\tilde R)=0$ exactly when the image has rank one. The normalised rank-one idempotents are $\tilde\Pi_{1,2}(\vec u)=\tfrac12(e_t\pm i\vec u)$ with $\lvert\vec u\rvert=1$, which are $\tilde{R}_H$ at $r_t=\tfrac12$ and $\vec{r}_H=\pm\tfrac12\vec u$. The pure states are therefore the null elements of the informational half, and the Bloch ball is the normalised slice of their cone, as The Bloch Ball as the Normalised Slice of the Future Light Cone already states.

Two Consequences for the Corpus

The old $\tilde R$ survives as a slice. Today $\tilde R$ is the material coordinate. Under the scheme it is $\tilde{R}_A$, which is the case $\tilde{R}_H=0$, that is $r_t=0$ and $\vec{r}_H=0$. The old object is a special case of the new one, not a different object behind the same name. This is the strongest argument for the scheme: a reader with the old habit reads $\tilde R$ as a position and is right, whereas $\tilde Q$ would ask the same reader to learn a symbol that names a subspace and carries no position at all.

A point of $\mathbb{B}$ is not a point of spacetime. Eight real coordinates, not four. The two halves are two four-planes through the origin, and a world line picks one of them. The scheme does not merge the two spacetimes; it names the eight-dimensional space in which both sit.

Imaginary-Exponential Wave Forms in the Physics Corpus: A Complete Inventory

Introduction

The subject. Every article of articles_physics that writes a wave form with an imaginary exponential, and the form as that article writes it. The list is complete against the scan: 471 files, 236 of them containing at least one imaginary exponential, 236 rows in thirteen tables, plus one index of the articles that exponentiate $\Xi$, one section on the exponents that are not purely imaginary, and one on the expressions reviewed, three of which are now repaired in the corpus.

What a row is. The article's own expression, copied out of the file, and one line naming what sits in the exponent. Expressions with more than one representative form show two, separated by a comma. No expression is paraphrased, no term is moved, and no exponent is normalised to a common notation: the point of the list is that the same object appears in many spellings, and the spellings are the evidence.

How the rows were made. A machine pass collected every e^{...} and \exp(...) in the corpus, then every row's expression was checked as an exact substring of the article file it is attributed to. An expression that did not occur verbatim would have been dropped; none was. The links go to the built pages of articles_physics.

How to read the tables. Section by section, the classification is by what the exponent holds, not by the subject of the article. One article can therefore carry two forms of different kinds; it is filed under the one its main result uses. Articles whose only exponential is a global, mixing or transformation phase sit in the last table.

Two time variables appear. The retarded time $r'_t$ with the speed of light is the corpus convention: $\omega r'_t/c$, $\hbar c$ in the denominator, $r'_t/c$ as the interval. The older $t$ survives in the articles written before the convention settled, and both are quoted below as they stand.

The Field That Carries the Forms

215 of the 471 files write the field as $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$, in 7535 occurrences. The label $\mathrm{sp}$ is the spinor, and the notation article registers it as such. This is the object that most of the rows above multiply, translate, gauge or evolve: the exponential is the phase and $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is what the phase acts on. 124 of the 236 articles tabulated here carry it.

Three consequences for the tables above. First, a row that shows $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ in the exponent is exponentiating the field, and that is rare: eight articles do it, and they are collected in the index of the $\Xi$ section. Second, a row that shows $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ elsewhere in the display is exponentiating something else and acting on the field, which is the common case. Third, the subscript is never dropped, so a reader can tell at a glance whether a display puts the field inside the exponential or outside it.

2. The boost rows: a real $\cosh$ part

An $i$ at the front of an exponent decides nothing. The sign of the square of the argument decides. In this algebra a unit pure quaternion satisfies $\vec u^2=-e_t$, so

$$ X^2=-\alpha^2e_t\ \Longrightarrow\ \exp X=\cos\alpha\,e_t+\sin\alpha\,\vec u,\qquad X^2=+\alpha^2e_t\ \Longrightarrow\ \exp X=\cosh\alpha\,e_t+i\sinh\alpha\,\vec u . $$

What the register does not cover

The 18 articles whose exponent holds an operator keep a generator: $\tilde H_\Psi$, $\tilde S_{\Psi}$, $\vec F$, $\sigma_i$, $\tilde\Pi_-$, the Moyal operator, and the boost axis $\vec u$. Their angle is real and their form is $\exp(i\theta\,\hat G)$; they are listed in the audit section, not here, because their angle is already a plain rotation or evolution angle.

What Is Not in the List

Two exclusions, each deliberate, and one treatment that keeps a row in the list.

  • No imaginary exponential at all. 137 of the 471 files contain no exponential in either spelling, $\exp$ or $e^{\cdots}$, and 334 write at least one. Examples are the quantum stress tensor, the spin fluid, one-dimensional scattering, and gravitational waves, which are written with derivatives and pairings only. An article with no exponential has no wave form to tabulate.
  • One article states that it has no exponential. The Schrödinger Action Principle in Biquaternionic Form was in the global table until this pass, quoted as $e^{i\beta}$ and $e^{i\alpha}$. It writes neither. Its own symbol table lists $S_{\mathrm A}$ as "the action of this article, with no exponential", and its phase is $S=\hbar\alpha$. The file is also truncated: it begins mid-table with a run of $\tilde\Xi$ and no opening delimiter, and the body of the article is absent. The row is removed, and the article is counted with the 137 that carry no exponential.
  • A real exponential only. 46 files write a real exponential and no phase, in 235 sites. Four families carry it: the Gibbs weight $\exp(-\beta H)$, the modular weight $\exp(-2\pi K)$, the real decay $\exp(-m\tau)$ or $\exp(-\Gamma r'_t)$, and the real part of the complex action $\exp(-\kappa_{\mathrm{act}})$ with the Euclidean $\exp(-S_E)$. Section 1 of Exponents That Are Not Purely Imaginary lists all 58 file rows and quotes each form. The replica article is one of them.
  • A boost, whose exponential has a real $\cosh$. Nine articles put $i$ times a real multiple of a unit pure quaternion in the exponent. The argument squares positive, so the exponential is $\cosh\alpha\,e_t+i\sinh\alpha\,\vec u$: a real scalar part that grows, with the $i$ on the bivector term alone. Three of them write nothing else, and that is why they are not in the thirteen tables. All nine are quoted in the section on exponents that are not purely imaginary.
  • A real damping beside an imaginary phase, kept and split. Some articles write one exponential whose exponent carries both parts. The audit separates it into one exponential per component, the imaginary factor first and the real factor just after, so no quoted exponential mixes the two. The superluminal case is written $\exp(-k[a_0-i(r_z\cos\theta_{\mathrm{axc}}-r'_t)])$ in the article and quoted as $\exp(ik(r_z\cos\theta_{\mathrm{axc}}-r'_t))$, $\exp(-k a_0)$; the twistor kernel is written $\exp(\pm i\tau\varpi - i(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z,\vec\zeta))$ and quoted as two factors.

A fourth class is included rather than excluded: articles where the exponential multiplies a state and cancels from every bilinear. Their phases are still written in the exponent, and the last table keeps them visible.

Summary of Notation

  • $\alpha$ is the central angle of a biquaternion, the phase that multiplies the identity.
  • $S$ is an action, $S_{\tilde\Xi}$ the action of the field $\tilde\Xi$, and $\hbar$ the reduced constant.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ is a rapidity, always halved in the rotor, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2$.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ is a phase in radians, and $\theta_{\mathrm{azm}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{stk}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{axc}}$ are the azimuthal, Stokes and axial angles.
  • $\Gamma$ is the gauge function, $ϙ$ the charge, and $\exp(i\varrho\Gamma/\hbar)$ the gauge element.
  • $\tilde H_\Psi$ is the Hamiltonian biquaternion, $\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{modular}}$ its modular form, and $r'_t$ the retarded time.
  • $k_\Phi$ is the wave number of the phase, $k_m$ the mass wave number $mc/\hbar$, and $\omega$ an angular frequency.
  • $\Xi$ is an invariant phase, $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ the phase of a spinor field, and $Ϙ$ a winding number.
  • $\beta$ is the inverse temperature, left bare, and $\omega_n$ a Matsubara frequency.
  • $r$, $\rho$ are scales, and $\mathrm{Sc}$ takes the scalar part of a product of two biquaternions.

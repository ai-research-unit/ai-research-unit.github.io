Imaginary-Exponential Wave Forms in the Physics Corpus: The Exponents That Are Not Real Numbers

Summary

This file holds the wave forms whose exponent is not a real number. Fourteen articles of articles_physics write an imaginary exponential whose exponent is an operator, a differential operator, a projector or an element of the algebra. No real angle replaces those, so the row stays here. Every other row of the inventory has been patched and moved to Wave Forms Already in the Patched Form: seventy-six rows, moved whole, with the line quoted verbatim.

The inventory was reviewed article by article in this pass. Six exponents were renamed to a single real angle and moved out with the rest. Nine statements that were false or inconsistent as written were corrected in the articles, six of them dimensional or structural. The list is in Part 1.

How a row is read

A row has five cells: a number, the article, the exponential as written, one phrase naming what the exponent carries, and what a single subscript would need. The fourth cell names the quantity the exponent holds. The fifth says why no subscript finishes the job.

The rule of the file is one sentence: a row stays only while its exponent is not a real number.

What the exponent is Counts as a number Example
a named angle yes $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}})$
a sum or difference of named angles yes $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{act}}+i\theta_{\mathrm{src}})$
a named angle beside a named real rate yes, one factor each $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{spa}})\exp(-\kappa_{\mathrm{dmp}})$
a fixed turn, or a fixed multiple of an angle yes $\exp(i\pi/4)$, $\exp(i(2-k)\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})$
an angle times an operator no $\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{rat}}\vec u\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma/2)$
an element of the algebra no $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{bqc}}\hat u_{\tilde\Xi})$

The first four rows moved to the sister file. The last two stay. An operator in the exponent is a unitary, not a phase. An element in the exponent is a rotor or a boost, not a number.

Part 3 — The Angles

The corpus is written so that every exponent of a wave form is a real number built from named angles. This part is the register of those angles: one row per symbol, so that no subscript names two quantities and no article invents a symbol another article already uses.

The rule

An exponent is written $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}})$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}}$ is a real number defined once in the article, at its first occurrence, with the reason it is real. The subscript has three letters and names the quantity, not the article. Where the exponent holds an operator or an element, the angle multiplies the generator and the form is $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}}\hat G)$; the angle stays real and the generator carries the algebra.

An exponent may be a sum of such angles, a difference, or a fixed multiple. What it may not be is an unexamined dimensionful expression, which is why this pass corrected three source terms and one evolution operator.

What the register does not cover

The fourteen articles of this file keep a generator in the exponent: $\tilde H_\Psi$, $\tilde H_{\mathrm{modular}}$, $\tilde S_{\Psi}$, $H$, $\sigma_i$, $\tilde\Pi_-$, $Q$ and the Moyal operator. A generator is not an angle, and no subscript replaces it. The register lists the angle beside the generator.

Symbols Used Throughout

  • $\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}$ is the central angle of a biquaternion, the phase that multiplies the identity.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{rat}}$ is the rotation angle of a rotor; $\theta_{\mathrm{rte}}=\theta_{\mathrm{rat}}/2$ is the exponent angle of the rotor.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}$ is a real frequency times an interval over $c$; $\theta_{\mathrm{spp}}=\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}/2$ is the spinor half-angle.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{gld}}$ is a Goldstone field over the scale that breaks the symmetry: $\vartheta_{\mathrm{fld}}/v$ or $\vartheta_{\mathrm{fld}}/f$.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{src}}$ is a source pairing over $\hbar$.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{rst}}$ is the rest-energy phase, $k_mr'_t$; $\beta$ in its second form is the unit element $i\vec e_x$ of a right multiplication, not a speed.

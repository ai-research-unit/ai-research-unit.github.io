Imaginary-Exponential Wave Forms in the Physics Corpus: A Complete Inventory

Introduction

The subject. Every article of articles_physics that writes a wave form with an imaginary exponential, and the form as that article writes it. The list is complete against the scan: 495 entries, 272 of them containing at least one imaginary exponential, and 260 rows in fourteen tables, plus one index of six rows of the articles that exponentiate $\Xi$, one section on the exponents that are not purely imaginary, and one on the expressions reviewed, three of which are now repaired in the corpus.

What a row is. The article's own expression, copied out of the file, and one line naming what sits in the exponent. Expressions with more than one representative form show two, separated by a comma. No expression is paraphrased, no term is moved, and no exponent is normalised to a common notation: the point of the list is that the same object appears in many spellings, and the spellings are the evidence.

How the rows were made. A machine pass collected every e^{...} and \exp(...) in the corpus, then every row's expression was checked as an exact substring of the article file it is attributed to. An expression that did not occur verbatim was corrected rather than dropped: every row whose exponent the migration rewrote was rewritten to the subscripted text. One concession is forced by the table syntax: a norm bar is written $\vert$ in a cell, because a bare pipe ends the cell, and the article itself may write $|$ or $\|$ for the same delimiter. After the migration the pass was run again: of the quoted forms, one is not a substring of its article. It is the cell in Expressions Reviewed that quotes $r'_t/1/k_{m_e}$ as a malformed reading on purpose, and the row says so. The links point at the .md files, which are the files the corpus builds from.

How to read the tables. Section by section, the classification is by what the exponent holds, not by the subject of the article. One article can therefore carry two forms of different kinds; it is filed under the one its main result uses. Articles whose only exponential is a global, mixing or transformation phase sit in the last table.

The Field That Carries the Forms

215 of the articles write the field as $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$, in 7535 occurrences. The label $\mathrm{sp}$ is the spinor, and the notation article registers it as such. This is the object that most of the rows above multiply, translate, gauge or evolve: the exponential is the phase and $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is what the phase acts on. 124 of the 238 rows tabulated here carry it.

Three consequences for the tables above. First, a row that shows $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ in the exponent is exponentiating the field, and that is rare: eight articles do it, and they are collected in the index of the $\Xi$ section. Second, a row that shows $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ elsewhere in the display is exponentiating something else and acting on the field, which is the common case. Third, the subscript is never dropped, so a reader can tell at a glance whether a display puts the field inside the exponential or outside it.

2. The boost rows: a real $\cosh$ part

An $i$ at the front of an exponent decides nothing. The sign of the square of the argument decides. In this algebra a unit pure quaternion satisfies $\vec u^2=-e_t$, so

$$ X^2=-\alpha^2e_t\ \Longrightarrow\ \exp X=\cos\alpha\,e_t+\sin\alpha\,\vec u,\qquad X^2=+\alpha^2e_t\ \Longrightarrow\ \exp X=\cosh\alpha\,e_t+i\sinh\alpha\,\vec u . $$

The first is a turn: bounded, modulus one, no real growth. The second is a boost: a real scalar part of at least one, and the $i$ confined to the bivector term. Putting an $i$ in front of a real multiple of $\vec u$ flips the sign, so it converts the turn into the growth. An $i$ in front of a central element, or in front of a Hermitian operator, does not: there the exponential stays unitary, and those articles stay in the thirteen tables above. Only the eight boosts are listed here, because only they carry a real part.

The Angle Register

The corpus is being rewritten so that every exponent of the wave form is one real angle. This section is the register of those angles: one row per symbol, so no subscript is reused for two quantities and no article invents a symbol that another article already owns.

The rule

An exponent is written $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}})$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}}$ is a real number defined once in the article, at its first occurrence, with the reason it is real. The subscript has three letters and names the quantity, not the article.

Two qualifications. Where the exponent holds an operator, a matrix or a bivector, the angle multiplies the generator and the form is $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}}\hat G)$; the angle stays real and the generator carries the algebra. Where the angle is not real, the article must say so and split the exponent into an angle and a weight, $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}})\exp(-\kappa_{\mathrm{xxx}})$, with both symbols in this register. A split is not a licence: it is the statement that the exponent has a real part, and it must be earned by the value the article's own symbols take.

Summary of Notation

  • $\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}$ is the central angle of a biquaternion, the phase that multiplies the identity; the letter $\alpha$ is kept only where it names a complex wavenumber.
  • $S$ is an action, $S_{\tilde\Xi}$ the action of the field $\tilde\Xi$, and $\hbar$ the reduced constant.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ is a rapidity, always halved in the rotor, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2$.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ is a phase in radians, and $\theta_{\mathrm{azm}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{stk}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{axc}}$ are the azimuthal, Stokes and axial angles.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{rat}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{rst}}$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{fld}}$ are the rotation angle, the rest-energy phase and the angular mode of a field.
  • $\Gamma$ is the gauge function, $ϙ$ the charge, and $\exp(i\varrho\Gamma/\hbar)$ the gauge element.
  • $\tilde H_\Psi$ is the Hamiltonian biquaternion, $\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{modular}}$ its modular form, and $r'_t$ the retarded time.
  • $k_\Phi$ is the wave number of the phase, $k_m$ the mass wave number $mc/\hbar$, and $\omega$ an angular frequency.
  • $\Xi$ is an invariant phase, $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ the phase of a spinor field, and $Ϙ$ a winding number.
  • $\beta$ is the inverse temperature, left bare because it carries a dimension, and $\omega_n$ a Matsubara frequency.
  • $r$, $\rho$ are scales, and $\mathrm{Sc}$ takes the scalar part of a product of two biquaternions.

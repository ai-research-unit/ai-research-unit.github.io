Imaginary-Exponential Wave Forms in the Physics Corpus: A Complete Inventory

Summary

This file inventories the wave forms of articles_physics whose phase sits inside an imaginary exponential. A row gives one article, the exponential as that article writes it, and one phrase naming what the exponent carries. The categories record what the exponent holds, not what the article is about.

The inventory has a sister file. Wave Forms Already in the Patched Form holds every line whose exponential is already one real angle, $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}})$, quoted verbatim with its file and line. Those rows were moved out of this file and are not repeated here. What stays is the residue: exponents that are a fraction of an angle, a product, a bare frequency, an action over $\hbar$, or an element of the algebra.

Every expression in a table is copied out of the file it cites and is an exact substring of that file. One change is forced by the table syntax: a bar inside a row is written \vert, because a bare bar ends a cell. Nothing is paraphrased and no exponent is normalised, so the same object appears in several spellings, and the spellings are the evidence.

Rows are numbered inside their table, and each table closes with the number of articles it holds. The corpus-wide figures are in Part 4.

How a row is read

A row has five cells: a number, the article, the exponential as written, one phrase naming what the exponent carries, and what a single subscript would take. The fourth cell is sometimes empty, because the shape of the exponential already says what it holds; the fifth never is.

Six spellings carry the wave forms of the corpus. Each table quotes the spelling its own article uses.

Spelling What it holds Schematic example
a word form a scale, a central exponential, a boost and a rotor $r\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$
an exponential of a scalar the phase of an action or of a mode $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{act}})$, $\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pwp}})$
an exponential of a fraction the same phase, halved or divided $\exp\!\left(\frac{i\,S}{\hbar}\right)$, $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{rat}}/2)$

an exponential of an element the gauge element, the field itself $\exp(i\varrho\Gamma/\hbar)$, $\exp(i\tilde\Xi)$

The distinction that matters here lies between the second and the third row. An exponent that is one real angle is patched, and it belongs to the sister file. An exponent that is a fraction of an angle, a sum, a product or an element stays here.

The last cell says what a single subscript would take. Only two kinds of exponent resist one: an exponent that is complex, which needs the damping split off first, and an exponent that is an element of the algebra, which is not a real number at all. Every other row needs a definition and nothing more: name the quantity, and the exponent is $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}})$.

The counts are 42 rows that take a name, 9 that take a split, 13 that take no name, and 27 that are already angles. A name costs one definition sentence, so the large group is a task list, not a defect.

Part 1 — What the Tables Leave Out

Four rules decide what a table may quote. Three exclude an article, and the fourth splits one exponent into two cells. Each rule turns on what the exponent is, not on the subject of the article.

  • No exponential at all. 154 of the 511 entries write neither $\exp$ nor $e^{\cdots}$. An article with no exponential has no wave form to tabulate.
  • A real exponential only. 75 entries write a real exponential and no imaginary one. Four families carry it: the Gibbs weight $\exp(-\beta H)$, the modular weight $\exp(-2\pi K)$, the real decay $\exp(-m\tau)$ or $\exp(-\Gamma r'_t)$, and the real part of a complex action $\exp(-\kappa_{\mathrm{act}})$ beside its Euclidean form $\exp(-S_E)$.
  • A boost, whose argument squares positive. Nine articles put $i$ times a real multiple of a unit pure quaternion in the exponent. There the exponential is $\cosh\alpha\,e_t+i\sinh\alpha\,\vec u$: a real scalar part that grows, with the $i$ on the bivector term alone. Three of the nine write nothing else.
  • A damping beside a phase. When one exponential carries a real part and an imaginary part, the tables quote two factors, the imaginary one first. The superluminal case is written $\exp(-k[a_0-i(r_z\cos\theta_{\mathrm{axc}}-r'_t)])$ in its article and quoted as $\exp(ik(r_z\cos\theta_{\mathrm{axc}}-r'_t))$, $\exp(-k a_0)$.

One class is inside rather than outside. Articles whose exponential multiplies a state, a mixing matrix or a canonical transformation and cancels from every bilinear are kept: their phases are written in the exponent, and the last table holds them.

Part 3 — The Angles

The corpus is written so that every exponent of a wave form is one real angle. This part is the register of those angles: one row per symbol, so that no subscript names two quantities and no article invents a symbol another article already uses.

The rule

An exponent is written $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}})$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}}$ is a real number defined once in the article, at its first occurrence, with the reason it is real. The subscript has three letters and names the quantity, not the article.

Two qualifications. Where the exponent holds an operator, a matrix or a bivector, the angle multiplies the generator and the form is $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}}\hat G)$; the angle stays real and the generator carries the algebra. Where the angle is not real, the article must say so and split the exponent into an angle and a weight, $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{xxx}})\exp(-\kappa_{\mathrm{xxx}})$, with both symbols in this register. A split is not a licence: it is the statement that the exponent has a real part, and it must be earned by the value the article's own symbols take.

Symbols Used Throughout

  • $\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}$ is the central angle of a biquaternion, the phase that multiplies the identity; the letter $\alpha$ is kept only where it names a complex wavenumber.
  • $S$ is an action, $S_{\tilde\Xi}$ the action of the field $\tilde\Xi$, and $\hbar$ the reduced constant.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ is a rapidity, always halved in the rotor, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2$.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ is a phase in radians, and $\theta_{\mathrm{azm}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{stk}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{axc}}$ are the azimuthal, Stokes and axial angles.
  • $\theta_{\mathrm{rat}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{rst}}$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{fld}}$ are the rotation angle, the rest-energy phase and the angular mode of a field.
  • $\Gamma$ is the gauge function, $ϙ$ the charge, and $\exp(i\varrho\Gamma/\hbar)$ the gauge element.
  • $\tilde H_\Psi$ is the Hamiltonian biquaternion, $\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{modular}}$ its modular form, and $r'_t$ the retarded time.
  • $k_\Phi$ is the wave number of the phase, $k_m$ the mass wave number $mc/\hbar$, and $\omega$ an angular frequency.
  • $\Xi$ is an invariant phase, $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ the phase of a spinor field, and $Ϙ$ a winding number.
  • $\beta$ is the inverse temperature, left bare because it carries a dimension, and $\omega_n$ a Matsubara frequency.
  • $r$, $\rho$ are scales, and $\mathrm{Sc}$ takes the scalar part of a product of two biquaternions.

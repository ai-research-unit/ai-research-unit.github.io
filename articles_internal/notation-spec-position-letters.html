Row 6 is settled, in capitals. The subscript of the element is the label of the sector the half lives in, so $\tilde R_\Psi\in\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ and $\tilde R_\Phi\in\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$: a subscript and a sector cannot drift apart, because they are the same letter. Lowercase $\psi$ keeps its other jobs — the wavefunction in physics, the propositional variable in logic, the Chebyshev function, a spinor operand — and lowercase $\varphi$ keeps the generic map, the test function and the linear functional. The pass is applied to articles_physics/, articles_maths/ and articles_internal/: zero lowercase sector subscripts survive on $\tilde R$, $\vec r$, $\vec u$ or $\tilde Q$.

The three decidable rules

Rule A — the test for row 1 against row 2. $t'$ is a coordinate when it sits inside a scalar that is a length or an interval, or when it names one point of a pair. It is a parameter when something is differentiated or exponentiated with respect to it. Operationally: if the symbol is the denominator of a $\partial$ or $d$, or the argument of an exponential, or the variable of a one-parameter group, keep $t'$. Otherwise it is a coordinate and becomes $r'_t$.

Rule B — why $t'$ survives at all. $r'_t$ is a length; $t'$ is a duration. The corpus reads time in metres on the coordinate slot and in seconds on the parameter slot, and $c$ is the only object that converts. Keeping both is what makes $\exp(-iHt'/\hbar)$ dimensionally honest while $i c r'_t$ stays a length.

Rule C — why $h$ does not survive. The bridge $h_t=c r_t$, $h_k=r'_k$ is not a description of a symbol to keep; it is the licence to delete it. It says the two writings denote the same number. A notation that carries two names for one number is the defect being repaired, not part of the repair. So the operator letters go:

$h_t$ $c r_t$ the bridge, read left to right
$h_x,h_y,h_z$ $r'_x,r'_y,r'_z$ the bridge, read left to right
$\vec h$ $\vec r_\Psi$ the vector part of $\tilde R_\Psi$, in the parallel already used for the material half

The parallel that decides it is already in the corpus: the material half is written $\tilde R_\Phi=i c r'_t\,e_t+\vec r_\Phi$ in 5 articles, with its vector part carrying the half's own subscript. The informational half must be written the same way, $\tilde R_\Psi=c r_t\,e_t+i\vec r_\Psi$. Today the two halves are asymmetric — one in position letters, one in operator letters — and that asymmetry is the whole of the inconsistency.

The collision I wrongly feared. $\vec r$ is the Bloch vector of the state $\tilde\varsigma=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec r)$; $\vec h$ is the vector part of the observable. The Born weight is $\tfrac12(h_t+\vec r\cdot\vec h)$. Renaming $h$ to $\vec r$ would indeed have collapsed the two, but that is not the rename: the target is $\vec r_\Psi$, which stays distinct from the Bloch vector. The formula becomes $\tfrac12(c r_t+\vec r\cdot\vec r_\Psi)$ — Bloch direction against the observable's vector part, which is what it always meant.

Rule D — the one place an exception is allowed. $H$ survives, and only as a declared abbreviation inside an article that actually does evolution: $H:=\tilde R_\Psi$ in its energy reading, with $\hbar$ in the exponent $\exp(-iHt'/\hbar)$ and not in the coefficient. It is declared once, at first use, in each of the 4 articles that write $H=h_t e_t+i\vec h$. Everywhere else the element is $\tilde R_\Psi$. A bare $h$ without a subscript stays the Planck constant.

The bridges, declared once, introduction-to-the-biquaternion-universe:82:

$$r_t=t,\quad r'_t=t',\qquad r_k=x_k,\quad r'_k=x'_k,\qquad c r_t=ct,\quad c r'_t=ct' .$$

The rule, therefore. The layer of a display fixes its letters, and the derivative takes the coordinate slot of that same layer. There is no mixing to repair, because $\partial/\partial r_k$ and $\partial/\partial x_k$ and $\partial/\partial Q_k$ are one derivative under three names on the bridge.

Consequences, measured.

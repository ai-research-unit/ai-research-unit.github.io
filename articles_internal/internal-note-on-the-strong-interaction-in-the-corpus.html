Internal Note: The Strong Interaction in the Biquaternion Corpus

The Answer

The corpus transcribes the strong sector and constructs its near half, and here the boundary is a theorem. The menu draws it: The Strong Interaction is one of the six parts of the category, and it holds twenty-four articles. The algebra reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and it does not reach $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. Colour is outside, and the exclusion is proved twice — by dimension and by module parity — so it is not an unfinished construction but a closed door with a price list beside it.

The decisive correction. The non-abelian factor of the framework is the rotation group of space. Read as an internal symmetry it is weak isospin, and it fails; read as geometry it is the spatial half of a spin connection, and the corpus already has that reading. The octet of QCD is a different object in a different slot, and no relabelling of the framework's factor produces it.

One force, three spins. Spin $1$ supplies the eight carriers, spin $\tfrac12$ the matter they act on, spin $0$ the Goldstones of the broken chiral symmetry and the soliton that stands in for the baryon. That is why the part needs its own spin bands, and why the pion sits beside the proton rather than beside the photon.

The consequence, in one line. What the part can do, it does with the algebra's own $\mathrm{SU}(2)$: instantons inside the compact factor, the chiral field and its Maurer–Cartan form, the Skyrme soliton, the chiral condensate, the multiplet bookkeeping, and the shape of the running. What needs colour — the triplet, the octet's colour factor, confinement — is imported with its status marked on every article.

What the Strong Interaction Is

One interaction, one charge, and the charge is never seen alone. Above the confinement scale the degrees of freedom are eight massless vector fields in the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and three species of coloured fermion. Below it, no coloured object is free: the spectrum is colour-singlet hadrons, and the potential between a separated pair grows with distance.

The group is $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$, with $N_c=3$. Eight generators, structure constants $f_{abc}$, and the adjoint is eight-dimensional. The fundamental is three-dimensional and complex. Both of those numbers matter for the ceiling, and they matter in different ways: one is a dimension the algebra does not have, the other is a module it cannot build.

Two properties have no classical counterpart. Asymptotic freedom: the coupling falls with energy, so the short-distance theory is weakly coupled and computable. Confinement: the long-distance theory has no coloured asymptotic states, and no perturbative argument produces the mass gap. The two live in the same beta function with opposite signs.

Chiral symmetry breaking is the third pillar. The vacuum carries a fermion bilinear, the axial symmetry is broken by it, and the pions are the pseudo-Goldstones of that breaking. The framework's version of this is its strongest single result in the sector, and it is a statement about the form of the relation and not about the scale.

Two Layers

What the algebra supplies

A compact four-dimensional algebra, and its derived algebra. $\mathbb{B}_\Phi=\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$, four real dimensions, against the eight that $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ needs. The commutators of the units give more than the compact part:

$$ [\mathbb B,\mathbb B]=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C),\qquad \dim_\mathbb R\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)=6, \qquad \mathbb B=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb C_{\mathbb B}, \qquad \mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}\subset\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}. $$

The derived algebra is six-dimensional and non-compact, and the compact factor inside it is three-dimensional. Neither number is eight, and no basis change moves either.

The instanton, inside the compact factor. The BPST solution is built on $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{span}_\mathbb{R}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}\subset\mathbb{B}_\Phi$, with the Bogomolny bound fixing its action. The saddle of the semiclassical expansion is therefore an object the algebra makes, and not one it receives.

The abelian topological density, as the imaginary part of the norm. In the Maxwell sector the Pontryagin density is the framework's own invariant up to a constant:

$$ \sum_{{\imath},{\jmath}}F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}\star F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}=\frac{4i}{c}\,I_2\ \ (\text{Lorentzian}),\qquad \sum_{{\imath},{\jmath}}F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}\star F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}=4\,I_2\ \ (\text{Euclidean}),\qquad I_2=\vec E\cdot\vec B . $$

So the theta term's density is native. Its coefficient is not, and the note returns to that below.

The Maurer–Cartan structure, and with it the chiral field. The group-valued field $\tilde U$ takes values in the unit real quaternions, $\langle\tilde U\rangle_\natural=\tilde U\tilde U^{\natural}=e_t$, and the biquaternion norm is the invariant metric on the group. The nonlinear sigma model's target space is therefore the framework's own group, and its action is the norm of the Maurer–Cartan form. This is the part's single most native object: the target of the chiral Lagrangian, its current, and its topological charge are one construction in the algebra.

The pion, as the adjoint of the material sector. $\vec\pi=\sum_{k\in\{x,y,z\}}\pi_k\vec e_k$ with $[\vec e_j,\vec e_k]=2\sum_l\epsilon_{jk l}\vec e_l$, normalised by the plain weight of the units. The triplet of pions is the adjoint three of the algebra's compact factor, which is why the isospin bookkeeping and the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ bookkeeping agree where they overlap.

The Skyrme field, its current and its winding. The skyrmion field is the pion's chiral field, a map from spacetime to the group; the current is its Maurer–Cartan form; the topological current is the contraction of three of them,

$$ B_{\imath}=\frac{1}{24\pi^2}\sum_{{\jmath},\rho,\sigma}\varepsilon_{{\imath}{\jmath}\rho\sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(j_{\jmath} j_\rho j_\sigma\right),\qquad j_{\imath}=\tilde U^{-1}\partial/\partial R_{\imath}\,\tilde U , $$

and its conservation is an identity, not an equation of motion. Baryon number is a winding number with no colour in it.

The axial phase, as two opposite phases on two values. The algebra carries one chirality operator and one mass term, and the axial rotation is the pair of opposite phases on the two values. That is the framework's version of the axial anomaly's setting: two values, one coupling, one phase difference, at no cost.

The multiplet algebra, which is group theory and not colour. The degree formula, the mod $3$ rule, the Okubo ansatz and the Gell-Mann–Okubo relation are $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ representation theory written in the corpus's coordinates. The algebra's own $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ sits inside that $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as the isospin subgroup, and the ceiling permits exactly that.

The shape of the running, and the trace anomaly. The renormalisation-group machinery supplies the form of the beta function, and the conformal anomaly supplies the statement that the proton's mass is the trace of the energy–momentum tensor. Neither supplies a coefficient.

The Four Refusals

First: colour inside the algebra. Two independent proofs. The compact algebra has four real dimensions and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has eight. And every $\mathbb{B}$-module has even complex dimension, while the triplet and the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ are odd-dimensional. The second proof is the sharper one, because it does not depend on how the algebra is enlarged.

Second: confinement. No string tension, no area law, no mass gap. The part says so in every article that touches it, and it does not dress the absence as a research programme.

Third: a derivation of $\theta_{\mathrm{vac}}$. The framework supplies the density, its parity-odd character as $I_2$, its periodicity and its role in the vacuum. It does not supply the number, and the reason is structural: a coefficient of a total derivative is not fixed by an equation of motion. The measured bound $|\bar\theta|\lesssim10^{-10}$ then sits in the corpus as a fact the framework records and cannot explain.

Fourth: the baryon-number–colour connection. The winding exists, colour does not, so the statement that a baryon is three quarks in a colour singlet has no home here. The skyrmion is a baryon by winding and by nothing else.

One root under all four. Representation is a function of spin. A field's representation is fixed once its spin is fixed, so a colour triplet, which is a choice of representation independent of spin, is not available at any spin.

The Dimension Ceiling, at Four Levels

The mismatch $4\ne8$ is a count, and the count is the weakest form of the argument.

Level one: dimension. The compact algebra of $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ is four-dimensional; $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is eight-dimensional. This alone kills an embedding of the group into the algebra.

Level two: module parity. Every module over $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ has even complex dimension. The colour triplet is three-dimensional and complex; the adjoint is eight-dimensional and real, odd as a complex space. So neither the fundamental nor the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ can be a carrier, at any spin, in any basis.

Level three: the derived algebra. $[\mathbb{B},\mathbb{B}]=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$, of real dimension six, non-compact, and $\mathbb{B}$ is that algebra plus one central direction. The compact part is the centre and three generators. Nothing in this decomposition is eight-dimensional and compact.

Level four: the ambient group. Enlarging the carrier to $M_n(\mathbb{B})\cong M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$ gives the intrinsic group $\mathrm{U}(2n)$, and any compact group of dimension at most $4n^2$ with a faithful $2n$-dimensional representation embeds. $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ embeds at $n=2$. But then the statement under test has changed: it is about the group of a matrix algebra over $\mathbb{B}$ and not about the group of $\mathbb{B}$. The enlarged route is a legitimate construction and it is not a refutation.

The three false routes, named so that they are not mistaken for arguments. "Three letters, so three colours": the three letters are the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and colour needs three fundamental complex dimensions. "Eight real dimensions, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has eight": a count between a carrier and a symmetry, in the wrong slot. "The octonions contain $\mathrm{SU}(3)$": true, with $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{O})=G_2$ of dimension fourteen, and the price is associativity, which every construction in the corpus uses.

The Instanton, and the One Coincidence

The saddle is the framework's and the density is not, and the part says which is which.

What is native. What Would Count as a Derivation

A derivation is a chain from the algebra to a number, and the chain is only as good as its weakest link. The corpus never claims a derivation where a link is missing, and this note holds itself to the same standard. The standard sits here rather than in an article, because a document whose content is a requirement is not a result.

Six links, in the order a derivation of colour would have to supply them.

  1. A carrier of odd complex dimension. This is the first link and the only one that is a theorem today: it is false, and every later link inherits the falsity.
  2. The colour group as an endomorphism of that carrier. With the triplet available, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ would have to act on it inside the algebra's own structure and not as an added factor.
  3. The confining dynamics. A string tension, an area law, or a mass gap, from the algebra and not from a lattice.
  4. The value of $\bar\theta$. A coefficient the algebra can value rather than record.
  5. The coefficients. $f_\pi$, $e$, $f_a$ and $\Lambda^4$, each computed or declared, with the ledger showing which.
  6. The condensate's formation. Why the vacuum carries the bilinear, from the algebra's own dynamics.

What does not count. A dimension match. An isomorphism of Lie algebras. A coincidence of counts. A relabelling of one group as another. An identification made after the result is known. Each of the first four appears somewhere in the corpus, each is labelled there as what it is, and none of them is a link.

The corpus against the six links. The first is proved false, which is the part's central result. The fifth is supplied in form and imported in value. The rest are open, and the third and sixth are open in the strong sense: nothing in the corpus points at a route.

The One Problem That Would Change the Verdict

A module of odd complex dimension. It is the only object whose existence would flip the ceiling, because the dimension count is the weaker of the two proofs and would fall to an enlargement, while the parity argument would fall only to a counterexample.

It is stated here and not proposed as an article. An article whose content is a hope is worse than a note that says where the hope lies, and the corpus has already priced the two known routes — non-associativity through the octonions, and an added factor through an enlarged carrier.

Why it is the only one. Every other open row asks for a number the framework must be given: a coupling, a scale, a coefficient. This one asks whether the even-dimension theorem is a theorem about $\mathbb{B}$ or only about the way the corpus builds modules over it. That is a question about the algebra, and it is the only question in this part that the algebra could still answer.

Summary

The object. The symmetry group QCD needs is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, eight-dimensional and compact, with a complex three-dimensional fundamental. The algebra's compact part is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, four-dimensional, isomorphic to $(\mathrm{Spin}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$. The two are not the same group in different words.

The ceiling. Two proofs, dimension and module parity, and neither is lifted by a change of basis. The second is the stronger: every $\mathbb{B}$-module has even complex dimension, so the triplet cannot be a carrier at any spin. The only counterexample that would matter is a module of odd complex dimension.

The native half. The four refusals. Colour inside the algebra; confinement; a derivation of $\theta_{\mathrm{vac}}$; the baryon-number–colour connection. Each has a reason and each reason is stated where it is used.

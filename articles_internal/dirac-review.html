Dirac Review

Scope

This file is an index, not an article and not an entry of a menu. It lists the articles of the physics corpus whose title names Dirac, gives a direct link to each, and states in one line what each carries.

What is counted. A row is owed to an article whose title names Dirac — that is, whose file name contains dirac. There are twenty-three of them among the 415 articles of AI Physics, and every one is in the physics menu. The test is mechanical, so the list can be rebuilt by listing articles_physics/*dirac*.md; nothing is included or excluded by judgement.

What is not counted. Articles that use the Dirac equation without naming it in the title are gathered in §Neighbours and are not part of the twenty-three. The mathematics corpus is out of scope; §Off-Corpus names the two articles there that the physics articles cite for the spinor and for the chirality.

What a row is not. The one-line content is the subject of the article's sections, not an assessment of it. A row is not a verdict, and no comparison between articles is intended. The review of substance recorded in §Review State covers one article only.

Review State

One article of the twenty-three has been read line by line for the pair and the mass: The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form. The finding there was a defect of vocabulary and not of equation — the subscripts $L$ and $R$ were described as values carried across by the mass, while the mass is a central coefficient and the value is read by the basis operator — and the repair is recorded in that article's §The Gradient and the Equation, in the paragraph The letters of the pair, and the values of the basis operator.

The same distinction appears under the name of chirality in several of the articles above. The article that fixes the notion is the mathematics one named in §Off-Corpus, and the wording is settled there and quoted from there. The migration file that tracked the rows article by article has been retired to _reserve/others/chirality-migration.md.7z and is no longer in the working tree, so no row here points at it.

The remaining twenty-two rows of this file record subject only. No row here is a claim that the article behind it has been checked, and the list is not a ranking.

Batches one and two, applied. Fourteen articles of the family now use the reference article's vocabulary: the subscripts name the two components of the pair, the value is what the basis operator returns, and the mass is the central coefficient that relates the components and moves no value across. Eleven sentences that made the mass carry the value across were rewritten, and the phrase the two values of the subscript was retired throughout the family. Where an article uses a value-reversing operator — the action $\mathcal{D}$ of the solutions companion, the multiplier $\beta$ of the Hamiltonian form — the text now says so and says which coefficient the same mass is on the other side. The deeper question raised by that pass is recorded in §Open Point below.

Open point: the letters and the values cannot both be primitive. If the subscripts $L$ and $R$ denote the two values returned by the basis operator, then the printed pair $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{L}$ has no nonzero massive solution, because a left multiplier and a central coefficient each keep the value, so the two sides of a line carry different values and both must vanish. The dimension of the definite-value massive solution space is $0$; with a value-reversing crossing operator in place of the left gradient it is $2$, and at $m=0$ the kernel splits into one state of each value. So either the letters name components that are not value eigenstates, which is the reference article's reading, or the crossing operator must multiply on the right, which is the solutions companion's. Both readings are self-consistent; a text that mixes them is not, and the notation row of Special Element Conventions that reads $\tilde Q_L,\tilde Q_R$ as the two values of the subscript is the place where the mixing would have to be resolved.

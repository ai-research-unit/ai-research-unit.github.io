The letters: the corpus does decide, and its equation does not follow

What the conventions say. Three articles fix the letters, and they agree with each other.

Special Element Conventions, §The Spinor Convention: "A Dirac spinor is an element $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}+\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ of $\mathbb{B}$, the two parts being its two components in the decomposition of $\mathbb{B}$ into minimal left ideals, $\mathbb{B}=S_+(\vec u)\oplus S_-(\vec u)$"; and "The two parts carry the chirality letters. The decomposition is the decomposition into the two values of the chirality: the basis operator $-iR_{\vec u}$ is $-1$ on $S_+(\vec u)$ and $+1$ on $S_-(\vec u)$".

Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation: "Chirality is the subscript: an element is $L$ or $R$, and those are the two values."

The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form, §The Value Is a Rule: "$\tilde X\vec e_z=-i\tilde X\Longrightarrow\tilde X$ carries the value $L$", plus the rule "left multiplication keeps the value".

So the conventions settle it: the two parts are the two minimal left ideals, and those are the two values. The letters are values, not arbitrary component names. That is one reading, not two.

The equation cannot deliver it, and this is a theorem, not a preference. A minimal left ideal is closed under left multiplication, $M\,S_\pm\subseteq S_\pm$, so the gradient keeps the letter: it is diagonal in the value. A pair whose two lines equate different letters therefore has only the zero solution:

$$\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{left side carries }R,\ \text{right side carries }L\ \Longrightarrow\ \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=0 .$$

Checked on $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ with $e_t=\mathrm I$, $\vec e_k=i\sigma_k$, $\vec e_z=i\sigma_3$: the value-$L$ ideal is $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_+$ with $\tilde\Pi_+=\tfrac12(\mathrm I+i\vec e_z)$, a random left multiplier maps it to itself, and $\tilde\nabla$ sends value-$L$ to value-$L$ and value-$R$ to value-$R$.

The operator that does deliver it is already in the corpus. The two-sided action $$D:\ \tilde X\longmapsto\sum_\mu e_\mu\,\frac{\partial\tilde X}{\partial x_\mu}\,\vec e_{\nu(\mu)},\qquad \nu(t)=x,\ \nu(k)=y,$$ carries a right slot on every term, and that slot is $\vec e_x$ or $\vec e_y$, both of which reverse the value. So $D$ is off-diagonal: it maps $S_+\to S_-$ and $S_-\to S_+$, checked on the same matrices. Its square is $\Box$ in the corpus's normalization, $\sum_k\partial_k^{2}-\partial_t^{2}=\partial^{2}/\partial R_t^{2}+\Delta$, which is the identity already verified for this article's neighbour. With $D$ in the pair, the mass term $k_m$ multiplies in place and stays diagonal, the two parts are the two values, and the solution count per momentum is Dirac's: on shell $D$ restricted to $S_-$ is invertible onto $S_+$, so one arbitrary element of $S_-$ (two complex numbers, the spin) determines the whole field.

The corpus's own sentences are the evidence that this was the intent. The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form, §Statement prints "It is linear in $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and off-diagonal in the value: each line holds the part with one value on the left and the part with the other value on the right" — a description of $D$, not of $\tilde\nabla$. §Massive Plane Waves prints "one polarization biquaternion per value". §Chirality prints "at $m=0$ ... they are its left-handed and right-handed parts". All three are true of the pair built on $D$ and false of the pair built on $\tilde\nabla$. One passage, §The Massive Case, says the opposite — "its two letters name two parts and not two values, and a field whose two parts had definite values, one of each, would satisfy both lines only by vanishing" — and it is right about the printed equation. It is a patch written to fit the operator, and it contradicts the conventions.

Diagnosis. The definitional pair lost its right slot. The one-sided gradient is the gradient of the algebra, not the Dirac operator; the Dirac operator of this algebra is the two-sided action whose two slots are occupied, which is why the $\gamma$ display needs two multipliers, $\gamma_\mu=iL_{e_\mu}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}$, and why $\gamma_5=-iR_{\vec e_z}$ anticommutes with all four. A one-sided gradient commutes with $\gamma_5$ and cannot be a Dirac operator of a chiral field.

The repair, stated once. Replace the off-diagonal operator of the pair, and of $\mathbb{D}$, by $D$ and its conjugate: $$\mathbb{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\bigl(D\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R},\ D^{\natural}\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}\bigr),\qquad \mathbb{D}^{2}=\Box I,\qquad (D-k_m)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0 .$$ Every statement the corpus already makes about the letters then holds: they are values, the mass couples the two of them, the massless limit gives two Weyl equations, and the field has Dirac's four solutions.

Blast radius, measured. $85$ articles write the pair; $18$ of them also state that left multiplication keeps the value, $12$ say "one per value", $5$ say "off-diagonal in the value". The three sentences that describe the $D$ structure are in the reference article itself, so the repair restores the description rather than overturning it.

Order of work, if it is undertaken. (1) The definition of $\mathbb{D}$ in Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Pair Operator and Its Symbol (now The Two First-Order Equations and Their Symbol, the glyph retired), which is one display and is where the pair operator is fixed. (2) The boxed pair in the reference article, §The Equation in the Algebra and §Statement. (3) The plane-wave algebra, where $\tilde\nabla\Xi=i\tilde K\Xi$ becomes $D\Xi=i\sum_\mu e_\mu K_\mu\Xi\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}$; the mass shell, the dispersion and the Klein–Gordon consequence are unchanged, because they use only $D^{2}=\Box$. (4) The remaining $82$ articles, which quote the pair and do not differentiate it.

What I did not do. I did not apply this to $85$ files on my own judgement. The step that precedes it is a decision, and the decision is now stated in the corpus's own terms rather than left open: the letters are the two values, and the operator is the two-sided action. If that decision is taken, step (1) and step (2) are an afternoon; step (3) is the careful part.

Where the clash came from, and the three edits that state it

The origin is a loan. The pair is not native to the physics corpus. The First Order Root of the Biquaternion d'Alembertian (mathematics) asks for a first-order factorisation of $\Box-\lambda$ and proves there is no single square with element coefficients: four elements would have to anticommute pairwise with squares in the ratio one to three, the algebra supplies the three spatial units and no fourth. What survives is the factorisation $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla=\Box$, and the massive equation is written there as the pair with $\lambda=m^{2}$. In that article the two letters are the two factors, the two fields are related by left multiplications, and the article says so in as many words: "the mass is carried by the pair; no single field carries a scalar mass". No chirality claim is made and none is needed.

The physics corpus imported the pair and kept the letters, but the letters had acquired a meaning in the meantime: Special Element Conventions defines the Dirac spinor as the split into the two minimal left ideals and says "the two parts carry the chirality letters", and Matrix Representations Conventions says "chirality is the subscript ... those are the two values". So the same two letters now name two different decompositions, and only one of them is compatible with a left-multiplication operator.

That is the whole of the confusion, and it is a loan that was not renegotiated. The pair is a factorisation; the letters are chirality values; the factorisation's two fields carry one value. Three true statements, one display.

The edits made. Each replaces a claim the algebra refutes with the claim it supports, and none touches an equation.

  1. The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form, §Statement: "off-diagonal in the value" became "off-diagonal in the part and diagonal in the value", with the reason in the same sentence. The printed operator is a left multiplication; it is diagonal in the value, and the old phrasing described an operator the display does not contain.
  2. The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form, §The Massive Case: the argument was kept and its conclusion corrected. The passage now states that the printed pair relates two parts of one value, exhibits the relation $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=\frac{i}{k_m}\tilde{K}_{\Phi}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ in momentum space, calls the pair the factorisation of $\Box$ by the one-sided gradient, and names the two-sided operator $\gamma_\mu=iL_{e_\mu}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}$ — whose right slot is $\vec e_x$ or $\vec e_y$ and therefore reverses the value — as the operator under which the letters are the two values. It also says where the readings agree, at $m=0$.
  3. The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form, §Massive Plane Waves: "one polarization biquaternion per value" became "for each part", which is what the momentum-space system actually delivers.
  4. The First Order Root of the Biquaternion d'Alembertian, scope paragraph: the two letters are declared to be the two factors there, with the two uses told apart by the operator and not by the letters. This closes the loan at its source.

What remains, and why it is one decision and not eighty-five. Making the letters name the values in every article means putting the right slot back into the pair, that is replacing $\tilde\nabla$ by $D$ in $\mathbb{D}$ and in the pair. It cannot be done article by article: the pair is quoted in $85$ articles and differentiated in perhaps $30$, and a half-migrated corpus would have some displays in one operator and some in the other. It is one change, made once, in the order below, and it should be made because a one-sided gradient gives a doubled spectrum where the two-sided operator gives Dirac's: two complex solutions per momentum, the spin, against four.

Order: the definition of $\mathbb{D}$ in Operator and Calculus Conventions; the two boxed displays in the reference article; the plane-wave algebra in the reference article; then the remaining articles, which quote and do not compute.

Retraction, and the correct repair

What I got wrong in the previous section. I recommended replacing the one-sided gradient by the two-sided action $D$ inside the pair, on the strength of $D$ being off-diagonal between the two ideals. Two mathematical articles, read after that recommendation, make it wrong: Spinors and Dirac Spinors in the Biquaternion Algebra, §What Carries One Part to the Other, and Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra, §What Operator Chirality Is Not. Both assign the exchange to the right slot, and both say that a left multiplier keeps every letter. Spinors, §What Carries One Part to the Other, last remark: "The derivative terms act on the left and stay in their part. The two-sided form of the equation, in which the derivative acts from the left and the mass from the right, is therefore not a rewriting: it is the only side on which the mass can act." So the exchange belongs to the mass, and putting it into the derivative is the one thing the corpus forbids. Withdrawn.

What the two articles add that settles the letters question. Three statements, none of which I had.

  1. The two parts are a multiplicity, not two representations. "As a left module $\mathbb{B}\cong S\oplus S$ with $S$ the simple module … the decomposition into the two parts is a multiplicity, not a direct sum of two different representations. The Dirac spinor is a carrier of spin $\tfrac12$ with multiplicity two, and the second factor is what the labels $L$ and $R$ index." So the two parts are the same module twice; the letters index the copy.
  2. The labels carry no invariant content. "The two labels $L$ and $R$ are a convention and carry no invariant content. Exchanging them is the substitution $\vec u\mapsto-\vec u$ … A statement that treats the two parts differently is a statement about the declared axis and not about the algebra." So "the two values" is a statement about the test and the frame, not an invariant of the algebra.
  3. Two readings, named. On $\Delta=S\oplus S^{\mathrm c}$ the second factor is the conjugate module, on which $Q$ acts as $\bar Q$; the subspaces are the same and only the declared action differs. "A statement about the two parts therefore has to name its reading."

So my sentence "that is one reading, not two" was wrong, and so was calling the letters invariant. What is true is narrower: the letters are the two eigenspaces of $R(i\vec u)$, hence the two minimal left ideals, hence two copies of one module, labelled relative to the declared axis.

A sharper separation than I had. Operator Chirality keeps two notions apart that the physics corpus runs together. Operator chirality is the slot of an operator, a membership in $\mathrm{End}_\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{B})$; it is the article's own subject and it is not a property of an element. The letters on an element come from a chosen basis operator: "a different $u$ gives a different pair of halves … That is why the split is a property of the chosen element." And the article refuses the ideals as a home for the label: "a proof that some ideal is chiral would be a confusion of the two spaces rather than a theorem." The physics corpus uses "chirality" for both, which is where the vocabulary drifted.

The correct repair, verified. Put the right factor in the mass: $$\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}i\vec v,\qquad \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}i\vec v,\qquad \vec v\perp\vec u,\ \lvert\vec v\rvert=1 .$$ Checks on $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ with $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_k)=-i\sigma_k$, axis $\vec e_z$, so $S_+(\vec e_z)$ is value $L$:

  • $\tilde\nabla$ keeps the value, as required: $S_+\to S_+$, $S_-\to S_-$;
  • right multiplication by $i\vec e_x$ exchanges: $S_+\to S_-$, $S_-\to S_+$;
  • the printed pair has kernel dimension $0$ — with $\tilde\Xi_L\in S_+$ and $\tilde\Xi_R\in S_-$, the first line equates a value-$R$ element to a value-$L$ one and both fields vanish;
  • the repaired pair has kernel dimension $2$ per on-shell momentum, which with the two energy branches is Dirac's $4$;
  • the square is unchanged, $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla=\Box$ and $(i\vec v)^{2}=+1$, so $\Box\tilde\Xi_R=k_m^{2}\tilde\Xi_R$ and $(\Box-k_m^{2})\tilde\Xi=0$ holds.

Consistency with what the corpus already prints. §An Explicit Dictionary with Conjugation in the Mass Term writes $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}F=-ik_m\bar F\vec e_z$ — derivative on the left, mass on the right, no slot in the derivative. That is the repaired structure, with the conjugation standing where the perpendicular unit stands; Spinors proves the exchange is unique up to a scalar, so the two realisations lie on one complex line. The dictionary was right and the boxed pair was the abbreviation.

What was changed in the base article. §Statement: the pair is named as a factorisation whose letters name its two factors, with a pointer to the values form. §The Massive Case: the "no nonzero solution" statement is now made exactly, the factorisation reading is named with its source, the exchange is assigned to the mass and to the right slot with citations to the two mathematical articles, and the repaired display is printed with its square and its count. No equation of the corpus was altered.

Why nothing else was touched. The $85$ articles quote the pair; the repair changes what the quotient means, not the algebra of any single article, and it is one edit per display. Doing it in the base article first, and recording the reason, is the only step that can be taken without a corpus-wide decision.

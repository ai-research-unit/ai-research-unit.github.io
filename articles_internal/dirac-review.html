Dirac Review

Scope

This file is an index, not an article and not an entry of a menu. It lists the articles of the physics corpus whose title names Dirac, gives a direct link to each, and states in one line what each carries.

What is counted. A row is owed to an article whose title names Dirac — that is, whose file name contains dirac. There are twenty-three of them among the 415 articles of AI Physics, and every one is in the physics menu. The test is mechanical, so the list can be rebuilt by listing articles_physics/*dirac*.md; nothing is included or excluded by judgement.

What is not counted. Articles that use the Dirac equation without naming it in the title are gathered in §Neighbours and are not part of the twenty-three. The mathematics corpus is out of scope; §Off-Corpus names the two articles there that the physics articles cite for the spinor and for the chirality.

What a row is not. The one-line content is the subject of the article's sections, not an assessment of it. A row is not a verdict, and no comparison between articles is intended. The review of substance recorded in §Review State covers one article only.

Review State

One article of the twenty-three has been read line by line for the pair and the mass: The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form. The finding there was a defect of vocabulary and not of equation — the subscripts $L$ and $R$ were described as values carried across by the mass, while the mass is a central coefficient and the value is read by the basis operator — and the repair is recorded in that article's §The Gradient and the Equation, in the paragraph The letters of the pair, and the values of the basis operator.

The same distinction appears under the name of chirality in several of the articles above. The article that fixes the notion is the mathematics one named in §Off-Corpus, and the wording is settled there and quoted from there. The migration file that tracked the rows article by article has been retired to _reserve/others/chirality-migration.md.7z and is no longer in the working tree, so no row here points at it.

The remaining twenty-two rows of this file record subject only. No row here is a claim that the article behind it has been checked, and the list is not a ranking.

Batches one and two, applied. Fourteen articles of the family now use the reference article's vocabulary: the subscripts name the two components of the pair, the value is what the basis operator returns, and the mass is the central coefficient that relates the components and moves no value across. Eleven sentences that made the mass carry the value across were rewritten, and the phrase the two values of the subscript was retired throughout the family. Where an article uses a value-reversing operator — the action $\mathcal{D}$ of the solutions companion, the multiplier $\beta$ of the Hamiltonian form — the text now says so and says which coefficient the same mass is on the other side. The deeper question raised by that pass is recorded in §Open Point below.

Open point: the letters and the values cannot both be primitive. If the subscripts $L$ and $R$ denote the two values returned by the basis operator, then the printed pair $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{L}$ has no nonzero massive solution, because a left multiplier and a central coefficient each keep the value, so the two sides of a line carry different values and both must vanish. The dimension of the definite-value massive solution space is $0$; with a value-reversing crossing operator in place of the left gradient it is $2$, and at $m=0$ the kernel splits into one state of each value. So either the letters name components that are not value eigenstates, which is the reference article's reading, or the crossing operator must multiply on the right, which is the solutions companion's. Both readings are self-consistent; a text that mixes them is not, and the notation row of Special Element Conventions that reads $\tilde Q_L,\tilde Q_R$ as the two values of the subscript is the place where the mixing would have to be resolved.

The equations, checked against the reference article

What was compared. The twenty-three articles were read for the six objects that carry the physics: the massive chiral pair, the mass wave number, the mass shell of the four-momentum, the mass shell of the wave vector, the conjugate form of the mass term, and the second-order operator with the dispersion it implies. The comparison is against the reference article's §The Gradient and the Equation, §The Mass Shell and §Plane-Wave Solutions.

The pair is one pair. The orientation is $\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ everywhere. The transposed orientation — plain gradient on the part named $L$, natural on the part named $R$ — occurs zero times in the physics corpus, against 276 and 178 occurrences of the two canonical lines. Sixty-three articles state the pair, so the agreement is wider than the family.

The constants agree. $k_m=mc/\hbar$ is stated once and used everywhere, with $1/k_m=\hbar/(mc)$ as the mass length. The mass shell reads $\tilde P_{\Phi}\tilde P_{\Phi}^{\natural}=-m^{2}c^{2}$ in every article that states it, and $\tilde K_{\Phi}\tilde K_{\Phi}^{\natural}=-k_m^{2}$ wherever the wave-vector form appears. The two are the same statement under $\tilde K_{\Phi}=\tilde P_{\Phi}/\hbar$, and the plane-wave dispersion $k_{t}^{\prime 2}=\lvert\vec k_\Phi\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2}$ follows from either.

Four defects were found and repaired. Each is a symbol written for another, not a changed equation.

  1. The Minimal Coupling of the Biquaternion Dirac Field to Electromagnetism: the chain $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}_{\mathrm{gco}}\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}\tilde{Ξ}_{R}=m\,\tilde\nabla^{\natural}_{\mathrm{gco}}\tilde{Ξ}_{L}=k_m^{2}\tilde{Ξ}_{R}$ carried $m$ in the middle, where its own boxed pair gives $k_m$; and the wave operator $\bigl(\sum_\mu\tilde\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{gco},\mu}+\frac{k_m i ϙ}{mc}\tilde F-m^{2}\bigr)\tilde{Ξ}_{R}=0$ carried $m^{2}$ where the corpus writes $k_m^{2}$. Three occurrences, all repaired. The article does not declare $m$ as an alias of $k_m$, and the middle term cannot equal $k_m^{2}\tilde{Ξ}_{R}$ unless the coefficient is $k_m$.
  2. Dual Equivalence of the Dirac and Topologically Massive Gauge Fields: the mass term was written $m\,\Gamma\overline F$ in three places and equated in one display to $-k_m i\overline F\vec e_z$. Since $\Gamma$ is right multiplication by $-i\vec e_z$, the coefficient is $k_m$; repaired to $k_m$.
  3. Exercise: Plane-Wave Solutions: the chain $-m^{2}c^{2}=\hbar^{2}k_m^{2}$ has the wrong sign, because $\hbar^{2}k_m^{2}=+m^{2}c^{2}$; repaired to $-\hbar^{2}k_m^{2}$. The step it supports, $\Box=k_m^{2}$ on the wave, is unaffected.
  4. The Nonlinear Dirac Equation and the Thirring Model: the sentence that grounds the metric said "the $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ frame of the conventions article, whose metric has $g_{tt}=+1$ and $g_{xx}=-1$". The conventions article states $g_{tt}=-c^{2}$ and, on the length coordinates, $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$; no other article in the corpus carries $g_{tt}=+1$. The attribution was corrected and the difference declared where the $\Box$ is defined.

One neighbour was repaired with them. Bosonization in Biquaternionic Form states $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=m\,\Gamma\overline{\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}}$ while citing the reference article, which states the same line with $k_m$; repaired to $k_m$.

Open, and needing a decision rather than a substitution. The nonlinear article is the only one in the corpus whose $\Box$ carries the minus on the spatial term, $\Box=\partial^{2}/\partial x_t^{2}-\sum_k\partial^{2}/\partial x_k^{2}$, against the corpus's $\partial^{2}/\partial R_t^{2}+\Delta$. Its own first-order equation is the reference pair verbatim, and the square of that pair is the corpus's $\Box$; so the two writings it calls "one dynamics" have squares that differ in sign on the space part. Either the $\Box$ is the $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ $\Box$ and the signature sentence is wrong, or the metric is $(+,-,-,-)$ and the quoted first-order pair must be re-derived in that signature. The choice belongs to the author; the text now says which signature it uses.

A second open point, of notation only. The path integral article writes the square as $\mathbb{D}^{2}-m^{2}c^{2}=\hbar^{2}(\Box-k_m^{2})$, so its $\mathbb{D}$ carries momentum; the oscillator and Klein–Gordon articles write $\mathbb{D}^{2}=\Box$ and $(\mathbb{D}-k_m)\tilde{Ξ}=0$, so their $\mathbb{D}$ carries inverse length. Both are internally correct; the letter is doing two jobs across the family.

Resolution of the two open points

The $\Box$ conflict was a sign error, not a choice of metric, and it is now repaired. I said earlier that the metric was the author's to choose. That was too weak: a relative sign between $\omega^{2}$ and $k_m^{2}$ is physical, and the article as printed gave the wrong one. The check is exact, on the $2\times2$ representation of $\mathbb{B}$ with $e_t=\mathrm I$, $\vec e_k=i\sigma_k$:

  • the two-sided action as printed, $i\sum_\mu e_\mu\partial_\mu(\cdot)\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}$, has square $\partial_t^{2}-\Delta$, which is $-\Box$ for the corpus's $\Box=\partial^{2}/\partial R_t^{2}+\Delta$; the free equation $D\tilde\Xi=k_m\tilde\Xi$ then gives $\omega^{2}=k^{2}-k_m^{2}$;
  • the same action without the prefactor, $\sum_\mu e_\mu\partial_\mu(\cdot)\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}$, has square $-\partial_t^{2}+\Delta$, which is $+\Box$; the same free equation then gives $\omega^{2}=k^{2}+k_m^{2}$.

The corpus's own display decides which is meant. Its $\Box$ is $\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, the square of the gradient, and the $\gamma$ display of the reference article is $i$ in every slot; the $\gamma$ sum is therefore not a square root of $\Box$ and the article's $i$ prefactor made its action the $\gamma$ sum. So the prefactor was dropped, the $\Box$ was written in the corpus's form $\sum_k\partial_k^{2}-\partial_t^{2}=\partial^{2}/\partial R_t^{2}+\Delta$, the six field equations lost the same prefactor, and the two Lagrangians lost the inner one. The article now states $({\Box}-k_m^{2})\tilde\Xi=0$ and $k_{t}^{\prime2}=\lvert\vec k_\Phi\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2}$, the reference article's equation and dispersion.

The metric sentence was separately wrong and is corrected. It read $g_{tt}=+1$, $g_{xx}=-1$ while defining $\varepsilon_\mu=-g_{\mu\mu}$ to be $+1$ on time: the two statements cannot both hold. The conventions article gives $g_{tt}=-c^{2}$ with $g_{kk}=+1$, so $\varepsilon_t=+1$ and $\varepsilon_k=-1$, which is what the article's own $\varepsilon$ needs. The attribution now matches.

The $\mathbb{D}$ clash is resolved in favour of the conventions. Operator and Calculus Conventions fixes $\mathbb{D}$ as the pair operator with $\mathbb{D}^{2}=\Box I$ and gives the symbol the mark, $\mathbb{D}_p^{2}=p^{2}$. Fifty-odd articles follow that; the path-integral article was the only one that did not, in two sentences that wrote $(\mathbb{D}-mc)$, $\mathbb{D}^{2}=\hbar^{2}\Box$ and $\hbar^{-2}(\mathbb{D}+mc)\Delta_F$. Those two sentences now read $(\mathbb{D}-k_m)$, $\mathbb{D}^{2}=\Box$ and $(\mathbb{D}+k_m)\Delta_F$, which is what the same article states twelve lines further up and what its own dimensional remark requires ("both operators carry the dimension of a wave number, $\mathbb{D}$ and $k_m$ alike"). No other article needed a change.

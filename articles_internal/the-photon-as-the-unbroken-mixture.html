The Photon as the Unbroken Mixture: One Null Direction, Three Parents

Introduction

The question. The electromagnetic section of the particle category is twenty-nine articles long and its equations are exact. What does the migration add to it, given that Maxwell's equations are unchanged?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; A_\mu=\frac{e}{g_L}W^{3}_{L\mu}+\frac{e}{g_R}W^{3}_{R\mu}+\frac{e}{2g_{B-L}}B_\mu, \qquad \frac{1}{e^{2}}=\frac{1}{g_L^{2}}+\frac{1}{g_R^{2}}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^{2}}, \qquad \text{and the ratios are }(1,1,\sqrt{\tfrac23}). \;} $$

What this note is, in the migration. New article $9$ of the eleven listed in internal-note-the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md §8.7, and the article that states the frame's modification of the electromagnetic section. It adds one claim and removes none: the equations stay, the field's identity changes, and the change is a statement about where the field comes from rather than about what it does.

What is claimed. That the photon is a particular linear combination of three gauge fields; that the combination is fixed by two vacuum conditions and requires no choice; that the coefficients are the same triple $(1,1,\tfrac23)$ that gives $\sin^{2}\theta_W=\tfrac38$; that the electromagnetic $U(1)$ is the last stabiliser of the chain and therefore the most mixed object in the frame; and that the corpus's twenty-nine articles survive verbatim except for one inserted section.

What is not claimed. That the photon's dynamics changes. That photon mass or charge quantisation is modified. That the mixture is observable: it is not, at any energy, and §5 says why — which is the reason the electromagnetic section can keep its title and its content.

1. Why the Photon Is the Mixed Object

One sentence, and it is a structural statement about the chain rather than about the field.

1.1 In the Standard Model the photon is a mixture of two fields, $W^3$ and $B$, and this is taught as a fact about electroweak unification. In the frame there are three neutral fields, because $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ contributes a third, and the photon is a mixture of all three.

1.2 The unbroken group is the last stabiliser. The chain's three steps are three stabilisers, and the surviving group is the intersection:

$$ \mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(\ell_1)\cap\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(2)_R}(\ell_2)\cap\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y}(\ell_3) . $$

A group defined by three conditions is generated by the one direction that satisfies all three, and in a three-dimensional neutral space three conditions leave one direction. So the photon's being "the most mixed" is not a dynamical accident; it is what three stabilisers in three dimensions must produce.

1.3 The counting, which is the whole argument. Three neutral fields, two independent vacuum conditions, one null direction. A frame with two neutral fields and one condition would have to choose which combination is the photon; this frame does not have to choose.

2. The Coefficients, Derived Three Ways

Three derivations that must agree, and they do.

2.1 From the mass matrix's null vector. With the neutral mass matrix of the spectrum note,

$$ \mathcal M^2=\sum_{i=1,2}v_i^{2}a_ia_i^{\mathsf T}, \qquad a_1=\bigl(0,-\tfrac12,\sqrt{\tfrac38}\bigr)v_R, \qquad a_2=\bigl(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,0\bigr)v , $$

the null vector is $a_1\times a_2\propto\bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac38},\sqrt{\tfrac38},\tfrac12\bigr)$, whose ratios are

$$ (1,\ 1,\ \tfrac12\cdot\sqrt{\tfrac83})=(1,\ 1,\ \sqrt{\tfrac23})=(1,\,1,\,0.816497) . $$

Verified numerically to nine digits.

2.2 From canonical normalisation. The photon is the canonically normalised combination, so its coefficient in each field is that field's coupling divided by the photon's:

$$ c_L=\frac{e}{g_L},\qquad c_R=\frac{e}{g_R},\qquad c_B=\frac{e}{2g_{B-L}} . $$

With $g_L=g_R=g_4$ and $g_{B-L}^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}$, the ratios are

$$ 1:\ 1:\ \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3/8}}=1:1:\sqrt{\tfrac23} . $$

The same triple, from a completely different argument, and that is the check that matters: the eigenvector knows nothing about normalisation and the couplings know nothing about the mass matrix, so agreement between them is not circular.

2.3 From the charge formula. $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ with the eigenvalues of the electron, $(-\tfrac12,0,-1)$ in the three-cartans basis, gives the photon's composition in the generator basis; the coefficient of each gauge field is the generator's eigenvalue times the inverse coupling, normalised. This is the same as §2.2 rewritten, and it is included because it is the derivation a reader will try first.

2.4 What "the same triple" means. $1$, $1$, and $\tfrac23$: two equal contributions from the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factors and two thirds of one from the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Cartan. Those three numbers are also the three terms of $1/e^{2}$. The amplitude in a field is proportional to that field's coupling precisely because both come from the same inverse-coupling sum, and that is the one-sentence explanation of an identity that otherwise looks like a coincidence.

3. The Coupling, and the $\tfrac38$

The number that the electromagnetic section inherits, and the check that closes it.

$$ \frac{1}{e^{2}}=\frac{1}{g_L^{2}}+\frac{1}{g_R^{2}}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^{2}} \;\;\xrightarrow[g_L=g_R=g_4]{\;g_{B-L}^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}\;}\; \frac{1}{g_4^{2}}\Bigl(1+1+\frac{1}{4\cdot\tfrac38}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{g_4^{2}}\Bigl(1+1+\tfrac23\Bigr)=\frac{8}{3g_4^{2}} , $$

so

$$ e^{2}=\tfrac38\,g_4^{2}, \qquad \sin^{2}\theta_W=\frac{e^{2}}{g_L^{2}}=\tfrac38\quad\text{at }M_{PS}. $$

Three remarks, in order of importance.

3.1 The $\tfrac38$ is not the electromagnetic section's claim; it is the chain's. The electromagnetic section inherits $e^{2}$ as a low-energy number, measured, and the frame relates it to $g_4$ at a high scale. Putting the $\tfrac38$ into the electromagnetic section would be putting a unification statement into a Maxwell article, and the migration does not: the $\tfrac38$ stays in the chain article and the electromagnetic section cites it once.

3.2 The $1/4$ is where the two-thirds comes from. $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ puts a factor $\tfrac12$ on the $B-L$ field, and a factor $\tfrac12$ in a coefficient becomes $\tfrac14$ in the inverse square. Combined with $g_{B-L}^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}$ it gives $\tfrac{1}{4\cdot3/8}=\tfrac23$, and the whole difference between Pati–Salam's $\tfrac38$ and the naive $\tfrac12$ (which one would get with two equal contributions and no $B-L$) is this one factor.

3.3 The check that it is not fitted. $g_{B-L}^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}$ follows from $\operatorname{tr}(T^{15})^{2}=\tfrac12$ and the eigenvalue array $(1,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$, with no freedom. A different choice of the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ normalisation would change $g_4$ and leave $e$ alone, which is exactly what a normalisation must do.

One inserted section, one sentence changed, and nothing else.

4.1 The inserted section. It is a single section, placed before the corpus's existing sections, and it says three things:

4.2 What is unchanged, and this is the important list. Maxwell's equations, the wave equation, the gauge invariance $A_\mu\to A_\mu+\partial_\mu\chi$, the field strength, the stress tensor, the Lagrangian $-\tfrac14F^2$, the quantisation, the Feynman rules, the Coulomb potential, the radiation formulae, the twenty-nine article titles, the six exercises. Every equation in the section is the same equation.

4.3 The one sentence whose meaning changes. Where the corpus says the photon is the gauge boson of $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$, it must now be able to say the $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ whose generator is $Q$, and whose gauge field is this mixture. The sentence is not wrong before; it is incomplete, and the migration completes it in one place rather than in twenty-nine.

4.4 Why one section and not twenty-nine. Because the mixture is a fact about the field's identity, and the field's identity is used in the section once — at its definition. Everything after that is dynamics, and dynamics does not care which linear combination of heavy fields a light field is made of. This is the same reason the Standard Model's own textbooks state the $W^3$–$B$ mixture once and then never again.

5. Why the Mixture Is Unobservable

The reason the electromagnetic section can keep its content, and the reason a critic cannot ask for a measurement.

5.1 At low energy the heavy fields are absent. The $Z$ and $Z_R$ are massive, so at $q^{2}\ll M_Z^{2}$ the photon's couplings to matter are the only ones that survive, and they are $eQ$ with $Q$ exact. The mixture is invisible because its partners cannot be produced.

5.2 There is no low-energy observable that distinguishes "photon = $B$" from "photon = mixture". Any such observable would need a coupling to a field the photon is mixed with, and those fields are at $10^{2}$–$10^{5}$ TeV. The statement is therefore a statement about the theory's structure, not about its predictions — which is worth saying plainly, because it bounds what the migration can claim.

5.3 What would be observable. A deviation of the photon's coupling from $eQ$ at loop level, from leptoquark exchange; suppressed by $(v/v_R)^{2}$, hence unobservable. And, in principle, a $Z$–$Z_R$ mixing angle, which is a property of the massive states and not of the photon.

5.4 The honest formulation. The migration adds a true statement about the photon's identity that no experiment can test. That is not a weakness of the statement — a structural statement need not be testable — but it does mean the electromagnetic section's experimental content is untouched, and the migration must not present it as newly supported.

6. The Photon and the Corpus's Own Electromagnetism

One connection the migration can make and the literature cannot, and it is worth stating carefully.

6.1 The corpus's electromagnetic articles derive Maxwell from the algebra's structure, not from a gauge principle: the field strength is a bivector, the equations are a projection, and $\mathrm{U}(1)$ appears as a phase. Nothing in that derivation needs a mixture.

6.2 The frame adds a second description of the same field, and the two must be compatible. The corpus's description says the photon is a $U(1)$ connection; the frame's says the $U(1)$ is a stabiliser of three vacua and its connection is a combination. These are compatible because a linear combination of connections is a connection, and the stabiliser's generator is $Q$.

6.3 The check that they are compatible, stated as a test. Take the frame's $A_\mu$ and compute its field strength; it is $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$ with no extra term, because the combination is constant in field space. So the corpus's bivector construction applies to the mixture verbatim, which is why the section's equations survive.

6.4 What would break the compatibility. A mixing angle that ran with energy — i.e. if the coefficients were scale-dependent. They are not: the coefficients are ratios of couplings, and the ratios run, but the identity of the unbroken direction does not. That distinction is subtle and worth one sentence in the inserted section.

Summary

The statement. $A_\mu=\frac{e}{g_L}W^3_L+\frac{e}{g_R}W^3_R+\frac{e}{2g_{B-L}}B$, ratios $(1,1,\sqrt{2/3})$, forced by two vacuum conditions in a three-dimensional neutral space.

The coupling. $1/e^{2}=1/g_L^{2}+1/g_R^{2}+1/(4g_{B-L}^{2})=\tfrac{8}{3g_4^{2}}$, hence $e^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}$.

The mechanism. The photon is the last stabiliser of the chain: three conditions, one direction.

The addition to the section. One section of three statements, before the existing twenty-nine articles, which are unchanged.

The limits. The mixture is unobservable, the equations are untouched, and the $\tfrac38$ belongs to the chain rather than to Maxwell.

The compatibility. A linear combination of connections is a connection with no extra term in $F$, so the corpus's bivector electromagnetism applies verbatim.

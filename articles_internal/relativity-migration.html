Introduction

The seven sections are The Transformation and Its Two Displays (2), The Group (2), The Representations (3), The Group at Work (3), The Larger Groups and Their Modules (3), The Kinematics of One and Two Particles (6) and The Action, the Current and the Flow (2).

The decision. The block becomes fifteen sections and fifty-seven entries. Of the fifty-seven, twenty stay as they are, one is the survey turned into a portal, nine are split out of that survey, nine are imported from other blocks, nine are physical readings of sections that the mathematics already proves and declines to read physically, and nine are new.

The rule that decides every entry. A physics entry is earned by a physical object: a frame, a rod, a clock, a signal, a sky, a momentum, a vacuum, a detector. An entry that has only an algebra element is not earned. It belongs in articles_maths, and the physics corpus carries a comment and a link. This rule is what the previous three drafts of this plan broke: five of the proposed entries restated mathematics articles that already exist, and three restated or partly restated physics articles that already exist. §What Was Proposed Before and Rejected lists them.

The second rule. A section of the survey article is split out only when the section is thin and the subject is large. The ten substantive sections of the survey occupy about three hundred of its $493$ lines, so each is about thirty-five lines, and each split produces an article ten times its present length. A split is not a copy. It is the material the survey did not have room for.

The Thesis: One Object, Three Layers

The block is about one object, the Lorentz group, and that object has three layers. Every section below belongs to one of the three.

The ground. The algebra is the matrix algebra, and the Lorentz algebra is its traceless part. The chain

$$ \mathbb{B}\;=\;\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}\;\cong\;M_2(\mathbb{C}) $$

is an isomorphism of associative complex algebras, of complex dimension four. The Lorentz algebra does not sit in it as an algebra but under the commutator, and the distinction matters:

$$ \underbrace{\mathbb{B}\ \text{under }[-,-]}_{\mathfrak{gl}(2,\mathbb{C}),\ \dim_{\mathbb{R}}8} \;\supset\; \underbrace{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}_{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}6}\;\cong\;\mathfrak{so}(1,3). $$

The traceless part of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the real Lie algebra of the Lorentz group; the full algebra under the bracket is $\mathfrak{gl}(2,\mathbb{C})$, which is not a Lorentz algebra. The complexification is a different object again and is not a subalgebra of $\mathbb{B}$:

$$ \mathfrak{so}(1,3)_{\mathbb{C}}\;\cong\;\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}), \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}=6, $$

the two summands being the self-dual and the anti-self-dual bivectors. This direct sum, and not the algebra $\mathbb{B}$ itself, is the ground of the representation theory: an irreducible Lorentz representation is a pair of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ representations, one for each summand, and that pair is the label $(m,n)$.

The convention box for the matrix model. The image

$$ M(\tilde Q)=Q_tI-iQ_x\sigma_x-iQ_y\sigma_y-iQ_z\sigma_z $$

with $Q\in\mathbb{C}^{4}$ has

$$ \det M(\tilde Q)=Q_t^{2}+Q_x^{2}+Q_y^{2}+Q_z^{2}=N(\tilde Q), $$

the algebra's own quadratic form, which is complex. It is not the Minkowski interval. The interval appears on the real slice $Q_t=q_t$ real and $Q_j=iq_j$ real, where $M=q_tI+\vec q\cdot\vec\sigma$ is Hermitian, $\det M=q_t^{2}-\vec q^{2}$, and $N$ takes the same value. Verified numerically: on that slice the image is Hermitian to machine precision, $\det M=N$, and $\det M=q_t^{2}-\lvert\vec q\rvert^{2}$. Every entry that says "determinant equals interval" means the interval on this slice, and every entry that says "$N$" means the complex form.

The two roles of the unit factor. With $\Rho$ a chosen modulus, $\tilde U=\tilde Q/\Rho$ lies in $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, which is the spinor group of the restricted Lorentz group $SO^{+}(1,3)$: the group acts on four-vectors by conjugation and on spinors by left multiplication, two representations of one group.

The decompositions. One element carries two splits, and both are group-theoretic. The first is the polar word, which is the Cartan decomposition $SL(2,\mathbb{C})=P\cdot K$. The second is the spectral decomposition, which is the eigenvalue decomposition in the defining representation. The two splits agree exactly on the normal elements, and the size of the disagreement is a physical number: the relative angle of the two axes.

The representations. The finite-dimensional representations are the $(m,n)$ and none of them is unitary. Unitarity lives in the infinite-dimensional series. The orbits of the group on momentum space and their stabilisers are the little groups, and the little-group labels are mass and spin.

The three layers are the three parts of the block. A reader who has the ground can read the decompositions. A reader who has the decompositions can read the representations. A reader who has the representations can read the orbits, the little groups and the fields.

The Progression

The order is not decorative. It follows from four dependencies.

The algebra before the group. A reader who does not know that the traceless part of $\mathbb{B}$ under the commutator is the Lorentz algebra will read every later article as an analogy between two unrelated objects. §The Algebra Is the Lorentz Algebra therefore comes second, before any decomposition.

The group before its decompositions. The word is a product of two subgroup elements, so the subgroups must be in place. §The Group and Its Two Actions comes before §The Two Decompositions.

The decompositions before the kinds. A classification of transformations by their fixed sets is a statement about which of the two angles vanish, so the angles must be in place.

The representations before the orbits. The orbits are picked out by the two Casimir operators, and the little-group labels are weights. Both are representation theory.

The five parts of the block are these dependencies read off in order.

  1. Write $A=M(\tilde Q)$ and factor out the central part, $A=\lambda A_0$ with $\lambda=re^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$ and $\det A_0$ positive real. Then $\det A=\lambda^{2}\det A_0$, so $\det K=\det A/\lvert\det A\rvert$ is a pure phase unless this step is done.

The Nine New Articles and the Two Splits That Are Nearly New

Each one is stated with the object it needs, because the object is what earns it, and with the claim it must not make. Two of the eleven paragraphs below describe entries coded S, not N: 3 and 34 each have a survey section as their seed, and the seed is a page where the article is a chapter, so the provenance stays S and the paragraph is kept here because it states what is new inside the split.

3. The Isomorphisms and Their Dimensions. The object is the algebra and its dimensions. The claim is that the associative isomorphism is one statement and the Lie-algebra statement is a second, and that writing them as one chain equates a four-dimensional algebra with a six-dimensional one. It proves both, gives each a use, and refuses the reading in which the algebra is a notation for matrices. It states the convention in the article: the chain is canonical up to the choice of Pauli basis, and no physical prediction depends on the choice.

4. The Matrix Model. The object is the $2\times2$ array. The claim is that the model is a faithful display that loses exactly two things: the distinction between the scale and the phase, which are both a modulus, and the distinction between the two walls, which both become a degenerate determinant. It must not claim that the matrix is the algebra.

13. The Two Casimirs of the Lorentz Algebra. The object is a label. The claim is that two numbers classify an irreducible representation and that the two numbers are the Casimirs of the two $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ halves. Verified: with $\vec J_{\pm}=(\vec J\pm i\vec K)/2$ the two triples commute and each closes as $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; $\vec J^{2}-\vec K^{2}=2(\vec J_{+}^{2}+\vec J_{-}^{2})$ and $(\vec J\cdot\vec K+\vec K\cdot\vec J)/2=i(\vec J_{+}^{2}-\vec J_{-}^{2})$, both invariant; on the vector representation $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ the first has the eigenvalue $-2\cdot\tfrac32$ in the sign convention of the check and the second has eigenvalue $0$. The article must fix its own normalization, because the factors $2$ and $i$ are conventional, and the review's form $m(m+1)\pm n(n+1)$ is that pair only after the factor is fixed. It must also say what the two Casimirs cannot do: they do not separate $(\tfrac12,0)$ from $(0,\tfrac12)$ unless the sign of one of them is used, and they are not the Poincar\'e Casimirs.

14. The Cartan Subalgebra, the Roots and the Three Real Forms. The object is the signature. The claim is that the Lorentz algebra, the Euclidean algebra and the split algebra are three real slices of one complex algebra, and that the physical choice of signature is a choice of slice. This is the article that makes The SO(4) Route intelligible. It must not claim that the three slices are equivalent physically.

15. The Exponential Map from Algebra to Group. The object is the group element reached from the identity. The claim is that the exponential is surjective and that the elements needing two generators are exactly the non-normal ones in the group of invertibles. It carries the logarithm, the branch and the fibre. Verified: $\lVert\exp\log A-A\rVert\le1.1\times10^{-15}$ on five elements, including the Jordan one.

28. $P$, $T$ and the Representation Labels. The object is the label under a reflection. The claim is that parity exchanges $(m,n)$ with $(n,m)$, which is why the Dirac pair is the minimal parity-invariant fermion and why chirality is not defined for the full group. Everything else about the discrete symmetries is already written: the operators in The Reflection and the Rotation in Biquaternionic Form, charge conjugation in Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass, the combined statement in The CPT Theorem, and the order-four quotient — which is the four components of $O(1,3)$ — in Parra's Four Options. This entry adds the exchange of labels and nothing else.

32. Wigner's Classification. The object is a momentum. The claim is that mass is the orbit and spin is the little-group label, and that the algebra's finite carriers are not the physical modules. It separates what the corpus proves — the sign of $P^{2}$ from the Casimir, the little-group structure, the weight — from what it imports, the unitary representation theory of the little groups. The spacelike orbit is a closing subsection, not an entry of its own: $SU(1,1)$, the continuous series, and the plain statement that no tachyon is observed.

33. The Little Group of a Massive Particle. The object is the rest frame. The claim is that the stabiliser of a timelike momentum is $SU(2)$ and that the boost is the transport whose failure to be a homomorphism is the Wigner rotation. It is the article the Thomas exercise assumes.

34. The Little Group of a Null Momentum. The object is a null momentum, and the split is nearly new because the survey's section is a list of stabilisers with no proof. The claim is that the stabiliser is $ISO(2)$, that the two translations act trivially on a finite-helicity state, and that the compact part supplies the helicity. It hands the label to Helicity in Biquaternionic Form and takes only the group.

38. Aberration and the Sky. The object is the sky. The claim is that a boost is a Möbius flow on the sphere of null directions, with two fixed points, and that the aberration formula is that flow written in an angle. It extends The Celestial Sphere rather than repeating it: the one builds the coordinates and this one moves them.

39. The Invariant Measure on the Mass Shell. The object is the volume of momentum space. The claim is that $d^{3}p/(2E)$ is invariant, a statement about the group that the corpus assumes everywhere and proves nowhere.

Three statements of the analysis are corrected rather than carried.

The eigenvalues are not the two chiral halves. The eigenvalues $\lambda_{\pm}$ of $M(\tilde Q)$ are the two weights of the defining representation $\mathbb{C}^{2}$. The two chiral halves are $(\tfrac12,0)$ and $(0,\tfrac12)$, which are different representations of the same group, and the eigenvalues are data shared by both. The correct statement is that the two eigenlines are the two weight lines of the defining representation, and that the two chiral halves are read on the two-sided action.

Semisimplicity is a property of the element, not of a representation. "$B^{2}\neq0$ is a semisimple representation" should read "$B^{2}\neq0$ is a semisimple element". A non-semisimple element carries a Jordan block, and every finite-dimensional representation of the group is semisimple because the group is reductive; what can fail to be semisimple is the element, not the representation.

The word does not cover $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ alone. The four-factor word includes the scale and the phase, so the group it generates is $GL_2(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cdot SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, not $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$. The two factors are not a direct product: $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cap SL(2,\mathbb{C})=\{\pm I\}$, and correspondingly the data cover $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ exactly twice, since $(r,\theta,\tilde U)$ and $(r,\theta+\pi,-\tilde U)$ are the same matrix. That redundancy is the two-to-one cover of entry 10 appearing one level down, at the level of the coordinates rather than the group. The Cartan statement is the unit-norm case, where the scale and the phase are trivial.

The Three Interactions in Pati–Salam Model: A Translation

Introduction

The question. Can electromagnetism, the weak interaction and the strong interaction be written in the Pati–Salam formalism rather than the Standard Model one, and how much of the corpus's language does that recover?

The answer, in four lines. Yes, completely, and the translation is not a reinterpretation but a substitution of a group for a group. Every Standard Model object has an address in $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y\ \subset\ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R,\qquad 12\ \subset\ 21 . $$

The translation is exact above the Pati–Salam scale and reduces to the Standard Model below it, so nothing measured changes. What changes is the origin of four things the Standard Model imports: the number of colours, the quantisation of charge, the hypercharge assignments, and the cancellation of anomalies. All four become consequences of one group instead of four independent facts.

The price, named at the start. The translation rests on one identification that the corpus has explicitly refused to make: its own $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, the group of the carrier $\mathbb{B}$, is not yet the Pati–Salam $\mathrm{SU}(4)_c$. §1.3 states the refusal, states the one argument that could overturn it, and proceeds conditionally. Everything after §1.3 is a translation under that declaration, and it is flagged as such.

What the note delivers. §2 to §4 translate the three interactions one at a time. §5 gives the couplings. §6 is the dictionary, in one table. §7 shows the anomalies collapsing. §8 is the ledger of what the translation changes in the corpus's own accounting. §9 is what it cannot do.

1. The Frame and the One Declaration

1.1 The group and its fifteen

The group.

$$ G_{PS}=\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R,\qquad \dim=15+3+3=21,\qquad \mathrm{rank}=3+1+1=5 . $$

The fifteen, split by what each part does. Under $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ the adjoint decomposes as

$$ \mathbf{15}=\mathbf{8}_{0}\oplus\mathbf{1}_{0}\oplus\mathbf{3}_{+}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-},\qquad 15=8+1+6, $$

and the four pieces are the four objects of the strong and lepton sectors:

piece count what it is $B-L$
$\mathbf8_0$ $8$ the gluons $0$
$\mathbf1_0$ $1$ the $B-L$ gauge boson $0$
$\mathbf3_{+}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-}$ $6$ the leptoquark bosons, colour triplet and antitriplet carried

So the gluons and the leptoquarks are one multiplet. They are fifteen generators of one simple algebra, split only after the breaking. This is the single most consequential sentence in the translation and §2 returns to it.

The matter. One generation is two multiplets, and the second is the conjugate of the first:

$$ \Psi_L=(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1),\qquad \Psi_R=(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2),\qquad 8+8=16 . $$

The conjugate is the corpus's involution. $\Psi_R$ is not an independent object; it is $\Psi_L$ conjugated and given the other $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The corpus writes transposition and conjugation as an involution $*$ between the two sides, and here the same involution appears as the bar on the $\mathbf4$. The two chiralities are the two tensor factors, and the bar is the swap.

1.2 The corpus's $D$ is $B-L$

The corpus already displays the generator. SU4-meaning.md writes the abelian direction inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ as

$$ D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1), $$

the relative phase of the unit and the vector part. In the ordering where $e_t$ is the lepton, this is $-3$ times the Pati–Salam $B-L$:

$$ B-L=-\tfrac13D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13),\qquad \operatorname{tr}\bigl((B-L)^{2}\bigr)=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(D^{2})=\tfrac{12}{9}=\tfrac43 , $$

and the tracelessness that makes $D$ traceless is exactly the tracelessness that quantises charge. The corpus's generator and the Pati–Salam generator are the same matrix up to a factor of three and a sign, and the factor of three is the number of colours.

That coincidence is the hook. It is not a derivation, because §1.3 explains why it cannot be one. It is the reason the translation is worth writing.

1.3 The declaration, and why the unit line forces it

What the corpus says, in its own words. SU4-meaning.md is explicit:

It is not $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{c}$. […] The dimension agrees with the fifteen of this note and nothing else does: the carrier here is $\mathbb{B}$, the four basis vectors are $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, and the subgroup singled out by the algebra is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ at the unit, not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ at a colour. An identification of the two would be a claim about physics that this note does not make and the corpus does not support.

The refusal is correct and the note must not pretend otherwise. A dimension count cannot identify two groups. What follows is therefore a translation under an assumption, and the assumption must be stated before it is used.

The assumption, in one line.

(A) The fourth colour of the Pati–Salam quartet is the unit line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ of $\mathbb{B}$, so the lepton is the state that sits on the canonical line.

The argument for it, which is structural and not verbal. The corpus proves three facts, and together they are the whole of the Pati–Salam structure:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(3)=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(e_t),\qquad \mathrm{SU}(4)\big/\mathrm{SU}(3)\cong S^{7},\qquad \mathrm{SU}(4)\big/S(\mathrm{U}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1))\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}. $$

The first says the colour group is the stabiliser of the fourth direction. The second says the carrier splits as a singlet plus a triplet. The third says the space of possible fourth directions is $\mathbb{CP}^3$, the quotient that separates Pati–Salam from left–right.

Now add the fact from SU3-meaning.md: the line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is the only line fixed by every automorphism. Every other line — every axis, every direction in the vector part — is moved. So the question "which of the four is the lepton" has, in this algebra, exactly one frame-free answer:

$$ \text{the lepton must be the state on } \mathbb{C}e_t,\qquad \text{because it is the only line that no automorphism moves.} $$

That is the strongest available statement, and it is still an assumption. It says the identification is forced once one accepts that the lepton's line must be canonical; it does not prove that the fourth colour is a line in $\mathbb{B}$ at all. A derivation would need a map from $\mathbb{C}^4_{\text{colour}}$ to $\mathbb{B}$ that intertwines the two $\mathrm{SU}(3)$'s, and no such map exists in the corpus. The corpus note says the embedding fails, and this note agrees with it.

Everything below is written under (A). Where a statement depends on it, the dependence is stated.

2. The Strong Interaction in Pati–Salam Model

2.1 Gluons are eight of the fifteen

The statement. The gluon field is not a separate object with its own group. It is the part of the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ gauge field that leaves the fourth direction alone:

$$ A_\mu=A_\mu^{a}T^{a}_{(4)},\qquad a=1,\dots,15,\qquad \underbrace{A^{1..8}}_{\text{gluons}}\oplus\underbrace{A^{15}}_{B-L}\oplus\underbrace{A^{9..14}}_{\text{leptoquarks}} . $$

The covariant derivative, in full.

$$ D_\mu=\partial_\mu-ig_4A^{a}_\mu T^{a}_{(4)}-ig_LW^{i}_{L\mu}\tau_i-ig_RW^{i}_{R\mu}\tau_i , $$

and the first term is the whole of the strong and lepton-number physics, one coupling, one multiplet. There is no separate $g_3$ in the high-energy theory; there is $g_4$, and $g_3$ is its low-energy name.

What the strong interaction is, in this language. It is the residual of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ after the fourth direction is fixed:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\ \xrightarrow{\ \langle(4,1,2)_H\rangle\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L},\qquad \mathbf{4}=\mathbf{3}\oplus\mathbf{1}. $$

The eight gluons are the stabiliser; the six leptoquarks acquire mass and leave; the one $B-L$ boson remains and mixes into hypercharge. Colour confinement is unchanged: it is a statement about $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ at low energy, and the low-energy group is the same group.

2.2 Why there are exactly eight

The Standard Model answer. Because $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has $3^{2}-1=8$ generators. It is a fact about a group that is put in.

The Pati–Salam answer. Because

$$ 8=15-1-6, $$

the dimension of the algebra, less the abelian direction, less the six that move the fourth index into a colour index. And the three is not independent either:

$$ N_c=4-1=3 . $$

The honest status of that. It explains why the number of colours is one less than the number of colours-plus-lepton, which is a genuine relation: it says the strong group is the stabiliser of a line and the line is one of the four. It does not explain why the quartet has four members. So the translation replaces "eight because $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has eight generators" with "eight because fifteen, less one, less six", and replaces "three colours" with "four minus one". That is a real gain in unity and not a gain in number.

3. The Electroweak Interaction in Pati–Salam Model

3.1 The doublets

The left multiplet. $\Psi_L=(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ is a doublet of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and a singlet of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$. Its $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ indices are the ordinary weak ones:

$$ \Psi_L=\begin{pmatrix} u_L^{r}&u_L^{g}&u_L^{b}&\nu_L\\ d_L^{r}&d_L^{g}&d_L^{b}&e_L\end{pmatrix}, $$

two rows, four columns, the four columns being the quartet. The lepton doublet is a column of the same matrix as the quark doublets, and that is the literal content of "lepton number is the fourth colour".

The right multiplet. $\Psi_R=(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ is the conjugate, and it is a doublet of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$:

$$ \Psi_R=\begin{pmatrix} u_R^{r}&u_R^{g}&u_R^{b}&\nu_R\\ d_R^{r}&d_R^{g}&d_R^{b}&e_R\end{pmatrix}. $$

The right-handed neutrino is not an addition. It is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of $u_R$ in the same way that $e_R$ is the partner of $d_R$. This is the structural difference from the Standard Model, in one sentence: the Standard Model has to add $\nu_R$ as a singlet by hand, and Pati–Salam has nowhere else to put it.

3.2 The charge formula, and hypercharge demoted

The formula.

$$ Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L),\qquad Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L),\qquad Q=T_{3L}+Y . $$

Hypercharge is no longer fundamental. It is a combination of two generators of the high-energy group, one from each factor. In the Standard Model $Y$ is a free $\mathrm{U}(1)$ whose assignments are chosen to fit the data and checked against anomalies; here it is the diagonal survivor of the broken $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ mixed with $B-L$, and its assignments follow from the multiplet.

Pati–Salam changes the multiplets and leaves the arithmetic alone.

3.3 The two-step breaking and the boson masses

The chain.

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R \ \xrightarrow{\ \langle(4,1,2)_H\rangle\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{\ \langle(1,2,2)_H\rangle\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}} . $$

The first step uses a scalar in the $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ with a vacuum value along the fourth entry, $\langle(4,1,2)_H\rangle\propto\operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,1)$. It breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and gives mass to the six leptoquarks.

The second step uses a bidoublet $(1,2,2)_H$, whose two vacuum values break $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\to\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ and give the $W$ and $Z$ their masses. The bidoublet is the object the corpus cannot build, because a spin-zero carrier is a line and a line cannot carry two indices; §9.

The $Z$ is a mixture of three neutral fields, $W^3_L$, $W^3_R$ and $B$, and the mixing is fixed by the charge formula. That is the content of §4.1.

3.4 Custodial symmetry, promoted

In the Standard Model the custodial $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$ is an accidental global symmetry of the scalar potential. Nothing requires it; the doublet happens to have it, and that is why $\rho=1$ to a part in a thousand.

In Pati–Salam the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is a gauge symmetry, and the custodial $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$ is its diagonal, which survives the breaking. So $\rho=1$ stops being an accident and becomes a consequence of a symmetry that was gauged and then broken.

What it costs. The minimal bidoublet does not by itself give $\rho=1$ for arbitrary vacuum alignment; the usual repair is a triplet of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, which is a further scalar. So the promotion of custodial symmetry from accidental to structural is real, and it is not free. A gain in principle, paid in scalars — which is the pattern of this whole translation.

4. Electromagnetism in Pati–Salam Model

4.1 The unbroken generator and the photon as a mixture

The unbroken direction. $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ is generated by $Q$ of §3.2, and it is the only generator that annihilates both vacuum values. The photon is the corresponding gauge field, a mixture of the three neutral ones:

$$ A_\mu=c_LW^{3}_{L\mu}+c_RW^{3}_{R\mu}+c_BB_\mu,\qquad c_L=\frac{e}{g_L},\quad c_R=\frac{e}{g_R},\quad c_B=\frac{e}{2g_{B-L}} . $$

Normalisation gives the electric charge in terms of the three couplings:

$$ \frac{1}{e^{2}}=\frac{1}{g_L^{2}}+\frac{1}{g_R^{2}}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^{2}},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \frac1{e^2}=\frac1{g_L^2}+\frac1{g_Y^2},\qquad \frac1{g_Y^2}=\frac1{g_R^2}+\frac1{4g_{B-L}^2}. $$

The two forms agree, and that agreement is a check. The first writes the photon as a mixture of three fields; the second uses hypercharge as the intermediate step. They are the same statement because $Q=T_{3L}+Y$.

The two massive neutral combinations are orthogonal to the photon and are the $Z$ and the $Z_R$. So the Standard Model's $Z$ is one of three neutral states in Pati–Salam, and the count $3\to2+1$ is the count of the two-step breaking.

4.2 Charge quantisation, derived

This is the deepest gain of the translation, and it is one line.

In the Standard Model the hypercharges are assigned by hand. The assignment is constrained by anomaly cancellation, which fixes the ratios, but the overall normalisation is free, and the fact that the charges come out in exact thirds is a numerical accident of the assignment.

In Pati–Salam $B-L$ is a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, hence traceless on the quartet:

$$ \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0 . $$

That single equation is charge quantisation. Read it as a relation between the quark and lepton charges:

$$ 3q+\ell=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=-\tfrac13\ell , $$

so the quark charge is forced to be a third of the lepton charge, and the fractional charges are not chosen but computed from the tracelessness of a diagonal matrix. The corpus's own display is the same equation: $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ is traceless, and $-3+1+1+1=0$ is "$-3$ and three ones", which is "$-1$ and three thirds".

So the translation answers a question the Standard Model does not ask. "Why is the proton charge exactly minus the electron charge?" Standard Model: because the hypercharge assignments make it so. Pati–Salam: because the generator that distinguishes the fourth direction from the third is traceless, and the fourth direction carries one unit while the three carry a third each.

5. The Couplings and the Boundary Conditions

At the Pati–Salam scale the four couplings collapse to two, or to one.

$$ g_3(M_{PS})=g_4(M_{PS}),\qquad g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})\equiv g_4(M_{PS})\quad\text{(if parity is imposed)} . $$

The $B-L$ coupling is not free either, because $B-L$ sits inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$:

$$ g_{B-L}(M_{PS})=\sqrt{\tfrac38}\,g_4(M_{PS})=0.612\,g_4(M_{PS}),\qquad \text{with }\operatorname{tr}(T^{a}T^{b})=\tfrac12\delta^{ab}. $$

The check of that number is the Weinberg angle. With $g_L=g_R=g_4$ and the $B-L$ value above, the formulas of §4.1 give

$$ \sin^{2}\theta_W(M_{PS})=\frac{3}{8}, $$

and the same value follows from the trace ratio, independently of any normalisation:

$$ \sin^{2}\theta_W=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(T_{3L}^{2})}{\operatorname{tr}(T_{3L}^{2})+\operatorname{tr}(Y^{2})}=\frac{2}{2+\tfrac{10}{3}}=\frac{6}{16}=\frac38 . $$

The counting behind the $2$ and the $\tfrac{10}{3}$.

$$ \operatorname{tr}(T_{3L}^{2})=\underbrace{8\cdot\tfrac14}_{8\ \text{doublet states}}=2,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(Y^{2})=\underbrace{\tfrac{6}{36}}_{Q_L}+\underbrace{\tfrac{4}{3}}_{u_R}+\underbrace{\tfrac13}_{d_R}+\underbrace{\tfrac12}_{L_L}+\underbrace{1}_{e_R}=\tfrac{10}{3} . $$

What $3/8$ is and is not. It is a boundary condition at $M_{PS}$, not a prediction at accessible energy. Running it down to $M_Z$ reproduces $0.231$ only after the intermediate thresholds are chosen, so the theory trades a sharp boundary condition for two unknown scales. This is the same trade $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ makes, and it is the honest way to state it.

7. Anomalies: Four Conditions Become None

The Standard Model's four conditions. With $U(1)_Y$ the only abelian factor, consistency requires

$$ \sum Y=0,\qquad \sum Y^{3}=0,\qquad \sum_{\mathbf3}Y=0,\qquad \sum_{\mathbf2}Y=0 , $$

the gravitational, cubic, $\mathrm{SU}(3)^{2}U(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)^{2}U(1)$ conditions, and they hold only for the specific hypercharge table. They are a miracle of the assignment: nothing forces the table, and the table happens to pass.

In Pati–Salam all four hold identically, and the reason is that the sixteen is built from a $\mathbf4$ and a $\bar{\mathbf4}$:

The cubic one is the sharpest. In the Standard Model $\sum Y^{3}=0$ requires the hypercharges of quarks and leptons to balance in a specific way, and the balance is only visible after the table is written. Here it cancels between the quartet and the antiquartet before any table is written, and it cancels because $\nu_R$ is present. Remove $\nu_R$ and the quartet is incomplete and every line of the table fails.

So the translation makes anomaly cancellation structural. That is the fourth of the four status changes of §6, and it is the one that requires no assumption beyond (A): it is a statement about the multiplet, and the multiplet is the definition of the theory.

The scalar sector is worse because Pati–Salam needs two scalar multiplets where the Standard Model needs one, and the corpus's obstruction counts multiplets.

9. What the Translation Cannot Do

Three things, and the first two are the corpus's own walls.

It cannot build the chiral doublet. The corpus proves that a chiral non-abelian one-sided multiplication does not exist, because the centraliser of a chirality-respecting one-sided operator is abelian. Pati–Salam needs $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ to act on different letters, and the translation does not supply the map from a side to a letter. The wall is exactly where it was. Pati–Salam is a better address for the group; it is not a construction of the group.

It cannot build the scalar. Worse: the bidoublet $(1,2,2)_H$ carries two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ indices and a spin-zero carrier in the corpus is a line. A line cannot carry two indices, so Pati–Salam's scalar requirement is strictly larger than the Standard Model's, and the corpus's obstruction is hit harder.

It cannot repair the mass relation. One bidoublet gives $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$ at $M_{PS}$, and the observed $m_d/m_e\simeq2.2$ contradicts the first. The translation therefore imports a false prediction that the Standard Model does not make, and the repair costs an extra scalar and the clean boundary condition.

And one thing it cannot decide. The scale $M_{PS}$ is a parameter. The leptoquark bounds put it above roughly $10^{2}$ to $10^{3}$ TeV; nothing in the corpus puts it anywhere.

What the translation does not need. No new algebra, no exceptional structure, no octonion, no dimension beyond the four the carrier already has. The translation is a change of gauge group inside objects the corpus already owns, which is exactly why it is worth doing and exactly why it cannot be a derivation.

10. Verdict

Yes, and the translation is worth making, with one declaration and one accounting.

The declaration is (A): the fourth colour is the unit line. It is the only line in the algebra that no automorphism moves, so it is the only frame-free candidate for the object that distinguishes the lepton from the quark. The corpus refuses the identification, and this note agrees that it is a physics claim rather than a theorem, so the translation is a programme and not a result.

The accounting is §8's ledger: four rows improve, four are unchanged, two get worse. Net plus two, and the two losses are the two the corpus already could not fix.

What the corpus gains, stated as three sentences.

Hypercharge stops being fundamental: it is $T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, and the assignments follow from the multiplet rather than being fitted.

Charge quantisation stops being an accident: it is $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$, which is the corpus's own tracelessness of $\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$.

The anomalies stop being a miracle: they cancel between the $\mathbf4$ and the $\bar{\mathbf4}$, and the $\bar{\mathbf4}$ is the conjugate, which is the corpus's involution.

What the corpus does not gain, stated as two sentences.

The chiral wall is untouched: Pati–Salam names the two chiralities and the corpus still cannot tie a chirality to a side.

The scalar carrier is untouched and more strained: the bidoublet is a two-index object and the corpus's spin-zero carrier is a line.

The one-sentence verdict. The three interactions translate completely, three couplings become one, four imported facts become derived, and the corpus's two walls stand exactly where they stood — so the translation is a better description of the gauge sector and no progress at all on the sector the corpus lacks.

11. The Pati–Salam Migration

What this section is. A plan for the corpus, not a plan for this article. It says which categories are renamed, which sections are created, which articles move, which are rewritten, which are added, and in what order. Every number below was measured on the tree today: twelve categories and $663$ linked articles in physics.md, of which $93$ are in Biquaternion Particle Physics and $29$ in Biquaternion Electromagnetism. The plan reduces the twelve to eleven, by dissolving the second of those into the first.

The governing sentence, and it is a freeze. The frame changes the menu and the framing; it does not change the physics below the Pati–Salam scale. Everything measured below $M_{PS}$ is exact in the Standard Model's variables and stays written in them. A migration that rewrote $663$ articles in $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ language would be a fabrication of twelve hundred pages of algebra that is already correct.

11.9 What the Migration Buys, and What It Costs

The buy, in four lines, each already computed in this note.

The corpus stops describing nature with a group it cannot build: $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is the group of its own carrier.

Hypercharge becomes a combination instead of an import, $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$.

Charge quantisation becomes tracelessness, $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=3\cdot\tfrac13-1=0$, which is the corpus's $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$.

Four anomaly conditions become none, and the cubic one cancels as $-\tfrac29+\tfrac29=0$ between the quartet and its conjugate: $-\tfrac29$ from $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$, $+\tfrac29$ from $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$.

The cost, in four lines.

Eleven new articles, eight renames and five text repairs, which is a month of careful writing and not a weekend.

One assumption becomes load-bearing: (A), the fourth colour as the unit line. Everything in phases $1$ to $5$ leans on it, and if it is wrong the corpus has a renamed menu and a false frame.

One category leaves the menu, so the corpus's most common entry point becomes a section rather than a page; the section title, its first sub-section and the heading id pay for it, and the price is real (§11.2).

Two walls do not move. The chiral carrier and the spin-zero carrier are exactly where they were, so the corpus's two deepest obstructions survive a migration that is otherwise its largest structural gain.

The verdict on the migration. Worth doing, in this order, with the declaration first and the menu last. The corpus already knows how to run a migration of this shape — the Lagrangian one renamed sixty articles and rebuilt a family under a freeze sentence, and the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ one renamed three and rewrote twenty-eight. This one is smaller than the first and larger than the second, and it has the same discipline: one freeze sentence, one ledger, one check per phase.

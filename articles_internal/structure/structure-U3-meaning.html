The Meaning of $\mathrm{U}(3)$ in the Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra

Introduction

The question. The Meaning of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ in the Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra ends with a negative result: inside $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ no basis of the Cartan can treat the three axes $x,y,z$ alike, because the three weights would have to be real, of equal modulus, and sum to zero, which forces all three to vanish. This note asks the next question. Drop tracelessness, keep anti-Hermiticity, and the corner widens by exactly one dimension. What is that dimension, what group does it give, and does it buy the missing symmetry?

The answer in one paragraph. The corner gains one dimension and no more: the centre, spanned by $iP_V=i\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1,1)$. The group becomes $\mathrm{U}(3)$, the stabiliser of the unit vector $e_t$ inside $\mathrm{U}(4)$ rather than inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, with $\mathrm{SU}(3)=\ker\det$. The new direction is central, so it is invisible in the adjoint action, and it is not a derivation of the algebra, so it acts on the carrier and not on the multiplication. What it does buy is real but structural rather than dynamical: with the trace constraint released, the diagonal space becomes three-dimensional, the cyclic permutation $P$ of the axes acts on a nine-element basis in three free orbits of size three, and a symmetric triple of equal-weight generators $\{u_x,u_y,u_z\}$ exists, with $u_x+u_y+u_z=iP_V$.

Three claims, in advance. First, the trade is exactly one dimension for the symmetry of the basis, and the extra dimension is the one that cannot be built from two words. Second, the symmetry itself was never broken: $P$ is an element of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and what $\mathrm{U}(3)$ restores is the visibility of that symmetry in a basis, not the symmetry. Third, the basis so obtained is $\mathbb Z_3$-equivariant and not axis-free; the only genuinely axis-free statements remain the orbit decomposition and the symmetric functions.

The carrier, as in the companion notes. $\mathbb{B}$ has basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ with $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$ cyclically and $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$. An operator is written in the sixteen Conway words $e_\mu[\,]e_\nu=L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$, the length $\ell$ is the number of nonzero coefficients, and the corner is cut out by three conditions: anti-Hermitian, killing $e_t$, preserving $B_V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}(\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$.

The Corner, Widened by One

The four cases. With the Hermitian form $\langle X,Y\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(XY^{*})$ fixed, and the display $\operatorname{diag}(0,R)$ on $\mathbb C e_t\oplus B_V$ with $R\in M_3(\mathbb C)$:

dropped condition algebra display group $\dim_{\mathbb R}$
nothing $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ $R$ anti-Hermitian, $\operatorname{tr}R=0$ $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ $8$
tracelessness $\mathfrak u(3)$ $R$ anti-Hermitian $\mathrm{U}(3)$ $9$
anti-Hermiticity $\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb C)$ $\operatorname{tr}R=0$ $\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb C)$ $16$
both $\mathfrak{gl}(3,\mathbb C)$ none $\mathrm{GL}(3,\mathbb C)$ $18$

This note is the second row. The decomposition is a direct sum of Lie algebras and is the whole content of the change:

$$ \mathfrak u(3)=\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathbb R\,iP_V,\qquad \mathrm{U}(3)/\mathrm{SU}(3)\cong\mathrm{U}(1),\qquad \mathrm{SU}(3)=\ker\det . $$

The group name, precisely. $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is the stabiliser of the vector $e_t$ inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, of real dimension fifteen; $\mathrm{U}(3)$ is the stabiliser of the same vector inside $\mathrm{U}(4)$, of real dimension sixteen. The corner is the same corner, cut one condition wider, and the group is the same group, one central circle larger. Note that it is the stabiliser of the vector and not of the line: the stabiliser of the line $\mathbb C e_t$ in $\mathrm{U}(4)$ is $\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3)$, of dimension ten, and the extra circle there moves the unit, which the corner forbids.

Why $\mathrm{U}(3)$ is not inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. For $g=\operatorname{diag}(1,G)$ the determinant is $\det g=\det G$, which is an arbitrary unit modulus. So the determinant map $\mathrm{U}(3)\to\mathrm{U}(1)$ is onto and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is its kernel; the missing direction $\mathbb R I_3$ is the one that changes the determinant without changing anything else. This single sentence is the difference between the two groups.

The New Element: $iP_V$

The element. Put $P_V=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1,1)$, the orthogonal projector onto $B_V$, and

$$ iP_V=\operatorname{diag}(0,i,i,i)=\tfrac{i}{4}\bigl(3E_t+E_x+E_y+E_z\bigr),\qquad E_\mu=L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\mu}. $$

The four-word form is the shortest possible: $E_t=\mathrm{I}$, and $E_x+E_y+E_z=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$, so $3E_t+\sum_aE_a=4P_V$ and no coefficient vanishes. Verified: coefficients $\tfrac34,\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14$ times $i$, length $4$, trace $3i$, Hilbert–Schmidt norm squared $3$.

What it does. $\exp(\theta\,iP_V)=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\theta},e^{i\theta},e^{i\theta})$: the unit is fixed, and the whole vector part receives one common phase. That is the only new one-parameter family, and it is a scalar on $B_V$.

What it is not. It is central in $\mathfrak u(3)$, verified, so it commutes with all eight generators and contributes nothing to the adjoint action: the derived algebra of $\mathfrak u(3)$ is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, and the centre is one-dimensional. It is also not a derivation of the algebra. Every inner derivation is $L_{\tilde Q}-R_{\tilde Q}$, every such operator is traceless, and $\operatorname{tr}iP_V=3i$; so $iP_V\ne L_{\tilde Q}-R_{\tilde Q}$ for every $\tilde Q$, and since $\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb{B})=\mathbb B/\mathbb C e_t$ is exhausted by the inner ones, $iP_V$ does not act on the multiplication at all. It rescales the carrier and nothing else.

Why the Trace Was the Obstruction

The arithmetic, once. Every diagonal element of the corner reads $i\sum_a\alpha_aE_a$ with $\alpha_a\in\mathbb C$ and the unit entry $-i\sum_a\alpha_a$, which the corner sets to zero. Splitting off the centre as $\mu\,iP_V$ with $\mu$ real gives the display $i\operatorname{diag}(0,\ \mu-2\alpha_x,\ \mu-2\alpha_y,\ \mu-2\alpha_z)$ with $\sum_a\alpha_a=0$. The three moduli $\lvert\mu-2\alpha_a\rvert$ are the weights the element gives the three axes.

  • In $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ one has $\mu=0$ and $\alpha_a$ real, so the weights are $2\lvert\alpha_a\rvert$ with $\sum_a\alpha_a=0$: equal moduli force all three to vanish. That is the obstruction, and it is arithmetic, not geometric.
  • In $\mathfrak u(3)$ the parameter $\mu$ is free, and equal moduli become $\mu-2\alpha_a=\pm r$ for one common $r>0$. Since $\sum_a\alpha_a=0$ gives $\mu=\tfrac13\sum_a(\mu-2\alpha_a)=\tfrac r3\sum_as_a$, every sign pattern is a solution, and each line appears with both overall signs, so the eight patterns collapse to exactly four lines:
signs $(\mu-2\alpha_x,\mu-2\alpha_y,\mu-2\alpha_z)$ $\mu$ $(\alpha_x,\alpha_y,\alpha_z)$ element
$(+,+,+)$ $r$ $(0,0,0)$ $r\,iP_V$
$(-,+,+)$ $\tfrac r3$ $(\tfrac{2r}{3},-\tfrac r3,-\tfrac r3)$ $r\,u_x$
$(+,-,+)$ $\tfrac r3$ $(-\tfrac r3,\tfrac{2r}{3},-\tfrac r3)$ $r\,u_y$
$(+,+,-)$ $\tfrac r3$ $(-\tfrac r3,-\tfrac r3,\tfrac{2r}{3})$ $r\,u_z$

The all-negative pattern is $-r\,iP_V$, the same line as the first, and each triple row likewise has its negative. So the diagonal part of $\mathfrak u(3)$ is three-dimensional — as many dimensions as there are axes — and it contains precisely four equal-weight lines, one central and three transversal. Lifting to $\mathfrak u(3)=\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$ releases the trace constraint, opens the three-dimensional diagonal space, and the cyclic permutation $x\mapsto y\mapsto z\mapsto x$ acts there freely, with one fixed line (the centre) and three lines in a single orbit.

The Symmetric Nine

The basis. Three families of three, each a single orbit of the cyclic permutation:

family elements words block trace
rotations $A_a=L_{\vec e_a}-R_{\vec e_a}$ $2$ real antisymmetric, $2$ in two slots $0$
symmetric sums $S_{ab}=-\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(L_{\vec e_a}R_{\vec e_b}+L_{\vec e_b}R_{\vec e_a}\bigr)$ $2$ $i$ in two slots $0$
equal-weight diagonals $u_a=\tfrac{i}{4}\bigl(E_t+3E_a-E_b-E_c\bigr)$ $4$ $i\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1)$ cyclically $i$

The triple, explicitly. With $(a,b,c)$ cyclic,

$$ u_a=\tfrac{i}{4}\bigl(E_t+3E_a-E_b-E_c\bigr)=K_a+\tfrac{i}{3}P_V, \qquad u_x+u_y+u_z=iP_V , $$

and on $B_V$

$$ u_x=i\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1),\qquad u_y=i\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1),\qquad u_z=i\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1). $$

The three axis entries of every $u_a$ have modulus $1$, the axis block has determinant $4$, and the triple is independent although it sums to the centre.

The word counts are the price list. Two is the floor and four is the ceiling of the Conway bound. Rotations and symmetric sums sit at the floor; the three equal-weight diagonals sit at the ceiling. There is no three-word symmetric diagonal in this normalisation, and there is no two-word one: the symmetric direction is exactly the direction that needs all four axis words. Nothing is free; the symmetry of the basis is bought with the longest elements in the algebra.

The frame is orthogonal and tetrahedral. The Hilbert–Schmidt Gram matrix of the nine is block diagonal,

$$ G_A=8\,\mathrm{I}_3,\qquad G_S=2\,\mathrm{I}_3,\qquad G_u=4\,\mathrm{I}_3-J_3 , $$

so the three families are mutually orthogonal, and inside the third family $\langle u_a,u_a\rangle=3$ while $\langle u_a,u_b\rangle=-1$, a cosine of $-\tfrac13$. That is the tetrahedral angle $109.47^{\circ}$, not the $120^{\circ}$ of the triples $h_a$ and $K_a$, and the reason is visible: $h_x+h_y+h_z=0$ and $K_x+K_y+K_z=0$ lie in a plane, while $u_x+u_y+u_z=iP_V$ points out of it. The centre is the fourth vertex of the tetrahedron.

The Group Law in the Nine-Element Basis

The brackets close on the nine, with every constant in $\{0,\pm\tfrac12,\pm1,\pm2,\pm4\}$. With $(a,b,c)$ cyclic, $S_{ab}$ the symmetric sum on the pair and $S_{\hat a}$ the symmetric sum on the two axes other than $a$:

$$ [A_a,A_b]=2\varepsilon_{abc}A_c,\qquad [A_a,S_{ab}]=2S_{ac},\qquad [A_a,S_{ac}]=-2S_{ab}, $$ $$ [A_a,S_{bc}]=2\bigl(u_c-u_b\bigr),\qquad [S_{ab},S_{ac}]=\tfrac12A_a,\qquad [S_{ab},S_{bc}]=-\tfrac12A_b, $$ $$ [A_a,u_b]=-4S_{bc},\qquad [A_a,u_c]=+4S_{bc},\qquad [A_a,u_a]=0, $$ $$ [S_{ab},u_a]=+A_c,\qquad [S_{ab},u_b]=-A_c,\qquad [S_{ab},u_c]=0,\qquad [u_a,u_b]=0 . $$

Three things are worth reading off. The rotations act on the Cartan diagonally, $[A_a,u_b]=0$ exactly when $a=b$, with a single constant $4$ instead of the mixed $-4,+2$ of the $h$ basis. The symmetric sums also act sparsely, $[S_{ab},u_c]=0$ unless $c$ is in the pair, again with one constant instead of $-\tfrac12,+1$. And the Cartan part is abelian, so the nine split as $3+3+3$ with the third family carrying the centre.

The Cartan in the new letters. From $u_a-u_b=2h_c$,

$$ h_c=\tfrac12\bigl(u_a-u_b\bigr),\qquad (a,b,c)\ \text{cyclic}, $$

so the traceless part of the triple is the $K$ triple of the companion note, $K_a=u_a-\tfrac13iP_V$, and

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)=\operatorname{span}\{A_a,S_{ab}\}\oplus\operatorname{span}\{u_x-u_y,\ u_y-u_z\},\qquad \mathfrak u(3)=\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathbb R\,iP_V . $$

The equal-weight triple therefore contributes two dimensions to $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and one to the centre, and the two-dimensional contribution is exactly the one that has lost equal weights.

The Centre as the Symmetric Sum

The sum is the invariant. $u_x+u_y+u_z=iP_V$ is the unique (up to scale) element of the corner that is symmetric in $x,y,z$, anti-Hermitian, and fixed by every permutation of the axes. It is therefore also the answer to the question the companion note had to leave open. In $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ the symmetric element does not exist; in $\mathfrak u(3)$ it exists, it is the centre, and it is the reason the three equal-weight generators can be independent. A symmetric element that is also traceless would have to be zero, and the centre is precisely the direction that tracelessness deletes.

Its trace is the obstruction, and its length is the price. $\operatorname{tr}iP_V=3i\neq0$ is what removes it from $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, and $\ell(iP_V)=4$ is what makes it expensive. Both statements are the same statement seen twice: the symmetric direction is the one that cannot be written with fewer than four one-sided products.

What Is Gained

A basis on which the relabelling acts as a permutation. $P=\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde u}$ with $\tilde u=\tfrac12(e_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+\vec e_z)$ sends

$$ A_a\mapsto A_{a+1},\qquad S_{ab}\mapsto S_{bc},\qquad u_a\mapsto u_{a+1}, $$

verified, so on the nine-element basis the cyclic permutation is a permutation of basis vectors, three free orbits of size three, with no basis vector fixed. That is the sense in which the frame is symmetric, and it is a genuine gain over the two-element Cartan basis.

The nine are a basis of $\mathfrak u(3)$, rank $9$ verified, and the three families are mutually orthogonal for the Hilbert–Schmidt form, so the decomposition is not only $P$-covariant but metric-orthogonal. No such orthogonal $3+3+3$ splitting exists in $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, since there the third family collapses to two dimensions.

A single $P$-eigenbasis. The equal-weight triple is what makes the Fourier pair of the companion note natural: $H_\omega=h_x-\omega h_y$ and $H_{\bar\omega}$ are the two nontrivial Fourier modes of the triple, and the missing trivial mode $\tfrac13(E_x+E_y+E_z)$ is traceless but does not kill $e_t$. In $\mathfrak u(3)$ that mode is exactly $iP_V$, up to the shift by $\tfrac{i}{3}I$. So the Fourier picture closes: three modes, one central and two transversal, and the central one is the symmetric sum of the triple.

What Is Not Gained

The symmetry was never broken. $P$ is an element of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, not an element of $\mathrm{U}(3)\setminus\mathrm{SU}(3)$; it already permutes the axes, in the companion note, and already cycles $h_x\to h_y\to h_z$. What $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ cannot do is exhibit a basis with equal weights. Enlarging the group therefore does not add a symmetry of the object; it adds a basis in which the existing symmetry is visible in the weights. The distinction matters, because a reader who takes "$\mathrm{U}(3)$ restores the symmetry" at face value will expect dynamics, and there is none.

The new direction is dynamically empty. $iP_V$ is central, so $[iP_V,\mathfrak u(3)]=0$: it is invisible in the adjoint action, it does not appear in any structure constant, and it is not a derivation. It generates a circle that acts on the carrier by a common phase on $B_V$. Nothing in the multiplication of $\mathbb B$ responds to it.

$\mathrm{U}(3)$ is not a subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, so it is not a group of determinant-one transformations of the carrier, and its elements do not preserve the volume form that $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ preserves. The three obstructions of The Gluon: An Octet Inside the Carrier's Group are untouched: they concern the compact algebra of dimension four, the odd-dimensional modules, and the missing symmetric invariant $d_{abc}$, and adding a central phase changes none of the three.

The basis is equivariant, not axis-free. "Axis-free" would mean that no element of the basis names an axis. That is false here: $u_x$ names $x$, and so does $A_x$. What is true is that the basis is permuted by the relabelling, and that the orbits and the symmetric functions name no axis. The honest formulations are:

$$ \{u_x,u_y,u_z\}\ \text{as a set},\qquad u_x+u_y+u_z=iP_V,\qquad \sum_au_a^{2}=-\tfrac83P_V,\qquad \text{the three orbits themselves}. $$

A statement that uses one $u_a$ alone is a statement about a labelling, exactly as a statement that uses one $E_a$ alone. The gain is that the labelling now enters only through a permutation, never through an unequal weight.

The physics reading is formal. That $iP_V$ is a singlet, central, of weight $+1$ on $B_V$ and $0$ on $e_t$, makes it look like a hypercharge or baryon-number direction, and the Casimir shift computed below, $-\tfrac13$, is the shape of a $Y^{2}$ contribution. This is an analogy at the level of the corner's block structure. It is not a derivation of any quantum number, the corpus's ceiling stands, and the group here is a group of operators on the carrier, not a gauge group.

The Only $P$-Stable Cartan

A correction worth stating. The companion note argues that no two-element basis of the Cartan is stable under the cyclic element, because $P$ acts on the Cartan with characteristic polynomial $\lambda^{2}+\lambda+1$. That argument is correct for the standard Cartan, the axis-diagonal one, and it must not be read as a statement about all Cartans. Maximal tori are conjugate, and the fixed space of $\mathrm{Ad}_P$ on $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is two-dimensional — verified, eigenvalues $1,1$ and $\omega^{\times3},\omega^{2\times3}$ — so there is exactly one $P$-stable maximal torus, the one diagonal in the Fourier basis:

$$ \mathfrak t_F=\Bigl\{\,i\,\mathrm{diag}_{\text{Fourier}}(0,a,b,c)\ :\ a+b+c=0\,\Bigr\},\qquad \mathrm{Ad}_P\big|_{\mathfrak t_F}=\mathrm{id}. $$

What that means. A $P$-stable pair of Cartan generators therefore exists, but only in $\mathfrak t_F$, and there $P$ acts as the identity: the pair is stable because the relabelling does nothing on it. A pair that $P$ genuinely permutes cannot exist, because a swap would need the eigenvalue $-1$, which is not an eigenvalue of an operator of order three, and a pointwise-fixed pair needs eigenvalue $1$, whose eigenvectors lie in $\mathfrak t_F$. And no element of $\mathfrak t_F$ has equal weights either, since its weights are $\lvert a\rvert,\lvert b\rvert,\lvert c\rvert$ with $a+b+c=0$, the same arithmetic obstruction. So the dichotomy is: a pair is either stable because the symmetry is invisible on it, or visible but not stable; only a triple can be both.

The Verdict

$\mathrm{U}(3)$ is the more natural frame; $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is the more natural object. The frame, because only after the trace constraint is released does a basis exist in which the cyclic symmetry is a permutation with equal weights, and only there do the generators split into three orthogonal families of three. The object, because the unit line, the Hermitian form and the automorphism group of $\mathbb B$ single out $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and say nothing about the centre, and because the central direction is inert: it does not act on the multiplication, it appears in no structure constant, and it costs the longest elements in the algebra.

The one-line summary. Adding tracelessness back deletes exactly the symmetric generator, deletes the three-family splitting, and deletes the reflection-free basis; adding it back changes no dynamics whatsoever.

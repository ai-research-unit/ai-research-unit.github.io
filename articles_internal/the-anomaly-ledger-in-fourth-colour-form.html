The Anomaly Ledger: Four Conditions Become None, and Why

Introduction

The question. The Standard Model's hypercharge assignment is fixed by anomaly cancellation, which is four conditions on the charges of one family. What becomes of those four conditions in the frame?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathrm{tr}\,Y=0,\quad \mathrm{tr}\,Y^{3}=0,\quad \mathrm{tr}(T_{3L}^{2}Y)=0,\quad \mathrm{tr}(T_{3R}^{2}Y)=0, \qquad\text{all four hold identically, and none is a constraint on a charge.} \;} $$

What this note is, in the migration. New article $8$ of the eleven listed in internal-note-the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md §8.7, phase $2$. It is the article that makes the migration's strongest technical claim inspectable: the frame does not satisfy the anomaly conditions by choosing charges well; it satisfies them without choosing charges at all.

What is claimed. The four sums, computed exactly; that they vanish because $Y$ is a traceless generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and because the sixteen is a pair of conjugate multiplets; that the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ anomalies vanish structurally; that the Witten anomaly is absent for both factors; and that no condition in the ledger is a constraint, so the ledger has no free parameters to fix.

What is not claimed. That this proves the frame. Anomaly cancellation is necessary and cheap: any vector-like theory satisfies all four, and a theory can be anomaly-free and wrong. §6 says exactly what the ledger does and does not buy.

1. The Ledger, as the Standard Model States It

Four conditions, and the reason they are constraints rather than identities.

1.1 With one Higgs doublet and the standard content, the conditions are

$$ \mathrm{tr}\,Y=0,\qquad \mathrm{tr}\,Y^{3}=0,\qquad \mathrm{tr}(T_{3L}^{2}Y)=0,\qquad \mathrm{tr}\bigl(T_{3L}^{a}\{T_{3L}^{b},T_{3L}^{c}\}\bigr)=0, $$

where the traces run over one family of left-handed fermions, with right-handed fields written as their conjugates. The fourth is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L^{3}$ condition, which vanishes automatically for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ because $d^{abc}=0$. So there are three real conditions.

1.2 Why they are constraints in the Standard Model. The hypercharges are six numbers per generation, assigned by hand; the three conditions remove three of them. The result is a one-parameter family, and the parameter is the overall normalisation of $Y$ — which is why the Standard Model cannot say why the proton charge is minus the electron charge. The conditions fix ratios and leave the scale free.

1.3 With $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ gauged there is a fourth condition, because $T_{3R}$ is now a gauge generator:

$$ \mathrm{tr}(T_{3R}^{2}Y)=0 . $$

So the frame's ledger has four rows, and the migration has to run all four.

Every number below is exact in $\mathbb Q$, computed with fractions.Fraction, not floating point.

2.2 The rows.

$$ \mathrm{tr}\,Y=3\cdot\tfrac16+3\cdot\tfrac16-\tfrac12-\tfrac12+3\cdot\Bigl(-\tfrac23\Bigr)+3\cdot\tfrac13+0+1 =\tfrac12+\tfrac12-1-2+1+1=0 . $$

$$ \mathrm{tr}\,Y^{3}=\underbrace{\Bigl[\tfrac{6}{216}-\tfrac{2}{8}\Bigr]}_{-\tfrac29} +\underbrace{\Bigl[-\tfrac{24}{27}+\tfrac{3}{27}+1\Bigr]}_{+\tfrac29}=0 . $$

$$ \mathrm{tr}(T_{3L}^{2}Y)=\tfrac12\Bigl[3\cdot\tfrac16+3\cdot\tfrac16-\tfrac12-\tfrac12\Bigr]=\tfrac12\bigl[1-1\bigr]=0 . $$

$$ \mathrm{tr}(T_{3R}^{2}Y)=\tfrac12\Bigl[3\cdot\Bigl(-\tfrac23\Bigr)+3\cdot\tfrac13+0+1\Bigr]=\tfrac12\bigl[-2+1+1\bigr]=0 . $$

Four zeros, and each one is a different mechanism. §3 separates them, because the separations are the content.

3. Why Each Row Vanishes

Four mechanisms, and only the second is a numerical accident of the assignment.

3.1 $\mathrm{tr}Y=0$ is the tracelessness of a generator. $Y$ is a linear combination of $T^{15}$ and $T_{3R}$, both traceless on any finite-dimensional representation of a compact group. So $\mathrm{tr}Y=0$ is not a condition on the charges; it is the statement that $Y$ is a generator at all.

3.2 $\mathrm{tr}Y^{3}=0$ is the conjugation pairing. The two contributions are $-\tfrac29$ from $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $+\tfrac29$ from $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, and the second is the first with every $Y$ reversed:

$$ -\tfrac29\quad\text{and}\quad+\tfrac29=\bigl(-\tfrac29\bigr)\Big|_{Y\to-Y} . $$

So the cancellation is between a multiplet and its own conjugate, which is the structural statement: a complex representation paired with its conjugate cancels every odd power of $Y$ automatically, and a real (vector-like) assignment would cancel trivially with nothing to check. The frame's sixteen is exactly such a pair, and that is why row two is an identity.

3.3 $\mathrm{tr}(T_{3L}^{2}Y)=0$ is the doublet count. The sum is $\tfrac12$ times the sum of $Y$ over the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ doublets, and the doublets are the quark quartet (three colours, $Y=\tfrac16$) and the lepton doublet ($Y=-\tfrac12$):

$$ 3\cdot\tfrac16-\tfrac12=0 . $$

That is one equation, and it is the one place where the frame looks like the Standard Model: the ratio of quark to lepton hypercharge is fixed by the doublet content. But in the frame it is not a condition — $Y$ is already determined by the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ embedding, and the equation is a check on the embedding, not a constraint on a charge. The check passes because $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$.

3.4 $\mathrm{tr}(T_{3R}^{2}Y)=0$ is the same statement on the other side, with the roles of the quartet and the conjugate exchanged. It holds for the same reason and it is the row that a left–right model without the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ would have to impose by hand.

3.5 The structural rows.

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)^{3}:\ \mathrm{tr}\,T^{a}\{T^{b},T^{c}\}=0\quad\text{for the fundamental of }\mathrm{SU}(N),\ N\ge3, $$

because the symmetric invariant $d^{abc}$ vanishes; and the content is vector-like under $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ anyway, since $\mathbf4$ and $\bar{\mathbf4}$ appear in equal measure. So the $\mathrm{SU}(4)^{3}$ anomaly is zero twice over.

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)^{2}\,\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}:\ \mathrm{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=1+3\cdot\bigl(-\tfrac13\bigr)=0 . $$

That zero is charge quantisation, and the charge-quantisation article is where it is read.

4. The Witten Anomaly, Which Is the One People Forget

A global anomaly, not a local one, and it is a parity count.

4.1 The statement. An $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge theory with an odd number of left-handed fermion doublets is inconsistent, because $\pi_4(\mathrm{SU}(2))=\mathbb Z_2$ and the fermion determinant changes sign under a large gauge transformation.

4.2 The count in the frame. Per family, the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ doublets are the three quark colours plus the lepton: four, which is even. The $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ doublets are the same four, from the $\bar{\mathbf4}$: also even. So

$$ n_{L}=4\equiv0\pmod 2,\qquad n_{R}=4\equiv0\pmod 2 . $$

4.3 Why the frame cannot fail this one. Because the quartet has four components and the doublet structure comes from the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index, the number of doublets is $4$ for any content built from whole quartets. A frame with three colours would have had three doublets and failed; the fourth colour is what makes the parity even, and that is a genuine, if small, structural gain.

4.4 The trap. Someone will check anomalies with right-handed fields written as right-handed and conclude that $\mathrm{tr}Y$ is $\pm$ twice the correct value. The convention has to be fixed once and stated, and this article's convention — everything left-handed, conjugates with $Y\to-Y$ — is the one that makes the ledger's rows transparent.

5.2 The count, stated once. The Standard Model has three constraints and one free normalisation. The frame has zero constraints and no free normalisation: $Y$ is determined by the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ embedding, the ratios follow, and the overall scale is fixed by $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$. That is the migration's strongest single technical claim, and it is the one that survives every other criticism of the frame.

5.3 The precise form of the claim, so that it is not overclaimed. The frame's hypercharge assignment is not chosen; it is inherited. The anomaly conditions then hold automatically, and holding automatically is a check on the inheritance, not a proof that the inheritance is physically realised. §6.

6. What the Ledger Does and Does Not Buy

Four things it buys, three it does not, and the list is the point of the article.

6.1 It buys the absence of a free hypercharge. No normalisation to choose, no ratios to fix. This is a real gain over the Standard Model, and it is not available to $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ either, where the hypercharge is fixed only up to the embedding's normalisation convention.

6.2 It buys charge quantisation. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$ forces $3q+\ell=0$, which is the subject of the next article.

6.3 It buys the even doublet count. §4, and it would have failed with three colours.

6.4 It buys a check that the embedding is consistent. Every row that is not structural is a test, and all the tests pass.

6.5 It does not buy the representations. The sixteen is declared, not derived; anomaly cancellation cannot select it, because a vector-like theory cancels automatically and the sixteen is a specific complex pair.

6.6 It does not buy chirality. A vector-like theory is anomaly-free and non-chiral; the ledger is silent on the chiral wall.

6.7 It does not buy the frame. Any consistent theory passes this ledger; the ledger is necessary and nowhere near sufficient. A migration that presented four zeros as evidence for Pati–Salam would be mistaking a hygiene requirement for a prediction.

Summary

The ledger. Four conditions in the Standard Model's counting, six rows once $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is gauged and the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ conditions are added.

The values. All zero, exactly, in $\mathbb Q$.

The mechanisms. Tracelessness, conjugate pairing, $B-L$ tracelessness on the quartet, and $d^{abc}=0$.

The gain. Zero constraints and no free normalisation: hypercharge is inherited from the embedding, not chosen.

The parity. Four doublets per family for both factors, so the Witten anomaly is absent by the quartet's size.

The limit. Every consistent theory passes this ledger; it is necessary, not sufficient, and it says nothing about the chiral wall.

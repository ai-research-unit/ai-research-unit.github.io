Spin Migration: The Spin as a Property of a Subspace

Introduction

This article lists every article of the corpus that discusses the spin, states for each one the approach it takes, and fixes the approach the corpus now takes. It is an internal article. It fixes the definition and it edits nothing beyond the menu move named in §The Main Maths Article. The patches it lists are work still to be done.

The decision. The spin is a property of a subspace of the algebra, and the three elementary spins are the three subspaces the algebra offers:

spin carrier element $\dim_\mathbb{R}$ how the spin is seen
$0$ $\mathbb{B}_{C}=\mathbb{C}e_t$ $q\,e_t$ $2$ the adjoint action fixes it
$\tfrac12$ $S_\pm=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_\pm$ $q_t(e_t\pm i\vec e_z)+q_x(\vec e_x\mp i\vec e_y)$ $4$ left multiplication acts on it irreducibly
$1$ $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\{i\,q'_t\,e_t+\vec r\}$ the four-vector $4$ the adjoint action, with the trace removed

What changes is the order of the two statements. The corpus has always carried both: the module picture, in which the spin is the highest weight of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ read on a module, and the carrier picture, in which the spin is the dimension of a subspace. The carrier picture has been the subordinate one, stated late and in a physics article. It becomes the definition, and the module picture becomes a reading of it.

Why the order was wrong. The algebra has one simple module, up to isomorphism. A label attached to a module therefore cannot distinguish three spins: the same module serves two of them, and the third is not a module at all. The label has to attach to something the algebra has three of, and that is the carrier. This is the substance of §Why the Definition Moves.

The census. Eighty-four articles discuss the spin: thirty-eight in articles_maths, forty-six in articles_physics, and four internal. The tables of §The Maths Articles, §The Physics Articles and §The Internal Articles list each one with its link and its approach, and mark it keep, patch or rewrite.

The counts. Of the eighty-four, seventy-four keep their approach — one of them gains links and one section — twelve take a patch that aligns the vocabulary, and two are rewritten, one of them renamed. The main maths article is rewritten and moved up the biquaternion category.

The Decision

The old definition. The spin is the highest weight $s$ of the rotation algebra: the Casimir is $C=s(s+1)$, the carrier space has dimension $2s+1$, and the classification is the highest-weight classification of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. That definition is correct as far as it goes and it is what the corpus stated in Spin and the Three Elementary Carriers in the Biquaternion Algebra, now split into the four articles of the carriers section.

Why it is incomplete. It names a number and a dimension, and it does not say which part of the algebra holds the field. A reader who has the definition cannot answer the question a physics article has to answer: which subspace is the field in. The answer has to be supplied afterwards, one case at a time, and the corpus does supply it — in Spin and the Three Elementary Carriers and in The Determination of the Possible Spins — but in a physics article, after the mathematics has been declared complete.

The new definition. The spin of a field is the spin of its carrier: the smallest subspace of $\mathbb{B}$ that holds the field's value and is closed under the action the field's equation uses. The three elementary carriers are the three such subspaces the algebra has, and their spins are $0$, $\tfrac12$ and $1$ because their dimensions are $1$, $2$ and $4$ real.

The dimension is the whole of the arithmetic. A carrier of complex dimension $n$ holds the spin $s=\tfrac{n-1}{2}$, because the representation space it has to contain is $2s+1=n$. For the centre $n=1$, so $s=0$. For an ideal $n=2$, so $s=\tfrac12$. For the material sector the carrier is a real form and not a complex space, of four real dimensions, and the spin-one value is its traceless part: four real components of the potential, three of the spin, the trace being the scalar admixture that the field equation removes. That counting is the whole of §The Third Carrier of The Three Elementary Carriers and the Four Sectors of Regularity and Degeneracy.

The carrier and the representation space are the same object twice, and they differ by one entry. For $0$ and for $\tfrac12$ the carrier's dimension is $2s+1$ exactly: one and two. For $1$ the carrier is four real-dimensional and the spin-one space is three-dimensional, the fourth component being the trace. That single component is what the Proca constraint removes, and it is why the arithmetic obstruction $1+2+3=6>4$ is stated about the representation spaces and not about the carriers, which add to $1+2+4=7$ and never needed to fit inside one module.

The three carriers are three because the algebra has three. The centre is the fixed space of the adjoint action. The minimal left ideals are the simple left modules, one for each idempotent of the frame. The material sector is the real form the conjugation fixes. No fourth subspace is closed under an action the algebra supplies, and the reason is arithmetic rather than a matter of taste: the three irreducible representation spaces have dimensions $1$, $2$ and $3$, and $1+2+3=6$ exceeds $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}=4$, so the three cannot sit inside one module.

What the definition is not. It is not a label of an element: an element has whatever carrier its field has, and the same element can be read in two carriers when the two meet, which they do on the lines $i\mathbb{R}e_t$ and $\mathbb{R}i\tilde\Pi_\pm$. It is not a label of a module: the module is unique and the carriers are three. And it is not a label of an operator family: the two-sided operator $QXQ^{*}$ is the rotor action, and it acts on a carrier that is fixed before the operator is named.

Higher spin. A spin above one is not a carrier. It is a field with more than one index: the Rarita–Schwinger field is a four-vector of Dirac fields, and the spin-three-halves value is reached by tensoring the defining module and imposing the constraint that removes the spin-half admixture. The corpus's ceiling statement must therefore be rephrased, and the rephrasing is one of the two rewrites of §The Contradictions the Migration Must Remove: the algebra has three carrier subspaces and no fourth, and it is the carriers that stop at spin one, not the spins.

Why the Definition Moves

One simple module, three carriers. $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is simple, so it has a single isomorphism class of simple modules, of complex dimension two. Every module is a direct sum of copies of it. So a spin read off a module can only ever be $\tfrac12$, or a sum of copies of $\tfrac12$; the values $0$ and $1$ arrive by a different route, one from the centre and one from the adjoint action, and the corpus has to say so in a parenthesis each time. The carrier statement says it once.

What the carrier pins. Each carrier fixes one of the two invariants the sectors article cuts the algebra with: the centre has $B^{2}=0$ at every point, the ideal has $N=0$ at every point, the sector has both free. So the carrier decides the reach of the field in the partition, and the spin decides the carrier. That chain — spin, carrier, invariants, region — is the structure the corpus was missing, and it runs in one direction only.

The value is not the carrier. A field's value is an element; the carrier is where the element is allowed to live. The distinction is what lets the same element be read twice: $i\tilde\Pi_\pm$ is a material four-vector and a spinor value at once, because the two carriers meet on that line. The corpus already uses this coincidence in The Proca Equation and in The Massless Regime; the migration makes it a stated fact rather than a coincidence discovered twice.

The test. Three questions, in this order, and the third is the new one:

question answer
what spin does the field have? $0$, $\tfrac12$ or $1$, from the observed particle or from the equation's index structure
which carrier holds that spin? the centre, an ideal, or the material sector
which action makes it a carrier? the adjoint action for $0$ and $1$, left multiplication for $\tfrac12$

The order matters because the third row is the only one with content. The first row is an input; the second is a table lookup; the third is the theorem. A corpus that states the first and the third in different articles, in different vocabularies, and the second nowhere, cannot be read in one pass.

The Five Readings the Corpus Uses

The census below sorts the eighty-four articles by what they take the spin to be. The sorting is the article's main diagnostic: four of the five readings are correct and none of them is the definition.

reading the spin is articles status after the migration
the carrier a subspace, and the spin is its dimension 4 the definition
the module the highest weight on a simple module, or an irreducible Clifford module 15 a reading of the carrier, and the correct one for $\tfrac12$
the generator the Casimir $C=s(s+1)$ and the weights of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ 9 the label, derived on the carrier
the versor the rotor, a two-sided operator and its double cover 14 the action on the carrier, not the carrier
the column a tuple of Peirce coordinates 8 a display of the carrier in a basis

The module and the column are two displays of the same carrier. An ideal is a subspace; a column is a subspace read in a Peirce basis. So the eight coordinate articles and the module articles are not rivals of the carrier statement: they are it, written twice, once without a basis and once with. The migration asks them for one sentence each and no more.

The generator is derived and must be labelled so. $C=s(s+1)$ is a function of the carrier's dimension, $s=\tfrac{n-1}{2}$, and the weights $\pm s$ are the eigenvalues of the rotation generator on the carrier. Both are consequences. The migration's one demand of the generator articles is that the word definition not appear where the word reading belongs.

The versor is the action and not the carrier. A rotor maps one carrier to another and has a double cover; a carrier has a dimension and no double cover. The two are easy to confuse because both are called the spin, and the confusion is old: it is the reason Operator Migration could write "the spin is a tied two-side operator with a unitary parameter" and be right about the operator while saying nothing about the field.

The field variable is a different subject. A spin density, a spin tensor and a spin polarisation are functions of the field and of its frame. They presuppose the carrier and they do not compete with it. The twelve articles of this row need no patch, and the migration leaves them alone.

The Main Maths Article

What it was. Angular Momentum and Spin in the Biquaternion Algebra, 5832 words, fourteen body sections and five subsections. The order was: the algebra, the rotation generators, the Casimir and the definition, the ladder and the classification, the left module, the adjoint module, the subcarriers, the spectrum and the criterion, the orbital realisation, the total angular momentum, tensor products, real forms, the four uses of the word, and the back matter.

What it is, as rewritten. Spin and the Three Elementary Carriers in the Biquaternion Algebra, 8227 words. The order is: the algebra, the three carriers with their invariant table, the definition of the spin as the dimension of the carrier's representation space, the rotation generators, the spin-zero carrier, the spin-half carrier, the spin-one carrier, the generator reading of the Casimir and the ladder, the spectrum and the criterion, the orbital realisation, the total angular momentum, tensor products and the absence of a spin-two carrier, real forms, the five uses of the word, and the back matter. The Casimir theorem and the classification are kept word for word and now stand after the carriers, as the machinery that recomputes the number the carriers already give.

Why that order is wrong for the new definition. The definition stands in the fourth section and is a definition of a number. The carriers, which are the definition now, stand in the seventh section, are called subcarriers, and are stated as a closure theorem about the module rather than as the answer to the question what the field is in. A reader who stops after the definition has the old picture, and the article's own conclusion — that there are three subcarriers of dimensions $1$, $2$ and $3$ — arrives as a remark on a module.

The move, already applied in the menu. The entry has left ##### Spin and the Rotation under #### Operators and now stands alone under a new heading #### The Carriers and the Spin, immediately after #### The Modules in the Biquaternion Algebra and before #### The Spectral Theory in the Biquaternion Algebra. The old heading is dissolved. The entry now sits at line 6665 of maths.md, where it stood at line 6822; the arithmetic of the biquaternion category now reads: the algebra, the matrix representations, the modules, the carriers and the spin, the spectral theory, the four products, the forms, the norms, the strata, the operators.

The move reverses a decision of Operator Migration. Item 11 of that article keeps the entry where it was, on the ground that the angular-momentum article is a consumer of cell D: the rotation operators are the tied two-side operators with a unitary parameter. That reading of the operators is right and the placement is not, because the article is not primarily about the operators. It is the article that says what the spin is, and the operators are its second section. The supersession is recorded in item 11 itself.

Why there and not later. The four sections before it — the algebra, its matrix representations, its modules, its carriers — are a ladder: the object, its display, the spaces it acts on, and the subspaces a field can occupy. The spin is the last rung of that ladder and the first statement of the physics. Everything after it in the category is a study of one element or one operator, which presupposes a carrier.

What §The Four Uses of the Word Spin becomes. Five uses, in three groups: the carrier is the definition; the highest weight and the Casimir are two readings of one number, $s$ and $s(s+1)$; the Peirce index is a name of one of the two ideals and not a value of $s$; the chirality is a central sign. The section is the article's strongest asset and it keeps its place at the end, where it now closes an argument rather than repairing one.

What the rewrite must not do. It must not import the sector vocabulary of the physics corpus into a mathematics article, beyond naming the subspaces. The three carriers are $\mathbb{B}_{C}$, $S_\pm$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, and the third is called the material sector because that is its name and not because the article develops the sector. The invariants $N$ and $B^{2}$ are stated once, in a table, as the text that the carrier fixes where the field's value can fall, and the development of the partition stays in the sectors article. The mathematics article states the spin of a subspace and stops.

Two corrections the rewrite forced. First, the decision names $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ as the spin-one carrier, and that is right for the field but not exclusive for the algebra: $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}=i\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ carries the same $1\oplus0$ under conjugation, because $i$ is central. The rewrite says so, and gives the reason the corpus names the material sector — that is where the four-vectors live. Second, the spin-one space is the traceless part of the sector and not the sector: four real dimensions against three, and the fourth entry is the scalar line that a vector field's constraint removes. A reader who takes the carrier's dimension for the representation space's dimension gets $s=\tfrac32$ and a spin no field has, so the definition is stated as the dimension of the representation space, with the subtraction explicit.

The Contradictions the Migration Must Remove

Five statements in the corpus deny the new definition, and each needs a named fix rather than a rewrite.

1. The ceiling, stated about modules. Higher Spin from Tensor Products carries the title "why the biquaternion algebra admits only spin zero and one-half", and the title is false under the new definition, since the spin-one carrier is the material sector and the article's own text says so two sentences later ("its traceless part spin one"). The fix is a rename and a rescoped introduction: the theorem is about carriers, of which there are three and no fourth, and the higher spins live in tensor powers and in fields with more than one index. The body's mathematics stands.

2. The list, distributed over two structures. The Determination of the Possible Spins opens by saying that the three subcarriers are "distributed over two module structures". The distribution is real and the count is right; the word module is what fails, because the third is not a module. The fix is to say three carriers, two of them modules, and to cite the carriers article for the table.

3. The spin as an operator. Operator Migration says "the spin is a tied two-side operator with a unitary parameter, and the Dirac spinor is the tied one-side pair". Under the new definition the spin is a subspace and the operator is the action on it. The fix is one clause: the tied two-side operator is the rotor, which is the action on the carrier, and the tied one-side pair is the pair of values, whose carriers are the two ideals.

4. Spin zero escaping a module. The Scalar Field in the Centre explains the escape as a statement about the state module: the centre is not a module, so the scalar is not in it. That is true and it is not the reason. The reason is that the centre is a carrier of dimension one and the scalar needs nothing more. The fix is a paragraph, and the article's title becomes accurate rather than merely clever.

5. The trace in the spin-one carrier. The Proca Equation, The Self-Dual and Anti-Self-Dual Split and The Massless Regime each treat the four-component vector as the spin-one object, and each removes one component by a constraint without saying which spin the removed component carried. The fix is one sentence in each: the carrier is $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, of four real dimensions, and the spin is its traceless part, the trace being the scalar admixture that the constraint removes.

What Is Not Patched

The Clifford, Pin and Spin-group articles. Fourteen articles treat the spin groups of a quadratic form. Their subject is the covering of an orthogonal group by versors, and it is a different question from the one the migration answers: which subspace of $\mathbb{B}$ holds a field. They keep their vocabulary, and the migration asks only that they not be cited as the definition of the spin of a field.

The coordinate articles. The four rank-specific articles of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ and the general one state that a spinor is a column of a Peirce array. They say so as a statement about coordinates, with the basis declared, and they are right. The migration's sentence in item 6 is the only one they need.

The field-variable articles. Twelve articles work with a spin density, a spin vector, a spin tensor or a spin polarisation. These are functions of a field and of its frame. They presuppose a carrier, and the carrier of the field is stated once, in the maths article.

The exercises. Five exercises work the spin-$\tfrac12$ machinery in the $2\times2$ model. None of them makes a claim about what the spin is, and all of them remain valid under either reading.

Summary

  • The spin is a property of a subspace of $\mathbb{B}$: the centre for $0$, the minimal left ideals for $\tfrac12$, the material sector for $1$, with the spin-one value in the traceless part.
  • The change is an inversion of order: the carriers were the subordinate statement and become the definition; the highest weight, the Casimir, the module, the versor and the column become readings of it.
  • The reason is that the algebra has one simple module and three carriers, so a module cannot label three spins and a subspace can.
  • Eighty-four articles discuss the spin. Fourteen of them change: two by a rewrite, one of those also by a rename, and twelve by a patch of vocabulary. The remaining seventy-four keep their approach, and one of them gains a link and a section.
  • The main maths article moves from ##### Spin and the Rotation under #### Operators to #### The Carriers and the Spin after the modules, and its definition moves from the fourth section to the first.
  • The five contradictions are the ceiling stated about modules, the list distributed over two module structures, the spin called an operator, spin zero escaping a module, and the trace in the spin-one carrier. Each has a named fix.

The Phi and Psi Migration: An Inventory by Role, and Chi as the Generic Field

Introduction

The physics corpus writes two letters for almost everything. The letter $\phi$ names a potential, a field, a phase, an angle, a second state and a carrier function. The letter $\psi$ names a spinor, a rapidity, a kernel, a Grassmann variable and a dummy element of the algebra.

The two overloads do not have the same shape. $\psi$ is dominated by one role: the spinor and the state carry 2839 of its 4584 occurrences in 211 of the 412 physics articles. $\phi$ has no dominant role, but it has a large one: the scalar field carries 1119 of its 2006 occurrences in 123 articles, the auxiliary scalar 200, the angle 167, the phase 110, the second state 61, and one article uses it for a rapidity.

The corpus does not fix a glyph to a role. It fixes a glyph to an object, and a bare letter is then read for whatever that object's coordinate is. That is why one letter can carry six roles: nothing in the notation says which object it belongs to, so the reader supplies the object from the sentence. A migration that removes the overload has to supply that statement, and it can supply it only in the glyph.

A rapidity is not the coordinate of the Dirac field, and it is not a spinor. The letter $\psi$ carries both: 62 articles use it as a rapidity, and 131 use it as a spinor. That collision is the one a migration must settle first, because it puts two different objects of physics on one glyph for no reason.

The decision taken here is one glyph for a generic field and no role in the low slot. The generic field is $\chi$, three-letter caret superscripts carry the precision, and the count of $\chi$ in the corpus is the price. Each section below ends with the replacements that role admits, ranked by what each one costs.

How the Census Was Taken

The count is the number of occurrences of the control sequence, excluding $\varphi$. The pattern matches the control sequence followed by anything but a letter, so a subscript and a superscript are counted and a longer control sequence is not.

The role is read from the sentence that carries the occurrence, not from a keyword count. Two articles can write the same token and mean different objects; Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Klein-Gordon Field and The Scalar Field in the Centre both write $\phi$ as the scalar field, while Electromagnetism in Media writes $\phi/c$ for the potential and $\phi$ alone for a carrier function.

A row lists a role that carries at least a quarter of the glyph in that article. Small secondary uses are left out of the tables, so the rows of an article sum to less than its own count. The last column always gives the article's own count, and it is the number to use when the whole article is migrated.

The totals were recomputed by a second method: a plain search for the control sequence over the whole corpus returns 2006 for $\phi$, 4584 for $\psi$ and 1020 for $\chi$. The two methods agree.

$\chi$ is chosen on what its uses are, not on how many there are. The Caret, and No Role in the Low Slot

The corpus has an instrument for this, and it is the caret. A caret carries an upright three-letter label, lowercase, and the index of those labels, in Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions, §The Three-Letter Labels, holds 78 rows.

A role goes to the caret, not to the low slot. The index as it stands sends a role low, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$, because a role is a part of an object. The migration amends that one line: a caret label may name the role of a glyph when the glyph is bare. The low slot is then left to the index, where it carries an index and a part, and it carries no role of this migration.

The amendment costs one sentence and buys the whole migration. Under it, $\chi^{\mathrm{scl}}$ is the scalar field, $\chi^{\mathrm{phs}}$ is a phase, $\chi^{\mathrm{grs}}$ is a Grassmann variable, and no letter of the alphabet is spent. The alternative, $\chi_{\mathrm{scl}}$, is a defect under the index as it stands, because the low slot names a part of a named object and a bare $\chi$ names no object yet.

A new label has to be free of the 78 rows by spelling. pot names a potential energy, so the scalar potential cannot take it. geo names the geometric phase, so a bare phase cannot take it either and takes phs. src names the source point of a kernel, so the kernel is not given a label at all: it is given the glyph $G$.

Twelve labels and three glyphs carry the migration. $\chi$ for a generic field, $\eta$ for the rapidity, $G$ for the kernel, and twelve caret labels for the precisions. No role is written low anywhere, and no letter outside the two is moved.

A label is read on the object it qualifies, so a bare $\chi$ is a statement. $\chi$ alone means "a field, unspecified". That is the reading the migration wants for the dummy of the left and right actions, $L_a\chi=a\chi$, and for a general element of the algebra. Precision is then an addition, never a default.

1.3 The Angle and the Coordinate

An angle is a real coordinate on a circle or on a sphere: the azimuth of $(\theta,\phi)$, the precession angle of the Euler triple, the Hannay angle, the Wigner angle, the Foucault angle. Here $\phi$ is read and never exponentiated.

The role holds 167 occurrences in 18 articles. In 100 of them the letter stands inside a named pair or triple — $(\theta,\phi)$ in seven articles, $(\theta,\phi,\psi)$ in the rigid-body article — and there it is not a free choice: $\theta$ assumes it and the reader supplies the rest. In the other 67 it stands alone and nothing beside it says "angle" unless the sentence does.

The role is not an overload, and that is the reason to leave the letter where it is. An overload is one glyph for two objects. Here the glyph names one object, a coordinate, and the corpus reads it as a number. The pair $(\theta,\phi)$ is the convention the physics literature shares with the mathematics, and the corpus uses it on the sphere, the Bloch sphere, the Hopf map, the polarization sphere, the coherent state and the ellipsoid.

The ambiguity is local and it is one caret away. Where the azimuth stands alone — the shock-wave azimuth, the polarization ellipse, the mode azimuth — the glyph is bare and a reader could take it for the potential. A three-letter label settles that occurrence and leaves the pair untouched.

A decomposition by name is exact, if the corpus wants it. The 167 split into five named angles with no remainder: azimuth 106 in 14 articles, precession 22 in 1, Wigner 21 in 1, Hannay 16 in 1, Foucault 2 in 1. Every one of those labels is free by spelling in the 78 rows, with one exception: pre is the presheaf, so the precession label is prc.

The cost is five labels, and the migration currently spends twelve. A label is worth spending where the glyph is silent. It is silent in the 67 standalone occurrences and not in the 100 that sit inside a pair. So the recommendation is the letter alone, and the five labels are offered for the articles that need them.

Possible replacements

Sixteen candidates, measured on the role as it stands. The first is the recommendation; the next five are the caret family that decomposes the role by name; the rest are rejected with the load that rejects them.

  • $\phi^{\mathrm{azm}}$ — accepted where the azimuth stands alone: 106 occurrences in 14 articles, the largest block of the role and the one that includes the $(\theta,\phi)$ pairs. It is free by spelling in the 78 rows.
  • $\phi^{\mathrm{prc}}$ — accepted for the precession angle of the Euler triple: 22 occurrences in 1 article, Rigid-Body Dynamics and the Biquaternion Rotor. The obvious label pre is the presheaf, so the precession label drops one vowel.
  • $\phi^{\mathrm{wgn}}$ — accepted for the Wigner angle: 21 occurrences in 1 article, Exercise: Boosting a Four-Velocity and Rapidity Composition. wig was available and wgn was chosen for the three letters of the name.
  • $\phi^{\mathrm{han}}$ — accepted for the Hannay angle: 16 occurrences in 1 article. Free by spelling; har is the harmonic Fischer projection and is one letter away.
  • $\phi^{\mathrm{foc}}$ — accepted for the Foucault angle: 2 occurrences in 1 article. Small enough that the bare letter is equally defensible.
  • the five labels sum to 167 — the decomposition is exact, azimuth 106, precession 22, Wigner 21, Hannay 16, Foucault 2, with no remainder. That is what makes the family an option rather than a gesture.
  • $\theta$ — rejected. 3859 occurrences in 216 articles, the other coordinate of the pair, the polar angle of 838 occurrences and an index in 538 more. Moving the azimuth onto it puts the pair backwards and collides with the largest glyph in the role's own neighbourhood.
  • $\alpha$ — rejected. 3033 occurrences in 186 articles: the fine-structure constant in 18 articles, a coefficient, and an index in 596 occurrences.
  • $\beta$ — rejected. 2812 occurrences in 147 articles: a velocity ratio, a coupling, and a spinor or representation label in 278 occurrences.
  • $\vartheta$ — rejected. 40 occurrences in 4 articles. It is a variant of $\theta$, so it splits the pair into two glyphs that a reader must tell apart for no gain.
  • $\chi^{\mathrm{ang}}$ — rejected. 1020 occurrences of the glyph. An angle is not a field, and a field's glyph carrying a role label states the wrong kind of object even when the label is right.
  • $\psi$ — rejected. 4584 occurrences in 211 articles, and one article already writes a Euler angle with it. That article is the overload, not the cure.
  • $\phi'$ — rejected. A prime marks the imaginary parameter of a coefficient, $Q_\mu=q_\mu+iq'_\mu$. An angle is a real coordinate and has no imaginary part to mark.
  • $\phi^{\mathrm{az}}$ — rejected. The caret carries three letters in this corpus; a two-letter label is a second convention for the same slot.
  • $\phi^{\mathrm{pol}}$ — rejected. pol is the polarisation vector in the 78 rows, and the polarization sphere is one of the articles in this role.
  • no slot, no subscript — stated as a non-option. An angle is not a component of a four-vector and takes no unit index; the low slot is the wrong instrument entirely.

What the Inventory Says About a Migration

The alphabet is saturated and the caret is not. The migration therefore opens one glyph, moves one, borrows one, and spends twelve labels, and it says why at each step.

The generic field is $\chi$, and it carries no label when no precision is needed. The scalar field moves first, because it is the largest role in either letter: 1119 occurrences in 50 articles. The dummy element of the algebra moves with it, because a dummy element is a generic element, and $\chi$ is exactly that. The migration is then one statement — $\chi$ is a field — with precisions added where an article needs them.

The three uses of $\chi$ that are not fields take a label, and that is what frees the glyph. The gauge function, the cell and the Helmholtz kernel are named objects, and a name is the caret's office. The central grading already carries $\chi_{\mathrm{ad}}$ and needs no change. Three labels buy the whole alphabet.

The spinor keeps $\psi$. The role is 2839 of 4584 occurrences in 131 articles, and the glyph is fixed by the notation table, which writes $\psi^{\mathrm{fop}}$ for the Fock operator. A migration that moves it renames a coordinate that already has the right name.

The rapidity leaves $\psi$ for $\eta$, and the rotor pair is re-cut. The boost is the second half of a pair, $\theta$ for the rotation and $\psi$ for the boost, and 62 articles write it that way. The pair is not worth a collision with the spinor. It becomes $(\theta,\eta)$: $\eta$ is the pseudorapidity of the literature, its load here is 600 occurrences, and its four uses all carry a mark, so a caret separates them.

The kernel goes to $G$. The corpus already writes $G$ for a Green function and $G^{\mathrm{rgs}}$ for the regularised one. This role needs no new glyph and no new label, because the object already has a glyph.

The potential goes to the temporal parameter $a'_t$, and the corpus is patched. The coefficient rule gives $A_t=a_t+i\,a'_t$, the material sector sets $a_t=0$, and $a'_t=\phi/c$. The substitution i\phi/c $\to$ i\,a'_t is made in the 31 articles that carry the potential, 71 occurrences, and the definitions are in Algebra and Element Conventions and Maxwell's Equations. The potential stays $\phi$ where it is used as a number, $\vec E=-\nabla\phi$ and $\varrho\phi$.

The angle keeps its letter in both glyphs. $(\theta,\phi)$ is the spherical pair and $(\phi,\theta,\psi)$ is the Euler triple. An angle is the one role where the letter is not an overload but half of a pair, and no migration should touch it.

The auxiliary row dissolves. Its four objects take four names: $\chi^{\mathrm{car}}$ for a carrier, $\chi^{\mathrm{trc}}$ for a trace part, $\chi^{\mathrm{flw}}$ for a flow, and $\phi$ for the meson, which is the one place the letter is the physics.

Nothing else moves into a Greek letter. $\xi$, $\sigma$, $\pi$, $\theta$, $\rho$, $\omega$, $\lambda$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are all taken, most of them above 2000 occurrences, and the light ones are variants that state nothing. The literature's ranking of generic scalars is a ranking of a different corpus.

A migration that states a role is worth more than a migration that states a letter. Three glyphs and twelve caret labels state every role that either letter was carrying. The corpus gains a statement of what each glyph is doing, and loses one letter to a collision it can now describe.

Summary

$\phi$ appears 2006 times in 123 articles, $\psi$ appears 4584 times in 211 articles, and $\chi$ appears 1020 times in 122 articles.

$\phi$ splits without a dominant role: the scalar field 1119, the auxiliary scalar 200, the angle 167, the phase 110, the second state 61.

$\psi$ splits into one dominant role and one rival: the spinor 2839, the rapidity 1113, the dummy element 183, the Grassmann variable 139.

$\chi$ takes the generic field, bare where no precision is needed: the scalar field $\chi^{\mathrm{scl}}$ 1119, the dummy element 183, the Grassmann variable $\chi^{\mathrm{grs}}$ 139, the phase $\chi^{\mathrm{phs}}$ 110, the second state $\chi^{\mathrm{alt}}$ 61, the carrier $\chi^{\mathrm{car}}$ and the trace part $\chi^{\mathrm{trc}}$ inside the auxiliary row, and the four named uses that give up the bare glyph.

The roles, not the letter count, decide the migration. $\psi$ keeps the spinor and gives up the rapidity to $\eta$. $\phi$ gives up the field to $\chi$ and keeps the angle. The kernel goes to $G$ and the potential slot to $a'_t$. No role is written in the low slot.

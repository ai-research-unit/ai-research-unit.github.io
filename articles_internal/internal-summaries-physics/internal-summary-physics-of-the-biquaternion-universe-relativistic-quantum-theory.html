1. Generalities: States, Observables, Spin

The observable and the state.

$$ \tilde H_\Psi=h_t\,e_t+i\vec h_\Psi,\qquad h_t\in\mathbb{R},\ \vec h_\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^3, $$

$$ \tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t+i\vec s_{\Psi}\bigr),\qquad |\vec s_\Psi|\le1,\qquad B^{3}=\{\vec s_\Psi:|\vec s_\Psi|\le1\}. $$

The pure states are the boundary $|\vec s_\Psi|=1$; the maximally mixed state is the centre.

The two pairings, and which is which.

$$ \langle\tilde H_{\Psi,1},\tilde H_{\Psi,2}\rangle_{*}=h_{t,1}h_{t,2}+\vec h_{\Psi,1}\!\cdot\!\vec h_{\Psi,2}, \qquad \langle\tilde H_\Psi\rangle_{\natural}=h_t^{2}-|\vec h_\Psi|^{2}. $$

The Hermitian pairing is positive; the quaternionic norm is indefinite. The states live on the cone of the first, the mass shell on the level set of the second.

The spin and the possible values. The spin operator is the adjoint action of the vector part, and the eigenvalue equation gives the half-integers and the integers:

$$ \tilde S_\Psi^{2}=\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}s(s+1)e_t,\qquad \langle\vec S\rangle=\frac{h}{2\pi}\,s\,\vec u,\qquad \langle\vec S^{2}\rangle=\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}s(s+1), $$

$$ \tilde S_{\Psi}^{2}=\tfrac34\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}e_t\quad\text{on the spin-}\tfrac12\text{ carrier},\qquad \tilde S_{\Psi,\mathrm{adj}}^{2}=2\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\operatorname{id}_V\quad\text{on the adjoint (spin-1) carrier}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The possible spins are determined and not assumed: the spin-$\tfrac12$ carrier is the idempotent-and-nilpotent module, whose two states are $\tfrac{h}{4\pi}$ and $-\tfrac{h}{4\pi}$; the spin-1 carrier is the adjoint action on $\mathbb{B}_V$, and its three states are $\pm \frac{h}{2\pi}$ and $0$. No other finite carrier occurs.

$$ \tilde S_{\Psi,3}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}=\frac{h}{4\pi}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+},\qquad \tilde S_{\Psi,3}\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},-+}=-\frac{h}{4\pi}\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},-+}, $$

$$ \tilde S_{\Psi,\mathrm{adj},3}\varepsilon_\pm=\pm \frac{h}{2\pi}\,\varepsilon_\pm,\qquad \tilde S_{\Psi,\mathrm{adj},3}\varepsilon_0=0 . $$

The three wavenumbers. One four-momentum, divided by $h$, gives three numbers:

$$ \tilde\Upsilon_\Phi=\frac{\tilde P_\Phi}{h}=i\,\nu_t^{\prime}\,e_t+\vec\nu_\Phi,\qquad \nu_t^{\prime}=\frac{E}{h c},\quad \vec\nu_\Phi=\frac{\vec p}{h},\quad \nu_m=\frac{mc}{h}, $$

$$ \tilde\Upsilon_\Phi\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi^{\natural}=-\nu_t^{\prime\,2}+\nu_\Phi^{2}=-\nu_m^{2}\quad\text{that is}\quad \nu_t^{\prime\,2}=\nu_\Phi^{2}+\nu_m^{2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The three wavenumbers are the three components of one element; the mass shell is the norm of that element. An agent that treats $\nu_m$ as an independent parameter breaks the identity.

The d'Alembertian is a product.

$$ \boxed{\;\Box_\Phi=\tilde\nabla_\Phi(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}=(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde\nabla_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial {r'_t}^{2}}\;} $$

$$ \tilde\nabla_\Phi=\sum_{\imath} e_{\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial R_{\imath}},\qquad (\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}=\sum_{\imath} \varepsilon_{\imath} e_{\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial R_{\imath}},\qquad \varepsilon_t=+1,\ \varepsilon_{x,y,z}=-1 . $$

and it is central, so it commutes with left multiplication: $\Box_\Phi(\tilde Q_1\tilde\Xi)=\tilde Q_1(\Box_\Phi\tilde\Xi)$.

The action and the stationary principle. The relativistic action of the corpus is

$$ S=-mc\int\sqrt{-d\tilde R_\Phi\,d\tilde R_\Phi^{\natural}}, $$

and the field Lagrangian is

$$ \mathcal L=+\mathrm{Sc}\bigl[((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde\Xi^{*})(\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde\Xi)\bigr]+4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\mathrm{Sc}\bigl[\tilde\Xi^{*}\tilde\Xi\bigr]. $$

The path integral, and what its weight is. The weight is a central element, not a complex number:

$$ \boxed{\;\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{act}})=\cos\!\Big(\frac{2\pi S}{h}\Big)e_t+\sin\!\Big(\frac{2\pi S}{h}\Big)i e_t\in\mathbb{B}_{C}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_t,ie_t\}\;} $$

$$ \frac{S}{h}=\int_{r'_{t,i}}^{r'_{t,f}}\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi\,d\tilde R_\Phi^{\natural}\bigr),\qquad \exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{act}})\bigl(\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{act}})\bigr)^{\dagger}=e_t . $$

The non-trivial claim. The Feynman weight is $*$-unitary but not $\natural$-unitary unless $\frac{2\pi S}{h}\in\pi\mathbb{Z}$. The distinction matters: the path integral lives in the centre, where both the phase and its conjugate are available, and that is why the two sectors can be summed over one measure.

The measurement, in three lines.

$$ p_\pm=\frac{2\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}(\vec u_\Psi)\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}\bigr)}{2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}},\qquad \Gamma_{\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}}(\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi})=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}=p_\pm\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}, $$

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,d}=p_+\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1+p}{}_-\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t+i(\vec u_\Psi\!\cdot\!\vec s_\Psi)\vec u_\Psi\bigr)\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The collapse is the congruence by the idempotent, and the post-measurement state is the mixture of the two outcomes with the Born weights. No separate projection postulate is needed; the projection is a multiplication.

The decomposition of a measurement apparatus. Every instrument splits into a positive part and a unitary part:

$$ \tilde Q_3=\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{pos}}\tilde Q_p,\qquad \tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{pos}}=\sqrt{\tilde Q_3\tilde Q_3^{*}}, $$

which is the polar decomposition read as the split between the strength of the measurement and its channel.

Helicity. The helicity is the spin projected on the momentum, and it is an element of the informational sector:

$$ \lambda=\vec u_p\!\cdot\!\vec S,\qquad \vec u_p=\frac{\vec p}{|\vec p|},\qquad \lambda=\frac{h}{4\pi}i\vec u_p\in\mathbb{B}_{\Psi},\qquad \lambda=\frac{i h}{4\pi}\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u_p}, $$

$$ \lambda(-\vec p)=-\lambda(\vec p),\qquad \lambda(\vec p')=\tilde u\,\lambda(\vec p)\,\tilde u^{*}. $$

2. Spin Zero: the Klein–Gordon Equation

The equation, in one product.

$$ \boxed{\;\bigl(\tilde\nabla_\Phi(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}-4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\bigr)\tilde\Xi=0,\qquad \tilde\Xi=\Xi_t e_t+\Xi_x\vec e_x+\Xi_y\vec e_y+\Xi_z\vec e_z\;} $$

The componentwise reduction, and it is immediate because the d'Alembertian is central:

$$ \bigl(\Box_\Phi-4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\bigr)\Xi_{\imath}=0,\qquad {\imath}\in\{t,x,y,z\}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Four real scalar fields, one biquaternion. The vector part is not a physical vector: the four components are independent solutions and the algebra's product does not mix them. The corpus therefore calls the spin-zero field the centre field and states that it lies outside the state module.

The first-order root. The operator factors, and the two factors are the chiral pair of the spin-$\tfrac12$ case:

$$ \tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde Q_1=2\pi\nu_m\tilde Q_2,\qquad \tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde Q_2=2\pi\nu_m\tilde Q_1, $$

$$ \bigl((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}+2\pi\nu_m\bigr)\bigl(\tilde\nabla_\Phi-2\pi\nu_m\bigr) =\Box_\Phi-2\pi\nu_m(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}+2\pi\nu_m\tilde\nabla_\Phi-4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}. $$

The polar variables, and the linearisation. Writing $\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi_0\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pwp}})$ and separating real and imaginary parts gives the Madelung pair: the Hamilton–Jacobi equation with the quantum potential, and the continuity equation. This is the bridge to the fluid-physics category.

The coupled case. Two fields of masses $m_1$, $m_2$ couple through a term in the Lagrangian; the diagonalisation of the mass matrix is again a congruence, and the two eigenmasses are the two eigenvalues of the $2\times2$ matrix of couplings. No new algebraic object appears.

The propagator. The Green's function is the inverse of the boxed operator in the transform variable, and its pole structure is the two-sheeted mass shell. The contour is the statement of which sector is propagated.

3. Spin One Half: the Dirac Equation

The equation, and it is a pair.

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde\Xi_{R}=2\pi\nu_m\tilde\Xi_{L},\qquad (\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{L}=2\pi\nu_m\tilde\Xi_{R}\;} $$

$$ \nu_m=\frac{mc}{h}. $$

The non-trivial claims, four of them.

The two lines are the two chiral halves, and the mass is the only coupling between them. Setting $\nu_m=0$ decouples and gives the two Weyl equations:

$$ \tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde\Xi_R=0,\qquad (\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_L=0 . $$

The chirality is a right multiplication, not a left one:

$$ \tilde X\vec e_z=-i\tilde X\ \Longrightarrow\ \tilde X\text{ carries the value }L,\qquad \tilde X\vec e_z=+i\tilde X\ \Longrightarrow\ \tilde X\text{ carries the value }R . $$

This is obstruction O22 made constructive: left multiplication cannot see chirality, so the corpus carries it on the right.

The square of the Dirac operator is the Klein–Gordon operator, one line of algebra:

$$ (\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde\Xi_R=\Box_\Phi\tilde\Xi_R=2\pi\nu_m(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_L=4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\tilde\Xi_R, $$

$$ \boxed{\;\bigl(\Box_\Phi-4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\bigr)\tilde\Xi_{R}=0,\qquad \bigl(\Box_\Phi-4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\bigr)\tilde\Xi_{L}=0\;} $$

The plane wave is a null element times an idempotent:

$$ \tilde\Upsilon_\Phi=i\nu_t^{\prime}\,e_t+\nu_t^{\prime}\vec e_z=2i\nu_t^{\prime}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},-},\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},-}=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z). $$

The current and the Gordon decomposition. The density is a Hermitian form, and the current splits into a convection part and a magnetisation part:

$$ j_0=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Xi^{*}\tilde\Xi\bigr)=|\tilde\Xi_t|^{2}+|\tilde\Xi_x|^{2}+|\tilde\Xi_y|^{2}+|\tilde\Xi_z|^{2}, $$

$$ \boxed{\;\bar{\tilde\Xi}\,i\,e_{\imath}\,\tilde\Xi\,\vec e_{{\jmath}({\imath})}=\frac{i}{4\pi\nu_m}\bigl(\bar{\tilde\Xi}\,\partial_{\imath}\tilde\Xi-\overline{\partial_{\imath}\tilde\Xi}\,\tilde\Xi\bigr)+\frac{1}{2\pi\nu_m}\partial_{\jmath}\bigl(\bar{\tilde\Xi}\,\sigma_{{\imath}{\jmath}}\,\tilde\Xi\bigr)\;} $$

with $\jmath(t)=x$ and $\jmath(\eta)=y$. The two terms are the convection current and the spin current; the second one is what makes $g=2$ and not $g=1$.

The antilinear structure and the two kinds of mass.

$$ \tilde Q^{*}=\overline{\tilde Q^{\natural}}=\bigl(\bar{\tilde Q}\bigr)^{\natural},\qquad \tilde Q^{\flat}=-\tilde Q^{*}, $$

$$ \boxed{\;\text{Dirac field}: 8\text{ real components},\qquad \text{Majorana field}: 4\text{ real components}\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The Majorana condition is a reality condition with respect to a conjugation that is antilinear in the coefficients; the Dirac field has twice the components because the mass term pairs a field with its conjugate field, not with itself. The mass term is written

$$ \mathcal L_{\text{Dirac}}=-m_D\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Xi^{*}i\tilde\Xi\vec e_x\bigr). $$

Minimal coupling. The gradient is replaced by the locally covariant gradient, and the coupling is a left multiplication by the potential:

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi}=\tilde\nabla_\Phi+\frac{2\pi i\,ϙ}{h}\tilde A_\Phi,\qquad (\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi})^{\natural}=(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}+\frac{2\pi i\,ϙ}{h}\tilde A_\Phi^{\natural}\;} $$

The conjugate of the operator is the quaternion conjugate, not the operator of the opposite charge. That asymmetry is the reason the electric and magnetic entries cannot be read as two independent couplings.

Foldy–Wouthuysen. Split the Hamiltonian into an even part and an odd part with respect to the mass grading:

$$ H=\beta\bigl(mc^{2}+\beta O\bigr),\qquad H^{2}=m^{2}c^{4}+O^{2},\qquad O^{2}=c^{2}\vec p^{\,2}\ \text{free}. $$

The FW transformation is the unitary that removes $O$ order by order; in the algebra it is a congruence by a rotation rotor. The eigenvalues are $\pm\sqrt{m^{2}c^{4}+c^{2}\vec p^{2}}$, the mass shell again.

Zitterbewegung. The velocity operator is $\pm c$ on the two branches, so the position has a drift and a trembling term:

$$ \boxed{\;\vec r(r'_t)=\vec r(0)+\frac{c^{2}\vec p}{H}r'_t-\frac{i h c}{4\pi H}\bigl(e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{evo}}}-1\bigr)\vec A\;} $$

with $\theta_{\mathrm{evo}}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde H_\Psi)\frac{2\pi \,r'_t}{h\,c}$ and $\vec A=\vec\alpha(0)-c\vec p/H$. Because $\{\alpha_k,H\}=2cp_k\,\mathrm{id}$, the velocity splits exactly into a drift and a trembling term, and the trembling frequency is $\frac{2\pi 2E}{h}$. The non-trivial point is that the frequency is twice the energy over $\frac{h}{2\pi}$: the interference is between the two branches.

The hydrogen atom. The Dirac equation with the Coulomb potential $V(r)=-\kappa/r$, $\kappa=\mathfrak{Z}e^{2}/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$, gives the exact fine structure. The spin–orbit coupling is written with the spin generator

$$ \tilde S_{\Psi,k}=\frac{h}{4\pi}i\vec e_k, $$

and the $\vec\pi$-expansion carries the relativistic kinetic correction, the Darwin term and the spin–orbit term in one bracket. The energy levels are the Dirac levels; the degeneracy in $\ell$ is lifted by the spin–orbit term.

The Klein paradox, the oscillator, pair creation. The Klein paradox is the statement that a step potential higher than $2mc^{2}$ produces a transmitted current with the sign of the antiparticle; the algebraic reason is that the transmitted wave is a superposition with the other idempotent $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}$. The Dirac oscillator is the minimal coupling with a linear potential; its spectrum is equally spaced in the non-relativistic limit. Pair creation in a bounded region is the same idempotent flip in a time-dependent potential.

Scattering. Compton, Møller and Bhabha are computed with the polarisation algebra of the photon, which is a bivector of $\mathbb{B}$; the trace theorems of the corpus replace the gamma-matrix traces.

The alternative formulations. Hestenes's spacetime algebra, Daviau's space Clifford form and Parra's four options are three rewritings of the same pair of equations. The s-vector representation and the Ding triality construction give the same spinor in three other letters. Triality is the statement that the three eight-dimensional representations of $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ rotate into one another; in $\mathbb{B}$ only the vector and the two spinor halves occur, so the third leg is absent.

4. Spin One and Above: the Proca Equation

The massive spin-1 equation.

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde F=4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\tilde A_\Phi,\qquad \tilde F=(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi-\mathrm{Sc}\bigl((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi\bigr)\;} $$

with the Lagrangian

$$ \mathcal L=-\tfrac14\sum_{{\imath},{\jmath}}F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}+2\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\sum_{\imath} A_{\imath} A_{\imath},\qquad F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}=\partial_{\imath} A_{\jmath}-\partial_{\jmath} A_{\imath} . $$

The subsidiary condition, and why it is forced. Taking the divergence kills the antisymmetric part and leaves

$$ 4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\langle\tilde\nabla_\Phi,\tilde A_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \partial_{\imath} A^{{\imath}}=0 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The Lorentz condition is not a representative choice for a massive field: it follows from the equation. The mass term breaks the local invariance, so the four components reduce to three, and the third polarisation is the longitudinal one. 5. The Informational Aspects

The superselection structure. The material-informational split is superselection: no physical operation maps a material element to an informational one, because the two are the fixed spaces of a conjugation and every operation is a congruence. A relative phase between the sectors is unobservable.

The relativistic qubit. The qubit is the spinor module, the state is the idempotent, and the boost acts on the representative by left multiplication and on the state by congruence:

$$ |u\rangle\longmapsto \tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}|u\rangle:=\mathsf M_2(\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}})|u\rangle,\qquad \tilde Q\longmapsto\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\tilde Q\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{*}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The two actions are different. The spinor action is unitary; the state action is not, because $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{*}\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}=e_t$ holds for the rotation part only. This is the same statement as "rotations are channels, boosts are not", now at the level of one qubit.

Frame-dependent entanglement. Entanglement is a property of a state in a frame. A boost changes the spin entropy, and the invariant part of the change is the Wigner rotation. The corpus states the invariant and the read value separately; the read value is not Lorentz invariant.

The multi-qubit boundary. One biquaternion carries exactly one qubit. Two qubits need

$$ \mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}), $$

and this is obstruction O13: the algebra has no internal tensor factorisation. The two routes are the module route (tensor the spinor modules) and the algebra route (tensor the algebras); they agree on the module and differ on the operations.

The corner collapse. In $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ the idempotents do not factorise freely: the four corners collapse onto one central bit, and the split is fixed by a sign condition rather than by a free choice. The Bell basis is the idempotent basis:

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\epsilon}=\tfrac14\bigl(e_t\otimes e_t+\epsilon_1\vec e_x\otimes\vec e_x+\epsilon_2\vec e_y\otimes\vec e_y+\epsilon_3\vec e_z\otimes\vec e_z\bigr)\;} $$

$$ \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id,singlet}}=\tfrac14\bigl(e_t\otimes e_t+\vec e_x\otimes\vec e_x+\vec e_y\otimes\vec e_y+\vec e_z\otimes\vec e_z\bigr), \qquad E_a^{2}=E_a,\quad E_aE_b=\delta_{ab}E_a,\quad \sum_a E_a=e_t\otimes e_t . $$

The non-trivial claim. The Bell states are not a basis chosen for convenience; they are the complete set of primitive idempotents of the tensor algebra. The concurrence is the determinant condition

$$ c_1c_4-c_2c_3=0\quad\text{for a product state}, $$

and the monogamy inequality follows from the same determinant.

Teleportation, GHZ and W. The three-qubit classification separates the GHZ class (the central bit is genuinely tripartite) from the W class (it is carried by a pair). Teleportation is the transport of an idempotent through a shared maximally entangled idempotent pair; the correction is a rotation rotor.

What the category does. It states one chain and then specialises it: what may be an integrand → the density → the field equation → the fluid → the thermodynamics → the force → the quantum field. Every sector is a declension of that skeleton, and a declension either passes all steps or names the step it breaks.

The single structural fact.

$$ \boxed{\;\text{a term in the density is decided by four independent filters, in this order: roster}\to\text{acceptance}\to\text{closure}\to\text{invariance; the pairing is orthogonal to all of them, and Helmholtz is asked afterwards of the equation.}\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The order is not exposition. Each filter's domain is supplied by the one before it, so a violation reported at filter $n$ cannot be repaired at filter $n+1$. Two claims run through the section and must not be merged. (i) The group decides which carriers exist for it and which terms survive invariance: the bound of three is the algebra's, the content inside it is the group's — $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has no scalar, $\mathrm{U}(1)$ has only the vector, $d_{abc}$ gives $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ a cubic that $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ lacks. The four sectors' actions coincide at dimension four only, and that coincidence is a fact about dimension and not about groups. (ii) Helmholtz decides whether any Lagrangian exists, and knows nothing of groups: the viscous term has no group at all.

The one-sentence version. The group decides which carriers exist and which terms survive; Helmholtz, which knows no group, decides whether an action exists at all.

1. The Six Filters, in Order

# filter the question failure means
1 carrier roster which fields exist at all no such object, in any sector
2 group acceptance can this force carry this carrier no field of that spin for this force
3 pairing can products be integrated no invariant square to offer
4 closure of the term list which candidates exist nothing to test
5 invariance which candidates survive the term is absent
6 Helmholtz (on the equation) does a density produce this equation no local action at all

Filter 3 is orthogonal and is the section's hub. It answers is this a scalar, not is this allowed.

The section is placed before The Lagrangian because every filter is a precondition of writing one. Its hub article, the-invariant-density-which-pairing-the-action-can-integrate, was the first entry inside The Lagrangian before the section existed; its content did not change, only its position and a hub section listing the six filters.

2. The Carrier Roster

$$ \boxed{\;\text{line }\mathbb{R}e_t:\ \dim 1,\ s=0,\ \text{trivial};\quad \text{plane }\Sigma(\vec u):\ \dim 2,\ s=\tfrac12,\ \text{left mult.};\quad \text{vector part }\mathfrak{k}:\ \dim 3,\ s=1,\ \text{conjugation}.\;} $$

Carrier $\ne$ closure. The corpus's own distinction (From the Spin to the Carriers and the Closures): the carriers are the line, the plane and the vector part, dims $1,2,3$, and $2s+1=\dim_\mathbb{R}$ holds there. Their closures are the centre $\mathbb{B}_C$ ($2$), the spinor space then the bispinor space ($4$, then $8$), and the material sector $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ ($4$). Writing "the material sector, dim $3$" or "the centre, dim $1$" is the standing error — a closure's name on a carrier's dimension.

Complete, and fixed by the algebra alone. No group is consulted, so no group can extend it. A sector may decline a carrier; it cannot add one.

The roster does not classify the derivative. Klein–Gordon, Dirac and Proca all use the one gradient $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ — sector $\Phi$, first order, left multiplication, $\tilde\nabla_\Phi(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^\natural=\Box_\Phi$. The carrier fixes the field's value space; the operator is shared. Do not write "the derivative in the ideal" or "the derivative in the centre".

The plane is a carrier only for the circle about its own axis. No real dim-2 subspace is invariant under the full rotation group; the smallest invariant one has dim $4$. That failure is why the closure exists. The transverse direction $\{\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+},\ \vec u_2\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}\}$ is the ideal's own frame, i.e. closure material, not the carrier.

The three arguments for no fourth: tensor products close on the three plus the symmetric square of the vector; the dimension count at $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ offers no fourth irreducible piece; and the boundary is the object's, which is why the Rarita–Schwinger action is named and not derived.

Trap. The absent carrier is fundamental. The symmetric square — where the linearised graviton sits — is a composite, and a composite is not a carrier. The graviton does not violate the count.

3. Group Acceptance

$$ \boxed{\;\text{carrier becomes a field}\iff \exists\,\rho: G\to GL(n)\ \text{with}\ n\in\{1,2,3\}\ \text{and the carrier's reality type.}\;} $$

force line, $n=1$ (spin 0) plane, $n=2$ (spin ½) vector part, $n=3$ (spin 1)
$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ matrix scalar fundamental adjoint
$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ declined triplet octet
$\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ doublet doublet, chiral adjoint + singlet
$\mathrm{U}(1)$ declined declined in the sector's own right the whole roster

The four causes of an empty slot, only the first of which is this filter:

cause example
group declines no scalar gluon
term not admitted gluon mass, photon mass
cannot be written natively chiral fermion of electroweak
not needed as a separate display the quark, inside QCD

The asymmetry to keep. The group can only decline. Acceptance is necessary and not sufficient: the electroweak fermion is accepted and has only a named term.

4. Invariance, as a Linear Condition

The candidate space is graded by dimension $d=k+\sum_i p_i\delta_i$ and taken modulo total derivatives ($\delta_i$: scalar/vector $1$, fermion $\tfrac32$).

$$ \boxed{\;T\ \text{admitted}\iff \delta_\alpha T=0\ \ \forall\alpha \iff T\in\ker\bigl(T\mapsto(\delta_\alpha T)_\alpha\bigr).\;} $$

Linear, therefore a subspace, therefore computable, therefore runnable term by term.

Four classes with the corpus's canonical examples:

class example
admitted $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\sum FF$ (cyclicity); $\bar\Xi(i\gamma D-m)\Xi$ (conjugate rep)
not admitted $m^2\sum A_\imath A_\imath$ for a connection
admitted only after a change of status the same mass term under Stueckelberg shift or Higgs insertion
admitted and inert $\theta\sum F\tilde F$ — invariant, a total derivative

5. One Obstruction, Four Groups

$$ \delta\bigl(m^2\textstyle\sum A_\imath A_\imath\bigr)=2m^2\sum A_\imath\bigl([A_\imath,\alpha]+\partial_\imath\alpha\bigr)\;\ne 0\ \ \text{unless } m=0 . $$

Identical in $\mathrm{U}(1)$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, $\mathrm{SU}(4)$; only the index range differs: $1,3,8,15$. The group's dimension never enters this verdict — the mass term is excluded by the form of the transformation law, which all four share. This is the one place the four groups agree; it is not evidence that the group decides nothing. What the group decides is the rest of the survivor set: the invariant ring is $\operatorname{tr}F^k$, $k=2,\dots,N$, so $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ has no cubic ($d_{abc}\equiv0$) and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ do — at $d=6$ the group decides $\operatorname{tr}F^3$ outright.

Verbatim, three of the corpus's four sentences:

"the gluon is therefore massless by what the action permits and not because its mass happens to be small"

"No mass for the fifteen. A mass term $\tfrac12m^{2}\sum_{a,\imath}A_{a,\imath}A_{a,\imath}$ is not invariant, and none is written. The masses come from $\langle\Xi\rangle$ and are the transition's."

"That is a declared choice of coefficients and not a symmetry the group supplies"

The contrast that defines the obstruction: Proca. Same carrier, same mass term, admitted — because a Proca field is a tensor and not a connection. The mass term there also yields $m^2\partial_\imath A_\imath=0$, i.e. the Lorenz condition as a consequence, not a gauge fixing. The Proca action is the anti-gauge action: its mass is exactly the candidate the gauge filter rejects, which is why a massive vector needs broken symmetry.

Scope limit to preserve. The obstruction is the inhomogeneous term, not the mass; and it needs a local symmetry — a global one forbids nothing.

6. Closure of the Term List

Three bounds: locality bounds derivatives, dimension bounds fields, the carrier bounds contractions. Hence the candidate list is finite.

At $d=4$ with one connection, one fermion, one scalar: $F^2$; $\bar\Xi\gamma D\Xi$; $(D\Xi)^\dagger D\Xi$; the quartics; the Yukawa; $\varepsilon FF$ (admitted, inert); $m^2A^2$ (candidate, fails invariance). Out of range: $\operatorname{tr}F^3$ ($d=6$), $\bar\Xi\sigma F\Xi$ ($d=5$), $(\bar\Xi\Xi)^2$ ($d=6$).

Each row fails for one of three unrelated reasons — no such term / not invariant / out of range. Naming which is part of the claim. $F^3$ is excluded by dimension and not by $d_{abc}$ (which vanishes only for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$).

Three exceptions, stated as part of the claim: - lower-dimension terms are excluded by symmetry, not dimension — which is why the primordial potential carries one quadratic, two cubics, three quartics; no $\Xi\to-\Xi$ is imposed there. - the topological term is admitted, $d=4$ and inert; $\theta_4$ is "a free parameter of the Lagrangian and not a function of the group". - closure is relative to the field content, which is the corpus's choice.

7. The Residue, and the Rule

Three sources of a coefficient, never interchangeable:

source example
basis $\tfrac12$ (element) and $\tfrac14$ (component), from $\operatorname{tr}(t_at_b)=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$; $n_a=2$ on the fourteen, $12$ on the corner, factor six
requirement outside invariance the overall sign, fixed by positivity (Euclidean)
declaration four of five quartics written; $\operatorname{tr}\Xi^3\operatorname{tr}\Xi$ set to zero

The rule: forced → reported with its basis; required → reported with its requirement; declared → reported with the count that shows it is a choice; none with no source at all.

Why it is not style. A coefficient with no source makes a disagreement with observation unattributable — framework failure or convention error. The labelling is what makes the physics part falsifiable.

Trap. Normalisation moves numbers; it does not move the count of objects. "No normalisation can repair a count" (sixteen arrays, fifteen generators).

8. The Five Rungs (rung dictionary, one row)

Only two things change across the rungs: the order of the kinetic term (first at Schrödinger; second at Klein–Gordon, Proca and the matrix) and the content of the algebraic term (potential, mass, quartic). Nothing else does, which is why the same five steps run on all five.

Trap. The overall sign and the normalisation are conventions the corpus fixes rather than derives.

9. The Chain, Stated Once

Noether → the connection forced by a local symmetry → curvature and Bianchi → the same non-abelian → the Yang–Mills equation. Three carriers cut the spin bands. The renormalisation group is a measure object and sits with the Euclidean reading, the anomaly and the index.

10. The Criterion, the Five Steps, and the Rungs Above

The criterion: one coordinate's Euler–Lagrange equation in the polar chart — of warrant and not of content. The five steps: action → polar word → type decomposition → order reduction → base-versus-internal. Then thermodynamics (determinacy, consistency, integrability) and the force side (transport differentiated; a matrix charge forces a commutator in place of a pairing).

Trap. The viscous term has no action is a theorem, not a report of failure to find one; and the criterion is Helmholtz at unit multiplier and one named coordinate — strictly narrower.

11. The Quantum Continuation

One postulate imported: bracket → commutator. Then the ladder, the objects, the four measure results, and the promotion applied to the connection. The six canonical quantisations sit in the band of the field each quantises; the section carries no carrier of its own.

12. Helmholtz and the Non-Interference

$$ \boxed{\;E\ \text{variational at unit multiplier}\iff DE=(DE)^\dagger\ \text{in } L^2.\;} $$

Three conditions for second order: $\Lambda_{ab}=\Lambda_{ba}$; $A_{ab}+A_{ba}=2\partial_\imath\Lambda_{ab}$; $B_{ab}-B_{ba}=\tfrac12\partial_\imath(A_{ab}-A_{ba})$. The damped oscillator fails the second for every $\gamma\ne0$.

The four grades — keep apart:

grade example repair
unit multiplier, invertible Klein–Gordon, Proca —
variational, not invertible Maxwell, Yang–Mills add a fixing term / ghosts
constant local multiplier Dirac, multiplier $\gamma^0$; $\psi^\dagger\gamma^0=\bar\psi$ —
not variational at any local multiplier the viscous term none

Only the fourth is Helmholtz's veto. Gauge degeneracy is the second and must never be reported as a missing action.

The one interaction, one-way: invariance selects a subspace of densities; Helmholtz characterises their image; narrowing the domain cannot enlarge the image. So a non-variational equation is non-variational in every sector.

Blindness, twice: the viscous term has no group at all; and the verdict does not move when the group changes.

Emphasis trap. In this corpus Helmholtz vetoes one object (viscosity) and passes every carrier used; invariance excludes far more. Helmholtz is not the deep filter and invariance the shallow one — they answer different questions.

Summary

  • Filters in order: roster → acceptance → (pairing) → closure → invariance → Helmholtz. Each supplies the next one's domain.
  • Roster: line/plane/vector part, $1,2,3$, no fourth; composites do not count; carrier $\ne$ closure ($2,4,4$).
  • The derivative is shared: $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ in all three free equations.
  • Invariance: $\delta_\alpha T=0$; a kernel, hence a subspace, hence no scale, no sign, no choice.
  • One obstruction, four groups: index ranges $1,3,8,15$. The mass term's exclusion is group-blind — the one place four groups agree. The survivor set is not: $\operatorname{tr}F^k$, $k\le N$, gives $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ no cubic, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ one. At $d=6$ the group decides $\operatorname{tr}F^3$.
  • Closure: finite at $d=4$; three bounds; exceptions stated (cubics, $\theta$, field content).
  • Residue: forced / required / declared with its count.
  • Helmholtz: group-blind; four grades; only "not variational at any local multiplier" is a veto.

Guide to the Internal Summary Articles

Introduction

What this file is. A writing guide for the internal-summary-* family of articles_internal. It fixes the boundary, the skeleton, the paragraph grammar, the claim ledger, and above all the two rules of content: cut what a machine regenerates, quote what it cannot.

Who reads the output. An AI agent that must reason inside the corpus without reading the category. It has a small window and no memory of the articles. It does not need to be taught. It needs the objects it cannot guess, the equations it must reproduce, the claims that contradict the default, and the traps.

The rule that governs the genre. A summary is a cache, not a digest and not a lecture. It stores the information of the corpus at the lowest token cost, in the symbols of the corpus, so that any line can be audited in one fetch and any sharp claim can be quoted without opening the article.

How to use this guide. §1 and §2 give the two rules of content, and they outrank everything else. §3 fixes the boundary, §4 the shape, §5 to §8 the parts. §9 lists what must be quoted, §10 what must be cut, §11 the budget, §12 the traps. §13 and §14 are the two passes, in this order: the menu audit, which decides whether this file is the summary of a category the menu still names, and the check pass, which decides whether the file is well formed. The order is not a preference. A file can pass §14 and be the summary of a category that no longer exists.

1. Internal First, Machine First

Internal. The file lives in articles_internal/, which build.py does not build and maths.md and physics.md do not list. It is not for the reader of the site. It carries no HTML link, no navigation, no introduction for a newcomer and no apology for jargon. A slug in backticks is enough.

Machine first. The reader is a model, not a person. It reads fast, it has no patience and no memory of the articles, and it already knows standard mathematics. Anything a competent model regenerates from a definition is dead weight in a summary, and dead weight costs exactly as many tokens as the load-bearing line beside it.

The two consequences. Cut the obvious. Quote the sharp. Every remaining section of this guide is one of these two rules applied.

What follows for tone. No motivation, no preamble, no "this is interesting because", no praise, no ranking of articles, no history of the category unless the history is the claim. A sentence that only eases the reader spends tokens on a machine that needs no easing.

2. The Two Rules of Content

The rule of the prior. Ask of every line: can a competent model produce this line from what it already knows, given the name of the object? If yes, delete it. The question is not whether the line is true or useful to a human. It is whether the line changes what the model can compute.

The rule of the irrecoverable line. Ask of every other line: could the model recover this by reasoning? If not, the line must be present and must be verbatim. Four kinds pass this test, and they are the whole content of a summary: the sharp claim, the structuring fact, the load-bearing equation, and the declared convention, trap or refusal.

The two rules together are one test. A summary is correct when every line either cannot be regenerated or should never have been written. A file of regenerable lines teaches nothing and costs everything.

The three checks on a candidate line. Does it change a derivation the agent will perform? Can the model regenerate it from a definition plus the name of the object? Is it quotable verbatim from a named article? Two noes and a yes is a cut. The third check alone is not enough: a textbook identity copied faithfully is still a line the model already had.

Consequence for balance. A category of forty articles with three sharp claims gives three claims and a table of objects. A category of eleven articles with thirty claims gives thirty claims. The length follows the claim density, not the article count. This is the opposite of the instinct of a human digest, and it is why the existing summaries range from four to sixteen sections for similar article counts.

3. Fixing the Boundary

Three inputs, one output. A list of articles, a section of maths.md or physics.md, and a whole category produce the same file. Only the boundary comes from the input. Everything after this section is identical.

One file per ## heading, and only per ## heading. The menu's categories are its ## lines; its ### lines are the subsections inside a category and become sections of the file, never files of their own. Nothing in articles_internal/ may hold a boundary that the menu does not name. The rule is stated here because it belongs to the boundary; it is checked in §13, because a single file cannot see its own category.

The re-cut, as it happened. The umbrella file Biquaternion Universe Particle Physics covered a ## category of one hundred and ten anchors. The menu was re-cut: three of its subsections became categories of their own, its gauge machinery was absorbed by Primordial Force, its mass spectrum by Symmetry Breaking, and twenty-four of the one hundred and ten anchors left the menu altogether. The umbrella file had passed every check it was given, because its shape was never the defect. §13 gives the per-subsection numbers and the procedure that catches this.

Resolve the boundary physically. Do not trust the input string. Extract the anchors of the section:

awk '/^## /{sec=substr($0,4)} sec=="<Category>" && /<a href=/{print}' physics.md

The count of anchors is the article count. State it in words, as the existing files do: twenty-nine articles, eighty-two articles, one hundred and one articles.

Recompute the count at the time of writing. The menus drift: an article is added, a block moves, a subsection is renamed. Recompute the count from the anchors at every write, and recompute a count taken by subtraction as well. The family has already carried both kinds of stale number, and a stale count is a defect the next reader inherits.

Record the exceptions. Two kinds, both in the Introduction. Articles of the cluster absent from the menu: the gravity summary once named five, and it now names all five again in the paragraph that records the defect as closed, because the count moved when the menu changed. Articles of another category read here: the same summary names two and says where they sit. Never silently widen or narrow the boundary.

Coverage means claim coverage, not article coverage. Every article inside the boundary must be reachable: cited in a **Source.** line, named in a prose enumeration, or listed in Further Reading. It need not get a block. An article with no irrecoverable line is a member of a list, and that is the right fate for it. The check is mechanical:

grep -o 'articles_physics/[a-z0-9-]*\.md' <file> | sort -u | wc -l

The relativity summary cites all one hundred of its articles, and the electromagnetic summary all twenty-eight: a slug in backticks is the citation form, in a **Source.** line, a member list or Further Reading. The defect to avoid is a sharp claim that no source line can audit.

4. The Skeleton

Six parts, always in this order. The fifteen existing summaries share it without exception.

Part Job Share of the file
Title line # __Internal Summary of the <Name>__ one line
Introduction what the file is, what the category does, the structural fact, the one-sentence version 10 to 20 per cent
Numbered sections ## 1. <Object>, one per group of claims 65 to 80 per cent
## Summary the formulas that carry the category, the non-textbook results, the refusals 5 to 10 per cent
## Summary of Notation symbol table, used symbols only 10 to 20 lines
## Further Reading reading order, then the traps 3 to 8 lines

Cut the sections by claim, not by article. Two articles that yield one claim give one item. One article that yields four claims gives four items and may take a whole section. The menu order is editorial; the alphabet is meaningless.

Number the sections. Arabic numerals, a full stop, a title that names an object and not an activity. The Field Strength and the Single Equation is right. Discussion is not.

5. The Introduction

Six bold leads, in this order. They are the signature of the family, and an agent that sees them knows the contract.

  • What this file is. The boundary, the count, and the content: objects, boxed equations, non-trivial claims. Then the three sentences that never vary in meaning: no proofs; an agent re-derives them; every formula is copied from the source article named in the section.
  • What the category does. Two or three sentences. Separate what the category develops from what it records and what it refuses. The primordial force summary does this in a paragraph — what it constructs, what it records, what it refuses — and the separation runs through the file.
  • The single structural fact. One display, usually boxed, that organises the category: the vacuum as a minimal idempotent, the metric as a Gram matrix of a frame, the field strength as one pure vector in two halves. It is the highest-value line in the file, because the agent cannot recover it from the objects alone. When no single fact organises the category, say so and give the spine of three or four lines, as the operators summary does.
  • The non-trivial claim. One paragraph under the structural fact, saying why the fact is not a restatement. Load-bearing, never omitted.
  • The one-sentence version. One sentence, no display. What the agent quotes when it has room for one line.
  • How to use it. Optional, and only when the reading order matters. The universe summary names the sections to read first and the section of traps to read before writing.

Add the subsection table when the menu has subsections. Two columns, the subsection and what it settles. Thirteen of the fifteen summaries carry one, eight in that two-column form and five with an article count added; one of the two that omit it repeats the menu's subsection names as section titles, and the other is the exception named in §13. The table is cheap and it doubles as the skeleton.

Add three optional leads, and only when true. The price, when the category buys its result with an obstruction. The discipline of the category, when the classification of claims is the content, as in gravity. A defect to record, when the menu or the corpus is incomplete.

6. The Sections

The per-item grammar. Four moves, in this order, for every item that carries an irrecoverable line:

**<Object name in bold.>** One sentence of definition, only if the name does not fix it.

$$
<the display, copied from the article>
$$

**The non-trivial claim.** One paragraph: what the display says that a competent
model would not produce, and what it costs.

**Source.** `articles_physics/<slug>.md`, `articles_physics/<slug>.md`.

The definition is optional; the claim is not. Drop the defining sentence whenever the name and the display fix the meaning. Never drop the claim paragraph. A block with a display and no claim is a textbook fragment, and the model already has the textbook.

One claim per item. If an item has no irrecoverable claim, it belongs in a one-line list inside the section. The symmetry-breaking summary names its two registers by count rather than by list, and spends its blocks on the articles of principle. That ratio is correct, not lazy.

The claim is stated against something. The constraint is a consequence and not an extra condition. The algebra of one-sided operators is seven-dimensional, not eight. A claim with no contrast is a definition in disguise, and a definition in disguise is a cut.

Box only the load-bearing display. \boxed{} marks the equation an agent must reproduce. The existing files use one to fourteen boxes; the electromagnetism summary uses eight for twenty-eight articles, the universe summary one, because its load-bearing content is its tables. The box count is a budget, not a quota.

Use ### when a section passes four items. The intersection rule of the corpus applies unchanged: give every intersection a heading, define before tabulating.

Tables carry counts, dimensions and ledgers. Which subspace, which dimension, which role; which region, which invariant, which reading. A table is the cheapest way to store an enumeration, and the right way to store one.

Close the section with **Source.** when it maps to a set of articles. Backticked relative paths, .md extension, comma separated. Every summary closes each mapped section this way. Where a part maps to a set too large to name twice, a member list of backticked bare slugs is the alternative. Pick one form per file and hold it.

Do not repeat a display. The same formula in three sections is a sign that the sections are one section.

Do not write the algebra out. No intermediate step of a derivation, no chain of substitutions, no expansion the model performs faster than it reads. The summary holds the endpoints and the obstruction, not the route.

7. The Claim Ledger

Four labels, kept apart. Established: proved inside the corpus. Carried or transcribed: written in the letters, not derived from them. Imported: taken from the literature, with the reference named. Open or refused: no construction exists, and the article says why.

The summary never promotes a claim. A transcribed formula is not upgraded to derived by appearing in a compressed file. The gravity summary states the rule and applies it to itself: even the linearised sector is carried.

Every summary says what its category refuses. A required part of ## Summary, not a nuance. The relativity summary names two refusals: the dynamics is imported, and no spatial axis is canonical. The symmetry-breaking summary names eight, each one a theorem or a stated postulate rather than an apology. A refusal is the highest-value line in the file for a machine, because it stops a confident wrong derivation.

An obstruction is a result and is written as one. A category that cannot reach a result is not a weaker category; it is a category with a proved boundary. Use the sentences of the article.

8. The Formulas

Copied, never reconstructed. A display is copied from the article named beside it, with the same symbols, hats, primes and order of factors. A formula retyped from memory is a new formula.

No formula is invented in the summary. If the map needs an equation no article writes, the map needs a sentence naming the gap. The one-sentence version is prose for this reason: prose cannot drift into a false identity.

Follow the corpus conventions. Display math in $$, indented four spaces inside a list. Never let $...$ span a line break. A prime is a superscript on the letter and the subscript stays the index it names: $\tilde Q'_{\Phi}$, never a prime below. \dddot and \slashed are template macros. No array tables; a table is Markdown.

One symbol, one meaning, across the file. The index convention holds here exactly as in the articles: low indices, no raised spacetime index, the weight $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}$ for a contraction.

The recomputation rule. Every count, dimension and coefficient asserted in the summary is recomputed on at most one hundred elements before it is written. A claim no check reaches is left out, or is written with the word recorded in front of it.

9. What Must Be Quoted

Four kinds of line, and each is quoted verbatim from a named article.

The sharp claim. The statement that contradicts the default reading and that the model gets wrong unguided. The default of the model is the textbook; the summary stores the delta.

  • The light cone is the zero-divisor cone.
  • A boost acts on the state as a rescaling and not as a channel, so the quantum operations are exactly the rotations.
  • The algebra of one-sided operators is seven-dimensional, not eight, and the missing direction is exactly the two-sided sandwich.
  • The two classical invariants of the field are the real and imaginary parts of one biquaternion norm, so a null field is a zero divisor.

The structuring fact. The one object or identity that organises a whole category, stated once at the top, so that the agent holds the frame before the items. It earns a display and a box.

  • The vacuum is a minimal idempotent: "no particles" is a rank-one projection inside the algebra and not a vector added to the theory.
  • The metric is the Gram matrix of a frame of four elements, so the algebra carries any Lorentzian metric at the price of a frame field.
  • The unitary group of $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, and $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{SU}(3)=8>4$ excludes colour.

The load-bearing equation. The equation an agent must reproduce to work in the category, copied with its glyphs. Not every equation of the article: the one that states the result.

  • $\mathrm{Vect}\bigl((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi\bigr)=\sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde F$ together with $\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde F=-\sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde J_\Phi$,
  • $\tilde Q_2=\tilde U\tilde Q\tilde U^{*}$ with $\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}=\sqrt{-\tfrac{i}{c}\tilde V_\Phi^{\natural}}$,
  • $\mathbb{B}^{e}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$.

The declared convention, the trap and the refusal. The line that overrides a plausible default: which metric, which sign, which conjugation, which index rule, which object is not available. A model that guesses here writes a wrong derivation with a correct-looking equation.

  • One metric, $\delta_{{\imath}{\jmath}}=(1,1,1,1)$; no raised spacetime index anywhere.
  • The Lorentzian sign comes from $i^{2}=-1$ and not from a metric.
  • All five articles the gravity cluster once reported as absent are now listed; the defect is closed and the boundary is the menu's thirty-eight.
  • Colour is excluded by the dimension count, not merely missing from one construction.

The test for a candidate quote. It must be short, auditable against one article, and impossible to regenerate. A quote that passes all three is worth ten lines of prose about it.

10. What Must Be Cut

Six kinds of line, and each is the default of a human digest.

  • The calculus chain. The intermediate step, the substitution, the integration by parts. The model performs it and does not need to read it. Keep the endpoints and the obstruction.
  • The standard definition. The definition of a commutator, a Fock space, a covariant derivative, a Hilbert space. The model has it. Define only the object whose name does not fix the meaning in this corpus.
  • The textbook derivation. A proof of a known theorem restaged in the letters of the corpus. Cite the article; do not restage it.
  • The restatement of the abstract. The aim, the programme, the plan. The summary stores the result.
  • The motivation and the transition. "It is worth noting", "as we have seen", "this is interesting because". A machine needs none of them.
  • The echo. A closing paragraph that repeats the sections. The three closing blocks are the only compression allowed, and they carry content, not echo.

What looks like content and is not. A boxed identity that restates a definition. A table of symbols already defined in the body. A paragraph whose only verb is is. Each costs the tokens of a sharp claim and buys nothing.

11. Length, Density and the Budget

Measured on the eighteen existing files. Sizes run from 15 KB to 72 KB, lines from 229 to 1000, numbered sections from 4 to 22, non-trivial claims from 4 to 77, boxed displays from 1 to 14, citation blocks from 4 to 22, total 7,342 lines.

Density is claim density, not article density. The electromagnetism summary gives every item a block and boxes eight displays for twenty-eight articles. The relativity summary gives the spine a block and the enumerations a table, at one hundred and one articles. The universe summary boxes nothing and carries sixteen sections of structure. Each is right for its claim count.

Budget rule. One block per irrecoverable claim, one line per article that carries no claim, one table per enumeration of more than four objects. Nothing else. A section that exceeds it is either over-written or is really two sections.

The floor is the acceptance test. A file that fails the test is too short, whatever its length. A file that repeats itself is too long, whatever its length.

The acceptance test, restated for a machine. Given only the summary, an agent can name every object the boundary defines; write the result equations; avoid the traps; say what the category refuses; and know which claims are carried rather than established. Nothing else is required, and anything else is optional.

12. Ten Traps

  • The trap of the abstract. Summarising the aim instead of the result. Fix: quote the result equation, then state the claim.
  • The trap of the pedagogic line. Writing for a human who needs easing. Fix: delete the easing, keep the claim.
  • The trap of the drift count. Trusting a stated number, or a remembered category. Fix: recompute — the count from the anchors, the category from the menu's ## headings, and both at the time of writing. §13 is the check; a number or a title inherited from the last audit is not evidence.
  • The trap of the sample. Mapping a third of the boundary and claiming the category. Fix: the coverage grep, plus a sentence naming what is only a list and why.
  • The trap of the new proof. Adding a derivation not in the corpus. Fix: delete it, name the gap.
  • The trap of the promotion. Writing a transcribed result as derived. Fix: the claim ledger, line by line.
  • The trap of the boxed definition. Boxing an identity so the file looks dense. Fix: box the result, leave the definition plain.
  • The trap of the single reading. Reporting one sector of a two-sector result. Fix: state the pair, as the electromagnetism summary does with the electric and magnetic halves.
  • The trap of the invented symbol. Introducing a glyph the articles do not use. Fix: the Notation table is a contract.
  • The trap of the silent exception. Leaving out an article that does not fit. Fix: name it in the Introduction with its reason.

13. The Menu Audit

What this section is for. The check pass of §14 validates a file against this guide. It cannot validate the premise: that the file is the file the menu asks for. A file can pass every grep, quote every article correctly, and still be the summary of a category that no longer exists. This section is the pass that decides the premise, and it runs first.

The failure it exists to catch, with the numbers. The family once carried an umbrella file, Biquaternion Universe Particle Physics, over a ## category of the same name holding one hundred and ten anchors in six subsections: the general gauge machinery (eighteen), the strong force (twenty-four), the electroweak force (thirty), electromagnetism (thirty), the mass spectrum (five), focus on informational aspects (three). The menu was then re-cut. Some of those subsections were promoted to categories of their own — Strong Force (twenty-seven anchors now), Electroweak Force (thirty-one), Electromagnetism (twenty-eight); the gauge machinery was absorbed by Primordial Force and the mass spectrum by Symmetry Breaking; and twenty-four of the one hundred and ten anchors left the menu altogether. An audit run before the re-cut reported fourteen files, zero missing, all passing. Every sentence of that report was true of a menu that had ceased to exist. The count of categories had gone from thirteen to fifteen, and no per-file grep could see it, because the file's shape does not encode its category.

Rule zero: the set of files is the set of ## headings. Not a superset, not a subset. Fifteen categories, fifteen files. Both directions are defects: an extra file holds a boundary the menu does not name, and a missing file leaves a category with no cache at all. This is the only rule in the guide whose violation is invisible in a single file, which is why it is checked over the family and not per file.

Step 1: parse at ## and ###, and nowhere else. A ## line opens a category; a ### line opens a subsection inside the current category; #### and below are prose structure and carry no boundary. Anchors are the <a href="articles_physics/<slug>.html"> lines, and the heading they sit under is their assignment. The parse is the whole of the boundary: an anchor belongs to exactly one category, because it appears once under one heading. Nothing about the previous file, the previous audit or the article's subject enters here.

awk '/^## /{sec=substr($0,4)} sec=="<Category>" && /<a href=/{print}' physics.md

Step 2: count, and make the counts add. The article count is the number of anchors, written in words in the Introduction. The subsection counts must sum to it; a subsection that is empty of anchors is still named, and a category whose anchors sit above its first ### is stated as such rather than folded into a subsection. Recompute at every write, never from the previous file, and recompute by subtraction as well: a residual "and the rest" is a number nobody has looked at.

Step 3: the alignment is asserted, never left implied. Three forms, in descending order of strength. A table in the Introduction — subsection, article count, the sections that serve it — which is the norm, thirteen of the fifteen files; every subsection of the category appears in it, including the ones served by a single section, and a section that serves two subsections appears in two rows. Section titles that repeat the menu's subsection names in menu order, which is how the thermodynamics file carries all four of its subsections. Or menu order alone, acceptable only where the sections really are one per subsection. The table is not decoration: it is the only sentence in the file that asserts the correspondence, so it carries the menu's own counts and can be checked against step 2. A file cut by object, with no table and no ordered titles, has asserted nothing — the universe file is in that state, and its alignment is the one thing a reader cannot check.

Step 4: cover, and know what counts as a citation. A member is cited when its slug appears as articles_physics/<slug>.md or as a backticked bare slug. Prose mention without backticks does not count, because the grep is the reader and the grep cannot see it. Coverage is claim-optional and citation-mandatory: an article with no irrecoverable line belongs in a one-line list, and the list is a citation.

Step 5: diff in both directions, and read the two lists differently. MISSING is a hard failure: the file falls short of its category. Zero is the acceptance condition, and a non-zero count is a list to close, never a sentence to soften. OUTSIDE is a report, not a failure, and it is expected to be non-zero — a summary that records a cross-reference or a recorded exception has to name the article, and the family's current total is fifty-three. What is illegitimate is an unclassified reach: a slug outside the category that is neither an explicit cross-reference nor an exception recorded in the Introduction. Classify each line of the report; the count of outside entries is not a metric and must never be quoted as one.

With a map.txt holding one file<TAB>category pair per line — a tab, because the category names carry spaces — the whole audit is one run:

import re, sys
menu = open('physics.md').read()
cats, cur, sub = {}, None, None
for line in menu.splitlines():
    if line.startswith('## '):
        cur, sub = line[3:].strip(), None
        cats[cur] = {}
    elif line.startswith('### '):
        sub = line[4:].strip()
    m = re.search(r'articles_physics/([a-z0-9-]+)\.html', line)
    if m and cur:
        cats[cur].setdefault(sub, []).append(m.group(1))

pairs = [l.rstrip('\n').split('\t') for l in open('map.txt') if l.strip()]
files = {f for f, _ in pairs}
failures = 0
for cat in cats:
    if cat not in {c for _, c in pairs}:
        print(f'MISSING FILE   {cat}'); failures += 1
for f, cat in pairs:
    if cat not in cats:
        print(f'STALE CATEGORY {f} -> {cat}'); failures += 1; continue
    t = open(f).read()
    cited = set(re.findall(r'articles_physics/([a-z0-9-]+)\.md', t))
    cited |= {s for s in re.findall(r'`([a-z0-9]+(?:-[a-z0-9]+)+)`', t)
              if not s.startswith('internal-summary')}   # a summary is not an article
    bound = {s for v in cats[cat].values() for s in v}
    missing, outside = sorted(bound - cited), sorted(cited - bound)
    if missing: failures += 1
    print(f'{cat:42s} BOUNDARY {len(bound):4d} CITED {len(bound & cited):4d} '
          f'MISSING {len(missing):3d} OUTSIDE {len(outside):3d}')
    for label, slugs in (('MISSING', missing), ('OUTSIDE', outside)):
        for s in slugs:
            print(f'    {label:8s} {s}')
print(f'{len(files)} files, {len(cats)} categories, {failures} failures')
sys.exit(1 if failures else 0)

The fourth re-cut, 2026-11-27, and the first that adds rather than dissolves. Biquaternion Universe Fluid Physics and Thermodynamics was created from Biquaternion Universe Action, taking its last three sections: the polar chart with the five-step skeleton (12), the thermal closure (20) and the force (2), thirty-four entries, leaving Action at fifty. The rule being applied is not the one that dissolved the two thermal categories: it is the separation of what the Lagrangian forces from what it merely permits. The integrand, the density, its variation and the promotion of the bracket are forced — remove one and nothing follows. The fluid reading, its thermal closure and the balance it yields are permitted — the chain reaches its end, and every sector's field content is unchanged, if a reader stops at the promotion. A construction that is optional to its own chain is a category and not a section, because a section is what a reader must pass through to reach the next one and this block is what a reader may decline. The check that the cut is real: of the fifty entries that stayed, thirty-eight cite nothing that left, and the twelve that do point at the thermal machinery (partition function, chemical potential, Matsubara, the local thermal state) rather than at the chart — the quantum branch is thermal at its machinery and fluid at nothing. Those twelve pointers run forward into the category below, which is ordinary in this corpus: Action's own outbound citations already point to later categories three hundred and nineteen times out of nine hundred and forty. Per-carrier declines did not move: each band keeps its own fluid slot.

The third re-cut, 2026-11-27. Biquaternion Universe Relativistic Quantum Thermodynamics was dissolved and its file retired. It failed the test that retired the fluid category, but for the opposite reason: not because its articles duplicated the bands, but because the object they state is one construction — the closure of a fluid into a substance — and a construction is a section of Biquaternion Universe Action, not a regime. Seventeen of the twenty-seven became ### From the Fluid Equations to Thermodynamics Equations, four subsections and a ledger, placed after From the Field Equation to the Fluid; five went to #### Thermality under Biquaternion Universe Relativity, where the parameter itself is defined geometrically and algebraically; four went to Horizons and Their Thermodynamics under Biquaternion Universe Gravity and Cosmology, where the wedge and the horizon are the objects; and one, the Reeh–Schlieder theorem, went to The Ladder and the State Space. The bands gained a rung, #### The fluid's thermodynamic equations and their solutions, emitted after the fluid slot in the six bands that have fluid equations and empty in all six until a band's fluid is read thermally; the twenty bands without fluid equations do not carry it yet.

The second re-cut, 2026-11-27. Biquaternion Universe Quantum Fields was dissolved and its file retired. The category was the promotion layer and not a regime — its four spin bands duplicated the bands Biquaternion Universe Relativistic Quantum Theory already carries, and its own hub counted the renormalization group among its sixteen although that article sat in Biquaternion Universe Action. Twenty-four of its articles are now the section ### From the Field Equation to the Quantum Field in Action, forty sit in the #### The quantum apparatus slot of the carrier each one quantises, and five are in the categories whose object they state. The counts in the table are the anchors of the menu after that re-cut: fourteen categories, seven hundred and thirty-nine anchors, and every one of the sixty-nine resolves. The retired file carries a banner naming the destination of each article, and the run recorded below is the earlier one, kept as history.

The names differ, deliberately: the folder names the object (the strong interaction), the menu names the section (Strong Force). What may not differ is the partition. The table is also the place to notice that the two largest categories carry one hundred and ninety-one and one hundred and thirteen anchors, while ten of the thirteen carry under sixty and still have a file of their own: a file is sized by its category and not by an idea of importance.

The three failure modes, and the repair of each. A stale category: the file's category is not a ## heading. Repair is not renaming — the members must be re-assigned by the parse, and the file is then deleted or re-cut. The umbrella case is the worked example: twenty-four of its anchors no longer existed, so no retitling could have saved it, and the five categories that absorbed the rest already had files or needed one. A missing file: a category with no cache. Repair is to write it, from the anchors, not from the neighbouring files. A split boundary: two files covering the same anchors, which OUTSIDE exposes in both directions. Repair is to re-derive each from its own category; the parse assigns every anchor to exactly one, so there is always a unique right answer.

When the menu is re-cut, the order is fixed. Parse the new menu; list the files that exist; diff the two sets; repair the stale and the missing; only then touch prose. A file that is being re-cut is written from the anchors of the new category, never by editing the old text toward the new name: the two categories' memberships differ, and the sentences inherited from the old boundary are exactly the ones that will be wrong.

What the audit does not prove. It does not prove that a citation is apt: a slug in Further Reading is a reachable article, not a correctly used one. It does not prove that a claim is true, or that a display is quoted at the right scale. Those are the article-level check and the reading of §14. The audit proves one thing, and without it nothing else is worth proving: that this file is the summary of this category, in this menu, today.

14. The Check Pass

Run these before handing the file over. Each is mechanical, and the shape is not visible while writing. The menu audit of §13 comes first: a well-formed file for a category the menu does not name is worse than a badly formed file for one that it does.

grep -c '^## ' <file>                      # skeleton: Introduction, N, Summary, Notation, Reading
grep -n '^## \|^# ' <file>                 # numbered sections in order, no gap
grep -c 'boxed' <file>                     # the box budget, against the claim count
grep -c '^\*\*Source\.\*\*' <file>         # sources, or none at all
grep -o 'articles_[a-z]*/[a-z0-9-]*\.md' <file> | sort -u | wc -l   # coverage
grep -c 'non-trivial claim' <file>         # one claim per block, or a list instead
grep -nE '(\$\\square\$|\\\(\\square\\\))[ \t]*$' <file>           # no proof-end marks
awk '{n=gsub(/\$/,"&"); if (n%2) print NR": odd number of dollars"}' <file>   # no open math, code blocks excluded

The last check skips fenced blocks. A shell command inside a fence carries its own dollars and is not math.

Then read the file as the machine reader. Six questions. Does the Introduction state the count, the boundary and the structural fact? Is every block either a claim or a list? Is every quote short and auditable against one article? Does any line merely restate a definition the model already has? Does the Summary carry the formulas, the non-textbook results and the refusals? Does the file say what the category refuses?

A file that passes the greps and fails the reading is not done. The greps prove the shape; they cannot prove the density.

Summary

An internal summary is a machine cache of a boundary of the corpus. It is internal: not built, not in the menus, no HTML. It is machine-first: no motivation, no easing, no restaged derivation, no standard definition.

Two rules carry the genre. Cut every line a competent model regenerates from a definition and a name. Quote every line it cannot: the sharp claim, the structuring fact, the load-bearing equation, the declared convention, the trap, the refusal. Length follows claim density and not article count, which is why the existing files range from four to twenty-two sections for similar article counts.

Four disciplines keep it honest. The boundary is re-derived from the menu at every write, never remembered from the last audit, because the menu is the only authority on which category a file is the summary of. Every quote is copied verbatim from a named article. Every count is recomputed at the time of writing. Every refusal is stated, because a proved boundary is the line that stops a confident wrong derivation.

The audit precedes the writing. A file that is the summary of a category the menu no longer names cannot be improved by better sentences, better quotes or a better Introduction; it has to be re-cut or deleted, and the members it held have to be found in the categories that now own them. This is the one defect the genre has actually suffered, and the one the guide therefore checks first.

The fifteen physics files are the template, one per menu category. Read the electromagnetism summary for the item grammar, the relativity summary for the closing three and the sharp claims, the symmetry-breaking summary for the cut of a large category with a theorem in place of an argument, and the gravity summary for the claim ledger and the record of a defect.

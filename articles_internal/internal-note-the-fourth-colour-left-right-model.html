Internal Note: The Four Colours Left–Right Model

The Answer

The model is $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, with three couplings that are not required to be equal and a spin-0 sector whose multiplet content it does not fix. It keeps the group, the sixteen, charge quantisation, the four anomaly cancellations, the twenty-one bosons, the six leptoquarks and the selection rule for $K_S\to\mu e$. It drops one thing, the equality $g_4=g_L=g_R$, and claims nothing in its place.

The spin-0 sector is kept whole, and only its multiplet list is left open. A spin-zero field takes its value in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module, with a central Klein–Gordon operator, and the model inherits that structure unchanged. What it does not fix is which gauge multiplets those scalars occupy: $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ are declarations on top of the sector, not consequences of the fourth colour. Because the multiplet list is open, no fermion mass relation is predicted, and none can contradict the model.

It contains the Standard Model. Break $\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ at $M_C$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\to\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ at $M_R$, and what is left is exactly $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$, with the sixteen reducing to the fifteen Standard Model fields plus $\nu_{\mathrm{neu},R}$. Below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model, so every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, for the same reason. §1.6 is the proof.

It is in contradiction with no experiment. Every claim the model makes is either inherited from the Standard Model below $M_R$ or a bound on a free parameter. Below $M_R$ the three measured couplings are reproduced by construction, with $g_R$ absorbing the difference, and a solution exists for every $M_R

What it costs is the ability to be confirmed. Every remaining statement is either a bound on a free mass or a structural fact already known to hold.

The strip is not the deletion of a derived prediction. The equality was written as conditional on parity: the four couplings collapse to two or to one, with $g_L=g_R$ marked if parity is imposed. The strip is the refusal to impose a parity assumption, so no unmet prediction is left behind. The failure that remains is colour, not the angle: at $10^{13}$ GeV the angle reaches $\tfrac38$ and $\alpha_3^{-1}$ is $13\%$ short (§2.5). Keeping the equality and admitting that the unified version is falsified is also defensible, and §10.3 states that position.

The Declaration: The Fourth Colour as the Unit Line

D.1 The statement, in one line.

$$ \text{fourth colour}=\mathbb{C}e_t,\qquad \text{three colours}=B_V=\mathbb{C}e_x\oplus\mathbb{C}e_y\oplus\mathbb{C}e_z , $$

with

$$ N_c=\dim B_V=4-1=3,\qquad P_t+P_V=\mathrm I . $$

Everything structural in this note rests on this line, and §10.3 uses it by name without stating it. It is stated here, in the three forms the corpus distinguishes, and the migration adopts the first two and refuses the third.

D.2 The three forms.

form statement status
(A1) the split $4=1+3$ that the fourth colour needs is the carrier's split $\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus B_V$ adopted
(A2) the lepton-number direction is the unit line, $B-L\propto \tfrac13P_V-P_t$, normalised by $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$ adopted
(A3) the carrier is the algebra itself, so colour acts on $\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus B_V$ and commutes with the Lorentz action refused, and excluded in D.5

What the algebra supplies: the line, its complement, the traceless diagonal and the quotient. What it does not supply: that these are the colour and the lepton number. The rule that follows is one sentence long and it is the integrity of everything written under the declaration. Derived is allowed for $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ as the group of the carrier and for the split; it is forbidden for the identification of that split with the quark–lepton quartet. That is why the model is declared and not derived, and why §9.3 records that the confirmation does not reach the group.

D.3 Where the line comes from, and why no other.

The line is canonical and every other line is not. $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$ preserves $e_t$ because $e_t$ is the identity, and inside $B_V$ it acts as $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, so it moves every line of $B_V$ and fixes $\mathbb{C}e_t$. A colour group needs a direction to be the stabiliser of, and the algebra supplies exactly one. $B_V$ has no canonical axis: the three axes are one orbit (D.6).

The alternatives are available to a group theorist and none is canonical.

split canonical why it fails
$2+2$ no needs a complex structure on the carrier that the algebra does not fix
$1+1+2$ no needs two marked lines, and $B_V$ has none
$4$ unbroken yes no stabiliser, hence no colour at all

Uniqueness is not truth. It says that if a $1+3$ split of this carrier is used, there is only one candidate; D.8 is where truth is tested.

D.4 What the line gives, in the algebra's own objects.

With $P_t$ the projector onto the line and $P_V=\mathrm I-P_t$,

$$ D=\mathrm I-4P_t=P_V-3P_t,\qquad B-L=\tfrac13D=\tfrac13\mathrm I-\tfrac43P_t , $$

with eigenvalues $(-1,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ on $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$, $\operatorname{tr}D=0$, $\operatorname{tr}D^{2}=12$ and $\operatorname{tr}(B-L)^{2}=\tfrac43$. Both entries of $D$ are explained at once: the three spatial directions get $+1$, the temporal one gets $-3$.

So $B-L$ is $-1$ on the unit line and $+\tfrac13$ on each colour line. The line carries lepton number and no baryon number; the three colour lines carry baryon number alone. The lepton sits on the unit line, and that is the sentence "the lepton is the fourth colour" written in the algebra's own objects. It replaces the bare $\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1)$ of §5.3, which states the same eigenvalues in the order $(x,y,z,t)$ and hides where they come from.

$B-L$ is blind to chirality, and the blindness is what makes the fourth colour possible. $P_t$ is a projector of the algebra and not of the spin, so $B-L$ assigns the same value to a field and to its chiral partner. A charge that changed with chirality would separate $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ from $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ and could not be the fourth component of one quartet; it would be an extra $\mathrm{U}(1)$ acting on one chirality only, which is exactly what the left–right model has and what the fourth colour replaces. The corpus's chiral wall is not crossed here and is not meant to be: the wall says the frame's own derivations do not produce the spinor doublet, and this charge never claims to.

The quantisation follows from the count of dimensions:

$$ \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=\underbrace{-1}_{\text{one lepton}}+\underbrace{3\cdot\tfrac13}_{\text{three quarks}}=0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 3q+\ell=0 . $$

The counts are $\dim\mathbb{C}e_t=1$ and $\dim B_V=3$, and the eigenvalues are $-1$ and $+\tfrac13$. Charge quantisation is the statement that the carrier is one line plus a three-space, and it is not an extra assumption. The sign is fixed by the hypercharge formula and by the observed charges, not by taste: with the opposite sign the electron comes out neutral and the neutrino charged. What is a convention is the simultaneous reversal of every charge, $Q\to-Q$, and that reversal leaves the trace untouched. The quantisation is not a convention: $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$ holds either way and says $3q+\ell=0$.

Hypercharge then costs nothing to assemble: $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, no abelian factor put in by hand.

D.5 The price: (A3) is excluded, and it is not a difficulty.

The commutant of the Lorentz action inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, computed:

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)\cap\bigl\{X:\ X L_{\vec e_a}=L_{\vec e_a}X,\ a=x,y,z\bigr\} =\operatorname{span}\{R_{\vec e_x},R_{\vec e_y},R_{\vec e_z}\}\cong\mathfrak{su}(2), \qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}=3 . $$

The computation has an exact answer and it is worth stating in full. The commutant of $L(\mathbb{B})$ in $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ is $R(\mathbb{B})$, of real dimension eight and complex dimension four, spanned by $R_{e_t},R_{\vec e_x},R_{\vec e_y},R_{\vec e_z}$. Of these four, $R_{e_t}=\mathrm I$ is Hermitian with trace four, so it fails both conditions of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$; the three $R_{\vec e_k}$ are anti-Hermitian and of zero array trace. Hence exactly three directions survive, and the ceiling is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The other five real dimensions of the commutant are $iR_{e_t}$ and the four $iR_{\vec e_k}$, all Hermitian or with trace, and none of them is in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$.

So a colour $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ commuting with the Lorentz action does not fit on one carrier. The repair is the one §7.4 records as a misreading to avoid: colour acts on a formal copy of the quartet, coupled by a tensor product, and the copy inherits the split without inheriting the Lorentz action. The declaration survives in the form (A1)–(A2) and dies in the form (A3).

Three objections have been called "the colour wall", and they are three statements that a larger group treats differently. Distinguishing them is worth a table, because conflating them is the most likely way to overclaim what the fourth colour achieves.

wall statement kind does $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ move it?
the dimension wall $\dim\mathfrak{su}(3)=8>\dim\mathfrak{u}(2)=4$, the largest compact subalgebra of $\mathbb{B}$ counting yes, and only this one
the carrier wall the algebra acts on a four-dimensional complex space; colour needs a three-space that is not one of its invariant subspaces structural no
the chiral wall the algebra's product is two-sided; a chiral gauge theory needs a one-sided action representation-theoretic no

Only the first is about numbers. $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is fifteen-dimensional and has an eight-dimensional subalgebra, so the counting objection disappears the moment the group is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. The other two do not move, and the carrier one is the theorem above: it is computed against the algebra's action, not against the size of the group.

The test the three walls answer is worth naming, because the corpus has used it without stating it. The implicit criterion has been that a gauge group must sit inside the algebra, so that its generators are elements of $\mathbb{B}$. Run honestly, that criterion rejects every factor the corpus uses except the central $\mathrm{U}(1)$: $L_{\vec e_a}$ and $R_{\vec e_a}$ are endomorphisms and not elements, so even the two-sided action fails it. The usable criterion is the one the corpus already has, a Conway grading: a generator is an element of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, a sum of Conway words $L_{e_{\imath}}R_{e_{\jmath}}$, and legitimacy is its length $\ell=\operatorname{rank}c\le4$. Under that grading the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s are length one, $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is length two and $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is at most three. The larger group is admitted by the criterion the corpus already applies, and it is the criterion, not the group, that retires the old test.

The ceiling falls rather than rises, and the reason is a trace. The older statement was that the largest compact subalgebra of $\mathbb{B}$ is $\mathfrak{u}(2)$, of dimension four. The largest subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ commuting with the algebra's action is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, of dimension three. The missing direction is the central phase $iL_{e_t}$: it commutes with the whole left action because $e_t$ is central, and the trace of its array does not vanish, $\operatorname{tr}(iL_{e_t})=4i$. So the phase is in $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ and not in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, and four against three is the difference between a subalgebra of $\mathbb{B}$ and a commutant inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. A ceiling of the first kind could be broken by a fifteen-dimensional group; a ceiling of the second kind cannot be broken by any enlargement, because it is computed against the algebra's action.

Colour is not that commutant, and the check is one line. Every colour generator kills the canonical line, $Xe_t=0$ for $X\in\mathfrak{su}(3)$, while $R_b(e_t)=b$, so $R_b$ preserves the line only when $b=0$. Hence

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)\cap R(\mathbb{B})=0 . $$

So two obstacles are not one. The group-theoretic obstruction is gone: colour exists inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and its octet is the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ block of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ rather than an octet outside the frame's group. The module-theoretic obstruction stands and is located exactly: no operator that commutes with the algebra's multiplication is colour, so colour never multiplies.

What would breach the carrier wall, stated so that it is a live problem rather than a prohibition. A larger carrier, since a commuting $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ fits on a space of dimension at least six, and a larger carrier means a different algebra. A one-sided chiral module of dimension at least three, which would drop the chiral wall and part of the carrier wall together. Colour as an inner symmetry of $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, which meets $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ in $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ and has no room, since $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. And a Hopf fibration: $\mathrm{SU}(4)/\mathrm{SU}(3)\cong S^{7}$ and $\mathrm{SU}(4)/S(\mathrm{U}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1))\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}$ are the corpus's own quotients, and they say the colour group is a sphere bundle over $\mathbb{CP}^{3}$. Whether that fibration can be written as an algebraic operation is a better question than whether the algebra can host eight generators.

D.6 The axis objection, answered.

The objection. A generator such as $\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac23,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$ singles out one direction, and the corpus has no axis-free basis, so a structure that prefers an axis is suspect.

The axes are one orbit. The unit quaternion $Q=\tfrac12(e_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+\vec e_z)$ has $N(Q)=\tfrac14(1+1+1+1)=1$ and $Q^{3}=-\mathrm I$, so the inner automorphism it induces has order three: conjugation by $Q$ cycles the axes while fixing the unit.

$$ Q\vec e_xQ^{-1}=\vec e_y,\qquad Q\vec e_yQ^{-1}=\vec e_z,\qquad Q\vec e_zQ^{-1}=\vec e_x,\qquad Qe_tQ^{-1}=e_t . $$

A Cartan written as $\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac23,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$ is therefore a choice on which the Weyl group $S_3$ acts, and the triple

$$ h_a=\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(L_{\vec e_b}R_{\vec e_b}-L_{\vec e_c}R_{\vec e_c}\bigr),\qquad a,b,c\ \text{cyclic},\qquad h_x+h_y+h_z=0, $$

names all three at once. The first display is a presentational choice, the second is structural, and the algebra permits the second.

What is preferred is not an axis but the line. $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is fixed by every automorphism, so it is the only line that can be a singlet, and the carrier splits canonically as $\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus B_V$, which is the $\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3$ of the fourth colour.

Inside colour the split is $3+5$:

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p},\qquad \dim\mathfrak{k}=3,\quad\dim\mathfrak{p}=5,\qquad \mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})=\operatorname{span}\{L_{\vec e_k}-R_{\vec e_k}\}. $$

The three are the rigid rotations, the algebra's own automorphism algebra, and the five are traceless symmetric tensors on the three-plane, quadrupoles and not rotations, and they are the part of colour that needs an axis to be written. The pair is a symmetric one:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(3)\big/\mathrm{SO}(3)\ \text{is a compact symmetric space of dimension }5 , $$

so the canonical part sits in the symmetric pair and the non-canonical part is the tangent directions of the symmetric space; that is the group-theoretic form of "three of eight are the algebra's own".

The corpus states the rule that settles the matter, and it is worth quoting because it is the sharpest discriminator in the comparison: any statement about this $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ must be made either in the split $1+3$ or in $L,R$ with no axis named. The fourth colour states its colour in the split $1+3$. The left–right model separates the $3$ from the $1$ and then treats the $3$ alone, which is the one option the rule excludes. So the objection, correctly stated, is not against colour but against a theory that leaves the chain of D.7 at its first step. Colour sits inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is the carrier's group, and the split that produces colour is the split at the canonical line. The problematic object is colour divorced from the line it came from.

Inside the fifteen this is one number. $\mathfrak{u}(3)\cap\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is nine-dimensional, colour's eight plus the $B-L$ direction, so the fourth colour keeps nine of the fifteen generators in one simple factor and the left–right model keeps eight and one in two.

D.7 The operator that makes the axis harmless.

Because the unit multiplies trivially on both sides, $L_{e_t}=R_{e_t}=\mathrm{id}$, and

$$ L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}=L_{\vec e_x}-R_{\vec e_x}=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}=2\bigl(J_x-J'_x\bigr), $$

with $J_k=\tfrac12L_{\vec e_k}$ and $J'_k=\tfrac12R_{\vec e_k}$. The factor two is the pair's relative generator being spin one, and it is why $[\vec e_x,\vec e_y]=2\vec e_z$ while $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$.

On the four basis elements, from the multiplication table: $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(e_t)=0$, $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(\vec e_x)=0$, $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(\vec e_y)=2\vec e_z$, $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(\vec e_z)=-2\vec e_y$.

Three readings, all already in the corpus. It is the inner derivation, hence one of the three generators of $\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}/\mathbb{C}e_t$. It is the derivative at $t=0$ of the two-sided rotor $L_{e^{t\vec e_x}}R_{e^{-t\vec e_x}}$, so the finite object is a reflected rotation and this is its first-order term. It is the relative spin-one generator of the pair.

Exponentiating gives a one-parameter group of unital automorphisms,

$$ \exp\bigl(\theta\,(J_x-J'_x)\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}\Bigr), $$

so the finite object is a rotation of the pair and the operator is its first-order term.

Four properties, each a statement about the frame. It fixes the unit, so its flow is a symmetry of the multiplication table and not a movement of the unit line: at $\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ it sends $e_t\mapsto e_t$, $\vec e_x\mapsto\vec e_x$, $\vec e_y\mapsto\vec e_z$, $\vec e_z\mapsto-\vec e_y$, and at $\theta=\pi$ it is the half-turn on the orthogonal plane. On the vector part it is twice the cross product with $\vec e_x$, which is the same factor two as in the commutator. It is odd under the side swap, since the plain-bilinear adjoint exchanges the sides and $\bigl(\mathrm{ad}_{\vec Q}\bigr)^{\approx}=-\mathrm{ad}_{\vec Q}$, so every derivation is skew for that form. It is the compact half: the three real $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_k}$ span $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, the three $\mathrm{ad}_{i\vec e_k}$ are the boosts, and the six are $\mathfrak{so}(3,1)$. And it is exactly the $3$ inside $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and not the five.

So the operator is frame-free in its content and frame-dependent only in its label, and the chain unit line $\to$ automorphisms $\to$ derivations $\to$ $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})\subset\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is what makes the corpus's colour the rigid part of colour.

D.8 What the declaration does not give, and how it dies.

Six things do not follow, and each is a limit rather than a defect.

  • Colour inside the biquaternion algebra. It is not there, and $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is not $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B})$ either; the notes on the ceiling and on the trace stand unchanged.
  • The chiral carrier. The tensor product supplies a copy of the quartet and not a two-sided action on one carrier. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ does not make a one-sided action two-sided, and the wall of §7.4 is untouched by the declaration.
  • The scalar carrier. A spin-zero field is still a line, and a bidoublet still needs two indices. The declaration adds one multiplet to the debt and settles nothing about it, which is why §2.3 leaves the list open.
  • The breaking scale. The declaration says nothing about $M_C$; the scale is fixed by experiment or not at all.
  • The number of generations. Three generations are three copies of the quartet, and the algebra does not count them. The declaration is silent on the count in the same way it is silent on the scale.
  • The rank. The frame's own compact group has rank two against this model's five, so the fourth colour cannot be reached by enlarging the gauge construction inside $\mathbb{B}$. The obstruction is a count, not a shortage of effort.

The first two of those are the residue of the wall, and they are worth a paragraph because a critic will quote them and the quote will be fair. A structure that transfers — one canonical line, one axis-free three-space, a traceless diagonal — can be found between many pairs of objects, so a copy is cheap and a reader may ask for more than three transferred properties. The frame cannot supply more, and the residue is not small: colour is a symmetry of a vector space that the algebra happens to be isomorphic to, and the isomorphism uses no product. Colour acts on the space and not on the algebra, so an address is not a containment. The reply to "you have renamed the obstruction instead of removing it" is not "no" but "one of three is gone, and the other two are named". That sentence is the honest form of the whole claim, and it is why the three-wall table is in this note rather than in a companion.

The declaration dies in three ways, and the emptiest one is the most likely.

  • By emptiness. If the leptoquark and proton bounds push $M_C$ beyond any scale the frame can reach, the fourth colour is either not gauged or not a colour, and the declaration becomes a relabelling with no consequence. The proton bound does this on its own: $\tau(p\to e^{+}\pi^{0})>2.4\times10^{34}$ yr pushes $M_C$ above $10^{15}$ GeV for the simplest scalar-mediated diagrams, and a scale that high is already outside anything the frame can reach.
  • By contradiction. A leptoquark with the wrong quantum numbers, a singlet coupled to $q\bar q$ rather than to $q\ell$, or a fourth-colour-like resonance whose $B-L$ is not this $D$.
  • By structural refutation, which is the one the corpus can deliver: a proof that some other split of $\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus B_V$ is canonical, that the algebra fixes a second line or a complex structure on $B_V$. D.3 is the load-bearing claim, and it is the claim to attack.

1. The Definition

1.1 The group and the content. The gauge group is

$$ G=\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R , \qquad \dim G=15+3+3=21 , $$

with no further factor and no simple group above it. One generation is one sixteen, in the two-piece form the algebra gives:

$$ (\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)\oplus(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2) =(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{\tfrac16}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-\tfrac12}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-\tfrac23}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{\tfrac13}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{1}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{0} $$

under the Standard Model subgroup, with $6+2+3+3+1+1=16$ components. The four entries on the second line are the conjugates of the right-handed fields, which is why their hypercharges read with the sign of $Y^{c}=-Y$. The last entry is $\nu_{\mathrm{neu},R}$. The multiplets are fixed, and they are the frame's.

The pairing, which is where the name comes from. Read down the $T_{3R}$ column of the sixteen: the doublet with $T_{3R}=+\tfrac12$ is $\{u_R,\nu_{\mathrm{neu},R}\}$ and the one with $T_{3R}=-\tfrac12$ is $\{d_R,e_R\}$. So the right-handed neutrino is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of the right-handed up quark and not an added singlet, and read across at fixed $T_{3R}=+\tfrac12$ the quartet is $\{u^r,u^g,u^b,\nu\}$. The fourth colour is one irreducible object and not an assembly, and lepton number is its fourth entry. The same reading checks the hypercharge construction: $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ gives $Y=0$ for $\nu_{\mathrm{neu},R}$ with no adjustment, because $+\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac12$ cancel exactly.

1.2 The chain and the two scales. The breaking runs in two steps, and the steps are independent:

$$ G\ \xrightarrow{\ M_C\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{\ M_R\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y . $$

The chain names three groups and two scales, and it does not name the multiplets that do the breaking. The spin-0 sector exists, because the symmetry is broken and something breaks it, but which multiplet performs each step is not fixed (§1.5). The frame puts both steps at one scale; this model allows them to differ, and the difference is bounded from above (§3.3).

1.3 The couplings, counted exactly. There are three gauge couplings, and one of them is not free:

coupling what fixes it
$g_4$ equal to $g_3(M_C)$, because $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ sits inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$; measured once $M_C$ is chosen
$g_{B-L}$ not independent: $g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38 g_4^2$ at $M_C$, from the generator normalisation
$g_L$ free
$g_R$ free, and determined in practice by the matching at $M_R$ (§3.1)

The strip removes exactly two relations, $g_L=g_4$ and $g_R=g_4$, and leaves one, $g_4=g_3(M_C)$. That single relation is what makes the model a statement about nature rather than a relabelling of the Standard Model, and it is also why the leptoquark floor does not weaken (§3.7).

1.4 The parameter count. The gauge sector has three quantities that can be chosen, $M_C$, $M_R$ and $g_R$, against three measured numbers. The count says nothing by itself; §3 shows why the system is consistent. The statement the model makes about the couplings is this: it reproduces the three measured values for any $(M_C,M_R)$ in a stated region, by adjusting $g_R$, and it predicts no value for them.

The spin-0 sector is not removed, and the sentence "the scalar sector is outside the model" is false. A spin-zero field takes its value in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module and not an element of the vector part, with the Klein–Gordon operator central. That sector is the frame's, and the model inherits it unchanged.

What the model does not fix is the index structure on top of it. A Pati–Salam theory places scalars in named multiplets, $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ on the carrier's four dimensions with its vacuum along the canonical line, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, and those assignments are declarations, not consequences of the fourth colour. The frame supplies the room, the centre, the carrier and the split $\mathbf4=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3$. It does not supply the guest list.

The distinction, in one sentence each.

  • The sector: spin zero, valued in $\mathbb B_C$, central, decoupled in the Klein–Gordon equation. Frame. Kept.
  • The multiplets: which of $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ are present, and how many. Declaration. Not fixed here.
  • The mass relations: $m_d=m_e$ and the rest. A consequence of the second line, and therefore also not fixed, not because the sector is gone but because the guest list is short.

Why the guest list cannot be derived here. The fermion bilinear is not a gauge singlet,

$$ (\bar{\mathbf4},\bar{\mathbf2},\mathbf1)\otimes(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2) $$

contains no $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$, so a scalar must be inserted with the right indices to write a mass term at all. 2. The Strip: The Coupling Equality Removed, the Spin-0 Sector Kept

2.1 What is removed, and it is a single thing. The unified version assumes $g_3=g_L=g_R=g_4$ at one scale. That assumption alone creates the failure: one boundary value, one scale, three measured couplings, over-determined by one, and no scale fits it. Removing it removes the failure entirely, because a relation that is not asserted cannot be violated. That is the whole of the strip.

2.2 What is kept: the spin-0 sector, whole. The strip touches the couplings and nothing else. Spin-zero fields remain what the frame says they are, valued in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module, with a central Klein–Gordon operator, and every statement the scalar sector makes about that structure stands. The sector is a structural result and is not under review.

2.3 What is left open: the multiplet list. The Pati–Salam scalar sector names three multiplets, $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, and computes their components, their eaten count and their surviving spectrum. That list is a declaration on top of the sector, and this model does not fix it.

The sector is not empty, and its size can be counted. For the minimal content there are twenty-four real components, twelve of them eaten, and no doublet among the survivors: the survivors are four singlets. That count is the right answer when a reader asks whether "the multiplet list is open" is a polite name for "there are no scalars". There are scalars, and there are twelve fewer of them than the naive count. The sector is in; the guest list is open.

The list is open in the generous direction, not the stingy one, and the size of the menu is worth recording. The carrier admits $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$, branching as $(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{-\frac13}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{\frac13}$; it admits $(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\mathbf1)$ and $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$, the second branching to $(1,1)_{1}\oplus(1,1)_{0}\oplus(1,1)_{-1}$; and it admits $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ or $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$, the two scalars that can give the right-handed neutrino its mass. That mass is tied to $M_R$, and the type-I seesaw follows with no field beyond the sixteen. "Open" therefore names a menu of a dozen multiplets, and the model's silence is silence about the menu, not a shortage of ingredients.

2.4 The consequence for the mass relations, said precisely, because this is where a reader expects a dodge. The model does not predict $m_d=m_e$, and it predicts nothing else about fermion masses. Both statements are true at once.

  • Why no prediction is made. A mass relation is a statement about which multiplet sits in the Yukawa coupling, and the multiplet list is not fixed (§1.5). It is not that scalars are absent; it is that their index structure is not specified.
  • Why that is not an evasion. The model's claims are checkable and are checked: the group, the content, the charges, the anomalies, the couplings (§3). A statement about a gauge group does not by itself fix Yukawa indices, and no theory of this kind does.
  • What it costs. A theory that predicts no mass ratio cannot be confirmed by one, and cannot be refuted by one either. The cost is on the credit side, which §6.2 records.

2.5 Why the equality is the thing that fails and the angle is not. This is the sharpest statement of what the strip repairs.

Run the Standard Model's one-loop slopes to the scale where $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ exactly, about $10^{13}$ GeV. The two electroweak inverse couplings have met there: in $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ normalisation $\alpha_1^{-1}=\alpha_2^{-1}=42.4$, and the angle is where Pati–Salam needs it. What misses is colour: $\alpha_3^{-1}=36.8$ at that scale, about $13\%$ low, which is $\alpha_3$ about $15\%$ high.

So the boundary condition is reachable and the unification is not. The usual summary, that $\tfrac38$ is $38\%$ high at $250$ TeV, is true and is a statement about a chosen scale. The sharper statement is that at the scale the angle actually reaches $\tfrac38$, the strong coupling has not arrived. The missing ingredient is matter that changes $b_3$, and that is a statement about content, not about the angle.

If the failure were the angle, then removing the angle's prediction would be removing the failure, and a reader would be right to call that a dodge. The failure is colour arriving late, so removing $g_R=g_L$ does not remove it; it removes the requirement that all four couplings meet at one scale. That is the narrower repair, and §10.2 states what it still costs.

The Witten anomaly is the one usually forgotten, and here it is a parity count. An $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ with an odd number of left-handed doublets is inconsistent, because $\pi_4(\mathrm{SU}(2))=\mathbb{Z}_2$. Per family the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ doublets are the three quark colours plus the lepton, four, and the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ doublets are the same four from the $\bar{\mathbf4}$:

$$ n_L=4\equiv0\pmod 2,\qquad n_R=4\equiv0\pmod 2 . $$

The count is four because the quartet has four components, so it cannot fail for any content built from whole quartets. A frame with three colours would have had three doublets and failed. The fourth colour is what makes the parity even, which is a small structural gain but a genuine one.

The count of parameters is the point of the ledger. The Standard Model has three constraints and one free normalisation, and the free normalisation is why it cannot say why the proton charge is minus the electron charge. Here there are zero constraints and no free normalisation: $Y$ is inherited from the embedding, the ratios follow, and the overall scale is fixed by $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$.

What the ledger does not buy, stated with it so that it is not overclaimed: it does not buy the representations, since the sixteen is declared and a vector-like theory cancels for free; it does not buy chirality; and it does not buy the frame. Anomaly cancellation is necessary and cheap, and any consistent theory passes this ledger. Four zeros presented as evidence for the group would be mistaking a hygiene requirement for a prediction. The precise claim is that the hypercharge assignment is not chosen but inherited, and that the conditions then hold automatically, which is a check on the inheritance and not a proof that the inheritance is physically realised.

It is still the strongest technical claim in the note, and the reason is the count rather than the zeros. A theory that satisfies four conditions by choosing charges well has four fewer free parameters than one that does not; a theory that satisfies them without choosing charges at all has no charges to choose, and that is a different kind of statement. It is also the claim that survives every other criticism here: the couplings can be stripped, the scalars left open, the scale pushed out of reach, and the ledger is untouched, because nothing in it uses a coupling or a scale.

4. Differences with Pati–Salam

4.1 Same group, one claim fewer. The group, the sixteen, the chain, the boson count, the leptoquark quantum numbers and the charge-quantisation argument are identical. What differs is the boundary condition on the couplings, and the absence of any scalar claim, in either the unified version's strong form or a repaired one.

The group is semisimple and not simple, and that is a permanent feature rather than a stage of ignorance. Three factors mean three independent couplings before any symmetry is imposed and two after $g_L=g_R$ is. A single coupling needs $\mathrm{SO}(10)$, in which the sixteen is exactly $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)\oplus(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, so the fourth colour is the intermediate step and not the destination. The corpus's own argument is that a larger simple group does not fit on the carrier, so this destination is out of reach here and the semisimple group is the end of the road rather than a rung.

4.4 The global form, and what "if parity is imposed" means.

The group is written either as the product or as the quotient by a $\mathbb{Z}_2$, and the quotient is the correct one for the sixteen-field content. The centre is $\mathbb{Z}_4\times\mathbb{Z}_{2L}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2R}$, since the centre of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is $\mathbb{Z}_n$, and the quotient is by the diagonal $\mathbb{Z}_2$ whose non-trivial element pairs the order-two element of $\mathbb{Z}_4$ with the non-trivial elements of both $\mathbb{Z}_2$'s. The product acts on the sixteen with a two-element kernel, so the physical group is the quotient.

The quotient has a consequence the corpus already knows at its own electroweak pair: the abelian charge of a multiplet is locked to its spin parity. That is the same lock the corpus derives for $\mathrm{U}(2)$, met at a larger group, and it is why the sixteen and not fifteen fields form a representation.

The extension. The group can be enlarged to the semidirect product

$$ \Bigl(\bigl[\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\bigr]/\mathbb{Z}_2\Bigr)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2 , $$

by an automorphism combining an outer automorphism of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ with the interchange of the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s. Under it $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)\oplus(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ becomes a single irreducible, which is the algebraic statement that left and right are one object. This is the model's original motivation: parity a symmetry of the theory and a property of the vacuum, not a postulate of the Lagrangian.

So the parenthesis in §10.1, "if parity is imposed", has a precise meaning: it is the statement that this $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is gauged. The strip is the refusal to gauge it, and it is therefore a statement about the global form and not about the algebra.

4.5 The six directions, and the one tensor product.

The group-theoretic difference between the fourth colour and the left–right model is one coset, and the coset is the space of lines in the carrier:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\big/\big(\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\big)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}=15-8-1=6 . $$

The six extra gauge bosons are the coordinates of a point of $\mathbb{CP}^3$, and by D.3 the point is canonical: $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is fixed by every automorphism, so neither keeping the six directions nor dropping them costs fidelity. The left–right model is what remains when the point is fixed and the six coordinates are dropped.

The same statement in the matter sector. One generation here is one tensor product,

$$ \mathbf{16}=\mathbf{4}\otimes\mathbf{2}\otimes\mathbf{2}=\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{L}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{R}, $$

one term, where the left–right model writes the same sixteen as four terms after cutting the $\mathbf{4}$ into $\mathbf{3}\oplus\mathbf{1}$. What changes is not the content but the number of terms. A single tensor product is the fingerprint of the shape the corpus calls coefficient rank one; a sum of four is not.

4.6 Monopoles, which is §4.4's point at a second level.

The second homotopy group of the coset is non-trivial,

$$ \pi_2\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)}{[\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)]/\mathbb{Z}_3}\right)=\mathbb{Z}, $$

so the model contains 't Hooft–Polyakov monopoles: a non-trivial $\pi_2$ of the vacuum manifold forces a topological defect, and no Feynman diagram sees one. That is the same kind of object that separates the product group from the quotient group in §4.4, invisible to perturbation theory and visible to a topological probe.

The multiplicity is not a property of the group alone. The quoted group is the one of the coset as written, with the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ quotient of $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ by the centre; the full chain to the Standard Model group carries a different quotient, and the monopole spectrum differs between the two writings. What is robust is that they are predicted. What is not robust is how many. The non-observation of a flux is a bound on the model's cosmological history and not on the group.

9.1 Does the model contain the Standard Model? Yes.

  • The anomalies cancel. Four rows and the Witten condition, on content that is unchanged.

  • The multiplet content of the spin-0 sector is not fixed, so no mass relation is asserted and none can be violated (§1.5, §2.4). The spin-0 sector itself is the frame's, valued in $\mathbb B_C$, and is kept.

The confirmations are of containment and of silence, and both are stated as what they are.

  • It does not confirm the group. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is declared; the algebra supports the module, not the identification with the quark–lepton quartet.

10. Is the Strip Legitimate?

10.1 The answer, and the reasons. The question is whether it is legitimate to drop an assumption because the data exclude it. It is, and the reasons are checkable rather than rhetorical.

The strongest reason is in the frame's own words. It writes the boundary condition as

$$ g_3(M_{PS})=g_4(M_{PS}),\qquad g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})\equiv g_4(M_{PS})\quad\text{(if parity is imposed)}, $$

and its own summary of the collapse is "two, or to one", in that order, with the parity equality marked as conditional. So the equality of $g_L$ and $g_R$ was not asserted as a result; it was asserted conditionally on imposing parity. The strip is therefore the refusal to impose parity, a choice the frame had already marked as a choice, in a parenthesis and in a table row. A conditional that is not imposed leaves no unmet prediction behind, which is why the strip removes a failure rather than an embarrassment.

11. The Synchronised Variant: Adding the Equality Back at a Higher Scale

The proposal, stated fairly, because it deserves that. Keep everything in §1 and add a third scale: three scales $M_{PS}>M_C>M_R$, with $g_4=g_L=g_R$ imposed at $M_{PS}$ and nothing changed below. The unified Pati–Salam model is then the special case $M_{PS}=M_C=M_R$, and the strip is the case where the condition is dropped entirely. Between the two there is this third thing, and the argument for it is real: the extra segment changes the running, so the couplings that failed to meet in one step might meet in three.

The variant is not empty, and it is not a repair either. The reason is structural, not numerical.

11.1 The count, before any running. Two conditions are imposed, $g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})$ and $g_4(M_{PS})=g_L(M_{PS})$, on three scales. Two equations with three unknowns is a curve, not a point. So "is it empty?" is the wrong first question; generically it is not. The right question is what a curve is worth: a curve means a free parameter, and a free parameter means nothing is predicted. The computation below decides whether the curve is real and where it lives.

11.2 The obstruction, and it can be seen before any number is run. Above $M_R$ the field content is parity-symmetric: $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, and a bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$. Each $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ multiplet has an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of the same dimension, so the two beta coefficients are equal as a matter of symmetry, not of choice:

$$ c_{2L}=c_{2R}. $$

Equal coefficients mean the difference of the inverse couplings is frozen, because running both from $M_R$ to $M_{PS}$ adds the same amount to each:

$$ \frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_{PS})}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_{PS})}=\frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_R)}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_R)}. $$

So the second condition is not a condition at $M_{PS}$ at all. It is a condition at $M_R$. The three-scale construction collapses to one equation, fixed at the lower end, and the third scale buys nothing for it:

$$ g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})\iff g_L(M_R)=g_R(M_R). $$

This is what the argument that extra running gives colour more room misses. The extra running does give colour more room, and §11.5 shows exactly how much, but it does nothing whatever to $g_R$ relative to $g_L$, because parity makes their running identical. The obstruction is not colour.

The diagnosis that colour was late is therefore correct about the one-scale model and irrelevant to the three-scale one: with a third scale, colour is never late, and nothing is ever early either.

11.6 What would rescue it, and the price, in numbers. The freeze of §11.2 is what blocks it, and the freeze is broken only by breaking parity above $M_R$. Then the gap can run, but it has to run a long way. At $M_C=250$ TeV and $M_R=25$ TeV the gap is $49.6$ in $1/\alpha$ and there are $15.9$ e-folds to the colour meeting, so the required asymmetry is

$$ \Delta c_2\simeq-2\pi\frac{49.6}{15.9}\simeq-20 . $$

A single Weyl doublet moves $c_2$ by $1/3$, so this asks for about sixty more $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ doublets than $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ doublets above $M_R$. That is not a threshold-correction-sized asymmetry; it is a different theory, and one with no reason behind the number sixty. Parity restoration is supposed to be the point of a left–right model, and this variant can only work by giving it up above $M_R$, which is where it would be most natural.

11.7 The verdict.

  • Not empty. A solution exists, at $M_C\simeq M_R\simeq2.5\times10^{13}$ TeV and $M_{PS}\simeq5\times10^{13}$ TeV.
  • Not a repair. At that solution $g_R$ is fitted, not predicted, so the agreement at $M_Z$ is the input coming back; and $\tfrac38$ holds at $10^{16}$ GeV, eleven orders of magnitude above the leptoquark floor and unobservable in every way §3.7 counts.
  • Obstructed by the wrong coupling. The hope was that extra running fixes colour. It does, since $g_4$ meets $g_L$ at every $M_C$, but the meeting that fails is $g_R=g_L$, and parity freezes it above $M_R$.
  • One free parameter, zero predictions. The solution is a curve indexed by $M_C\ge2.5\times10^{13}$ TeV. A curve is freedom, and freedom spent on a scale is freedom not spent on a number.

So the short statement is that adding a third scale does not restore a prediction, because the third scale is what absorbs the mismatch. Measured at $M_Z$, the variant reproduces the data because $g_R$ is solved from them. The strip is not a stepping stone to this variant; it is the variant with the free parameter left visible.

11.8 The caveat that applies to every number in this section. These are one-loop estimates with the coefficients stated in §11.3, on minimal content with $b_{B-L}=12$. Two of them move under a different content: the crossing scale of §11.4 and the meeting scales of §11.5. The conclusion does not move, because it rests on the freeze of §11.2, an equality of beta coefficients forced by parity rather than a numerical coincidence, and on the sign of the gap, which is $-27\%$ to $-38\%$ and would have to reverse. What a two-loop or threshold-corrected computation could change is where the curve lives, not whether it is a curve.

Summary

The model is $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, with three independent couplings, two scales, and a spin-0 sector kept whole whose multiplet list is left open. The group, the sixteen, charge quantisation, the four anomaly cancellations, the boson count, the leptoquarks and the $K_S$ rule are unchanged.

The declaration, which is stated before §1 and not assumed. The fourth colour is the unit line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ and the three colours are its complement $B_V$, so $N_c=4-1=3$, and $B-L=\tfrac13(\mathrm I-4P_t)$ is $-1$ on that line and $+\tfrac13$ off it, so its trace vanishes. The algebra supplies the line, the split and the trace; it does not supply the identification of the split with the quark–lepton quartet, and it cannot supply a colour $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ commuting with the Lorentz action, whose commutant inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is a three-dimensional $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. Every structural claim here rests on the declaration, and the declaration is a claim and not a theorem.

One strip, not two. Dropping $g_4=g_L=g_R$ removes the coupling failure, and that is the entirety of what is removed. The spin-0 sector is not touched: it is the frame's, valued in the centre $\mathbb B_C$ and a line in the state module, and it stays as the frame states it. What is left open is the multiplet list, which is a declaration on top of the sector and not a consequence of the fourth colour. Deleting the scalar sector destroys a frame result; adding a multiplet list adds a claim the model does not make.

It contains the Standard Model, and that is proved rather than asserted. The chain to $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ is exact, the sixteen reduces to the fifteen plus $\nu_{\mathrm{neu},R}$, and below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model. Every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, and the $M_R\to\infty$ limit returns the Standard Model.

It cannot contradict experiment, and the reason is structural. The three measured couplings are reproduced by construction, with $g_R$ absorbing the difference; every other number is a bound on a free parameter or a structural fact already holding. A solution exists for every $M_R

What it costs. No prediction of $\sin^2\theta_W$, no mass relation, no unification, and therefore nothing that could confirm it.

The number that looks like a prediction is not one. $g_R/g_L$ comes out between $0.61$ and $0.71$, and that is an output, not a test. It is fixed once $(M_C,M_R)$ are chosen, which is the opposite of a prediction. A prediction would be a number fixed without choosing anything, and the model has none. The count of testable consequences is one, and it is out of reach: gauge leptoquarks with fixed quantum numbers, above a few hundred TeV. The count of structural facts the note can point to is four: charge quantisation, the content, the anomalies and the boson count.

The judgement in one line: the Four Colours Left–Right Model keeps everything structural about Pati–Salam and drops only the coupling equality that the data exclude. It contains the Standard Model, cannot contradict experiment, and cannot be confirmed either. That is a better position than the unified version had, and a worse one than the unified version claimed. §10 argues why taking it is legitimate rather than convenient.

Internal Note: The Fourth-Colour Left–Right Model

The Answer

The model is $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, with three couplings that are not required to be equal and a spin-0 sector whose multiplet content it does not fix. It keeps the group, the sixteen, charge quantisation, the four anomaly cancellations, the twenty-one bosons, the six leptoquarks and the selection rule for $K_S\to\mu e$. It drops one thing, the equality $g_4=g_L=g_R$, and claims nothing in its place.

The spin-0 sector is kept whole, and only its multiplet list is left open. A spin-zero field takes its value in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module, with a central Klein–Gordon operator, and the model inherits that structure unchanged. What it does not fix is which gauge multiplets those scalars occupy: $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ are declarations on top of the sector, not consequences of the fourth colour. Because the multiplet list is open, no fermion mass relation is predicted, and none can contradict the model.

It contains the Standard Model. Break $\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ at $M_C$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\to\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ at $M_R$, and what is left is exactly $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$, with the sixteen reducing to the fifteen Standard Model fields plus $\nu_R$. Below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model, so every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, for the same reason. §1.6 is the proof.

It is in contradiction with no experiment. Every claim the model makes is either confirmed or a bound on a free parameter. Below $M_R$ the three measured couplings are reproduced by construction, with $g_R$ absorbing the difference, and a solution exists for $M_C/M_R$ up to about $10^{6}$–$10^{7}$, with an edge that is a consistency requirement rather than a measurement.

What it costs is the ability to be confirmed. Every remaining statement is either a bound on a free mass or a structural fact already known to hold.

The strip is not the deletion of a derived prediction. The equality was written as conditional on parity: the four couplings collapse to two or to one, with $g_L=g_R$ marked if parity is imposed. The strip is the refusal to impose a parity assumption, so no unmet prediction is left behind. The failure that remains is colour, not the angle: at $10^{13}$ GeV the angle reaches $\tfrac38$ and $\alpha_3^{-1}$ is $13\%$ short (§2.5). Keeping the equality and admitting that the unified version is falsified is also defensible, and §10.3 states that position.

1. The Definition

1.1 The group and the content. The gauge group is

$$ G=\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R , \qquad \dim G=15+3+3=21 , $$

with no further factor and no simple group above it. One generation is one sixteen, in the two-piece form the algebra gives:

$$ (\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)\oplus(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2) =(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{\tfrac16}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-\tfrac12}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-\tfrac23}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{\tfrac13}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{1}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{0} $$

under the Standard Model subgroup, with $6+2+3+3+1+1=16$ components. The four entries on the second line are the conjugates of the right-handed fields, which is why their hypercharges read with the sign of $Y^{c}=-Y$. The last entry is $\nu_R$. The multiplets are fixed, and they are the frame's.

1.2 The chain and the two scales. The breaking runs in two steps, and the steps are independent:

$$ G\ \xrightarrow{\ M_C\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{\ M_R\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y . $$

The chain names three groups and two scales, and it does not name the multiplets that do the breaking. The spin-0 sector exists, because the symmetry is broken and something breaks it, but which multiplet performs each step is not fixed (§1.5). The frame puts both steps at one scale; this model allows them to differ, and the difference is bounded from above (§3.3).

1.3 The couplings, counted exactly. There are three gauge couplings, and one of them is not free:

coupling what fixes it
$g_4$ equal to $g_3(M_C)$, because $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ sits inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$; measured once $M_C$ is chosen
$g_{B-L}$ not independent: $g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38 g_4^2$ at $M_C$, from the generator normalisation
$g_L$ free
$g_R$ free, and determined in practice by the matching at $M_R$ (§3.1)

The strip removes exactly two relations, $g_L=g_4$ and $g_R=g_4$, and leaves one, $g_4=g_3(M_C)$. That single relation is what makes the model a statement about nature rather than a relabelling of the Standard Model, and it is also why the leptoquark floor does not weaken (§3.7).

1.4 The parameter count. The gauge sector has three quantities that can be chosen, $M_C$, $M_R$ and $g_R$, against three measured numbers. The count says nothing by itself; §3 shows why the system is consistent. The statement the model makes about the couplings is this: it reproduces the three measured values for any $(M_C,M_R)$ in a stated region, by adjusting $g_R$, and it predicts no value for them.

The spin-0 sector is not removed, and the sentence "the scalar sector is outside the model" is false. A spin-zero field takes its value in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module and not an element of the vector part, with the Klein–Gordon operator central. That sector is the frame's, and the model inherits it unchanged.

What the model does not fix is the index structure on top of it. A Pati–Salam theory places scalars in named multiplets, $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ on the carrier's four dimensions with its vacuum along the canonical line, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, and those assignments are declarations, not consequences of the fourth colour. The frame supplies the room, the centre, the carrier and the split $\mathbf4=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3$. It does not supply the guest list.

The distinction, in one sentence each.

  • The sector: spin zero, valued in $\mathbb B_C$, central, decoupled in the Klein–Gordon equation. Frame. Kept.
  • The multiplets: which of $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ are present, and how many. Declaration. Not fixed here.
  • The mass relations: $m_d=m_e$ and the rest. A consequence of the second line, and therefore also not fixed, not because the sector is gone but because the guest list is short.

Why the guest list cannot be derived here. The fermion bilinear is not a gauge singlet,

$$ (\bar{\mathbf4},\bar{\mathbf2},\mathbf1)\otimes(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2) $$

contains no $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$, so a scalar must be inserted with the right indices to write a mass term at all. Which indices is a choice among the multiplets the carrier admits, and the algebra does not select one. The frame's scorecard records the same fact as a number: the scalar axis scores zero for both Pati–Salam and the left–right model, because the spin-zero carrier is a line.

So the sector is in, the multiplet list is open, and the mass relations go with the list. §2.4 and §3.4 use exactly this boundary.

1.6 Does the model contain the Standard Model? Yes, and the proof runs in two steps.

The group. Break $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ at $M_C$ and what remains is the Standard Model's colour times one circle:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}, \qquad 15=8+1+3+\bar{\mathbf3}, $$

so the fifteen generators split into the octet, one $B-L$ boson and the six leptoquarks. The hypercharge. Break $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ at $M_R$, with

$$ Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L), $$

and what remains is $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$. Composing the two steps:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R \ \xrightarrow{M_C}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{M_R}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y . $$

The group below $M_R$ is exactly the Standard Model's, with no extra factor and no extra $\mathrm{U}(1)$.

The content matches too, and it is the check that matters. The sixteen of §1.1 reduces to

$$ (\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{\tfrac16}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-\tfrac12}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-\tfrac23}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{\tfrac13}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{1}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{0}, $$

which is the fifteen Standard Model fields of one generation plus $\nu_R$, at the measured hypercharges. Nothing is missing and nothing is added.

The consequence is why the model cannot contradict a measurement. Below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model: same group, same fields, same renormalisable Lagrangian, so every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, with the same numbers. A reader who wants the Standard Model back gets it by taking $M_R\to\infty$. This is a containment and not an approximation. The price is that a model which contains another inherits all of its successes and none of its explanations.

2. The Strip: The Coupling Equality Removed, the Spin-0 Sector Kept

2.1 What is removed, and it is a single thing. The unified version assumes $g_3=g_L=g_R=g_4$ at one scale. That assumption alone creates the failure: one boundary value, one scale, three measured couplings, over-determined by one, and no scale fits it. Removing it removes the failure entirely, because a relation that is not asserted cannot be violated. That is the whole of the strip.

2.2 What is kept: the spin-0 sector, whole. The strip touches the couplings and nothing else. Spin-zero fields remain what the frame says they are, valued in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module, with a central Klein–Gordon operator, and every statement the scalar sector makes about that structure stands. The sector is a structural result and is not under review.

2.3 What is left open: the multiplet list. The Pati–Salam scalar sector names three multiplets, $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, and computes their components, their eaten count and their surviving spectrum. That list is a declaration on top of the sector, and this model does not fix it.

The sector is not empty, and its size can be counted. For the minimal content there are twenty-four real components, twelve of them eaten, and no doublet among the survivors: the survivors are four singlets. That count is the right answer when a reader asks whether "the multiplet list is open" is a polite name for "there are no scalars". There are scalars, and there are twelve fewer of them than the naive count. The sector is in; the guest list is open.

2.4 The consequence for the mass relations, said precisely, because this is where a reader expects a dodge. The model does not predict $m_d=m_e$, and it predicts nothing else about fermion masses. Both statements are true at once.

  • Why no prediction is made. A mass relation is a statement about which multiplet sits in the Yukawa coupling, and the multiplet list is not fixed (§1.5). It is not that scalars are absent; it is that their index structure is not specified.
  • Why that is not an evasion. The model's claims are checkable and are checked: the group, the content, the charges, the anomalies, the couplings (§3). A statement about a gauge group does not by itself fix Yukawa indices, and no theory of this kind does.
  • What it costs. A theory that predicts no mass ratio cannot be confirmed by one, and cannot be refuted by one either. The cost is on the credit side, which §6.2 records.

2.5 Why the equality is the thing that fails and the angle is not. This is the sharpest statement of what the strip repairs.

Run the Standard Model's one-loop slopes to the scale where $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ exactly, about $10^{13}$ GeV. The two electroweak inverse couplings have met there: in $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ normalisation $\alpha_1^{-1}=\alpha_2^{-1}=42.4$, and the angle is where Pati–Salam needs it. What misses is colour: $\alpha_3^{-1}=36.8$ at that scale, about $13\%$ low, which is $\alpha_3$ about $15\%$ high.

So the boundary condition is reachable and the unification is not. The usual summary, that $\tfrac38$ is $38\%$ high at $250$ TeV, is true and is a statement about a chosen scale. The sharper statement is that at the scale the angle actually reaches $\tfrac38$, the strong coupling has not arrived. The missing ingredient is matter that changes $b_3$, and that is a statement about content, not about the angle.

If the failure were the angle, then removing the angle's prediction would be removing the failure, and a reader would be right to call that a dodge. The failure is colour arriving late, so removing $g_R=g_L$ does not remove it; it removes the requirement that all four couplings meet at one scale. That is the narrower repair, and §10.2 states what it still costs.

3. Why It Cannot Contradict Experiment

3.1 The mechanism. Below $M_R$ the theory is the Standard Model: the same group, the same fields, the same couplings. The measured values of $g_3$, $g_L$ and $g_Y$ at $M_Z$ are therefore reproduced by construction, not predicted. The matching at $M_R$,

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}, $$

is one equation, and it is solved for $g_R$, which no experiment measures. The model spends its freedom there, and the price is that it says nothing about the electroweak angle.

3.2 The computation, and where the solution exists. Fix $M_C$ and $M_R$; then $g_4=g_3(M_C)$ and $g_{B-L}(M_C)=\sqrt{\tfrac38}\,g_4$; run $g_{B-L}$ down to $M_R$; and solve the matching for $g_R$. A real $g_R$ requires that the bracket be positive:

$$ \frac{1}{g_R^2}=\frac{1}{g_Y^2}-\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{B-L}(M_R)>\tfrac12\,g_Y(M_R) . $$

A model that exceeded it would have no real $g_R$, which is a statement about the model rather than about nature. The number also moves with $b_{B-L}$, so every appearance of it names the coefficient it was computed for, $b_{B-L}=12$, the one-loop value for the minimal Pati–Salam content. Because the multiplet list is not fixed (§2.3), a change of list requires recomputing the edge, and a quotation of $10^{6}$ without the coefficient quotes a result whose premise it hides.

3.6 What this costs.

  • It is not confirmation. A null result in $K_L\to\mu e$ is what the model says, and it is also what a hundred other models say. A null result is consistent with the model and is not support for it.
  • The leptoquark floor does not weaken. $g_4$ is pinned to $g_3(M_C)$, so the bound $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)<4.7\times10^{-12}$ still gives $M_X\gtrsim2\times10^{2}$ TeV. The error to avoid is treating $g_4$ as free: it is tied to $g_3$ by $\mathrm{SU}(3)\subset\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and the floor is unchanged by the strip.
  • $g_R$ comes out below $g_L$ in every row of §3.2, by $6\%$ to $33\%$. That is the one number the model produces rather than assumes, and it is produced by the requirement that hypercharge come out right, not by a measurement of $g_R$.
  • Nothing is testable below the leptoquark mass. The model is a framework with a floor at a few hundred TeV.

5. Differences with the Left–Right Model

5.1 The containment. $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ is what remains when $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is broken. The fourth-colour model is the left–right model plus six gauge bosons and one relation.

5.2 The one relation the strip keeps, and why it is not a prediction. $g_3(M_C)=g_4$ is the statement that colour and $B-L$ are one factor above $M_C$. It is a relation between two couplings at a scale, not a value, so it survives the strip intact: no experiment measures a number that it fixes. It is what the left–right model does not have, where $g_3$ and $g_{B-L}$ are unrelated.

5.3 Charge quantisation, which is the left–right model's real loss. In $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, $B-L=\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1)$ is traceless on the quartet because it is a generator, so $3q+\ell=0$. In the left–right model $B-L$ is an independent $\mathrm{U}(1)$ and the relation must be imposed by hand. That is a derivation the strip does not touch.

5.5 Which to prefer, and on what ground. The left–right model is safer because it has no leptoquark and no floor. This model is richer because colour and lepton number are one factor, which is the only reason charge is quantised rather than assumed. A reader who wants safety takes the left–right model; a reader who wants the fourth colour takes this one. Neither can be excluded at current sensitivity, so the choice is structural and must be argued as such.

7. What Carries Over from the Translation of the Three Interactions

The translation of the strong, weak and electromagnetic interactions into Pati–Salam form survives almost intact, because it is a translation of the group and not of the couplings. What must be re-read is the part that used $g_4=g_L=g_R$.

7.1 Strong. The fifteen $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ generators split as $8+1+6$: the octet of gluons, the $B-L$ boson and the six leptoquarks. That is a statement about the adjoint and is untouched. $\alpha_s=\alpha_4$ at $M_C$ remains true. What changes is only the sentence that says $\alpha_s$ also equals $\alpha_L$ and $\alpha_R$ there.

7.2 Weak. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ is unbroken in the sense that matters: below $M_R$ its coupling runs exactly as in the Standard Model, and that is why the matching of §3.1 works. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is new and its coupling is computed, not assumed equal. The $W_R$ and $Z_R$ content, their charges and their couplings to fermions are unchanged.

7.3 Electromagnetic. The photon is still the unbroken mixture of three neutral states, $W_L^3$, $W_R^3$ and the $B-L$ boson, with coefficients proportional to the couplings. The mixture is unchanged; only the number $\tfrac38$ that it produced at one particular scale is gone. The derivation of the $Z$ coupling as $T_3-\sin^2\theta_WQ$ is untouched, because it is a statement about one rotation.

9. The Two Confirmations, Stated Plainly

9.1 Does the model contain the Standard Model? Yes.

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R \ \xrightarrow{M_C}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{M_R}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y , $$

with $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, and with the sixteen reducing to the fifteen Standard Model fields plus $\nu_R$ at the measured hypercharges. Below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model, same group, same fields, same Lagrangian, so it inherits every Standard Model success, and the limit $M_R\to\infty$ returns the Standard Model exactly. §1.6 is the proof.

9.2 Is it in contradiction with any experiment? No.

  • The couplings are not predicted, so no coupling measurement can be wrong. Below $M_R$ they are the measured ones by construction; the matching at $M_R$ determines $g_R$, which nothing measures (§3.1–§3.2).
  • The charges are derived and correct. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$ gives $3q+\ell=0$, hence $Q_p+Q_e=0$, confirmed to $10^{-21}$.
  • The anomalies cancel. Four rows and the Witten condition, on content that is unchanged.
  • The leptoquarks and $W_R$ are heavy and free. Their masses are floored, not fixed: $M_X\gtrsim2\times10^{2}$ TeV, $M_{W_R}\gtrsim$ a few TeV. A bound on a free parameter is a bound, not a refutation.
  • Proton decay is dimension nine. $M^{-6}$, unobservably long, and not a conflict either way.
  • The multiplet content of the spin-0 sector is not fixed, so no mass relation is asserted and none can be violated (§1.5, §2.4). The spin-0 sector itself is the frame's, valued in $\mathbb B_C$, and is kept.

§3.5 is the full table, and its verdict column contains no entry reading excluded. The statement is therefore that no measured number contradicts any claim the model makes, and not that the model is confirmed, and not that the model is safe. The confirmations are of containment and of silence, and both are stated as what they are.

9.3 What the confirmation does not cover.

  • It does not confirm the group. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is declared; the algebra supports the module, not the identification with the quark–lepton quartet.
  • It does not confirm the leptoquarks. They are permitted and heavy; nothing has seen them.
  • It does not confirm the two scales. Both are free above their floors.
  • It does not confirm the fourth colour's physical reality. It confirms only that the fourth colour costs nothing measurable.

10. Is the Strip Legitimate?

10.1 The answer, and the reasons. The question is whether it is legitimate to drop an assumption because the data exclude it. It is, and the reasons are checkable rather than rhetorical.

The strongest reason is in the frame's own words. It writes the boundary condition as

$$ g_3(M_{PS})=g_4(M_{PS}),\qquad g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})\equiv g_4(M_{PS})\quad\text{(if parity is imposed)}, $$

and its own summary of the collapse is "two, or to one", in that order, with the parity equality marked as conditional. So the equality of $g_L$ and $g_R$ was not asserted as a result; it was asserted conditionally on imposing parity. The strip is therefore the refusal to impose parity, a choice the frame had already marked as a choice, in a parenthesis and in a table row. A conditional that is not imposed leaves no unmet prediction behind, which is why the strip removes a failure rather than an embarrassment.

The remaining reasons:

10.2 What the strip is not. It is not a way to keep a dead model alive by deleting whatever killed it, and the distinction decides the case.

  • It removes one assumption. Not a prediction that failed, not a measurement, not a structural result.
  • It does not touch the scalar sector (§2.2), the anomalies, the charges or the leptoquark content.
  • It does not claim the model is right. The table of §3.5 says no measurement contradicts it, and §9.2 says plainly that this is not confirmation.

A theory that retreats from a claim each time it is tested and never gives anything up is not legitimate. The test is whether the retreat costs something, and here it does: the model loses its only sharp electroweak prediction and its claim to unification. That is a real price, and §6.2 lists it.

10.3 The alternative, stated fairly, because it is also defensible. Keep $g_4=g_L=g_R$ and admit that the unified version is falsified by the coupling triple. That position has the merit of a theory that made a prediction and lost, and a reader who prefers it is not being unreasonable.

the strip keep the equality
the coupling failure removed with the assumption admitted: the unified version is excluded
what survives the group, the content, the charges, the anomalies, the leptoquarks, the $K_S$ rule the same, and the $\tfrac38$ prediction that failed
the cost no sharp electroweak prediction, no unification claim a falsified model, kept as falsified

The first column is the better choice, and the reason is narrow: the structural content the frame actually derives, charge quantisation from $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, the four anomaly cancellations, the fourth colour as the unit line, does not depend on the equality. Losing the equality costs a prediction; losing the group would cost the derivations. The strip keeps the second and drops the first, which is the right way round.

10.4 What would settle it. Not a measurement, because every remaining quantity is a bound on a free parameter (§3.7). What would settle it is a derivation: a reason, from the algebra or from the carrier, why $g_R$ should equal $g_L$ at some scale, or why it should not. The frame has neither, and until it does, the strip is a choice between two defensible positions rather than a consequence.

11. The Synchronised Variant: Adding the Equality Back at a Higher Scale

The proposal, stated fairly, because it deserves that. Keep everything in §1 and add a third scale: three scales $M_{PS}>M_C>M_R$, with $g_4=g_L=g_R$ imposed at $M_{PS}$ and nothing changed below. The unified Pati–Salam model is then the special case $M_{PS}=M_C=M_R$, and the strip is the case where the condition is dropped entirely. Between the two there is this third thing, and the argument for it is real: the extra segment changes the running, so the couplings that failed to meet in one step might meet in three.

The variant is not empty, and it is not a repair either. The reason is structural, not numerical.

11.1 The count, before any running. Two conditions are imposed, $g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})$ and $g_4(M_{PS})=g_L(M_{PS})$, on three scales. Two equations with three unknowns is a curve, not a point. So "is it empty?" is the wrong first question; generically it is not. The right question is what a curve is worth: a curve means a free parameter, and a free parameter means nothing is predicted. The computation below decides whether the curve is real and where it lives.

11.2 The obstruction, and it can be seen before any number is run. Above $M_R$ the field content is parity-symmetric: $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, and a bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$. Each $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ multiplet has an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of the same dimension, so the two beta coefficients are equal as a matter of symmetry, not of choice:

$$ c_{2L}=c_{2R}. $$

Equal coefficients mean the difference of the inverse couplings is frozen, because running both from $M_R$ to $M_{PS}$ adds the same amount to each:

$$ \frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_{PS})}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_{PS})}=\frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_R)}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_R)}. $$

So the second condition is not a condition at $M_{PS}$ at all. It is a condition at $M_R$. The three-scale construction collapses to one equation, fixed at the lower end, and the third scale buys nothing for it:

$$ g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})\iff g_L(M_R)=g_R(M_R). $$

This is what the argument that extra running gives colour more room misses. The extra running does give colour more room, and §11.5 shows exactly how much, but it does nothing whatever to $g_R$ relative to $g_L$, because parity makes their running identical. The obstruction is not colour.

11.6 What would rescue it, and the price, in numbers. The freeze of §11.2 is what blocks it, and the freeze is broken only by breaking parity above $M_R$. Then the gap can run, but it has to run a long way. At $M_C=250$ TeV and $M_R=25$ TeV the gap is $49.6$ in $1/\alpha$ and there are $15.9$ e-folds to the colour meeting, so the required asymmetry is

$$ \Delta c_2\simeq-2\pi\frac{49.6}{15.9}\simeq-20 . $$

A single Weyl doublet moves $c_2$ by $1/3$, so this asks for about sixty more $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ doublets than $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ doublets above $M_R$. That is not a threshold-correction-sized asymmetry; it is a different theory, and one with no reason behind the number sixty. Parity restoration is supposed to be the point of a left–right model, and this variant can only work by giving it up above $M_R$, which is where it would be most natural.

11.7 The verdict.

  • Not empty. A solution exists, at $M_C\simeq M_R\simeq2.5\times10^{13}$ TeV and $M_{PS}\simeq5\times10^{13}$ TeV.
  • Not a repair. At that solution $g_R$ is fitted, not predicted, so the agreement at $M_Z$ is the input coming back; and $\tfrac38$ holds at $10^{16}$ GeV, eleven orders of magnitude above the leptoquark floor and unobservable in every way §3.7 counts.
  • Obstructed by the wrong coupling. The hope was that extra running fixes colour. It does, since $g_4$ meets $g_L$ at every $M_C$, but the meeting that fails is $g_R=g_L$, and parity freezes it above $M_R$.
  • One free parameter, zero predictions. The solution is a curve indexed by $M_C\ge2.5\times10^{13}$ TeV. A curve is freedom, and freedom spent on a scale is freedom not spent on a number.

So the short statement is that adding a third scale does not restore a prediction, because the third scale is what absorbs the mismatch. Measured at $M_Z$, the variant reproduces the data because $g_R$ is solved from them. The strip is not a stepping stone to this variant; it is the variant with the free parameter left visible.

11.8 The caveat that applies to every number in this section. These are one-loop estimates with the coefficients stated in §11.3, on minimal content with $b_{B-L}=12$. Two of them move under a different content: the crossing scale of §11.4 and the meeting scales of §11.5. The conclusion does not move, because it rests on the freeze of §11.2, an equality of beta coefficients forced by parity rather than a numerical coincidence, and on the sign of the gap, which is $-27\%$ to $-38\%$ and would have to reverse. What a two-loop or threshold-corrected computation could change is where the curve lives, not whether it is a curve.

Summary

The model is $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, with three independent couplings, two scales, and a spin-0 sector kept whole whose multiplet list is left open. The group, the sixteen, charge quantisation, the four anomaly cancellations, the boson count, the leptoquarks and the $K_S$ rule are unchanged.

One strip, not two. Dropping $g_4=g_L=g_R$ removes the coupling failure, and that is the entirety of what is removed. The spin-0 sector is not touched: it is the frame's, valued in the centre $\mathbb B_C$ and a line in the state module, and it stays as the frame states it. What is left open is the multiplet list, which is a declaration on top of the sector and not a consequence of the fourth colour. Deleting the scalar sector destroys a frame result; adding a multiplet list adds a claim the model does not make.

It contains the Standard Model, and that is proved rather than asserted. The chain to $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ is exact, the sixteen reduces to the fifteen plus $\nu_R$, and below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model. Every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, and the $M_R\to\infty$ limit returns the Standard Model.

It cannot contradict experiment, and the reason is structural. The three measured couplings are reproduced by construction, with $g_R$ absorbing the difference; every other number is a bound on a free parameter or a structural fact already holding. A solution exists for $M_C/M_R$ from unity to about $10^{6}$–$10^{7}$; the edge is the reality of $g_R$, not a measurement.

What it costs. No prediction of $\sin^2\theta_W$, no mass relation, no unification, and therefore nothing that could confirm it.

The number that looks like a prediction is not one. $g_R/g_L$ comes out between $0.67$ and $0.94$, and that is an output, not a test. It is fixed once $(M_C,M_R)$ are chosen, which is the opposite of a prediction. A prediction would be a number fixed without choosing anything, and the model has none. The count of testable consequences is zero, and the count of confirmed structural facts is four: charge quantisation, the content, the anomalies and the boson count.

The judgement in one line: the fourth-colour left–right model keeps everything structural about Pati–Salam and drops only the coupling equality that the data exclude. It contains the Standard Model, cannot contradict experiment, and cannot be confirmed either. That is a better position than the unified version had, and a worse one than the unified version claimed. §10 argues why taking it is legitimate rather than convenient.

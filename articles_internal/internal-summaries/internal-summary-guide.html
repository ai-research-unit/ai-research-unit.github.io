Guide to the Internal Summary Articles

Introduction

What this file is. A writing guide for the internal-summary-* family of articles_internal. It fixes the boundary, the skeleton, the paragraph grammar, the claim ledger, and above all the two rules of content: cut what a machine regenerates, quote what it cannot.

Who reads the output. An AI agent that must reason inside the corpus without reading the category. It has a small window and no memory of the articles. It does not need to be taught. It needs the objects it cannot guess, the equations it must reproduce, the claims that contradict the default, and the traps.

The rule that governs the genre. A summary is a cache, not a digest and not a lecture. It stores the information of the corpus at the lowest token cost, in the symbols of the corpus, so that any line can be audited in one fetch and any sharp claim can be quoted without opening the article.

How to use this guide. §1 and §2 give the two rules of content, and they outrank everything else. §3 fixes the boundary, §4 the shape, §5 to §8 the parts. §9 lists what must be quoted, §10 what must be cut, §11 the budget, §12 the traps, §13 the check pass.

1. Internal First, Machine First

Internal. The file lives in articles_internal/, which build.py does not build and maths.md and physics.md do not list. It is not for the reader of the site. It carries no HTML link, no navigation, no introduction for a newcomer and no apology for jargon. A slug in backticks is enough.

Machine first. The reader is a model, not a person. It reads fast, it has no patience and no memory of the articles, and it already knows standard mathematics. Anything a competent model regenerates from a definition is dead weight in a summary, and dead weight costs exactly as many tokens as the load-bearing line beside it.

The two consequences. Cut the obvious. Quote the sharp. Every remaining section of this guide is one of these two rules applied.

What follows for tone. No motivation, no preamble, no "this is interesting because", no praise, no ranking of articles, no history of the category unless the history is the claim. A sentence that only eases the reader spends tokens on a machine that needs no easing.

2. The Two Rules of Content

The rule of the prior. Ask of every line: can a competent model produce this line from what it already knows, given the name of the object? If yes, delete it. The question is not whether the line is true or useful to a human. It is whether the line changes what the model can compute.

The rule of the irrecoverable line. Ask of every other line: could the model recover this by reasoning? If not, the line must be present and must be verbatim. Four kinds pass this test, and they are the whole content of a summary: the sharp claim, the structuring fact, the load-bearing equation, and the declared convention, trap or refusal.

The two rules together are one test. A summary is correct when every line either cannot be regenerated or should never have been written. A file of regenerable lines teaches nothing and costs everything.

The three checks on a candidate line. Does it change a derivation the agent will perform? Can the model regenerate it from a definition plus the name of the object? Is it quotable verbatim from a named article? Two noes and a yes is a cut. The third check alone is not enough: a textbook identity copied faithfully is still a line the model already had.

Consequence for balance. A category of forty articles with three sharp claims gives three claims and a table of objects. A category of eleven articles with thirty claims gives thirty claims. The length follows the claim density, not the article count. This is the opposite of the instinct of a human digest, and it is why the existing summaries range from four to sixteen sections for similar article counts.

3. Fixing the Boundary

Three inputs, one output. A list of articles, a section of maths.md or physics.md, and a whole category produce the same file. Only the boundary comes from the input. Everything after this section is identical.

Resolve the boundary physically. Do not trust the input string. Extract the anchors of the section:

awk '/^## /{sec=substr($0,4)} sec=="<Category>" && /<a href=/{print}' physics.md

The count of anchors is the article count. State it in words, as the existing files do: twenty-nine articles, eighty-two articles, one hundred and one articles.

Recompute the count at the time of writing. The menus drift: an article is added, a block moves, a subsection is renamed. Recompute the count from the anchors at every write, and recompute a count taken by subtraction as well. The family has already carried both kinds of stale number, and a stale count is a defect the next reader inherits.

Record the exceptions. Two kinds, both in the Introduction. Articles of the cluster absent from the menu: the gravity summary names five and says the listing is incomplete. Articles of another category read here: the same summary names two and says where they sit. Never silently widen or narrow the boundary.

Coverage means claim coverage, not article coverage. Every article inside the boundary must be reachable: cited in a **Source.** line, named in a prose enumeration, or listed in Further Reading. It need not get a block. An article with no irrecoverable line is a member of a list, and that is the right fate for it. The check is mechanical:

grep -o 'articles_physics/[a-z0-9-]*\.md' <file> | sort -u | wc -l

The particle physics summary cites all ninety-three of its articles, in six lists of bare slugs, one list per part; a slug in backticks is the citation form. The defect to avoid is a sharp claim that no source line can audit.

4. The Skeleton

Six parts, always in this order. The fifteen existing summaries share it without exception.

Part Job Share of the file
Title line # __Internal Summary of the <Name>__ one line
Introduction what the file is, what the category does, the structural fact, the one-sentence version 10 to 20 per cent
Numbered sections ## 1. <Object>, one per group of claims 65 to 80 per cent
## Summary the formulas that carry the category, the non-textbook results, the refusals 5 to 10 per cent
## Summary of Notation symbol table, used symbols only 10 to 20 lines
## Further Reading reading order, then the traps 3 to 8 lines

Cut the sections by claim, not by article. Two articles that yield one claim give one item. One article that yields four claims gives four items and may take a whole section. The menu order is editorial; the alphabet is meaningless.

Number the sections. Arabic numerals, a full stop, a title that names an object and not an activity. The Field Strength and the Single Equation is right. Discussion is not.

5. The Introduction

Six bold leads, in this order. They are the signature of the family, and an agent that sees them knows the contract.

  • What this file is. The boundary, the count, and the content: objects, boxed equations, non-trivial claims. Then the three sentences that never vary in meaning: no proofs; an agent re-derives them; every formula is copied from the source article named in the section.
  • What the category does. Two or three sentences. Separate what the category develops from what it records and what it refuses. The particle physics summary does this in a paragraph, and the separation runs through the file.
  • The single structural fact. One display, usually boxed, that organises the category: the vacuum as a minimal idempotent, the metric as a Gram matrix of a frame, the field strength as one pure vector in two halves. It is the highest-value line in the file, because the agent cannot recover it from the objects alone. When no single fact organises the category, say so and give the spine of three or four lines, as the operators summary does.
  • The non-trivial claim. One paragraph under the structural fact, saying why the fact is not a restatement. Load-bearing, never omitted.
  • The one-sentence version. One sentence, no display. What the agent quotes when it has room for one line.
  • How to use it. Optional, and only when the reading order matters. The universe summary names the sections to read first and the section of traps to read before writing.

Add the subsection table when the menu has subsections. Two columns, the subsection and what it settles. Eight of the fifteen summaries carry one, seven in that two-column form and one with an article count added. The table is cheap and it doubles as the skeleton.

Add three optional leads, and only when true. The price, when the category buys its result with an obstruction. The discipline of the category, when the classification of claims is the content, as in gravity. A defect to record, when the menu or the corpus is incomplete.

6. The Sections

The per-item grammar. Four moves, in this order, for every item that carries an irrecoverable line:

**<Object name in bold.>** One sentence of definition, only if the name does not fix it.

$$

$$

**The non-trivial claim.** One paragraph: what the display says that a competent
model would not produce, and what it costs.

**Source.** `articles_physics/<slug>.md`, `articles_physics/<slug>.md`.

The definition is optional; the claim is not. Drop the defining sentence whenever the name and the display fix the meaning. Never drop the claim paragraph. A block with a display and no claim is a textbook fragment, and the model already has the textbook.

One claim per item. If an item has no irrecoverable claim, it belongs in a one-line list inside the section. The particle physics summary lists its seven shared-machinery articles in a single sentence and spends its blocks on the ten articles of principle. That ratio is correct, not lazy.

The claim is stated against something. The constraint is a consequence and not an extra condition. The algebra of one-sided operators is seven-dimensional, not eight. A claim with no contrast is a definition in disguise, and a definition in disguise is a cut.

Box only the load-bearing display. \boxed{} marks the equation an agent must reproduce. The existing files use zero to fourteen boxes; the electromagnetism summary uses eight for twenty-nine articles, the universe summary none, because its load-bearing content is its tables. The box count is a budget, not a quota.

Use ### when a section passes four items. The intersection rule of the corpus applies unchanged: give every intersection a heading, define before tabulating.

Tables carry counts, dimensions and ledgers. Which subspace, which dimension, which role; which region, which invariant, which reading. A table is the cheapest way to store an enumeration, and the right way to store one.

Close the section with **Source.** when it maps to a set of articles. Backticked relative paths, .md extension, comma separated. Every one of the seventeen summaries now closes each mapped section this way; the particle physics summary is the one exception, and it uses *Articles.* with bare slugs, one list per part. Pick one form per file and hold it.

Do not repeat a display. The same formula in three sections is a sign that the sections are one section.

Do not write the algebra out. No intermediate step of a derivation, no chain of substitutions, no expansion the model performs faster than it reads. The summary holds the endpoints and the obstruction, not the route.

7. The Claim Ledger

Four labels, kept apart. Established: proved inside the corpus. Carried or transcribed: written in the letters, not derived from them. Imported: taken from the literature, with the reference named. Open or refused: no construction exists, and the article says why.

The summary never promotes a claim. A transcribed formula is not upgraded to derived by appearing in a compressed file. The gravity summary states the rule and applies it to itself: even the linearised sector is carried.

Every summary says what its category refuses. A required part of ## Summary, not a nuance. The relativity summary names two refusals: the dynamics is imported, and no spatial axis is canonical. The particle physics summary names one, with the dimension count that excludes colour. A refusal is the highest-value line in the file for a machine, because it stops a confident wrong derivation.

An obstruction is a result and is written as one. A category that cannot reach a result is not a weaker category; it is a category with a proved boundary. Use the sentences of the article.

8. The Formulas

Copied, never reconstructed. A display is copied from the article named beside it, with the same symbols, hats, primes and order of factors. A formula retyped from memory is a new formula.

No formula is invented in the summary. If the map needs an equation no article writes, the map needs a sentence naming the gap. The one-sentence version is prose for this reason: prose cannot drift into a false identity.

Follow the corpus conventions. Display math in $$`, indented four spaces inside a list. Never let `$...$` span a line break. A prime is a superscript on the letter and the subscript stays the index it names: $\tilde Q'_{\Phi}$, never a prime below.\dddotand\slashedare template macros. Noarray` tables; a table is Markdown.

One symbol, one meaning, across the file. The index convention holds here exactly as in the articles: low indices, no raised spacetime index, the weight $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}$ for a contraction.

The recomputation rule. Every count, dimension and coefficient asserted in the summary is recomputed on at most one hundred elements before it is written. A claim no check reaches is left out, or is written with the word recorded in front of it.

9. What Must Be Quoted

Four kinds of line, and each is quoted verbatim from a named article.

The sharp claim. The statement that contradicts the default reading and that the model gets wrong unguided. The default of the model is the textbook; the summary stores the delta.

  • The light cone is the zero-divisor cone.
  • A boost acts on the state as a rescaling and not as a channel, so the quantum operations are exactly the rotations.
  • The algebra of one-sided operators is seven-dimensional, not eight, and the missing direction is exactly the two-sided sandwich.
  • The two classical invariants of the field are the real and imaginary parts of one biquaternion norm, so a null field is a zero divisor.

The structuring fact. The one object or identity that organises a whole category, stated once at the top, so that the agent holds the frame before the items. It earns a display and a box.

  • The vacuum is a minimal idempotent: "no particles" is a rank-one projection inside the algebra and not a vector added to the theory.
  • The metric is the Gram matrix of a frame of four elements, so the algebra carries any Lorentzian metric at the price of a frame field.
  • The unitary group of $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, and $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{SU}(3)=8>4$ excludes colour.

The load-bearing equation. The equation an agent must reproduce to work in the category, copied with its glyphs. Not every equation of the article: the one that states the result.

  • $\mathrm{Vect}\bigl((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi\bigr)=\sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde F$ together with $\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde F=-\sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde J_\Phi$,
  • $\tilde Q_2=\tilde U\tilde Q\tilde U^{*}$ with $\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}=\sqrt{-\tfrac{i}{c}\tilde V_\Phi^{\natural}}$,
  • $\mathbb{B}^{e}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$.

The declared convention, the trap and the refusal. The line that overrides a plausible default: which metric, which sign, which conjugation, which index rule, which object is not available. A model that guesses here writes a wrong derivation with a correct-looking equation.

  • One metric, $\delta_{{\imath}{\jmath}}=(1,1,1,1)$; no raised spacetime index anywhere.
  • The Lorentzian sign comes from $i^{2}=-1$ and not from a metric.
  • The five unlisted articles of the gravity cluster are absent from the menu; the listing is incomplete.
  • Colour is excluded by the dimension count, not merely missing from one construction.

The test for a candidate quote. It must be short, auditable against one article, and impossible to regenerate. A quote that passes all three is worth ten lines of prose about it.

10. What Must Be Cut

Six kinds of line, and each is the default of a human digest.

  • The calculus chain. The intermediate step, the substitution, the integration by parts. The model performs it and does not need to read it. Keep the endpoints and the obstruction.
  • The standard definition. The definition of a commutator, a Fock space, a covariant derivative, a Hilbert space. The model has it. Define only the object whose name does not fix the meaning in this corpus.
  • The textbook derivation. A proof of a known theorem restaged in the letters of the corpus. Cite the article; do not restage it.
  • The restatement of the abstract. The aim, the programme, the plan. The summary stores the result.
  • The motivation and the transition. "It is worth noting", "as we have seen", "this is interesting because". A machine needs none of them.
  • The echo. A closing paragraph that repeats the sections. The three closing blocks are the only compression allowed, and they carry content, not echo.

What looks like content and is not. A boxed identity that restates a definition. A table of symbols already defined in the body. A paragraph whose only verb is is. Each costs the tokens of a sharp claim and buys nothing.

11. Length, Density and the Budget

Measured on the seventeen existing files. Sizes run from 13 KB to 41 KB, lines from 178 to 612, numbered sections from 4 to 16, non-trivial claims from 1 to 16, boxed displays from 0 to 13, citation blocks from 4 to 16, total 5,639 lines.

Density is claim density, not article density. The electromagnetism summary gives every item a block and boxes eight displays for twenty-nine articles. The relativity summary gives the spine a block and the enumerations a table, at one hundred and one articles. The universe summary boxes nothing and carries sixteen sections of structure. Each is right for its claim count.

Budget rule. One block per irrecoverable claim, one line per article that carries no claim, one table per enumeration of more than four objects. Nothing else. A section that exceeds it is either over-written or is really two sections.

The floor is the acceptance test. A file that fails the test is too short, whatever its length. A file that repeats itself is too long, whatever its length.

The acceptance test, restated for a machine. Given only the summary, an agent can name every object the boundary defines; write the result equations; avoid the traps; say what the category refuses; and know which claims are carried rather than established. Nothing else is required, and anything else is optional.

12. Ten Traps

  • The trap of the abstract. Summarising the aim instead of the result. Fix: quote the result equation, then state the claim.
  • The trap of the pedagogic line. Writing for a human who needs easing. Fix: delete the easing, keep the claim.
  • The trap of the drift count. Trusting a stated number. Fix: recompute from the anchors or the file system.
  • The trap of the sample. Mapping a third of the boundary and claiming the category. Fix: the coverage grep, plus a sentence naming what is only a list and why.
  • The trap of the new proof. Adding a derivation not in the corpus. Fix: delete it, name the gap.
  • The trap of the promotion. Writing a transcribed result as derived. Fix: the claim ledger, line by line.
  • The trap of the boxed definition. Boxing an identity so the file looks dense. Fix: box the result, leave the definition plain.
  • The trap of the single reading. Reporting one sector of a two-sector result. Fix: state the pair, as the electromagnetism summary does with the electric and magnetic halves.
  • The trap of the invented symbol. Introducing a glyph the articles do not use. Fix: the Notation table is a contract.
  • The trap of the silent exception. Leaving out an article that does not fit. Fix: name it in the Introduction with its reason.

13. The Check Pass

Run these before handing the file over. Each is mechanical, and the shape is not visible while writing.

grep -c '^## ' <file>                      # skeleton: Introduction, N, Summary, Notation, Reading
grep -n '^## \|^# ' <file>                 # numbered sections in order, no gap
grep -c 'boxed' <file>                     # the box budget, against the claim count
grep -c '^\*\*Source\.\*\*' <file>         # sources, or none at all
grep -o 'articles_[a-z]*/[a-z0-9-]*\.md' <file> | sort -u | wc -l   # coverage
grep -c 'non-trivial claim' <file>         # one claim per block, or a list instead
grep -nE '(\$\\square\$|\\\(\\square\\\))[ \t]*$' <file>           # no proof-end marks
awk '{n=gsub(/\$/,"&"); if (n%2) print NR": odd number of dollars"}' <file>   # no open math, code blocks excluded

The last check skips fenced blocks. A shell command inside a fence carries its own dollars and is not math.

Then read the file as the machine reader. Six questions. Does the Introduction state the count, the boundary and the structural fact? Is every block either a claim or a list? Is every quote short and auditable against one article? Does any line merely restate a definition the model already has? Does the Summary carry the formulas, the non-textbook results and the refusals? Does the file say what the category refuses?

A file that passes the greps and fails the reading is not done. The greps prove the shape; they cannot prove the density.

Summary

An internal summary is a machine cache of a boundary of the corpus. It is internal: not built, not in the menus, no HTML. It is machine-first: no motivation, no easing, no restaged derivation, no standard definition.

Two rules carry the genre. Cut every line a competent model regenerates from a definition and a name. Quote every line it cannot: the sharp claim, the structuring fact, the load-bearing equation, the declared convention, the trap, the refusal. Length follows claim density and not article count, which is why the existing files range from four to sixteen sections for similar article counts.

Three disciplines keep it honest. Every quote is copied verbatim from a named article. Every count is recomputed at the time of writing. Every refusal is stated, because a proved boundary is the line that stops a confident wrong derivation.

The fifteen files are the template. Read the electromagnetism summary for the item grammar, the relativity summary for the closing three and the sharp claims, the particle physics summary for the cut of a large category, and the gravity summary for the claim ledger and the record of a defect.

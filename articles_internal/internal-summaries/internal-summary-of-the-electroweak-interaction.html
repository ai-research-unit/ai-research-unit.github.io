What the part does. It develops one thing and imports everything else. The single structural fact.

$$ \boxed{\;\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathbb{R}(ie_t)\oplus\mathrm{SU}(2),\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{U}(2)=4,\qquad \mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}\subset\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The compact algebra is four-dimensional and its group is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, so the electroweak algebras fit and the electroweak theory does not. The $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ present is the rotation group of space — the group of rotors of the spatial rotations, the double cover of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ — not an internal isospin. The dimension match of four directions is a match of counts, not of kinds: three spacetime and one internal, where the Standard Model has four internal. The $\mathrm{U}(1)$ present is the central phase, which is vector-like and therefore cannot be $Y$.

Why the split is a theorem and not a lapse. Representation is fixed by spin. The gauge group is the spin group, so a field cannot carry a representation chosen independently of its spin. The Standard Model needs the two independent; this framework forbids the separation. Three of the four mismatches in the part reduce to that one sentence.

The letter, after the migration. The group keeps its name. It is $SU(2)$, and the biquaternion statement is that $SU(2)$ is the spin group of the spatial rotations: $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{Spin}(3)=S^3$, the unit real quaternions under multiplication, acting on a vector by the two-sided map $\tilde u\vec v\tilde u^{*}$. The identity is not an embedding and not an analogy, and it is the reason the part can use the algebra instead of a matrix display. Each article names $SU(2)$ where the group is introduced, states the identification in the same sentence, and then writes the rotor, $\mathrm{Sd}$, $L_{\tilde u}$ and $\vec e_a$ where the rotor does the work; the convention is fixed once in articles_physics/representation-is-a-function-of-spin.md, §The Rotor Conventions. Four consequences run through the part:

  • $SU(2)$ acts on the algebra by several operators, and a gauge field gets one of them. The four are $\mathrm{Sd}$, the Hermitian pair; $\operatorname{Ad}$, the conjugation; $\operatorname{ad}$, the commutator; and $L$, the left multiplication. A connection on a carrier gets $L$, which is why the carrier is a minimal left ideal and the action is one-sided.

  • The group is a quotient. The electroweak group is $(\mathrm{SU}(2)\times S^1)/\{\pm e_t\}$, not the product. The compact factor and the centre share the two points $\pm e_t$, and that sharing is the parity lock $q\equiv2j\pmod 2$ — the group-theoretic source of the half-angle obstruction.

  • The action is one-sided. A gauge field acts by left multiplication, $L_{\tilde u}$, so the carrier is a minimal left ideal of dimension two and the action cannot be made two-sided. The chirality operator of the EDM article is a right multiplication precisely because the left side is taken.
  • The scalar is central. The framework's scalar is a central singlet, so it cannot break the non-abelian factor; the abelian mechanism is the one the algebra carries.

The rotor is used, not only named. The condition on the part is that the equivalence of $SU(2)$ to a spatial rotor is explained and used. 1. The Group, the Centre and the Split on the Element

Two gauge principles, one algebra. The abelian one is localisation of the central phase; the non-abelian one runs on the compact factor inside the material sector.

$$ \mathrm{U}(1)=\{\lambda=\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}):\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathbb{B}_{C}, \qquad \tilde A_{\Phi,2}=\tilde A_{\Phi}-\tilde\nabla_\Phi\Gamma, \qquad D=\tilde\nabla_\Phi+\frac{2\pi i\,ϙ}{h}\tilde A_{\Phi}, $$

$$ \boxed{\;[e_a,e_b]=2\,\varepsilon_{abc}\,e_c,\qquad T_a=\tfrac12 e_a,\quad [T_a,T_b]=\varepsilon_{abc}T_c,\quad 2\,\mathrm{Sc}(T_aT_b)=-\tfrac12\delta_{ab}\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The global symmetry available for free is the centre, so the only gauging the algebra offers without enlargement is a central phase, and it gives a vector-like $\mathrm{U}(1)$: one charge for both sides of the element. The non-abelian factor is the span of the three letters with the quaternion commutator as its bracket, and its normalisation is fixed by the plain weight $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(T_aT_b)=-\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$, not chosen.

Three grades, which the ceiling article states and the whole part obeys. Theorem: the compact subalgebra of $\mathbb{B}$ has real dimension at most $4$; every $\mathbb{B}$-module has complex dimension divisible by two. Interpretation: that the first is a no-go of the Coleman–Mandula type — that a compact internal symmetry commuting with the Lorentz action is what the count excludes — is a reading, not a corollary. Forbidden inference: that the ceiling makes the Standard Model impossible in principle. A construction that enlarged the algebra, or reached the internal factors by another route, is untouched by the count. Mixing the three is how a framework acquires a reputation for claiming more than it proves.

3. Hypercharge: Four Conditions, Three Rays

The system, not the table. The hypercharges of the fifteen fields of one generation are the solution of four equations: three linear and one cubic.

$$ \underbrace{2Y_Q-Y_u-Y_d=0}_{SU(3)_c^2\,\mathrm{U}(1)_Y}, \qquad \underbrace{3Y_Q+Y_L=0}_{SU(2)_L^2\,\mathrm{U}(1)_Y}, $$

$$ \underbrace{6Y_Q-3Y_u-3Y_d+2Y_L-Y_e=0}_{\text{gravitational}}, \qquad \underbrace{6Y_Q^3-3Y_u^3-3Y_d^3+2Y_L^3-Y_e^3=0}_{\mathrm{U}(1)_Y^3}. $$

$$ M=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&-1&0&0\\3&0&0&1&0\\6&-3&-3&2&-1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf p=(1,1,1,-3,-6),\qquad \mathbf q=(0,1,-1,0,0). $$

The non-trivial claim. The linear part has rank three on five unknowns, so a two-parameter family survives, $Y_Q=p$, $Y_u=p+q$, $Y_d=p-q$, $Y_L=-3p$, $Y_e=-6p$; the cubic cuts it to three rays, and the observed charges ($p=\tfrac16$, $q=\tfrac12$) select the first. The second ray is the $u\leftrightarrow d$ relabelling; the third has $Y_Q=0$ and is anomaly-free but gives the wrong quark charges, so it is excluded by measurement, not by consistency. That is the precise content of "hypercharge is not arbitrary": two continuous parameters and one discrete choice, fixed by four conditions and three measured charges.

Two traps. The mixed writing — the minus-sign form applied to a conjugate hypercharge, or the plus-sign form to a table hypercharge — gives $\tfrac23$ instead of $0$. And the gravitational row is not a combination of the other two: removing it leaves $Y_e$ free.

The Witten condition constrains the same assignment, and it is the one place where the number of colours enters a consistency condition rather than a coupling: with an odd number of doublets the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ global anomaly survives.

Why the framework cannot supply it. Not a bigger algebra and not a different group: what is needed is a generator whose left action and right action differ, so that the two sides of an element carry different charges. The corpus's operator chirality is a slot, and a slot is exactly such an object. That is §4.

4. The Chiral Wall and the Letter Law

The letter is read, not carried. Chirality is a basis operator, $\xi=-iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z}$, whose two values name the two parts. It is not a sign, a grading, a root, an idempotent, a Peirce component, an ideal or a Clifford element.

$$ \xi=-iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z},\qquad \xi\,Q=-i\,Q\,\vec e_z, \qquad b=\tfrac{ϙ_L+ϙ_R}{2}\,e_t-\tfrac{i}{2}(ϙ_R-ϙ_L)\,\vec e_z . $$

The letter law, which is the part's central obstruction. A left multiplication keeps the letter of both parts, so an action $Q\mapsto aQ$ applies one element to the two parts and cannot give them separate charges, whatever $a$ is. Independent charges therefore need the right slot, and they need an element of two unequal values, because a right multiplication by an element of a single value multiplies both parts by the same factor. A central multiplier is the extreme case: it is the same factor on both sides, which is why the framework's own $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is vector-like.

The mass selection rule. The bilinear is

$$ b=\tfrac12(\varkappa_L+\varkappa_R)e_t-\tfrac{i}{2}(\varkappa_R-\varkappa_L)\vec e_z, $$

and its consequence is the rule the whole flavour sector runs on: a bare Dirac mass is invariant if and only if $\varkappa_L=\varkappa_R$. A chiral mass term is therefore not forbidden by fiat; it is forbidden by the algebra, and the compensating object must carry the difference $\varkappa_L-\varkappa_R$, which is non-central.

What would lift it. A carrier that is not a left ideal — a two-slot structure, or an enlarged algebra on which the left action and the letter are independent. On a left ideal the letter is a right-multiplication eigenvalue and left multiplications commute with right multiplications, so nothing smaller lifts it.

5. The Centre Bound and the Intrinsic Group

The quantised central phase.

$$ \boxed{\;L=\pi i m\,e_t+\pi n\,\vec U,\qquad m\equiv n\pmod 2\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The allowed central phases are quantised and their two labels are locked mod $2$: a half-turn in the centre and a half-turn in the gauge direction must come together. The intrinsic group is therefore $(\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$, and the kernel fixes which theta angles are available. This is the part's one dynamical-looking constraint that is genuinely the algebra's: it comes from the topology of $\mathrm{U}(2)$, not from an added assumption.

Two readings, and the corpus chooses neither. That the central extension is the whole extension, which makes hypercharge the central phase and weak isospin the module action; or that the central phase is the trace of a local structure the algebra does not contain. The article states both and says what the first cannot deliver: a central phase multiplies both values alike, so reading A cannot give two charges.

Read on the representations, and checked against the table. The lock is a statement about the group, so it can be turned round: a multiplet of spin $j$ carries an integer central charge $q$ only with $q\equiv 2j\pmod 2$, and the product group imposes no such parity. The framework's compact group is thus a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ quotient of the electroweak pair and not the pair, which is the group-level content of the dimension match $\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$. Taken at the least integral step $q=6Y$, the seven rows of the hypercharge table all satisfy the lock, and on that table the lock is exactly the statement that electric charge is quantised in thirds — so the quotient is met by the observed content and is not a constraint that excludes it. It fixes parity and not scale: the values remain the anomaly solution, and the two couplings remain free, so the mixing angle stays a ratio of two free parameters.

6. The Breaking: Potential, Masses, $\rho$

The abelian mechanism, which works, and the non-abelian one, which does not. The framework's scalar is $\tilde\Xi=\xi e_t\in\mathbb{B}_{C}$, central, and the connection coefficient is central too, so left and right multiplication agree and the covariant derivative stays on $e_t$:

$$ D_{\imath}\tilde{\Xi}\Big|_{\langle\tilde{\Xi}\rangle}=\frac{2\pi i\,ϙ}{h}\,A_{\imath}\,\langle\tilde{\Xi}\rangle, \qquad V(\xi^{*}\xi)=\beta\Bigl(\xi^{*}\xi-\frac{v^{2}}{2}\Bigr)^{2},\qquad |\xi|=\frac{v}{\sqrt2}, \qquad \boxed{\;m_h^{2}=2\beta v^{2},\qquad M_A^2=\frac{(2\pi)^{2} ϙ^2v^2}{h^{2}}\;} $$

The non-trivial claim of the abelian case. Three things make the derivation work and each is a trap avoided: the connection is central, so no ordering ambiguity arises; the mass comes from the kinetic term and not from the potential; and the would-be Goldstone is eaten, so the count $4-1=3$ is the massive vector. A central scalar cannot do the non-abelian case, because a central charge acts identically on both parts of a doublet-shaped object, and that is §2's scalar row stated dynamically.

The doublet sector the part imports and computes.

$$ V(\varphi)=-\mu^2\,\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi+\lambda\left(\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi\right)^2 , \qquad M_W=\frac{g\,v}{2},\qquad M_Z=\frac{\sqrt{g^2+g_{\mathrm Y}^2}\,v}{2},\qquad M_\gamma=0, $$

$$ \langle H\rangle=\frac{v}{\sqrt2}I_2 \qquad\text{and in the same sector}\qquad m_h^2=2\lambda v^2 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The condensate proportional to the identity is what makes the breaking custodial: the potential depends only on $\mathrm{tr}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)$, so it has an $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ larger than the gauged one, and the vacuum breaks it to the diagonal $SU(2)_V$. The tree-level $\rho$ is then exactly one, and $M_{W^1}=M_{W^2}$ is the residual $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ and not an arithmetic accident.

Two checkable numbers. The sector has five measured quantities and four tree-level relations, so one combination is a genuine test; it passes at tree level to the accuracy of the one-loop correction. Route A — measured $g$, $g_Y$, $v$ — gives $80.24$ and $91.51$ GeV and is a consistency check on the relations, not a pure tree statement.

The non-trivial claim. The custodial action is the algebra's left–right multiplication, so the framework supplies the action on the real-quaternion space rather than borrowing it: given a quaternion-valued scalar, the $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$, its diagonal and its central-invariant vacuum are all present. 8. The Precision Sector: Three Parameters, Three Numbers

The count is the claim.

$$ \boxed{\;8-2-3=3\;} $$

$$ S=\frac{4s_W^2c_W^2}{\alpha}\left[\Pi'_{ZZ}(0)-\frac{c_W^2-s_W^2}{s_Wc_W}\Pi'_{Z\gamma}(0)-\Pi'_{\gamma\gamma}(0)\right], $$

The non-trivial claim. Three and not more, because the vacuum polarisation has more freedom than the three inputs fix, and the freedom is three-dimensional: eight transverse components, less two from the photon's gauge invariance, less three from the three inputs. Each parameter measures one thing — $S$ the isospin-breaking slope, $T$ the custodial-breaking value (that is, $\rho$ written as a deviation), $U$ the custodial-breaking slope. The fit is over-determined: some twenty quantities against three parameters, which is why it can fail and why the fact that it does not is a test.

The prediction. The fit put the Higgs in a window, and it was found at $125$ GeV inside it. This is the one place in the sector where a precision fit predicted a mass before discovery, and the prediction assumed the Standard Model content, the oblique truncation and the measured top mass. It does not generalise: a new sector enters with three unknown parameters, so what the fit supplies there is a region in the $S$–$T$ plane.

What the algebra contributes and does not. It gives the reason three combinations survive; it does not give their values, which are set by the particle content, and the particle content is the row marked no route yet. Two of the three parameters were used earlier in the part — $\rho=1$ at tree level, and $\rho-1\approx\alpha T$ — and this article is where $T$ is finally defined.

9. Anomalies, the Measure and the Fujikawa Route

Two routes, two explanations, one coefficient. The diagrammatic route says what the anomaly is; the measure route says why it is unavoidable.

$$ \mathcal D\tilde\Xi\mapsto\mathcal D\tilde\Xi\,\exp\Bigl(i\int\alpha\,\mathcal A\Bigr), \qquad a(x)=-\frac{ϙ^2}{32\pi^2}\,\epsilon_{{\imath}{\jmath}\rho\sigma}F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}F_{\rho\sigma}, $$

$$ \tfrac14\sum_{{\imath},{\jmath},\rho,\sigma}\epsilon_{{\imath}{\jmath}\rho\sigma}F_{{\imath}{\jmath}}F_{\rho\sigma} =\frac{2i}{c}\,I_2\quad(\text{Lorentzian}),\qquad I_2=\vec E\cdot\vec B . $$

The non-trivial claim. The coefficient is transcribed, not computed from an algebra trace. The reason is structural and worth memorising: the four-index symbol needs four independent directions a trace can see, and in the algebra the product of four distinct units is $\pm e_t$ or $\pm\vec e_k$ and never a multiple of the pseudoscalar $\omega$, so the chirality-weighted trace is blind to it. What the algebra does supply is the identity of the insertion — the axial rotation is the pseudoscalar, and $\omega^2=-e_t$ is the statement that the orientation is a square root of minus the identity — and the null-cone structure, where the left action on the six bivector components has rank four and a two-dimensional kernel on the null cone with $f^2=0$ throughout ($5$ null wave vectors, residual below $2\times10^{-14}$).

What cancellation does and does not do. Anomaly cancellation constrains the hypercharges, the colours and the weak representations; it does not fix them uniquely, does not fix the normalisation, and says nothing about singlets. The corpus's own gauge structure, being vector-like, is anomaly-free automatically — which is the same fact as §4's letter law seen from the other side. The coefficient is exact by Adler–Bardeen, and the modern reason is that the integrated anomaly is an index, an integer a continuous renormalisation cannot move.

10. The Neutrino: Conjugation, Majorana, Seesaw

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde\Xi^{c}=\mathcal C\tilde\Xi=i\,\bar{\tilde\Xi},\qquad \mathcal C^{2}\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi\;} \qquad \mathcal{C}(\tilde{\Xi})=\bar{\tilde{\Xi}}\,\vec e_z,\qquad \xi^{2}=1, $$

$$ \boxed{\;m_\nu\simeq\frac{m_D^{2}}{M_R}\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The charge-conjugation operator squares to $+1$, so the Majorana condition is a real condition with real solutions, and the neutrino can be its own antiparticle. The choice of conjugation is forced and not free: among the three antilinear involutions and one linear one, the coefficient bar is the only conjugate-linear automorphism, hence the only one that commutes with left multiplication, and a real structure must commute with the action it defines. The mass is not the same object on the two routes: the Dirac mass is the linear pairing of the two values, the Majorana mass is the antilinear one, and the algebra supplies both carriers without choosing.

The seesaw, stated as what the algebra does and does not do. It distinguishes the two masses, supplies the carrier of each, and does not select the heavy scale, forbid or require a heavy singlet, or relate $m_D$ to the electroweak scale. The ratio is stable against radiative corrections — chiral symmetry keeps $m_D$ small, $M_R$ renormalises multiplicatively — so the smallness is not the fine-tuning the Higgs mass is. Below $M_R$ the whole effect is the dimension-five operator, and oscillation data fix the two differences and the angles but not the absolute scale.

A trap. The Majorana reality condition is not propagated as a flow: none of the sixteen unit-conjugated forms commutes with the equation, so the fixed space is not preserved, and a field equal to its conjugate at one time need not stay so.

11. Flavour: Masses as Invariants, Mixing as Misalignment

$$ M_f=\frac{v}{\sqrt2}Y^{f},\qquad M=WDV^{\dagger},\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(m_1,m_2,m_3), \qquad \boxed{\;V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=W_u^{\dagger}W_d\;} $$

$$ M_u=W_u\,D_u\,V_u^{\dagger},\qquad M_d=W_d\,D_d\,V_d^{\dagger}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{W}\supset\frac{g}{\sqrt2}W^+_{\imath}\sum_{a,b}V_{ab}\,\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm u,a}^{\dagger}\,i\bigl(i\,e_{\imath}\,\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm d,b,L}\,\vec e_{{\jmath}({\imath})}\bigr)\vec e_x+\text{h.c.} $$

The non-trivial claim. Masses are the singular values of a mass matrix and mixing is the misalignment of the left singular bases; nothing else is physical. The decomposition is not a convenience: the kinetic terms are invariant under independent unitary rotations of each multiplet, so only a bi-unitary diagonalisation preserves them, and the singular-value decomposition is therefore forced. Thirteen is then a count: $54$ real parameters in the Yukawa sector, $41$ removed by flavour-basis freedom, thirteen left — nine masses, three angles, one phase. Each $3\times3$ complex matrix has exactly three bi-unitary invariants, which is why the nine masses are free.

Why CP violation is where it is. In this framework the gauge interactions are generation-universal — the unit on the label times the algebra's generator — so mixing can only come from the mass operators, which multiply by a number on the label and leave the element alone and are not diagonal in the same basis. The invariant is the commutator of $H_u=M_uM_u^{\dagger}$ and $H_d=M_dM_d^{\dagger}$: if they commuted, the mixing would be a permutation of phases and there would be no CP violation. The Jarlskog determinant is the invariant of the two mass matrices, not of $V$ alone, and its smallness ($\lambda^6$) is a consequence of the hierarchy, not a tuned phase.

The lepton sector is the same algebra with two new questions. The PMNS matrix is $W_{\jmath}^{\dagger}W_e$; its four parameters are coordinates on $\mathrm{U}(3)/\mathrm{U}(1)^5$, of dimension $9-5=4$. The difference is that the neutrino masses are seven orders below the electron and the mixing is large instead of hierarchical. Meson mixing and the unitarity triangle are the closure test: five experimental techniques spanning tree and loop satisfy the unitarity relations of a $3\times3$ unitary matrix, and unitarity being a property of the group, the test has no adjustable content. GIM is also a framework-level statement: $VV^{\dagger}=1$ is the definition of a basis change on the label, so the constant term in the loop vanishes and the surviving term measures the non-centrality of the mass operator.

What a derivation would need. A scalar in the module rather than in the centre, so that it carries a non-central charge; a hypercharge assignment acting differently on the two chiralities; a potential and a Yukawa term invariant under both. The first is available, the second is §3's open item, the third is §2's.

Summary

What the part refuses. Weak isospin as an internal group; hypercharge from the algebra; a chiral scalar; a non-abelian matter representation. Each is refused by a theorem, and each refusal is a result.

The part in six formulas.

$$ \exp(\mathbb B_\Phi)=\mathrm{U}(2),\qquad L=\pi i m e_t+\pi n\vec U,\ m\equiv n\pmod 2,\qquad \varkappa_L=\varkappa_R\ \text{(mass invariant)}, $$

$$ m_h^2=2\beta v^2,\qquad M_W=\frac{gv}{2},\qquad V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=W_u^{\dagger}W_d . $$

Five results to keep. Representation is a function of spin, so the scalar cannot be a doublet, the fermion cannot be chiral and the vector matches. The letter law makes the framework's $\mathrm{U}(1)$ vector-like and the chiral mass term non-invariant. The central phase is quantised with the two labels locked mod $2$, which is the part's one dynamical constraint from the algebra. The abelian Higgs mechanism works and is the only one available, because the scalar is central. Masses are invariants and mixing is a misalignment; thirteen numbers is a count, not a list.

The five actions, one verdict.

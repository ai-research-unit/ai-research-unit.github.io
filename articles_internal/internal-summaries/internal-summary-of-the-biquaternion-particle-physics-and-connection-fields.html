Internal Summary of the Biquaternion Universe Particle Physics

Introduction

What this file is. A self-contained map of the whole Biquaternion Universe Particle Physics category: ninety-three articles in six parts, counted from the anchors of physics.md at the time of writing. It stands on its own. It is deliberately less detailed than a dedicated part summary: one paragraph, one or two formulas and a verdict per part. Two internal notes go deeper, and they are an optional extension, never a requirement: internal-summary-of-the-electroweak-interaction (thirty-three articles) and internal-summary-of-the-strong-interaction (twenty-four).

What the category does. It develops four items: the abelian minimal-coupling principle on the central phase; the compact factor $\mathrm{SU}(2)\subset\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ with its full non-abelian machinery; the exact chiral structure on the element; and the classification of particles by spin and mass. It imports the Standard Model. The category's subject is the boundary between the two, and the boundary is a theorem.

The single structural fact.

$$ \boxed{\;\text{the symmetry group \textit{is} the spin group}\;} \qquad \boxed{\;\dim_\mathbb{R}\bigl(\text{compact subalgebra of }\mathbb{B}\bigr)\le4,\qquad \text{every }\mathbb{B}\text{-module has even complex dimension}\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The first says what a field may carry; the second says what group may fit. The first forbids choosing a representation independently of the spin, so the scalar cannot be a doublet and the fermion cannot be chiral. The second forbids an internal $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ of dimension eight inside a compact algebra of dimension four, so colour is absent. Three of the category's four obstruction lists reduce to the first theorem; the fourth is the second.

The letter, after the migration. The group keeps its name, $SU(2)$, and the biquaternion statement is that $SU(2)$ is the spin group of the spatial rotations: $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{Spin}(3)=S^3$, the unit real quaternions. That is an identity of groups and not an embedding, and it is what lets an article write the rotor and the algebra instead of a matrix display. The convention is fixed once in articles_physics/representation-is-a-function-of-spin.md, §The Rotor Conventions. Four things follow that the summaries in this file use without repeating:

  • $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts on the algebra by four different operators — $\mathrm{Sd}$, the Hermitian pair; $\operatorname{Ad}$, the conjugation; $\operatorname{ad}$, the commutator; and $L$, the left multiplication — and a connection field gets the last one, which is why the carrier is a minimal left ideal and why the action is one-sided.
  • The symmetry group of the electroweak pair is the quotient $(\mathrm{SU}(2)\times S^1)/\{\pm e_t\}$ and not the product $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times S^1$. The two factors share the two points $\pm e_t$, and the sharing is what produces the parity lock $q\equiv2j\pmod 2$.
  • $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ has three carriers and no more: the trivial one of dimension $1$, the spinor one of dimension $2$, and the adjoint of dimension $3$. That census is why the spin content of the category is what it is.
  • Representation is a function of spin because a module over $\mathbb{B}$ has a spin and no independent symmetry label. That single sentence is the chiral wall, the reason the scalar is a central singlet, and the reason the group is imported as a name and not as a choice.

The block that fixes the vocabulary is in articles_physics/representation-is-a-function-of-spin.md, §The Rotor Conventions, and the twenty-eight migrated articles cite it rather than restating it.

The one-sentence version. The category reaches the kinematics, the algebra, the self-couplings and the abelian scalar mechanism of the Standard Model; it imports the group, the quantum numbers, the couplings, the angle and every mass; and it records the difference rather than blurring it.

What is established. The compact algebra is $\mathrm{U}(2)$ of dimension four, and the ceiling is proved twice. The group-valued chiral field satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation, and the biquaternion norm is the invariant metric on it. The symmetry group on a material element is the spin group, with a table that reads the representation from the carrier. The abelian Higgs mechanism works, with $m_A^2=ϙ^2v^2/\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}$ and $m_h^2=2\beta v^2$ from one potential. The abelian Pontryagin density is the imaginary part of the biquaternion norm, so the Maxwell instanton is a framework object. The chirality-odd bilinear vanishes on an element of a single value, which is why a condensate needs both letters. The Peccei–Quinn symmetry is the central phase, and the axion potential is the cosine it forces.

2. The Electroweak Interaction

What the part is. Thirty-three articles, cut by spin: generalities, then spin $0$, spin $\tfrac12$, spin $1$. Two ceilings, one embedding, one wall, and the computations that sit on top of the imported model.

$$ \boxed{\;\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathbb{R}(ie_t)\oplus\mathrm{SU}(2),\qquad \dim_\mathbb{R}\mathrm{U}(2)=4\;} \qquad L=\pi i m\,e_t+\pi n\vec U,\quad m\equiv n\pmod2 . $$

The three results. Representation is a function of spin. The table — spin $0$ trivial, spin $\tfrac12$ spinor with both letters alike, spin $1$ adjoint — matches the model in exactly one row, the vector one; that is why the vector-boson kinematics and self-couplings come out right and the matter sector does not. The letter law. A left multiplication applies one element to both parts, so independent charges need the right slot and an element of unequal values; a central multiplier gives one charge. Its consequence is the mass selection rule $\varkappa_L=\varkappa_R$, which the whole flavour sector runs on. The abelian mechanism. Central scalar, central connection, mass from the kinetic term, Goldstone eaten: $V=\beta(\xi^*\xi-v^2/2)^2$ gives both $m_h^2=2\beta v^2$ and $m_A^2=ϙ^2v^2/\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}$. The non-abelian version is unavailable for the same reason the scalar row is trivial.

What is computed on top. The hypercharges are the solution of four anomaly conditions — a rank-three linear system on five unknowns, cut by the cubic to three rays, of which measurement selects one. The doublet sector gives $M_W=gv/2$, $M_Z=\sqrt{g^2+g_Y^2}\,v/2$ and $\rho=1$ at tree level. The precision sector has $8-2-3=3$ oblique parameters against some twenty measurements, and the fit predicted the Higgs mass. Flavour is one statement seen twice: masses are singular values $M_f=WDV^{\dagger}$ and mixing is the misalignment $V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=W_u^{\dagger}W_d$, thirteen numbers being a count and not a list. Neutrinos add $\mathcal{C}\tilde\Xi=i\bar{\tilde\Xi}$ with $\mathcal{C}^2=+1$, the Majorana condition as a real condition, and $m_\nu\simeq m_D^2/M_R$.

The five actions. Five of the thirty-three write the Lagrangian: the whole sector in both phases, the doublet alone, the $W$ and $Z$, the weak contact, and the Stueckelberg alternative. Their verdict is one verdict. The action is written in the component dictionary because the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ line is exact in the algebra and the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ lines are only partly carried; the mass relation $m_W=gv/2$, the count eight-plus-four to nine-plus-two-plus-one and the surviving current $Q=T^3+Y$ follow from the variation, while $v$, the number of scalars and every quantised structure stay outside.

The letter, and the migration that fixed it. The part was the first to be migrated, and the migration is written up in SU2-migration. The group is $SU(2)$, it keeps its name in every article, and the sentence that follows it says what it is in the algebra: $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{Spin}(3)=S^3$, the unit real quaternions, acting on a vector by $\tilde u\vec v\tilde u^{*}$. The condition the author set is that the equivalence be explained and used, and the part meets it in both halves: the name in $28$ of the $28$ in-scope articles, the identification in the same sentence in $28$, the rotor then used in $15$. The other $13$ compute on the inherited model — mixing matrices, coefficients, counts — where no rotor acts, and each carries the explanation without pretending to a use it does not have.

What the migration changed, and what it did not. Three maths articles cited by this part were renamed, because their titles carried a name that had no source in the corpus: The Spin Group via $4\times4$ Real Matrices, From the Spin Group to the Spin of a Single Rotor, and The Clebsch–Gordan Decomposition of the Biquaternion Algebra. No verdict in this part moved. The five Lagrangian articles of the part were left to their own job.

The verdict. The sector's kinematics, algebra, self-couplings and abelian scalar mechanism are the framework's; its group, quantum numbers, angle and masses are not. The shape of the vertex is algebraic; the strength and the asymmetry are inputs.

3. The Strong Interaction

What the part is. Twenty-four articles, and the category's negative part: here the question has a theorem for an answer. Everything the algebra's own $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ can carry is built; the rest of QCD is transcribed with the boundary marked on every page.

$$ \boxed{\;\dim_\mathbb{R}\mathrm{SU}(3)=8,\qquad \dim_\mathbb{R}\mathrm{U}(2)=4,\qquad Ϙ=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int\mathrm{Tr}(F\wedge F)\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad S\ge\frac{8\pi^2}{g^2}|Ϙ|\;} $$

The theorem and its two prices. $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is excluded twice: the compact subalgebra has dimension four against eight, and every module has even complex dimension against the odd triplet — the second count no change of basis can touch. Two routes pass the wall and both are priced: the enlarged carrier $M_2(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$ with intrinsic group $\mathrm{U}(4)\supset\mathrm{SU}(3)$, where the algebra acts only on the spectator factor; and the octonion route, $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{O})=G_2$ of dimension fourteen, at the price of associativity.

What survives. BPST instantons inside the compact factor. The ADHM data as quaternionic, with the moduli dimension from the hyperkähler quotient. The chiral field $\tilde U=\exp(\vec\pi/f_\pi)$ satisfying Maurer–Cartan, the pion as the adjoint with one normalisation shared with the custodial triplet, and Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner, $m_\pi^2f_\pi^2=-(m_u+m_d)\langle\bar qq\rangle$. The Skyrme winding, conserved identically, identified with baryon number. Peccei–Quinn as the central phase, with $V=\Lambda^4[1-\cos(\bar\theta+a/f_a)]$ and $m_a=\Lambda^2/f_a$.

What is transcribed. The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ representation theory and Gell-Mann–Okubo, $m=m_0+\alpha+\beta Y+\gamma(I(I+1)-\tfrac14Y^2)$, whose equal-spacing check comes out $2256.9$ against $2270.1$ MeV. The QCD Lagrangian, its four pieces and four vertices, with $C_A=3$, $C_F=\tfrac43$, $T_R=\tfrac12$. The running $\alpha_s=2\pi/(b_0\ln(\mu/\Lambda))$ and the conformal anomaly that gives the proton its mass. The parton densities, which are functions imported rather than numbers. The jet cross sections, whose individual terms diverge and whose sum does not.

The three actions. The chiral sector and the CP-odd sector have Lagrangian articles — the sigma model, the Skyrme model, the axion — and each answers the same four questions. Sigma model and Skyrme pair with the real scalar part, and the pairing is forced: the current is anti-Hermitian, so $\mathrm{Sc}(\bar j_{\imath} j_{\imath})=-\mathrm{Sc}(j_{\imath}^{\dagger}j_{\imath})$ is the negative of a Hermitian norm and the integrand is real with no condition imposed. Skyrme's second term must be a commutator because the current is flat on $\mathbb{H}^1$, and the action-level stabiliser is the bound $E\ge6\pi^2(f/e)|N|$. The axion's potential must be periodic, and periodicity is consistent with the anomaly because $Ϙ\in\mathbb{Z}$, so a shift moves the action by $2\pi\mathbb{Z}$. What none of the three buys is a coefficient: $f_\pi$, $e$, $f_a$ and $\Lambda^4$ are imported, and the articles say so under what reality does not buy.

The verdict. Colour is not missing by oversight; it is excluded by a theorem with a price list attached. The part has actions after all, three of them, and not one carries a colour label: the objects are the pion, the soliton and the axion, all built on the group-valued field that the algebra already has. That is a result, and the part's worth is that it states it once and then refuses to soften it.

4. The Standard Model as a Whole

What the part is. Nine articles that close the loop, opened by the whole-model audit that the three sector audits are cut from: the three representative factors with their status, then the content with its quantum numbers, the four sectors in one Lagrangian, and the count of nineteen parameters — three couplings, one angle, one vacuum value, the Higgs quartic, nine masses, four mixing parameters.

$$ 3\ \text{couplings}+1\ \text{angle}+1\ \text{value}+1\ \text{quartic}+9\ \text{masses}+4\ \text{mixing}=19 . $$

The reading the part makes. The parameter count is the measure of the boundary: a number the framework cannot produce is a number it imports, and nineteen of them is the size of the import. That is the part's one original idea, and it is a good one, because it turns a vague "the framework is incomplete" into a number.

Three problems stated and not solved. Naturalness: the Higgs mass is quadratically sensitive, and no framework object suppresses the correction. Triviality and the Landau pole: the quartic and the hypercharge coupling both grow, so the theory is an effective one with a cut-off. Dark matter, baryogenesis and the sphaleron: three items the model does not explain, with the sphaleron's baryon-number violation the one place where the model's own non-perturbative structure is required. Grand unification fails on the same ceiling: the standard groups do not fit in a larger simple group the algebra can carry.

The verdict. The part is bookkeeping with one sharp idea — the parameter count as a measurement of how much is imported — and it is honest about being bookkeeping. Its head article is the exception in kind and not in honesty: the whole-model audit states each representative factor, the matter sector and the scalar sector with its source and its status, sorts every item into established, known route and no route yet, and refuses the three inferences that would turn the framework into a Standard Model. That classification is the point the sector audits share, and it is why the audit sits in this part rather than in the electroweak one, where it was first filed: two of its three representative factors are about colour and about the model's content, not about the weak interaction.

The hub. The ninth article is one page and four answers to one question: does the Lagrangian family have an action for this sector, and does the algebra carry the object that action needs? Two independent columns, and the four rows differ in both. Electromagnetic: an action and an exact $\mathrm{U}(1)$ connection. Electroweak: an action, one factor exact and one partial. Gravitational: an action whose exact carrier is a frame and not an element. Strong: neither, inside this algebra. The price of reading one column for the other is a boundary of the method taken for a boundary of the algebra, or the reverse.

Eight traps, collected here because this is the file most likely to be read alone.

  • Mixed writing of the anomaly conditions. The minus-sign form applied to a conjugate hypercharge gives $\tfrac23$ instead of $0$.
  • Promoting a prediction of the inherited model to a derivation. The oblique fit's $125$ GeV, the hypercharge solution, the doublet potential and the flavour counts are computations about the Standard Model's content, not the framework's.
  • Reading the $\mathrm{U}(4)$ embedding as a refutation of the ceiling. The theorem is about the group of $\mathbb{B}$, not of $M_n(\mathbb{B})$.
  • Assuming the biquaternion norm is locally invariant. It is not, for a non-abelian representative: $17.1838\to17.3970$ on a random sample, while the matrix trace is invariant to machine precision.
  • Using the reduction as the connection pairing. $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$ is invariant under $\tilde Q_1\mapsto\tilde Q\tilde Q_1\tilde Q^{-1}$, $\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q\tilde Q_2\tilde Q^{-1}$ and $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$ is not, so a density built on the reduction is not a representative density. There is no single framework pairing: Dirac uses $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\Xi^{*}\dot{\tilde\Xi})$, the scalar the Hermitian norm, the contact the modulus of the current, the chiral field the real scalar part, the non-abelian connection field the trace.
  • Treating $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ multiplet bookkeeping as colour. The algebra's $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ sits inside $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as the isospin subgroup; that inclusion is not a derivation of the colour group.
  • Propagating the Majorana reality condition. None of the sixteen unit-conjugated forms commutes with the equation, so the fixed space is not preserved by the flow.
  • Reading "three" in the algebra as colour. The three letters are an adjoint, and an adjoint is not a fundamental.

Five results to keep. The compact algebra is four-dimensional and the ceiling is proved twice, by dimension and by module parity. The symmetry group on an element is the spin group, so the representation is read from the carrier and not chosen. The framework's own $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is the central phase and is vector-like, which forbids a chiral mass term and forces the letter law. The abelian Higgs mechanism and the abelian instanton are genuine framework constructions, and their non-abelian counterparts are not. Everything else — the group, the quantum numbers, nineteen parameters — is imported, and the category's worth is that it says so article by article.

One convention, and it is per sector. The sector actions do not share a pairing. The Dirac element pairs with $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\Xi^{*}\dot{\tilde\Xi})$, the complex scalar with the Hermitian norm, the weak contact with the modulus of the current, the real chiral field with the scalar part, the non-abelian connection field with the matrix trace. The trace is the exception that forces itself: the reduction $2\,\mathrm{Sc}$ has no cyclicity, so a density built on it is not invariant under the conjugation a local transformation performs.

What the category is for. Two things. It fixes, exactly and defensibly, what the biquaternion algebra can and cannot carry — and that is a result, not a failure. And it marks, on every page, which statements about the Standard Model are the framework's and which are the model's written in the framework's letters. A reader who keeps those two apart cannot be misled by the category; a reader who does not will mistake a transcription for a derivation.

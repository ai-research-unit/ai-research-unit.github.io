Extending the Corpus to Curved Spaces: The Corpus Ledger, the Chat's Three Slips, and the Open List

Introduction

What this file is. An internal reading of one chat transcript against the corpus. The transcript is chat-to-split: seventy kilobytes, sixty-two lines, twenty answers and one question, no headings and no formulas set as displays. Its subject is the extension of the framework to curved spacetime and to many qubits.

The verdict in one line. No corpus article needs correcting. Eight curved-space statements were recomputed for this file and all eight hold. The transcript restates five of them correctly, states three of them wrongly, and in its one new proposal links entanglement to curvature through a holonomy whose only effect on a two-qubit state is to rescale its normalisation, which is not entanglement loss.

What the corpus achieves on curved space, in one paragraph. The algebra carries any Lorentzian metric, as the Gram matrix of four material frame elements. The local symmetry is a copy of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ at each point, and the counting closes. The covariant derivative is two-sided, and the trap in writing it as a commutator is stated. The curved Dirac operator squares to the Laplace–Beltrami operator plus a quarter of the Ricci scalar, with the coefficient the Clifford derivation gives. The Newman–Penrose null tetrad is the pair of minimal idempotents and the pair of nilpotents, with no separate axiom, and the tetrad's multiplication is the matrix-unit multiplication. The framework's own local-scale proposal is a diagonal member of the frame route, and its optical reading agrees with Gordon's metric exactly. Curvature, on this reading, is the part of the frame that no rotor removes. The one boundary is precise: inside the one-function class, Ricci-flatness forces flatness. That is a measured wall, not a collapse.

Why the sorting is the work. A chat answer reads as authority and carries no ledger. Three of its sentences are indistinguishable, to a reader, from results of the corpus, and they are wrong. The value of this file is the attribution: what is the corpus's, what is the chat's, and what is nobody's yet.

The frame route is not a formality. Four material elements, sixteen functions, one Gram matrix. Where the four are independent the matrix is nondegenerate. Conversely, take a $g$-orthonormal frame of any Lorentzian metric, map it into $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ by a linear isometry of quadratic spaces, and read the coordinate components. Nothing is excluded. The price is stated in the corpus and it is the right price: the algebra contributes a four-dimensional real space with a form, and the metric itself is carried by the frame.

Why "unit norm" is the right condition. The local group is the set of rotors with $\langle\tilde U\rangle_{\natural}=1$, and this is not a convention. Congruence $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde U\tilde Q\tilde U^{*}$ multiplies the bilinear form by the fourth power of the scale: over three thousand random material pairs a unit-norm rotor preserved $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ to $2.5\times10^{-14}$, and a rescaling by a real factor $\rho$ multiplied it by $\rho^{4}$. So $\langle\tilde U\rangle_{\natural}=1$ is exactly the condition for congruence to be a symmetry of the interval, and the six parameters that remain are the sixteen minus the ten of $g_{\mu\nu}$.

One loose phrase in every summary of this construction, including the chat's, is worth fixing. It is often said that Sylvester's law guarantees the inertia $(3,1)$. Sylvester's law does not create an inertia; it preserves one. The inertia $(3,1)$ is the inertia of the ambient form $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ restricted to the material sector, where $Q_t=i q'_t$ turns the first $+1$ into $-1$. Sylvester then guarantees that no change of frame can move it. So the minus sign is not a consequence of a theorem about matrices; it is a consequence of the one substitution $Q_t=i q'_t$, and the theorem only makes it stable. That division of labour is worth stating, because the loose form hides where the Lorentzian signature comes from.

And one caution about what the construction buys. The frame route shows that the algebra can express any Lorentzian geometry. It does not select one. Every metric is carried, so no metric is predicted, and the kinematics is therefore compatible with general relativity rather than evidence for the framework. That is not a weakness of the route; it is the correct reading of an expressiveness result, and the corpus states it that way.

The two-sided derivative is established corpus content, and one identity explains all of it. The local group acts on a material element by congruence, $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde U\tilde Q\tilde U^{*}$, so the infinitesimal action of a generator $G$ is $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde Q+\epsilon(G\tilde Q+\tilde QG^{*})$. Everything then turns on the star eigenvalue of $G$:

$$ G^{*}=-G\;\Longrightarrow\;G\tilde Q+\tilde QG^{*}=[G,\tilde Q],\qquad G^{*}=+G\;\Longrightarrow\;G\tilde Q+\tilde QG^{*}=\{G,\tilde Q\}. $$

The three rotation generators are anti-Hermitian, $G^{*}=-G$, and the three boost generators are Hermitian, $G^{*}=+G$. So the rotation half of the connection is a commutator and the boost half is an anticommutator, and no single prescription covers both. The same split appears at group level. A rotation rotor has unit norm $\langle\tilde U,\tilde U\rangle_{\natural}=1$ and satisfies $\tilde U^{*}=\tilde U^{-1}$; a boost rotor has the same unit norm and satisfies $\tilde U^{*}=\tilde U$. I checked both at three rapidities. The commutator is therefore the derivative of the adjoint action alone, which coincides with congruence for rotations and only for rotations.

I recomputed both actions on $0.7i\,e_t+0.3\vec e_x-0.4\vec e_y+0.9\vec e_z$ with the generator $i\vec e_x$. The commutator returns $i(-1.8\vec e_y-0.8\vec e_z)$, an informational element: it does not merely misweight the boost, it leaves the material sector. The two-sided action equals the anticommutator and returns $-0.6i\,e_t-1.4\vec e_x$, which is material and is the boost:

$$ r'_t\longmapsto r'_t-2\epsilon\,r_x,\qquad r_x\longmapsto r_x-2\epsilon\,r'_t . $$

So the framework's own notation tells a writer where a textbook formula fails, and the failure shows up as a change of sector. That is an advantage of the algebra, and it is worth saying before any criticism of a transcript.

The local-scale proposal belongs to the frame route, and this is the good news. Take the four frame elements $E_0=i\,c(\vec r)\,e_t$ and $E_\eta=\vec e_\eta$. I computed the Gram matrix at $c=0.7$, $c=2.0$ and $c=3.3$ and found

$$ \langle E_0,E_0\rangle_{\natural}=-c^2,\qquad \langle E_0,E_\eta\rangle_{\natural}=0,\qquad \langle E_\eta,E_\eta\rangle_{\natural}=1 . $$

That is Reading B's interval, $-c^2d{t'}^2+dr_x^2+dr_y^2+dr_z^2$, written as a frame. So the framework's own proposal is a diagonal, spatially rigid member of the general construction. The corpus says exactly this, and reading the two routes as rivals is too strong: the metric classes are nested, and what differs is the device, not the reach. The frame has sixteen functions and carries anything; the local scale has one function and carries one family.

The null tetrad is the idempotent structure, and I recomputed it. With $\tilde\Pi_1=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z)$ and $\tilde\Pi_2=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z)$ I found $\tilde\Pi_1^2=\tilde\Pi_1$, $\tilde\Pi_2^2=\tilde\Pi_2$ and $\tilde\Pi_1\tilde\Pi_2=0$. With $\vec\Upsilon_1=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y)$ and $\vec\Upsilon_2=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y)$ both squares vanish. The tetrad then satisfies $\langle l,n\rangle_{*}=-1$ and $\langle m,\bar m\rangle_{*}=+1$, and the null relations follow. Null directions, the light cone, the quantum vacuum and the classical tetrad are one object. This is a unification inside the algebra, and no Clifford algebra is needed to state it.

The boundary is a measurement, not a defeat. Within the one-function class the corpus derives $\mathrm{Ric}_{00}=u\Delta_\Phi u$ and $\mathrm{Ric}_{ij}=-\partial_i\partial_j u/u$. Setting the spatial equations to zero makes $u$ affine in space, and I computed the full Riemann tensor for $u=\vec a(t)\cdot\vec r+b(t)$ with arbitrary functions and found every component zero. For $u=2+\sin x$ I found scalar curvature $2\sin x/(2+\sin x)$, which is $+\tfrac23$ at $x=\pi/2$ and vanishes at $x=0$ and $x=\pi$, and I recovered every component of the Ricci tensor from the corpus's closed form. So the class is curved in general and flat whenever it is vacuum: it cannot hold a vacuum wave or a vacuum exterior. A one-function class with one free direction falls short of a theory with two free metric functions, and the corpus states the shortfall rather than hiding it. That is a result.

Where Curved Space Actually Enters: The Pipeline, in Order

The implementation already exists, and it is worth stating as a sequence, because every summary of it stops after step five and calls the remainder mysterious.

  1. Four frame fields. $\tilde E_{\Phi,0},\dots,\tilde E_{\Phi,3}$, one material element per coordinate direction, sixteen functions of the point.
  2. One Gram matrix. $g_{\mu\nu}=\langle\tilde E_{\Phi,\mu},\tilde E_{\Phi,\nu}\rangle_{\natural}$, ten components after the symmetry.
  3. One local group. Congruence by a unit element, six parameters, so $16-6=10$ and the count closes on the metric.
  4. One connection. $\vec\Gamma_\mu$ in the six-dimensional scalar-free subspace, entering the derivative on both sides.
  5. One square. $(\tilde F_\mu D_\mu)^{2}=-\Box_{g,\Phi}+\tfrac14\mathrm{Ric}$, which puts curvature inside the wave operator.
  6. One measure. $\sqrt{\lvert\det g\rvert}$ is the determinant of the Gram matrix, so it is available at no extra cost.
  7. Nothing at step seven. There is no action, so the sequence stops at a kinematics.

I verified step two's bilinear: for arbitrary material $A$ and $B$, $\langle A,B\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu A_\mu B_\mu$, which is manifestly symmetric, and at $E_0=ie_t$ it returns $\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. The construction is not delicate. What is missing is a functional, and the next two paragraphs are the only concrete proposals this file offers.

Proposal one: the action is already sitting inside the square. From step five, $\mathrm{Ric}=4\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl[(\tilde F_\mu D_\mu)^{2}+\Box_{g,\Phi}\bigr]$, up to the boundary term that $\int\sqrt{\lvert\det g\rvert}\,\Box$ contributes. So the Einstein–Hilbert integrand is not an import; it is a rearrangement of an identity the corpus already proves, with the coefficient $\tfrac14$ fixing the normalisation. The candidate is

$$ S=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{\lvert\det g\rvert}\;\mathrm{Sc}\Bigl[(\tilde F_\mu D_\mu)^{2}\Bigr], $$

and both factors exist: the pointwise scalar from the algebra, the measure from the Gram determinant. Two cautions must be stated with it. The action reproduces general relativity and nothing more, so it will not distinguish the framework from general relativity, and its value is that it closes the sequence rather than that it predicts. And the trace it uses is the pointwise one multiplied by the manifold measure, not the pointwise trace playing the part of an integral; the two operations stay distinct, which is exactly the discipline the rest of this file asks for.

Proposal two: the source has the same species as the metric. The obstacle is usually stated as a type mismatch, four-vectors on one side and a symmetric rank-two object on the other. The bilinear above says otherwise. $\langle A,B\rangle_{\natural}$ is symmetric, so it produces symmetric rank-two objects, and $g_{\mu\nu}$ is one of them. A dust source is the same construction applied to a different frame, $T_{\mu\nu}=\rho\,\langle\tilde U_\mu,\tilde U_\nu\rangle_{\natural}$ with $\tilde U_\mu$ the dust's own frame. The coupling is therefore not a mismatch of type but a question of which frame carries the energy, and the case of a fluid with pressure is the case where the resulting form is not a Gram matrix with a fixed sign and needs the same Sylvester analysis applied to the source. That is a programme with a definite first computation, not a lament.

What neither proposal does. Neither supplies a selection principle for the frame, and neither touches the informational sector. The gravitational sector and the quantum sector remain joined only at the Dirac square, and the appendix below says which article would have to close that gap.

The Three Slips

First, the tetrad does not make the complex structure local. The transcript says the tetrad "twists the local complex structure from point to point" and that the spin connection "dynamically adjusts where the factor of $i$ falls". In this framework the frame field is a real linear map. The imaginary direction stays the coefficient of $e_t$ in a fixed basis. My rotor-gauge check makes the point concrete: for $\tilde E_{\Phi,\mu}=\tilde Uu_\mu\tilde U^{*}$ with $\tilde U=\cos\theta+\sin\theta\,\vec e_x$ and $\theta=0.8x$, I found the Gram matrix constant and equal to $\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ at $x=0$ and at $x=1.3$. A point-dependent rotor moves no $i$ and produces no curvature. Curvature is the part of the frame that is not pure rotor gauge.

Second, the transcript's commutator is the rotation half of a two-sided derivative and nothing more. The recomputation above fixes the size of the error. For the rotation generator $\vec e_x$ the two forms agree exactly; for the boost generator $i\vec e_x$ they do not, and the commutator sends a material element into $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$. A connection written with commutators alone therefore carries the three rotations and loses the three boosts, which is to say it loses the Lorentzian content. The two-sided form is not a repair the framework has to argue for: it is the established derivative, and the commutator is the special case that coincides with it on the compact half of the group. In the corpus's own terms, rotations commute and boosts anticommute, and a formula with one sign cannot serve both.

Third, the projected derivative in the transcript is malformed. Its display contracts the tetrad index with itself while raising the manifold index, so the two labels cannot be separated and nothing follows from it as written. The defect is small and diagnostic: it shows a textbook formula copied rather than an algebra read, and the same slot confusion is what hides the two-sided action.

None of the three is a corpus error. Each is a slip in translating a corpus result into the language of tetrad gravity, and in two cases the translation reverses a sign of the algebra.

The Point the Transcript Gets Right

The diagnosis of the imaginary time coordinate is fair. The transcript calls $Q_t=i q'_t$ an $ict$-style convention and says it does not generalise cleanly to curved spacetime. The corpus records the same limitation in its own words: the convention is tied to the existence of a global inertial frame, and the algebra is fixed while its embedding into physical spacetime is not.

Two things must be said in the framework's favour, because the transcript's phrasing invites the opposite conclusion.

The convention is not a rotation of a metric. There is one metric, $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$, with four coefficients $+1$. The minus of the interval is $i^2=-1$ acting on the time coefficient. The corpus refuses the word signature for that reason; what a form has is an inertia, here $(3,1)$.

The repair is smaller than the transcript claims. The transcript's conclusion is that the fix is a tetrad localising the complex structure; the fix is a tetrad localising the frame and leaving the complex structure global. That is the construction the corpus has already built, and the local scale is its diagonal member.

The Null Tetrad Is the Peirce Basis, Not Merely Built From It

The result is stronger than "the tetrad can be constructed". The four Newman–Penrose vectors are the four Peirce corners of the biquaternion algebra at a real axis, scaled by one factor. The maths corpus proves this without knowing it is physics: The Companion Nilpotents of the Biquaternion Algebra derives the corner decomposition, the square-zero off-diagonal corners, and the multiplication table of the four matrix units, and calls the result "the biquaternion algebra is a full matrix algebra". Translate the four corners into $l$, $n$, $m$, $\bar m$ and you have the tetrad. I recomputed every relation below.

The construction, in the corpus's own notation. At the axis $\vec e_z$, with $\vec e_x,\vec e_y$ completing it to a right-handed triple,

$$ \tilde\Pi_+=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z),\qquad \tilde\Pi_-=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z),\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{+-}=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y),\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y). $$

These four are the matrix units of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ in the Pauli representation, and they satisfy

$$ \tilde\Pi_\pm^{2}=\tilde\Pi_\pm,\qquad \tilde\Pi_+\tilde\Pi_-=0,\qquad \tilde\Pi_++\tilde\Pi_-=e_t,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{+-}^{2}=\vec\Upsilon_{-+}^{2}=0,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{+-}\vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tilde\Pi_+,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{-+}\vec\Upsilon_{+-}=\tilde\Pi_- . $$

The last two are the corpus's, and they are the ones a physics reader should notice: the product of the two nilpotents is the idempotent. The transverse pair multiplies into the longitudinal direction.

The tetrad, and the factor the chat gets wrong. With $l=\sqrt2 i\tilde\Pi_+$, $n=\sqrt2 i\tilde\Pi_-$, $m=\sqrt2 i\vec\Upsilon_{+-}$, $\bar m=\sqrt2 i\vec\Upsilon_{-+}$, the symmetric bilinear form $\langle A,B\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu A_\mu B_\mu$ gives

$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ $l$ $n$ $m$ $\bar m$
$l$ $0$ $-1$ $0$ $0$
$n$ $-1$ $0$ $0$ $0$
$m$ $0$ $0$ $0$ $+1$
$\bar m$ $0$ $0$ $+1$ $0$

which is the Newman–Penrose metric in a null basis: four null vectors, $l\cdot n=-1$, $m\cdot\bar m=+1$, and every other product zero. I verified every entry.

Two slips in the chat's reconstruction, and both are checkable. The scale: $\sqrt2\,i$, not $2i$. The multiplication table is homogeneous of degree one in the scale — I checked that it closes with $\sigma=c$ for $c=i,\,2i,\,3i,\,\sqrt2 i,\,1,\,0.7i$ — so the table cannot fix $c$ at all. The inner products do, and the law is $\langle l,n\rangle_{\natural}=c^{2}/2$ against $\langle m,\bar m\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}/2$, so $c^{2}=-2$ and $c=\sqrt2\,i$. The $2i$ is the square root of $-4$ taken for the square root of $-2$, and its signature is exact: it doubles the scale and squares the inner products, giving $-2$ and $+2$ where the tetrad wants $-1$ and $+1$. This slip is unusually stable, and the reason is structural rather than careless: the table is blind to it, since the table closes for every $c$. Only the normalisation can detect the error, so a reader who checks the table — which is the natural thing to check — finds nothing wrong. The record bears this out: the same $2i$ is restated after being corrected three times, twice of them alongside an endorsement of the correction. The accompanying sign also flips, to $\langle l,n\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}/2$, which is the negative of the true law and would make $c=\sqrt2$ rather than $\sqrt2\,i$. Two errors in one clause, both invisible to a table check, is the sharpest case in this file of a slip that only the metric catches. And the signs come from the symmetric bilinear form, not from the Hermitian form the chat names. I computed both. On the real material basis $\{i e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$ the bilinear form is $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$ and the Hermitian form is $\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$, so the Hermitian reading is exactly the negative: indefinite of inertia $(1,3)$. It is not positive definite, and it is not a metric. A metric is symmetric bilinear; a Hermitian form is conjugate-symmetric and cannot be one. That is the species error this file keeps finding, with the right numbers attached to the wrong pairing.

Which gauge the identification lives in. A Peirce basis is three parameters — a point of $S^{2}$ for the axis and a point of $S^{1}$ for the transverse direction — while a null tetrad has six. So the tetrads that are literally $\sqrt2\,i$ times a Peirce basis form a three-dimensional slice, and three of the six Lorentz parameters take a tetrad off it. A boost along the axis is one of them, and the reason is exact: $l=\sqrt2\,i\tilde\Pi_+$ under $l\mapsto A l$ becomes a scaled idempotent with scale $A\sqrt2\,i$, while $n\mapsto A^{-1}n$ gets scale $A^{-1}\sqrt2\,i$, so the two legs no longer share the factor $\sigma$. I verified both halves: each leg alone stays $\varpi\tilde\Pi$ for some $\varpi$, and the common $\sigma$ is gone. So the identification "the tetrad is the Peirce basis" is a statement in the unit-boost gauge, and $\sigma$ is a normalisation rather than an invariant. That qualification costs one sentence and prevents an overclaim.

The pairing explains why the basis is null rather than orthonormal. On the four Peirce corners the form is not diagonal. It pairs corner $11$ with corner $22$ and corner $12$ with corner $21$, and vanishes on the other four combinations:

$$ \langle\tilde Q_{11},\tilde Q_{22}\rangle_{\natural}=\tfrac12,\qquad \langle\tilde Q_{12},\tilde Q_{21}\rangle_{\natural}=-\tfrac12,\qquad \langle\tilde Q_{11},\tilde Q_{12}\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q_{11},\tilde Q_{21}\rangle_{\natural}=0 . $$

So the metric is block-antidiagonal in the Peirce grading: two hyperbolic planes, one spanned by $(l,n)$ and one by $(m,\bar m)$. That is the structural reason a null tetrad exists. The corners are eigenspaces of an idempotent, the form is a duality between them, and a duality forces a null basis.

And the algebra closes on the tetrad. The multiplication table, with $\sigma=\sqrt2 i$ throughout, is

$$ l^{2}=\sigma l,\quad n^{2}=\sigma n,\quad ln=nl=0,\quad mm=\bar m\bar m=0, $$ $$ lm=\sigma m,\quad \bar ml=\sigma\bar m,\quad m\bar m=\sigma l,\quad \bar mm=\sigma n,\quad l\bar m=nm=ml=\bar mn=0 . $$

Read three lines of it physically. $ln=0$: the two real null vectors annihilate each other. The two null directions of the tetrad are zero divisors of one another, which is the algebraic form of the statement that they span a null plane. $m\bar m=\sigma l$: the transverse pair multiplies into the longitudinal vector. And $lm=\sigma m$ while $ml=0$: left multiplication by $l$ keeps $m$ and right multiplication by $l$ kills it, which is the corner rule $\tilde\Pi_+\vec\Upsilon_{+-}=\vec\Upsilon_{+-}$, $\vec\Upsilon_{+-}\tilde\Pi_+=0$ written in NP letters. The tetrad is not an outside structure placed on the algebra; it is the algebra's own matrix-unit basis, and the NP multiplication is the matrix-unit multiplication.

And the null vectors $l,n,m,\bar m$ are material, because the factor $i$ flips the star eigenvalue. That factor is therefore not cosmetic. The same $i$ that makes a boost generator Hermitian in the previous section makes a projector into a light ray.

Why this is the section that matters for curved space. A frame field is a choice of Peirce basis at each point, and the Peirce bases at a point are the unit tangent bundle of the sphere of idempotents, $S^{2}\times S^{1}$ read as axis plus transverse direction. So the fibre of the frame bundle is $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ and the Peirce slice inside it is three-dimensional, which is the same count as in the gauge paragraph above. The architecture is therefore: a section gives the metric, the section exists locally without any condition on curvature, and what curvature measures is how far the section is from being a pure rotor gauge. That sentence is the honest one, and I had it wrong in an earlier draft, so the next section states the distinction properly: the geometric content of the NP construction is that the algebra's own coordinate system is the one tetrad gravity uses, not that curvature selects it.

The Multi-Qubit Half: The One Link to Curvature, and Why It Fails

The only link between the multi-qubit half and curved space is the holonomy. Everything else in that half is flat-space algebra: the tensor product, the sector labels, the twelve products, the two sesquilinear forms, the concurrence. None of those sees a metric. The single point of contact with curvature is the statement that parallel transport around a closed loop acts on the fiber. The corpus owns that object: the Wilson loop is the path-ordered exponential of the connection, its trace is the gauge-invariant observable, and the article on it states the spin factor explicitly, with the holonomy taken in the representation of the spin rather than the fundamental one. So the link exists and the corpus supplies it.

The transcript's logic is right about gauge invariance. A polynomial in the amplitudes is invariant under a change of frame at a point, so a pointwise transformation cannot move it. Only the loop, which is gauge-covariant, can. That is correct and it is the standard reason local invariants are blind to a connection.

The invariant half is the corpus's, and the corpus is sharper. The alternating form of one qubit tensors to the concurrence, and I confirmed $\omega\otimes\omega(\psi,\psi)=2\det c$ on twenty random two-qubit states, with $C=1$ on the Bell state and $C=0$ on a product state. The parity rule then fixes the degree: the tensor square of the form is symmetric, and the tensor cube vanishes on the diagonal, so no degree-two invariant exists at three qubits and the three-qubit measures are quartic. I checked Cayley's quartic on the two standard states and found $1/4$ for the normalised GHZ and $0$ for $W$, with tangle $1$ and $0$. The transcript asks for "higher-degree invariants"; the corpus has computed them and explained why the degree jumps where it does.

The mechanism the transcript proposes cannot do the work it claims, but not for the reason usually given. The spin connection lives in the six-dimensional scalar-free subspace, so its holonomy is an element of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ acting on one copy of $\mathbb{C}^{2}$, and two qubits on two worldlines acquire the joint action $U_1\otimes U_2$. An earlier draft of this file, and every commentary that followed it, called that "a product of local unitaries" and concluded that entanglement cannot move. That word is wrong and it matters. $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is not unitary: a boost of $0.7$ has $U^{\dagger}U$ with eigenvalues $0.497$ and $2.014$ and trace $2.51$, where a unitary would give $I$ and trace $2$. The statement "concurrence is invariant under local unitaries" is therefore a true theorem about a group that does not act here.

What is actually invariant, and what only looks like degradation. Write the two-qubit pure state as a $2\times2$ amplitude matrix $M$, so that concurrence is $C=2|\det M|/\|M\|^{2}$. A local action sends $M\mapsto A M B^{\mathsf{T}}$, so $\det M\mapsto(\det A)(\det B)\det M$. For $A,B\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ the determinant is $1$, so the unnormalised concurrence $2|\det M|$ is exactly preserved; over two thousand random $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})^{\otimes2}$ maps the largest change was $7\times10^{-15}$. What is not preserved is the norm, and the normalised concurrence therefore moves: a Bell state boosted by $0.9$ on each qubit reads $C=0.322$, and by $1.5$ reads $C=0.099$. Both numbers are real, and both are entirely a normalisation effect, because $C_{\mathrm{norm}}\|M\|^{2}=C_{\mathrm{un}}$ is an identity that I checked to $10^{-12}$. So a boost holonomy does move the number the transcript points at, and the movement is the norm, not the entanglement. The transcript is wrong to call it entanglement loss, and the reason is sharper than unitarity: the only quantity that moves is the one that depends on the frame's normalisation.

The dimension count makes the obstruction structural rather than accidental. The real dimension of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is $6$; the real dimension of $\mathfrak{u}(4)$ is $16$. One geometry gives one six-parameter holonomy per worldline, and never a generic two-qubit unitary. A single connected spacetime cannot generate an entangling operation, because all of its qubits ride the same connection and each carries its own copy of it. But note what this count does not say: it forbids entangling, and it does not forbid the norm change above, which is why the transcript's number moves while its claim still fails.

What would actually degrade entanglement, and what it costs. The invariant is the unnormalised $2|\det M|$, and it falls only when either the determinant changes — which needs $\det\neq1$, and no holonomy in the framework has that — or when degrees of freedom are discarded: the state is traced over an environment, and what remains is mixed. The Bell state reduced by tracing out one qubit has purity $\tfrac12$, and that is a genuine loss. In a gravitational field the physical version of this is the non-invariance of the vacuum: a Bogoliubov transformation mixes positive and negative frequencies, the notion of particle becomes observer-dependent, and a detector that traces over the modes it cannot see records a thermal state. That is the Unruh effect, and the Hawking effect is the same mechanism at a horizon.

So the honest requirement is not "a Born weight" but a vacuum. A Bogoliubov transformation presupposes a Fock space with a distinguished vacuum, and the corpus constructs neither on a curved background. The transcript never mentions a vacuum, a Fock space, or a trace. Its mechanism moves a normalisation and nothing else, and the mechanism that works is the one the corpus has no machinery for. That is also why the boost case matters here rather than being a technicality: the framework's natural inner product on $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ is indefinite, so its transport is non-unitary, so "the norm of a state" is not a notion the framework supplies — which is the vacuum problem of open item 5 seen from the other side.

The verdict on this half is therefore narrower than "placed". The invariant half is the corpus's, and sharper there. The holonomy half is the corpus's, and inert as the transcript uses it. The proposal is not refuted as physics, because the Unruh route is real. It is refuted as a consequence of the framework, because the framework's holonomy changes only the normalisation of a state and its vacuum is unbuilt.

What Curvature Obstructs, and What It Does Not

The distinction is worth stating because I got it wrong once. An earlier draft of this file said that curvature is the obstruction to choosing a frame section. That is false, and the error is instructive: it confuses flatness with existence. Every Lorentzian manifold admits a local orthonormal frame by Gram–Schmidt in a chart, and the algebra plays no part in that. So curvature cannot obstruct a section. What it obstructs is the section being pure rotor gauge, and that is a different and much weaker statement, which happens to be exactly the one the framework can make.

The check is already in this file. A pure rotor gauge $\tilde u_\mu\mapsto\tilde U\tilde u_\mu\tilde U^{*}$ with $\tilde U=\cos\theta+\sin\theta\,\vec e_x$ and $\theta=0.8x$ leaves the Gram matrix constant and equal to $\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. No point-dependent rotor produces curvature. So curvature is, by definition here, the part of the frame that no rotor removes. That is a clean statement and it needs no manifold theory.

The same statement in loop language is the holonomy, and that is the one place the algebra runs out. "No rotor removes it" is local. The invariant version is global and goes through loops: the connection is flat exactly when the parallel transport around every closed loop is the identity, and parallel transport is the path-ordered exponential. Now look at what that object is. It is a limit of products of algebra elements along a path, and no such operation exists inside $\mathbb{B}$, which is a point algebra with one product. The path-ordered exponential is therefore the first object in this whole construction that the algebra hosts but does not generate. That is the wall, and it is a wall about integration, not about the tetrad, the connection, or the curvature tensor.

One object in three roles, and this answers the question of how the two halves meet. The spin connection appears three times in this file and it is the same six-real-dimensional thing each time:

Role Where What it does
connection on the frame $D_\mu\tilde Q=\partial_\mu\tilde Q+\vec\Gamma_\mu\tilde Q+\tilde Q\vec\Gamma_\mu^{*}$ makes the derivative covariant; its generators split into commutator and anticommutator halves

transport around a loop the Wilson loop and the multi-qubit holonomy acts on one copy of $\mathbb{C}^{2}$ as an element of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$

So the answer to "how do the bundle obstruction and curvature relate to the holonomy and the spin connection" is: they are one object seen at three levels of integration. The connection is the infinitesimal datum, curvature is its failure to commute at second order, and holonomy is its accumulation at finite distance. The multi-qubit half of this file then attaches at the third level only, which is why its claim is about loops and not about local entanglement.

What is not curvature, and must not be folded into it. Two things. First, the topological obstruction: a spin structure exists only when the second Stiefel–Whitney class vanishes, and that is a condition on the manifold, not on any connection. Second, the gluing data. A frame field on a non-parallelisable manifold needs an atlas, and the transition functions are rotors. The algebra is a point, so it can write each patch and cannot write the cocycle. Both items are already on the corpus's open list under global and topological structure, and this paragraph is the reason they belong there rather than under curvature.

The hierarchy, in one line each. Pointwise algebra, with no position. Frame, which carries the metric. Connection, six parameters per point. Curvature, the failure of the connection to be pure gauge. Holonomy, the same failure accumulated around a loop. Topology, which is not a failure of anything and is outside the algebra entirely. Each line needs one more structure than the line above, and the corpus reaches the fourth with rigour and the fifth only by importing it.

Summary

The framework's curved-space ledger is strong, and it is closed at one end. The algebra carries any Lorentzian metric as a Gram matrix of four frame elements. The local group is a pointwise $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ and the counting closes. The covariant derivative is two-sided, and the star eigenvalue of the generator splits it: the rotation half is a commutator, the boost half an anticommutator, and only the first survives a commutator prescription. The curved Dirac operator squares to the Laplace–Beltrami operator plus a quarter of the Ricci scalar, inside the algebra. The null tetrad is the Peirce basis, and the NP article states that much; what it does not yet state is that the multiplication closes on the tetrad and that the pairing is block-antidiagonal in the Peirce grading. The framework's own local scale is the diagonal member of the frame route, and its optical reading agrees with Gordon's metric. The one boundary is exact: within the one-function class, vacuum forces flatness.

The transcript restates that ledger, mostly correctly. Five of its six curved-space answers agree with the corpus. The three that do not are a misreading of the frame as a local complex structure, a commutator form of the derivative that drops the boosts, and a malformed projected derivative. No corpus article needs correcting: eight curved-space statements were recomputed for this file and all eight hold. The null-tetrad audit adds six obligations for the NP rewrite — four results the maths side owns, one label correction, one clarification about the scale — and all six are verified here.

One proposal is new, and its mechanism is inert. The holonomy account of entanglement is the only link between the multi-qubit half and curvature, and the link is real: the corpus owns the Wilson loop. But the holonomy is an element of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$, which is not unitary, and the honest statement is narrower than invariance: the unnormalised concurrence $2|\det M|$ is preserved to $7\times10^{-15}$ because the local determinants are $1$, while the normalised concurrence does move — a Bell state boosted by $0.9$ reads $0.322$ — and that movement is exactly the change of norm. Entanglement falls only under a non-unitary channel, and the physical one in a gravitational field is the Unruh mechanism, which needs a vacuum and a Fock space. The corpus has neither, so the claim is not refuted as physics and is refuted as a consequence of the framework.

Curvature obstructs flatness and not existence. Every manifold carries a frame locally, so curvature cannot be what blocks a section; what it blocks is the section being pure rotor gauge, and that is exactly what a point algebra can express. The three levels — connection, curvature, holonomy — are one object seen at increasing order of integration, and the path-ordered exponential is the single operation in the chain that $\mathbb{B}$ does not generate. That is where the framework stops, and it is a wall about integration rather than about the tetrad.

Pati–Salam versus Left–Right: What Separates Them, and Which the Algebra Prefers

Introduction

The question. Two entries of Structural GUT Comparisons stand above the rest: Pati–Salam at nine points and the left–right model at eight. They share the factor $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ and they share the sixteen-field generation. A reader asks whether they are really two theories, which one the biquaternion framework prefers, and what the experiments say.

The answer, in five sentences.

They are not peers. The left–right group is a subgroup of the Pati–Salam group, of the same rank, so every affinity the left–right model has, Pati–Salam has too, and the comparison reduces to what Pati–Salam adds.

What Pati–Salam adds is exactly the six-dimensional coset

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\big/\big(\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\big)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}, $$

the space of complex lines in the carrier, so the difference between the two theories is a single geometric object: a choice of line.

The algebra supplies that line canonically, because the carrier is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}$ and the unit line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is fixed by every automorphism, which is the sharpest possible sense in which a line can be canonical.

On structure, therefore, Pati–Salam is the more faithful theory, and the reason is not that it is larger but that it discards nothing.

On experiment the order reverses: the left–right model is the safer theory, because it has no gauge leptoquark, no proton decay, and no fermion-mass relation to fail.

What this note adds beyond the ranking. Three things. A treatment of the axis question, which is a real objection and has a real answer. An exact reading of the operator $L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}$ and its geometry. And the physics comparison, which the ranking does not attempt.

The order. §1 the containment. §2 where each group is built. §3 the axis question. §4 the operator. §5 the physics. §6 the scorecard. §7 the verdict.

1. The Containment

The chain. Both groups have rank five, and one contains the other:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\ \supset\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R, $$

$$ \underbrace{3+1+1=5}_{\text{Pati–Salam}}\ =\ \underbrace{2+1+1+1=5}_{\text{left–right}} . $$

The embedding is the standard one, $\mathrm{SU}(4)\supset\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ with the circle generated by the traceless diagonal

$$ B-L=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1\bigr),\qquad 3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0 . $$

The consequence, which decides the shape of this note. Any statement true of the left–right model is true of Pati–Salam, because the group is bigger and the multiplet is the same sixteen. So Pati–Salam cannot lose an axis for a reason the left–right model escapes. It can only lose by having more to justify. The comparison is therefore not symmetric, and a table that reads as though it were is misleading.

The coset, and why it is the whole story. The quotient has dimension

$$ \dim\mathrm{SU}(4)-\dim\mathrm{SU}(3)-\dim\mathrm{U}(1)=15-8-1=6, $$

and it is the six-dimensional space of complex lines through the origin of the carrier:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\big/\big(\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\big)\ \cong\ \mathbb{CP}^{3}. $$

So the difference between the two theories is not a list of particles. It is one point of $\mathbb{CP}^3$. Pati–Salam's extra gauge bosons are the coordinates of that point; the left–right model is what remains when the point is fixed and the coordinates are dropped. A theory that fixes a point by hand is a theory with one unexplained datum, and the whole question of this note is whether the algebra supplies it.

The multiplet is shared, and it is not a rearrangement. One generation is sixteen in both:

$$ \text{Pati–Salam: }(\mathbf{4},\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\bar{\mathbf{4}},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2}),\qquad \text{left–right: }(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\bar{\mathbf{3}},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2}). $$

The first reduces to the second under $\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$. What changes is not the content but the number of terms: one tensor product against four. §2 shows that the single product is the algebra's own shape and the four are not.

2. Where Each Group Is Built

Pati–Salam's $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. Native, at the operator layer, with no choice:

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)=\text{compact real form of }\mathfrak{sl}(4,\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{E}'_{\mathbb{C}},\qquad \dim=15,\ \mathrm{rank}=3,\ \cong\mathfrak{so}(6). $$

It is the traceless part of the operator algebra $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$, and it acts on the carrier $\mathbb{C}^4=\mathbb{B}$ in its fundamental. So the group, the module and the layer are all fixed by the algebra.

Left–right's $\mathfrak{su}(3)$. Not native. It is a subgroup of that same $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, reached by discarding a line:

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)\ \supset\ \mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1),\qquad \text{the split } \mathbb{C}^4=\mathbb{C}^3\oplus\mathbb{C}. $$

So $\mathfrak{su}(3)_c$ is a fragment of the operator layer's own group, obtained after a choice, and the choice is the point of $\mathbb{CP}^3$ from §1. The corpus never builds $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ on its own; it builds $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ and everything else is a quotient or a subalgebra of it.

The layer budget, restated precisely.

object dim layer native?
$L(\mathfrak{k})\oplus R(\mathfrak{k})=\mathfrak{so}(4)$ $6$ element yes, free
the central circle, $\mathrm{U}(1)$ $1$ element yes, free
$\mathfrak{su}(4)$ $15$ operator yes, whole
$\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$ $9$ operator subalgebra, after a line is chosen
$\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$ $12$ mixed $A_2\oplus A_1\not\subset A_3$

Two readings, and they point opposite ways. Read as economy, the left–right model is cheaper: it uses nine of the fifteen dimensions of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ and does not need the other six. Read as structure, Pati–Salam is cheaper: it needs no choice, because a group used whole needs no justification and a group used in part needs the rule that selects the part. The corpus ranks on structure, and §3 is why that is the right criterion here rather than a prejudice.

3. The Axis Question

The objection. If colour is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, then a generator such as $\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac23,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$ singles out one direction, and the corpus has no axis-free basis. So the colour group seems to prefer an axis, and a structure that prefers an axis is suspect in an algebra whose whole discipline is to avoid frames.

The answer, in three parts. No axis is preferred. What looks like a preference is a choice of Cartan, and the Weyl group acts on the set of such choices. But something is preferred, and it is not $x$, $y$ or $z$: it is the unit line $\mathbb{C}e_t$, and the corpus has it for free.

3.1 The axes are one orbit

Verified. The order-three unit quaternion

$$ Q=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+\vec e_z\bigr),\qquad N(Q)=\tfrac14(1+1+1+1)=1, $$

acts by conjugation and permutes the three axes cyclically while fixing the unit:

$$ Q\vec e_xQ^{-1}=\vec e_y,\qquad Q\vec e_yQ^{-1}=\vec e_z,\qquad Q\vec e_zQ^{-1}=\vec e_x,\qquad Qe_tQ^{-1}=e_t . $$

So $\{x,y,z\}$ is a single orbit under the automorphism group and no member of it is invariant. A statement that appears to single out $x$ either is not invariant and should be corrected, or is an artefact of the display.

The stabiliser, so the statement is exact. The element $u=e_t+\vec e_x$ is invertible, and

$$ ue_xu^{-1}=e_x,\qquad ue_yu^{-1}=e_z,\qquad ue_zu^{-1}=-e_y . $$

So the stabiliser of an axis is the circle of rotations in the orthogonal plane, and that circle fixes the axis only as a set of rotations, not as a distinguished object. The orbit is $\mathbb{P}^2$; the axes are equivalent.

What this means for a Cartan basis. A Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is a choice, and the Weyl group $S_3$ permutes the three possible choices. Writing the Cartan as $\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac23,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$ names one; writing it as

$$ h_a=\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(L_{\vec e_b}R_{\vec e_b}-L_{\vec e_c}R_{\vec e_c}\bigr),\qquad a,b,c\ \text{cyclic},\qquad h_x+h_y+h_z=0, $$

names all three at once, and the triple is cyclically closed. The first display prefers an axis; the second does not. The algebra permits the second, so the first is a presentational choice and not a structural one.

3.2 What is actually preferred is the unit

The claim, and it is the correction of the objection. In $\mathbb{B}$ there is exactly one line that every automorphism fixes, and it is not an axis:

$$ \mathrm{Ad}_u(e_t)=ue_tu^{-1}=e_t\quad\text{for every invertible }u, $$

because $e_t$ is the unit of the algebra. The line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is therefore canonical in the strongest possible sense: it is invariant under the whole automorphism group $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$, hence under every group the algebra can carry, hence it is the only line that can be a singlet.

So the carrier splits canonically as

$$ \mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{C}}=\underbrace{\mathbb{C}e_t}_{\mathbf{1},\ \text{canonical}}\ \oplus\ \underbrace{\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}}_{\mathbf{3},\ \text{one orbit under }S_3},\qquad 1+3=4, $$

and this is the $\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{3}$ of Pati–Salam, with the singlet canonical and the triplet symmetric. The split that Pati–Salam needs is not an extra assumption; it is the corpus's own decomposition of $\mathbb{B}$ into the unit and the vector part.

3.3 Where the colour group sits, and the honest accounting

The three inside the eight. The corpus's own account of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ at the operator layer separates a part that is canonical from a part that is not, as a symmetric pair:

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p},\qquad \dim\mathfrak{k}=3,\quad \dim\mathfrak{p}=5,\qquad \mathrm{SU}(3)\big/\mathrm{SO}(3)\ \text{a compact symmetric space of dimension }5, $$

and the three-dimensional part is exactly the derivation algebra:

$$ \mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{L_{\vec e_k}-R_{\vec e_k}\}. $$

So the axis-free part of colour is the rigid rotation part, and it is the algebra's own automorphism algebra, the Lie algebra of $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong PGL_2(\mathbb{C})\cong SO(3,\mathbb{C})$. The remaining five directions are traceless symmetric tensors on the three-plane, they are not automorphisms at all, and the corpus calls them quadrupoles rather than rotations. They are the part of colour that has no rigid geometric meaning, and they are the part that needs an axis to be written down.

The corpus's own rule, which settles the matter. The instruction is explicit: any statement about this $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ must be stated either in the split $1+3$ or in $L,R$ with no axis named. That is precisely the choice between the two theories. Pati–Salam states its colour in the split $1+3$. The left–right model separates the $3$ from the $1$ and then treats the $3$ alone, which is the one option the rule excludes.

The accounting, stated plainly.

what the theory needs what the corpus supplies is it canonical?
a $\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{3}$ split of the carrier $\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}$ yes, unique canonical line
a triplet permuted by $S_3$ $\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$ under the Weyl group yes, one orbit
the rigid part of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})=\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\mathfrak{so}(4)$ yes, the automorphism algebra
the quadrupole part of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ five symmetric directions no, and it needs an axis to be written

The conclusion, and it answers the objection exactly. The objection is right that $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ written as a stand-alone fundamental group is the poorer object, and the reason is precise: five of its eight dimensions have no frame-free expression. But the objection is wrong to conclude that colour is therefore an artefact of a frame. Colour sits inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is native, and the split that produces colour is the split at the unit line, which is canonical. The problematic object is not colour; it is colour divorced from the line it came from. And that is precisely what the left–right model does and Pati–Salam does not.

One sentence. $\mathfrak{su}(3)_c$ is not a group that prefers the $x$ axis; it is a group obtained by separating the vector part from the unit, and a theory that keeps the unit in the same simple factor keeps the only canonical structure the algebra has.

4. The Operator $L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}$

Why this operator is the right one to ask about. It is the simplest two-sided combination that pairs an axis with the unit, so it is exactly the place where a frame would show up if a frame were needed. Its reading settles §3 twice over.

4.1 The identity, in one line

Because the unit multiplies trivially on both sides — $L_{e_t}=R_{e_t}=\mathrm{id}$ — the display collapses:

$$ L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}=L_{\vec e_x}-R_{\vec e_x}=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}. $$

Verified on the four basis elements. With $\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde QX-X\tilde Q$:

$$ \mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(e_t)=0,\qquad \mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(\vec e_x)=0,\qquad \mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(\vec e_y)=2\vec e_z,\qquad \mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}(\vec e_z)=-2\vec e_y . $$

So the operator in the question is the inner derivation along $\vec e_x$, and nothing else. The $e_t$ in it is not a chosen axis; it is the neutral element, and its presence is what makes the expression a difference of the two sides rather than a product. Written in general,

$$ L_{a}R_{b}-L_{b}R_{a}\Big|_{b=e_t}=L_a-R_a=\mathrm{ad}_a , $$

so the display is the antisymmetric pairing of the two sides at the identity. It measures the disagreement of the left and right actions on an element that is itself fixed. That is why it is the natural generator of an automorphism: moving both slots by the same amount preserves the product, and the difference is the amount.

4.2 Three readings, all in the corpus

Reading one: a derivation. The Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra proves that every derivation of $\mathbb{B}$ is inner and that

$$ \operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}/\mathbb{C}e_t,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}=3, $$

with the display $\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}(x)=\tilde Qx-x\tilde Q$ and the remark that this is the only place where the two regular operators occur, and only through their difference. So $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}$ is one of the three generators of the entire derivation algebra, and $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})$ is the whole of it.

Reading two: the derivative of a rotor. Differentiate the one-parameter group of inner automorphisms:

$$ \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0}\Bigl(L_{e^{t\vec e_x}}R_{e^{-t\vec e_x}}\Bigr)=L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}. $$

So the finite object is the two-sided operator with the right parameter inverted, and the display is its first-order term. The corpus proves that this finite object is a reflected rotation on the vector part, $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{-1}}|_{\mathfrak{k}}=-\rho_{\tilde Q}$, with the sign repaired by inserting $-\tilde Q$ instead. The finite member is a reflection, the composition of two is a rotation, and $\mathrm{ad}$ is the generator of rotations.

Reading three: the relative spin-one generator of the pair. From the Spin of a Single Rotor to the Spin of a Pair defines $J_k=\tfrac12 L_{\vec e_k}$, $J'_k=\tfrac12 R_{\vec e_k}$ and identifies the diagonal of the pair as $J_k^{\mathrm{tot}}=J_k-J'_k$. Therefore

$$ \mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}=2\bigl(J_x-J'_x\bigr). $$

So the factor of two is not a slip. One $L$ is spin $\tfrac12$, the pair's relative generator is spin $1$, and the derivation is twice it — which is also why $[\vec e_x,\vec e_y]=2\vec e_z$ while $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$.

4.3 The geometry

The flow. Exponentiating gives a one-parameter group of unital automorphisms:

$$ \exp\bigl(\theta\,(J_x-J'_x)\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}\Bigr). $$

Verified at $\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$:

$$ e_t\longmapsto e_t,\qquad \vec e_x\longmapsto\vec e_x,\qquad \vec e_y\longmapsto\vec e_z,\qquad \vec e_z\longmapsto-\vec e_y . $$

So the flow fixes the unit and the axis and turns the orthogonal plane by the full angle, and at $\theta=\pi$ it is the half-turn $\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1)$ on that plane. On the vector part it is twice the cross product with $\vec e_x$.

Four properties, and each one is a statement about the framework.

It fixes the unit, so it is a rigid symmetry of the multiplication table and not a movement of the unit line. This is why it is an automorphism: the unit is preserved, and the product with it is preserved.

It is odd under the side-swap $\sigma(L_aR_b)=L_bR_a$, because $\sigma(\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q})=-\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}$. So it measures the disagreement of the two sides, which is why it is the relative generator $J-J'$ and not $J+J'$, and why it lies in the antisymmetric half of the operator space. This is checkable in one line and it is the structural content of the operator:

$$ \bigl(\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}\bigr)^{\approx}=-\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}, $$

since the plain-bilinear adjoint exchanges the sides, $L^{\approx}_{\tilde Q}=R_{\tilde Q}$ and $R^{\approx}_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}$. Every derivation is skew for the plain form, without exception.

It is the compact half. The three real $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_k}$ span $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; the three $\mathrm{ad}_{i\vec e_k}$ are the boosts, Hermitian and outside $\mathfrak{u}(4)$. Together the six are $\mathfrak{so}(3,1)$, the derivation algebra read as a real Lorentz algebra.

It is exactly the $3$ inside $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ of §3.3, and no more. The five quadrupole directions are not of this form.

4.4 What this settles

The operator asked about is frame-free in its content and frame-dependent only in its label. Its form $L_{\tilde Q}-R_{\tilde Q}$ mentions no axis; the label $\vec e_x$ is one of three basis elements of an orbit, and the cyclic permutation of §3.1 carries it to either of the others and to the correspondingly relabelled operator. So no axis is preferred, and the operator is the canonical way to write a rotation generator.

And the operator is the reason the corpus's colour is the rigid part of colour. The derivations are the automorphisms' tangent space, the automorphisms preserve the unit, and the unit is the canonical line. The chain runs: unit line $\to$ automorphisms $\to$ derivations $\to$ $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})\subset\mathfrak{su}(3)$. A structure built along that chain is native; a structure that leaves the chain at the first step is not.

5. Physics: What the Experiments Separate

The shared part, said first, because it is most of the physics. Both theories have $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ and therefore both have a $W_R$ and a $Z_R$, both have a right-handed neutrino as a gauge state, both require sixteen fields, and both allow neutrinoless double beta decay through the heavy neutrino. No $W_R$ search distinguishes them. A $W_R$ seen at the LHC would confirm both, and a bound on $M_{W_R}$ constrains both equally.

The four genuine separators.

Separator one: charge quantisation. In Pati–Salam, $B-L$ is a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and is therefore traceless on the quartet, $3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0$. That single condition relates quark and lepton charges and gives the fractional charges without further input. In the left–right model $B-L$ is an independent $\mathrm{U}(1)$ whose normalisation is free, so charge quantisation is not explained; it has to be imposed, or inherited from a larger group such as $\mathrm{SO}(10)$. This is a real explanatory difference and it favors Pati–Salam.

Separator two: the gauge leptoquarks. Pati–Salam's coset $\mathbb{CP}^3$ carries six gauge bosons that couple a quark to a lepton. They mediate $K_L\to\mu e$ and, through the scalar sector, proton decay. The measured bound $\mathrm{BR}(K_L\to\mu e)<4.7\times10^{-12}$ pushes the Pati–Salam scale up, and the proton lifetime bound $\tau(p\to e^+\pi^0)>2.4\times10^{34}$ yr does real work. The left–right model has no gauge leptoquark, because $B$ and $L$ are separately conserved at the gauge level, so the proton is stable at the renormalisable level and $K_L\to\mu e$ is not generated by gauge exchange. This favors the left–right model.

Separator three: the fermion-mass relation. Pati–Salam's minimal scalar sector, one $(4,1,2)$ and one $(1,2,2)$, gives $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$ at the Pati–Salam scale. The measured spectrum violates both, and the second badly. Viability needs a $(15,2,2)$ or a second bidoublet. The left–right model's bidoublet gives two independent Yukawa matrices and predicts no such relation, so it does not fail this test. This favors the left–right model.

Separator four: the number of couplings. Pati–Salam's $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ relates the strong coupling to the $B-L$ coupling at its breaking scale, one relation; imposing $g_L=g_R$ adds a second. The left–right model relates nothing: $g_3$, $g_{2L}$, $g_{2R}$ and $g_{B-L}$ are four independent parameters. So Pati–Salam is more predictive and more falsifiable, and the left–right model is safer. This is a genuine trade and not a preference.

Two readings of that table, and both are true.

Read as safety, the left–right model wins four lines to two. It is the model that is hardest to exclude, and that is why it survives with a breaking scale anywhere above a few TeV.

Read as content, Pati–Salam wins where a theory can be wrong. Its two winning lines are both derivations, its four losing lines are all constraints that a sufficiently heavy scale or a slightly larger scalar sector repairs, and none of them kills the group. A theory that derives charge quantisation and can be excluded is doing more work than a theory that derives nothing and cannot be excluded.

6. The Scorecard, Axis by Axis

Where the two differ, and why. The grades are from Structural GUT Comparisons; this section states the reason for each difference rather than repeating the arithmetic.

So the ranking separates them on exactly one axis, and the axis is the group. That is a cleaner statement than the totals suggest, and it makes the comparison decidable: everything except axis A is shared, because the left–right group is a subgroup of the same rank.

And axis A is not a matter of taste. Its three-point grade is defined as every factor is a compact real form of $\mathbb{B}$'s or $\mathcal{E}$'s Lie algebra. Pati–Salam's three factors are $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, $\mathfrak{su}(2)$: the first is the compact real form of $\mathcal{E}'_{\mathbb{C}}$ and the other two are the two sides at the element layer. The left–right group's $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is neither a real form of anything native nor a side; it is a subalgebra of a native algebra, reached by fixing a point of $\mathbb{CP}^3$. The rule's two-point grade describes exactly that: a factor that is only a subgroup of a native one.

What would give the left–right model its point back. Nothing available at the group level, because the left–right group's $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is a stabiliser rather than a whole factor and nothing makes it whole. What it lacks is canonicity, not legitimacy — see the paragraph below. What would lift both by three points is the same object: a gauge-charged scalar. It is one missing object, it is shared, and it is what keeps both entries out of the top grade.

Axis A measures nativity, and nativity is the old test under another name. The three-point grade asks that each factor be a compact real form or a side; that is containment in the algebra wearing a scoring rule. Under the frame's criterion both groups are legitimate, because both are admitted by the carrier, the canonical split and the two-sided action — $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as the stabiliser of the line, the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s as the two sides. So the one-point margin is not a difference of legitimacy and must not be quoted as one. It is a difference of canonicity: $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ uses the carrier whole and needs no choice of point, while $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ requires a choice of point in $\mathbb{CP}^3$. That difference is real and it is weaker than the one the score suggests. The verdict of §7 survives with reasons two, three and four intact; reason one changes from it uses the native group whole to it uses the carrier without a choice, which is what the comparison actually supports.

7. The Verdict

On structure, Pati–Salam is the more in line, and there are four reasons that do not depend on the score.

Reason one: it uses the carrier without a choice. $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is the compact real form of the traceless part of the operator algebra. Using it whole requires no selection rule. Using nine of its fifteen dimensions requires the rule that says which nine, and that rule is a point of $\mathbb{CP}^3$. Both groups are licensed; only one is canonical, and the reason is canonicity rather than legitimacy.

Reason two: its split is the algebra's own splitting. The carrier is $\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}$, the singlet is the unit line, the triplet is the vector part, and the Weyl group $S_3$ permutes the three without fixing one. So Pati–Salam's $\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{3}$ is not an assumption about the carrier; it is the carrier's canonical decomposition read as a representation.

Reason three: its multiplet is one tensor product. One generation is

$$ \mathbf{16}=\mathbf{4}\otimes\mathbf{2}\otimes\mathbf{2}=\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{L}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{R}, $$

one term. The left–right model writes the same sixteen as four terms after cutting the $\mathbf{4}$. The corpus's own statement is that products of two parameters fill a cone of coefficient rank one; a single tensor product is the fingerprint of that shape and a sum of four is not.

Reason four: the axis objection does not damage it. The objection says a generator prefers the $x$ axis. §3 and §4 answer it: the axes are one orbit, the Cartan is a choice, the canonical line is the unit, and the operator $L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}$ is $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}$, frame-free in form. The objection, correctly aimed, hits the left–right model and not Pati–Salam, because it is the model that keeps colour away from the line that made it canonical.

On experiment the order reverses, and that is not a contradiction. The left–right model is harder to exclude because it says less. Pati–Salam derives charge quantisation, relates two couplings, and predicts a mass relation that fails — which is what a theory with content does. The corpus's criterion is structural fidelity, and on that criterion a theory that can be wrong in a specific way is worth more than a theory that cannot be wrong at all.

The honest summary in one sentence. Pati–Salam is what the algebra gives when nothing is thrown away; the left–right model is what remains when the line is dropped, and dropping the line is what makes it safe.

8. What Would Flip It

Three developments, in order of force.

A chiral carrier. A map tying the operator side to the spinor letter, so that $L$ acts on one letter and $R$ on the other. Both entries gain on axis C, and neither gains more than the other, so the order does not change. What it would change is the gap to the Standard Model.

A gauge-charged scalar. A spin-zero carrier that is not the unit line. Both entries gain three points on axis D, Pati–Salam to twelve and the left–right model to eleven, and the gap is unchanged at one point. So this is the development that would matter most in absolute terms and least in relative terms, which is a useful thing to know before looking for it.

A native $\mathfrak{su}(3)$. This is the only development that would flip the order. It would require the algebra to supply three commuting directions at a layer where it currently supplies only the split of a four, and the corpus's own accounting says it does not: $\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\mathfrak{so}(4)$ is three-dimensional and the remaining five require an axis. So the flip is blocked by a computation, not by effort.

The experimental flip, for completeness. If a $W_R$ were found together with a leptoquark of the Pati–Salam type, Pati–Salam would be confirmed and the left–right model would be its low-energy truncation. If a $W_R$ were found and the Pati–Salam scale were pushed above the reach of $K_L\to\mu e$ and proton decay, the two would remain degenerate experimentally and only the structure would decide — which is the situation the corpus is already in.

Summary

The containment. $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)^2\supset\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)^2$, at equal rank five. The quotient is $\mathbb{CP}^3$, the space of lines in the carrier. The difference between the theories is one point of $\mathbb{CP}^3$.

The axis question. No axis is preferred: the order-three unit quaternion $\tfrac12(e_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+\vec e_z)$ cycles $x\to y\to z$ and fixes $e_t$, so $\{x,y,z\}$ is one orbit. A Cartan is a choice on which $S_3$ acts. What is preferred is the unit line $\mathbb{C}e_t$, fixed by every automorphism, and it is the only line that can be a singlet.

The operator. Because $L_{e_t}=R_{e_t}=\mathrm{id}$,

$$ L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}=L_{\vec e_x}-R_{\vec e_x}=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}, $$

the inner derivation along $\vec e_x$: verified as $\vec e_y\mapsto2\vec e_z$, $\vec e_z\mapsto-2\vec e_y$, fixing $e_t$ and $\vec e_x$; equal to $2(J_x-J'_x)$, the relative spin-one generator of the pair; skew for the plain bilinear form, hence the generator of a rigid automorphism; and one of the three generators of $\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}/\mathbb{C}e_t$. Its flow is a rotation of the orthogonal plane that fixes the unit, so it is a symmetry of the multiplication table and not a movement of the unit line.

The physics. Four genuine separators. Pati–Salam derives charge quantisation and relates two couplings; the left–right model has no gauge leptoquark, no proton decay, no mass relation and no prediction. Structure prefers Pati–Salam; experiment prefers the left–right model's safety.

The verdict. Pati–Salam, on axis A, for one reason that is a single sentence: it uses the native group whole and keeps the unit in the same simple factor as the vector part, so it needs no point of $\mathbb{CP}^3$ and no axis. The left–right model keeps nine of fifteen dimensions and must choose which nine.

The one number. $9$ against $8$, and the point is axis A alone.

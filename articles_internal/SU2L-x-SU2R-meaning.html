$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$: The Group, the Two Sides, and Why the Standard Model Does Not Gauge It

Introduction

The observation that prompts this note. The two entries at the top of Structural GUT Comparisons are Pati–Salam and the left–right model, and both contain the factor $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$. The Standard Model contains only half of it. A reader then asks the obvious question: is the ranking measuring the presence of this group?

The answer, in four lines. Largely yes, and for a reason that is structural rather than aesthetic. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is the one non-abelian group the biquaternion algebra supplies at the element level, for free, as the two one-sided families of its vector part:

$$ L(\mathfrak{k})\oplus R(\mathfrak{k})=\mathfrak{so}(4),\qquad \dim=3+3=6 . $$

Every other non-abelian group in the table costs a layer. And the Standard Model does not gauge it because the Standard Model is the broken phase of a theory that does: its hypercharge is the diagonal survivor,

$$ Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L),\qquad Q=T_{3L}+Y . $$

So the honest answer to "why does the Standard Model not use it" is: it uses it twice as a global symmetry, and it uses its broken remnant as hypercharge. What it does not do is gauge it unbroken.

The trap, named first. The corpus writes left and right for three different things — the slot of an operator, the label of an element, and the helicity of a field — and Objects That Split Left and Right separates them once. This note keeps them separated throughout, because $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ contains the word left in a fourth sense again: the name of a gauge factor. Five meanings of one word is how this subject goes wrong.

The order. §1 fixes the group as an abstract object. §2 gives the corpus reading, which is the two one-sided families. §3 separates the two modules the same group can act on, spacetime and internal, and this separation is the technical heart. §4 lists the three roles the group plays in physics and shows that the Standard Model already uses two of them. §5 answers the question in the title. §6 explains the ranking. §7 states what would settle it.

1. The Group

The algebra. Complexified, the Lie algebra is

$$ \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}),\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}=6,\qquad \mathrm{rank}=2, $$

two simple ideals of dimension three that commute. It is not simple, and that is its defining property here: a semisimple group with a two-dimensional Cartan subalgebra, split into two halves that cannot talk to each other through the bracket.

The compact form. The compact real form is

$$ \mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2),\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}=6,\qquad \mathrm{rank}=2, $$

whose group is $\mathrm{Spin}(4)=\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$, the double cover of $\mathrm{SO}(4)$:

$$ 1\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_2\longrightarrow \mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathrm{SO}(4)\longrightarrow 1 . $$

The display, which the corpus owns. The map $\rho$ is not an abstraction in this corpus. It is constructed from a pair of rotors acting on two sides:

$$ \rho:\mathrm{Spin}(3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(3)\longrightarrow SO(4),\qquad \ker\rho=\{\pm(\mathrm{I}_4,\mathrm{I}_4)\}, $$

and the companion computation gives the spins a pair of operators can carry: each factor carries $s=\tfrac12$, the addition rule gives $0$ and $1$, and nothing else. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is the spin group of four-dimensional space, and it is the corpus's own construction of a pair.

The invariants.

object $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}$ rank centre simple?
$\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ $6$ $2$ trivial no, two ideals
$\mathfrak{so}(4)$ $6$ $2$ trivial no, two ideals
$\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ group $2$ $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (diagonal) no
$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ alone $3$ $1$ trivial yes

The rank is two and the dimension is six, and both numbers will be compared against the corpus's budget in §6.

The three real forms, and one warning. The complex algebra has more real forms than the compact one: the split $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$, the compact $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$, and the Lorentz one, which is not a product.

The Lorentz group is the diagonal. The complex algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ contains the diagonal

$$ \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}),\qquad X\longmapsto (X,X), $$

and that diagonal is the Lorentz algebra, with group $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{SO}^{+}(1,3)$. So the relation between the two groups of this note is exact and simple:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\ =\ \text{compact form of the doubled algebra},\qquad \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\ =\ \text{its diagonal}. $$

This is the single most useful sentence in the note, because it says that the left–right group and the Lorentz group are not two unrelated structures. They are the untied and tied versions of one object. §2 shows that the corpus builds exactly this tie, with the involution $*$ as the tie.

2. The Corpus Reading: Two Families, One Vector Part

The three addresses of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The internal note The Meaning of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ in the Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra settles that the algebra's rotor group enters the endomorphisms at three addresses, with $\mathfrak{k}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$:

$$ L(\mathfrak{k})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{L_{\vec e_x},L_{\vec e_y},L_{\vec e_z}\},\qquad R(\mathfrak{k})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{R_{\vec e_x},R_{\vec e_y},R_{\vec e_z}\},\qquad \mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{L_{\vec e_k}-R_{\vec e_k}\}, $$

with

$$ L(\mathfrak{k})\oplus R(\mathfrak{k})=\mathfrak{so}(4),\qquad \mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})=\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\mathfrak{so}(4). $$

So the group of this note is the first two addresses together. Not a subgroup of the algebra and not an import:

$$ \boxed{\ \mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\ =\ \exp\!\bigl(L(\mathfrak{k})\bigr)\times\exp\!\bigl(R(\mathfrak{k})\bigr)\ =\ \mathrm{Spin}(4)\ } $$

at the element level, inside $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, of dimension six. No operator layer, no Clifford algebra, no imported object. The two factors are the two sides of one multiplication.

Why they commute, and why that is the point. $L_a$ and $R_b$ commute for all $a,b$, because they act on the two ends of the argument. So the two factors of the product are independent, and the group is a product and not a quotient. A group that is a product of two commuting factors is exactly what a left–right symmetric theory needs, and the corpus supplies it with no work.

The self-dual split, which names the two factors. The two factors are not interchangeable labels. On the six-dimensional space of two-forms,

$$ \mathfrak{so}(4)=\Lambda^{2}_{+}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{-},\qquad \star^{2}=\mathrm{id}, $$

the self-dual and anti-self-dual halves, and the corpus proves which is which:

$$ L(\vec e_x)=-\omega_1,\quad L(\vec e_y)=-\omega_2,\quad L(\vec e_z)=-\omega_3\ \in\Lambda^{2}_{+}, $$

$$ R(\vec e_x)=-\tau_1,\quad R(\vec e_y)=-\tau_2,\quad R(\vec e_z)=-\tau_3\ \in\Lambda^{2}_{-}, $$

with the brackets

$$ [L(\vec e_x),L(\vec e_y)]=2L(\vec e_z),\qquad [R(\vec e_x),R(\vec e_y)]=-2R(\vec e_z). $$

The sign difference is the mirror. The two triples obey the same table with opposite sign, so the two factors are mirror images, and the corpus states this as the exact sense in which they differ. The left factor is the self-dual half, the right factor the anti-self-dual half, and the Hodge star exchanges them.

This is what the group means in this algebra. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is the rotation group of the four-dimensional carrier, split into the half that rotates the self-dual planes and the half that rotates the anti-self-dual planes. It is the two angles of a four-dimensional rotation made into two groups.

The sandwich, and the tie. On an element the two factors act by

$$ \tilde Q\longmapsto g_L\,\tilde Q\,g_R^{*},\qquad g_{L},g_{R}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}, $$

and a central vacuum value is invariant under the diagonal $g_L=g_R$. Setting $g_R=g_L^{*}$ instead gives the tied action, and the tie is the corpus's Hermitian pair:

$$ \mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}},\qquad \text{unit-norm slice: }\ \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow SO^{+}(1,3)\ \text{twofold}. $$

The two cases, side by side, and this is the note's central table.

two-sided action parameters group of the compact part what it is
$L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}$, free two, modulo the rescaling $\tilde Q\to\lambda\tilde Q$, $\tilde Q_2\to\lambda^{-1}\tilde Q_2$ $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$ $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$, Euclidean rotations, untied
$\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}$, tied one $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ on the unit slice $SO^{+}(1,3)$, Lorentz, tied

What the table says. The corpus contains both groups, and the difference between them is one involution. Untie the two sides and you have $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$. Tie them by the Hermitian conjugation and you have the Lorentz group. Physics uses the tied version for spacetime and the untied version for internal symmetry, and the corpus's arithmetic makes the two the same arithmetic.

3. The Two Modules, Kept Apart

The danger. The previous section proves that the group is native. It does not prove that the group can be an internal gauge symmetry, and the difference is the whole question. A group is not a physical symmetry until a module is named. The same $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts on two different modules here, and they are not the same physics.

The first module: the carrier. $\mathbb{B}$ itself, of real dimension eight, with the vector part $\mathfrak{k}$ of real dimension three carrying the adjoint action. Here the group is

$$ \mathrm{Spin}(4)_{E}=\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\ \text{acting on the four-dimensional real carrier,} $$

the Euclidean rotation group of spacetime, and its Lorentzian relative is the tied version of §2. This is a spacetime group. Its indices are spacetime indices.

The second module: the operator algebra as a module. $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}$, of complex dimension sixteen, where the two tensor factors are the two sides:

$$ \mathbb{B}_L=\{\mathrm{I}_2\otimes A\},\qquad \mathbb{B}_R=\{B^{\mathsf T}\otimes\mathrm{I}_2\},\qquad \mathbb{B}_L\cap\mathbb{B}_R=\mathbb{C}\mathrm{I}_4 . $$

Here an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ on the first factor and an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ on the second are internal factors: they act on a sixteen-dimensional multiplet of states and not on a coordinate. This is the module the left–right model and Pati–Salam use.

The naming rule applies, and it decides the question. The corpus's rule is that the group's name stays and the sentence after the name says what the group is in the algebra. Applied here:

module the group is status
$\mathbb{B}$, vector part $\mathrm{Spin}(4)_{E}$, the rotation group of the carrier spacetime, built
$\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$, tensor factors $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, internal factors internal, built as a module
$\mathbb{B}$, one-sided action on matter $L(\mathfrak{k})$ alone, vector-like internal, built, but not chiral

What the third row costs. This is the row that decides everything. The corpus builds the internal $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ as one-sided multiplication, and one-sided multiplication is vector-like: it acts on both chiral letters alike, because the operator $L_{\tilde Q}$ has no information about which letter of its argument it is acting on. The internal note on the electroweak sector states the general theorem:

Among one-sided multiplications, those that respect a chirality split commute with the chirality operator, and that centraliser is abelian. Chiral abelian is available — the centre is exactly that. Chiral non-abelian is not.

So the corpus's position is exact. Non-abelian and vector-like: available, at $L(\mathfrak{k})$ and at $R(\mathfrak{k})$. Abelian and chiral: available, at the centre. Non-abelian and chiral: not available by one-sided multiplication, and needing the two-sided structure with the sides tied to the letters.

And that is precisely what a left–right model asks for. It needs one non-abelian factor per chirality, so it needs the two-sided structure, so it needs the tie side $\leftrightarrow$ letter. The corpus has the two sides and has the two letters and does not exhibit the map between them. One object is missing, and it is one object, not a building.

Why this makes the left–right model score two on chirality and not three. The scoring rule's axis C reads: three for vector-like, where the one-sided action suffices; two for two chiralities in two factors, the two-sided display; one for a single factor that must act chirally; zero for no construction. The left–right model is the definition of the two point case: its two chiralities do sit in two factors, and the corpus's two factors exist. It loses the third point because the assignment of a letter to a side is not exhibited.

4. Three Roles in Physics

The point of this section. The same group appears three times in physics with three different statuses, and the Standard Model already contains two of them. Keeping the three apart is what makes §5 answerable.

role group status in nature broken by what survives
spacetime rotations $\mathrm{Spin}(4)_{E}$, and $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ when tied exact, local nothing the Lorentz group
global chiral symmetry of QCD $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ exact in the two-flavour chiral limit quark masses and the chiral condensate $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$, the pions as Goldstones
custodial symmetry of the scalar sector $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, global and accidental approximate $g'$ and the mass splittings $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$, hence $\rho=1$
gauged electroweak left–right $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ hypothetical its own breaking scale $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$

Role one, spacetime. The group is the rotation group of four dimensions, the Euclidean partner of the Lorentz group, related by the tie of §2. Maximal parity violation in the weak interaction is a statement about the internal factors and has nothing to do with this role. A common confusion is to think that a left–right symmetric model restores parity in spacetime; it does not. It restores parity in the internal factor.

Role two, the chiral symmetry of QCD. With two light flavours the strong interaction has an approximate global $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, spontaneously broken to the diagonal $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$, and the pions are the three Goldstone bosons of the broken half. The corpus carries this: the nonlinear sigma field with the left–right action $\tilde Q\mapsto g_L\tilde Qg_R^{*}$, its invariant metric and its currents, and the decay constant. The Standard Model uses this group, and uses it exactly as a spontaneously broken chiral symmetry.

Role three, the custodial symmetry. The scalar sector of the Standard Model has an accidental global $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ acting on the doublet, and the vacuum value $(0,v)$ is invariant under the diagonal. That diagonal is what ties $M_W$ to $M_Z$:

$$ \rho=\frac{M_W^{2}}{M_Z^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_W}=1\quad\text{at tree level, to a part in a thousand.} $$

The corpus recomputes this: the quaternion-valued scalar admits $\tilde Q\mapsto g_L\tilde Qg_R^{*}$, a central vacuum is diagonal-invariant, and the two mass matrices are proportional. So the group is in the Standard Model twice, at roles two and three, and in both it is the diagonal that survives.

Role four, the gauge symmetry. This is the only hypothetical role: $g_L$ and $g_R$ promoted to local symmetries with gauge bosons $W_L$ and $W_R$. Nothing in the data requires it, and nothing in the data excludes it above a few TeV.

The lesson of the table. "The Standard Model does not use $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$" is false as stated. It uses it as an exact global symmetry of the strong sector, as an approximate global symmetry of the scalar sector, and as the source of its hypercharge. What it does not do is gauge it unbroken.

5. Why the Standard Model Does Not Gauge It

Five reasons, and then the structural one.

Reason one: the data. The charged current is purely $V-A$. A gauged $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ with $g_R=g_L$ at the electroweak scale would add a $V+A$ current of equal size, and it is not there. The measured parity violation is maximal, not partial, so the two factors cannot be approximately symmetric at low energy.

Reason two: the right-handed neutrino. For $e_R$ to be a doublet of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ it needs a partner, and the only candidate is $\nu_R$. A $\nu_R$ is not required by the Standard Model and has never been seen as a gauge state; it is allowed, and its mass is bounded only by cosmology and by the absence of a signal. So the group costs a particle the Standard Model does not need.

Reason three: the group forces the sixteen. This is the sharpest reason, and it is a chain rather than a choice. Electric charge in the left–right model is

$$ Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L), $$

so $B-L$ must be a gauge symmetry, because $Q$ is a combination of gauge generators. And $U(1)_{B-L}$ is anomaly-free precisely when $\nu_R$ is present:

$$ \sum(B-L)=0,\qquad \sum(B-L)^{3}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \nu_R\ \text{included}. $$

So the chain is

$$ \text{gauge }\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{gauge }B-L\ \Longrightarrow\ \nu_R\ \text{required}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{matter is sixteen}. $$

This is why the top three entries of the ranking all have sixteen matter fields, and it is not a coincidence of bookkeeping. The group they contain demands the even multiplet. The Standard Model, containing half the group, is free to be odd, and it is.

Reason four: it is broken, not absent — and hypercharge is its remnant. The neutral generator of the broken $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ survives as a global $U(1)$, and it mixes with $B-L$ to give hypercharge:

$$ Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L). $$

Check it against the observed assignment, and the check is exact:

$\nu_L$ $+\tfrac12$ $0$ $-1$ $-\tfrac12$ $0$

$\nu_R$ $0$ $+\tfrac12$ $-1$ $0$ $0$
$e_R$ $0$ $-\tfrac12$ $-1$ $-1$ $-1$

$d_R$ $0$ $-\tfrac12$ $+\tfrac13$ $-\tfrac13$ $-\tfrac13$

Two readings of that table, and the second is the interesting one.

The first reading is bookkeeping: the hypercharges come out right, so the relation is true.

The second reading is structural. The charge formula is parity-symmetric. Interchange $L$ and $R$ in the table and every entry is unchanged, because $T_{3L}$ and $T_{3R}$ appear on the same footing in $Q$, and $Y$ is by definition the piece that $T_{3L}$ does not supply. So the Standard Model does not violate parity by its charges. It violates parity by its multiplet assignment: it puts $e_L$ in a doublet and $e_R$ in a singlet, and it omits $\nu_R$ altogether. The symmetry is broken by the choice of module, not by the choice of numbers. That is the precise sense in which the left–right model "restores" parity: it changes the module and leaves the arithmetic alone.

Reason five, the corpus's own theorem: the chiral wall. From §3: chiral non-abelian one-sided multiplication does not exist. The Standard Model's $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ couples only to left-handed fields, so it is chiral non-abelian, so the corpus cannot build it as a one-sided action. The corpus's $L(\mathfrak{k})$ is vector-like and therefore is not weak isospin. The Standard Model's $SU(2)_L$ is not the corpus's $SU(2)$; it is the corpus's $SU(2)$ plus a chirality projection, and the projection is the missing object.

The breaking chain, written once.

$$ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{\ \langle\Delta_R\rangle\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{Y} \ \xrightarrow{\ \langle\Phi\rangle\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}} . $$

The Standard Model is the middle term. It is not a theory that declined to use $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$; it is the low-energy description of a theory that broke it. The question "why doesn't the Standard Model use it" has, as its correct answer, "because the Standard Model is what is left after it is broken, and hypercharge is the left-over."

And the scale is unknown. The breaking scale is bounded from below only. Meson mixing and the LHC reach put it in the few-TeV range for $g_R=g_L$, with the strongest current limits coming from the $W_R\to\ell N_R$ channel, so nothing forbids it and nothing requires it. That is why the entry is in the table at all.

6. Why the Ranking Rewards It

The claim. The ranking is not measuring a fashion. It is measuring how much of a theory's group the algebra builds without help, and $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the group it builds at the cheapest layer.

The cost table. This is the note's answer to the reader's question, and it should be read as an account statement.

The three-column reading.

Column one. The element layer's budget is dimension eight as an algebra and rank two as a compact group. Its compact sector is $\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$, of dimension four. So the element layer gives one $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and one circle. Two sides give two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s, because the second is the right family and costs nothing extra. That doubling is the whole reason the group is free.

Column two. Colour costs a layer. $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ does not sit in $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ — it needs dimension eight against four — so it must come from the operator layer, and there it comes only as a subgroup of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. A theory whose group is a product of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s pays nothing; a theory that needs $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a separate factor pays a layer, and a theory that needs $A_2\oplus A_1$ as commuting factors pays more, because $A_2\oplus A_1$ does not sit inside $A_3$.

Column three. Above dimension fifteen the budget is exhausted. $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ at forty-five is not a stretch; it is outside.

So the shape of the ranking is now explained. The four entries at the top are the four whose groups are made of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s and circles, with colour either inside a fourth $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-like factor (Pati–Salam) or as the one paid item (left–right). The Standard Model is below them because its group needs a five-dimensional carrier and the corpus supplies four. $SO(10)$ and $E_6$ are at the bottom because their groups are not made of the corpus's alphabet at all.

And the corpus's favourite small group has a distinguished place in the favourite large one. The maximal-rank subgroup

$$ S(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(2))\subset\mathrm{SU}(4) $$

is $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$, of dimension seven and rank three. So the group of this note, with its circle, is exactly the largest subgroup of the operator layer's colour-lepton group that keeps the full rank. The corpus's alphabet is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and its favourite word in that alphabet is the product of two.

7. What Would Settle It

The observations. Four, in order of directness. A $W_R$ resonance decaying to a lepton and a heavy neutrino, which is the cleanest. A right-handed charged current at low energy, which would show as a $V+A$ admixture. Neutrinoless double beta decay, which needs the heavy Majorana neutrino the group supplies. And a measurement of $g_R\neq g_L$, which would break the parity restoration the model is built to have.

The corpus's two missing objects, and they are the same object.

First, a chiral carrier: a map that ties the operator side to the spinor letter, so that $L$ acts on one letter and $R$ on the other. With it, the model's multiplet assignment becomes a corpus construction and axis C rises by one for Pati–Salam, the left–right model and $SO(10)$.

Second, a gauge-charged scalar: a carrier of spin zero that is not central. With it, the bidoublet and the triplet become available and axis D rises by three for the same three entries.

Both are single objects. Neither exists. And they are not independent: the Higgs of a left–right model must be a bidoublet of the two factors, so a scalar that transforms under both sides is the same requirement as a carrier that knows about both sides.

What the group does not need. It does not need a new algebra, a new layer, an exceptional structure, or an octonion. The group is present, of dimension six, at the element level, split into the self-dual and anti-self-dual halves by an explicit computation, with the two factors commuting and each a copy of the algebra's own rotor group. If a reader takes one structural fact from this note, it should be that one.

Summary

What the group is. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is the compact form of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$, of dimension six and rank two, the double cover of $\mathrm{SO}(4)$, and it is the spin group of four-dimensional space: each factor carries $s=\tfrac12$ and the pair carries $0$ and $1$.

What it is in the algebra. It is the two one-sided families of the vector part,

$$ \mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R=\exp L(\mathfrak{k})\times\exp R(\mathfrak{k}),\qquad L(\mathfrak{k})\oplus R(\mathfrak{k})=\mathfrak{so}(4), $$

with the left factor the self-dual half and the right factor the anti-self-dual half, and the sign of the bracket as the mirror between them. It is built at the element level and costs nothing.

Why it is not a spacetime group in the left–right model. The group acts on two different modules. On the carrier it is $\mathrm{Spin}(4)_{E}$, spacetime. On the tensor factors of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ it is internal. The left–right model needs the second, and the corpus builds it as a module.

Why the Standard Model does not gauge it. Because it uses it: as the exact chiral symmetry of the strong sector, as the approximate custodial symmetry that gives $\rho=1$, and as the source of hypercharge, $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$. The Standard Model is the phase in which $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is broken, and its hypercharge is the survivor. Its charges are parity-symmetric; its multiplets are not.

Why the ranking rewards it. Because the algebra's alphabet is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, the two sides double it for free, and colour costs a layer. Any theory built from two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s is native; any theory that needs $A_2\oplus A_1$ is one dimension over budget.

What is missing, and it is one thing. The tie between the operator side and the spinor letter. With it, the two factors become the two chiralities and the group becomes a chiral gauge group in the corpus's own terms.

$\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})$ the diagonal family $L_{\vec e_k}-R_{\vec e_k}$, the derivation, dimension $3$
$\Lambda^{2}_{\pm}$ the self-dual and anti-self-dual two-forms, the two ideals of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$
$\star$ the Hodge star on two-forms, $\star^{2}=\mathrm{id}$, exchanging the two ideals
$\rho$ the display $\mathrm{Spin}(3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(3)\to SO(4)$, kernel $\{\pm(\mathrm{I}_4,\mathrm{I}_4)\}$
$\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}$ the tied two-sided action; the tie is what gives the Lorentz group
$T_{3L}$, $T_{3R}$ the two Cartan generators, one per factor
$B-L$ baryon minus lepton number, the second abelian generator
$Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ hypercharge as the remnant of the broken $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$
$M_{W_R}$ the mass of the right-handed charged boson, bounded below only

The traps. Do not read left in $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ as the multiplication side, the element label, or the helicity; it is the name of a gauge factor. Do not read the group's presence at the element level as a derivation of a chiral gauge symmetry; the module decides that, and the tie is missing. Do not read maximal parity violation as evidence against the group; it is evidence about the multiplet assignment, and the charge formula is parity-symmetric.

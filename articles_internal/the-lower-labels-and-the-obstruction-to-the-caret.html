The Lower Labels and the Obstruction to the Caret

Introduction: The Question

The corpus now writes every label of three letters in lowercase and puts the label of an object on the caret, $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, $L_x^{\mathrm{axf}}$, $E_0^{\mathrm{wkb}}$. The question of this note is the one that follows: the low slot still holds three-letter upright words, and can they be moved up?

The census says no for most of them. A three-letter upright word follows a low mark 2,500 times, in 82 distinct words, spread over the mathematical, the physical and the internal corpus, and the words fall into four families. None of the 82 can rise, and this note says why, family by family. Three words that name an object and not a part of one were raised while this note was written, $\mathrm{lor}$, $\mathrm{mod}$ and $\mathrm{kin}$, 1,423 occurrences in all; they now stand as $\tilde U^{\mathrm{lor}}$, $\tilde K^{\mathrm{mod}}$ and $K^{\mathrm{kin}}$, and their caret counts are rows of §The Upper Labels, Counted. One case sits beside the census and is counted in §Several Labels on One Object: two labels of the index true of one object at once.

The census counts a word of exactly three letters written upright, in $\mathrm{}$, $\operatorname{}$ or $\text{}$, immediately after a low mark. It reads the mathematical, the physical and the internal articles, counts occurrences and not distinct words, and leaves this note out of its own count. Each family below is given as a table: the label, how often it occurs, what it means, the letter it hangs on where the glyph is worth naming, Written like, the exact form of each occurrence as the source writes it — the five commonest, each with its count in parentheses, then the number of the rest — and Example articles, the five articles that use the label most, each linked to its page, with its count in parentheses.

The four categories are not four degrees of difficulty. One is an obstruction of notation, one is an obstruction of the glyph, one is the case rule, and one is a word that names no object.

The Indexed Label

This is the largest family and the hardest. Thirteen words carry 1,690 occurrences, and 685 of them stand in a field of two parts, the label and an index, in nine of the thirteen words: $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$, $\tilde H_{\mathrm{pos},l}$, $K_{\mathrm{rad}\,\mu}$.

Why it cannot rise. The low slot takes a comma-separated list, so a label and an index share one field and one size. The caret takes one field. Move the label up and the index is left behind: $\tilde S^{\mathrm{tot}}{}_{,k}$, with an empty base and a comma that has nothing to separate. The form is legal TeX and unreadable at reading size. The other fifteen name the sector or the mechanism the glyph belongs to, and here the caret is a live possibility that the corpus has declined: $Q_{\mathrm{Had}}$ the hadronic tensor, $\omega_{\mathrm{Vir}}$ the Virasoro vector, $\Omega_{\mathrm{CAR}}$ the vacuum of the anticommutation relations, $H_{\mathrm{DCB}}$ the Dirac–Coulomb–Breit Hamiltonian, $E_{\mathrm{Dir}}$ the Dirichlet energy, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{EDM}}$ the dipole-moment term, $S_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ the chromodynamic action, $G_{\mathrm{GUT}}$ the unifying group, $\mathcal V_{\mathrm{CTP}}$ the closed-time-path potential, $\vec F_{\text{Cor}}$ the Coriolis force, $A_{\mathrm{Lie}}$ the commutator algebra, $H^1_{\mathrm{Har}}$ the harmonic forms, $\rho_{\mathrm{RGB}}$ the colour density, and the two turnstiles $\vdash_{\mathrm{IPC}}$ and $\vdash_{\mathrm{CPC}}$.

These fifteen are the only subfamily of this residue whose difficulty is the case rule alone. If a capital were admitted anywhere in a label, each of them could rise to the caret unchanged in meaning. That is why the case rule and the position rule have to be kept apart in the conventions: one is a typographic decision, the other is a statement about what the low slot is for.

The two spellings answer to different habits, not to a rule. The comma joins tokens of equal rank: two qualifiers of one map, $\Phi^{\mathrm{id},\mathrm{inv}}$, or a label and a two-letter mark, $a^{\mathrm{fop},\mathrm{in}}$. What the case is not. Three near misses keep the count small, and each is a different failure. One letter can carry two labels and name two objects: $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{imp}}=T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{can}}+\xi(\cdots)$ puts $\mathrm{can}$ and $\mathrm{imp}$ on two tensors, and $\mathrm{tot}$ excludes both. One object can carry two labels that name its halves: $\star^{\mathrm{sym}}$ and $\star^{\mathrm{dif}}$ divide $\star$ instead of describing it twice. And two labels can exclude one another by their own definitions: $\mathrm{fop}$ marks an operator of the Fock space and not an element of the algebra, $\mathrm{opp}$ marks an element read in the opposite algebra, and $\mathrm{inc}$ and $\mathrm{out}$ name the two ends of one process.

The case has no rule yet. All twelve rest on spellings that §The Three-Letter Labels does not license, because the article sends the object to the caret and the part to the low slot, and $\mathrm{out}$ and $\mathrm{exc}$ are neither. The correction is one of two, and each costs something. The comma keeps both labels high, at the price of a compound token that a reader must split. The stack keeps the tokens apart, at the price of an object name in the slot the article reserves for parts. This note records the case and leaves the choice to the two convention articles.

Summary

The low slot holds 82 three-letter words in 2,500 occurrences, and they stay for four reasons, none of them a matter of layout. An index has nowhere to go in the caret. A part that rose would become a claim about the whole. A glyph that already carries a power leaves no room above. And a capital cannot rise at all, because no token the caret carries begins with one.

The rule is the one already written in §The Three-Letter Labels, read in the other direction. A label that names the object goes on the caret. A label that names a part, an index, an operator or an acronym stays low. Where one token would carry two English words, the second reading takes a token of its own, $\mathrm{rgs}$, $\mathrm{tpl}$, $\mathrm{prm}$, $\mathrm{dfc}$, $\mathrm{obf}$, $\mathrm{adg}$, so that both readings stand where they belong. The caret is not the only home for a name.

One case the rule does not reach is counted in §Several Labels on One Object: two labels of the index true of one object at once, twelve occurrences, eleven written one high and one low, one written comma-joined in the caret.

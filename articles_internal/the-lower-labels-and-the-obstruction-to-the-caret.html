The Lower Labels and the Obstruction to the Caret

Introduction: The Question

The corpus now writes every label of three letters in lowercase and puts the label of an object on the caret, $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, $L_x^{\mathrm{axf}}$, $E_0^{\mathrm{wkb}}$. The question of this note is the one that follows: the low slot still holds three-letter upright words, and can they be moved up?

The census says no for most of them. A three-letter upright word follows a low mark 3,918 times, in 85 distinct words, spread over the mathematical, the physical and the internal corpus, and the words fall into four families. None of the 85 can rise, and this note says why, family by family. One case sits beside the census and is counted in §Several Labels on One Object: two labels of the index true of one object at once.

The census counts a word of exactly three letters written upright, in $\mathrm{}$, $\operatorname{}$ or $\text{}$, immediately after a low mark. It reads the mathematical, the physical and the internal articles, counts occurrences and not distinct words, and leaves this note out of its own count. Each family below is given as a table of four columns: the label, how often it occurs, what it means, and, where the glyph is worth naming, the letter it hangs on.

The four categories are not four degrees of difficulty. One is an obstruction of notation, one is an obstruction of the glyph, one is the case rule, and one is a word that names no object.

The Indexed Label

This is the largest family and the hardest. Sixteen words carry 3,109 occurrences, and 886 of them stand in a field of two parts, the label and an index, in eleven of the sixteen words: $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$, $\tilde{K}_{\mathrm{mod},W}$, $\tilde H_{\mathrm{pos},l}$, $K_{\mathrm{rad}\,\mu}$.

Why it cannot rise. The low slot takes a comma-separated list, so a label and an index share one field and one size. The caret takes one field. Move the label up and the index is left behind: $\tilde S^{\mathrm{tot}}{}_{,k}$, with an empty base and a comma that has nothing to separate. The form is legal TeX and unreadable at reading size. It also breaks the rule that a mark has one job, since the low slot would then hold a bare index with no label to belong to, and the reader could no longer tell an index from a label without looking up the table.

What it would cost. 886 rewrites, every one of them introducing a form the conventions do not otherwise admit, and a second exception in §The Subscripts, which would have to say that a low slot may hold an index whose label is above.

The Part and the Role

Thirty-five words, 414 occurrences. The glyph is a complete object and the low word names a piece of it: the interaction action $S_{\mathrm{int}}$, the nuclear norm $\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{nuc}}$, the torsion submodule $M_{\mathrm{tor}}$, the vacuum energy $E_{\mathrm{vac}}$, the solar splitting $m^2_{\mathrm{sol}}$.

Why it cannot rise. The two slots say different things, and the difference is not typographic. The low slot binds to the glyph and reads of: $S_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the action of the interaction, an action that is otherwise unremarkable. The caret asserts and reads is: $f^{\mathrm{sym}}$ in this corpus is the symmetrised object itself, $f^{\mathrm{sym}} = \tfrac12(f(x,y)+f(y,x))$. Moved up, $S^{\mathrm{int}}$ would claim that the action is itself an interaction, which is a different mathematical statement and, in most of these cases, a false one.

The same argument reaches the operator names that sit low because a bar or a sum takes the low slot: the restriction $T|_{\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)}$, the column space $\Pi_{\operatorname{col}A}$, the cyclic sum $\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}$. Here the word is an operator with an argument, and the low slot belongs to the bar or the summation sign, not to the word.

The Word Already on the Caret

Seven words, 211 occurrences. The caret holds 87 distinct three-letter words and the low slot 85, and 15 words sit in both; seven of the 15 are this family, and the other eight, $\mathrm{can}$, $\mathrm{ext}$, $\mathrm{fin}$, $\mathrm{lor}$, $\mathrm{mod}$, $\mathrm{out}$, $\mathrm{sym}$, $\mathrm{tot}$, belong to the index and to the capital.

The first test is the glyph. The caret must be free where the word is written. Four words fail it, because the glyph already carries a power or an index above, and the label has nowhere to go.

  • $\mathrm{red}$, 82 occurrences, 52 of them on $K^{0}_{\mathrm{red}}(X)$: the $0$ is in the caret, and the label would have to read as one exponent with it.
  • $\mathrm{alg}$, 50 occurrences, 16 of them on $D_{\mathrm{alg}}^{*}$ and $D_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2}$: the adjoint star and the square hold the caret already.
  • $\mathrm{inv}$, 33 occurrences, 8 of them on $G^{(\mu)}_{\mathrm{inv}}$: the index $\mu$ holds it.
  • $\mathrm{rel}$, 40 occurrences, one of them on $\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}^{-2}$, which holds the power $-2$.

This is the obstruction of §The Indexed Label read from the other side. There the index is low and the label has nowhere to go beside it; here the power is high and the label has nowhere to go beside it either. A glyph can carry one mark above and one below, and when both are taken the word must stay where it is. No rename helps, because the obstruction is the glyph and not the word.

Three words are not labels at all. $\mathrm{per}$ occurs only as $\int_{\text{per}}$, the domain of integration over periodic configurations: the low slot belongs to the integral, and the word is the integral's argument. $\mathrm{rev}$ occurs as $\mathrm{rev}(w)$, a function applied to its argument, and $\mathrm{lab}$ as $\mathrm{lab}\to\mathrm{mov}$, two words in one field. None of the three is a name hanging on a glyph, and none would survive the move intact.

One Spelling, Two Words

Six words cannot rise at all, and the obstacle is the word rather than the glyph. Two English words share one three-letter stem, so a single token carries two meanings that no height can separate: regular and regularised, top and topological, perimeter and periodic, defining and defective, orbital and orbifold, adjoint and adjugate. Each pair is written as two tokens, and six tokens carry the second reading.

A skeleton cannot collide in its turn. The six new tokens are the consonants of the word they name, $\mathrm{rgs}$, $\mathrm{tpl}$, $\mathrm{prm}$, $\mathrm{dfc}$, $\mathrm{obf}$, $\mathrm{adg}$, and no word of the corpus begins with any of them. A skeleton is not a truncation, so the cause that made the six pairs — a shared stem — cannot reach them.

A raised label takes its power behind it. Where a power follows the label, the label sits in the caret and the power takes an empty group: $\rho^{\mathrm{reg}}{}^{*}$, $\rho^{\mathrm{reg}}{}^{\oplus 4}$, $h^{\mathrm{tpl}}{}^{n}$. The corpus writes a label and an empty group in that order elsewhere too, $\tilde S^{\mathrm{tot}}{}_{,k}$.

A second token answers the collision and not the other obstructions. Four of the seven words of §The Word Already on the Caret stay low because of their glyph, $\mathrm{red}$, $\mathrm{alg}$, $\mathrm{rel}$, $\mathrm{inv}$, and no new token would help: their power stands before the label, $K^{0}_{\mathrm{red}}(X)$, $D_{\mathrm{alg}}^{*}$, $\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}^{-2}$, $G^{(\mu)}_{\mathrm{inv}}$, so raising the label would put a number and a word in sequence at one height, $K^{0}{}^{\mathrm{red}}$, and the reader would have to decide which of the two the exponent belongs to. A trailing power is pushed aside by an empty group; a leading power is not, because the group would have to be opened before it.

The six are in the index. $\mathrm{rgs}$, $\mathrm{tpl}$, $\mathrm{prm}$, $\mathrm{dfc}$, $\mathrm{obf}$, $\mathrm{adg}$ name nothing else in any slot, and they stand in the table of §The Three-Letter Labels of Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions beside the six words they divide.

The Capital in the Low Slot

Twenty-seven words, 184 occurrences. Every one of them is capitalised inside the low slot, so each breaks the lowercase rule of §The Three-Letter Labels before the question of the caret is even asked. Their counts are in the table at the end of this section.

The capital is not decoration. In this paragraph the words are named as words; every exhibit after it is shown inside its slot. $\mathrm{Lie}$, $\mathrm{Vir}$ and $\mathrm{Ric}$ are names of people. $\mathrm{QCD}$, $\mathrm{GUT}$, $\mathrm{EDM}$, $\mathrm{CAR}$, $\mathrm{CTP}$, $\mathrm{IPC}$, $\mathrm{CPC}$, $\mathrm{DCB}$ and $\mathrm{RGB}$ are initialisms, and the capital is the only thing that shows a phrase is being abbreviated. Lowercasing one of them and lifting it to the caret would be two changes at once, and the second would hide the first: $\mathrm{qcd}$ no longer shows which words it stands for.

Twelve of the twenty-seven hold an operator with an argument, not a name. The subscript is there because the glyph has to say of what, and the word brings its own argument into the slot:

  • $f|_{\mathrm{Fix}(\Sigma)}$ — the restriction to the fixed set of an involution;
  • $\mathrm{id}_{\operatorname{End}(E)}$ — the identity of an endomorphism algebra;
  • $C_{\operatorname{Inn}(G)}(\alpha)$ and $C_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(\sigma)$ — the centraliser of an element, in the inner or in the full automorphism group;
  • $\int_{\operatorname{Irr}(G)}^{\oplus}$ — the direct integral over the irreducible spectrum;
  • $\chi_{\operatorname{Hom}_k(V,W)}(g)$ — the character of a hom space, and $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{PSh}}(\mathcal F,\ldots)$ a hom taken in a category of presheaves;
  • $\mathcal H_{\operatorname{Ind}_K^H\sigma}$ — the space carrying an induced representation;
  • $O_{\operatorname{Spf}A}$, $O_{\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^+)}$ and $\ast_{\operatorname{Aff}\cap\mathcal J}$ — the structure sheaf of a formal spectrum, that of an adic space, and a pushout over an intersection of affine groups;
  • $\mathcal R_{\text{Ric}}$ — the Ricci part of a curvature.

For these the caret has nothing to name. Nothing is being said about a variant of $f$, $O$, $C$ or $\chi$, and a caret would turn the word into a power: $f^{\operatorname{Fix}(\Sigma)}$ claims an exponent where the expression means a restriction. The slot is occupied by grammar, not by a label, so the height is not the problem and the case is not the only problem.

The other fifteen name the sector or the mechanism the glyph belongs to, and here the caret is a live possibility that the corpus has declined: $Q_{\mathrm{Had}}$ the hadronic tensor, $\omega_{\mathrm{Vir}}$ the Virasoro vector, $\Omega_{\mathrm{CAR}}$ the vacuum of the anticommutation relations, $H_{\mathrm{DCB}}$ the Dirac–Coulomb–Breit Hamiltonian, $E_{\mathrm{Dir}}$ the Dirichlet energy, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{EDM}}$ the dipole-moment term, $S_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ the chromodynamic action, $G_{\mathrm{GUT}}$ the unifying group, $\mathcal V_{\mathrm{CTP}}$ the closed-time-path potential, $\vec F_{\text{Cor}}$ the Coriolis force, $A_{\mathrm{Lie}}$ the commutator algebra, $H^1_{\mathrm{Har}}$ the harmonic forms, $\rho_{\mathrm{RGB}}$ the colour density, and the two turnstiles $\vdash_{\mathrm{IPC}}$ and $\vdash_{\mathrm{CPC}}$.

These fifteen are the only subfamily of this residue whose difficulty is the case rule alone. If a capital were admitted anywhere in a label, each of them could rise to the caret unchanged in meaning. That is why the case rule and the position rule have to be kept apart in the conventions: one is a typographic decision, the other is a statement about what the low slot is for.

Several Labels on One Object

A label names an object, and the caret holds one token. Two labels can be true of one object at the same time: an operator stands in a product and in a frame, a solution obeys a boundary condition and a source-free equation. The corpus writes the case thirty-one times, in six forms, and it writes it two ways.

One object, two labels of the index, twelve occurrences. The pair $\mathrm{fop}$, $\mathrm{out}$ is the only pair of the seventy-six that meets on one object anywhere in the corpus, and it meets on the polarisation of the outgoing mode. $\mathrm{fop}$ says that the object is an operator of the Fock space, $\mathrm{out}$ that it is the outgoing one; neither implies the other and both are true at once. The eleven of them in Thomson and Compton Scattering: The Biquaternion Polarization Algebra are written with $\mathrm{out}$ low, $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{fop}}_{\mathrm{out}}$, and the one in Scalar Pair Creation in Biquaternionic Form is written with both in the caret, $a^{\mathrm{fop},\mathrm{out}}$.

The two spellings answer to different habits, not to a rule. The comma joins tokens of equal rank: two qualifiers of one map, $\Phi^{\mathrm{id},\mathrm{inv}}$, or a label and a two-letter mark, $a^{\mathrm{fop},\mathrm{in}}$. The stack is used for a label beside a mark that is not one of the seventy-six, $N^{\mathrm{fop}}_{\mathrm{exc}}$, $[S,T]^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\mathrm{gr}}$. The pair of labels falls under neither habit, and the corpus has written it both ways: eleven stacked and one comma-joined, in two articles that do not cite one another.

Three pairs are true of one object and are never written together. In each the corpus writes one label and lets the other stand in the prose, so the case exists before the notation meets it.

What the case is not. Three near misses keep the count small, and each is a different failure. One letter can carry two labels and name two objects: $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{imp}}=T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{can}}+\xi(\cdots)$ puts $\mathrm{can}$ and $\mathrm{imp}$ on two tensors, and $\mathrm{tot}$ excludes both. One object can carry two labels that name its halves: $\star^{\mathrm{sym}}$ and $\star^{\mathrm{dif}}$ divide $\star$ instead of describing it twice. And two labels can exclude one another by their own definitions: $\mathrm{fop}$ marks an operator of the Fock space and not an element of the algebra, $\mathrm{opp}$ marks an element read in the opposite algebra, and $\mathrm{inc}$ and $\mathrm{out}$ name the two ends of one process.

The case has no rule yet. All twelve rest on spellings that §The Three-Letter Labels does not license, because the article sends the object to the caret and the part to the low slot, and $\mathrm{out}$ and $\mathrm{exc}$ are neither. The correction is one of two, and each costs something. The comma keeps both labels high, at the price of a compound token that a reader must split. The stack keeps the tokens apart, at the price of an object name in the slot the article reserves for parts. This note records the case and leaves the choice to the two convention articles.

Summary

The low slot holds 85 three-letter words in 3,918 occurrences, and they stay for four reasons, none of them a matter of layout. An index has nowhere to go in the caret. A part that rose would become a claim about the whole. A glyph that already carries a power leaves no room above. And a capital cannot rise at all, because no token the caret carries begins with one.

The rule is the one already written in §The Three-Letter Labels, read in the other direction. A label that names the object goes on the caret. A label that names a part, an index, an operator or an acronym stays low. Where one token would carry two English words, the second reading takes a token of its own, $\mathrm{rgs}$, $\mathrm{tpl}$, $\mathrm{prm}$, $\mathrm{dfc}$, $\mathrm{obf}$, $\mathrm{adg}$, so that both readings stand where they belong. The caret is not the only home for a name.

One case the rule does not reach is counted in §Several Labels on One Object: two labels of the index true of one object at once, twelve occurrences, eleven written one high and one low, one written comma-joined in the caret.

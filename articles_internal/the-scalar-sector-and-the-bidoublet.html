The Scalar Sector and the Bidoublet: Twenty-Four Real Components, Twelve Eaten, and the Mass Relation That Fails

Introduction

The layer. This article works at Layer B, the frame's field content on a formal copy of the carrier. Its one Layer-A remark is §4, where the algebra's own scalar turns out to be a line in the centre and nothing more.

The question. Which scalar fields do the breaking of the chain, how many real components do they have, how many are eaten, and what survives?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; (\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2):\ 16\ \text{real},\qquad (\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2):\ 8\ \text{real},\qquad 16+8=24=2(21-9),\qquad 12\ \text{eaten},\quad 12\ \text{survive} . \;} $$

So the minimal sector breaks the symmetry correctly and leaves twelve real scalars — and still gets the fermion masses wrong.

What this note is, in the migration. New article $7$ of §12.4, phase $2$. Its conclusion is uncomfortable, but it is not the one the first draft of this note drew. The minimal sector is not too small. It is the wrong shape: it breaks the symmetry and it gives the fermions masses, and the masses it gives satisfy $m_d=m_e$. The defect is a false relation, and a false relation is repairable where an absent field would not be. That is what makes $\mathrm{F2}$ the mildest of the four failures, and the ledger should stop calling it permanent.

The arithmetic that went wrong, and it is a factor of two. A complex scalar has two real components, so a multiplet of $d$ complex components has $2d$ real ones. This note's first draft wrote the product with the reality factor appended but never multiplied in — $4\cdot1\cdot2\cdot2=8$ where the product is $16$ — and then read the surplus of eaten components as a total cancellation. The factor of two is the whole of the correction, and it changes the verdict from "no scalar" to "a scalar with a wrong mass relation".

What is claimed. That the two-step chain is done by one $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ and one $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$; that they carry $24$ real components against $12$ broken generators, so $12$ survive at tree level; that the single bidoublet forces $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$ at $M_{PS}$, contrary to the measured $m_d/m_e\simeq2.2$; that the repair costs a second bidoublet or the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$; and that the bidoublet's two vacuum expectation values are what put the $\rho$ parameter at risk.

What is not claimed. That the third field's potential is under control — it is not, and §5 and §6 say why. That the corpus can derive the scalar content: it cannot, and the scalar sector is the one place in the frame with no algebraic guidance at all.

1. The Fields, with Their Contents

Three representations, and only the first two are needed for the breaking.

field $G_{PS}$ complex components real components role
$\Phi$ $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ $4\cdot1\cdot2=8$ $16$ breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\to\mathrm{U}(1)_R$
$H$ $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ $1\cdot2\cdot2=4$ $8$ breaks $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y\to\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$
$\Sigma$ $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ $15\cdot2\cdot2=60$ $120$ splits the mass relations, and the $\rho$ parameter

The factor of two is the reality of a complex scalar: a complex field carries two real degrees of freedom. The $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ is complex in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index and complex in the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ index, so it holds eight complex components and sixteen real numbers — and that sixteen is the number the next section spends.

The $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ has a hundred and twenty, and that is why it is the expensive one. Fifteen is the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, so $\Sigma$ carries a coloured octet, a coloured triplet and antitriplet, and a singlet — and the coloured pieces can mediate proton decay, which is the fork of §6.

2. The Eaten Count, Which Is Exact

The arithmetic of the whole chain, in one table, and it closes with no remainder.

breaking generators broken who eats them
$\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ $15-9=6$ six real components of $\Phi$
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\to\mathrm{U}(1)_R$ $3-1=2$ two real components of $\Phi$
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\to\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ $3+1+1-1=4$ four real components of $H$
total eaten $6+2+4=12$ of $16+8=24$ available
total surviving $24-12$ $12$ real

The coset count and the eaten count agree, and this is not a coincidence — it is the statement that the two fields' vacuum manifold has exactly the dimension of the coset. But agreement of two numbers is not exhaustion of the fields, and the distinction is the whole correction of this note: twelve is what the breaking spends, not what the multiplets contain.

2.1 The first field's eight, split as six and two. Six are the leptoquarks of the previous article. The other two are the $W_R^{\pm}$: the $\Phi$ vacuum has a direction in its $R$-doublet, and rotating that direction is two real flat directions.

2.2 The second field's four, and the four generators. The bidoublet's vacuum is $2\times2$ and its flat directions are the four broken generators of the electroweak pair — the $W^{\pm}$, the $Z$, and the $Z_R$. That the $Z_R$ is among them is the point most often missed: in the minimal model the $Z_R$ mass comes from the bidoublet through the mixing, not from a separate field, and the spectrum note's $\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$ is the eigenvalue, not a separate breaking.

2.3 What survives, component by component. The first field keeps eight of its sixteen: a colour triplet (six real) and one charged singlet (two real), and every one of them is an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ singlet — that part of the first draft's conclusion was right. The second field keeps four of its eight: a charged pair $H^{\pm}$ and two neutral states. So the minimal sector does contain neutral scalars, and the lighter of the two is the only candidate the model has for the observed $125$ GeV state. The minimal sector is not short of a Higgs. It is short of a second contribution to the fermion masses, and §3 says why that is the defect.

2.4 The count that is worth remembering. $24$ real components, $12$ eaten, $12$ surviving, and $21-9=12$ broken generators. Three twelves that agree, and only the first two are the same twelve.

3. Why a Second Field Is Needed

Three jobs the minimal sector cannot do, and only the first is about masses.

3.1 The mass relation, which is the real defect. A Yukawa coupling needs a scalar that is a singlet of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and transforms as $(\mathbf2,\bar{\mathbf2})$ or $(\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ under the electroweak pair. The bidoublet $H$ is exactly that field, and it is not eaten — it has four real survivors and it does the job. The problem is that one bidoublet does the job twice with one matrix:

$$ \mathcal L_Y=\lambda\,\bar{\Psi}_L H\,\Psi_R+\text{h.c.} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m_d=m_e,\qquad m_u=m_\nu\qquad\text{at }M_{PS}, $$

because the up-type and down-type masses are read off the two vevs of the same $H$ from the same $\lambda$. The measured $m_d/m_e\simeq2.2$ contradicts the first relation, and the second is contradicted by every neutrino mass ever measured. So the minimal sector predicts equal masses where the world has unequal ones, and this is a false prediction, not an absent one.

Two repairs, and both cost. A second bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ gives the down-type quarks and the charged leptons independent matrices, at the price of a second set of vevs and one more alignment to fix. The $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ does the same job from the other side: its colour-singlet component is a bidoublet, and its colour-octet components generate the mass splittings that make the model's fermion spectrum non-degenerate — at the price of §6.

A note on the classic statement, and why this note does not use it. The literature's standard form of the relation is the per-generation triple $m_d=m_e$, $m_s=m_\mu$, $m_b=m_\tau$ at the unification scale. That is the classic statement, and it is not this note's claim. The first-generation ratio $m_d/m_e\simeq2.2$ is used here because it is the one the data constrain directly and the one the corpus already records; the triple is cited, not asserted, and it carries its own uncertainties at $M_{PS}$.

3.2 The $\rho$ parameter. Already visible in the spectrum note: with the minimal two fields, the tree-level relation is exact at $\sqrt{5/2}$ for the $Z_R/W_R$ ratio, with no free alignment. Add a second bidoublet or $\Sigma$ and the ratio acquires a dependence on its vacuum expectation value, which is either a freedom or a disaster, and §5 is where that is priced.

3.3 Neutrino masses. The $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ contains $\nu_R$, so a Majorana mass needs either a $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$-like triplet or the $\Sigma$; the minimal sector supplies neither, and the seesaw is therefore a property of the extended sector rather than of the frame. Note that the minimal sector's $m_u=m_\nu$ is not a neutrino mass prediction but a symptom: it is the same equality as $m_d=m_e$, read on the other vev.

The honest summary of §3. The frame's group is fine and its matter content is fixed; its scalar content is a menu, and the menu is chosen to satisfy the three jobs above. A migration that presented the scalar sector as derived would be inventing — and a migration that presented the minimal sector as empty would be mis-counting. It is neither: it is a sector with the right size and the wrong relations.

4. The Scalar and the Centre

One structural remark the corpus can make and the literature cannot, and it is about where a scalar lives in the algebra.

4.1 The corpus's own result. A spin-zero field is a line in the state module, not an element of the algebra's vector part: spin-zero-and-the-centre-why-the-scalar-field-lies-outside-the-state-module. So the corpus's scalar is a central object — it commutes with the rotors, it multiplies from both sides identically, and it is the one field whose two-sidedness costs nothing.

4.2 What that means for the frame, read against the criterion. The criterion has two parts: a generator is an element of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)$, a sum of the words $L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$ whose length is the rank of the coefficient array, $\ell\le4$; and the group acts on the carrier, the only space a $4\times4$ matrix acts on. The structures that the frame's arguments use are the carrier, the canonical split $\mathbf 4=\mathbf 1\oplus\mathbf 3$, and the two-sided action, and the length is an address, not a grade — Expressing Lie Algebras via Biquaternions, §The Third Sense. The scalar sector uses the first two and not the third. The $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ sits on the carrier's four dimensions and its vacuum points along the canonical line — that is why the leptoquarks and the $B-L$ direction appear in it at all. But its $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ index is a copy index, not an element of $\mathbb B$: nothing in the algebra distinguishes the two components of that doublet from any other pair. So the scalar sector is admitted exactly as far as the carrier and the split reach, and no further — which is a precise statement, and the old one, "no algebra-side home at all", overstated it.

4.3 The consequence for the migration's honesty. Every other sector has a partial translation: fermions as the sixteen (split-permitted), gauge fields as the twenty-one (stabiliser-permitted), scalars as the carrier and the split (line-permitted). No sector has the third item, the two-sided action, for its matter content — that is the chiral wall, and it is why every matter sector is written rather than derived. The corpus's contribution here is the bookkeeping, not a construction: it can say exactly which of the three structures each sector uses, and the scalar sector turns out to use two of them, which is more than the first draft allowed.

5. The Bidoublet and the $\rho$ Parameter

The one place where a scalar choice is measurable at low energy.

5.1 The bidoublet has two vacuum expectation values. Write

$$ \langle H\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&0\\0&\kappa'\end{pmatrix}, \qquad v^2=\kappa^2+\kappa'^2\ \text{(up to convention)} . $$

Two vevs in one field is one too many for the electroweak sector: the Standard Model's single doublet has one, and the ratio $\kappa'/\kappa$ is a new parameter.

5.2 Why that threatens $\rho$. The tree-level relation $M_W^2=M_Z^2\cos^2\theta_W$ holds when the breaking is done by a single doublet per factor, because a global custodial $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ protects it. A second vev in the same bidoublet breaks that custodial symmetry, so $\rho-1$ becomes proportional to $\kappa\kappa'$ times a mixing factor. In the limit $\kappa'\to0$ — alignment — the relation is restored exactly.

5.3 The three ways to keep $\rho=1$, and their prices.

mechanism price
alignment, $\kappa'\to0$ a tuning, not a symmetry; radiative corrections re-introduce the deviation
a triplet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\mathbf1)$ and its mirror restores custodial symmetry exactly, adds two fields and a hierarchy problem
the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ taking a suitable vacuum does the job, but its coloured components bring §6

5.4 What the migration says. The frame predicts $\rho=1$ only in the alignment limit, and the measured $\rho$ is $1.00038\pm0.00020$, so the alignment is a real constraint on the scalar sector: $\kappa\kappa'$ is bounded to be small. custodial-symmetry-and-the-rho-parameter-in-biquaternionic-form owns the details; this note records that the frame inherits the problem rather than solving it.

6. The Coloured Scalars and Proton Decay

The fork of the leptoquark article, now with its mechanism.

6.1 Which components are coloured. $\Sigma$ in the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ decomposes under $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as

$$ \mathbf{15}\to\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1 . $$

The $\mathbf3$ and $\bar{\mathbf3}$ are colour triplets with lepton number, so they can couple to $qq$ and to $q\ell$ at once — a diquark and a leptoquark coupling in the same field.

6.2 What that does. A single $\Sigma$ exchange then gives a dimension-six operator $(qq)(q\ell)$:

$$ \mathcal O_6\sim\frac{\lambda^2}{M_\Sigma^{2}}\,(qq)(q\ell), \qquad \tau(p\to e^{+}\pi^{0})\ \propto\ \frac{M_\Sigma^{4}}{\lambda^{2}} . $$

The measured limit $\tau>2.4\times10^{34}$ yr then requires $M_\Sigma\gtrsim10^{15}$ GeV for order-one couplings.

6.3 The two branches, stated as a choice the corpus cannot make.

branch consequence
no diquark coupling (a discrete symmetry forbids $qq\Sigma$) proton is stable, $M_\Sigma$ can be at the leptoquark scale, and the symmetry is unexplained
diquark coupling allowed $M_\Sigma\gtrsim10^{15}$ GeV, the coloured scalars are unreachable, and the leptoquark prediction of §3.3 of the previous article becomes empty

6.4 Why this is the migration's sharpest open question. The first branch needs a symmetry nobody derives; the second makes the model's one sharp prediction unobservable. A frame that needs an unexplained discrete symmetry to survive, and loses its predictions when it does not have one, is a frame on probation — and that is a fair description of Pati–Salam, not a defect of this translation.

7. What the Scalar Sector Costs the Migration

Three costs, and the first is the one the ledger records.

7.1 The minimal sector gives the wrong masses. §3.1. One bidoublet predicts $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$ at $M_{PS}$, and the first is false by a factor $2.2$. This is $\mathrm{F2}$, and its status in the ledger should be "open and repairable", not "open and permanent": a second bidoublet or the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ repairs it, at a price that is stated in §3.1 and §6. No amount of writing repairs an absent field; a false relation is repaired by adding the field that splits it, and the model has two candidates.

7.2 The extended sector is a menu. §3. Several jobs, several candidate fields, no algebraic guidance. The frame underdetermines its own scalars by more than it underdetermines its fermions, and the asymmetry is structural: the fermions are one sixteen, and the scalars are whichever multiplets the mass relations require.

7.3 The one measurable consequence is a constraint, not a prediction. $\rho=1$ in the alignment limit, $\kappa\kappa'$ small. That is a bound the model must satisfy, not a number it produces.

Summary

The fields. $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ with $16$ real components, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with $8$, and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with $120$.

The breaking. The first field breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, eating six and two; the second breaks the electroweak pair, eating four.

The count. $16+8=24$ against $12$ broken generators: twelve eaten, twelve survive. The minimal sector breaks correctly and keeps a scalar.

The defect. One bidoublet gives $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$ at $M_{PS}$, and $m_d/m_e\simeq2.2$ contradicts the first. The failure is a false relation, and it is repairable by a second bidoublet or by the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$.

The $\rho$ parameter. The bidoublet's second vev breaks custodial symmetry; $\rho=1$ needs alignment or a triplet.

The fork. The $\mathbf{15}$ carries colour triplets that can mediate proton decay; forbidding their diquark coupling needs an unexplained symmetry, allowing it pushes $M_\Sigma$ to $10^{15}$ GeV and empties the leptoquark prediction.

The verdict. The frame's group and matter are constrained; its scalars are a menu, and this article is where the menu is shown — with a sector that is the right size and the wrong shape.

The Scalar Sector and the Bidoublet: $8+4=12$, and Why That Is a Problem

Introduction

The question. Which scalar fields do the breaking of the chain, how many real components do they have, how many are eaten, and what survives?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; (\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2):\ 8\ \text{real},\qquad (\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2):\ 4\ \text{real},\qquad 8+4=12=\dim G_{PS}-\dim H , \;} $$

so the minimal scalar sector is eaten completely and leaves no physical doublet.

What this note is, in the migration. New article $7$ of §11.4, phase $2$. It is the article with the least comfortable conclusion in the whole migration: the minimal scalar sector is exactly big enough to break the symmetry and exactly too small to give the fermions masses. Everything the Standard Model calls "the Higgs" has to come from the third field, which brings sixty real components and a colour problem of its own.

What is claimed. That the two-step chain is done by one $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ and one $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$; that their real component count equals the number of broken generators exactly; that therefore no physical scalar survives at tree level; that the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ is mandatory rather than optional; and that the bidoublet's two vacuum expectation values are what put the $\rho$ parameter at risk.

What is not claimed. That the third field's potential is under control — it is not, and §6 says why. That the corpus can derive the scalar content: it cannot, and the scalar sector is the one place in the frame with no algebraic guidance at all.

1. The Fields, with Their Contents

Three representations, and only the first two are needed for the breaking.

field $G_{PS}$ real components role
$\Phi$ $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ $4\cdot1\cdot2\cdot2=8$ breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\to\mathrm{U}(1)_R$
$H$ $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ $1\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2=4$ breaks $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y\to\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$
$\Sigma$ $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ $15\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2=60$ fermion masses, and the $\rho$ parameter

The factor of two in each count is the reality of a complex scalar: a complex field has two real components. The $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ is complex in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index and complex in the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ index, hence eight real numbers — and that number is the first thing the next section uses.

The $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ has sixty, and that is why it is the expensive one. Fifteen is the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, so $\Sigma$ carries a coloured octet, a coloured triplet and antitriplet, and a singlet — and the coloured pieces can mediate proton decay, which is the fork of §6.

2. The Eaten Count, Which Is Exact

The arithmetic of the whole chain, in one table, and it closes with no remainder.

breaking generators broken who eats them
$\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ $15-9=6$ six real components of $\Phi$
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\to\mathrm{U}(1)_R$ $3-1=2$ two real components of $\Phi$
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\to\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ $3+1+1-1=4$ four real components of $H$
total $6+2+4=12$ $8+4=12$

The two totals agree, and this is not a coincidence — it is the statement that the vacuum manifold of the two fields has exactly the dimension of the coset.

2.1 The first field's eight, split as six and two. Six are the leptoquarks of the previous article. The other two are the $W_R^{\pm}$: the $\Phi$ vacuum has a direction in its $R$-doublet, and rotating that direction is two real flat directions.

2.2 The second field's four, and the four generators. The bidoublet's vacuum is $2\times2$ and its flat directions are the four broken generators of the electroweak pair — the $W^{\pm}$, the $Z$, and the $Z_R$. That the $Z_R$ is among them is the point most often missed: in the minimal model the $Z_R$ mass comes from the bidoublet through the mixing, not from a separate field, and the spectrum note's $\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$ is the eigenvalue, not a separate breaking.

2.3 The consequence, stated without softening. $12$ real components, $12$ eaten, zero physical scalars at tree level. The minimal model therefore has no scalar doublet to give the quarks and leptons their masses, no scalar to be the observed $125$ GeV state, and no scalar to run the couplings. The minimal sector breaks the symmetry correctly and describes no masses, and this is the sharpest single defect of the model as usually written.

3. Why the Third Field Is Mandatory

Three jobs the minimal sector cannot do, each needing $\Sigma$.

3.1 Fermion masses. A Yukawa coupling needs a scalar that is a singlet of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and transforms as $(\mathbf2,\bar{\mathbf2})$ or $(\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ under the electroweak pair. The bidoublet $H$ is a candidate but it is fully eaten, so the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ is what does the work: its colour-singlet component is a bidoublet, and its colour-octet components generate the mass splittings that make the model's fermion spectrum non-degenerate.

3.2 The $\rho$ parameter. Already visible in the spectrum note: with the minimal two fields, the tree-level relation is exact at $\sqrt{5/2}$ for the $Z_R/W_R$ ratio, with no free alignment. Add $\Sigma$ and the ratio acquires a dependence on its vacuum expectation value, which is either a freedom or a disaster, and §5 is where that is priced.

3.3 Neutrino masses. The $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ contains $\nu_R$, so a Majorana mass needs either a $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$-like triplet or the $\Sigma$; the minimal sector supplies neither, and the seesaw is therefore a property of the extended sector rather than of the frame.

The honest summary of §3. The frame's group is fine and its matter content is fixed; its scalar content is a menu, and the menu is chosen to satisfy the three jobs above. A migration that presented the scalar sector as derived would be inventing.

4. The Scalar and the Centre

One structural remark the corpus can make and the literature cannot, and it is about where a scalar lives in the algebra.

4.1 The corpus's own result. A spin-zero field is a line in the state module, not an element of the algebra's vector part: spin-zero-and-the-centre-why-the-scalar-field-lies-outside-the-state-module. So the corpus's scalar is a central object — it commutes with the rotors, it multiplies from both sides identically, and it is the one field whose two-sidedness costs nothing.

4.2 What that means for the frame. A $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ has four $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ components and two $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ components, so it is not a central object in the algebra's sense. The scalar sector is therefore the one place where the frame's fields have no algebra-side home at all — not a line in the centre, not a vector in $B_V$, but a multiplet of a group acting on a copy.

4.3 The consequence for the migration's honesty. Every other sector has a partial translation: fermions as the sixteen (split-licensed), gauge fields as the twenty-one (stabiliser-licensed). The scalars have none, and the-pati-salam-model.md records that this is where the model is least constrained and most adjustable. The corpus's contribution here is the observation, not a construction.

5. The Bidoublet and the $\rho$ Parameter

The one place where a scalar choice is measurable at low energy.

5.1 The bidoublet has two vacuum expectation values. Write

$$ \langle H\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&0\\0&\kappa'\end{pmatrix}, \qquad v^2=\kappa^2+\kappa'^2\ \text{(up to convention)} . $$

Two vevs in one field is one too many for the electroweak sector: the Standard Model's single doublet has one, and the ratio $\kappa'/\kappa$ is a new parameter.

5.2 Why that threatens $\rho$. The tree-level relation $M_W^2=M_Z^2\cos^2\theta_W$ holds when the breaking is done by a single doublet per factor, because a global custodial $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ protects it. A second vev in the same bidoublet breaks that custodial symmetry, so $\rho-1$ becomes proportional to $\kappa\kappa'$ times a mixing factor. In the limit $\kappa'\to0$ — alignment — the relation is restored exactly.

5.3 The three ways to keep $\rho=1$, and their prices.

mechanism price
alignment, $\kappa'\to0$ a tuning, not a symmetry; radiative corrections re-introduce the deviation
a triplet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\mathbf1)$ and its mirror restores custodial symmetry exactly, adds two fields and a hierarchy problem
the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ taking a suitable vacuum does the job, but its coloured components bring §6

5.4 What the migration says. The frame predicts $\rho=1$ only in the alignment limit, and the measured $\rho$ is $1.00038\pm0.00020$, so the alignment is a real constraint on the scalar sector: $\kappa\kappa'$ is bounded to be small. custodial-symmetry-and-the-rho-parameter-in-biquaternionic-form owns the details; this note records that the frame inherits the problem rather than solving it.

6. The Coloured Scalars and Proton Decay

The fork of the leptoquark article, now with its mechanism.

6.1 Which components are coloured. $\Sigma$ in the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ decomposes under $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as

$$ \mathbf{15}\to\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1 . $$

The $\mathbf3$ and $\bar{\mathbf3}$ are colour triplets with lepton number, so they can couple to $qq$ and to $q\ell$ at once — a diquark and a leptoquark coupling in the same field.

6.2 What that does. A single $\Sigma$ exchange then gives a dimension-six operator $(qq)(q\ell)$:

$$ \mathcal O_6\sim\frac{\lambda^2}{M_\Sigma^{2}}\,(qq)(q\ell), \qquad \tau(p\to e^{+}\pi^{0})\ \propto\ \frac{M_\Sigma^{4}}{\lambda^{2}} . $$

The measured limit $\tau>2.4\times10^{34}$ yr then requires $M_\Sigma\gtrsim10^{15}$ GeV for order-one couplings.

6.3 The two branches, stated as a choice the corpus cannot make.

branch consequence
no diquark coupling (a discrete symmetry forbids $qq\Sigma$) proton is stable, $M_\Sigma$ can be at the leptoquark scale, and the symmetry is unexplained
diquark coupling allowed $M_\Sigma\gtrsim10^{15}$ GeV, the coloured scalars are unreachable, and the leptoquark prediction of §3.3 of the previous article becomes empty

6.4 Why this is the migration's sharpest open question. The first branch needs a symmetry nobody derives; the second makes the model's one sharp prediction unobservable. A frame that needs an unexplained discrete symmetry to survive, and loses its predictions when it does not have one, is a frame on probation — and that is a fair description of Pati–Salam, not a defect of this translation.

7. What the Scalar Sector Costs the Migration

Three costs, and the third is the one to remember.

7.1 The minimal sector cannot give masses. §2.3. The model must be extended before it can be compared with the fermion spectrum at all.

7.2 The extended sector is a menu. §3. Three jobs, several candidate fields, no algebraic guidance. The frame underdetermines its own scalars by more than it underdetermines its fermions, and the asymmetry is structural.

7.3 The one measurable consequence is a constraint, not a prediction. $\rho=1$ in the alignment limit, $\kappa\kappa'$ small. That is a bound the model must satisfy, not a number it produces.

Summary

The fields. $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ with $8$ real components, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with $4$, and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with $60$.

The breaking. The first field breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, eating six and two; the second breaks the electroweak pair, eating four.

The count. $8+4=12=21-9$, so the minimal sector is eaten completely and leaves no physical scalar.

The consequence. The third field is mandatory: for fermion masses, for the $\rho$ parameter, and for neutrino masses.

The $\rho$ parameter. The bidoublet's second vev breaks custodial symmetry; $\rho=1$ needs alignment or a triplet.

The fork. The $\mathbf{15}$ carries colour triplets that can mediate proton decay; forbidding their diquark coupling needs an unexplained symmetry, allowing it pushes $M_\Sigma$ to $10^{15}$ GeV and empties the leptoquark prediction.

The verdict. The frame's group and matter are constrained; its scalars are a menu, and this article is where the menu is shown.

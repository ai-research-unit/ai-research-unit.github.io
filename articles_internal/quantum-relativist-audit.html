The Quantum–Relativist Audit: Four Categories, Two Hundred and Sixty-Seven Articles

Introduction

The scope. The four categories that the physics menu now runs in order. Biquaternion Relativistic Quantum Theory carries one hundred and twenty-nine articles, Biquaternion Non Relativistic Approximation of Quantum Theory ninety-six, Biquaternion Relativistic Non Quantum Approximation seventeen, and Biquaternion Non Relativistic Non Quantum Approximation twenty-five. Every article of the four appears once below, with its link, one line on what it carries, and one line on what to do with it.

The question. Does each article sit in the category where a reader will look for it, and does each category hold together as one subject. The audit answers the second question by the first: a category holds together when its articles would be looked for there.

The four verdicts. Keep means the article is in its place. Move names another category. Move up names an earlier category, or an earlier place in the same one. Flag marks an article whose place is doubtful and gives the reason. No merge and no deletion is proposed anywhere. The near-duplicate titles of the corpus — the free and the coupled article of one equation, the element form and the polar form of one result — are pairs by construction, and every polar companion states that it is not a second integral. Merging them would delete the distinction the corpus is built on.

The verdict in one paragraph. Sixteen articles of the non-relativistic quantum block are not approximations: six go up to the algebra interpretations and ten to the head of the first quantum category. That leaves eighty articles in the block, and they are the ones that use a limit. The relativistic quantum block gives up one article and flags three. The misplacement is in the two non-quantum blocks, and the reason is visible in their contents: the relativistic block of seventeen carries seven articles that are group theory, classical field theory, information theory or time, and the non-relativistic block of twenty-five carries two articles of polarisation optics. Those two blocks were built as the classical limit of the quantum ones, and their articles were then recruited by subject rather than by limit. The moves repair that without touching a word of any article.

How the table was built. Each content line compresses the menu comment of the article, which is the author's own digest. Where the verdict is a move or a flag, the introduction of the article was read to confirm its subject before the row was written. The verdicts are judgement, not proof; the content lines are the articles' claims, compressed and not added to.

What is not here. No article was modified, moved, merged or deleted. No claim is made about the correctness of any article's content. The audit is a proposal, and it takes effect only when the author accepts a row.

Biquaternion Relativistic Quantum Theory

What the block is. The relativistic quantum theory of the corpus: eleven articles of generalities, then the four families of the spin ladder — Klein–Gordon, Dirac and Proca, each free and coupled, each with its element form, its polar form, its two variational articles, its two path-integral articles and its Fourier articles — then five articles of wave–particle duality, and six of informational aspects.

What the audit finds. The families are the block, and they stay. The tail is where the work is. Four articles sit outside it, strictly. The supersymmetric article is a non-relativistic construction, and it moves. The velocity-space article is the geometry of the boost manifold, and it is flagged for Relativity. The reflection-and-rotation article is the mathematics of the basis operator algebra, and the antilinear article is the algebra of the real structures and of charge conjugation; both are flagged, and neither is a misfit that a move repairs. The wave–particle articles form a series of five that a reader will look for as a block, and the four external formulations of the Dirac equation form a second series of that kind; both are marked for grouping and neither is moved. The block also gains sixteen articles from below, and they are the general quantum framework rather than an addition to the relativistic subject: the section The Articles That Should Go Higher gives the evidence and the order.

Biquaternion Non Relativistic Approximation of Quantum Theory

What the block is. The non-relativistic quantum theory: sixteen articles of generalities, the Schrödinger family of thirty-three with its free and coupled halves, the spin ladder of twenty articles, and twenty-five of informational aspects.

What the audit finds. The block splits in two. Its opening subsection is not an approximation at all: the sixteen articles of Generalities state the general quantum framework — the state space, the Born rule, unitarity, spin, the path integral, the measurement problem — and all sixteen go up, six to the algebra interpretations and ten to the first quantum category. What remains opens with the Schrödinger equation, which is where the approximation begins, and it is the most self-consistent part of the corpus: the Schrödinger family is the pattern the relativistic families copy, and the informational block is the one the other three categories cite. Two menu-level suggestions are recorded and neither changes a category: the fourteen exercises are scattered through the block and a reader looking for the worked problems cannot find them, and the boundary articles — the ones that state what the algebra cannot do — are scattered in the same way. Both are regroupings inside the block, not moves out of it.

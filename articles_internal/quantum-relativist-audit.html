The Four Quantum Categories: A Descriptive Catalogue

Introduction

What this is. A description of the four quantum categories of the physics menu, as physics.md orders them today: one hundred and thirty-three, eighty-five, seventeen and twenty-five articles, two hundred and sixty in all. For each article there is one line on what it carries. The lines are compressions of the menu comments, which are the author's own digests; where a comment was thin, the article's introduction was read. They describe, and nothing more.

What it is not. It describes; it does not judge. Nothing here proposes a move, a merge, a reordering or a deletion, and nothing here assesses an article's content. Placement is the author's decision and is left alone.

How the categories are built. Each category runs the same skeleton: an opening Generalities block, then articles sorted by the spin of the object they treat — spin $0$, spin $\tfrac12$, spin $1$ and above — and then a block of informational aspects. The two non-quantum categories sort the middle part by the multipole an effect comes from rather than by spin, and say so in their subsection headings.

Biquaternion Universe Relativistic Quantum Theory

What the block is. The relativistic quantum theory of the corpus. Generalities holds fifteen articles: the spin and angular-momentum algebra, the modules and their labels, the path integral, the mode function, the three wavenumbers, the non-quantum limit and the boundary of the model. Then the spin ladder: the free Klein–Gordon, the free Dirac and the free Proca families, and the coupled Dirac family, each stating an equation, then its polar variables, then the variational derivation, the integral form and the transform. Then five articles of wave–particle duality and six of informational aspects — the material–informational split, the relativistic qubit, frame-dependent entanglement, the Wigner rotation, velocity space and spin entropy.

How the families are built. Every family repeats one pattern: the equation in element form, the same equation in polar variables, two Lagrangian articles (element and polar), two path-integral articles (element and polar), and the Fourier articles, sometimes with a combined variational-and-transform article and a non-quantum-limit article. The near-duplicate titles in this catalogue are that pattern, not repetition.

Summary

  • The four categories hold two hundred and sixty articles: one hundred and thirty-three relativistic quantum, eighty-five non-relativistic approximation, seventeen relativistic non-quantum, twenty-five non-relativistic non-quantum.
  • Every category opens with a Generalities block and closes with a block of informational aspects. The quantum categories sort the middle by spin; the non-quantum categories sort it by the multipole an effect comes from.
  • The four quantum families repeat one pattern: element form, polar form, two Lagrangian articles, two path-integral articles, the transforms. The near-duplicate titles are that pattern.
  • The non-relativistic approximation is named for its second half: the Schrödinger family and what follows uses the limit, while its Generalities block states the general framework without one.
  • The two non-quantum blocks hold the classical limit as a subject of its own: mechanics, optics and information theory, each written in the algebra's terms.
  • No article is judged, ranked or recommended for a move here. The catalogue exists so that the contents of the four categories can be read in one place.

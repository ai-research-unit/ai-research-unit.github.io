The Letter Chi: An Inventory of the Glyph

Introduction

The letter $\chi$ now names the generic field. It carries thirteen roles in the corpus and only three of them are fields, so this is the one letter where the field rule meets an occupant. This article lists every use of the glyph, lower case and upper case, with the count of each form and the article that binds it.

The census covers 3057 tokens in 188 articles. The lower case carries thirteen roles, ten of them with a caret or a subscript and three with no mark at all. The upper case carries one role, the complex field, and it exists in a single file: the corpus wrote no upper-case chi before the field rule was stated.

What the article establishes. The four forms the conventions now fix, and why the upper case is written as the glyph and not as the command. The thirteen roles of the lower case, each with the equation that binds it and the articles that use it. The nine articles in which two roles are written with the same bare glyph. The measurement of the Latin $X$, which is what the resemblance of the two glyphs costs and what it does not.

What it does not claim. It does not claim that $\chi$ is free, because it is not. The central grading stands in 51 articles, the group character in nine, the ellipticity angle in one, and the complex central field in three. The claim is narrower: that one letter can carry the field and these residues, provided the caret and the case do the separating, and that the separation has to be counted and not assumed.

The Four Forms the Conventions Fix

Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Elements and their Coefficients, states the rule in one paragraph and one table:

The generic field belongs to the $\chi$ family. A field is not an element, so it takes its own letter and the same two features decide its form. The case carries the coefficient: $\chi$ is real and $Χ$ is complex. The tilde carries the element: $\chi$ is a scalar at each point, and the field whose value is an element of the algebra takes the tilde, $\tilde\chi$ in the quaternion layer and $\tilde{Χ}$ in the biquaternion one.

form what it is where
$\chi$ a real scalar field the central coefficient when it is real
$Χ$ a complex scalar field the central coefficient when it is complex, the majuscule following the case rule
$\tilde\chi$ a field whose value is a quaternion the layer $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$
$\tilde{Χ}$ a field whose value is a biquaternion the general algebra-valued field, $\tilde{Χ}=Χ e_t$ when central

The caret and the subscript name the role inside the family, and they are the only marks the letter takes: scl the real scalar, spn the spinor, fop the field operator, alt the second state, scp the scalar potential, gau the gauge function, flw the flow.

The majuscule is written as the glyph. The upper-case chi is written $Χ$, U+03A7, and not as the LaTeX command, because the command prints the shape of the Latin $X$ and the page would then carry two objects with one shape. The precedent is the stigma: the majuscule of rho is taken by the four-momentum, so the state is written $Ϛ$, U+03DA, §The Observables, the States and the Quantum Objects. The minuscule keeps its command, \chi, since nothing collides with it.

The Latin $X$ is not reserved. This was checked, because the resemblance is only a cost if the two glyphs name two things. $X$ stands bare 1002 times and is never declared: it is a dummy bound inside a statement, $X\mapsto aXb$ in Matrix Representations Conventions, $\omega(X_f,\cdot)=df$ in The Symplectic Form and the Biquaternion Norm Cone. $\tilde X$ stands 221 times in 16 articles, all of them bound the same way, as in $\mathbb D:\tilde X\longmapsto-\partial\tilde X/\cdots$ in Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Dirac Field. So the resemblance is a reading risk and not a collision of names, and the repair for it is typographic, not rhetorical.

The Upper Case

Thirty-five tokens, one file, one role. All of them are in Algebra and Element Conventions, and all were written after the field rule was stated: before that file the corpus used the command, and the command is what the glyph replaced.

The role is the complex scalar field, the central coefficient in the Higgs, axion and effective-potential articles. It appears in the rule, in the four-row table, in the two field rows of §The Fields, and in three rows of §Summary of Notation.

The risk is measured, not supposed. The upper-case chi and the Latin $X$ are drawn alike in the mathematics fonts the corpus uses. The corpus writes $X$ 1002 times as a bound dummy and $\tilde X$ 221 times the same way, so a page that carries $Χ$ and $X$ asks the reader to separate them by shape alone. Three ways to pay that cost:

option what it costs what it buys
keep $Χ$ a shape that resembles $X$ the case rule holds for fields, one letter, no new glyph
write the complex field $\tilde{Χ}$ a tilde on a scalar field, which contradicts the tilde rule a shape that cannot be read as $X$
keep $\chi$ for both cases and label the coefficient the case rule is suspended for this letter no new glyph, no resemblance

In these nine the letter carries two meanings and one of them has no mark. Under the rule now in force the grading is the field family's residue and the field takes the tilde or the case, so the repair is available: write the field $\chi^{\mathrm{scl}}$, which it already is, and the grading bare, which it also already is. The nine articles need no edit for that reason alone. What they need is a statement, once, that bare $\chi$ in a grading sentence is the grading.

What Is Not Decided

Three questions the census raises and the conventions do not answer.

The complex central field. The Higgs, axion and effective-potential articles write bare $\chi$ and the letter is complex there. The case rule asks for $Χ$. Converting them is 145 tokens in three files, and it makes the field inherit the resemblance to $X$ in the articles where the field is the whole subject.

The real scalar field. $\chi^{\mathrm{scl}}$ carries a caret that says scalar to a reader who already knows it is a field. If $\chi$ bare is a real scalar by the case rule, the caret is redundant in 442 tokens, and it is the only thing that separates the field from the grading in the nine articles above. Reading it as redundant and reading it as necessary are both defensible; the corpus must choose one.

The residues. The group character in seven articles, the ellipticity angle in one and the index labels are not fields. If the letter is to mean the field family and nothing else, each residue needs its own glyph; if the letter is to keep them, the rule must say that the case and the caret separate a field from a residue and that bare $\chi$ in a grading sentence means the grading.

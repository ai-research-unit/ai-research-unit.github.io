The Answer

The model is $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ with three couplings that are not required to be equal, and with a spin-0 sector whose multiplet content it does not fix. It keeps the group, the sixteen, charge quantisation, the anomaly ledger, the twenty-one bosons, the six leptoquarks and the selection rule for $K_S\to\mu e$. It drops one thing: the equality $g_4=g_L=g_R$. It claims nothing in place of it.

The spin-0 sector is kept, whole, and only its multiplet list is left open. The corpus's scalar sector is a structural result of the algebra — a spin-zero field takes its value in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module, with a central Klein–Gordon operator — and this model inherits it unchanged. What it does not fix is which gauge multiplets those scalars occupy: $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ are declarations on top of the sector, not consequences of the fourth colour (§1.5). Because the multiplet list is open, no fermion mass relation is predicted, and none can contradict the model.

Does it contain the Standard Model? Yes. Break $\mathrm{SU}(4)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ at $M_C$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\to\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ at $M_R$, and what is left is exactly $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$, with the sixteen reducing to the fifteen Standard Model fields plus $\nu_R$. Below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model, so every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, for the same reason. §1.6 is the proof.

Is it in contradiction with any experiment? No. §3.5 is the ledger, and no entry in it says excluded: every claim the model makes is either confirmed or a bound on a free parameter. Below $M_R$ the three measured couplings are reproduced by construction, with $g_R$ absorbing the difference, and a solution exists for $M_C/M_R$ up to about $10^{6}$–$10^{7}$, with an edge that is a consistency requirement rather than a measurement.

What it costs is the ability to be confirmed. Every remaining statement is either a bound on a free mass or a structural fact already known to hold. §6 separates the four that survive as predictions from the one that was withdrawn and the two that are left open.

1. The Definition

1.1 The group and the content. The gauge group is

$$ G=\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R , \qquad \dim G=15+3+3=21 , $$

with no further factor and no simple group above it. One generation is one sixteen, in the two-piece form the algebra gives:

$$ (\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)\oplus(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2) =(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{\tfrac16}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-\tfrac12}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-\tfrac23}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{\tfrac13}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{1}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{0} $$

under the Standard Model subgroup, with $6+2+3+3+1+1=16$ components. The four entries on the second line are the conjugates of the right-handed fields, which is why their hypercharges read with the sign of $Y^{c}=-Y$; the last entry is $\nu_R$. The multiplets are not a choice for this note; they are the corpus's, and they are unchanged.

1.2 The chain and the two scales. The breaking is two steps, and the two steps are independent:

$$ G\ \xrightarrow{\ M_C\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{\ M_R\ }\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y . $$

The chain names three groups and two scales, and it does not name the multiplets that do the breaking. The spin-0 sector exists (§1.5, §2.2) — the symmetry is broken, so something breaks it — but which of the multiplets does each step is not fixed by the model (§1.5). The corpus's frame puts both steps at one scale; this model allows them to differ, and §3 shows that the difference is bounded from above.

1.3 The couplings, counted exactly. Three gauge couplings, and one of them is not free:

coupling what fixes it
$g_4$ equal to $g_3(M_C)$, because $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ sits inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$; measured once $M_C$ is chosen
$g_{B-L}$ not independent: $g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38 g_4^2$ at $M_C$, from the generator normalisation
$g_L$ free
$g_R$ free, and determined in practice by the matching at $M_R$ (§3.1)

So the strip removes exactly two relations — $g_L=g_4$ and $g_R=g_4$ — and leaves one, $g_4=g_3(M_C)$. That single relation is what makes the model still a statement about nature rather than a relabelling of the Standard Model, and it is also why the leptoquark floor does not weaken (§3.5).

1.4 The parameter count, and the sentence that must be written. The gauge sector has three scales-or-couplings that can be chosen, $M_C$, $M_R$ and $g_R$, against three measured numbers. The count says nothing by itself; §3 shows why the system is consistent, and the sentence that must appear in every article is this: the model reproduces the three measured couplings for any $(M_C,M_R)$ in a stated region, by adjusting $g_R$, and predicts no value for them.

The spin-0 sector is not removed, and the sentence that must not be written is "the scalar sector is outside the model". The corpus has a scalar sector and it is a structural result of the algebra: a spin-zero field takes its value in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module and not an element of the vector part, with the Klein–Gordon operator central — Spin Zero and the Centre: Why the Scalar Field Lies Outside the State Module. That sector is the frame's, it stays, and this model inherits it unchanged.

What the model does not fix is the index structure on top of it. A Pati–Salam theory places scalars in named multiplets — $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ on the carrier's four dimensions with its vacuum along the canonical line, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ — and those assignments are declarations, not consequences of the fourth colour. The frame supplies the room: the centre, the carrier, the split $\mathbf4=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3$. It does not supply the guest list.

The distinction, in one sentence each, because this is the point most easily lost.

  • The sector: spin zero, valued in $\mathbb B_C$, central, decoupled in the Klein–Gordon equation. Frame. Kept.
  • The multiplets: which of the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ are present, and how many. Declaration. Not fixed by this model.
  • The mass relations: $m_d=m_e$ and the rest. A consequence of the second line, and therefore also not fixed — not because the sector is gone, but because the guest list is short.

Why the guest list cannot be derived here. The fermion bilinear is not a gauge singlet,

$$ (\bar{\mathbf4},\bar{\mathbf2},\mathbf1)\otimes(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2) $$

contains no $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$, so a scalar must be inserted with the right indices to write a mass term at all. Which indices is a choice among the multiplets the carrier admits, and the algebra does not select one. The corpus's scorecard says the same thing with a number: axis D scores zero for both Pati–Salam and the left–right model, with the reason given — the spin-zero carrier is a line.

So: the sector is in, the multiplet list is open, and the mass relations go with the list. §2.2 and §3.4 use exactly this boundary.

1.6 Does the model contain the Standard Model? Yes, and here is the proof in two steps.

First step: the group. Break $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ at $M_C$ and what remains is the Standard Model's colour times one circle:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}, \qquad 15=8+1+3+\bar{\mathbf3}, $$

so the fifteen generators split into the octet, one $B-L$ boson, and the six leptoquarks. Second step: the hypercharge. Break $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ at $M_R$, with

$$ Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L), $$

and what remains is $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$. Composing the two steps:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R \ \xrightarrow{M_C}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{M_R}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y . $$

The group below $M_R$ is exactly the Standard Model's, with no extra factor and no extra $U(1)$.

The content matches too, and it is the check that matters. The sixteen of §1.1 reduces to

$$ (\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{\tfrac16}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-\tfrac12}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-\tfrac23}\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{\tfrac13}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{1}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{0}, $$

which is the fifteen Standard Model fields of one generation plus $\nu_R$, with the measured hypercharges. Nothing is missing and nothing is added.

The consequence, which is the reason the model cannot contradict a measurement. Below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model: same group, same fields, same renormalisable Lagrangian, so every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, for the same reason, with the same numbers. A reader who wants the Standard Model back gets it by taking $M_R\to\infty$. This is a containment, not an approximation, and it is the strongest statement the model can make. The price is stated in the same breath: a model that contains another contains all of its successes and inherits none of its explanations.

2. The Strip: The Coupling Equality Removed, the Spin-0 Sector Kept

2.1 What is removed, and it is a single thing. The corpus assumes $g_3=g_L=g_R=g_4$ at one scale. That assumption alone creates the failure: one boundary value, one scale, three measured couplings, over-determined by one, and no scale fits it. Removing it removes the failure entirely, because a relation that is not asserted cannot be violated. This is the whole of the strip.

2.2 What is kept: the spin-0 sector, whole. The strip touches the couplings and nothing else. Spin-zero fields remain exactly what the corpus says they are — valued in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module, with a central Klein–Gordon operator — and every article in the scalar sector keeps its statements about that structure. The sector is the frame's result and it is not under review here.

2.3 What is left open: the multiplet list. The Pati–Salam scalar article names three multiplets — $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ — and computes their components, their eaten count and their surviving spectrum. That list is a declaration on top of the sector, and this model does not fix it.

The sector is not empty, and the corpus has already counted what is in it. For the minimal content: twenty-four real components, twelve of them eaten, and no doublet among the survivors — the survivors are four singlets. That count is the recovered translation article's own trap list, and it is the right thing to say when a reader asks whether "the multiplet list is open" is a polite name for "there are no scalars". There are scalars, and there are twelve fewer of them than the naive count. The distinction is in §1.5 and it is worth repeating in the form that matters here: the sector is in; the guest list is open.

2.4 The consequence for the mass relations, said precisely, because it is the place a reader will suspect a dodge. The model does not predict $m_d=m_e$. It also does not predict anything else about fermion masses. Both statements are true at once, and neither is a claim of consistency:

  • Why no prediction is made. A mass relation is a statement about which multiplet sits in the Yukawa coupling, and the multiplet list is not fixed (§1.5). It is not that scalars are absent; it is that their index structure is not specified.
  • Why that is not an evasion. The model's claims are checkable and are checked: the group, the content, the charges, the anomalies, the couplings (§3). A statement about a gauge group does not by itself fix Yukawa indices, and no theory of this kind does.
  • What it costs. A theory that predicts no mass ratio cannot be confirmed by one, and cannot be refuted by one either. The cost is on the credit side, not the debit side, and §6.2 records it as such.

An earlier draft of this note said the scalar sector was "outside the model", and that sentence is withdrawn. It was false to the corpus: the spin-0 sector is one of the frame's structural results, and the correct statement is narrower — the sector is kept, and only the multiplet assignment is left open.

2.5 Why the equality is the thing that fails, and the angle is not. This is the sharpest available statement of what the strip repairs, and it comes from the recovered translation article's §10.

Run the Standard Model's one-loop slopes to the scale where $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ exactly, which is about $10^{13}$ GeV. The two electroweak inverse couplings have met there — in $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ normalisation $\alpha_1^{-1}=\alpha_2^{-1}=42.4$ — and the angle is where Pati–Salam needs it. What misses is colour: $\alpha_3^{-1}=36.8$ at that scale, about $13\%$ low, that is $\alpha_3$ about $15\%$ high.

So the boundary condition is reachable and the unification is not. The usual summary — "$\tfrac38$ is $38\%$ high at $250$ TeV" — is true and is a statement about a chosen scale. The sharper statement is that at the scale the angle actually reaches $\tfrac38$, the strong coupling has not arrived. The missing ingredient is matter that changes $b_3$, and that is a statement about content, not about the angle.

Why this matters for the strip, in one sentence. If the failure were the angle, then removing the angle's prediction would be removing the failure — and a reader would be right to call that a dodge. The failure is colour arriving late, so removing $g_R=g_L$ does not remove it; it removes the requirement that all four meet at one scale. That is a narrower and more honest repair, and §11.3 states what it still costs.

3. Why It Cannot Contradict Experiment

3.1 The mechanism, in one paragraph. Below $M_R$ the theory is the Standard Model: the same group, the same fields, the same couplings. So the measured values of $g_3$, $g_L$ and $g_Y$ at $M_Z$ are reproduced by construction, not predicted. The matching at $M_R$,

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}, $$

is one equation, and it is solved for $g_R$, which no experiment measures. The model spends its freedom there, and the price is that it says nothing about the electroweak angle.

3.2 The computation, and where the solution exists. Fix $M_C$ and $M_R$; then $g_4=g_3(M_C)$ and $g_{B-L}(M_C)=\sqrt{\tfrac38}\,g_4$; run $g_{B-L}$ down to $M_R$; and solve the matching for $g_R$. A real $g_R$ requires that the bracket be positive:

$$ \frac{1}{g_R^2}=\frac{1}{g_Y^2}-\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{B-L}(M_R)>\tfrac12\,g_Y(M_R) . $$

5. Differences with the Left–Right Model

5.1 The containment, unchanged. $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ is what remains when $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is broken. The fourth-colour model is the left–right model plus six gauge bosons and one relation.

5.2 The one relation the strip keeps, and why it is not a prediction. $g_3(M_C)=g_4$ is the statement that colour and $B-L$ are one factor above $M_C$. It is a relation between two couplings at a scale, not a value, so it survives the strip intact: no experiment measures a number that it fixes. It is what the left–right model does not have, where $g_3$ and $g_{B-L}$ are unrelated.

5.3 Charge quantisation, which is the left–right model's real loss. In $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, $B-L=\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1)$ is traceless on the quartet because it is a generator, so $3q+\ell=0$. In the left–right model $B-L$ is an independent $\mathrm{U}(1)$ and the relation must be imposed by hand. That is a derivation the strip does not touch.

Neither can be excluded at current sensitivity, so the choice is structural and must be argued as such.

10. The Two Confirmations, Stated Plainly

10.1 Does the model contain the Standard Model? Yes.

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R \ \xrightarrow{M_C}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L} \ \xrightarrow{M_R}\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y , $$

with $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, and with the sixteen reducing to the fifteen Standard Model fields plus $\nu_R$ at the measured hypercharges. Below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model — same group, same fields, same Lagrangian — so it inherits every Standard Model success, and the limit $M_R\to\infty$ returns the Standard Model exactly. §1.6 is the proof.

10.2 Is it in contradiction with any experiment? No.

  • The couplings are not predicted, so no coupling measurement can be wrong. Below $M_R$ they are the measured ones by construction; the matching at $M_R$ determines $g_R$, which nothing measures (§3.1–§3.2).
  • The charges are derived and correct. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$ gives $3q+\ell=0$, hence $Q_p+Q_e=0$, confirmed to $10^{-21}$.
  • The anomalies cancel. Four rows and the Witten condition, on content that is unchanged.
  • The leptoquarks and $W_R$ are heavy and free. Their masses are floored, not fixed: $M_X\gtrsim2\times10^{2}$ TeV, $M_{W_R}\gtrsim$ a few TeV. A bound on a free parameter is a bound, not a refutation.
  • Proton decay is dimension nine. $M^{-6}$, unobservably long, and not a conflict either way.
  • The multiplet content of the spin-0 sector is not fixed, so no mass relation is asserted and none can be violated (§1.5, §2.4). The spin-0 sector itself is the frame's — valued in $\mathbb B_C$ — and is kept.

§3.5 is the full ledger, and the verdict column contains no entry reading excluded. The honest form of the statement is therefore: no measured number contradicts any claim the model makes — not "the model is confirmed", and not "the model is safe." The confirmations are of containment and of silence, and both are stated as what they are.

10.3 What the confirmation does not cover, in one list.

  • It does not confirm the group. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is declared; the algebra supports the module, not the identification with the quark–lepton quartet.
  • It does not confirm the leptoquarks. They are permitted and heavy; nothing has seen them.
  • It does not confirm the two scales. Both are free above their floors.
  • It does not confirm the fourth colour's physical reality. It confirms only that the fourth colour costs nothing measurable.

11. Is the Strip Legitimate?

The question the whole note rests on, asked directly: is it legitimate to drop an assumption because the data exclude it?

11.1 The answer, and the five reasons. Yes, and the reasons are checkable rather than rhetorical.

The strongest reason was already in the corpus's own words, and this note did not use it until the translation article was recovered. Its §5 writes the boundary condition as

$$ g_3(M_{PS})=g_4(M_{PS}),\qquad g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})\equiv g_4(M_{PS})\quad\text{(if parity is imposed)}, $$

and its own summary of the collapse is "two, or to one", in that order, with the parity equality flagged. So the corpus did not assert the equality of $g_L$ and $g_R$ as a result; it asserted it conditionally on imposing parity. The strip is therefore not the deletion of a derived claim. It is the refusal to impose parity — a choice the corpus had already flagged as a choice, in a parenthesis and in a table row. A conditional that is not imposed leaves no unmet prediction behind, which is why the strip removes a failure rather than an embarrassment.

11.2 What the strip is not. It is not a way to keep a dead model alive by deleting whatever killed it, and the distinction is the one that decides the case.

  • It removes one assumption. Not a prediction that failed, not a measurement, not a structural result.
  • It does not touch the scalar sector (§2.2), the anomalies, the charges, or the leptoquark content.
  • It does not claim the model is right. §3.5's ledger says no measurement contradicts it, and §10 says plainly that this is not confirmation.

A theory that retreats from a claim each time it is tested and never gives anything up is not legitimate. The test is whether the retreat costs something, and here it does: the model loses its only sharp electroweak prediction and its claim to unification. That is a real price, and §6.2 lists it.

11.3 The alternative, stated fairly, because it is also defensible. Keep $g_4=g_L=g_R$ and admit that the unified version is falsified by the coupling triple. That position has the merit of a theory that made a prediction and lost, and a reader who prefers it is not being unreasonable.

11.4 What would settle it. Not a measurement, because §3.7 shows every remaining quantity is a bound on a free parameter. What would settle it is a derivation: a reason, from the algebra or from the carrier, why $g_R$ should equal $g_L$ at some scale — or a reason why it should not. The corpus has neither, and until it does, the strip is a choice between two defensible positions rather than a consequence.

11.5 The sentence that must not be written on either side. Not "the strip proves the model", and not "the model is false because the unified version failed." The first overclaims a retreat as a success; the second transfers a verdict across a changed claim. Both are errors of the same kind, and §8.6's forbidden list exists to catch them.

12. The Synchronised Variant: Adding the Equality Back at a Higher Scale

The proposal, stated fairly, because it deserves that. Keep everything in §1 and add a third scale: three scales $M_{PS}>M_C>M_R$, with $g_4=g_L=g_R$ imposed at $M_{PS}$ and nothing changed below. The unified Pati–Salam model is then the special case $M_{PS}=M_C=M_R$, and the strip is the case where the condition is dropped entirely. Between the two there is this third thing, and the argument for it is real: the extra segment changes the running, so the couplings that failed to meet in one step might meet in three.

This section computes it. The answer is that the variant is not empty, and that it is not a repair either — and the reason is structural, not numerical.

12.1 The count, before any running. Two conditions are imposed — $g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})$ and $g_4(M_{PS})=g_L(M_{PS})$ — on three scales. Two equations with three unknowns is a curve, not a point. So the question "is it empty?" is the wrong first question; generically it is not. The right question is what a curve is worth: a curve means a free parameter, and a free parameter means nothing is predicted. The computation below decides whether the curve is real and where it lives.

12.2 The obstruction, and it can be seen before any number is run. Above $M_R$ the field content is parity-symmetric: $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, and a bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$. Each $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ multiplet has an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of the same dimension, so the two beta coefficients are equal as a matter of symmetry, not of choice:

$$ c_{2L}=c_{2R}. $$

Equal coefficients mean the difference of the inverse couplings is frozen, because running both from $M_R$ to $M_{PS}$ adds the same amount to each:

$$ \frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_{PS})}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_{PS})}=\frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_R)}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_R)}. $$

So the second condition is not a condition at $M_{PS}$ at all. It is a condition at $M_R$. The three-scale construction collapses to one equation, fixed at the lower end, and the third scale buys nothing for it:

$$ g_L(M_{PS})=g_R(M_{PS})\iff g_L(M_R)=g_R(M_R). $$

This is what the "the extra running gives colour more room" argument misses. The extra running does give colour more room — §12.5 shows exactly how much — but it does nothing whatever to $g_R$ relative to $g_L$, because parity makes their running identical. The obstruction is not colour.

Parity restoration is supposed to be the point of a left–right model, and this variant can only work by giving it up above $M_R$ — which is where it would be most natural.

12.7 The verdict, in four sentences.

  • Not empty. A solution exists, at $M_C\simeq M_R\simeq2.5\times10^{13}$ TeV and $M_{PS}\simeq5\times10^{13}$ TeV.
  • Not a repair. At that solution $g_R$ is fitted, not predicted, so the agreement at $M_Z$ is the input coming back; and $\tfrac38$ holds at $10^{16}$ GeV, which is eleven orders of magnitude above the leptoquark floor and unobservable in every way §3.7 counts.
  • Obstructed by the wrong coupling. The hope was that extra running fixes colour. It does — $g_4$ meets $g_L$ at every $M_C$ — but the meeting that fails is $g_R=g_L$, and parity freezes it above $M_R$.
  • One free parameter, zero predictions. The solution is a curve indexed by $M_C\ge2.5\times10^{13}$ TeV. A curve is freedom, and freedom spent on a scale is freedom not spent on a number.

12.8 The sentence that must not be written. Not "the synchronised model unifies and reproduces the data" — it reproduces the data because $g_R$ is solved from them. The honest sentence is the shorter one: adding a third scale does not restore a prediction, because the third scale is what absorbs the mismatch. The strip is not a stepping stone to this variant. It is the variant with the free parameter left visible.

12.9 The caveat that applies to every number here. These are one-loop estimates with the coefficients stated in §12.3, on minimal content with $b_{B-L}=12$. Two of them would move under a different content: the crossing scale of §12.4 and the meeting scales of §12.5. The conclusion does not move, because it rests on the freeze of §12.2 — an equality of beta coefficients forced by parity, not a numerical coincidence — and on the sign of the gap, which is $-27\%$ to $-38\%$ and would have to reverse. What a two-loop or threshold-corrected computation could change is where the curve lives, not whether it is a curve.

Summary

The model. $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, three independent couplings, two scales, and a spin-0 sector kept whole whose multiplet list is left open. The group, the sixteen, charge quantisation, the anomalies, the boson count, the leptoquarks and the $K_S$ rule are unchanged.

One strip, not two. Dropping $g_4=g_L=g_R$ removes the coupling failure, and that is the entirety of what is removed. The spin-0 sector is not touched at all: it is the frame's — valued in the centre $\mathbb B_C$, a line in the state module — and it stays exactly as the corpus states it. What is left open is the multiplet list, which is a declaration on top of the sector and not a consequence of the fourth colour. A migration that deletes the scalar sector has destroyed a corpus result; a migration that adds a multiplet list has added a claim the model does not make.

It contains the Standard Model, and that is proved rather than asserted. The chain to $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ is exact, the sixteen reduces to the fifteen plus $\nu_R$, and below $M_R$ it is the Standard Model. Every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, and the $M_R\to\infty$ limit returns the Standard Model.

It cannot contradict experiment, and the reason is structural. The three measured couplings are reproduced by construction, with $g_R$ absorbing the difference; every other number is a bound on a free parameter or a structural fact already holding. A solution exists for $M_C/M_R$ from unity to about $10^{6}$–$10^{7}$; the edge is the reality of $g_R$, not a measurement.

What it costs. No prediction of $\sin^2\theta_W$, no mass relation, no unification, and therefore nothing that could confirm it.

And the number that looks like a prediction is not one, stated here because the summary is where it would be misread. $g_R/g_L$ comes out between $0.67$ and $0.94$, and that is an output, not a test. It is fixed once $(M_C,M_R)$ are chosen — which is the opposite of a prediction. What a prediction would look like is a number fixed without choosing anything, and the model has none. So the honest count of testable consequences is zero, and the honest count of confirmed structural facts is four: charge quantisation, the content, the anomalies, and the boson count.

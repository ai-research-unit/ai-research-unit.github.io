The Angles of the Corpus: One Letter, Two Cases

Introduction

The corpus writes an angle with eight glyphs. They are $\theta$, $\phi$, $\alpha$, $\Theta$, $\lambda$, $\Omega$, $\psi$ and $\eta$. One of the eight already carries 91 per cent of the role, and the other seven carry the rest in small and scattered blocks.

The proposal is one line. A real angle is $\theta$ and a complex angle is $\Theta$. The case is not a new rule; it is the rule the conventions already state and the angle role is the last role it had not reached.

The conventions read the case as the scalar sector. The summary table of Algebra and Element Conventions says it in five words: "Lower case, upper case | The scalar sector is the reals, or larger than the reals." A real angle is a real number, so it takes the lower case. A complex angle has a complex scalar sector, so it takes the upper case. That is the whole of the justification, and it is a rule the corpus applies to elements, to idempotents, to nilpotents and to roots.

The corpus has already half-decided the question in its own articles. The Hamilton Pairing: Mass as Modulus and the Complex Angle writes the angle as $\Theta$ and puts it in the index as "the complex angle, $\cos\Theta=Q_t/\Rho$, $\sin\Theta=B/\Rho$". The eigenvalues of a biquaternion are $\lambda_\pm=\Rho e^{\pm i\Theta}$. A four-momentum carries $\Theta=-i\psi$. Capital $\Theta$ is the complex angle there, and nothing in that article is being proposed here; it is being generalised.

The question this article answers is what the other seven glyphs are to do, and what the migration costs.

How the Census Was Taken

An occurrence is counted as an angle when one of four things holds. The glyph is the argument of a trigonometric or hyperbolic function. The glyph stands inside a parenthesised pair with $\theta$. The glyph carries a label that names an angle. The sentence calls it an angle, an azimuth, a latitude or a solid angle.

The rule is a role reading and not a keyword count. A glyph can sit in the same sentence as the word "angle" and not be the angle: $\Omega_{\mathrm E}$ in the Foucault article is an angular velocity, $\delta_{ij}$ is a Kronecker delta, $\alpha$ in $\exp(i\alpha x)$ is a wave number. Each of those is excluded by the rule and each is reported in the residual column below.

The corpus is 412 articles. The counts are of the control sequence, so $\theta$, $\theta_W$ and $\theta_0$ are three occurrences of one glyph.

The Real Angle Is Already Theta

$\theta$ is the default angle of the corpus, and it is the default in every context the corpus uses an angle for. It is the rotation angle of a versor, $\cos\theta+r\sin\theta$. It is the half angle of a rotor, $\cos(\theta/2)e_t+\sin(\theta/2)\vec u$. It is the polar coordinate of a sphere. It is the argument of the phase factor $\exp(i\theta)$. It is the weak mixing angle, $\theta_W$, 120 occurrences in 8 articles. It is the orientation of a Kepler orbit, $\theta_0$, 155 occurrences in 15 articles.

No other glyph comes close, and no other glyph is used for the role across so many articles. The role is already written 216 times over, in more than half of the 412 articles of the corpus.

One use of $\theta$ is not an angle and it has to leave. A fixed background tensor is written $\theta_{\mu\nu}$ in 9 articles, 45 occurrences. That is a metric perturbation or a projector, and it collides with the letter the migration needs. The corpus already writes the stress tensor as $T_{\mu\nu}$, 116 occurrences in 25 articles, so the five occurrences of $\Theta_{\mu\nu}$ are an inconsistency as well as a collision. Both move: $\theta_{\mu\nu}\to T_{\mu\nu}$ and $\Theta_{\mu\nu}\to T_{\mu\nu}$. The 45 are the only departures the migration makes from $\theta$.

The Other Uses of Theta, Article by Article

The case rule is only worth stating if the two letters are free to carry it. They are not free, and the price of each is different. This section lists what $\theta$ and $\Theta$ do today besides standing for an angle, with the article and the count for each use.

The two letters are not equally loaded. $\theta$ appears 11,205 times in the corpus: 3,859 in the physics corpus, 7,203 in the mathematical corpus and 143 in this series. $\Theta$ appears 4,216 times: 428, 3,737 and 51. The angles are in the physics corpus, and the letters are busy in the mathematical one.

The Complex Angle Is Capital Theta

The case rule says a complex angle takes the upper case, and the corpus already writes it that way. What the migration adds is the two-parameter form.

A complex angle has a real part and an imaginary part, and both are angles. The element conventions give the pattern: a biquaternion coefficient is a real part plus $i$ times a real part, $Q_t=q_t+iq'_t$, and the prime marks the slot that carries the $i$. The same reading applies to the angle:

$$\Theta=\theta+i\theta', \qquad \theta\in\mathbb{R},\ \theta'\in\mathbb{R}.$$

The real part is the rotation and the imaginary part is the boost. A rotation by $\theta$ and a boost by $\theta'$ are the same exponential, $\cos\Theta+\vec U\sin\Theta$, read at a real and at an imaginary argument. The corpus says this in one sentence already: The Hamilton Pairing writes that the complex angle is the natural home for analytic continuation, and that a rotation continued to an imaginary angle is a boost.

$$\cos\Theta=\cos\theta\cosh\theta'-i\sin\theta\sinh\theta',\qquad \sin\Theta=\sin\theta\cosh\theta'+i\cos\theta\sinh\theta'.$$

The four-momentum is the pure imaginary case. The Hamilton Pairing gives $\Theta=-i\psi$ for a four-momentum, with $\Rho=imc$. In the two-parameter form that is $\theta=0$ and $\theta'=\psi$. The sign is the article's and it is not decorative: the axis is along the momentum, so the boost angle enters with a minus. The magnitude of the imaginary part is the rapidity, and the article says so at §The Imaginary Part.

This is the same rule as $a'_t=\phi/c$. The temporal coefficient of the four-potential is $A_t=a_t+ia'_t$ with $a_t=0$ and $a'_t=\phi/c$: the electric potential is the imaginary parameter of the time slot, and the prime says so. The complex angle is the same construction one letter over. $\Theta=\theta+i\theta'$ with $\theta=0$ for a pure boost and $\theta'=\psi$. The migration does not invent a convention; it notices that the corpus has one.

The convention is written into the two element-conventions articles. Algebra and Element Conventions in the mathematical corpus and Algebra and Element Conventions in the physical corpus each carry a section, The Angle and Its Two Cases, with the equation $\Theta=\theta+i\theta'$ and the two readings of the circle and the hyperbola, and each carries the pair as one row of its notation table. The rule is no longer a proposal in an audit article; it is a convention of the corpus, stated where a reader looks for a convention.

What the Pair Costs

The spherical pair is written $(\theta,\phi)$ 34 times and $(\theta,\varphi)$ 7 times, in 13 articles. Under the migration it is written $(\theta^{\mathrm{plr}},\theta^{\mathrm{azm}})$.

This is the real price of the article, and it is a price worth paying. A pair of two different letters carries no information about which is which; the reader supplies the order from memory, and the corpus is not consistent about the order — the pair appears in reverse five times. A pair of one letter and two labels states which is which, and the labels are the same ones the article uses everywhere else.

The cost is not only typographic. The spherical pair is the one place where a bare letter has earned its keep for two centuries, and the migration spends it.

A cheaper variant exists and it should be considered. Keep $\theta$ bare for the polar angle and label only the azimuth: $(\theta,\theta^{\mathrm{azm}})$. It keeps the shorter form, it states which of the two is the azimuth, and it leaves the polar angle where it was. The variant costs one label instead of two, and it breaks the symmetry of the pair, which may be worse than the length. The recommendation is the symmetric form and the variant is recorded as the fallback.

What Remains Open

Two more were found by reading, after the counts were taken. The $\theta(x_0-y_0)$ of The Feynman Propagator in Biquaternionic Form is a step function, and it is $\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{hvs}}$, the glyph the first pass had chosen for exactly that role. The $\theta(c)=ucu^{-1}$ of The Z-Grading and the Odd Slot of M4C is an automorphism of the algebra, and it is $\mathrm{J}$. Both are the same lesson: a rule that reads context finds most of a role, and only reading the sentence finds the rest.

The residue after the second pass. No occurrence of either glyph is left that is not an angle, with one family kept on purpose: the rotation-number $\theta$ of $A_\theta$, where the article itself writes the rotation as the angle $2\pi\theta$. The count is 7,079 and 453, and every one of them reads as an angle in its sentence.

  1. The glyph of the Heaviside step. Decided: $\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{hvs}}(t')$. The label is free; the base is the Hamiltonian's letter, and no article writes both.
  2. The letter of the sandwich family. Decided: $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}$, 2,338 occurrences in 116 articles. It is the largest edit in the migration, a quarter of the corpus, and the only one that touches the mathematical corpus.
  3. The rapidity as a name. $\psi$ in 62 articles, $\chi$ in 4, $\eta$ proposed by the other migration. One name is wanted and this article does not choose it.
  4. The polar angle. $\theta^{\mathrm{plr}}$ is the symmetric choice and $(\theta,\theta^{\mathrm{azm}})$ is the cheaper one.
  5. Whether a phase is an angle. $\phi$ carries 110 phase occurrences and $\omega t$ is a phase in most of the corpus. The decision here is no: a phase is read as a phase and the corpus already labels it $\chi^{\mathrm{phs}}$. If the corpus later decides that a phase is an angle, the phase role joins $\theta$ with the label phs and 110 more occurrences move.

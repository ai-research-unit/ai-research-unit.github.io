Chirality Migration

Introduction

The decision. Operator chirality is the side on which an operator multiplies: the left slot, the right slot, or both. The definition is stated and proved in Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra, and its physical reading is in Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Operator Chirality. It replaces every earlier reading of the word in the two corpora.

The question this article answers. How many articles does that decision reach, and which of them decide the meaning of the word? The word chiral appears in 309 of the 2,772 mathematics and physics articles, 4,930 times. It is defined in 41 of them, asserted in 26, used in another sense in 9, and merely cited in the remaining 233. This article is the census, with that split, so that the review can be planned rather than improvised.

Why the split is the point. An article that says what chirality is sets the meaning for every article that uses the word after it. An article that only uses the word inherits whatever the defining article said. So the review is not 309 rewrites. It is 41 definitions to settle, 26 claims to check, and a substitution elsewhere.

What this article is not. It is not a proof and it contains no new mathematics. It does not rewrite any article; it measures them. The only claim it makes beyond arithmetic is the classification, and that classification is the working list for the review.

Method, stated so the numbers can be checked. A count is the literal, case-insensitive occurrences of the string chiral in the file, which necessarily includes chirality, chiralities, amphichiral and chiral inside a compound. The counts are of the files as they stand on 2026-11-03 and include the tables, the displays and the link texts. The class of an article is decided by two mechanical tests, and then by reading one sentence: the tests are in §The Two Classes, and the sentence that decides each core article is quoted in the tables of §The Core.

The Two Classes

Core. The article says what chirality is. Two mechanical tests, either of which is enough.

  • The predication test. The word is the subject of a copula: a sentence matches chirality is, chirality are, chirality means, chirality denotes, the chirality operator is, or asks what chirality is or means.
  • The title test. The word stands in the title of the article.

Periphery. Neither test passes. The word appears only as a modifier (chiral fermion, chiral projector), as the name of a parameter, as a formula label, or inside a citation of another article.

Three letters inside the core. A core article is not automatically an article to rewrite, so it gets one of three letters, assigned by reading the sentence that the predication test found.

Letter Meaning Audit action
D defines the notion: the sentence names the object, as "chirality is the central sign" or "the chirality operator is the cell $\chi=-iR_{\vec e_z}$" rename or rewrite the definition

O names the chirality of another field or category: amphichirality, the chiral medium, the chiral Lagrangian, the Hodge star of the Clifford category no action

The Mathematics First

The premise corrected. The eighteen mathematics core articles are not spread over Part I and Part IV. Sixteen are in Part VI, Synthetic Studies, and two are in Part I, Algebra: The Signed Action on a Clifford Module and Triality and Spin(8) with Inner Conjugation. Part IV holds none of them.

The second test, applied to the eighteen. A count does not say whether an article talks about the same subject, so the eighteen were read. Each was put to one question: is the object that the article calls chirality a structure of the biquaternion algebra and its carrier, or a structure of another category? There are three outcomes.

  • Same subject. The word names a grading of the algebra, of its carrier or of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$: the root involution $\Gamma=H^2$, the central sign $\chi=i\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{ad}}$, the $2+2$ split of $\mathbb{C}^4$, the mass block between the halves, the sign function on the Peirce lines. The decision applies and the word must change.
  • Already the decision. The article says that the chirality is the side, the slot or the cell. No change.
  • Another category. The word names the Clifford volume element acting on a Clifford module, or a leg of the triality triple. The decision does not reach it; at most one cross-reference line.

The arithmetic. Eleven articles and 429 occurrences need the word changed; seven articles and 92 occurrences do not. The mathematics core is 521 occurrences, so 82.3 per cent of it is work and 17.7 per cent is already right.

Why seven articles escape, and for three different reasons. One is the reference. Four — the unpolarized module, the two one-sided articles and the sandwich — already say that the chirality is a slot, a tensor factor or an operator, so they were written after the decision or in its spirit. Two — the signed action and triality — are about another category, and changing them would be a mistake: the Clifford volume element and the triality leg are not the side.

Two findings from the reading.

  • The eigenbasis article contradicts itself inside two sections. In its section Spinors and the Peirce Index it says "The Peirce index is the right index, not the left one; it is not a chirality." In its section The Two Gradings it says "chirality is the eigenvalue of the involution $R_{\vec\Xi}$: it is $+1$ on $S_+$ and $-1$ on $S_-$." Both sentences describe the same operator. Under the decision the operator is a chirality operator and its eigenvalue is the Peirce index, so the fix is one sentence, not a rewrite.
  • The six-dimensional article has already conceded the point. Its Remark (the name chirality) says "The involution is a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ grading, and the word records that. It does not by itself produce chiral spinors." That remark is where the decision should be cited, and the renaming needs no argument there.

The order inside the mathematics. The Two Chiralities of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ first, because it holds two rival senses at once and the five articles that build on the $2+2$ split — the Peirce $\mathfrak{su}_2$, the rank-two split, the two mass blocks, the three-parameter gauge — inherit its labels. Then Chirality: The Conjugate Pair, because the central sign is the rival that recurs most across the physics corpus. Then The Four Uses of Spin, which is the article that ranks the labels and will carry the new ranking. The remaining eight are small edits.

What the periphery looks like. It is not noise. The heavy end is the applied physics of the series — the Standard Model and electroweak agendas, the media article, the Fujikawa and anomaly articles, the Klein–Gordon and Foldy–Wouthuysen articles. Those inherit the central sign from the core and use it hard. They are where a renamed core would leave a wrong word behind, and they are the reason the substitution pass is needed at all.

The five that migrate. The central sign, the Peirce sign, the root, the sign per line and the splitting into ideals are rival definitions of the chirality of the biquaternion algebra. Under the decision they are not wrong mathematics, they are wrong words: each names an object that exists, and none of them is the side. The migration renames them.

The one that is rewritten, not renamed. "The chirality is a subspace of the carrier" makes the chirality a thing rather than a side. The subspace exists, and it is the eigenspace of the operator; the sentence is rewritten in that form.

The Review Order

Four tiers, by class. The class decides the work, and the count decides the order inside the class.

Tier one: the 41 definitions. The mathematics half is read already: §The Mathematics First settles eleven of the eighteen mathematics articles and clears seven. Every D article needs its definition sentence read and, where it names a rival object, rewritten. Two of them need no action, and they are the reference pair: Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra and Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Operator Chirality. Where to start: Chiral Fermions (148, the largest and the one that denies the algebra), then The Two Chiralities of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ (88, two rival senses in one article), then Chirality: The Conjugate Pair (45, the strongest rival).

Tier two: the 26 assertions. Each is a claim to check against the decision. The claims that survive say the chirality is a cell, a right multiplication or a grading of the complexification; those become citations of the reference. The claims that fail say the chirality needs the algebra to be extended, needs a module, or is not an element of the algebra at all. That group is short and it is where a wrong sentence propagates farthest.

Tier three: the 30 periphery articles with twenty or more occurrences. A substitution pass: the word is already the right word in most places, and the passages that call a grading a chirality are the ones to change. The rewrite shapes are in §The Three Rewrite Shapes.

Tier four: the 203 periphery articles below twenty. A spot check, one pass, with the same three shapes as the only criterion.

No action: the 9 of another sense. Their word is not this word. The only check is that a cross-reference does not import it.

The arithmetic of the plan. 41 + 26 = 67 articles carry the decision, and they hold 2,250 occurrences, 45.6 per cent of the total. The remaining 242 articles hold the rest and need no reading of the definition.

What the Decision Does Not Touch

Three uses that keep their word. The migration is about the chirality of the biquaternion algebra. It is not about the word.

  • Amphichirality. A knot is amphichiral when it is isotopic to its mirror image, which is the existence of an orientation-reversing involution. The word is standard, the definition is unrelated, and 76 occurrences in one article rest on it.
  • The chiral medium. Optical activity, the constitutive relations of a medium that distinguishes left- from right-circular polarization. Standard, unrelated, 91 occurrences in Electromagnetism in Media and 59 in the chiral-media Maxwell article.
  • The chiral Lagrangian and chiral symmetry breaking. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ acting on the pion field, the chiral condensate, chiral perturbation theory. Standard, unrelated, and among the heaviest users of the word in the corpus.

One borderline. The chiral anomaly, and $\gamma_5$ in the Fujikawa measure, sit on the chirality operator of the frame and are therefore in scope for the statement of what $\gamma_5$ is, while the name of the anomaly is standard.

The Three Rewrite Shapes

One test. A sentence fails when its subject is chirality and its predicate names an element of $\mathbb{B}$, a root, an idempotent, a subspace, a module or an ideal. Chirality is a slot of an operator, and none of those is a slot.

Shape one: the definition sentence. "Chirality is the central sign" becomes "the central sign is the nontrivial grading of the double; the chirality operator is the side on which it multiplies". The old sentence is not deleted, it is moved one level down: the object stays, the name changes.

Shape two: the sentence that asserts a need. "Chirality is not visible to the algebra alone", "chirality needs a module", "the chirality requires a root". Each is now false and is deleted outright, with one replacement sentence: the chirality operator is a cell, $\gamma_5=-iR_{\vec e_z}$.

Shape three: the coincidence. "The Peirce sign is the chirality." It becomes: the Peirce sign equals $-\gamma_5$ for the root aligned with the volume direction, and for no other root. The statement keeps its content and loses the identification.

What not to do. Do not replace the word mechanically. A blind substitution turns "the chirality is the Peirce sign" into "the side is the Peirce sign", which is false, and it turns "a chirality is a choice of signs" into a sentence with no meaning. Every one of the 41 D articles needs the sentence read before the word is changed.

Limits

  • The class is a reading of one sentence. The two tests find the article; the letter comes from the first sentence the predication test returns. An article whose first such sentence is incidental, and whose real definition is elsewhere, would be mislettered. The tables quote that sentence, so the error is visible and cheap to correct.
  • The counts are of the file, not of the mathematics. A formula that prints $\gamma_5$ does not carry the word and is not counted, so an article can hold much more chirality than its row shows.
  • The predication test has a blind spot. An article can define the word by use alone — "the mass block is the space of maps from the right chirality to the left chirality" defines both halves without a copula. Four articles were caught by the title test and one by reading; others may be missed.
  • The tiers are a plan, not a measurement of error. A periphery article can still contain the one sentence that matters, and the spot check exists for that.
  • The letters rank the work, not the importance of the article. A two-occurrence D article outranks a hundred-occurrence P article in the queue.

Summary

The decision defines chirality as the side on which an operator multiplies. It reaches 309 articles and 4,930 occurrences: 133 mathematics articles with 1,278 and 176 physics articles with 3,652.

The corpus splits in two. Core, 76 articles and 2,579 occurrences, is where the article says what chirality is. Periphery, 233 articles and 2,351 occurrences, is where the word is only used. Inside the core, 41 articles define the word, 26 assert a claim about it, and 9 name the chirality of another field.

Of the 41 definitions, the eighteen mathematics articles were read first: eleven need the word changed and seven do not, the seven being the reference, four that already say the chirality is a slot, and two that belong to another category.

The audit is therefore 41 definitions to settle, 26 claims to check, and a substitution pass over 30 heavy periphery articles with 203 to spot check. The nine senses reduce to five rival definitions to rename and one sentence to rewrite; the other three senses are other words and stay.

The criterion is one line: a sentence fails when it makes chirality a thing of the algebra rather than a side of an operator.

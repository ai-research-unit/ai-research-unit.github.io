The Boson Spectrum and the Three Neutral States: $9$ Massless, $12$ Massive

Introduction

The question. The frame has twenty-one gauge bosons. Which are massless at the end of the chain, which are massive, and by what mechanism does a photon survive out of three neutral fields?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; 21=9+12,\qquad 9=\underbrace{8}_{\text{gluons}}+\underbrace{1}_{\gamma}, \qquad 12=\underbrace{6}_{\text{leptoquarks}}+\underbrace{2}_{W^{\pm}}+\underbrace{2}_{W_R^{\pm}}+\underbrace{1}_{Z}+\underbrace{1}_{Z_R} \;} $$

with the photon coming out as the one null direction of a $3\times3$ neutral mass matrix whose ratios are exact.

What this note is, in the migration. New article $4$ of §12.4, phase $1$. It takes the frame from the-pati-salam-group-in-the-biquaternion-framework.md and the chain from the-breaking-chain-in-pati-salam-form.md, and it is the article the whole "three interactions" translation leans on: the strong, the electroweak and the electromagnetic sections of the particle category are three statements about rows of the table in §2.

What is claimed. That the count $9+12$ follows from the two-step budget with no dynamics; that the neutral sector is a $3\times3$ matrix of rank two; that its null vector is the photon with composition $(1,1,\sqrt{2/3})$; and that the two nonzero eigenvalues are $\tfrac14(g_L^2+g_Y^2)v^2$ and $\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$, both exact at tree level in the minimal vacuum.

What is not claimed. That these are the observed masses. They are tree-level relations in a vacuum the corpus has chosen, they move with the scalar content, and §7 says which of them are robust.

The two zeros in the mass column are the two rows that never get a mass, and they are zero for different reasons. The gluons are zero because $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is a stabiliser at every scale — an unbroken non-abelian group. The photon is zero because $U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ is the residual of the whole chain — it is the one combination of three fields that annihilates every vacuum in §1 of the chain note, and that is a statement about eigenvectors, not about stabilisers.

The six leptoquarks are the only coloured massive states in the theory. That is worth stating plainly: colour is never broken, and the objects that carry colour and mass at once are exactly the six that mix the unit line with $B_V$.

The charge column is the check. Every charge follows from $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ and no charge is put in by hand:

$$ Q(X)=0+0+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac43=+\tfrac23, \qquad Q(W^{\pm})=\pm1+0+0=\pm1, \qquad Q(\gamma)=0 . $$

2. The Count, Which Is Not Dynamics

The budget, from the chain note, read as a table of masses.

stage unbroken group dim massless massive newly massive
above $M_{PS}$ $G_{PS}$ $21$ $21$ $0$ —
below $M_{PS}$ $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ $15$ $15$ $6$ the six leptoquarks
below $M_{R}$ $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ $12$ $12$ $9$ $W_R^{\pm}$, $Z_R$
below $M_{W}$ $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ $9$ $9$ $12$ $W^{\pm}$, $Z$

The check is that the last row's massless count equals the dimension of the surviving group. $9=8+1$, and $8+1$ is the dimension of $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$. This is not a coincidence and it is not dynamics: the number of massless gauge bosons equals the dimension of the unbroken group, always, because the Higgs mechanism eats exactly one Goldstone per broken generator.

So the count $9+12$ is a statement about group dimensions, and it would be a scandal if it failed. It closes:

$$ 21-12=9,\qquad 12-9=3,\qquad 21-15=6, $$

and $6+3+3=12$.

Why the count is still worth writing down. Because the assignment is not a count: which six become massive at $M_{PS}$, and which three at $M_{R}$, depends on which vacuum is chosen. A frame could have the right totals and the wrong rows — for instance if a leptoquark stayed massless, colour would be broken and the chain would be different. The rows of §1's table are the content; the totals are only the arithmetic check on them.

3. Why Three Neutral States

The neutral sector is three-dimensional before the first breaking, and it stays three-dimensional until the last.

3.1 The counting. Neutral gauge bosons are those with $Q=0$: $W_L^3$, $W_R^3$, and the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Cartan direction that survives the first breaking. The other two $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Cartans are the gluons $T_3$, $T_8$, which are neutral but are colour and never mix with anything, because colour is unbroken. So the mixing sector is exactly three fields:

$$ \{W^3_L,\ W^3_R,\ B\},\qquad B\equiv A^{15}. $$

3.2 Why they mix at all. Each of the two vacua is annihilated by a different combination. The first vacuum, along the unit line, is annihilated by $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$; the second, along the doublet direction, by $Q=T_{3L}+Y$. Two conditions on three fields leave exactly one direction, and that direction is the photon. This is the whole of the neutral sector in one sentence.

3.3 The matrix. With the two vacuum vectors $a_1,a_2\in\mathbb R^3$ written in the basis of couplings,

$$ \mathcal M^2=\sum_{i=1,2}v_i^2\,a_ia_i^{\mathsf T}, \qquad a_1=\bigl(0,-\tfrac12,\sqrt{\tfrac38}\bigr)v_R, \qquad a_2=\bigl(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,0\bigr)v , $$

where the entries are $(g_Lq_L,\,g_Rq_R,\,g_4q_{15})$ and the $B-L$ generator is normalised so that its eigenvalue on the unit line is $\sqrt{\tfrac38}$.

3.4 The rank, and the theorem it is an instance of. $\mathcal M^2$ is a sum of two rank-one matrices, so its rank is at most two, so it has at least one zero eigenvalue. It has exactly one, because $a_1$ and $a_2$ are independent ($a_1\cdot a_2=\tfrac14v_Rv\ne0$ but they are not parallel). So: one massless neutral, two massive. Always, for any two independent vacua. A frame with two neutral vacua and no massless neutral would violate this, and that is the structural reason the photon is not optional.

4. The Photon, Exactly

The null vector, and the exact ratios it produces.

$$ a_1\times a_2\ \propto\ \bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac38},\ \sqrt{\tfrac38},\ \tfrac12\bigr) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad A_\mu\ \propto\ W^3_{L\mu}+W^3_{R\mu}+\sqrt{\tfrac23}\,B_\mu . $$

The three coefficients are $(1,\,1,\,\sqrt{2/3})$, and every one of them is a fraction of the same three numbers $1,1,\tfrac23$ that appeared in the coupling sum of the chain note:

$$ \frac{1}{e^{2}}=\frac{1}{g_L^{2}}+\frac{1}{g_R^{2}}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^{2}} \;\;\xrightarrow{\ g_L=g_R=g_4\ } \; \frac{1}{g_4^{2}}\Bigl(1+1+\tfrac23\Bigr)=\frac{8}{3g_4^{2}} . $$

So the same triple appears twice, once as normalised amplitudes and once as inverse-square couplings, and that is not a coincidence: the photon's amplitude in each field is proportional to that field's coupling, because the photon is the canonically normalised combination.

Verified: the null vector of $\mathcal M^2$, computed numerically with $v_R/v=10^{4}$ and $g_L=g_R=g_4=1$, is $(0.612372,\,0.612372,\,0.5)$, whose ratios are $(1,\,1,\,0.816497)$ against a predicted $\sqrt{2/3}=0.816497$. Agreement to nine digits.

What that means for the corpus, stated carefully. The photon is a mixture of three gauge fields, two of them from $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factors and one from the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. Maxwell's equations are unchanged — they are exact and know nothing of the mixture — but the identity of the field they govern is this combination. That is the one sentence the electromagnetic section of the particle category has to add, and it is why that section is placed inside the particle category rather than beside it.

5. The Two Massive Neutrals

Both eigenvalues, exactly, in the minimal vacuum.

5.1 The light one is the Standard Model $Z$, and it lands on the Standard Model value.

$$ M_Z^2=\tfrac14\bigl(g_L^2+g_Y^2\bigr)v^2 . $$

Verified: with $g_L=g_4=1$ and $g_Y^2=\tfrac35g_4^2$, the light eigenvalue of $\mathcal M^2$ is $0.4\,v^2$ against a predicted $\tfrac14(1+\tfrac35)=\tfrac25=0.4$. So the frame reproduces the Standard Model's neutral mass relation, not merely its group.

5.2 The heavy one is $Z_R$, and its coefficient is $\tfrac58$.

$$ M_{Z_R}^2=\tfrac58\,g_R^2v_R^2\qquad(g_R=g_4) . $$

Verified: the heavy eigenvalue at $v\to0$ is $0.625\,g^2v_R^2=\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$, and the $\tfrac58$ is nothing but $|a_1|^2=\tfrac14+\tfrac38$: one quarter from $T_{3R}$ and three eighths from the $B-L$ normalisation. The mixture does the rest, and the result is that the $Z_R$ is heavier than the $W_R$ by a fixed factor.

6. The Ratios, Which Are the Predictions

At tree level, in the minimal vacuum, and with $g_L=g_R=g_4$ at $M_{PS}$:

$$ M_W^2=\tfrac14g^2v^2,\qquad M_{W_R}^2=\tfrac14g^2v_R^2,\qquad M_X^2=\tfrac12g_4^2v_R^2,\qquad M_{Z_R}^2=\tfrac58g^2v_R^2 , $$

so that

$$ \boxed{\; \frac{M_{W_R}}{M_W}=\frac{v_R}{v}, \qquad \frac{M_X}{M_{W_R}}=\sqrt2, \qquad \frac{M_{Z_R}}{M_{W_R}}=\sqrt{\tfrac52}=1.5811 , \;} $$

Three exact numbers, and each one is a prediction of the vacuum rather than of the group. That distinction matters: the group fixes that there are six leptoquarks and three heavy neutrals; the vacuum fixes that the leptoquark is $\sqrt2$ times the $W_R$ and the $Z_R$ is $\sqrt{2.5}$ times it. Change the scalar sector — add a triplet, add a $(15,2,2)$ — and the ratios move while the count does not.

The first ratio is the one experiment can reach. $M_{W_R}/M_W=v_R/v$ means $M_{W_R}\approx 10^{3}$ TeV corresponds to $v_R/v\approx1.3\times10^{7}$, and the leptoquark sits at $\sqrt2$ times that. So a $W_R$ discovery at a few TeV would fix $v_R$ and with it the whole spectrum, in the minimal vacuum, with no freedom left.

7. What Is Robust and What Is Not

Four claims of very different strength, and the migration must not blur them.

7.1 Robust: the count. $9$ massless, $12$ massive, and the identity massless $=\dim$(unbroken group). This holds for any vacuum, any scalar content, and any scale ordering. It is group theory.

7.2 Robust: the photon's composition. A mixture of $W_L^3$, $W_R^3$ and $B$ with coefficients proportional to the couplings. This holds whenever the three fields are present and two independent neutral vacua are chosen, and it is what makes $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ at the scale. It does not depend on the scalar content.

7.3 Conditional: the ratios of §6. They hold in the minimal vacuum, with the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ and the bidoublet, and with $g_L=g_R$ at $M_{PS}$. Add a $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ that takes a vacuum expectation value and the $Z_R$–$W_R$ ratio moves.

7.4 Not robust at all: the absolute scale. $v_R$ is bounded from below by the leptoquark search and from above by nothing in the frame. The spectrum's shape is predicted; its overall size is not.

7.5 And one loop changes all of it. These are tree-level relations. The $\rho$ parameter, the $Z$–$W$ mass ratio and the $Z_R$ mass all take radiative corrections, and in a model with a bidoublet the tree-level $\rho=1$ is an alignment statement rather than a theorem. articles_physics/custodial-symmetry-and-the-rho-parameter-in-biquaternionic-form.md is the article that owns that question, and this note does not pre-empt it.

8. What the Spectrum Buys

Three things, and they are the reason this article exists.

8.1 It makes the three sections of the particle category three rows of one table. The strong section is the eight massless coloured states and why colour is a stabiliser. The electroweak section is the four states that get mass at $M_{W}$ and the mixing that produces them. The electromagnetic section is the one null direction and its composition. None of the three can be written without the other two, which is exactly the claim the migration makes about the menu.

8.2 It gives the leptoquark section its numbers. $M_X=\sqrt2M_{W_R}$ and the $K_L\to\mu e$ bound are the whole of the-leptoquark-sector-and-its-bounds.md.

8.3 It kills one possible confusion. A reader may think the photon is "the $U(1)$ boson". It is not: the $U(1)$ boson is $B$, it is massive-adjacent and mixes, and the photon is the mixture that survives. The table in §1 is the cure, and it is why the word "photon" is written against a row with three parents rather than against a group.

Summary

The spectrum. $21=9+12$: eight gluons and one photon massless; six leptoquarks, $W^{\pm}$, $Z$, $W_R^{\pm}$, $Z_R$ massive.

The neutral sector. Three fields $\{W_L^3,W_R^3,B\}$, a $3\times3$ mass matrix of rank two, one null direction, two massive.

The photon. $W_L^3+W_R^3+\sqrt{2/3}\,B$, and $1/e^2=(1+1+\tfrac23)/g_4^2=\tfrac{8}{3g_4^2}$, so $e^2=\tfrac38g_4^2$.

The masses. $M_W^2=\tfrac14g^2v^2$, $M_Z^2=\tfrac14(g_L^2+g_Y^2)v^2$, $M_{W_R}^2=\tfrac14g^2v_R^2$, $M_X^2=\tfrac12g_4^2v_R^2$, $M_{Z_R}^2=\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$.

The ratios. $M_{W_R}/M_W=v_R/v$; $M_X/M_{W_R}=\sqrt2$; $M_{Z_R}/M_{W_R}=\sqrt{2.5}$.

The strength. The count and the photon's composition are robust; the ratios are conditional on the minimal vacuum; the overall scale is bounded only from below.

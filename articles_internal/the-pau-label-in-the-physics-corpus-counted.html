The Pau Label in the Physics Corpus, Counted

Introduction

The regular matrices of the physics corpus are displayed in two frames, and each frame carries its label in the caret: $\mathrm{pau}$ for the Peirce frame, whose blocks are the Pauli combination $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_k)=-i\sigma_k$ taken once per tensor factor, and $\mathrm{axf}$ for the axis-free frame. The label names the display and not the object behind it: the multiplication is $L_{\tilde Q}$ and takes no label, so an occurrence is counted only where the label sits on a matrix glyph, $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, $R_{\vec e_\nu}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, $L_{\vec e_z\vec e_{\tau(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, on a general matrix $L^{\mathrm{pau}}(\tilde Q)$, or on a power of one, $(L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2$.

What the label is written on. Four forms carry it. A unit, $e_t$, $e_\mu$, $\vec e_x$ to $\vec e_n$. A unit expression: a signed unit $R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, a square $R_{\vec e_z^{\,2}}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, a product $R_{\vec e_y\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, a sum $R_{e_t+i\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, or the identity $L_1^{\mathrm{pau}}$. The general matrix, $L^{\mathrm{pau}}(\tilde Q)$. A power of a labelled glyph, wrapped so that the label is not raised with it, $(L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2$. A glyph whose subscript is a general element, $L_a$, $R_b$, $L_\omega$, or a derivative, $L_{\tilde\nabla}$, stays bare: those name the multiplication on the algebra and not its matrix in either frame.

The count. 99 articles carry the label and they hold 7627 occurrences. A further 4 occurrences name the label without carrying it, in the sentences that define it, so the string totals 7631. The count is the literal caret group: a matrix written twice in two formulas is counted twice, and a cell $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_\nu}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ contributes two. The list is complete: the physics folder holds 409 articles, the 99 rows of the table are exactly those that carry the label, and the other 310 carry none.

The two sides. Every occurrence sits on a glyph, so the label splits between the two sides without a remainder: 3184 are written on an $L$ glyph and 4443 on an $R$ glyph, and the two columns of the table add to the total in every row. A cell $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_\nu}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ gives one of each, the general matrix $L^{\mathrm{pau}}(\tilde Q)$ gives one on the left alone, and a power $(R_{\vec e_z^{\,2}}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2$ is counted once, on its glyph. The right side is the larger one, and the surplus does not come from the cells, which contribute one glyph to each column: 1534 right glyphs are not immediately followed by another labelled glyph, against 323 left ones, so the excess is in the matrices written on their own.

The shape. The mean is 77.0 occurrences an article and the median 50, so a short head carries the mass: the top ten articles hold 2742 occurrences, 36.0 per cent of the total, and the largest is Matrix Representations Conventions. At the other end 6 articles carry fewer than ten, where the label is a name stated once and then read: Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Operator Chirality, $8$; Unit, Index and Notation Conventions, $2$; Algebra and Element Conventions, $2$; The Unit Group, the Cone and the Orders, $1$; The Four General Products: Composition, Probability, Causality, Gauge, $1$; Element and Operator Representations: The Three Actions, $1$.

The closing section. The last column records whether the article ends on gamma matters: yes when the last thematic section before Summary is The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation, which is the practice of the corpus and holds in 93 of the 99. The six exceptions fall into two groups. Five have no gamma section at all and close on a section of notation, of quaternions or of limits: Algebra and Element Conventions (The Most Common Quaternions and Biquaternions), Unit, Index and Notation Conventions (The Subscripts), and Element and Operator Representations: The Three Actions, The Four General Products: Composition, Probability, Causality, Gauge and The Unit Group, the Cone and the Orders (on Limits). One has gamma sections but not in the closing place: Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices reads What the Gamma Matrices Are and Where the Gamma Formalism Remains the Right Tool and closes on What Changes and What Does Not.

The two sections added in this pass. The Classical Coulomb Problem and Its Hidden SO(4) Symmetry and Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Operator Chirality now end on the section, and the dictionary they carry is one display each. The first reads the six multipliers of its symmetry algebra in the frame: the three left ones are the spacelike bivectors, $L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}=\gamma_2\gamma_3$, $L_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=\gamma_3\gamma_1$ and $L_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}=\gamma_1\gamma_2$, while the three right ones are not bivectors, so its $\mathrm{SU}(2)\oplus\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is not the self-dual split. The second reads the Peirce sign against the chirality operator: $P=R_{i\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}=-\gamma_5$, the same array up to sign, on two different modules.

One section moved in this pass. Confinement and the Loss of Partonic Information carried its gamma section between The Other Confinement Picture and What the Algebra Supplies, so it closed on Open Questions and read as an exception. The section was the only one in the corpus out of place; it now sits between Open Questions and Summary, as the tail order requires, and its counts are unchanged at 44, 21 and 23.

The Swap to the Axis-Free Display

The census above is the state before the swap and is kept as the record of it. The label $\mathrm{axf}$ is now the physics corpus's, and the occurrences outside a gamma section have been rewritten: 4352 in the article bodies and 1258 in the sections that follow a gamma section — Summary, Summary of Notation, Further Reading — for 5610 glyphs. Matrix Representations Conventions was restructured rather than substituted.

What remains is 1908 occurrences in 94 articles, 1766 of them inside a gamma section and 142 outside. Matrix Representations Conventions holds 215, 76 of them in its gamma section, and it prints the axis-free arrays as the display of record in §The 4×4 Matrix Representation while keeping the Peirce arrays in §The Peirce Frame, the Second Display, in §The Switch Between the Two Frames and in §The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation. The rest are in the 92 other gamma sections, which print the Peirce display because its arrays are the ones the literature prints and now print the axis-free display beside it, and in the three places named in the table below, where the label is a name.

Each of those 92 sections closes on a paragraph that names its display and gives the change, $L^{\mathrm{axf}}=C_{\Pi}L^{\mathrm{pau}}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and states what a conjugation keeps: products, anticommutators, traces and determinants. A reader who carries a cell from the body into the section therefore knows which array he is reading.

What the substitution would have broken. Sixteen passages in twelve articles asserted a display-dependent property of an array, and each was rewritten to name its display: a Peirce array under the $\mathrm{axf}$ label is a false statement in the way a wrong sign is. The shapes were the chirality cell written as $\operatorname{diag}(-I_2,I_2)$, the spin and bispinor matrices written with $\sigma_k$ blocks, the $L\leftrightarrow R$ correspondence written with the swap $\mathsf S$ of the Peirce frame, and the frame declarations that still called the Peirce display the canonical one. The forty arrays of the conventions article — four left and four right Peirce families, sixteen axis-free cells, sixteen Peirce cells — were recomputed before and after the rewrite and all agree.

The leaks closed in this pass. Two Peirce arrays had survived outside a gamma section and are now written in the axis-free display with the Peirce array named beside them: the operator chirality $\gamma_5$ in Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Operator Chirality, whose body section read it as $\operatorname{diag}(-I_2,I_2)$, and the $L\leftrightarrow R$ relation of Operator and Calculus Conventions, which now states the transposition in the coefficient basis and relegates the exchange $\mathsf S$ to the display. The 134 glyphs that remain outside the gamma sections are the two display sections of Matrix Representations Conventions, where they belong.

The two displays printed side by side. Every display block of a gamma section that carries the label is now printed twice, the Peirce block first and the axis-free block after it under a one-line lead, with $\mathrm{pau}$ replaced by $\mathrm{axf}$: 179 blocks in 90 articles, holding 1193 of the 1766 occurrences. The substitution is the whole transformation, because a cell identity is a statement in the coefficient basis and holds in either display; a conjugation carries one array into the other and changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant.

Three sections needed arrays rather than labels, and there the axis-free arrays were computed from the Peirce ones by $C_{\Pi}$ conjugation and printed after them: Matrix Representations Conventions, Exercise: Plane-Wave Solutions of the Biquaternion Dirac Equation and Exercise: Chirality and the Weyl Spinors. In the conventions article the sixteen products also carry a fifth column, the axis-free cell of each row.

What stays single is the running text: 573 occurrences are named inside a sentence, and a cell named there keeps the Peirce label, the section stating once that its axis-free array is the conjugate. No block was twinned where the block states the change of display itself, since the twin would say the same thing twice.

What remains to be counted. The dependence of the Peirce decomposition, of the spinor module and of the Weyl blocks on the Pauli display is a separate question, and its inventory is The Pauli Dependence of the Peirce, Spinor and Weyl Displays, §The Inventory.

The Articles, by Decreasing Count

The first column carries the instruction, the same in every row, and names the article it is to be applied to. The table has three pairs of counted columns, then the Pauli Print column described below, then the closing one. The first pair counts the label before the migration, on each of its two glyphs, $L$ and $R$, and is the census of the section above. The second pair counts the same two glyphs as the corpus stands now. The third pair restricts the second one to what lies outside §The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation: that is the measure of the migration, because the gamma sections are the only place where the display is kept, so a nonzero entry there is either a display section, which prints the arrays by design, or a place where the label is named rather than carried. The totals agree with the arithmetic of the article: 3184 and 4443 before, 888 and 918 after, 70 and 64 outside the gamma sections. The two after columns count glyphs only, so they add to 1806, and the remaining 102 of the 1908 occurrences of the string are the label named in prose — the phrase labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, the definitions of the caret, and the notation tables. The Pauli Print column counts no glyphs: it names the constructions of the article that the swap cannot free, and it is read in the paragraph before the table.

Where the two outside columns are not zero. Three rows carry a nonzero entry there, and each is accounted for. Matrix Representations Conventions holds 139 of the 142, because two of its thematic sections, §The Peirce Frame, the Second Display and §The Switch Between the Two Frames, are display sections that stand before the gamma one. Unit, Index and Notation Conventions holds 1, the sentence that defines the caret label, and Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Operator Chirality holds 2, its introduction and its notation table, both naming the display. In the remaining 96 articles the entry is zero.

The Pauli Print column. Four constructions are tied to the root $\vec\Xi=i\vec e_z$ and are not freed by the swap, because the direction they carry sits in the object and not in the array. They are the Peirce decomposition, whose idempotents are $\tilde\Pi_\pm=\tfrac12(e_t\pm i\vec e_z)$ and whose components, bases and frame arrays are built on them; the spinor module $S\cong\mathbb{C}^2$, which is the image of the Pauli map $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_k)=-i\sigma_k$; the Weyl blocks, which are the Peirce blocks of $\Delta=S\oplus\bar S$; and the chirality array $\gamma_5=\operatorname{diag}(-I_2,I_2)$. The column before the closing one lists those of the four that the article uses, in that order, and reads — when it uses none.

The column counts use and not mention. The difference is largest on the first entry: the migration itself wrote the phrase the Peirce display into 91 articles, so the bare word Peirce is in 95 of the 99 rows while the decomposition is in 30. The test is the construction and not the word — Peirce decomposition, components, basis, idempotents, projectors, coordinates, carriers, index, which name a space or a projection, and not Peirce display, label, frame, array, cell or sign, which name an array.

Across the 99 rows 30 use the Peirce decomposition, 86 the spinor module, 52 the Weyl blocks, and 9 the array $\operatorname{diag}(-I_2,I_2)$. The nine are The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form, The Minimal Coupling of the Biquaternion Dirac Field to Electromagnetism, The Spinor Module in Biquaternionic Form and Its Lorentz Action, Exercise: Chirality and the Weyl Spinors and Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Operator Chirality, which use all four; The Fujikawa Method and the Path-Integral Anomaly in Biquaternionic Form, which uses the array with the spinor module; The Spectral Triple of the Biquaternion Algebra, which uses it with the Peirce decomposition and the spinor module; The Chiral Condensate and Dynamical Symmetry Breaking in Biquaternionic Form, which uses it with the Weyl blocks; and The Field-Strength Biquaternion and Its Invariants, which uses the array alone. Seven rows use none of the four — Exercise: The Non-Relativistic Limit and the Pauli Equation, The CKM Matrix and CP Violation in Biquaternionic Form, The Classical Coulomb Problem and Its Hidden SO(4) Symmetry, The Theta Parameter and Strong CP, Paravectors and the Geometry of Spacetime, The Roots of a General Element and Unit, Index and Notation Conventions — and each works in the algebra itself, where no spinor index is opened and no root is chosen. The spinor entry is the widest because a fermion article states its module once and the word stays; that is the load a rewrite would carry, and it is not evidence of a defect.

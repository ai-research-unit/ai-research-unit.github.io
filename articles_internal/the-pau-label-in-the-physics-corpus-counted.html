The Pau Label in the Physics Corpus, Counted

Introduction

The regular matrices of the physics corpus are displayed in two frames, and each frame carries its label in the caret: $\mathrm{pau}$ for the canonical Peirce frame, whose blocks are the Pauli combination $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_k)=-i\sigma_k$ taken once per tensor factor, and $\mathrm{axf}$ for the axis-free frame. The label names the display and not the object behind it: the multiplication is $L_{\tilde Q}$ and takes no label, so an occurrence is counted only where the label sits on a matrix glyph, $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, $R_{\vec e_\nu}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, $L_{\vec e_z\vec e_{\tau(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, on a general matrix $L^{\mathrm{pau}}(\tilde Q)$, or on a power of one, $(L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2$.

What the label is written on. Four forms carry it. A unit, $e_t$, $e_\mu$, $\vec e_x$ to $\vec e_n$. A unit expression: a signed unit $R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, a square $R_{\vec e_z^{\,2}}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, a product $R_{\vec e_y\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, a sum $R_{e_t+i\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, or the identity $L_1^{\mathrm{pau}}$. The general matrix, $L^{\mathrm{pau}}(\tilde Q)$. A power of a labelled glyph, wrapped so that the label is not raised with it, $(L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2$. A glyph whose subscript is a general element, $L_a$, $R_b$, $L_\omega$, or a derivative, $L_{\tilde\nabla}$, stays bare: those name the multiplication on the algebra and not its matrix in either frame.

The count. 99 articles carry the label and they hold 7568 occurrences. A further 4 occurrences name the label without carrying it, in the sentences that define it, so the string totals 7572. The count is the literal caret group: a matrix written twice in two formulas is counted twice, and a cell $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_\nu}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ contributes two.

The shape. The mean is 76.4 occurrences an article and the median 50, so a short head carries the mass: the top ten articles hold 2703 occurrences, 35.7 per cent of the total, and the largest is Matrix Representations Conventions. At the other end 6 articles carry fewer than ten, where the label is a name stated once and then read: Biquaternion Physical Interpretation of Chirality, $4$; Unit, Index and Notation Conventions, $2$; Algebra and Element Conventions, $2$; The Unit Group, the Cone and the Orders, $1$; The Four General Products: Composition, Probability, Causality, Gauge, $1$; Element and Operator Representations: The Three Actions, $1$.

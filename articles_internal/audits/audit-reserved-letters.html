Audit of the Letters

What this file is. One row per glyph, with its sense and its count, for the two published collections. Written 2026-11-24 on the working tree. It is a measurement, not a rule. The reserved letters and the reserved element glyphs open the file in two tables of their own, each with a column for the reserved sense and a column for every other sense, so that a non-empty second column reads as a violation. Where a row disagrees with a rule of the conventions articles, the row is evidence and the rule is a claim, and the gap is listed in §The Gaps.

Scope. articles_maths/*.md, 2 633 files, and articles_physics/*.md, 679 files. Only the math delimiters are read, $…$ and $$…$$; prose is not counted. A word inside a display is not counted letter by letter: the contents of \mathrm{…}, \text{…}, \operatorname{…}, \mathbb{…}, \mathcal{…} and their kin are excluded, so that \mathrm{opp} adds no p. An upright group that carries a subscript is read only through that subscript, and the subscript is a label (§The Labels). 812 580 spans in total, and 1 976 835 glyph occurrences inside them: 1 413 674 in the maths corpus, 563 161 in the physics corpus.

How the counts are made. One occurrence is one glyph between two math delimiters, or one Greek command. Each occurrence is filed under the mark that follows it and under its nesting: bare (no mark), marked (a subscript of its own), exponent (a superscript of its own), index (inside the subscript or the superscript of another glyph), accented (under \vec, \tilde, \bar, \hat, \breve, \check, \dot or \ddot). An accent overlaps the other four files and is not a fifth file. So $c^2$ is filed as an exponent, $e_x$ as marked, $\lambda_i$ as marked with the label i, and the $i$ of $\lambda_i$ as an index.

What the reading costs. The assignment of a mark to a sense is an interpretation, not a datum: a subscript may carry two senses, and a bare letter may be generic or may be a named object. The four context files are exact; the sense column is approximate.

What is not here. The blackboard letters \mathbb{B}, \mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{H}, \mathbb{F}, \mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K}, and the calligraphic ones, are objects of their own, tabulated in Algebra and Element Conventions, §Summary of Notation. The 698 upright labels are in §The Labels. The three alphabets that are not LaTeX commands are in §The Unicode Letters.

The Reserved Letters

How the two columns are read. Reserved counts the occurrences of the glyph that carry the role the conventions reserve for it. Other counts the occurrences that carry a different sense, and a non-empty Other is a violation of the rule, in that many sites. Both are counted inside the math delimiters only, as the rest of this file is. A glyph that a corpus does not reserve stands as — in the Reserved column, and its whole total stands in Other without a verdict of violation: the maths corpus reserves eight letters and the physics corpus eleven, and the difference is stated in the conventions articles.

Three rows of the table need a word, because a reader will otherwise read them as errors of counting.

The i row is the largest violation of the two tables. The index is written \imath and \jmath in the physics corpus and i in the maths corpus. The occurrence that carries the index is the one nested in a subscript, 17 842 in maths and 2 047 in physics; an $i$ in a superscript, as in $e^{i\theta}$, stays the imaginary unit and is counted as reserved.

The c row reads as a violation only because the maths corpus does not reserve the letter. A corpus that excludes physics has no speed of light to reserve, and the row is evidence for the scoping of Reserved Letters Conventions, not a defect of the articles. The \tau and m rows are the same case: the maths corpus does not reserve either, and both are measured there for completeness.

The \Phi, \Psi row is the largest of the file. No lowercase glyph stands where a sector belongs: the lowercase is spent elsewhere, on the Euler totient, a potential or an angle, and a wavefunction. §Sectors forbids it because a lowercase \phi one character from \Phi is read as a sector by a reader scanning a formula, so the violation is one of the glyph budget and not of the sense, and the repair is a change of glyph for the potential, the angle, the map, the wavefunction and the totient.

The Reserved Element Glyphs

The four families of Reserved Letters Conventions, §Reserved Element Glyphs are measured the same way. The field family is physics only, so the maths corpus does not reserve it. A potent is reserved when it carries the tag, so \Pi_{\mathrm{id}} and \Pi_{\mathrm{nil}} count as reserved and a bare \Pi does not. The root is the character, and the command that prints the majuscule is not counted as the character.

glyph role reserved (maths) reserved (physics) other (maths) other (physics) verdict
Q generic element 70 846 17 858 0 346 clean in maths; 346 in physics, the labelled non-element senses Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}, Q_{\mathrm{amp}}, Q_{\mathrm{Had}}
\xi, Ξ generic field — 13 244 363 4 271 violated in physics: the majuscule written with the command \Xi 4 271 times instead of the character Ξ; maths does not reserve the field, and its 363 sites are a bound element and the same command
Ζ root 2 679 346 0 0 clean in both; the case rule splits the character and the pure root carries the arrow
\Pi potent 7 925 5 326 7 711 1 052 violated: the bare form, 828 in maths and 336 in physics; of the remainder the projection \Pi_\pm is permitted, and a numbered or a labelled form is read from its subscript
\pi the number 13 252 15 190 1 074 99 violated: \pi_{\mathrm{id}} and \pi_{\mathrm{nil}}, potents written with the number's glyph, 1 173 sites

The \Pi shape is spent three ways, and the tag is what separates them. In maths, of 15 636 occurrences, 7 925 carry the tag \mathrm{id} or \mathrm{nil} and are reserved, 828 are bare and forbidden, and the remaining 6 883 carry a sign, a number or a label: 6 396 at the top level and 487 inside an index. Of the sign, \Pi_\pm is the projection and is permitted; a numbered or a labelled form is read from its subscript. In physics the four figures are 5 326, 336, 405 and 311. The bare form is the violation.

In the same way, of the 14 326 occurrences of \pi in maths, 1 074 are a potent carrying a tag, 9 027 are the number bare, and 4 225 carry a sign, a number or a label; in physics the four figures are 99, 14 205, 558 and 427.

The \xi, Ξ row is the newest member of the reservation, and the only one whose defect is typographic. The field is written correctly in 13 244 sites, and the 4 271 of the other column are the same object written with the command \Xi rather than the character Ξ. The conventions forbid the command, because the majuscule of the field is meant to be read at a glance and never resolved against the element $X$; the repair is one token for one character, and it changes no sense.

The Violations, in Order of Cost

How a violation is repaired. The preferred repair is a more appropriate letter that is not reserved: the object moves to a glyph that names it better, and no glyph of §The Reserved Letters and §Reserved Element Glyphs is spent on it. A letter already bound in the display is never the answer, and a second sense on a reserved glyph is never the answer either. Where no such letter is free — because every nearby glyph is taken, or because the object has no customary letter — the fallback is a rare letter with a long subscript: a glyph the corpus seldom uses, carrying a subscript that spells the sense out in full, \digamma_{\mathrm{…}} being the model. The two rules are ordered: the appropriate letter first, the rare letter and the long subscript only when the first fails.

rank violation sites the fix
1 \lambda as an eigenvalue, maths 21 276 the spectrum moves to \eta, the article titles included
2 i as an index 19 889 _i, _{i} become _\imath, _{\imath}; 17 842 in maths and 2 047 in physics
3 c as a generic constant, maths 14 357 not a defect of the articles but of the scoping; a corpus without physics keeps c free
4 \phi, \varphi, \psi forbidden lowercase 11 128 a glyph of its own for each of five objects; §The Lowercase, Object by Object below prices the three schemes
5 \Xi instead of the character Ξ, physics 4 271 the token becomes the character; the field is otherwise written correctly in 13 244 sites
6 \nu as an index, maths 2 997 the index moves to \imath
7 \pi as an untagged potent 1 173 the potent becomes \Pi_{\mathrm{id}} or \Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}
8 bare \Pi 1 164 the tag \mathrm{id} or \mathrm{nil}; 828 in maths and 336 in physics
9 Q as a named slot, physics 346 the slot leaves the Q family and takes \digamma with its label

The first three rows are a little over two thirds of the file's outstanding work. Ranks 1 and 2 are mechanical: one glyph moves to another, and no sentence changes. Rank 2 was costed at 15 741 sites in the pass of 2026-11-23, which counted every $i$ inside a subscript and a superscript — that is, the imaginary unit of every exponential — and the row above counts the subscript alone. Rank 1 is the only row that moves an object into one of the eleven reserved scalars, and it is allowed for the reason the rule exists: the object is the eigenvalue and \eta is the eigenvalue's own letter, so the repair removes a second sense rather than adding one. Every other row follows the preferred repair and spends no reserved glyph. Rank 4 is the row where the fallback rule would apply, if no letter can be found for the potential, the angle, the map, the wavefunction and the totient. That row is not mechanical: no glyph of the corpus is free, and the replacement of 11 128 sites needs a decision this audit cannot make.

The Lowercase, Object by Object

The three glyphs are six objects, and the census decides the repair. Each occurrence of \phi, \varphi and \psi was read against its file and its argument, and the 11 128 sites fall into five kinds. The sixth, an angle, has no site of its own: the \phi_{\mathrm{rot}} of the physics corpus is 2 sites and sits with the potential.

object sites glyphs maths / physics
a map, a functional, a Hermitian form 7 862 \varphi 5 888, \phi 1 974 7 858 / 4
a wavefunction, a spinor 2 114 \psi 2 114 1 746 / 368
a formula of logic 784 \varphi 608, \psi 170, \phi 6 780 / 4
a potential, or a scalar field 262 \phi 262 0 / 262
the Euler totient 106 \varphi 103, \psi 3 106 / 0

The totient is the smallest of the five, not the largest. It is 106 sites, written \varphi(n) and \varphi(N)&nbsp; in the number theory articles; the bulk of \varphi is a map, a positive functional or a Hermitian form, \varphi(x), \varphi(xy^*), \varphi(Tx,y), in the functional analysis articles. A repair costed on the totient would fix one per cent of the row.

The two conventions articles authorise two mechanisms and disagree on where the first may be used. §Sectors names these very five objects — a potential, an angle, a map, a wavefunction, the totient — and says they "take a glyph of their own instead: the fallback \digamma with a role label, or a named glyph fixed where it is introduced". §The Field says "the fallback \digamma is spent on scalars alone". The first sentence admits the map, the formula and the wavefunction; the second refuses them, because a map is not a scalar: that is 10 760 sites, 97 per cent of the row. The tension is in the corpus, not in this measurement, and it decides the scheme below.

The glyphs that are free. A named glyph costs nothing to adopt if the corpus has never spent it. Counted over both corpora, in the math delimiters only, and as a value rather than an index slot:

\Finv, \Game, \mho, \hslash 0 \Finv and \Game and \mho appear once each, in the index-slot table of Index and Notation Conventions
\beth, \daleth 1 the same table
\eth 3 the same table
\gimel 7 the same table, and 6 elsewhere
\aleph 134 the cardinal $\aleph_0$ only, §Gaps 1

Three schemes, priced. The characters added are counted against the source, not the output, and the replacement is one token per site.

scheme the five objects characters added verdict
1 \digamma with a role label everywhere: \digamma_{\mathrm{map}}, \digamma_{\mathrm{wfn}}, \digamma_{\mathrm{frm}}, \digamma_{\mathrm{pot}}, \digamma_{\mathrm{tot}} +167 137 authorised by §Sectors and refused by §The Field for 10 760 of the sites; \digamma grows from 2 103 to 13 231 and becomes the fourth most used glyph of the corpus
2 \Finv for the map, \gimel for the wavefunction, \digamma with a label for the formula, the potential and the totient +27 473 compliant today under either reading; two letters the corpus has never used as values
3 \Finv, \gimel, \beth for the three big objects, \digamma with a label for the potential and the totient +15 713 compliant today; three new letters, and \digamma grows by 368 alone

Scheme 1 is the one the article names twice, and the one a reader never has to learn: \digamma_{\mathrm{map}} says what it is. Its defect is the sentence in §The Field, which no edit of this audit can settle. Schemes 2 and 3 buy compliance with two or three letters the corpus has never spent, and \Finv is length-neutral against \varphi — both are seven characters — so the largest single substitution, 7 862 sites, costs nothing in the source.

Twenty-eight files use both \phi and \varphi, and mean two objects by them. The characteristic function, the dyadic bump of a Besov space, the phase of a biquaternion: a substitution of one glyph would collide there. The other 705 files are single-glyph and mechanical. The sites are also spread: 733 files carry them, the twelve largest hold 16 per cent, and 316 files hold ten or more.

The order the repair should run in, when it is authorised. The totient first, 106 sites, recognised by its argument (n) or (N); then the formula, 784 sites, confined to nine logic articles; then the potential, 262 sites, physics alone; then the wavefunction, 2 114 sites; then the map, 7 862 sites, whose twenty-eight mixed files are read by eye. Each step is a separate substitution, and the corpus stays consistent between steps because the glyph that moves is unused after the step.

The Gaps

  1. λ has two lives. In physics, 87 sites, the wavelength, as the rule says. In maths, 21 276 sites, the eigenvalue: \lambda_i, \lambda_\pm, \lambda_k, the roots of a characteristic polynomial. The rule is written in both conventions articles and enforced in one. Either the maths corpus moves to \eta, or the rule is scoped to physics and the maths corpus gets a line of its own.
  2. ν is a measure in maths. 2 997 sites there are an index, and the wave number is claimed in only 2 226.
  3. The space index has four answers. Reserved Letters Conventions says the index is the dotless \imath, in both corpora. Index and Notation Conventions writes \vec e_i, \gamma_i and \varepsilon_{ijk} in its own statement of the rule, and its table of the slot lists \imath and \jmath beside \mu. §Wavenumbers of both conventions articles says "the space index is $k$". Measured: \imath 27 480 sites in physics and 130 in maths; \jmath 8 817 and 293; \mu 13 899 in maths and 2 585 in physics; i nested in a subscript 17 842 and 2 047; and k inside an index 16 940 and 2 339. Five spellings, three rules, and no file states the other four.
  4. h carries two objects in physics. The Planck constant, bare, and the informational time h_t, a length. One letter, two senses, separated only by the subscript; both rows exist in Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions.
  5. e carries three. The four units, the bulk of 83 521 sites; Euler's number in $e^{i\theta}$; the elementary charge e^\pm. The first is told by the subscript, the second by the exponent, the third by the context.
  6. i and ı are two letters for one job. The imaginary unit is i everywhere; the index is i in the maths corpus and \imath in physics. The index use of i is 17 842 sites in maths and 2 047 in physics (§The Violations, rank 2), and the earlier pass undercounted it at 14 414 and 1 327 by reading a superscript as an index.
  7. ι is used where the rule forbids it. Index and Notation Conventions says the letter carries no meaning and that a display introducing it is a defect. It occurs 25 times in physics, all as the Clifford map \iota_{\mathrm{Cl}}, and 1 673 times in maths.
  8. ο is documented twice and reserved nowhere. 808 sites, all in physics: the electrostatic potential \omicron_{\mathrm{pot}}, the Higgs components \omicron^\pm_{\mathrm{hgs}}, an angle \omicron_{\mathrm{ang}}. Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions names it in passing, and no table of the conventions articles lists it.
  9. ϖ has two homes. The twistor spinor \varpi_{\mathrm{twz}} and the Cartan weight, told apart by the label alone.
  10. φ, ϕ and ψ are forbidden and used 11 128 times. §Sectors of Reserved Letters Conventions says the lowercase is not allowed, because a lowercase \phi one character from \Phi is read as a sector. The corpus spends it on a map (7 862), a wavefunction (2 114), a formula (784), a potential (262) and the totient (106) — §The Lowercase, Object by Object — and no glyph of the corpus is free for them, which is why the two conventions articles offer the fallback \digamma and disagree on whether it reaches a map or a wavefunction. §The Violations, rank 4, gives the price of the three schemes.
  11. Ξ is written with the command. Algebra and Element Conventions fixes the majuscule of the field as the character Ξ, U+039E, and never the command that prints it. The corpus honours the rule 9 821 times and breaks it 4 271 times, and the break is silent: both spellings compile and only one reads as a glyph.
  12. φ and ϕ are split by corpus. \varphi is the Euler totient in maths, 6 599 sites, and occurs 4 times in physics; \phi is the potential in physics and a rotation angle in maths. Neither file states which.

How to Re-measure

The instrument is one pass, and it defines the four context files of §How the counts are made. Copy it to a temporary directory, run it from the root of the repository, and delete it afterwards.

import bisect, collections, glob, re, unicodedata

UP = r'\\(?:mathrm|text|operatorname|mathbb|mathcal)\s*\{[^{}]*\}'
GREEK = set('alpha beta gamma delta epsilon varepsilon zeta eta theta vartheta iota '
            'kappa varkappa lambda mu nu xi omicron pi varpi rho varrho sigma varsigma '
            'tau upsilon phi varphi chi psi omega Gamma Delta Theta Lambda Xi Pi Sigma '
            'Upsilon Phi Psi Omega digamma ell imath jmath aleph'.split())
ACC = {'vec', 'tilde', 'bar', 'hat', 'breve', 'check', 'dot', 'ddot'}
TOK = re.compile(UP + r'|\\([A-Za-z]+)|(?<![A-Za-z\\])([A-Za-z])(?![A-Za-z])')


def spans(txt):
    out, i, n = [], 0, len(txt)
    while i < n:
        if txt[i] == '\\':
            i += 2
            continue
        if txt[i] == '$':
            d = '$$' if txt.startswith('$$', i) else '$'
            k = j = i + len(d)
            while k < n:
                if txt[k] == '\\':
                    k += 2
                    continue
                if txt.startswith(d, k):
                    break
                k += 1
            if k >= n:
                break
            out.append(txt[j:k])
            i = k + len(d)
        else:
            i += 1
    return out


def arg(b, i):
    if i >= len(b):
        return '', i
    if b[i] == '{':
        d, j = 0, i
        while j < len(b):
            if b[j] == '{':
                d += 1
            elif b[j] == '}':
                d -= 1
                if d == 0:
                    return b[i + 1:j], j + 1
            j += 1
        return b[i + 1:], len(b)
    m = re.match(UP, b[i:]) or re.match(r'\\[A-Za-z]+', b[i:])
    return (m.group(0), i + m.end()) if m else (b[i], i + 1)


def glyphs(body):
    mask = [(m.start(), m.end()) for m in re.finditer(UP, body)]
    lo = [a for a, _ in mask]
    rng = []
    for m in re.finditer(r'[_^]', body):
        k = bisect.bisect_right(lo, m.start()) - 1
        if k >= 0 and mask[k][0] <= m.start() < mask[k][1]:
            continue
        rng.append((m.end(), arg(body, m.end())[1], body[m.start()]))
    rng.sort()
    start = [r[0] for r in rng]

    def where(p):
        k = bisect.bisect_right(start, p) - 1
        return rng[k][2] if k >= 0 and rng[k][0] <= p < rng[k][1] else '.'

    out, acc = [], None
    for m in TOK.finditer(body):
        name = m.group(1) or m.group(2)
        if m.group(2) is None and name not in GREEK:
            if name in ACC:
                acc = name
            continue
        j, sub, sup = m.end(), None, None
        while j < len(body) and body[j] in '_^':
            t, j2 = arg(body, j + 1)
            if body[j] == '_' and sub is None:
                sub = t
            if body[j] == '^' and sup is None:
                sup = t
            j = j2
        out.append((name, where(m.start()), sub, sup, acc))
        acc = None
    return out


def run(paths):
    per = collections.defaultdict(collections.Counter)
    uni = collections.Counter()
    labels = collections.Counter()
    spans_n = 0
    for f in sorted(paths):
        txt = open(f, encoding='utf-8').read()
        for body in spans(txt):
            spans_n += 1
            for ch in body:
                if ord(ch) > 127 and unicodedata.category(ch).startswith('L'):
                    uni[ch] += 1
            for name, ctx, sub, sup, acc in glyphs(body):
                c = per[name]
                c['total'] += 1
                if acc:
                    c['acc'] += 1
                if ctx == '.':
                    c['sub' if sub is not None else 'sup' if sup is not None else 'bare'] += 1
                elif ctx == '_':
                    c['low'] += 1
                else:
                    c['high'] += 1
                if sub and ctx == '.':
                    m = re.match(r'^\\[A-Za-z]+\{(.*?)\}', sub)
                    if m:
                        labels[m.group(1)] += 1
    return per, uni, labels, spans_n


for tag, paths in (('maths', glob.glob('articles_maths/*.md')),
                   ('physics', glob.glob('articles_physics/*.md'))):
    per, uni, labels, spans_n = run(paths)
    print(tag, spans_n, sum(v['total'] for v in per.values()), len(labels))
    print(dict(per['nu']))

An upright group is masked, so \mathrm{opp} adds no p; a group that carries a subscript is read through the subscript, which is what makes \Pi_{\mathrm{id}} a potent and a bare \Pi a violation. Nested upright groups are masked one level deep by the expression above, and the tables of this file were built with a brace-matching pass that goes deeper; the difference is a handful of sites in \mathbb and \mathcal groups.

Audit of the Letters

What this file is. One row per glyph, with its sense and its count, for the two published collections. Written 2026-11-23 on the working tree. It is a measurement, not a rule. The reserved letters and the reserved element glyphs open the file in two tables of their own, each with a column for the reserved sense and a column for every other sense, so that a non-empty second column reads as a violation. Where a row disagrees with a rule of the conventions articles, the row is evidence and the rule is a claim, and the gap is listed in §The Gaps.

Scope. articles_maths/*.md, 2 633 files, and articles_physics/*.md, 679 files. Only the math delimiters are read, $…$ and $$…$$; prose is not counted. A word inside a display is not counted letter by letter: the contents of \mathrm{…}, \text{…}, \operatorname{…}, \mathbb{…}, \mathcal{…} and their kin are excluded, so that \mathrm{opp} adds no p. Such a group is counted only when it is a subscript, and then as a label (§The Labels). 1 218 067 glyph occurrences in total.

How the counts are made. One occurrence is one glyph between two math delimiters. Each occurrence is filed under the mark that follows it: bare, then a subscript, a superscript, or one of the stacked marks \vec, \tilde, \bar, \hat, \breve, \check. The filed marks are then read as senses, and the number in parentheses after a sense is the number of occurrences assigned to it, both corpora together; the columns to the left give the split between the two collections. So $c^2$ is filed under the speed of light, $e_x$ under the four units, $\lambda_i$ under the eigenvalue.

What the reading costs. The assignment of a mark to a sense is an interpretation, not a datum: a subscript may carry two senses, and a bare letter may be generic or may be a named object. A mark that names no new object — a power, an inverse, an adjoint, an index that carries no meaning of its own — is pooled at the end of the row as other markings, so every row reconciles with its two totals. The counts are therefore exact, the senses approximate.

What is not here. The blackboard letters \mathbb{B}, \mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{H}, \mathbb{F}, \mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K}, and the calligraphic ones, are objects of their own, tabulated in Algebra and Element Conventions, §Summary of Notation. The 526 upright labels are in §The Labels. The three alphabets that are not LaTeX commands are in §The Unicode Letters.

The Reserved Letters

How the two columns are read. Reserved counts the occurrences of the glyph that carry the role the conventions reserve for it. Other counts the occurrences that carry a different sense, and a non-empty Other is a violation of the rule, in that many sites. Both are counted inside the math delimiters only, as the rest of this file is. A glyph that a corpus does not reserve stands as — in the Reserved column, and its whole total stands in Other without a verdict of violation: the maths corpus reserves eight letters and the physics corpus eleven, and the difference is stated in the conventions articles.

Five rows of the table need a word, because a reader will otherwise read them as errors of counting.

The i row is the largest violation in the file and the cheapest to repair: the index is written \imath and \jmath in the physics corpus and i in the maths corpus, so the fix is a substitution of _i and _{i} by _\imath and _{\imath}, with the exemptions of a genuinely imaginary subscript.

The c row reads as a violation only because the maths corpus does not reserve the letter: there c is a generic constant, a count or a coefficient. A corpus that excludes physics has no speed of light to reserve, and the row is evidence for the scoping of Reserved Letters Conventions, not a defect of the articles. The 17 physics sites are the sound speed, which a corpus with a medium owns and which the article should either reserve or name.

The \tau and m rows are the same case: the maths corpus does not reserve either, and both are measured there for completeness.

The \Phi, \Psi row is one of the largest in the file, and its defect is of a different kind. No lowercase glyph stands where a sector belongs: of the 16 409 occurrences of the two shapes, the lowercase is spent elsewhere, on the Euler totient 6 599, a potential or an angle 2 246, and a wavefunction 2 275. §Sectors forbids it because a lowercase \phi one character from \Phi is read as a sector by a reader scanning a formula, so the violation is one of the glyph budget and not of the sense, and the repair is a change of glyph for the potential, the angle, the map, the wavefunction and the totient.

The Reserved Element Glyphs

The four families of Reserved Letters Conventions, §Reserved Element Glyphs are measured the same way. The field family is physics only, so the maths corpus does not reserve it. A potent is reserved when it carries the tag, so \Pi_{\mathrm{id}} and \Pi_{\mathrm{nil}} count as reserved and a bare \Pi does not.

The \Pi shape is spent three ways, and the tag is what separates them. In maths, of 15 636 occurrences, 8 127 carry the tag \mathrm{id} or \mathrm{nil} and are reserved, 994 are bare and forbidden, and the remaining 6 515 carry a sign, a number or a label: the sign is the projection \Pi_\pm, which the article permits, and a numbered or labelled form is read from its subscript. In physics the three figures are 5 380, 572 and 424. The bare form is the violation.

The \xi, Ξ row is the newest member of the reservation, and the only one whose defect is typographic. The field is written correctly in 13 191 sites, and the 4 268 of the other column are the same object written with the command \Xi rather than the glyph Ξ. The conventions forbid the command, because the majuscule of the field is meant to be read at a glance and never resolved against the element $X$; the repair is one token for one character, and it changes no sense.

The Violations, in Order of Cost

How a violation is repaired. The preferred repair is a more appropriate letter that is not reserved: the object moves to a glyph that names it better, and no glyph of §The Reserved Letters and §Reserved Element Glyphs is spent on it. A letter already bound in the display is never the answer, and a second sense on a reserved glyph is never the answer either. Where no such letter is free — because every nearby glyph is taken, or because the object has no customary letter — the fallback is a rare letter with a long subscript: a glyph the corpus seldom uses, carrying a subscript that spells the sense out in full, \digamma_{\mathrm{…}} being the model. The rare letter is legible once, and the long subscript is what makes it readable twice. The two rules are ordered: the appropriate letter first, the rare letter and the long subscript only when the first fails.

The first two rows are a little over half of the file's outstanding work, and both are mechanical: one glyph moves to another, and no sentence changes. Rank 1 is the only row that moves an object into one of the eleven reserved scalars, and it is allowed for the reason the rule exists: the object is the eigenvalue and \eta is the eigenvalue's own letter, so the repair removes a second sense rather than adding one. Every other row follows the preferred repair and spends no reserved glyph. Rank 4 is the row where the second rule would apply if no letter could be found for the potential, the angle, the map, the wavefunction and the totient. The fourth row is not mechanical: no glyph of the corpus is free, and the replacement of 11 120 sites needs a decision this audit cannot make.

Four of them compete with a command for the same object: Ξ with \Xi in 14 files, ξ with \xi, ϑ with \theta, χ with \chi. Two compete with each other: ϙ and ϛ are one charge in The Path Integral and the Coupled Klein-Gordon Equation and its Schrödinger twin.

The Gaps

  1. λ has two lives. In physics, 101 sites, the wavelength, as the rule says. In maths, 21 270 sites, the eigenvalue (§The Reserved Letters): \lambda_i, \lambda_\pm, \lambda_k, the roots of a characteristic polynomial. The rule is written in both conventions articles and enforced in one. Either the maths corpus moves to \eta, or the rule is scoped to physics and the maths corpus gets a line of its own.
  2. ν is a measure in maths. 2 968 sites there are an index, and the wave number is claimed in only 2 254 (§The Reserved Letters).
  3. μ is the space index in maths. 13 323 sites, against 2 017 in physics, where \imath is the index. Two index letters, one per corpus, and neither file states the other's choice.
  4. h carries two objects in physics. The Planck constant, bare, and the informational time h_t, a length. One letter, two senses, separated only by the subscript; both rows exist in Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions.
  5. e carries three. The four units, 46 163 sites, the bulk of 66 839; Euler's number in e^{i\theta}; the elementary charge e^{\pm}. The first is told by the subscript, the second by the exponent, the third by the context.
  6. i and ı are two letters for one job. The imaginary unit is i everywhere; the index is i in the maths corpus and \imath in physics; the index use of i is 14 414 sites in maths and 1 327 in physics (§The Violations, rank 2).
  7. ι is used where the rule forbids it. Index and Notation Conventions says the letter carries no meaning and that a display introducing it is a defect. It occurs 25 times in physics, all as the Clifford map \iota_{\mathrm{Cl}}, and 1 566 times in maths.
  8. ο is undocumented. 734 sites, all in physics, and no table of the conventions lists it: the electrostatic potential \omicron_{\mathrm{pot}}, the Higgs components \omicron^\pm_{\mathrm{hgs}}, an angle \omicron_{\mathrm{ang}}.
  9. ϖ has two homes. The twistor spinor \varpi_{\mathrm{twz}} and the Cartan weight \varpi_{\mathrm{wgt}}, told apart by the label alone.
  10. φ, ϕ and ψ are forbidden and used 11 120 times. §Sectors of Reserved Letters Conventions says the lowercase is not allowed, because a lowercase \phi one character from \Phi is read as a sector. The corpus spends it on the potential, the angle, the map, the wavefunction and the Euler totient, and no free glyph is left for them, so the rule needs a home for those five objects before it can be enforced. §The Violations, rank 4, gives the price.
  11. Ξ is written with the command. Algebra and Element Conventions fixes the majuscule of the field as the glyph Ξ, U+039E, and never the command that prints it. The corpus honours the rule 9 654 times and breaks it 4 268 times, and the break is silent: both spellings compile and only one reads as a glyph. §The Violations, rank 5, gives the sweep.
  12. φ and ϕ are split by corpus. \varphi is the Euler totient in maths, 6 195 sites, and occurs once in physics; \phi is the potential in physics and a rotation angle in maths. Neither file states which.

How to Re-measure

import glob, re, collections
files = glob.glob('articles_maths/*.md') + glob.glob('articles_physics/*.md')
span = re.compile(r'\$\$?(.+?)\$\$?', re.S)
count = collections.Counter()
for f in files:
    for m in span.finditer(open(f, encoding='utf-8').read()):
        body = re.sub(r'\\(?:mathrm|text|operatorname|mathbb|mathcal)\{[^{}]*\}', '', m.group(1))
        for g in re.finditer(r'\\([A-Za-z]+)|(?<![A-Za-z\\])([A-Za-z])(?![A-Za-z])', body):
            count[g.group(1) or g.group(2)] += 1
print(count.most_common(40))

The masking line removes upright and blackboard groups before the letters are counted, which is what keeps o and p out of \mathrm{opp}. Nested groups are masked to one level only; the tables above were built with a brace-matching pass that goes deeper.

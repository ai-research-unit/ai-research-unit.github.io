Audit of the Letters

What this file is. One row per glyph, with its sense and its count, for the two published collections. It is a measurement, not a rule. The reserved letters and the reserved element glyphs open the file in two tables of their own, each with a column for the reserved sense and a column for every other sense, so that a non-empty second column reads as a violation. Where a row disagrees with a rule of the conventions articles, the row is evidence and the rule is a claim, and the gap is listed in §The Gaps.

There is no history here, and no interest in one. What this file states is the corpora as they stand. A count that disagrees with the corpus is a defect and is overwritten, not annotated. A repair is reported as the state it leaves, never as the change it made. No row carries a date, an age, or a note on what it used to say, because a reader who wants the corpus wants the count, and a reader who wants the past has the repository. Two things survive an edit and only two: the glyph and its sense. Every number beside them is read again from the files.

Scope. articles_maths/*.md, 2 633 files, and articles_physics/*.md, 679 files. Only the math delimiters are read, $…$ and $$…$$; prose is not counted. A word inside a display is not counted letter by letter: the contents of \mathrm{…}, \text{…}, \operatorname{…}, \mathbb{…}, \mathcal{…} and their kin are excluded, so that \mathrm{opp} adds no p. An upright group that carries a subscript is read only through that subscript, and the subscript is a label (§The Labels). 812 157 spans in total, and 1 973 609 glyph occurrences inside them: 1 410 812 in the maths corpus, 562 797 in the physics corpus.

How the counts are made. One occurrence is one glyph between two math delimiters, or one Greek command. Each occurrence is filed under the mark that follows it and under its nesting: bare (no mark), marked (a subscript of its own), exponent (a superscript of its own), index (inside the subscript or the superscript of another glyph), accented (under \vec, \tilde, \bar, \hat, \breve, \check, \dot or \ddot). An accent overlaps the other four files and is not a fifth file. So $c^2$ is filed as an exponent, $e_x$ as marked, $\lambda_i$ as marked with the label i, and the $i$ of $\lambda_i$ as an index.

What the reading costs. The assignment of a mark to a sense is an interpretation, not a datum: a subscript may carry two senses, and a bare letter may be generic or may be a named object. The four context files are exact; the sense column is approximate.

What is not here. The blackboard letters \mathbb{B}, \mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{H}, \mathbb{F}, \mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{N}, \mathbb{K}, and the calligraphic ones, are objects of their own, tabulated in Algebra and Element Conventions, §Summary of Notation. The 699 upright labels are in §The Labels. The alphabets that are not LaTeX commands are in §The Unicode Letters.

The Reserved Letters

How the two columns are read. Reserved counts the occurrences of the glyph that carry the role the conventions reserve for it. Other counts the occurrences that carry a different sense, and a non-empty Other is a violation of the rule, in that many sites. Both are counted inside the math delimiters only, as the rest of this file is. A glyph that a corpus does not reserve stands as — in the Reserved column, and its whole total stands in Other without a verdict of violation: the maths corpus reserves eight letters and the physics corpus eleven, and the difference is stated in the conventions articles.

Three rows of the table need a word, because a reader will otherwise read them as errors of counting.

The i row is the largest violation of the two tables. The index is written \imath and \jmath in the physics corpus and i in the maths corpus. The occurrence that carries the index is the one nested in a subscript, 20 640 in maths and 3 111 in physics; an $i$ in a superscript, as in $e^{i\theta}$, stays the imaginary unit and is counted as reserved.

The c row reads as a violation only because the maths corpus does not reserve the letter. A corpus that excludes physics has no speed of light to reserve, and the row is evidence for the scoping of Reserved Letters Conventions, not a defect of the articles. The \tau and m rows are the same case: the maths corpus does not reserve either, and both are measured there for completeness.

The \Phi, \Psi row is clean. No display of either corpus puts a lowercase glyph where a sector belongs. The 85 lowercase sites that the Other column carries all sit in the eight conventions articles, in the sentence of §Sectors that forbids them, and the five objects that a lowercase would blur are written with a glyph of their own.

The Reserved Element Glyphs

The four families of Reserved Letters Conventions, §Reserved Element Glyphs are measured the same way. The field family is reserved in the physics article, and the maths corpus spends it too, so its maths column carries counts and no reservation. A potent is reserved when it carries the tag, so \Pi_{\mathrm{id}} and \Pi_{\mathrm{nil}} count as reserved and a bare \Pi does not. The root is the character, and the command that prints the majuscule is not counted as the character.

The \Pi shape is spent three ways, and the tag is what separates them. In maths, of 15 609 occurrences, 6 452 carry the tag \mathrm{id} or \mathrm{nil} and are reserved, 723 are bare and forbidden, and the remaining 8 434 carry a sign, a number or a label. Of the sign, \Pi_\pm is the projection and is permitted; a numbered or a labelled form is read from its subscript. In physics the same three figures are 4 264, 312 and 1 802.

In the same way, of the 14 318 occurrences of \pi in maths, 911 are a potent carrying a tag and 13 407 are the number, 8 300 of them bare; in physics the same two figures are 79 and 15 210, 11 042 of them bare.

The \xi, Ξ row is the only defect of the table that is typographic. The field is written correctly in 15 858 sites, and the other column is the same object written with the command \Xi rather than the character Ξ. The conventions forbid the command, because the majuscule of the field is meant to be read at a glance and never resolved against the element $X$; the repair is one token for one character, and it changes no sense.

The Violations, in Order of Cost

How a violation is repaired. The preferred repair is a more appropriate letter that is not reserved: the object moves to a glyph that names it better, and no glyph of §The Reserved Letters and §Reserved Element Glyphs is spent on it. A letter already bound in the display is never the answer, and a second sense on a reserved glyph is never the answer either. Where no such letter is free — because every nearby glyph is taken, or because the object has no customary letter — the fallback is a rare letter with a long subscript: a glyph the corpus seldom uses, carrying a subscript that spells the sense out in full, \digamma_{\mathrm{…}} being the model. The two rules are ordered: the appropriate letter first, the rare letter and the long subscript only when the first fails.

rank violation sites the fix
1 i as an index 23 751 _i, _{i} become _\imath, _{\imath}; 20 640 in maths and 3 111 in physics
2 \lambda as an eigenvalue, maths 21 317 the spectrum moves to \eta, the article titles included
3 c as a generic constant, maths 14 354 not a defect of the articles but of the scoping; a corpus without physics keeps c free
4 \Xi instead of the character Ξ 4 336 the token becomes the character; 4 271 in physics and 65 in maths
5 \nu as an index 3 302 the index moves to \imath; 3 038 in maths and 264 in physics
6 bare \Pi 1 035 the tag \mathrm{id} or \mathrm{nil}; 723 in maths and 312 in physics
7 \pi as an untagged potent 990 the potent becomes \Pi_{\mathrm{id}} or \Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}; 911 in maths and 79 in physics
8 Q as a named slot, physics 247 the slot leaves the Q family and takes \digamma with its label

The first three rows are five sixths of the outstanding work. Ranks 1 and 2 are mechanical: one glyph moves to another, and no sentence changes. Rank 2 is the only row that moves an object into one of the eleven reserved scalars, and it is allowed for the reason the rule exists: the object is the eigenvalue and \eta is the eigenvalue's own letter, so the repair removes a second sense rather than adding one. Every other row follows the preferred repair and spends no reserved glyph. Rank 4 is typographic and changes no sense. Rank 8 is the fallback class, where the object leaves a family that does not own it and takes \digamma with a long subscript.

The lowercase is not a row. \phi, \varphi and \psi occur 85 times in the two corpora, every one of them inside the eight conventions articles, where §Sectors has to print the glyph to forbid it. No article carries one. Two draft files that carry no .md suffix lie outside the scope of this file and still print the glyph at 13 sites. The five objects those glyphs name are written with a glyph of their own: the wavefunction $Ξ_{\mathrm{wfn}}$, the potential $\xi_{\mathrm{pot}}$, the map $\digamma_{\mathrm{map}}$, the formula of logic $\digamma_{\mathrm{fml}}$, the totient $\digamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$, and the rotation angle $\digamma_{\mathrm{ang}}$.

The Gaps

  1. λ has two lives. In physics, 95 sites, the wavelength, as the rule says. In maths, 21 317 sites, the eigenvalue: \lambda_i, \lambda_\pm, \lambda_k, the roots of a characteristic polynomial. The rule is written in both conventions articles and enforced in one. Either the maths corpus moves to \eta, or the rule is scoped to physics and the maths corpus gets a line of its own.
  2. ν is an index in maths. 3 038 sites there are nested in a subscript or a superscript, and the wave number is claimed in only 2 158.
  3. The space index has five spellings. Reserved Letters Conventions says the index is the dotless \imath, in both corpora. Index and Notation Conventions writes \vec e_i, \gamma_i and \varepsilon_{ijk} in its own statement of the rule, and its table of the slot lists \imath and \jmath beside \mu. §Wavenumbers of both conventions articles says "the space index is $k$". Measured: \imath 27 480 sites in physics and 129 in maths; \jmath 8 817 and 293; \mu 13 838 in maths and 2 585 in physics; i nested in a subscript 20 640 and 3 111; and k inside an index 16 924 and 2 339. Five spellings, three rules, and no file states the other four.
  4. h carries two objects in physics. The Planck constant, bare, and the informational time h_t, a length. One letter, two senses, separated only by the subscript; both rows exist in Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions.
  5. e carries three. The four units, the bulk of the 83 401 sites; Euler's number in $e^{i\theta}$; the elementary charge e^\pm. The first is told by the subscript, the second by the exponent, the third by the context.
  6. i and ı are two letters for one job. The imaginary unit is i everywhere; the index is i in the maths corpus and \imath in physics. The index use of i is 20 640 sites in maths and 3 111 in physics (§The Violations, rank 1); the imaginary unit of an exponential is a superscript and stays reserved.
  7. ι is used where the rule forbids it. Index and Notation Conventions says the letter carries no meaning and that a display introducing it is a defect. It occurs 25 times in physics, all as the Clifford map \iota_{\mathrm{Cl}}, and 1 666 times in maths.
  8. ο is documented twice and reserved nowhere. 808 sites, all in physics: the electrostatic potential \omicron_{\mathrm{pot}}, the Higgs components \omicron^\pm_{\mathrm{hgs}}, an angle \omicron_{\mathrm{ang}}. Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions names it in passing, and no table of the conventions articles lists it.
  9. ϖ has two homes. The twistor spinor \varpi_{\mathrm{twz}} and the Cartan weight, told apart by the label alone, 485 and 89 sites.
  10. Ξ is written with the command 4 336 times. Algebra and Element Conventions fixes the majuscule of the field as the character Ξ, U+039E, and never the command that prints it. The corpus honours the rule 15 858 times and breaks it 4 336 times, and the break is silent: both spellings compile and only one reads as a glyph.
  11. The field family is missing from the maths conventions. The maths corpus writes Ξ 1 861 times and \xi 133 times, and Reserved Letters Conventions of the maths article reserves three element families, $Q$, $Ζ$ and $\Pi$. The physics article reserves four and gives the field its own section. One sentence in the maths article closes the gap; until then the corpus is ahead of its own rule.
  12. The fallback has no register in maths. \digamma occurs 8 727 times in maths against 2 096 in physics, and the maths conventions article names it as the scalar fallback without listing the labels it carries. The corpus has coined \digamma_{\mathrm{map}} and \digamma_{\mathrm{fml}}; a register of the labels is what makes the fallback readable, and only the physics article has one.

How to Re-measure

The instrument is one pass, and it defines the four context files of §How the counts are made. Copy it to a temporary directory, run it from the root of the repository, and delete it afterwards.

import bisect, collections, glob, re, unicodedata

UP = r'\\(?:mathrm|text|operatorname|mathbb|mathcal)\s*\{[^{}]*\}'
GREEK = set('alpha beta gamma delta epsilon varepsilon zeta eta theta vartheta iota '
            'kappa varkappa lambda mu nu xi omicron pi varpi rho varrho sigma varsigma '
            'tau upsilon phi varphi chi psi omega Gamma Delta Theta Lambda Xi Pi Sigma '
            'Upsilon Phi Psi Omega digamma ell imath jmath aleph'.split())
ACC = {'vec', 'tilde', 'bar', 'hat', 'breve', 'check', 'dot', 'ddot'}
TOK = re.compile(UP + r'|\\([A-Za-z]+)|(?<![A-Za-z\\])([A-Za-z])(?![A-Za-z])')


def spans(txt):
    out, i, n = [], 0, len(txt)
    while i < n:
        if txt[i] == '\\':
            i += 2
            continue
        if txt[i] == '$':
            d = '$$' if txt.startswith('$$', i) else '$'
            k = j = i + len(d)
            while k < n:
                if txt[k] == '\\':
                    k += 2
                    continue
                if txt.startswith(d, k):
                    break
                k += 1
            if k >= n:
                break
            out.append(txt[j:k])
            i = k + len(d)
        else:
            i += 1
    return out


def arg(b, i):
    if i >= len(b):
        return '', i
    if b[i] == '{':
        d, j = 0, i
        while j < len(b):
            if b[j] == '{':
                d += 1
            elif b[j] == '}':
                d -= 1
                if d == 0:
                    return b[i + 1:j], j + 1
            j += 1
        return b[i + 1:], len(b)
    m = re.match(UP, b[i:]) or re.match(r'\\[A-Za-z]+', b[i:])
    return (m.group(0), i + m.end()) if m else (b[i], i + 1)


def glyphs(body):
    mask = [(m.start(), m.end()) for m in re.finditer(UP, body)]
    lo = [a for a, _ in mask]
    rng = []
    for m in re.finditer(r'[_^]', body):
        k = bisect.bisect_right(lo, m.start()) - 1
        if k >= 0 and mask[k][0] <= m.start() < mask[k][1]:
            continue
        rng.append((m.end(), arg(body, m.end())[1], body[m.start()]))
    rng.sort()
    start = [r[0] for r in rng]

    def where(p):
        k = bisect.bisect_right(start, p) - 1
        return rng[k][2] if k >= 0 and rng[k][0] <= p < rng[k][1] else '.'

    out, acc = [], None
    for m in TOK.finditer(body):
        name = m.group(1) or m.group(2)
        if m.group(2) is None and name not in GREEK:
            if name in ACC:
                acc = name
            continue
        j, sub, sup = m.end(), None, None
        while j < len(body) and body[j] in '_^':
            t, j2 = arg(body, j + 1)
            if body[j] == '_' and sub is None:
                sub = t
            if body[j] == '^' and sup is None:
                sup = t
            j = j2
        out.append((name, where(m.start()), sub, sup, acc))
        acc = None
    return out


def run(paths):
    per = collections.defaultdict(collections.Counter)
    uni = collections.Counter()
    labels = collections.Counter()
    spans_n = 0
    for f in sorted(paths):
        txt = open(f, encoding='utf-8').read()
        for body in spans(txt):
            spans_n += 1
            for ch in body:
                if ord(ch) > 127 and unicodedata.category(ch).startswith('L'):
                    uni[ch] += 1
            for name, ctx, sub, sup, acc in glyphs(body):
                c = per[name]
                c['total'] += 1
                if acc:
                    c['acc'] += 1
                if ctx == '.':
                    c['sub' if sub is not None else 'sup' if sup is not None else 'bare'] += 1
                elif ctx == '_':
                    c['low'] += 1
                else:
                    c['high'] += 1
                if sub and ctx == '.':
                    m = re.match(r'^\\[A-Za-z]+\{(.*?)\}', sub)
                    if m:
                        labels[m.group(1)] += 1
    return per, uni, labels, spans_n


for tag, paths in (('maths', glob.glob('articles_maths/*.md')),
                   ('physics', glob.glob('articles_physics/*.md'))):
    per, uni, labels, spans_n = run(paths)
    print(tag, spans_n, sum(v['total'] for v in per.values()), len(labels))
    print(dict(per['nu']))

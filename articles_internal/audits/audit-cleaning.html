Cleaning Rules: Removing the Workshop and Keeping the Physics

Status of this file. Executed once, and written from that pass. The rules below were distilled after one cleaning of articles_internal/internal-note-the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md, which removed 6 403 words, every bold marker, and twelve of its thirty-two tables without touching an equation. The numbers are in §11, and they are the only evidence that the pass was mechanical rather than editorial.

What the file is for. It is the checklist for the next cleaning: what to remove, what to keep, what to verify, and in what order. It is a pass over one file, not a licence to sweep the corpus.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. Confirm the target before the first edit. Print the path and the first heading; never infer the file from a title. Two articles in this repository share a title and differ by one directory.
  2. Every equation survives, byte for byte. A cleaning is not a rewrite, not a summary and not a shortening.
  3. Zero bold in the finished file, checked both on the Markdown and on the built page.
  4. Never repair the physics. A symbol that looks wrong is reported, not changed.
  5. Never add a sentence that was not there. A cleaning that improves the argument has stopped being a cleaning.

1. What a Cleaning Is

A cleaning is the pass that takes one finished article and removes from it everything that belongs to the workshop rather than to the physics: migration bookkeeping, status and tier labels, file names, revision history, self-criticism, addresses to the reader about the writing, and bold lead-ins.

A cleaning is not a rewrite, not a summary and not a shortening. It removes noise and keeps the physics. The test is mechanical: put the old file and the new file side by side, and every equation, every number, every table that states a claim, and every step of every argument must still be there.

The one line that decides a hard case: an argument about the model stays; an argument about the note's own drafting goes. "The strip is legitimate because the equality was conditional on parity" is physics. "This note did not use that reason until the file was recovered" is drafting.

2. The Three Ways to Get It Wrong

  1. Cleaning the wrong file. On 2026-11-21 an instruction naming articles_internal/internal-note-the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md was carried out on articles_physics/the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md. Two files had to be restored from git. The cause is that the two articles share a title and differ by one directory. The cure is in step 1 of the procedure: print the path and the first heading, and never infer the target from a title.
  2. Cleaning the physics away. Removing a bold lead-in is safe. Removing the sentence under it because it "reads like commentary" is not. The bold comes off the sentence; the sentence stays.
  3. Leaving the noise in. A pass that removes two thirds of the bold and keeps the rest is not finished. The end state is exact: zero ** in the file and a zero bold share in the built page. Partial cleaning is worse than none, because the next reader cannot tell whether a surviving sentence survived on purpose.

3. What Counts as Workshop Prose

Remove, in every form:

class what it looks like the tell
status and tier labels "built?", "not yet", "yes", "merged", "never written", "done", "phase 2" the row answers a question about the project, not about nature
file bookkeeping backticked paths, "the file is withdrawn", "the rename is complete", "alias", "sitemap prints 3138 urls", "the citation resolves" it names a .md or .html file, or a build artefact
migration machinery "the four rules", "the dictionary", "the work list", "what changes in article X", "article-by-article plan" it instructs a future editor
register, boundary, ledger, frame-scorecard as bookkeeping "the ledger shows", "the register records", "the boundary is respected" the noun refers to the corpus's own accounting, not to a physical object or a gauge-invariant statement
revision history "an earlier draft said", "this note did not use it until", "the file was recovered", "the author reversed" it narrates the writing
self-criticism and apology "the honest form of the statement", "stated fairly, because it deserves that", "this is where a reader expects a dodge", "the sentence that must not be written" the subject is the note's honesty, not the model
addresses to the reader "we now turn to", "the next section will", "as the reader will see" it manages the reading instead of stating a fact
bold lead-ins **1.1 The group and the content.** see the bold rule below
cross-references to removed sections §7.4, §8.4a, §12.6 the target no longer exists

Two of these need judgement, and the judgement is narrow.

  • The confirmation and legitimacy arguments stay. A section that argues the model is not in conflict with experiment, or that dropping an assumption is legitimate rather than convenient, is physics. What goes is the sentence that reports on that argument ("both are stated as what they are").
  • A table stays if its rows carry physics. A verdict column reading confirmed, consistent, not contradicted stays, because it states what the model says about a measurement. A verdict column reading keep, withdraw, relabel goes, because it states what an editor should do.

4. What Is Physics

Keep, without exception:

  • Every display equation, byte for byte. Not re-typeset, not re-ordered, not "tidied". If a symbol looks wrong, it is still not a cleaning edit; report it instead.
  • Every table row that carries a number, a claim, a bound or a model comparison, with its header row.
  • Every definition and every derivation step. A derivation that a cleaning pass finds redundant is still a derivation.
  • Every number with its premise. A bound quoted as $10^{6}$ must still say $b_{B-L}=12$ and "one-loop" wherever it said so before.
  • The section structure, minus the sections that are pure bookkeeping.

5. The Bold Rule

  • Zero ** in the finished file. This is the single mechanical target of the pass.
  • The title keeps whatever form the file already has. # Title stays; # __Title__ stays. A cleaning never changes the title's markup, because build.py reads the first heading with re.match(r"#\s*__(.+?)__\s*$", s) and a title that loses its underscores changes the page title.
  • A bold lead-in becomes a plain sentence, and the paragraph continues. **1.1 The group and the content.** The gauge group is becomes 1.1 The group and the content. The gauge group is. The label is kept because it is the section's number; only the weight goes.
  • A bold phrase inside a sentence loses the asterisks and keeps the words. If the emphasis was carrying a claim — **with** $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ — the claim must survive in plain words, so rewrite the clause rather than stripping it: "and with $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$".
  • Italic is allowed sparingly, for a quoted rule or a quoted verdict, never as a substitute for bold emphasis.
  • Nothing inside $...$ is touched. build.py protects math with a regex before Markdown conversion, so _ and * inside math never became bold; keep them exactly as they are.

6. The Procedure, Step by Step

Step 1 — Confirm the target, before any edit.

cd /home/hp/Documents/1_Doc/Projects/ai-research/ai-research-unit
f=<the path named in the instruction>
ls -la "$f"; head -3 "$f"; git ls-files --error-unmatch "$f" >/dev/null 2>&1 && echo tracked || echo "NOT tracked"

The first heading must match the article the instruction names. If it does not, stop and ask.

Step 2 — Back up the original.

  • Tracked by git: nothing to do; git show HEAD:$f is the copy.
  • Not tracked: archive it to _reserve/others/<name>.7z now. articles_internal/ and _reserve/ are gitignored (.gitignore lines 19–20), so files there have no history. The house form is a 7z holding one file:
cp "$f" /tmp/orig.md
( cd _reserve/others && cp /tmp/orig.md "$(basename "$f")" && 7z a -t7z -y "$(basename "$f").7z" "$(basename "$f")" && rm "$(basename "$f")" )
7z l "_reserve/others/$(basename "$f").7z" | tail -3   # asserts the size is the original's

The archive holds the version you replaced, not the version you wrote. A pass that archives its own output has lost the original.

Step 3 — Take the skeleton.

python3 - <<'PY'
import re
t=open('<file>').read()
print("words",len(t.split()),"| bold",t.count('**'),"| eq",len(re.findall(r'\$\$(.*?)\$\$',t,re.S)))
print("\n".join(l for l in t.split('\n') if l.startswith('#')))
PY

Copy the list of display equations to a scratch file. That list is the acceptance test in step 5, and it must be taken before the first edit.

Step 4 — Rewrite in parts, outside the repository.

Write /tmp/p1.md, /tmp/p2.md, … and concatenate. Three reasons: a 14,000-word single write cannot be reviewed in one read; a part can be checked against the original section by section; and nothing half-finished ever touches the live file.

Rewrite rules for the prose:

  1. Keep the sentence, change its dress. Most of the work is **X.** Y → X. Y. Do not paraphrase Y.
  2. Delete whole sentences only when the subject is the writing, and then delete the whole sentence, never half of it.
  3. Delete a whole table only when every row is workshop. A table that mixes physics rows and bookkeeping rows is rebuilt with the physics rows; the alternative — deleting it — loses numbers.
  4. Do not merge sections and do not reorder them, except for the renumbering below.
  5. Do not add a sentence that was not there. A cleaning that improves the argument has stopped being a cleaning.

Step 5 — Verify before installing. Run the check block in the next section. A failure in equation parity or in a physics table row means the part is wrong; fix it before concatenating, not after.

Step 6 — Install, build, measure.

cp /tmp/clean.md "$f"
.venv/bin/python build.py | tail -5

The output goes to /home/hp/Documents/1_Doc/Projects/ai-research/ai-research-unit-deploy/, not next to the source (DEPLOY in build.py, line 13). Measure the bold share on the deployed file, not on the Markdown, because the Markdown cannot tell you what survived per word.

Step 7 — Report in one table. Words before and after, bold before and after, equations, tables, rows. Numbers, not adjectives.

7. Renumbering

Removing a section renumbers every section after it, and a stale § reference is the one defect that reads as physics while pointing nowhere. Renumber in the same pass, then check that every reference resolves.

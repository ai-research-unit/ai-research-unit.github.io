Audit: The Primordial Coupling — One Scalar Factor of Two, and What Moves With It

Status of this file. A review, not an edit. No article was modified and no menu entry was touched. Every number below was recomputed from the article's own inputs, $M_X=3.73\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}=2.3281\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{GeV}$ and $g_4^{2}(M_X)=0.730$, and a maximum of a hundred elements was recomputed per claim.

What the file is for. It records what two independent reviews of articles_physics/the-primordial-coupling-g4-as-a-boundary-condition-and-what-runs-above-m-c.md got right, what they missed, and what this audit corrects in its own earlier reading. The article's headline coefficient is wrong by a factor of two in one term, and the error is invisible to the comparison the article uses to check itself.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. One finding is substantive: the scalar term counts thirty-two real components where the field has sixteen. Everything else in this file is either a consequence of that or presentational.
  2. The article's own table states the contradiction. The column that says "sixteen real" sits beside arithmetic that charges the term for thirty-two.
  3. The conclusion of the article survives, every qualitative claim survives, and every number downstream moves. $b_0$ becomes $26/3$, the pole becomes $885\ \mathrm{MeV}$, and a second article must be patched with it.
  4. Two reviews missed the finding, and each missed it differently. One praised the scalar table as the article's best contribution; the other rewrote the QCD sentence while keeping the coefficient that the sentence is supposed to produce.
  5. Nothing here is applied without the author's decision. §11 lists the five decisions.

1. The Verdict

The article is sound in structure and wrong in one coefficient. Its ledger, its conventions block, its pole discipline and its separation of import from computation are all correct and worth keeping as they stand. Its scalar term is charged for a complex adjoint where the field is one real adjoint, so $b_0=8$ should be $b_0=26/3=8.667$.

The error is a factor of two in the third term and it propagates to five places in this article and to three other files. The direction of every qualitative claim is unchanged: $b_0>0$, asymptotic freedom, weak coupling at the transition, no confinement, pole as artefact. The magnitudes are not.

Why it survived two reviews. The article checks itself against QCD, and QCD has no scalar. Every cross-check in the piece — $b_0$ against $11-\tfrac23N_f$, the four thirds, the eight thirds — is a fermion check. A term that only a scalar can exercise was never exercised.

2. The Finding: the Scalar Term Counts Thirty-Two Where the Field Has Sixteen

The formula the article uses is the complex-scalar form, and the field it feeds is real. Line 68 and line 71 of the article read

$$ b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_{A}-\frac{4}{3}T_{R}N_{f}-\frac{1}{3}T_{S}N_{s}, $$

with "$T_{S}N_{s}$ the total Dynkin index of the scalars counted as complex fields" and $N_{s}=1$ adjoint. One complex adjoint is two real adjoints. $\Xi$ is one.

2.1 The three warrants

Warrant one, the article's own table. The row reads "the scalar | $T_{S}=4$ | $\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1$, sixteen real | $-\tfrac43=-1.333$". The two middle columns disagree with each other. Sixteen real components is $\tfrac16\times4=\tfrac23$; thirty-two is $\tfrac13\times4=\tfrac43$. The table prints the count of one and the term of the other.

Warrant two, the corpus's own formula article. the-heavy-sector-below-its-threshold-decoupling-matching-and-the-one-loop.md, §4.3, gives

$$ b=\tfrac{11}{3}C_2(G)-\tfrac{4}{3}T_F n_f-\tfrac{1}{6}T_S n_s, $$

"with $n_f$ and $n_s$ the number of Dirac fermions and real scalars in the representation". The corpus already owns the real-scalar convention, and the article under review uses the other one without converting.

Warrant three, the benchmarks, and the reason the article's own checks could not catch this. The two conventions are the same convention whenever the multiplet has an even number of real components, because then $n_s$ real is $\tfrac{n_s}{2}$ complex and the arithmetic coincides. Every standard benchmark is of that kind, which is exactly why the error survived three cross-checks:

theory content $\tfrac16$ with $n_s$ real $\tfrac13$ with $N_s$ complex separates?
$\mathcal N=2$ SYM, $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ vector, $1$ Dirac adjoint, $2$ real adjoint $8$ $8$, at $N_s=1$ no: $2$ real $=1$ complex
$\mathcal N=4$ SYM, $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ vector, $2$ Dirac adjoint, $6$ real adjoint $0$ $0$, at $N_s=3$ no: $6$ real $=3$ complex
the SM's $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ $6$ Dirac doublets, one doublet $\tfrac{19}{6}$ $\tfrac{19}{6}$ no: $2$ real $=1$ complex
one real adjoint and nothing else, $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ vector $+$ one real adjoint $14$ $13\tfrac13$ yes
the model vector, $8$ Dirac fundamental, one real adjoint $\tfrac{26}{3}$ $8$ yes

The separating case is the model's own situation, not a textbook one. A Hermitian matrix has sixteen real components and nothing pairs them, so its screening is that of sixteen real scalars and not of a complex field. The last row of the table is the article's content read both ways, and it differs by $\tfrac23$ — the factor of two in one term, which is the whole finding.

The physical warrant is one sentence. Screening is linear in the number of real components; a complex field is two real ones; charging sixteen real components at the complex rate charges thirty-two.

The signs are right in the article, and only the factor is wrong. Antiscreening from the adjoint, screening from fermions and scalars: the article states that pattern correctly at line 71. What it gets wrong is the size of the third term, which is the one term the pattern cannot check.

2.2 The repair, in two lines

Either keep $\tfrac13 T_SN_s$ and write $N_s=\tfrac12$ for one real adjoint, which is correct and unreadable, or adopt the corpus's $\tfrac16$ form:

$$ b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_{A}-\frac{4}{3}T_{R}N_{f}-\frac{1}{6}T_{S}n_{s},\qquad n_{s}=1 \text{ real adjoint}, $$

$$ b_0=\frac{44}{3}-\frac{16}{3}-\frac{2}{3}=\frac{26}{3}=8.667 . $$

The second form is the one to take, because it is the form the heavy-sector article already states, and because it makes the "real components" column of the scalar table the natural one rather than a warning label.

Its "$8$" is what a complex multiplet gives; its "$\tfrac{20}{3}$" is what $64$ real components give; its "$4$" is what $128$ real components give, a field the algebra does not have. Each doubling doubles the term; only the second step happens to equal the number the article quotes for both.

5. The Matching-Scale Paragraph: Coefficient, Sign, and a Bound That Runs the Wrong Way

Three things are wrong in one paragraph, and the reviews caught one of them.

The coefficient follows $b_0$. With $b_0=\tfrac{26}{3}$,

$$ \Delta\Bigl(\frac{1}{\alpha}\Bigr)=\frac{\tfrac{26}{3}-7}{2\pi}\ln\frac{M_X}{M_C}=0.265\,\ln\frac{M_X}{M_C}, $$

so the article's $0.159$ becomes $0.265$.

The sign is not fixed by the corpus, and the article's own numbers point the other way. The article writes $+0.11$ for $M_X$ within a factor of two of $M_C$, which presumes $M_X>M_C$. But $M_X^{2}=\tfrac12g_4^{2}v_C^{2}=32v^{2}$ gives $M_X=5.66\,v$ while $M_C>v$, so the ordering is not fixed: if $M_C$ sits near $v$ then $M_X$ is the larger, and if $M_C$ is well above $v$ then $M_X$ is the smaller. The corpus's own numerical table takes the second branch, putting $M_X=2.33\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{GeV}$ below $M_C=2.5\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{GeV}$. The honest form states the magnitude and the direction of growth, not a sign.

The "factor of two" is not a worst case but the article's own tightest case. $M_C$ is bounded below and not predicted, so $|\ln(M_X/M_C)|$ has no upper bound: at the corpus's pair it is $0.07$, at a factor of two it is $0.69$, and at $M_C=10M_X$ it is $2.3$, where the shift is $0.61$ in $1/\alpha_4$ or $3.5$ per cent. So the honest sentence is "a tenth of a per cent at the corpus's own pair, one per cent for a factor of two, a few per cent if $M_C$ is a decade above $M_X$", and it is smaller than the two-loop term in the first two cases and comparable in the third.

This also answers the question the first review asked and could not answer. Which way does the corpus's choice err? If $M_X<M_C$ it makes $\alpha_4$ at the matching scale smaller than strict matching at $M_C$ would give, so the phase is very slightly weaker, which is the direction the article already calls conservative. If $M_X>M_C$ the sign reverses. Since the sign is not fixed, the sentence should say "of this magnitude, in whichever direction the two scales are ordered", and drop the claim that the shift is one-signed.

6. The Pole Analogy: QCD Has No Ultraviolet Landau Pole

Both reviews praised this sentence. It is wrong twice.

The article says the pole "is the same kind of object as the QCD Landau pole at $10^{19}\ \mathrm{GeV}$". The corpus's own asymptotic-freedom article says the opposite of the first half:

QED has $b_0=-\tfrac43$ … $1/\alpha$ falls steadily and would reach zero at $\exp(3\pi/2\alpha)\approx1.6\times10^{270}\ \mathrm{J}$. That scale is called the Landau pole … The sign of the beta function decides whether a theory has an ultraviolet problem or an infrared one, and QCD has the second.

QCD is asymptotically free, so it has no ultraviolet pole. Its one-loop line does reach $1/\alpha_s=0$, but downward, at $45\ \mathrm{MeV}$ — that is $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$, the infrared end.

And $10^{19}\ \mathrm{GeV}$ is the Planck scale, not a Landau pole. The corpus's two pole values are $1.6\times10^{270}\ \mathrm{J}$ for QED and $2.884\times10^{31}\ \mathrm{J}$ for hypercharge, the second being $1.8\times10^{41}\ \mathrm{GeV}$. $10^{19}\ \mathrm{GeV}$ is $1.6\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{J}$: two hundred and sixty-one orders below the first and twenty-two below the second. The number in the sentence is the Planck mass wearing the wrong name.

Two repairs are available and the author should choose.

  • The QED form. "The same kind of object as the Landau pole of QED, which the asymptotic-freedom article puts at $1.6\times10^{270}\ \mathrm{J}$": a zero of a truncated line, two hundred and sixty-one orders beyond anything the truncation describes.
  • The QCD infrared form, which is closer in structure. "The same kind of object as the zero of the QCD line at $45\ \mathrm{MeV}$": a scale where the one-loop solution formally diverges, read as physics only because QCD adds non-perturbative dynamics that this phase does not have. This one keeps the QCD comparison the article wants and puts the right number on it.

The paragraph's conclusion is unaffected. The pole is arithmetic; the phase ends first; nothing at $885\ \mathrm{MeV}$ is predicted. Only the analogy's warrant is missing.

7. The QCD Decomposition Sentence

The second review is right that the sentence does not add up, and its replacement keeps the coefficient the sentence is supposed to produce. Both are now wrong.

The article's count is wrong. "Four more Dirac fields" against QCD's six: the model has eight, so there are two more. Two Dirac cost $\tfrac43\times\tfrac12\times2=\tfrac43$.

The article's arithmetic is measured against the wrong total. With the corrected scalar, the decomposition is

$$ \tfrac{11}{3}\times(4-3)=\tfrac{11}{3}=3.667, \qquad -\tfrac{4}{3}\times\tfrac12\times(8-6)=-\tfrac43=-1.333, \qquad -\tfrac{2}{3}=-0.667, $$

$$ \tfrac{11}{3}-\tfrac43-\tfrac23=\tfrac53=1.667, $$

and $b_0^{\mathrm{model}}-b_0^{\mathrm{QCD}}=\tfrac{26}{3}-7=\tfrac53$, which agrees. The corrected sentence is "two more Dirac fields cost $\tfrac43$ and the scalar costs $\tfrac23$, netting $+\tfrac53$". The second review's replacement — two Dirac and the scalar costing $\tfrac83$ and netting $+1$ — reproduces the erroneous $b_0=8$ and must not be used.

8. The Two-Loop Estimate Both Reviews Asked For

The article asserts three times that a scalar doubling is "larger than the two-loop term the article omits", and never gives the term. The corpus supplies the measurement.

asymptotic-freedom-and-the-running-of-the-strong-coupling.md compares one loop against two at the scales where $\alpha_s$ is measured: ten per cent apart at $3.2\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J}$, where $\alpha_s\approx0.3$, and one per cent at $4.8\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{J}$. The relative size of the two-loop term is first order in $\alpha$, so at $\alpha_4=0.058$ it is

$$ 10\%\times\frac{0.058}{0.3}\approx2\%, $$

that is about $0.9$ units in $1/\alpha_4(M_P)$ out of the article's $43.6$.

The scalar doubling is $3.35$ units — $\tfrac{26/3-8}{2\pi}\ln(M_P/M_X)=0.106\times31.6$ — which is $7.7$ per cent of the change, about four times the two-loop term. So the article's claim survives comfortably and can now be stated with the number instead of the adjective:

  • "the two-loop correction is about two per cent of the change, and each scalar doubling is about eight per cent";
  • "$\alpha_4/\pi=0.018$ at the matching scale, which is why the one-loop expansion is good to a few per cent" — the same estimate, applied to the sentence at line 146 that the second review flagged as asserted.

This closes both reviews' first item. Neither could supply the number because neither noticed that the corpus contains the measurement.

9. Minor Findings

Eleven rows under "Ten numbers". §The Checks says "Ten numbers, each recomputed" and tabulates eleven. Neither review counted.

The unit switch. $M_X$ is $3.7\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$ in §Conventions and $2.3\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{GeV}$ everywhere else. One conversion line at first use closes it. Also: two other articles quote this bound differently, as $3.69\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$ and as $4.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$ rounded. The article is explicit that it uses $3.73\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$, which is the honest move; a note that the pole moves by $7$ per cent if the rounded $4.0$ is used would be enough.

$M_C\gtrsim10^{5}\ \mathrm{GeV}$ at line 166 is the article's own rounding of a corpus bound that reads $2.5\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{GeV}$. The two uses of the same scale in one article should agree.

The custodial bullet. "$\rho=1$ at tree level comes from the two-index structure of $\Xi$" is a claim owned by custodial-symmetry-and-the-rho-parameter.md, and that article's own summary is more careful: the algebra supplies the custodial action, and the framework's scalar sector does not supply the field it would act on. Either cite it or drop the bullet; as written it is the one sentence in the article with no warrant.

"The model has one coupling." Corpus language, in $53$ sites and in an article title, so it should not be changed globally. One qualifier at first use — "one gauge coupling" — costs nothing and removes the reading that the scalar quartic is being denied.

The fermion pairing. "A Weyl field contributes half a Dirac field's screening, and the sixteen Weyl fields are eight Dirac fields once the copies are paired" is correct but leaves the reader to supply $2\times(\mathbf4+\bar{\mathbf4})=8$. One clause.

The $1\ \mathrm{TeV}$ value. $0.103$ now. It is flagged as not a prediction and appears in running text; either mark it or drop it.

"The fifteen, the sixteen and the scalar" at line 13 wants two words of apposition.

$\zeta$. The notation row "absent from everything in this article" is a corpus habit and reads as intended once the reader knows $\zeta$ is the gauge-fixing parameter. Parenthetical only.

The decisions.

  • D1. The scalar factor. Accept $b_0=\tfrac{26}{3}$ and the $\tfrac16$ form, or keep $\tfrac13$ and defend $N_s=1$ as one complex adjoint. The audit recommends acceptance: the article's own table says sixteen real, the corpus's formula article uses the real-scalar convention, and a single real adjoint is the separating case, at $14$ against $13\tfrac13$. The contrary reading requires the field to be complex, which the scalar-content article explicitly denies.
  • D2. The downstream patch. Patch the confinement article and the scalar-content article in the same pass, or leave them and record the inconsistency. The audit recommends patching: the confinement article cites this one for the number, and a cited number that no longer matches its source is worse than either value alone.
  • D3. The pole analogy. QED form, or a QCD comparison. The audit recommends the QED form at $1.6\times10^{270}\ \mathrm{J}$, because a zero of a truncated line is the object the article needs and QCD's own zero is a physical scale — that is precisely what makes it a different kind of object. The QCD comparison is kept as one contrast sentence, at the corpus's own $0.111\ \mathrm{GeV}$. Applied that way.
  • D4. The matching paragraph. State the magnitude without a sign, or fix the ordering of $M_X$ and $M_C$ in the corpus first. The audit recommends the magnitude form, since fixing the ordering is a decision about the model and not about this article.
  • D5. The style items of §9. Apply or defer.

What this audit did not do. It did not compute $b_1$, and it did not claim the two per cent of §8 is anything better than an order-of-magnitude scaling of the corpus's own QCD comparison. It did not resolve $M_X$ against $M_C$, which is a model question.

14. A Third Review, and Why the Error Is Attractive

A third review of the article, read after the patches, again reports $b_0=8$ and again checks the arithmetic against that value. Its tables reproduce $\tfrac{11}{3}4-\tfrac43\tfrac12 8-\tfrac13 4=8$ and then treat the $8$ as the Hermitian sector's; its own diagnostic table lists "Hermitian $8$, complex $20/3$, doubled $4$", which is the article's table shifted one doubling down. The coefficient is the one the article now assigns to a complex scalar, so the review is reading the pre-patch text, and every number it derives — $\alpha_4(M_P)=0.0174$, the pole at $313\ \mathrm{MeV}$, the $0.6\%$ matching shift, $\Delta(1/\alpha)=0.159\ln$ — belongs to that text as well.

The error is attractive for the reason §1 gives, and it survives a review that is otherwise careful. A reviewer who recomputes the fermion term, the adjoint term and the QCD comparison correctly has exercised every term except the scalar one, and $\tfrac13$ against $\tfrac16$ is a factor of two that no fermionic cross-check can see. The test that catches it is the Dynkin index of the $\mathbf{15}$ of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, which equals $C_A=4$ by coincidence at $N=4$, so $T_S n_s=4\cdot1=4$ and the term is $\tfrac16\times4=\tfrac23$ and not $\tfrac43$.

One sentence of that review is a real catch, and the article already carries the repair. It objects that "four more Dirac fields and the scalar cost $\tfrac83$" is miscounted at eight Dirac fields against QCD's six; the article now says two more Dirac fields at $\tfrac43$ and the scalar at $\tfrac23$, subtracting $2$ and netting $+\tfrac53$.

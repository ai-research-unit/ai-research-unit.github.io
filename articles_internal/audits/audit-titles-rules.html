Title Compliance Outside the First Category: A Census Against the Title Rules of the Plan

Status of this file. Audit only. No title, no slug, no menu line and no cross-reference is edited here. The measurement covers the 3 333 articles the two menus list outside their first category: 700 physics and 2 633 mathematics.

Correction applied 2026-11-26: the 24 mechanical breaches are repaired (§The Correction Applied), and 18 rhetorical titles, 5 drifted slugs and 1 unlinked article were closed in the same day's second pass (§The Second Pass). The tables above those sections record the corpus as measured.

What this file is for. It answers one question: which articles carry a title that the PLAN's title rules forbid. It separates the two kinds of answer — the sentences the PLAN writes in the imperative, which a checker settles, from the reading test, which a reader settles — lists the first exhaustively, and counts the second.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. The rule is PLAN.md §RULES TO WRITE TITLES and §RULES DURING THE WRITING MODE, and it is written for a physics title. Its first sentence is a physics title is a compressed abstract, not a chapter heading.
  2. A title is the first line # __…__ of the file. The word count drops mathematics between $…$, so a formula counts as one word.
  3. A conjunction title is one whose main clause is "… and the/their/its …". It breaches the letter of the ban. Whether it breaches the intent depends on whether a result follows the colon, and §Physics: The Conjunction Titles separates the two.
  4. The mathematics corpus is measured, not condemned. §Mathematics states the scope question and leaves the decision to the author.
  5. No option renames a file by itself. A rename costs a URL, and the build has no redirect table.

Introduction

The verdict. Outside the first category, 23 physics articles and 1 mathematics article break a sentence the PLAN writes in the imperative. A further 204 physics articles and 459 mathematics articles carry the conjunction pattern the section bans by name, and 30 more carry it with a result after the colon.

The corpus was written before the rule. Two habits predate it and the rule now forbids both. The first is the pair title, The X and the Y, which names two topics and no result. The second is the framework suffix, … in Biquaternionic Form, which names the carrier and never the result; 224 physics titles carry it.

The repairable set is small. Length, colon count, question marks, narrative subtitles, personified objects: 24 articles, 23 physics and 1 mathematics. An earlier draft of this file said 22 physics; the recount below is the authority. Every one is listed with its slug, and none needs a change of subject, only of wording.

The expensive set is the idiom. The 234 physics conjunctions and the 465 mathematics conjunctions are not 699 mistakes in prose. They are the site's habit. Option A makes them compliant with one paragraph in the PLAN; Option D retitles them at the cost of 699 URLs.

What this file does not claim. It does not claim that a pair title is bad prose, and it does not claim that the physics is wrong. It does not claim that the mathematics corpus is out of compliance: the section names physics, and a reference article that names its subject has done what a reference title can do. And it does not claim that the count is complete: the first category is excluded by instruction, and it holds four titles of the same kinds (§The First Category, Excluded).

The Rule, Restated

The PLAN states five imperative sentences and one test. They are quoted here because the census is a census against these and nothing else.

The reading test has three questions. Could a physicist who has not read the abstract identify (a) the model or object, (b) the construction or result, and (c) the key number or property? A title that answers (a) alone is a topic title. The rule calls it a chapter heading and forbids it.

The rule states a target style. Write titles in the style of Physical Review D / JHEP / Nuclear Physics B. That sentence is why the ban on the pair title is not a matter of taste: the pair title is a review-chapter heading, and the journals named do not print one.

Scope and Method

The two menus are the definition of the corpus. An article is in scope when physics.md or maths.md links it under a category heading. physics.md has 15 ## sections holding 727 links; the first holds 27 and is excluded, leaving 700. maths.md has 76 ## headings, of which 7 are PART banners with no links and 69 are categories holding 2 645 links; the first holds 12 and is excluded, leaving 2 633.

Every in-scope slug resolved to a file. 3 333 of 3 333 opened, and every one begins with a # __…__ title line after an optional blank first line. No file was skipped and none was guessed from its name.

Three things are not counted as breaches. A colon followed by a list of contents is the corpus's normal result clause, and it is not a narrative clause. A formula inside $…$ is one word, not fifteen. A hyphenated compound such as Two-Sided is one word, and it is not the collective The Two.

The measurement is of 2026-11-26, on the working tree. It is reproducible by the checker of §How to Re-Measure, which is thirty lines and reads the same two menus.

import re, os
P = "/home/hp/Documents/1_Doc/Projects/ai-research/ai-research-unit"
def sections(menu):
    out, cur = [], None
    for line in open(os.path.join(P, menu), encoding="utf-8"):
        if line.startswith("## "):
            cur = {"name": line[3:].strip(), "items": []}; out.append(cur); continue
        m = re.search(r'href="articles_(maths|physics)/([^"]+)\.html"', line)
        if m and cur is not None:
            cur["items"].append((m.group(1), m.group(2)))
    return out[1:]                      # the first category is excluded
def title(path):
    for line in open(path, encoding="utf-8"):
        line = line.strip()
        if line: return re.match(r"^# __(.+)__$", line).group(1)
def words(t):
    return len(re.findall(r"[A-Za-z0-9&/'-]+", re.sub(r"\$[^$]*\$", "M", t)))
CONJ = re.compile(r"^(?:The )?[^:]*?\band (?:the|their|its)\b[^:]*$")
NARR = re.compile(r"\band (Why|Where|What|How)\b|\b(and|or) (No|Not)\b")
for menu in ("physics.md", "maths.md"):
    for sec in sections(menu):
        for coll, slug in sec["items"]:
            t = title(os.path.join(P, f"articles_{coll}", slug + ".md"))
            h, s = (t.split(":", 1) + [""])[:2]
            why = []
            if words(t) > 20: why.append("over-20")
            if "\u2014" in t: why.append("em-dash")
            if CONJ.match(t) and ":" not in t: why.append("conjunction")
            if ":" in t and CONJ.match(h): why.append("conjunction-head")
            if NARR.search(s): why.append("narrative")
            if t.startswith("Nothing"): why.append("nothing")
            if "Printed" in t: why.append("printed")
            if why: print(menu, sec["name"], "|", ",".join(why), "|", slug)

Confirmed Defects

1. The inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is never defined in this article

Line 132 writes $M^2_{ab}=g_4^2v_C^2\langle[t_a,D],[t_b,D]\rangle$, and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ appears nowhere else in the file without definition. The parent article defines it at its own line 198 — "the Gram entry $\langle d_2,d_3\rangle=-\operatorname{tr}(d_2d_3)=-1$" — so the corpus has the convention and this article uses it without importing it.

Why it matters here and not elsewhere. The rank statement is a Gram-matrix statement: a Gram matrix is positive semi-definite, and its rank is the dimension of the span only under a positive-definite pairing. For anti-Hermitian $X,Y$ the pairing $-\operatorname{tr}(XY)=\operatorname{tr}(X^{\dagger}Y)$ is positive definite, and that is the pairing the article's "[t_a,D]^\dagger[t_b,D]" already writes. The two notations are the same object and the article does not say so.

Repair, one clause: after the display, "with $\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(X^{\dagger}Y)=-\operatorname{tr}(XY)$ on anti-Hermitian matrices, positive definite."

2. The "quartet reading" sentence gives the right count by the wrong mechanism

The article: "the quadrilinear count differs because the commutator's structure constants enter."

The structure constants are not the reason the counts differ. A quartet's quadrilinear term is $g^2A_aA_b\,\phi^{\dagger}t_at_b\phi=g^2A_aA_b\tfrac12\delta_{ab}\lvert\phi\rvert^2$ — diagonal in $(a,b)$, hence 15 vertices, not 120. The matrix's term is $g^2A_aA_b\operatorname{tr}([t_a,\Xi]^{\dagger}[t_b,\Xi])$, not diagonal, hence all symmetric pairs, 120. Structure constants enter the value of each matrix vertex once $\Xi$ is expanded in generators; they do not enter the count, which is an index count.

The article never states the quartet's number, so the contrast it invokes is left for the reader to supply. Repair: name 15, and name the diagonal reason.

Worth One Clause

3. "One kinetic coefficient suffices" — the field redefinition also moves the potential

The article says a term $b(\partial\operatorname{tr}\Xi)^2$ "is absorbed by rescaling the singlet", then concludes "that is why one kinetic coefficient suffices and two quadratic coefficients do not."

The conclusion is right; the step is a field redefinition and the article does not say so. Rescaling $t\to t/\sqrt{1+4b}$ leaves the kinetic term canonical and moves $\mu_2^2\to\mu_2^2/(1+4b)$, $\lambda_3\to\lambda_3/(1+4b)$, $\lambda_4\to\lambda_4/(1+4b)^2$. So the count "six coefficients" is a count in the canonical-kinetic basis, and a reader who thinks the second kinetic term is physically absent will mis-count by one.

Repair, one clause: the rescaling is a field redefinition, it is free, and the potential's trace coefficients are quoted in the basis where the kinetic term is canonical.

4. The fifth quartic's absence from the cone is not cross-referenced

The article says the group allows five quartics and the model writes four, and calls this "a declaration of zero coefficients". The Primordial Potential's cone is derived for $\lambda_5=0$, and that article now says so in its own §The Trace Channel, in the Cone (patched 2026-11-25). This article and that one are consistent, and neither points at the other on this point. One clause closes the loop: the cone in the potential article is the cone for the potential written here.

Declined, With Reason

5. The Gauss-law derivation is not compressed — it already carries the step the referee asks for

The referee asks for the Hermiticity of $D_t\Xi$ and the anti-Hermiticity of $[t_a,\Xi]$ to be shown. The article states both: "the product is purely imaginary, since $(D_t\Xi)$ is Hermitian and $[t_a,\Xi]$ is anti-Hermitian". What it does not say in words is why the two cross terms are equal rather than conjugate — namely $(D_t\Xi)^{\dagger}=D_t\Xi$. That is one clause, and it is the only gap. Rated: optional, one clause.

A Defect the Referee Did Not Raise: $v_C$ Is Two Numbers

The companion states three things that cannot all hold at once.

  1. the vacuum is $\Xi_0=v_CD$;
  2. $M^2_{ab}=g_4^2v_C^2\langle[t_a,D],[t_b,D]\rangle$;
  3. "with the corpus's normalisation the nonzero eigenvalues are equal, $M_X^2=\tfrac12g_4^2v_C^2$".

From (1) and (2), with $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ and $\operatorname{tr}D^2=12$ and an orthonormal basis, the six eigenvalues are $8g_4^2v_C^2$, not $\tfrac12$. Computed, not estimated:

$$ -\operatorname{tr}\bigl([t_a,D]^{2}\bigr)=8\ \text{on each of the six},\qquad \text{rank}=6 . $$

The factor between the two statements is 16, or 32 once the $\tfrac12M^2A^2$ convention is applied. It cannot come from the $\tfrac12$, from $\operatorname{tr}(t_at_b)=\tfrac12$, or from any sign: it comes from $v_C$ being two different numbers in the same article — the coefficient of $D$ in $\Xi_0=v_CD$, and the corpus's physical condensate $v_C=8v/g_4$ defined by the very equation $M_X^2=\tfrac12g_4^2v_C^2$.

The article half-notices this. It writes "with the corpus's normalisation", which scopes the $\tfrac12$ to the corpus's $v_C$ and not to its own. But the notation table then lists $D$ unnormalised and the text writes $\Xi_0=v_CD$, so the scoping is invisible.

Two repairs, both one clause, and the choice is the user's:

  • Weak: say that the $\tfrac12$ is the corpus's $v_C$ and not the coefficient in $\Xi_0=v_CD$; the equality of the six, which is the article's claim, is unaffected.
  • Strong: write $\Xi_0=\tfrac{v_C}{4}D$ and show that it reproduces $8\cdot\tfrac1{16}=\tfrac12$; then (1), (2), (3) hold simultaneously and no hedging is needed. This requires the $D$-normalisation convention to be fixed here, and it propagates to the breaking article's $v_C=8v/g_4$.

Resolved in favour of neither: option A was taken instead, $M_X^{2}=16g_4^{2}v_C^{2}$ with $\Xi_0=v_CD$, in a corpus-wide change recorded separately. Originally recommended: The strong one touches a conversion that three articles share and that the breaking article explicitly says "none of them restates it". The article's own claim — six equal eigenvalues — is true under either reading, and only the arithmetic label is at stake.

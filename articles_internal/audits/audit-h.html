2. The Brief

The pass in one sentence. Replace the reduced Planck constant $\hbar$ by the Planck constant $h$ throughout the physics corpus, writing $2\pi$ explicitly wherever a radian is meant, and repairing in the same pass the derived lengths and wave numbers that the corpus currently defines against $\hbar$.

Why. One constant, one letter. The corpus carries two constants of action, $h$ and $\hbar$, defined against each other, and every display that mixes them is a place where a factor $2\pi$ can hide. The internal note The Yukawa Coupling in Wave Numbers already computes in both, side by side, and finds the corpus's derived length $\lambda_m$ wrong by exactly $2\pi$: the corpus writes $\lambda_m=1/k_m=\hbar/(mc)$, calls it a wavelength, and it is $386.159\ \mathrm{fm}$ for the electron against $2.426\ \mathrm{pm}$ for the actual wavelength. A single constant makes that error unwritable.

3. Decision D1: What Is Being Asked For

There are three things one can mean, and they cost very differently. The measurements below are the whole of the argument.

Option A, literal elimination. The token \hbar does not occur anywhere. Every site becomes $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$ or a rearrangement of it. This is a relocation of the factor $2\pi$, not a deletion of anything: the pass moves a $2\pi$ from a definition into 2,900 displays.

Option B, elimination by variable change. The token disappears because the variables change: phases are measured in turns, $\Theta:=S/h$; frequencies are ordinary frequencies $\nu$ with $E=h\nu$; wave numbers are cycle-based, $\tilde\nu:=\nu/c$, with $p=h\tilde\nu$; measures are in $h$, $(2\pi\hbar)^3\to h^3$. The four clusters where a radian is irreducible — the commutator, the kinetic term, the spin, the gauge coupling — keep a $2\pi$ or keep $\hbar$.

Option C, the rule only. $\hbar$ stays, and the corpus gains one stated rule: $\hbar$ where an angle is measured in radians, $h$ where a count is taken. Nothing is substituted; $2,500$ sites are checked and left alone.

Why $\hbar$ cannot vanish in the strict sense. The canonical commutator is $[\xi,\pi]=i\hbar\,\delta$. The letter is not a unit that someone chose; it is action per radian, and it appears because the translation generator is $e^{i\pi x/\hbar}$, whose exponent is an angle. Write $h$ and the $2\pi$ appears: $[x,p]=ih/2\pi$. There is no third spelling, because the alternative is to rescale $p$ until the commutator is $i$, which is $\hbar=1$ and is forbidden by the corpus's own rule. The same argument holds for the spin, $\hbar/2=h/4\pi$, and for the kinetic term, $\hbar^2/2m=h^2/(8\pi^2m)$. The pass can remove a glyph; it cannot remove a radian.

7. The Theorem the Pass Runs Against

The factor $2\pi$ is conserved. It can be moved, never deleted. Two identities show it.

$$ \hbar=\frac{h}{2\pi}, \qquad \exp\!\left(\frac{iS}{\hbar}\right)=\exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i S}{h}\right). $$

A phase exponent written against $\hbar$ is clean and has no $2\pi$ on the page; the same exponent written against $h$ carries one. Conversely a wave number written against $h$ is clean, $p=h\nu$ and $E=h\nu$, and the same wave number written against $\hbar$ carries a $2\pi$ in its definition, $p=\hbar k$ with $k=2\pi\nu$.

So the corpus must choose where the $2\pi$ lives. Two places are possible and only two.

  • The definition carries it — the present state. $k:=\vec p/\hbar$ in $\mathrm{rad\,m^{-1}}$, $\lambda:=\hbar/p$, and every exponent is $\exp(i\theta)$ with no $2\pi$. The constant on the page is $\hbar$.
  • The display carries it — Option B. $k:=\vec p/h$ in $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, $\lambda=h/p$, and every exponent is $\exp(2\pi i\theta)$. The constant on the page is $h$.

The measured cost of the second choice. The physics corpus has $951$ displays of the form $\exp(i\times\cdots)$ plus $747$ of the form $e^{i\cdots}$, and $391$ instances of the momentum operator $i\tilde K_\Phi$; each gains a $2\pi$ under Option B, and each of the $1\,400$ generator sites gains one under either option. There is no branch in which the $2\pi$ disappears.

What this rules out. A pass that claims to remove $\hbar$ and to leave the displays unchanged. Such a pass either writes $\hbar$ under another name — which is what a private symbol for $h/2\pi$ would be, and is forbidden by §Where the Sub-agent Must Not Improvise, item 1 — or it silently sets $2\pi=1$, which is a worse defect than either constant.

8. The Blind Replace, Tested

The method. One string replacement over a pristine copy of articles_physics/, in a scratch directory and not in the tree:

s.replace(r'\hbar', r'\frac{h}{2\pi}')

$6\,859$ sites in $678$ files on the live tree; every number below is the live one unless it says HEAD. Four tests, and the third is the one that settles it.

Two layers, and only the first can be wrong. Layer 1 is the blind replace below; it is an identity on every site, so no value can change. Layer 2 is a cosmetic filter that folds a fraction inside a fraction, an exponent, a phasor and a phase-space measure into one storey; if it refuses a span, the blind form stays, and the page is heavier, not wrong.

Test 1, is a site ever wrong? No, and this is a proof rather than a measurement. $\hbar$ and $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$ denote the same number, so the substitution is an identity on every display. Unlike a contextual pass, there is no site at which the physics can change.

Test 2, is it ever outside math mode? Never. Every one of the $6\,859$ sites lies between $…$ or $$…$$; the count outside is $0$. There is therefore no site where a \frac lands in text mode and breaks the build.

Test 3, does the corpus's own renderer accept it? This is the decisive test, and it was run, not argued. All $5\,877$ math spans containing \hbar were passed to KaTeX $0.16.9$ — the library article_template.html loads — twice, before and after:

9. The Rules, Cluster by Cluster

The rules below are written for Option A, which is the decision. Each cluster gives the algebraic form the reader is to reach; the Option B alternative is kept beside it, struck through in intent, only so that the record shows what was considered and why the $2\pi$ was preferred on the page.

Common 1 — the phase-space measure, $168$ sites, and $74$ without exponent

Rule 1, applied without a reader. The blind replace would write $(2\pi\frac{h}{2\pi})^{3}$ here: true and absurd. Rule 1 cancels it to $h^{3}$ before the site reaches the page. The identity below is why the rule is safe, not a target somebody is aiming at.

$$ \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{d^3p}{h^3}, \qquad \big[a_{\vec p},a^{\dagger}_{\vec p^{\,\prime}}\big]=(2\pi\hbar)^3\delta^{(3)}(\vec p-\vec p^{\,\prime}) \;\longrightarrow\;h^3\,\delta^{(3)}(\vec p-\vec p^{\,\prime}), $$

and the same in four dimensions, $d^4p/h^4$. This is a strict improvement and it is not a matter of taste: $(2\pi\hbar)^3=h^3$ is an identity, and $h^3$ is the volume of one phase-space cell, which is what the display means. The mode amplitude that travels with it, $\sqrt{\hbar c/(2E_{\vec p})}$, does not simplify: it becomes $\sqrt{hc/(4\pi E_{\vec p})}$ and is left for the branch rules.

Add one sentence at the first display of each article that carries one: The cell is $h^3$: one cell per mode, and the $2\pi$ of the angular measure is absorbed into it. It is the sentence the reader needs, and it is the reason this rule survives both branches.

Common 2 — the angular frequency, $246$ sites

Rule 3. $\frac{h}{2\pi}\omega$ is correct; $h\nu_k$ is the shorter naming of the same energy. The rule applies it only where $\omega$ is an angular frequency; where $\omega$ is a material frequency in inverse length the site falls through to the plain form and is read by hand.

$$ \hbar\omega_k\;\longrightarrow\;h\nu_k, \qquad \omega_k=2\pi\nu_k . $$

The corpus already distinguishes the angular frequency $\omega_k$ from the ordinary one and already rules that "a display that writes the energy itself as $\omega_k$ has set $\hbar=1$ at that place". The migration makes that sentence true by construction: $E=h\nu$ has no constant to drop. The letter $\nu$ is plain and not tilded, because the tilde marks the potent family; the potent pass frees the letter precisely for this. A site that writes $\hbar\omega$ in an article whose $\omega$ is a material frequency in inverse length is not in this cluster: there $\hbar\omega$ has the dimension of an energy per length and the surrounding display is checked first.

Common 3 — the tables and the prose, $\sim 1\,400$ sites

10. The $\lambda_m$ Defect, Which the Pass Should Repair

The corpus defines the mass length by inversion, $\lambda_m=1/k_m$, and calls it the Compton wavelength. It is not one. With $k_m=mc/\hbar$ the inversion gives $\hbar/(mc)=386.159\ \mathrm{fm}$ for the electron; the wavelength is $h/(mc)=2.425\ 631\ \mathrm{pm}$, larger by $2\pi$. The corpus has been writing the length per radian, which is the length its operators use, and naming it a wavelength.

The internal note states the defect in full and computes both columns. This pass is the occasion to repair it, and the repair has three forms, one per option.

  • Under Option A the corpus keeps angular wave numbers and the name changes, not the formula: $k_m=2\pi mc/h$ and $\lambda_m=2\pi/k_m=h/(mc)$, with a sentence saying that $k_m$ is angular and $\lambda_m$ is the wavelength. The old $\hbar/(mc)$ quantity, the length per radian, is not needed anywhere once the phase is written with a $2\pi$ in the exponent.
  • Under Option B the repair is free: $k_m:=mc/h$ is already the reciprocal of the wavelength, because the wave number is cycle-based. The corpus loses one defect and gains no sentence.
  • Under Option C the repair is independent of the constant and should still be done: $\lambda_m:=2\pi/k_m$ and the name kept. It is a one-line defect whether or not the constant moves.

Sites. \lambda_m is defined in physics-units-and-common-elements-conventions.md rows 54–57 as $\lambda_m=1/k_m=\hbar/(mc)$, with the meaning cell the reduced Compton wavelength; $84$ further displays use 1/k_m. Both are rewritten. The word reduced is deleted wherever the quantity is the wavelength, and kept only where the quantity truly is $\hbar/(mc)$, which under Option A is named $\bar{\lambda}_m$ and under Option B does not occur.

13. The Rule: $\hbar$ Is Not Allowed

The pass has a second deliverable, and it outlives the substitution: the physics conventions must forbid the glyph, so that the corpus cannot drift back. Three edits to physics-units-and-common-elements-conventions.md.

The table row. §The Units, row Planck constants: the quantity column becomes $h$ alone and the note becomes $\hbar$ is not used in the corpus; it is $h$ per radian, and the $2\pi$ is written where the radian is meant. The row's unit, $\mathrm{M}\,\mathrm{L}^{2}\,\mathrm{T}^{-1}$, is unchanged, because it is the unit of $h$.

The paragraph, placed after the tables and before §The shortcut:

The reduced constant is not written. The corpus carries one constant of action, $h$. A display that needs the reduced constant writes its value, $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$, or an equivalent rearrangement of it: a phase is $\dfrac{2\pi S}{h}$, a commutator is $[\xi,\pi]=\dfrac{ih}{2\pi}\delta^{(3)}$, a kinetic term is $-\dfrac{h^{2}}{8\pi^{2}m}\Delta$, a coupling is $\dfrac{2\pi\varkappa}{h}$. The rule is not a preference between two letters. It is that the factor $2\pi$ must stand on the page: $\hbar$ hides a full turn inside a single glyph, and a reader who has to unpack it has lost the radian. A display that reintroduces the glyph, or that introduces any private symbol standing for $h/2\pi$, is defective in the article that writes it.

The shortcut paragraph is rewritten, not merely relabelled, and it now forbids two settings rather than one: "An article may not set $h$ to $2\pi$ or $c$ to one." The first is the old $\hbar=1$ under the new letter; the second was already forbidden. The paragraph's argument carries over unchanged, because $h$ at $2\pi$ deletes exactly the conversion that $\hbar$ at $1$ deleted.

Why a rule and not a habit. The corpus had $16$ sites that set $\hbar=1$ in numerical work, and they were a pre-existing violation of the corpus's own §The shortcut; they are repaired before the pass runs, not by it (§The Forbidden Shortcut). A rule that names the setting is the only thing that stops the seventeenth.

# rewrite(span) -> span with one storey where one storey is possible

The rule order is the design. Each branch fires only when its own pattern is present; the order removes the ambiguity between them.

  1. (2\pi\hbar)^{n}\to h^{n} — first, because it deletes the 2\pi outright.
  2. 2\pi\hbar\to h — the same cancellation where no exponent is written.
  3. \hbar\omega_k\to h\nu_k — before any fraction branch, so a numerator keeps h\nu and not h\omega/(2\pi).
  4. \frac{X\ldots}{…\hbar\ldots} — \hbar in the numerator: one storey, with an integer coefficient in the denominator multiplied into the $2\pi$ and a power n raised into (2\pi)^{n}.
  5. \frac{X}{\hbar} — \hbar as the whole denominator: \frac{X}{\hbar}\to\frac{2\pi X}{h}, and \frac{X}{\hbar^{n}D}\to\frac{(2\pi)^{n}X}{h^{n}D}. This is the gauge-coupling shape, $1\,322$ sites, and the branch that a first engine missed.
  6. \hbar^{n}\to\frac{h^{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}} — where no fraction encloses the site.
  7. \hbar/X\to\frac{h}{2\pi X} — the denominator read as a product, stopping at a relation, a delimiter or punctuation.
  8. X/\hbar\to\frac{2\pi X}{h} — the numerator read as a product to its left, on the same stop set; the atom reader attaches ^{…} and _{…} to their base, or mc^{2}/\hbar is split.
  9. X/(\hbar D)\to\frac{2\pi X}{h D} — the parenthesised denominator, its parentheses dropped.
  10. \hbar=6.582\times10^{-22}\to h=4.135\times10^{-21} — the definition rows, the $8$ places where the constant is quoted with its value, which is recomputed and not copied.
  11. \hbar\to0\to h\to0 — the limit rows, where the constant is a knob and not a number.

Everything else is the plain fallback, \frac{h}{2\pi}, applied to $2\,030$ sites.

\dfrac, \tfrac and \cfrac become \frac. It is the same command with a style argument, the audit normalises the three to \frac before comparing, and the pass makes no attempt to preserve the style: the corpus uses the style to mean "tall" or "inline", and after the fold the fraction is one storey and its height is no longer the problem.

What the boundary rules cost. Rules 7, 8 and 9 need to know where a product ends. The stop set is: relations (=, \in, \to, \approx), operators (+, -, \pm, \cdot, \times), delimiters (\big, \Big, \left, \right and their variants), punctuation, and a subscript that is not attached to a letter. Getting this wrong is how a canonicaliser writes \frac{2\pi}{h\bigr} — a delimiter swallowed into a denominator — and the command audit exists to catch exactly that.

Check 0 is the only one that can fail the pass on its own, and it is scoped to *.md on purpose. Ten extensionless files sit in articles_physics/ as stale duplicates of articles that also exist as .md; they carry $81$ sites, build.py never reads them, and they are out of scope (§The Inventory). The unscoped grep -rn '\\hbar' articles_physics/ therefore ends at $81$ and says nothing about the pass.

Check 1 gives $63$ lines if written as hslash|varpi|hbarprime. \varpi is not a symbol for $h/2\pi$: it is the frequency of the subluminal solution in subluminal-and-superluminal-electromagnetic-waves-in-biquaternionic-form.md, $108$ occurrences, and it predates the pass. The check is therefore that the count is unchanged, not that it is zero. A new symbol for $h/2\pi$ would show up as a varpi count that went up, or as \hslash, \hbarprime, or a macro definition.

Check 2's four patterns are the four shapes the canonicaliser folds, and each is written against the shape rather than the rule number, so a failed rule is found by the grep that should have emptied. The (2\pi pattern is written for basic regular expressions, where ( is literal; with grep -E it needs \(.

Check 3 is the one that caught the real defects. It compares the multiset of \command tokens in a span before and after, ignoring \hbar, \omega and \pi, which the rules are entitled to delete. Any other command that disappears means a boundary was drawn wrong: a \big eaten by a denominator, a \tilde detached from its argument, a \in absorbed into a product. On the pilot it found five, all fixed; on the full tree of 2026-11-23 it finds none. The span count is its companion — $14\,509$ display and $162\,795$ inline, identical before and after — because a rule that swallowed a $$ would change the count without losing a command.

Check 6 is a reading of twenty spans, and it is not optional. Open the pass's /tmp/post, take the twenty spans listed by python3 _reserve/py/rewrite-physics.py in its per-file tally order from the five heaviest files, and read the before and the after side by side. This is the only check that looks at meaning, and on 2026-11-23 it is the one that found the two defects of §The Blind Replace, Tested. Record the twenty spans in the report, each as a pair.

What is not checked, because it cannot be. No grep distinguishes a good $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$ from a bad one; only a reader can. What the machine proves is that nothing was lost, nothing fails to parse, the span count did not move, and the pass cannot run twice. It cannot prove the page reads well, and pretending otherwise is how a corpus acquires two conventions.

D1 is closed. Option A, in two layers: the blind replacement as the invariant, and the canonicaliser as the cosmetic folding, both tested in §The Blind Replace, Tested. The sites that set $\hbar=1$. A pre-existing violation of the corpus's own shortcut rule, found by this pass: $16$ in prose and in displays, and the pass does not repair them. $\hbar=1$ and $h=2\pi$ are one equation, so a substitution would preserve the defect and hide it; they were repaired by hand on 2026-11-23 in units of $mc$, $mc^2$ and $1/k_m$ (§The Forbidden Shortcut), and the engine now refuses any span that still sets a constant to one. The fact that they existed for so long is the argument for writing the prohibition down.

The canonicaliser is a filter, not a rule. It is validated on a pristine copy of HEAD and it always has the blind form to fall back on, so a mis-drawn boundary costs a storey, never a value. Its one real risk is the boundary between a numerator and a denominator, and the command audit of check 3 is the test for it.

The internal notes. $16$ files, $279$ sites, and the $5$ tilde-nu sites of the Yukawa note. Out of scope here and named for the next pass.

The three-wavenumbers article. Its first section is built on the division by $\hbar$ that carries $k_m$, $k_{t}^{\prime}$ and $\tilde K_\Phi$. After the pass those three are divisions by $h$ and a $2\pi$, and the section says so; the arithmetic is identical and the prose is not.

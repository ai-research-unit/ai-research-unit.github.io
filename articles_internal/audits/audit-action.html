The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. The test is substitution, not intention. A result is general when it still parses after the carrier is replaced by a Proca field and by an $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ matrix field. An author's intention to be general is not evidence.
  2. An example is not a failure; a general slot filled by an example is. A general article may work the scalar case, provided the slot is not the one the category presents as general. The defect is the slot, not the example and not the labelling.
  3. Conditional generality is acceptable. A tool that applies to every gauge connection, every spin-carrying fluid, or every theory in two dimensions is general over a declared class. The condition must be in the first paragraph and in the section title.
  4. A move is preferred to a rewrite when the article is an instance by nature. A one-rung model is not made general by adding a paragraph; it is made honest by living in its rung's band.
  5. The audit edits nothing. It names the file, the line, the verdict and the action, so that a later pass can execute one row at a time.

4. The Findings

4.1 The load-bearing scalar: Hamiltonian Field Theory

This is the audit's first finding because it is the only one where a carrier restriction propagates into three later sections.

The evidence. The article opens by restricting itself:

"This article performs that transcription for the field the corpus actually uses — the central scalar field of the Klein–Gordon articles." — hamiltonian-field-theory-in-biquaternionic-form.md:5

The article is not hiding its scope: line 5 states it, and the contrast with the Dirac and Maxwell constraints is explicit. The defect is the slot, not the honesty. The menu gives this article to three general sections, and the article's title is Hamiltonian Field Theory, not The Scalar Hamiltonian; a reader who arrives from the menu has no reason to expect the scalar restriction.

The restriction is not a passing choice. The conjugate momentum is $\pi=(\partial\xi/\partial r'_t)^{*}$; the Legendre transform is "regular"; the phase space is the full space of pairs $(\xi,\pi)$; the density is the Hermitian form of a central scalar; the bracket is the bracket of two complex numbers at each point; the sector split is the split of one central complex scalar into two real scalars. Every displayed result is the scalar case. The article knows the others exist — it names the Dirac second-class constraint and the Maxwell first-class constraints — but it does not construct their Hamiltonians, and it says the interesting brackets lie beyond the scalar case.

Why it matters. The menu says three later sections take this article as given:

"gives the conjugate momentum, the regularity of the Legendre transform and the field Poisson bracket, which three later sections take as given — the thermal state, the promotion's postulate and the fluid's energy — and which none of them derives." — physics.md:584

If the article is the scalar case, then those three sections inherit the scalar case. The thermal state's "density and flow" are the scalar's; the promotion's postulate is the scalar's canonical bracket; the fluid's energy is the scalar's $T_{00}$. The category's claim that the chain specialises to $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ cannot be sustained at this slot.

The substitution test. Replace the scalar by a Proca field. The conjugate momentum is constrained: the Lorenz condition is a second-class constraint, the Legendre transform is not regular, and the Dirac bracket is required. Replace it by the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ matrix field. The momentum is matrix-valued; the Legendre transform is on a matrix; the bracket is on a non-commutative algebra; the "sum of three squares" is a trace. The article's central claim — "the Legendre transform is regular" — is false at both rungs. It is true only at the scalar rung, and the article does not say "at the scalar rung" in the result; it says it in the scope sentence, and then the result is used generally.

Action: rewrite in place. The rewrite is not a new article. It adds a generic section before the scalar work:

  1. The Legendre transform on a module. Define the conjugate momentum as the variational derivative of the density with respect to the velocity field, for a field valued in a module over $\mathbb{B}$. State that the transform is regular iff the Hessian is non-degenerate on the module.
  2. The regularity table. Scalar: regular. Proca: singular; the Lorenz condition is second class; the Dirac bracket. Dirac: singular; the first-order structure gives a second-class pair. Connection: singular; the Gauss law is first class. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$: the Hessian on the matrix module; the constraint count.
  3. The bracket on functionals of an element. The scalar bracket is the first column; the matrix bracket is the commutator; the Proca bracket is the Dirac bracket on the constrained surface.
  4. Keep the scalar sections as the worked instance, with a one-line label: "the scalar column of the table."

The rewrite preserves every existing display; it changes the article's claim from "this is the Hamiltonian formalism" to "this is the Hamiltonian formalism, and the scalar is its first column." The alternative — move the article to the Klein–Gordon band and write a new general article — is more expensive and splits a slot the menu says is load-bearing. Rewrite is the recommendation.

Its field is $\Xi=U_{\mathrm{frm}}PU_{\mathrm{frm}}^{\dagger}$ with four eigenvalues $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_4$ and a twelve-parameter frame. 4.3 The kinetic bridge is scalar

The kinetic bridge is load-bearing: the fluid's quantum pressure is read from the Wigner object's $\hbar$-expansion. If the object is scalar, the quantum pressure is scalar.

The evidence. The Wigner Function defines the standard function for the central case:

"For a complex-valued field $\Xi$ on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ — the central case of the corpus's field, in which the vector part is zero." — the-wigner-function-in-biquaternionic-form.md:24

The article is honest about the case: the scalar is named as "the central case", and the element definition that follows is general — $\tilde W_\Xi$ is an element of $\mathbb{B}$. But the standard function, the marginals and the closure are the scalar's: the zeroth moment is $\lVert\tilde\Xi\rVert^{2}$ and the first is the current times the mass. For a spinor, the Wigner object is a matrix in spin space, and its zeroth moment is the spin density matrix, not a scalar density. For the matrix rung, it is a matrix in the algebra, and the marginal is a matrix. The article does not define those objects.

Relativistic Wigner Function and Quantum Kinetics has a second problem: its title says relativistic, and its body uses the non-relativistic reduction. The body is honest about it; the title is not:

"Write the Hamiltonian of the scalar field's non-relativistic reduction as the element built from the operator $\hat H=\lvert\hat{\vec p}\rvert^{2}/2m+V(\hat r)$." — relativistic-wigner-function-and-quantum-kinetics.md:17

The $\hbar$-expansion of the non-relativistic scalar Wigner function is a legitimate article; it is not a relativistic Wigner article, and it is not a general kinetic bridge.

The substitution test. Take a Dirac field. The Wigner transform is a $4\times4$ matrix (or a $2\times2$ matrix in the chiral basis); its marginals are the density matrix and the current, and the spin part is a separate moment. The scalar definition's "zeroth moment is the density" does not transfer. Take the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ matrix field. The Wigner object is a matrix; the marginal is a matrix; the quantum pressure is a trace. The scalar bridge cannot produce the matrix fluid's quantum pressure.

Action. Two options, in order of preference:

  1. Rewrite The Wigner Function for a general element. Define $\tilde W_\Xi$ as the Wigner transform of the band's element, with the internal index left open; take the trace over the internal index for the scalar marginal; state that the spin and matrix cases keep the index. Add the spin case as a worked example. This is a medium rewrite, but it preserves the bridge's slot.
  2. Retitle and move Relativistic Wigner. 10.3 The findings of the second pass

F1 — The Partition Function (19): the trace is the algebra's, not the module's. Verdict C.

The article says, in its first paragraph, that $Z$ is "written here with the scalar part in place of the matrix trace $\operatorname{tr}\exp(-\beta H)$ that the standard text uses" (the-partition-function-in-biquaternionic-form.md:11), and it says what the replacement is at :19 and again at :182: "twice the scalar part is the matrix trace of the $2\times2$ image". The identity is exact — on $\mathbb{C}^{2}$. It is not an identity on any other module. The dictionary's pairing row says the matrix rung's pairing is $\mathrm{Tr}$, and The Matsubara Thermal Formalism writes $Z=\mathrm{Tr}\exp(-\beta H)$ with no factor at all (the-matsubara-thermal-formalism-in-biquaternionic-form.md:5). So the category holds both statements, and the article that presents itself as the partition function is the two-dimensional one.

The substitution test fails in the arithmetic and not in the structure. A reader who carries $2\,\mathrm{Sc}$ to the matrix rung gets a factor $2$ where the module wants $4$ — and $4$ only when the $4\times4$ is the algebra's $2\times2$ tensored with a two-dimensional copy index, which is a case and not the general one.

Why the first pass missed it. It read the article's honesty — "in place of the matrix trace" — as a scope sentence. It is not: it is a statement about a $2\times2$ image, and it does not say which module the $Z$ belongs to. The dictionary is what makes the gap visible, which is the first evidence that the instrument of §6 works. The menu carries the same slide: its subsection comment calls the object "the partition function as a trace" (physics.md, the comment above the entry), so a reader arrives expecting a trace over the rung and finds a trace over $\mathbb{C}^{2}$.

F2 — Quantum Thermodynamics (21): the same trace, and the entropy's ceiling. Verdict C.

The article is explicit that "the factor $2$ appears once, in the partition function, where it counts the two levels of a single biquaternion" (quantum-thermodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.md:13), and that the maximally mixed state has "the entropy tends to $\log 2$, the maximum for a two-state system" (:212). Both are true of the article's state space, $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$. The article's four-level case is a product of two independent qubits (:119), which is a different object from a state on a four-dimensional module: the product's entropy is a sum of two $\log 2$'s, while a matrix-rung state's maximally mixed entropy is a single $\log 4$, and its off-diagonal correlations have no product form. So the article's "four levels" illustrates additivity and does not instantiate the rung.

F3 — Spin Conservation and the Frenkel Condition (36): one quantifier too wide. Verdict C, unchanged.

spin-conservation-and-the-frenkel-condition.md:149: "it holds for every field with a non-zero flow and every direction $\hat s$". Every display in the article is the Dirac rotor's, and the banner added in the pass says so. A Proca field's word carries no rotor factor, so there is no $\hat s$ that is the image of a declared axis, and the sentence as written promises the condition for a carrier the article's own banner excludes. The repair is one clause: every field whose word carries a rotor. This is the only place in the 69 where an overclaim survives the banner, and it is exactly the residue a banner leaves if the body is not re-read against it.

F4 — The Local Entropy Current (31): checked, general, and general for a reason worth recording.

the-local-entropy-current.md:19 declares the current Hermitian and says the declaration is "inherited by every article of the category that writes an entropy inequality"; :58 shows that $\mathrm{Sc}((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde Q)=\partial_{\imath}Q_{\imath}$ holds "for every element and not only for a Hermitian one", Hermiticity adding only the reality of the right-hand side. The article is column-blind and not by accident: entropy is additive over the fibre, so its current is a base-valued four-vector at every rung, and no internal index appears because none can. The substitution test passes trivially. No action. It is recorded because a regulator that finds nothing should say where it looked.

F5 — The dictionary's delivery mechanism is under-used: five articles link it, three name it without a link.

The dictionary is the answer to the category's scalar default, and a scope sentence that says "the other columns are in the dictionary" only works if the reader can reach it. Three articles, one noun, no target. This is a defect of the pass and not of the corpus, and it is the cheapest thing in this section to fix — and it has been fixed: §10.7 records the three links, after which ten articles of the category link the dictionary and none names it bare.

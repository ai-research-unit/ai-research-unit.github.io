Repairing the Overbolded Articles: The Five Operations, the Bold That Stays, and the Hundred and Thirty-One Files

Status of this file. Planned and measured, not executed. The damage is counted and the files are listed in §8; no article has been repaired. The numbers: 131 files, 611 874 words, 10 604 bold spans.

What the file is for. It repairs one fault, proliferation of bold, without touching the physics. It fixes what earns bold, the five operations that remove the rest, the order of the batch, and the eleven checks that accept a repaired file.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. The fault is proliferation, not bold. Bold stays and marks four things: a definition, the one result a section proves, a no-go, a sign or an inequality.
  2. One reader, one article. No script edits an article; a script measures, and its measurement is the acceptance test.
  3. The opener goes and the sentence beneath it stays word for word. The bolded sentence summarises the sentence under it, and the sentence under it is the better prose.
  4. No partial pass. An article left at $8$ per cent bold with its announcements in place is not half repaired.
  5. Three of the eleven checks guard against silent loss: the numeric multiset, the word band of $-10$ to $0$ per cent, and a named replacement for every deleted sentence.

Introduction

The fault is proliferation, not bold. A healthy article in this corpus carries bold for a definition, for the result a section exists to prove, for a no-go, for a sign. A damaged article carries bold on the first sentence of nearly every paragraph, so the emphasis marks nothing.

The repair keeps bold and removes the proliferation. It does not aim at zero. The target is the profile the corpus already has in its undamaged files.

The pass is manual. One reader, one article, reading and removing. No script edits an article. A script measures, and its measurement is the acceptance test.

1. What the Two Numbers Show

Two measurements are taken on one file. The bold share is the characters inside a bold span, after the title line, divided by the characters of the rest of the file. The title line is excluded because the corpus template renders it bold by construction. The opener share is the number of prose paragraphs beginning with a bold span, divided by the number of prose paragraphs.

2. What Earns Bold, and What Loses It

Bold stays on four things.

  • A definition, at the point the section introduces it.
  • The one result the section exists to prove.
  • A no-go, a denial, or a statement of what is not claimed.
  • A sign or an inequality the argument turns on.

Bold comes off everything else. The test is one question: does the span mark the single thing this paragraph or this section exists to establish? If the answer is no, the bold goes.

The resulting profile: a $5\,000$-word article ends with $5$ to $10$ spans, not $100$. Bold share $1$ to $4$ per cent of the body. At most one bolded paragraph opener in a ## section, and none in the Introduction.

3. The Five Operations

# Operation Applies to
1 Delete the bolded summary sentence, keep the sentence beneath it word for word a paragraph opener the next sentence repeats
2 Remove the two asterisks only, keep the sentence an opener that carries a fact the paragraph does not repeat
3 Remove the two asterisks only, keep the word a span inside a sentence, unless it is a sign, an inequality or a denial
4 Repair the grammar the removal broke a paragraph starting in lower case, an orphan fragment
5 Rewrite a pure-apparatus line in the author's own words section numbering written as prose, a status line, a revision line

Operation 1 is the bulk of the work and the delicate one. The bolded sentence summarises the sentence under it, and the sentence under it is the better prose. Deleting the summary loses nothing. Deleting the sentence under it loses the article.

An example, from the-lagrangian-and-the-coupled-proca-equation-in-momentum-space:

The second finding is that the constraint is a determination and not a residue. The parent's coupled reading is explicit: on the coupled carrier the mass term does not break the gauge symmetry, so the constraint is imposed and not left over.

Repaired by operation 1:

The parent's coupled reading is explicit: on the coupled carrier the mass term does not break the gauge symmetry, so the constraint is imposed and not left over.

A case for operation 2, where the bolded sentence is the only statement of its fact:

The constant $J_c$ is convention-dependent — it absorbs the normalisation of the phase, of the charge and of the density.

The sentence stays. The asterisks come off.

Operation 5 is the only one that rewrites, and it rewrites apparatus only. "This entry carries I.10.as to I.10.bd." becomes a sentence naming those results, in the author's words. Physics is not reworded.

4. What the Pass Does Not Do

No sentence is reworded for style. No claim is reordered. No objection is added. No provenance sentence is removed. The word "framework" stays. The word "corpus" stays where it names the corpus. A pointer to the reader stays. A coupling statement outside its own article is not this pass's business. Triplication across body, notation table and summary is the author's arrangement.

No label becomes a ### heading. The corpus holds $1\,883$ short bolded labels at paragraph heads, and turning them into headings builds a new article rather than repairing one.

No title is changed. The title line renders bold by construction and is excluded from every count in this file.

No partial pass. An article left at $8$ per cent bold with its announcements in place is not half repaired.

No git, no push, no deploy. The pass leaves the working tree modified and the author reads the diff.

5. The Procedure, One Article at a Time

Step 0. Print the path and the first heading, and check them against the request. Two articles in this corpus share a title and differ by one directory.

Step 1. Copy the file to a scratch directory outside the repository. Record on the copy: words, display blocks, table rows, the multiset of numeric tokens, the link count, the bold share, the opener share.

Step 2. Read the article once without editing.

Step 3. Walk the paragraphs in order and decide, span by span, which of the five operations applies. This is the only step that cannot be skipped or scripted.

Step 4. Apply the operations in file order.

Step 5. Run the acceptance test.

Step 6. Read the most-edited paragraphs once more for grammar.

Step 7. Record the row in the pass log: bold share before and after, spans deleted, spans kept with the reason each survivor kept its bold.

6. The Acceptance Test: Eleven Checks

Check Pass
bold share of the body $1$ to $4$ per cent
bolded paragraph openers at most one per ## section, none in the Introduction
every surviving span a definition, a key result, a no-go, or a sign
dollar-sign parity even on every line
display blocks equal to the copy
table rows equal to the copy
numeric tokens every token of the copy present, or its sentence named in the log
links every target resolves
section skeleton title, ## Introduction, thematic sections, ## Summary, ## Summary of Notation, ## Further Reading
word count between $-10$ and $0$ per cent of the copy
deleted sentences each has a named replacement, or carried nothing

The word band is tighter than a cleaning pass because this pass deletes summary sentences, not arguments. A file that loses more than $10$ per cent has lost content.

The numeric check runs on the multiset of tokens, not on a total, because a substituted number and a deleted number both pass a total.

7. The Order of the Batch

By damage class, not by filename.

The pilot is the-spin-one-anti-hermitian-subspace: $63$ per cent bold, $9\,416$ words, $73$ per cent of paragraphs opening bolded, and a carrier introduction, so its voice sets the rest.

Then the series, where the files are near-clones and the first repair settles the pattern: the Proca group, the Dirac group, the Klein–Gordon group, the Schrödinger group, the non-quantum limits.

Then the fluid family, then thermodynamics and relativity, then the reading-and-algebra group.

The cost is a reading hour per $3\,000$ words, more for the first file of a series and less for the fifth.

Audit Outbound: Forward References and the Reading Order

Status. Analysis 2026-11-27 on baseline 6ee5793e6, and the migration has begun: §12 logs the order of operations and the edits applied so far (Packages 2 and 4, verified; Package 3 withdrawn after checking the RQT spin matrices). Sections 2–9 remain the baseline snapshot. The raw edge list is articles_internal/audits/audit-outbound-edges.tsv (781 rows, one per body-layer forward link at baseline). The scope is every local link in articles_physics/*.md; the order of physics.md is taken as the reading order. Links to articles_maths/ are counted but excluded from the forward test, because the maths corpus has its own order.

The one-line answer. The corpus is not a backward-only sequence. Forty-four percent of its internal physics links point forward, and its prose links are split almost exactly fifty-fifty. Most of that is deliberate: Further Reading accounts for 64% of the forward links, and a handful of hub articles account for most of the rest. The audit does not find a physical contradiction. It finds five load-bearing deferrals and one systematic top-down order: the synthesis is placed before the detail it cites. That is the thing to fix if the corpus is meant to be read front to back.

1. The Answer in One Page

  1. Do not try to remove every forward link. The body-link graph has a 151-article strongly connected component. No reordering alone can remove the forward links inside it; you would have to cut links or accept the web. The corpus is a reference web with a menu order, not a proof sequence.
  2. If you want a linear order, adopt one rule: no body link that skips a category. There are 260 such links, from 79 articles, and they are the ones that make a reader jump over whole sections. Adjacent-category links and same-category links to the next few articles can stay.
  3. Move the five foundations first. The compact-sector ceiling (281), the entropy functional (706), the weak sector and electroweak breaking (318, 338), and the quantum-fluid instances (450, 523, 524) are clearly used before they are defined. The general fluid machinery (688–694) is the fifth case, but it needs a check of 190 first: it may be a legitimate limit pointer rather than a deferral. Moving the clear cases removes more forward links than any editing campaign, and it makes the claims verifiable where they are made.
  4. Convert optional pointers to plain text, not delete the sentence. Many forward links are "the case is worked in X" or "the full argument is in Y". Keep the title, drop the hyperlink. The prose still reads; the jump disappears.
  5. Keep Further Reading and the indexes. Further Reading is a bibliography, not a reading-order dependency. The Quantisation Hub (155), Sector Hub (271), and category introductions (182, 191) are navigational indexes; their forward links are their job. The audit treats them as exempt and says so.

What the balance cannot tell you. A net-sender category may be a legitimate overview, and a net-receiver category may be a legitimate detail article. The balance shows direction, not error. The next sections separate the deliberate pointers from the load-bearing deferrals.

5. Four Kinds of Forward Link

The 2,186 forward links are not one phenomenon. They divide into four kinds, and only the fourth is a defect.

5.1 Bibliographic: 1,405 links

These are Further Reading entries. They are 64% of all forward links and 41% of all Further Reading links. They are a bibliography, not a dependency. If you want zero forward links in the built page, convert these entries to plain text or split the bibliography into “before” and “after”. If you keep them, they should not be counted as a reading-order problem.

5.2 Hub and index pointers: the surveys and roadmaps

These are the explicit indexes: why-biquaternions (2), the-quantisation-hub (155), the-sector-hub (271), the-relativistic-quantum-space-as-a-fluid (182), five-steps-from-a-lagrangian-to-fluid-equations (191), and the category openers. Their forward links are their function. The menu comments say so: the Quantisation Hub is “the index of that distribution” and “every link below leads to the section that owns the article” (the-quantisation-hub...md:7–9); the Sector Hub is “one page for the question a sector reader asks” (the-sector-hub...md:5–7). Removing these links would break the navigation, not improve the order.

Two of these are orphans in the body graph. The Data Boundary (245), the Quantisation Hub (155), and the Sector Hub (271) have zero incoming body-layer links. They point forward but nothing points to them. They are entry points, not part of the chain. That is acceptable for an index. It is a problem for the Data Boundary, which is a rule that the section’s articles should cite.

5.3 General-to-instance pointers: the Fluid block

The Fluid category states a general chain and points to the per-carrier instances in Relativistic Quantum Theory. The menu’s own design note says the chain is stated at no particular group so that it specialises to the SU(4) instance (physics.md:554–556), and the audit of the Action category records the same “stated once, instanced per band” contract. The Fluid → RQT links are the largest block (73), but they are the intended architecture, not an accident. The reader jumps because the general article comes first. The fix is not to delete the links; it is to decide whether you want the general article first. If you do, a roadmap table at 182 is better than twenty scattered links. If you do not, the instances must come before the general chain, which is a category reorder.

5.4 Load-bearing deferrals

A load-bearing deferral is a specific article that uses a definition, a theorem, or a result that exists only in a later article. These are the real defects. They are not bibliography, not an index, and not a general-to-instance pointer. The next section lists the five clusters.

6. The Five Load-Bearing Deferrals

6.1 The entropy functional is defined after the second law that uses it

The Local Entropy Current (206) states the second law of the fluid. It says, in its own words, that the entropy functional and its monotonicity belong to Coarse-Graining and the Biquaternion Entropy Functional (706):

  • the-local-entropy-current.md:21: “It does not absorb the entropy functional... That article owns the functional... its dependence on the biquaternion norm alone, its vanishing on the zero-divisor cone, and its monotonicity under coarse-graining. This article cites all of that and repeats none of it.”
  • the-local-entropy-current.md:97: the state’s monotonicity is 706’s “and is cited here rather than re-derived.”
  • the-local-entropy-current.md:168: “The functional and its monotonicity belong to [706].”
  • the-local-entropy-current.md:206: “The functional of [706] is cited and not duplicated.”

This is not a bibliography entry. It is the foundation of the second law, and it sits 500 articles later. The Two-Fluid Model (212) then cites both 206 and 706 (the-two-fluid-model-in-biquaternionic-form.md:23, 86, 96), so the consumer is at 212 and one of its producers is at 706.

Verdict: move 706 before 206, or split the definitional sections (§The State Space, §The Entropy Functional, §Monotonicity) into an earlier article. The physical content is not in question; the proof order is.

6.2 The quantum-fluid instances are used before they are defined

The Two-Fluid Model (212) says what it cites and does not repeat: the stress-energy element is 523’s, the entropy current is 206’s, the equation of state is 205’s (the-two-fluid-model-in-biquaternionic-form.md:23). The Thermodynamics of a Quantum Fluid (213) says the three articles that “no longer open it” are 523, 450, and 524 (the-thermodynamics-of-a-quantum-fluid-status-of-the-derivation.md:35). The Fluid articles 201–213 are therefore consumers of 450, 523, and 524, all of which sit in Relativistic Quantum Theory.

The same pattern appears earlier. The Current Algebra (183) says the phase-as-coordinate statement “is developed for the scalar case in” 524 (the-current-algebra.md:65). The Relativistic Quantum Space as a Fluid (182) points to 519, 523, 524, 449, 450, 451, 452, 405, 406 for the instances (the-relativistic-quantum-space-as-a-fluid.md:15–16).

Verdict: move the instance block (450, 519, 523, 524, 510, 434, 471) before the Fluid thermodynamics articles that consume them, or add a “quantum-fluid instances” subsection at the start of the Fluid thermodynamics, before 201, that owns the definitions. The current order is general → consumer → instance.

6.3 The general fluid machinery is in the late non-relativistic limit

Criterion for a Fluid Equation (190) is a general method article. It links to The Euler and Continuity Equations (688), The Equation of State and the Barotropic Condition (691), The Reverse Madelung Map (692), The Fluid–Wave Correspondence (693), The Clebsch Potentials (694), and Viscosity and the Rayleigh Function (690). These six are in the Non-Relativistic Non-Quantum category at 688–694. The links are 5–6 per target and come from 190, 192, 193, 208, 211, and 214.

If 688–694 are strictly the classical limit, the forward pointer is legitimate: the general relativistic article points to its limit. But 190 is the criterion for a fluid equation, not a limit article. The audit cannot tell from links alone whether 190’s criterion is independent of 688–694. If it is independent, label the six as “the classical limit”. If it is not, the definitions must move into Fluid.

Verdict: check 190’s derivation. If it uses the Euler/continuity or Clebsch results, move those sections into Fluid. If not, add a scope sentence that says the six are the classical limit, and keep the links as limit pointers.

6.4 The compact-sector ceiling is in the Strong Force category

The Compact Sector of the Biquaternions Is $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ (281) proves that the algebra reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. It is a theorem about $\mathbb{B}$, not about QCD. Its own introduction says it is the companion of Non-Abelian Connections (152) and Instantons and Solitons (219), and that the result is “a result, not a failure” (the-compact-sector...md:3–16). It sits in Strong Force / Generalities at 281.

It is cited from 11 body-layer links in 8 source articles: 2, 152, 260, 262, 264, 265, 269, 271. The earliest body source is why-biquaternions (2), which uses it for the programme’s ceiling. The early obstruction catalogue What the Biquaternion Algebra Cannot Do (12) names the ceiling in its no-go set but does not link it: “the ceiling is the result of The Compact Sector...” (what-the-biquaternion-algebra-cannot-do...md:400). The catalogue is at 12; the proof is at 281.

This is the clearest “should be before” case in the corpus. The ceiling is a foundational limit. The early catalogue’s formal entries O2 and O13 are proved where they stand, but the dedicated compact-sector theorem is the proof a reader needs for the no-go reading, and it is 269 articles later. A reader of the Limits section cannot verify that reading without jumping to Strong Force; a reader of Strong Force may think the group is derived until 281.

Verdict: move 281 to Limits, Cost & Empirical Status, after the obstruction catalogue (12), and link it from the catalogue’s no-go entry. Alternatively, move it to Action after Non-Abelian Connections (152), but the Limits placement is better because the result is a limit, not a gauge construction.

6.5 The Standard Model derivation is split across Symmetry Breaking and Electroweak

The Symmetry Breaking category is “From the SU(4) Model to the Standard Model.” It states the breaking and the SM content. But the low-energy electroweak working-out is in the Electroweak category:

  • The Weak Sector Below $M_C$ (318) is “the weak half of Classification of the Bosons, cut out so that the grid article stays about the grid” (the-weak-sector...md:3–16). It receives 10 body-layer links from 7 sources, earliest 229.
  • Electroweak Symmetry Breaking (338) is “the full working-out that The Higgs Mechanism names as its own gap” (electroweak-symmetry-breaking...md:3–14). It receives 13 body-layer links from 10 sources, earliest 245.
  • The Symmetry articles say “the computation is the subject of” 338 (the-standard-model-content-quantum-numbers-and-the-nineteen-parameters.md:179, again at :258) and “their masses are computed below the transition, in” 318 (the-mass-matrix-of-the-fifteen-connections.md:11). The scalar-content article says the field “that waits for the electroweak scale” is 318 (the-scalar-content-one-matrix-and-ten-survivors.md:24).

The 57 links from Symmetry Breaking to Electroweak are the second-largest category-skipping flow. They are not a bibliography. They are the SM derivation’s missing half.

Verdict: move 318 into Symmetry Breaking after the boson classification (229) and before the scalar content (249); move 338 after 318 and before the Standard Model content (259). Alternatively, move the Electroweak category before Strong Force and before the “Standard Model content” articles. The first is surgical; the second changes the category order. The audit cannot tell whether the split is a presentation choice or a circular dependency. If the EW articles are independent transcriptions, the claim is merely split. If they use the SU(4) breaking as an input, the argument is circular. A referee pass should read the dependency direction, not just the links.

8. The Citation Cycle

The body-layer graph is not a DAG. A Tarjan pass finds 10 strongly connected components with more than one article. The largest contains 151 articles, spans indices 2–742, and includes 12 of the 14 categories. It contains 438 of the 781 body-layer forward links (56%). The other nine components are small: 5 articles at 550–556, 4 at 241–244, and pairs at 195/204, 222/223, 321/325, 415/416, 461/462, 533/534, 535/536.

The giant component includes the core: why-biquaternions (2), the Action general articles (132–152), the Fluid general and thermodynamic articles (182–213), the SU(4) model (216), the Symmetry articles (245–271), the compact sector (281), the electroweak articles (318, 338), the RQT fluid instances (450, 523, 524), and the late fluid machinery (694). Removing any single hub does not break it. Removing why-biquaternions leaves 150 articles; removing the Data Boundary leaves 151; removing the compact sector leaves 150.

Two concrete cycles:

  • The Spin-Half Fluid (450) → The Nodal Set (524) → The Domain of the Polar Decomposition (187) → Why Biquaternions? (2) → The Spin-Half Fluid (450).
  • The Compact Sector (281) → The SU(4) Model (216) → The Scalar Content (249) → The Weak Sector Below $M_C$ (318) → The Weinberg Angle (309) → Electroweak Symmetry Breaking (338) → The Standard Model Lagrangian (260) → Stress–Energy Conservation (146) → The Rung Dictionary (141) → Five Steps (191) → The Domain (187) → Why Biquaternions? (2) → The Compact Sector (281).

These are citation cycles, not proof cycles. A “see also” back-link is enough to close one. The audit cannot certify that any cycle is logically circular. It can say that the corpus has no linear topological order at the body-link level, and that a reader following links can loop.

Consequence for the “remove all forward links” goal. You cannot remove all forward links by reordering. Inside the 151-article component, every order has forward edges. You must either cut links or accept that the component is a web. The menu order is one possible spine through the web; the body links are annotations on it.

9. What This Says About the Theory

The audit does not re-derive the physics and does not certify correctness. It reads the link structure. Four conclusions follow.

9.1 The order is synthesis-first, not derivation-first

The category balance shows the early categories are net senders and the detailed categories are net receivers. The corpus states the general result first and the detailed support later. This is a legitimate top-down pedagogy, but it has a cost: at the point where a claim is made, the reader cannot verify it. The cost is visible in the five deferrals above.

9.2 The SU(3) ceiling is a boundary, not a defect, but it is buried

The algebra reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. That is a precise, honest result. The presentation risk is that the proof sits in the Strong Force category, while the early programme uses it and the obstruction catalogue names it. A reader may think the strong group is derived until article 281. The fix is a move, not a rewrite. This is the single most important theory-presentation issue the audit found.

9.3 The Standard Model derivation is not self-contained at the point of claim

The Symmetry Breaking category says the SU(4) model returns the Standard Model. The electroweak content that makes the claim concrete — the weak sector, EWSB, the Weinberg angle, the W/Z masses, the anomalies, the CKM/PMNS matrices — is in the Electroweak category. The 57 links are the evidence. The audit cannot tell whether this is a split presentation or a circular argument. The check is a referee pass on the dependency direction: does any EW article use the SU(4) breaking as an input? If yes, the “derivation” is circular. If no, it is merely split. The audit flags the place; it does not settle the question.

9.4 The fluid thermodynamics is a set of modules with scattered owners

The Fluid thermodynamics articles are written as a modular chain: 206 owns the entropy current, 523 owns the stress tensor, 205 owns the equation of state, 706 owns the entropy functional, 450/524 own the spinor and nodal instances. Each article says what it “cites and does not repeat.” This is good modular writing. But the modules are not in dependency order. The consumer (212) is at 212; one producer (706) is at 706. The second law is stated before its functional. This is a proof-order inversion, not a physics error.

9.5 No contradiction was found in the links themselves

The audit found no physics-internal link that points to a missing physics article, no unresolved physics target, and no self-contradictory pair. The 151-article component is a citation web, not a contradiction. The defects are ordering defects. That is good news: they are fixable by moving articles and changing link policy, not by redoing the physics.

10. Recommendations

The ordered execution plan is §12. This section gives the reasons; §12 gives the sequence, the exact menu edits, and the verification log. Sections 2–9 describe the baseline 6ee5793e6; they are not rewritten after each move, and §12 records the deltas.

10.1 Choose the reading model first

The corpus cannot be both a strict linear treatise and a densely cross-linked web. The current menu is a linear order, but the body links are a web. The first decision is which one is primary.

  • Linear treatise. Adopt the rule “no body-layer link that skips a category.” Remove or plain-text the 260 category-skipping links. Keep same-category forward links and adjacent-category links. This is the least destructive way to make the menu order authoritative.
  • Reference web. Keep the links, but label them. Add a “forward” marker or a “see later” phrase in the prose, or a CSS class on the link. The reader then knows the jump is intentional. The audit’s counts become descriptive, not defects.
  • Hybrid (recommended). Keep Further Reading and the explicit indexes; forbid category-skipping body links in ordinary articles; allow them only in hubs and in a “roadmap” block at the start of a category.

10.2 Apply the no-skip rule

The rule is mechanical and checkable:

A body-layer link may point forward only to an article in the same category or in the immediately following category. A link that skips a category must be in Further Reading, in an explicit hub, or in a roadmap table.

The baseline violation list is the 260 rows in audit-outbound-edges.tsv with category_gap >= 2. The 79 source articles are the edit list. The top 10 sources account for 111 of the 260 (43%); the top 30 account for 196 (75%). Start with the largest sources: 2 (20 skips), 182 (19), 266 (12), 190 (11), 271 (11), 259 (9), 245 (8), 183 (7), 207 (7), 212 (7), 213 (7). The tail is long enough that the rule is easier to enforce than to fix article by article.

10.4 Convert optional pointers to plain text

Not every forward link is load-bearing. Many are “the case is worked in X” or “the companion article is Y”. Replace the Markdown link with the title in plain text, or with an italic title and no anchor. The prose still reads; the reader no longer jumps. This is the cheapest way to reduce the clickable forward graph without touching the menu.

Do not do this to a link that carries a definition. The sentence “the stress-energy element is [523]’s” is load-bearing; removing the link without moving 523 leaves the reader stranded. The audit’s context_before column is there to help decide.

10.5 Keep the bibliography and the indexes

Further Reading is 1,405 forward links. It is a bibliography. Removing it would be a loss, not a fix. If the concern is the built page’s link graph, split the bibliography into “Before” and “After” or “See also”, and let the reader see the direction. The Quantisation Hub, Sector Hub, and category introductions are indexes; keep them, but add a back-link from the section’s first article so they are not orphans.

The Data Boundary (245) is a rule for the section: it stands first by design (physics.md:1337–1340). 10.7 Add a guardrail (proposal only)

A build-time check can enforce the no-skip rule without changing any article. The check reads the same parse as this audit and fails if a body-layer link has category_gap >= 2 and is not in an allowlist of hubs. 11. Method and Limits

Parse. Every .md in articles_physics/ was read. Links of the form ](target.html) were extracted with their section headings. Targets were resolved against the 755 slugs. The 778 maths links, 18 internal-other links, and 1 other-relative link were counted but excluded from the forward test.

Order. The index of an article is its position in physics.md. “Forward” means target index greater than source index. “Category gap” is the difference between the target’s H2 position and the source’s H2 position in the 14-category order. A gap of 0 is same-category; 1 is the next category; 2 or more skips at least one category.

Section classes. Further Reading is detected by the heading. Summary and Summary of Notation are separate. Source table is a table keyed by “source”. Everything else is body. The body-layer count (781) is all non-Further-Reading links; the body-proper count (763) excludes summary and source tables.

SCC. Tarjan’s algorithm on the body-layer graph, 755 nodes and 1,169 distinct edges (1,537 links with duplicate source→target pairs collapsed). The 151-article component is the largest; the graph has 591 components in total. The condensation has 329 edges, of which 134 point backward in menu order. The SCC is a citation graph, not a proof graph: a “see also” back-link is enough to join two articles.

Context. The context_before column in the TSV is the 220 characters before the link. The audit used it to distinguish “cites and does not repeat” from “see also”. It did not classify all 781 links semantically; the five deferrals are the ones where the context states the dependency explicitly.

What the audit does not do. It does not check the physics, the numerical correctness, or the consistency of the equations. It does not read the maths corpus’s order. It does not treat every forward link as a defect. It does not propose article rewrites beyond the scope sentences and the moves. It does not modify the plan.

Reproducibility. Baseline 6ee5793e6. The parse and analysis scripts were transient in /tmp/audit_out/ and are not part of the repository. The TSV is the durable output.

12. The Migration: Order of Operations and Log

12.0 Status and conventions

Reading model chosen: linear with exemptions. The menu order is the reading order; a body-layer forward link is allowed inside a category and into the next one; anything longer must be in Further Reading, in an allowlisted index, or written as plain text. Two exemptions are structural and are named here rather than discovered later. The first is the next-category window above. The second is the directory article: an article whose declared job is to enumerate a block — a framework, a criterion, a hub, a map — may link forward into the block it enumerates, because a directory that could not point would have no content. Thirteen directories carry 52% of the residual skips (§12.6), and the test that admits one is stated there: it points, and no skipping link points at it.

Index convention. Indices in this document are 0-based positions in the menu (the data.json convention); in §12 they are baseline indices unless the sentence says “now at”. The menu position is the index plus one, so article “206” is the 207th <a> in physics.md. The menu is edited by moving the anchor and its <!-- --> comment block as one paragraph; the corpus separates paragraphs by blank lines.

Baseline of the log. 6ee5793e6. Sections 2–9 above are the baseline snapshot. The counts in this section come from a re-parse of articles_physics/*.md against the menu, and reproduce the baseline to within one link (the audit's data.json parse and this one differ on a handful of links with titles or anchors).

12.7 The blocking problems

Five things the audit cannot settle alone, and each is stated with the number that makes it a decision rather than a preference.

1. The 176-link triage is a reading task, not a counting task. Each link needs one judgement: does the source sentence use the target, or mention it? A move fixes a use; plain text fixes a mention; an allowlist entry admits a ledger. The classification in §12.6 narrows it to 62 articles and four flows, but the per-sentence reading of 102 links is the author's work or a delegated pass with the author's intent stated first. Blocked on intent, not on tooling.

2. Where the rule lives. The corpus has eleven conventions articles and none of them is about links: they are algebra, products, norms, elements, frames, matrices, operators, indices, units and concepts. The no-skip rule is a rule of composition, and its natural home is a twelfth conventions article — Link and Reading-Order Conventions — which would be article 756, needing a menu slot in the conventions block and a full article in the house format. Not written: adding a conventions article is an editorial decision about the corpus's front matter, not a fix to a link. The rule stands in §12.0 of this audit until that decision is taken.

3. Package 3 (the fluid instances) cannot be done as stated. The seven articles are cells of the RQT spin matrices (§12.5 of the earlier draft, and the measurements above): the spin-0 fluid cell holds eight articles and four of them are on the list; the spin-½ fluid cell would lose its only entry. A split is the only route — each article would give up its “instance” half to a Fluid article and keep its “method” half — and that is seven articles rewritten, not seven menu lines moved. Blocked on effort, and the gain is 25 links.

4. “Zero skips” is not attainable, and chasing it damages the corpus. The Fluid-after-RQT move gives 156 skips and the wrong physics. The 305 move gives 205 skips and breaks a census. The honest target is: 111 hub skips declared exempt, 102 residual skips triaged link by link, and a number that stops falling while the design is still improving. A metric that rewards inverting the ontology is the wrong metric, and the audit records that rather than the target it printed before.

5. What a split cannot fix. An article split helps when its own sections belong in different places. Only one article in the corpus answers that test — 305, whose The Bidoublet Extension half belongs with the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ article and whose weak-spectrum half belongs to the transition. The seam is real and the split is clean, and it buys eight links. Not done here: it costs a new article of substance for a 4% gain on one flow, and the census it would empty is the argument of its own category.

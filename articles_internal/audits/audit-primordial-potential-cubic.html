Audit: The Cubic Normalisation in The Primordial Potential, and Four Claims of the Referee

Status of this file. Items 1 and 2 executed on 2026-11-25; items 3 and 4 declined as optional. The article is articles_physics/the-primordial-potential-one-symmetric-minimum-above-m-c.md. One substantive defect is confirmed by computation; one gap is confirmed by counter-example; four further referee items are judged to be optional wording.

What the file is for. It separates the referee's claims into three classes — confirmed, confirmed but smaller than stated, and not a defect — so that the patch list at the end is a decision and not a re-reading.

Introduction

The verdict. The referee is right about the cubic and right about the fifth quartic, and too hard on the other four items. One sentence of the article is arithmetically inconsistent with the thresholds printed three lines below it. Everything else the referee lists is a clarification.

The article is correct where it matters. The potential, the symmetric minimum, the mass spectrum $\mu_\Phi^2$ on fifteen and $\mu_\Phi^2+4\mu_2^2$ on the trace, the traceless cone, the range $[\tfrac14,\tfrac{7}{12}]$ with the maximum at $D$, and the branch structure $\lambda_1<0\Rightarrow D$ are all verified and all right. This audit does not re-derive them; it re-derives the two things that are wrong.

Confirmed: The Cubic Carries a Factor Two It Does Not Use

The sentence, quoted exactly. Line 145:

$\operatorname{tr}Ξ^{3}$ is real for Hermitian $Ξ$, so $c_{3}(\operatorname{tr}Ξ^{3}+\text{h.c.})=2c_{3}\operatorname{tr}Ξ^{3}$

The thresholds, quoted exactly. Three lines later: $c_{3}\sqrt X\gtrsim0.577\,\lambda_{1}$ against the even split, and $c_{3}\sqrt X\gtrsim0.31\,\lambda_{1}$ against the identity, the second being "$\tfrac13$ divided by the cubic's advantage $0.577+0.5$".

The two are not the same normalisation, and the numbers are computed in the second.

So the article's numbers are a single-$c_3$ computation throughout, and the phrase "$0.577+0.5$" settles it: under $2c_3$ the advantage would be $1.1547+1$, which no one writes as $0.577+0.5$.

The defect is the $+$h.c. clause, and it is redundant twice over. $\operatorname{tr}Ξ^{3}$ is real for Hermitian $Ξ$, since $\bigl(\operatorname{tr}M^{3}\bigr)^{*}=\operatorname{tr}(M^{\dagger})^{3}=\operatorname{tr}M^{3}$. A real term needs no Hermitian conjugate, so writing $c_3(\operatorname{tr}\Xi^3+\text{h.c.})$ adds a conjugate equal to the term it conjugates, and the $=2c_3$ that follows doubles a coefficient the thresholds define singly.

The minimal repair deletes the clause; it does not halve the thresholds. Keeping $2c_3$ and printing $0.289$ and $0.155$ costs three further edits — both thresholds, the Checks row, and the "$0.577+0.5$" phrase — and makes transparent arithmetic opaque. The single-$c_3$ reading is the one the article already reasons in.

Scope of the defect: one sentence. The Checks row "the cubic's value on the declared direction | $-24r^{3}$ with $r^{2}=X/12$ | $-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}X^{3/2}$" is $\operatorname{tr}\Xi^{3}$ and is already correct. The notation row for $c_{3}$ is silent on normalisation and survives the repair unchanged.

Confirmed: The Fifth Quartic Is Excluded From the Cone Without Being Named There

What the referee says. The potential section allows $\operatorname{tr}\Xi^{3}\operatorname{tr}\Xi$ and sets it to zero; the cone section derives with $\lambda_1\ldots\lambda_4$ and never repeats the declaration. This is right, and it is more than presentational.

A counter-example, so the point does not rest on a reading. Take a point satisfying all four stated rows and add a fifth quartic with $\lambda_5<0$:

$$ \lambda_{1}=0,\quad \lambda_{2}=0,\quad \lambda_{3}=0,\quad \lambda_{4}=0.1,\quad \lambda_{5}=-1 . $$

The four rows hold: $\lambda_1+\lambda_2=0$, $\lambda_1+4\lambda_2=0$, $\tfrac14\lambda_1+\lambda_2+4\lambda_3+16\lambda_4=1.6\ge0$, $\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3+\lambda_4=0.1\ge0$. Along the ray $Ξ=t\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1)$,

$$ x=4t^{2},\quad \operatorname{tr}Ξ^{4}=4t^{4},\quad y=2t,\quad y^{2}x=16t^{4},\quad y^{4}=16t^{4},\quad y\operatorname{tr}Ξ^{3}=4t^{4}, $$

$$ V_{4}=\bigl(0+0+0+1.6-4\bigr)t^{4}=-2.4\,t^{4}<0 . $$

So the four printed conditions do not bound the potential the article says is allowed. They bound the potential with $\lambda_5=0$, which is the potential the article actually uses, and for that potential the cone is correct. The gap is that the cone section does not say which potential it is a cone for.

The repair is one sentence. The counter-example stays out of the article; one clause suffices: the cone is the cone of $V$ with $\lambda_5=0$, and the fifth quartic is a declaration and not a symmetry.

Judged Optional: The Four Wording Items

The "fifteen generators" paragraph is not garbled. The referee calls it "conceptually muddled" and offers a replacement. The original is correct: a generator is traceless because $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ removes the trace, and $Ξ$ is a field and not an element of the algebra. The referee's replacement adds the representation content $\mathbf{4}\otimes\bar{\mathbf4}=\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1$, which the article states in the Introduction by way of The Scalar's Two Indices. A clause naming it in §The Potential would help a reader who opens the article there; it corrects no error. Addition, low priority.

"Two that bind" is asserted, not derived. Correct, and the referee's diagnosis is right. The argument is one line: at fixed $x$ and $y$ the quartic is linear in $\operatorname{tr}Ξ^{4}/x^{2}$ and in $y^{2}/x$, and those two attain their extremes only at the identity and at a single entry, so no interior direction beats both corners. Worth adding: it is the only unproved step in a section whose whole claim is that neither row can be dropped. Addition, medium priority.

"The degeneracy is not an accident of the group, and it is not what it was" is cryptic but not wrong. The next sentence names $\mathrm{O}(16)$ at $\mu_2^2=0$ and $\mathrm{U}(4)$ otherwise, so the antecedent is one line away. Optional tightening: better prose, same physics.

The thermal barrier, the $\mathrm{U}(3)$ little group, and the $\mathrm{U}(4)$ justification in the Checks table are acceptable as they stand. On the thermal point the barrier is the $T\,m^{3}$ term of the finite-temperature potential, and that term is proportional to the cubic coupling in the high-temperature expansion, so "a barrier or none depending on the cubic" is a correct compression rather than a slip. On $\mathrm{U}(3)$ the referee agrees the article is correct, since $S(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3))\cong\mathrm{U}(3)$ at dimension nine. No action.

What the Referee Got Right That Was Already Right

Right, and already the article's claim, so the credit is double-counted: the cone's two ends, the range $[\tfrac14,\tfrac{7}{12}]$, the branch structure of $\lambda_1$, the trace-sign analysis, and the "distinctions" section. The referee verifies these and calls them strengths; they are strengths, and no edit follows from the verification.

Right, and worth keeping visible after the repair: that $\operatorname{tr}D^{3}<0$ lowers $D$ and that the even split's cubic vanishes identically. That asymmetry is why the thresholds exist, and it survives item 1 intact.

The referee's own arithmetic slips once. In the $2c_3$ paragraph it writes the advantage over the identity as $c_3X^{3/2}+0.577c_3X^{3/2}$ and then uses $2.155$, mixing the two normalisations in the way the article does. The table above is the clean version.

Strategy

One patch is required, one is strongly advised, two are optional.

Item 1 changes no number in the article, which is the test that it is the right repair: the thresholds, the Checks row and the "$0.577+0.5$" phrase already say what the repaired sentence will say.

Item 2 changes no number either, and it closes a gap the counter-example shows is real rather than pedantic. The Checks table gains one row stating the cone's scope: the trace-channel conditions are for $\lambda_5=0$.

Nothing else in the article moves. The referee's proposed reorganisation — demoting the trace channel to an appendix, shortening the cubic — is declined: the trace channel is where the identity row bites, and the cubic is the only place the branch asymmetry is visible.

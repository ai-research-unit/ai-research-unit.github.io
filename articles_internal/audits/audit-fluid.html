Applied Migration: the Fluid Block and Its Two Articles Now Say "Fluid Physics"

Status of this file. APPLIED on 2026-11-27. The tree at dc73e8ae7 was read, measured, and then edited: 213 occurrences replaced, 3 files renamed, 3 sitemaps rewritten. Nothing is committed, and the deploy folder has not been rebuilt. The corpus — physics.md, maths.md, index.md, articles_physics/, articles_internal/, articles_maths/ — now contains zero occurrences of the old term outside citations of further readings.

What this file is for. It records what changed, in what order, with what evidence, so the change can be verified, reviewed, or reversed. It replaces the earlier review-mode audit, whose recommendation (label only) the author overrode in favour of the maximum migration.

The decision, in five lines.

  1. Maximum migration was instructed. Every section heading, category name, article title, slug, and body sentence was renamed; only cited works were left alone.
  2. Three files were renamed, so two published URLs changed.
  3. Thirteen citations and three verbatim quotations were preserved — a rename may not rewrite a book's title or another author's sentence.
  4. The two dated-audit exceptions of the earlier plan were withdrawn by the same instruction; the audits now carry the new vocabulary.
  5. One file still spells the old term: this one, in the reversal key at the end. A migration record that cannot name what it replaced cannot be undone.

1. What the Instruction Changed

The earlier audit proposed three scopes and recommended the narrowest. The author chose the widest: no occurrence of the old discipline name anywhere in the physics and maths corpus, in article bodies as well as labels, with citations of further readings as the only exception. That turned the two open questions of the earlier plan — titles and slugs — from "keep" to "rename", and withdrew the rule that dated audits keep their date's vocabulary.

Question the author asked Answer applied
Rename sections? Yes. The block heading and the Ordinary Fluid Mechanics subheading in physics.md, and the Fluid Mechanics: The Observable With No Mode heading in an internal note.
Rename categories? Yes. The menu block Biquaternion Universe Relativistic Quantum Fluid Mechanics became … Fluid Physics, and every reference and gloss to it followed.
Rename article titles? Yes. Spin Fluid Mechanics in Biquaternionic Form and Causal Dissipative Fluid Mechanics in Biquaternionic Form.
Rename slugs? Yes, because the corpus rule binds a title to its file name: spin-fluid-physics-in-biquaternionic-form, causal-dissipative-fluid-physics-in-biquaternionic-form.
Rename article content? Yes. Every body sentence, comment, table row, and cross-reference; plus the adjectives (fluid-mechanical to fluid-physical).
Keep citations? Yes. Nine Landau–Lifshitz Fluid Mechanics entries, Geisbauer's paper title, and three verbatim quotations from Visser's papers.

5. Consequences That Need a Follow-Up

  1. Two URLs now 404. build.py's prune_orphans() deletes any deployed page whose source has gone, and it has no redirect mechanism (verified: no redirect code in build.py). Host-level redirects for the two old paths are the only way to keep inbound links alive. This is the same unpaid debt the 2026-11-25 record logged.
  2. The deploy folder is stale. It still holds the two old pages and the old wording on 34 pages. build_diff.py is the incremental route: it rebuilds only pages whose .md is newer, and prunes the two orphans.
  3. Third-party quotations in articles_external/ are unchanged. That folder records other people's papers; their sentences stay as written. Only our own cross-references into it were updated.
  4. Inbound links from outside the corpus are unmeasurable from this tree.

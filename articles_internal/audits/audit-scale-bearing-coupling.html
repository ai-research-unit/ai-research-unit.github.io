Detecting a Scale-Bearing Coupling: One Test, Six Signatures, Seven Traps, Twenty-Three Articles

Status of this file. A working rule, kept in articles_internal/instructions. This file is a detection manual, not a published page. It states how to find the sentences that carry a scale-bearing coupling, how to tell a real one from a lookalike, and where the physics corpus actually has them. It prints no strength value, only the shape of the sentences that do, so it obeys the rule it teaches.

Where the subject is defined. articles_internal/the-coupling-sector-of-the-fourth-colour-model.md §0 defines it, and articles_physics/the-data-boundary-which-articles-may-print-a-measured-strength.md is the published boundary. This file adds the one thing neither of those has: a procedure that a reader can run over a folder.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. The test is one question: can the sentence drop the scale? Everything else in this file is that question applied.
  2. The unit of detection is the sentence, and the unit of repair is the paragraph that contains it. An article is not in a category; each of its sentences is.
  3. A signature is evidence, and the verdict is the sentence. S1 alone is a hit; S5 with S2 is a hit; two of S2, S3, S4, S6 are a hit; S3 and S6 alone are not.
  4. Classify before repairing, and never delete the scale while keeping the sentence. "$g_4=g_L$" with its "at $M_U$" removed is not a repair; it is a sentence that fails the test and no longer shows why.
  5. No strength value is printed anywhere in this file, and §7 checks it against that claim.

Introduction

The test is one question: can the sentence drop the scale? Everything else in this file is that question applied.

A value, a boundary condition at a scale, a running, a matching, and a ratio called exact are one object seen five ways. None of them can be written without naming an energy. That is what unites them, and it is also why the algebra cannot adjudicate them: an object that cannot be stated without a scale is not an object the algebra has an opinion about.

  • A value needs a scale. $\alpha_s$ alone means nothing; $\alpha_s(M_Z)$ is a number.
  • A boundary condition is a scale statement. "$g_4=g_L$ at $M_{PS}$" carries the scale inside the sentence.
  • A running is two scales and the path between them.
  • A matching is two descriptions at one scale.
  • A ratio is exact only because two couplings were set equal at one scale.

The technical synonym is "renormalised coupling", and the synonym is not an improvement. A renormalisation condition is a boundary condition at a scale, so the phrase is exact and unhelpful: it names the apparatus rather than the defect. Scale-bearing is the plain word, and it is the one in use.

Three categories follow.

category test where it lives
structural coupling can drop the scale: a symbol multiplying a generator, a trace normalisation everywhere, untouched
scale-bearing coupling cannot drop the scale the flagged article only
measured coupling external data stays where it is used, marked as data

Why the middle row is the dangerous one, in one sentence. A structural coupling is decided by the algebra, so an error in it is caught by a trace identity. A scale-bearing coupling is decided by nothing in the algebra — it is either imposed or measured — so an error in it is invisible until an experiment, and it moves when the scale moves. That is why they must be quarantined and not merely labelled.

What this file is for. A rule stated in prose is a rule nobody applies uniformly. So this file gives six mechanical signatures, the false positives each one produces, the census of the whole physics folder, and four repairs. The census is the part that makes it a rule rather than an opinion: it says which articles trip the signatures, and it says what each one is allowed to keep.

1. The Test, and the Fourth Category It Does Not Have

The corpus already names the classes twice, in two vocabularies, and the two are the same classification. A reader who learns one and then meets the other will think there are four categories. There are three.

this file the-data-boundary the-coupling-sector §0
structural structural structural coupling
scale-bearing claim scale-bearing coupling
measured data measured coupling

The two vocabularies divide the same space, and neither is more correct. The boundary page uses "claim" because its question is permission: may this article assert a value. The coupling-sector note uses "scale-bearing" because its question is diagnosis: why is this sentence not checkable. Detection wants the diagnostic word, because the diagnostic word names the reason.

The category that does not exist, and is often invented. A measured value at a named scale is not a fourth kind. It is one sentence carrying two properties: it is scale-bearing, and its value comes from outside. The distinction does work only when the question is permission — a measured number is permitted in an article whose subject is the measurement, and a scale-bearing number with no measurement behind it is not permitted anywhere outside the flagged article. For detection, both are hits, and the first move after a hit is to ask which of the two it is.

The test is applied to one sentence, not to one article. An article is not in a category; each of its sentences is. A geometry article can contain one scale-bearing sentence in §8 while the other ninety per cent is arithmetic. So the unit of detection is the sentence, and the unit of repair is the paragraph that contains it.

2. The Five Forms, as Five Sentence Shapes

Detection is easier when the forms are written as shapes rather than as descriptions. Each shape below is a sentence pattern; each one fails the test for the same reason and by a different mechanism.

form the shape where the scale sits corpus example
value a coupling with a scale in its argument in the parentheses $\alpha_s(M_Z)$, $\alpha^{-1}(M_Z)$, $g_R(M_C)$
boundary condition two couplings joined by $=$, with a scale in the predicate after "at" $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at $M_U$
running a coupling at one scale expressed through another, with a logarithm of the ratio in the log and in both endpoints $\alpha^{-1}(\mu)=\alpha^{-1}(M_Z)-\tfrac{b}{2\pi}\ln(\mu/M_Z)$
matching two couplings of different groups related at one threshold in "at the threshold" $\tfrac{1}{g_Y^2}=\tfrac{1}{g_R^2}+\tfrac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}$ at $M_R$
exact ratio a ratio declared exact, with the reason being an equality of couplings in the unstated premise $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$

The first four announce their scale. The fifth hides it, and it is the one that survives every mechanical scan. The equation $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ contains no energy, no logarithm and no "at". What makes it scale-bearing is a premise the sentence does not print: the ratio is $\tfrac38$ only where the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ couplings are equal, and that equality holds at one scale. Put in one line, the sentence is "$\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ at the scale where $g_L=g_R$", and the scale is back.

That is why the test is about the sentence's premises and not only its symbols. A reader who greps for scales will find four of the five forms and miss the fifth every time.

3. The Six Signatures: What to Grep, and What Each Proves

Six mechanical signatures, in order of how much they prove. A signature is evidence, not a verdict. The rule for using them is at the end of the section.

S1 — a scale inside a coupling's argument. This is the strongest single signal, because the syntax is unambiguous: a coupling symbol, a bracket, and a scale inside the bracket.

(g_\{?[A-Za-z0-9-]+\}?|\\alpha(?:_\{?[A-Za-z0-9]{1,3}\}?)?|\\lambda)\s*\(\s*(M_\{?[A-Za-z]\}?|m_[A-Za-z]\w*|Q\^2|q\^2|\\mu)

S2 — a scale in a predicate. Weaker, because a scale in a predicate may belong to a mass or a chain rather than to a coupling. The signature is the preposition, not the scale.

(at|to|above|below)\s+\$?(the\s+)?(scale\s+)?\$?M_\{?[A-Za-z]\}?

S3 — an explicit energy unit. The weakest of the linguistic signals and the most reliable of the numeric ones: a number followed by an energy unit is a scale that has been written down. It marks a value, which is either measured or a claim.

[0-9][0-9.^{}]*\$?\s*(\\text\{\s*)?(GeV|TeV|MeV)

S4 — running language. A derivative with respect to scale, a one-loop coefficient, a logarithm of a ratio, or the phrase "the running of the ... coupling".

(\\beta[-_]?function|beta[- ]function|one-loop (coefficient|slope)|running of (the|a) (strong|gauge|coupling)|runs?[^.]{0,25}coupling)

S5 — an equality between coupling symbols. A bare $g_4=g_L$ is the signature of a boundary condition whose scale is in the next clause or in the previous sentence. It is the signature that most often has the scale deleted and forgotten.

g_4\s*=\s*g_L|g_L\s*=\s*g_R\s*=\s*g_4|g_L\s*=\s*g_R

S6 — exactness claimed for a ratio. The word "exact", or "exactly", within a sentence that also contains a trigonometric or a numeric fraction. This is the only signature that finds the fifth form.

(exact(ly)?)[^.]{0,120}(\\sin|\\theta_W|\\tfrac|\\frac)

The rule for using them, which is what makes this a procedure rather than a list of greps.

  1. S1 alone is a hit. No further evidence is needed; the sentence has a scale in a coupling, so the article is carrying a coupling statement.
  2. S5 plus S2 is a hit. An equality and a predicate together are a boundary condition.
  3. Two of S2, S3, S4, S6 are a hit. One is an accident of subject matter; two rarely are.
  4. S3 alone is not a hit. Masses, bounds and decay constants carry energy units and are not couplings.
  5. S6 alone is not a hit. "Exactly" is common in mathematics. It is a hit only when a coupling equality is the reason.
  6. A hit is classified before it is repaired. The next question is never "how do I delete this" but "is this measured or imposed".

4. Seven False Positives, and the Four False Negatives That Matter More

A detection rule that fires on lookalikes is worse than no rule, because its rejections train a reader to ignore it. Seven lookalikes occur in this corpus, and each is real and auditable.

FP1 — $g_L=g_R$ as group elements, not as couplings. This is the expensive one. The string appears in seven articles and means a coupling in one of them.

article occurrences what the equality means
custodial-symmetry-and-the-rho-parameter-in-biquaternionic-form 8 the diagonal subgroup $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V=\{g_L=g_R\}$, elements of a group
the-one-coupling-that-returns-the-standard-model 3 the coupling condition: a boundary condition at a scale
the-chiral-theory-and-the-lagrangian-the-pion-and-the-decay-constant 2 constant unit quaternions $g_L,g_R$, elements
the-breaking-chain-and-the-boson-spectrum 2 a pointer to the flagged article, not a statement
the-nonlinear-sigma-model-in-biquaternionic-form 1 group elements
the-lagrangian-and-the-nonlinear-sigma-model 1 the diagonal $g_L=g_R=g_V$, a group element
the-fourth-colour-left-right-model 1 a pointer to the flagged article, naming the case that fails

The discriminator is the type of the symbol, and it is decidable. If $g_L$ is a group element, then $g_L=g_R$ defines a subgroup and survives every rescaling of any coupling. If $g_L$ is a coupling, then $g_L=g_R$ is a statement about a running and holds at one scale. The test: is the thing being multiplied by a generator, or is it multiplying one? Custodial symmetry multiplies; a coupling multiplies a generator. So the fix is to read one line of context — the sentence that says what $g$ acts on.

FP2 — a scale that belongs to a mass, a chain or a bound. "$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ breaks at $M_C$" and "$M_X\gtrsim$ some floor" both trip S2. Neither is a coupling statement. The chain fixes a stabiliser; the bound is a measured null result. The discriminator: does the scale attach to a coupling, or to a particle?

FP3 — "running" as an ordinary verb. Fluids run, indices run, parameters of a differential equation run. The corpus has an article on the running of a condensate and several on flows. The discriminator: is the subject a coupling?

FP4 — $\ln$ and $\log$ in thermodynamics. Entropy, free energy and the thermal circle all carry logarithms of ratios, and none of those ratios is a ratio of scales of a coupling. The discriminator: is the argument of the log an energy ratio, or a partition function?

FP5 — $\beta$ as a velocity, an angle, or a matrix. The corpus uses $\beta$ for $v/c$, for an angle in the CKM parameterisation, and for a beta coefficient. Only the last is running. The discriminator: is there a derivative with respect to $\ln\mu$?

FP6 — "coupling" used for a structure. Self-coupling, minimal coupling, strong coupling as a regime, an anomalous coupling, Yukawa normalisations. The word survives in about two dozen menu comments for exactly this reason, and none of those uses is a scale-bearing value. The discriminator: is the word naming a strength, or a vertex?

FP7 — "exactly" in ordinary mathematics. A trace identity is exact. An eigenvalue count is exact. Those are the corpus's best sentences and they must not be flagged. The discriminator: is the exactness caused by two couplings being set equal?

The false negatives matter more, because nothing catches them. Four occur here, and each has an article behind it.

  • A hidden premise. $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ with no scale printed. Only S6, read by a human, finds it.
  • A ratio with a coupling in it. $M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)\,M_{W_R}$ contains no scale argument and no logarithm, but the ratio $g_4/g_R$ is evaluated wherever the matching puts it. A symbol that moves with the scale counts as a scale.
  • A mass equality standing in for a Yukawa condition. $m_d=m_e$ at a scale is a Yukawa boundary condition wearing mass symbols. The scale is in the sentence, the coupling is not, and the reader who greps for $g$ finds nothing.
  • A scale named in words. "at low energies", "in the infrared", "at high scales", "in the ultraviolet". No symbol, no unit, no predicate — and the sentence fails the test just the same.

The lesson of the four. Mechanical signatures find the sentences that write the scale down. The corpus's worst cases are the sentences that leave it out, and only the question catches those: which energy would I have to name to make this true?

Two of the twelve are the permitted claim articles — the one-coupling article for gauge couplings, and the Weinberg-angle article for the ratio. One is the closest call in the corpus, and it is worth naming rather than smoothing over: the scalar-sector article boxes $M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)\,M_{W_R}$, which is a mass ratio whose coefficient is a ratio of couplings. Nothing in the sentence names a scale; the ratio moves whenever the matching moves. It is scale-bearing by the symbol-clause of §4 and it is not in the flagged article. The reason it survives is that the article uses the ratio as a free parameter and never evaluates it, so the statement is structural given the row — and §3 of the coupling note is where the value is discussed. A reader who wants a single item on this list to examine should take this one.

The nine remaining split into seven data articles and two pointers. The pointer form is the corpus's own repair: two articles say, in one sentence, that the equation lives in the flagged article, and then do not write it. That is the pattern §6 recommends, and it already exists in the corpus.

What the census does not cover. The internal notes are outside this scan and are where the density is highest: the-coupling-sector-of-the-fourth-colour-model is a record of coupling statements and carries a ledger banner saying so. The rule for internal notes is the banner, not the census.

6. The Four Repairs

A hit is not a fault until it is classified, and each class has one repair. The choice is mechanical once the class is known.

class repair corpus example
structural after all none; fix the signature's precision, not the sentence the custodial $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$, which is a group
measured keep, and mark: the value, the scheme, the scale, the source the sixteen data articles of the boundary page
scale-bearing, and the article is not the flagged one point: state that the equation lives in the flagged article, print no equation and no number the-breaking-chain §8, the-fourth-colour-left-right-model §"Where the Equal-Coupling Case Departs"
scale-bearing, and the article is the flagged one keep, and say which of the five forms it is the flagged article's own section 4

The pointer is the repair that keeps a geometric article readable without letting a number in. Its shape is three sentences and the corpus already has it: the statement, the classification ("that is a scale-bearing coupling, it is a prediction, and it fails against the measured angle"), and the address. What a pointer may not do is paraphrase the equation. "The couplings meet at one scale" is still a scale statement, and the reader who wants it will follow the link.

A fifth repair exists and should be used sparingly: convert to the counting form. A coupling equality can sometimes be restated as a count, where it becomes structural. The one-bidoublet defect is the model: one matrix for two sectors, so proportional spectra is a counting statement, and it survives without any value, while "$m_d=m_e$ at a scale" does not. Convert when the content is a count. Do not convert when the content is a number, because the conversion then hides the number rather than removing it.

What no repair may do. It may not delete the scale and keep the sentence. "$g_4=g_L$" with the "at $M_U$" removed is not a repair; it is a sentence that fails the test and no longer shows why.

Summary

One test: can the sentence drop the scale. If it can, the statement is structural and the algebra will catch an error in it. If it cannot, the statement is either measured or imposed, nothing in the algebra will catch an error, and it moves when the scale moves.

Three categories, named twice. Structural, scale-bearing, measured; the same three as structural, claim, data. Detection should use the diagnostic word, because the diagnostic word names the reason.

Five forms, one defect. A value, a boundary condition, a running, a matching, and an exact ratio are all rewritings of "at this scale". The fifth hides the scale in an unstated premise, and it is the one a grep cannot find.

Six signatures, and a rule for using them. S1 alone is a hit; S5 with S2 is a hit; two of S2, S3, S4, S6 are a hit; S3 and S6 alone are not. A signature is evidence, and the verdict is the sentence.

Seven lookalikes, and the expensive one is $g_L=g_R$. The string is in seven physics articles and means a coupling in one. The discriminator is whether the symbol multiplies a generator or is multiplied by one.

The census. $598$ of $672$ articles are clean; $23$ carry two or more marks; $12$ carry three or more. Two of the twelve are the permitted claim articles, seven are data, two are pointers, and one is the closest call: $M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)M_{W_R}$.

Four repairs, and one rule about all of them. Classify, then repair: leave a structural statement alone, mark a measured one, point away from a scale-bearing one, and never delete the scale while keeping the sentence.

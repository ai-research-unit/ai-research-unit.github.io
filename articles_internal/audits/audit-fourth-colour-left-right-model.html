Migration: The Fourth-Colour Left–Right Model, and the Cleaning of Its Category

Status of this file. Half executed. The internal notes are done: the main note stands at 8 346 words from 14 749 with every equation kept, and four of the nine supporting notes were merged and deleted. The physics articles are not done: nineteen work items in eight batches remain, and §9 is the list.

What the file is for. It fixes the rules of one editing pass over the Biquaternion Particle Physics category, and it says what is left to do. The rules are checkable by a script, and §11 is that script.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. Delete a sentence only when its subject is the writing rather than the model. Everything else is dressed differently and kept.
  2. Every equation, every table that states a claim, and every step of every argument survives. A cleaning is not a rewrite.
  3. Bold is thinned, not removed, in a physics article; it is removed outright in an internal note. The two rules are different and the difference has already cost a rewrite.
  4. Recompute every number the pass touches, on at most 100 elements, and state the sample when the check is sampled.
  5. No new strength outside the one-coupling article. That rule survives the pass unchanged.

1. What This Pass Is

This note fixes the rules for one editing pass over the Biquaternion Particle Physics category, and it lists what is left to do. The pass removes from the physics articles everything that belongs to the workshop — migration bookkeeping, status labels, file names, revision history, self-criticism, editorial vocabulary — while keeping every equation, every number, every table that states a claim, and every step of every argument.

Two things make this pass different from an ordinary rewrite. The first is that it is mechanical: the end state is checkable by a script, and §Checks Before a Batch Is Closed is that script. The second is that it is not free: any number it touches must be recomputed before it stands, on a stated and small number of cases.

The pass is needed because the corpus grew through a long argument with itself, and it kept the record of that argument in the articles. The record was useful while the argument was open. It is now noise in a paper about physics.

2. The One Test

A sentence is deleted only when its subject is the writing rather than the model. Everything else is dressed differently and kept.

the sentence verdict why
"The strip is legitimate because the equality was conditional on parity." keep subject is the model
"This note did not use that reason until the file was recovered." delete subject is the note's drafting
"The data exclude the equality, so it is dropped." keep subject is the model
"The migration removes assertions; it adds none." delete subject is the editing pass
"At $10^{13}$ GeV the angle reaches $\tfrac38$ and $\alpha_3^{-1}$ is $13\%$ short." keep a number with its scale
"This row is flagged for recomputation." delete a status, not a result

The boundary case is a verdict column. A column reading confirmed, consistent, not contradicted states what the model says about a measurement, and it stays. A column reading keep, withdraw, relabel states what an editor should do, and it goes with the table it belongs to.

3. The Article Format

Every physics article keeps the same six parts, in this order:

  1. the title, in the corpus's # __Title__ form;
  2. an Introduction;
  3. the thematic sections, named after their physics;
  4. a Summary;
  5. a Summary of Notation;
  6. Further Reading.

The format is not a preference. Measured over the $672$ physics articles: $670$ have an Introduction, $672$ a Summary, $668$ a Summary of Notation, $671$ Further Reading. An article missing one of the last three is the exception, and a cleaning pass never removes one.

A cleaning pass may renumber sections. It may not merge two thematic sections, split one, or reorder them. The Introduction and the Summary are rewritten only where they carry the banned vocabulary of §The Banned Vocabulary; the physics statements inside them are left as they stand.

4. The Bold Rule, and Why the Target Is Not Zero

The rule has two halves, and they apply to different files.

For a physics article:

  • bold lead-ins stay, because the corpus uses them as section markers, and a reader navigating twenty sections of a long article needs them;
  • boxed or displayed results stay bold, for the same reason;
  • bold inside a running sentence is thinned to the corpus rate, which is a median of $4.1\%$ of words over $672$ files;
  • a bold phrase that only repeats the sentence's own claim loses the asterisks and keeps the words.

Lead-ins carry most of the existing bold, which is why the target is not zero. On a sample of $400$ physics articles, bold spans that open a paragraph hold $81\,661$ words and bold spans inside a sentence hold $41\,573$; two thirds of the bold is lead-in. Removing the second third brings an article of average composition to roughly the median, and removing the first would flatten the corpus's own structure.

For an internal note, the rule is different and simpler: no bold at all. The internal notes are working documents; their headings carry the structure, and bold inside them carries nothing.

This distinction was not clear in the first pass, and it cost a rewrite. The internal note on the fourth-colour model was cleaned to zero bold, which is right; the physics article beside it must not be cleaned to zero bold, which would be wrong.

5. The Banned Vocabulary

Five groups of words, measured over the $672$ physics articles:

group pattern hits files
editorial process the migration $25$ $20$
tier $10$ $9$
flagged $69$ $38$
the register $42$ $19$
boundary article $1$ $1$
revision history an earlier version, earlier draft, previous version $5$ $4$
too generous $1$ $1$
stance adverbs honest, honestly $248$ $177$
accounting metaphors the cost, the price $264$ $144$
claim blocks what is claimed $11$ $11$

None of these words is banned from the language. Each is banned from a physics article in its editorial sense. The tests are narrow:

  • cost is allowed when it means a physical quantity, as in the cost of a counterterm; it is not allowed when it means the price of giving up a prediction. The second use becomes the physics sentence it stands for: "the model loses its only sharp electroweak prediction" replaces "what it costs is its prediction".
  • honest is allowed in a quotation from another author; it is not allowed as the corpus's own assessment of its own sentence. "The honest form of the statement is" becomes the statement.
  • the register, the ledger, the boundary, the tier, flagged are never allowed, because the objects they name do not exist outside the editing pass.

The replacement rule is always the same: write the physics sentence the editorial sentence was standing in for. Not "remove the word", which leaves a hole, but "say the thing".

6. The Coupling Rule

The category carries exactly one article that may state a coupling value, a boundary condition, a running, a matching, or a ratio called exact: The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model. No article outside it introduces a new number of that kind.

The rule survives the pass unchanged, and the pass must not weaken it. Its practical consequence for editing is narrow and worth stating: when a rewrite removes the editorial sentence around a number, the number is either already in the one-coupling article, or it is data, or it must go. A number that appears in no other article and is not data has no home in the category.

7. The Numerical Rule

A number the pass touches is recomputed before it stands. The computation is bounded: at most $100$ elements, and the bound is deliberate.

"At most $100$ elements" means one of three things, whichever fits:

  • an exact evaluation, when the quantity is closed-form: evaluate the expression, do not cite it;
  • a scan of at most $100$ sampled cases, when the quantity is a property of a set, with the sample size and the seed stated in the article;
  • a numerical check against a limit that is known analytically, on at most $100$ points.

What is not allowed is a check that costs more than the result is worth. A running that needs a thousand points is not verified by a thousand points in an editing pass; it is either verified by an analytic limit or it is left as it stands, and then the article says which.

Two further constraints apply to every number that survives:

  • the number keeps its premise. A bound quoted as $10^{6}$ still says $b_{B-L}=12$ and one-loop wherever it said so before.
  • the number keeps its status. An input, a prediction, a bound and a fit are four different things, and the word that says which is part of the number.

8. The Two Blocks to Replace

Two shapes of prose are not editorial noise and not physics either. They are fossils of the argument, and each has a fixed replacement.

The first is the claim block. A table or a paragraph that says what is claimed and what is not claimed is replaced by the positive statement of the same content: what the model asserts, stated once, in the order the physics runs.

before after
"what is claimed: three couplings, independent; what is not claimed: the value of any coupling" "the three couplings are independent; the model predicts no value for them"
"what is not claimed: the multiplet content of the spin-0 sector" "the spin-0 sector's multiplet content is not fixed, so no mass relation is predicted"

The second is the price sentence. A sentence that weighs a model's losses as if the reader were buying something is replaced by the list of what the model does not do:

before after
"the cost is that it cannot be confirmed" "the model makes no prediction that could confirm it"
"that is a real price" "that leaves no testable consequence"
"honestly, the count of testable consequences is zero" "the count of testable consequences is zero"

The decontamination keeps the derivations and drops the verdicts.

11. The Data Boundary Decision

articles_physics/the-data-boundary-which-articles-may-print-a-measured-strength.md is $2\,092$ words and is, by its own description, a register: it carries no strength value and classifies the articles that do. A physics article may not be a register; that is the rule this whole pass exists to enforce.

The decision is therefore to retire it to articles_internal/ as a short appendix, cut to the part that is a rule rather than a list: the three categories, the test that separates them, and the one sentence that forbids the abuse. The list of sixteen articles it prints does not need to be public, because the rule makes the list recomputable — a reader who wants to know whether an article may print a strength applies the test to that article.

The alternative, keeping it as it stands, would leave the category with one article whose subject is the category's own housekeeping, and the next cleaning pass would have to clean it too. The appendix form costs about six hundred words and removes the recursion.

12. The Checks Before a Batch Is Closed

Run on every file in the batch, against the pre-edit copy.

  1. Every display equation of the original is present, matching modulo whitespace.
  2. The number of math delimiters is even, for display math and for inline math alike.
  3. Every table that stated a claim is still there, with its header and its row count; every lost row belonged to a table that was pure bookkeeping.
  4. Every table row has the same number of columns as its header.
  5. Every §x.y reference resolves to a subsection that exists in the file.
  6. The bold share is at or below $4.1\%$ for a physics article, and there is no bold marker at all in an internal note.
  7. The article has its Introduction, its Summary, its Summary of Notation and its Further Reading.
  8. grep for the five banned groups returns nothing except the allowed physical senses.
  9. Every number the batch touched was recomputed on at most $100$ elements, and the article states the sample size when the check was sampled.
  10. No new number was introduced outside the one-coupling article.
  11. The build runs without error and the page renders.
  12. The menu comment for the article is one line, and it says what the article now says.

A batch that fails any of the twelve is not reported as done. It is repaired, or the failure is reported with the repair proposed.

13. The Report

Each batch closes with a table and nothing else: words before and after, bold before and after, equations, tables, rows, and the list of sections removed or renumbered. Numbers, not adjectives. A sentence of prose is allowed for a defect the numbers cannot show, such as an equation that was wrong before the pass and is left as it was found.

Summary

The notes are cleaned, the physics is not, and the rules are five. The pass removes the workshop from the physics and keeps the physics intact.

Its end state is checkable: equations preserved, tables preserved, bold at the corpus median for a physics article and absent from an internal note, no editorial vocabulary, no revision history, no claim blocks, no price sentences.

The rules reduce to five:

  1. Say the physics sentence the editorial sentence was standing in for.
  2. Do not re-typeset what you did not recompute.
  3. Keep the lead-in and thin the middle.
  4. Keep one coupling article, and let no other article carry a new strength.
  5. Recompute on at most a hundred elements, and state the sample when the check is sampled.

The work list is nineteen items in eight batches. The first batch is the four heaviest articles of the category, at $24.2\%$ bold against a median of $4.1\%$. Each batch reports and stops.

Audit: Unit Declarations, and the Tool That Finds Them

Status of this file. A tool and its audit, frozen here as one document. The listing of §11 is the whole program, verbatim, and the runnable copy sits at _reserve/py/detect-unit-declarations.py (the docstring's older path, _reserve/detect-unit-declarations, was never created). The self-test prints 21 / 21 cases, and that is the check that accepts the copy.

What the file is for. It finds the chains of physical constants set to one — $\hbar=1$, $c=\hbar=m=1$, $G=c=\hbar=k_B=1$ — reports each with its file and line, and offers a repair that cannot write the defect back.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. A declaration is a chain, and it is found by splitting the span at =. Detection is exact and cheap; nothing is inferred from a glyph.
  2. A declaration is not repaired by substitution. It is repaired by arithmetic on the displays that follow, so --apply rewrites only a span whose whole content is the chain, and only into a unit statement.
  3. A chain inside a larger expression is refused, and the refusal is printed. The tool does not guess the surrounding arithmetic.
  4. A quoted form is not a violation. The conventions article is made of quotations of the prohibited form, and the window test of §8 keeps them out of the report.
  5. The exit code is the gate. 1 when a site is found, 0 when there is none.

1. What a Unit Declaration Is

A unit declaration is a chain of physical constants set to one:

$$ \hbar=1 \qquad \hbar=k_B=1 \qquad \hbar=c=k_B=1 $$ $$ c=\hbar=m=1 \qquad m=\hbar=\omega=1 \qquad G=c=\hbar=k_B=1 \qquad \hbar=m=k_t'=1 $$

Each is the same statement as $h=2\pi$, and the corpus conventions forbid it. The declaration hides the conversion inside a chain of equality signs, so a blind glyph substitution turns \hbar into \frac{h}{2\pi}=1, which is the forbidden form written out. The tool exists to catch the shape before and after a migration.

2. Why a Substitution Cannot Repair It

The defect is not a spelling. $\hbar=1$ is a physical statement — the action is measured in units of $\hbar$ — and the repair is a choice of scale written into every display that used it, in units the corpus allows: $mc$, $mc^2$, $1/k_m$, $\hbar\omega$, $k_BT$, $\hbar/(2m\omega)$. A machine that substituted would produce $\frac{h}{2\pi}=1$, which sets a constant to one under a longer name.

So --apply does one narrow thing: it rewrites a span whose whole content is the chain into the equivalent unit statement, $\hbar$ taken as the unit, which never sets a constant to one and never states a false equation. The arithmetic that used the declaration is then restored by hand, and the tool says so when it has patched.

The four hard atoms stand alone. hbar, h, kB and mu0 have no second reading in the corpus, so setting one of them to one is a declaration by itself.

The fifteen soft atoms are also values, labels or indices somewhere. c is a constant and also a coefficient; m is a mass and also a label; \mu is a constant, an index and a measure. Each is flagged only beside another constant, unless --strict finds it in a sentence about units.

4. The Aliases

Nineteen names, but more spellings, and every spelling has to canonicalise to one name:

  • \hbar, \hslash and the bare glyph ℏ are all hbar.
  • k_B and K_B are kB; \epsilon_0 and \varepsilon_0 are eps0; \omega and \Omega are omega.
  • Face modifiers are stripped before the lookup: \left, \right, \big, \Big, \bigg, \Bigg, their l and r forms, and \displaystyle.
  • Font commands are stripped too: \mathrm, \text, \rm, \mathit, \mathsf, \mathcal.
  • Spacing is stripped: \,, \;, \!, \:, \quad, \qquad and \.
  • Braces, spaces and line breaks go, and a leading _ or ^ goes with them.

5. Detection: Split at the Equals Sign

The span is cut at every = into pieces, and a run is the pieces just before a bare 1. A run is kept when it has at least two members and at least one of them is a known atom.

atomish decides how far back the walk may go: a piece of thirty characters or fewer that canonicalises to an atom, or whose part after a | or & does, can be crossed. A piece containing a backslash other than an atom stops the walk (\frac{h}{2\pi} is not a chain member), and so does a piece containing an operator.

A raw glyph outside math is always a finding. The bare ℏ is scanned in the text as well as in the spans, because a copy that lost its delimiters is exactly the case a migration leaves behind.

6. The Policy: When a Run Is a Declaration

A run is a declaration when any of three conditions holds:

  1. it contains a hard atom;
  2. it contains two or more known atoms;
  3. it contains one known atom and --strict is set and the surrounding sentence says units, setting, set, conventions, adopt, choose, prints, printed or source, within 120 characters before and 80 after.

Nothing else is reported. $\kappa=1$, $m=1$, $\mu=1$, $c^2=1$, $P_{\mathrm{id}}=1$, $n=1$ and $x=y=1$ stay silent, and the self-test holds each of them to that.

7. Patching: Whole-Span Only

--apply rewrites a site only when the span, stripped, is exactly the chain and every member is a known atom or a simple symbol — a piece with no backslash, no bar and no ampersand, such as k_t', E or r.

The rewrite is a sentence, not a formula:

  • one member: $\hbar$ taken as the unit;
  • two members: $\hbar$ and $k_B$ taken as the units;
  • more: $\hbar$, $c$ and $k_B$ taken as the units.

A bar, a prefix or a fraction means refusal. \Big|_{\hbar=c=k_B=1} is a site to look at, not a site to rewrite: the restriction carries meaning the tool cannot see. Refused sites are printed with file and line, and the count of them is printed beside the count of patches.

8. The Quotation Rule

An article that states the prohibition quotes the prohibited form, and the conventions article is made of such quotations. So a hit is dropped from the report when the text within 260 characters before and 140 after it carries one of: not allowed, is a defect, defect in, forbidden, never, may not, prohibit…, violation, banned, the shortcut, must not, illegal.

The window is deliberately wide: a table row that quotes the forbidden shape usually carries its verdict in the row above or in the sentence that introduces the table.

9. The Command Line, and the Exit Code as a Gate

python3 _reserve/py/detect-unit-declarations.py articles_physics
python3 _reserve/py/detect-unit-declarations.py articles_physics --strict
python3 _reserve/py/detect-unit-declarations.py articles_physics --apply
python3 _reserve/py/detect-unit-declarations.py --self-test

--json prints one object: sites, patched, refused, and the per-site report. The default run prints the sites as file:line and the chain as written.

The exit status is 1 when a site is found and 0 when there is none, so the default run is usable as a gate in a hook or a CI step. --self-test returns its own status: 0 when all cases pass.

The eleven that patch are \hbar=1, \hbar=m=1, \hbar=k_B=1, \hbar=c=k_B=1, c=\hbar=m=1, m=\hbar=\omega=1, G=c=\hbar=k_B=1, \hbar=m=k_t'=1, m=c=1, k_B=1 and \hbar=m=c=1. The nine silent ones are \kappa=1, m=1, \mu=1, c^2=1, \sigma^2=\hbar/(2m\omega), \frac{h}{2\pi}=m, P_{\mathrm{id}}=1, n=1 and x=y=1.

The last two rows are the ones that matter: \sigma^2=\hbar/(2m\omega) and \frac{h}{2\pi}=m are constants in a formula, and a detector keyed on the glyph rather than on the chain would flag both.

A *unit declaration* is a chain of physical constants set to one:

    $\\hbar=1$          $\\hbar=k_B=1$        $\\hbar=c=k_B=1$
    $c=\\hbar=m=1$      $m=\\hbar=\\omega=1$   $G=c=\\hbar=k_B=1$
    $\\hbar=m=k_t'=1$

Each of these is the same statement as $h=2\\pi$, and the corpus conventions
forbid it.  The declaration hides the conversion inside a chain of equality
signs, so a blind glyph substitution turns it into `\\frac{h}{2\\pi}=1`, which
is the forbidden form written out.  This tool exists to catch the shape before
and after a migration.

    python3 _reserve/detect-unit-declarations articles_physics
    python3 _reserve/detect-unit-declarations articles_physics --strict
    python3 _reserve/detect-unit-declarations articles_physics --apply
    python3 _reserve/detect-unit-declarations --self-test

Detection is exact and cheap: every math span is split at `=`, and a run of
constant atoms that ends in a bare `1` is reported with its file, line and the
chain as written.

Patching is deliberately narrow, and this is the point of the tool.  A
declaration cannot be repaired by substitution: the repair is arithmetic on
the displays that follow, and a machine that substituted would write the
defect back in the form the migration exists to remove.  So `--apply` rewrites
only a span whose whole content is the chain, and rewrites it into the
equivalent unit statement -- `$\\hbar$ taken as the unit` -- which never sets a
constant to one and never states a false equation.  A chain embedded in a
larger expression is reported as *refused*: it needs the surrounding
arithmetic restored by hand, and the tool says so rather than guessing.

Exit status is 1 when a site is found and 0 when there is none, so the default
run is usable as a gate in a hook or a CI step.

STRIP = re.compile(r'\\(?:left|right|bigl|bigr|Bigl|Bigr|big|Big|bigg|Bigg'
                   r'|displaystyle)(?![A-Za-z])')
FONT = re.compile(r'\\(?:mathrm|text|rm|mathit|mathsf|mathcal)(?![A-Za-z])')
SPACE = re.compile(r'\\[,;!:]|\\q?quad|\\ ')


def canon(atom):
    """Canonical constant name for one side of an `=`, or None."""
    s = atom.replace('\u210f', '\\hbar')
    s = STRIP.sub('', s)
    s = FONT.sub('', s)
    s = SPACE.sub('', s)
    s = s.replace('{', '').replace('}', '').replace(' ', '').replace('\n', '')
    s = s.lstrip('_^')
    if s in ('1', '+1'):
        return ONE
    return NORMAL.get(s)


def atomish(atom):
    """True when the piece could be one member of a chain (so a walk may cross it)."""
    t = atom.strip()
    if not t or len(t) > 30:
        return False
    if canon(t) is not None:
        return True
    # a piece like \Big|_{\hbar}: the chain starts after the bar
    if canon(re.split(r'[|&]', t)[-1]) is not None:
        return True
    if '\\' in t:                        # \frac{h}{2\pi} and the like
        return False
    if re.search(r'[+\-*/<>]', t.replace('^', '')):
        return False
    return bool(re.fullmatch(r"[A-Za-z0-9\\{}_,'^.|\\ ]+", t))

Summary

One chain, one split at the equals sign, one exit code. A unit declaration is a chain of physical constants set to one; the tool finds every chain, names its file and line, and refuses the repairs it cannot justify.

  • Detection splits the math span at = and reads the atoms: nineteen of them, four hard and the rest soft, with the aliases of §4 folded in.
  • The repair is arithmetic on the displays that follow, never a substitution. --apply rewrites a span only when the whole span is the chain, and only into a unit statement; a chain inside a larger expression is refused and the refusal is printed.
  • Quotation is not violation: a hit is dropped when the surrounding 260 characters before and 140 after carry one of the verdict words, which is what keeps the conventions article out of its own report.
  • The exit code is the gate: 1 when a site is found, 0 when there is none, so the default run drops into a hook or a CI step unchanged.

Audit: The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Instanton — Two Factors That Are Wrong and One That Is Not

Status of this file. A review, not an edit. No article was modified. Every number below was recomputed from the article's own inputs, $g_4^{2}(M_X)=0.730$, $\alpha_4(M_X)=0.0581$ and $\alpha_4(M_C)=1/17.31$.

What the file is for. It records what the posted review of articles_physics/the-su4-instanton-a-matrix-transcription-outside-the-algebra.md got right, what it got wrong, and what it missed. The review recommends one "must fix" and gets the physics of that fix wrong; it endorses the one paragraph that carries an error; and its one proposed derivation is arithmetically off by three.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. The review's "must fix" would entrench an error. The article says $\theta_4$ is physical because the content is anomaly-free. It is not physical: the content is massless and the axial rotation removes it. The review repeats the article's claim in clearer words, which makes it worse.
  2. The review praises the one paragraph that is wrong. The $\theta$ term's coefficient is off by a factor of two, and the article's two integrality displays cannot both hold — they differ by $g^{2}/2$, not by $2$.
  3. The review's proposed derivation of $4N-5$ gives $8$, not $11$. The number $11$ in the article is right; the derivation offered for it is wrong, and so is the article's own sentence justifying it.
  4. One error the review missed and one it caught partially. The standard embedding is not the maximal one; and $S(M_C)$ is $108.7$ or $108.8$, not $108.6$.
  5. Nothing is applied without the author's decision. §8 lists the four decisions.

Two of these are substantive and both sit in the $\theta_4$ material, which is exactly where the review put its attention and where it got the physics backwards.

2. Finding One: $\theta_4$ Is Removable, Not Physical

The article's chain. "A rotation of the fermions by a chiral phase shifts $\theta_4$ by an amount fixed by the anomaly coefficient, and the corpus's sixteen are paired so that the anomaly cancels — which is why the shift is zero and $\theta_4$ is physical rather than removable."

The review's replacement. "The content is vector-like, so the $\mathrm{SU}(4)^{3}$ anomaly vanishes identically; a chiral content would have let $\theta_4$ be rotated away."

Both use the same premise, and it is false: vector-like content does not make the $\theta$-shift vanish.

2.1 Two different anomalies, and the conflation

The one that cancels is the triangle with three gauge currents. For the content $2\cdot\mathbf4\oplus2\cdot\bar{\mathbf4}$,

$$ A(\mathbf4)+A(\bar{\mathbf4})=0 \quad\text{pairwise, so}\quad 2A(\mathbf4)-2A(\mathbf4)=0 . $$

That is what makes the theory consistent, and it is what the companion article means by "the anomaly cancels by counting".

The one that shifts $\theta$ is the mixed axial–gauge anomaly, the divergence of the axial current:

$$ \partial_{\imath}j^{5}_{\imath}=\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(F\tilde F\bigr)\sum_{f}2T(R_{f}) \quad\text{schematically}, $$

and every term in that sum is positive. The two chiralities of a Dirac field contribute with the same sign here, because the axial rotation acts with opposite signs on them and the Dynkin index does not. For this content the coefficient is $16\times2\times\tfrac12$, not zero.

So the two statements are not the same statement. Gauge anomaly zero says the theory is consistent. $\theta$-shift nonzero says a chiral rotation moves $\theta$. The article needs the second to be zero and it is not.

2.2 What follows, and it is the opposite of what the article says

With all eight Dirac fields massless — which the companion article establishes, and states as a choice rather than a theorem — the rotation costs nothing. Nothing else in the Lagrangian changes: no Yukawa, since $\langle\Xi\rangle=0$; no mass term, since $m=0$ is chosen. So $\theta_4$ is set to zero by a field redefinition and is not a parameter of the phase. Its effects are not suppressed by $10^{-47}$; they are absent.

The invariant is $\bar\theta_4=\theta_4+\arg\det m$, and the model sets $m=0$. That is the sharp form: $\theta_4$ alone was never the physical parameter, the combination was, and with the model's choice the combination is removable.

This has three consequences, and all three simplify the article.

  • The bullet "it is free" becomes "it is free in the Lagrangian and redundant in the phase".
  • The bullet "it is unobservable" becomes exact instead of an estimate at $10^{-47}$.
  • The bullet "there is no axion" becomes trivially satisfied: there is nothing to solve, so the absence of a Peccei–Quinn field is not a choice with a price.

It also answers the article's own "what would be wrong" test. The $\theta_4$ term is a total derivative, so it cannot affect the equations of motion; with massless fermions it cannot affect the physics either. That is a stronger statement than "the phase is not ours", and it is the one the corpus can defend.

2.3 What the correction does not disturb

The instanton itself is untouched. The action, the suppression, the moduli, the cutoff and the transcription argument all survive: they never used $\theta_4$. The $\theta$ term is one paragraph of the article, not its spine.

3. Finding Two: the $\theta$ Term Is Off by a Factor of Two

The article writes two integrality statements and calls them equivalent.

$$ \frac{g_4^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\int\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathbb{F}}\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z} \quad\text{"equivalently"}\quad \frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int\operatorname{tr}\bigl(F_{\imath\jmath}\tilde F_{\imath\jmath}\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}. $$

They are not equivalent, and neither holds. With $\operatorname{tr}(T_{a}T_{b})=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$ the trace is half the sum, and the BPST configuration has

$$ \int F_{a}\tilde F_{a}\,d^{4}x=\frac{32\pi^{2}}{g^{2}}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \int\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathbb{F}}\bigr)=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{g^{2}} . $$

expression value for one BPST instanton
$\dfrac{g^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\displaystyle\int\operatorname{tr}$ $\tfrac12$
$\dfrac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\displaystyle\int\operatorname{tr}$ $\dfrac{1}{g^{2}}=1.37$
$\dfrac{g^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\displaystyle\int\operatorname{tr}$ $1$ ✓
$\dfrac{g^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\displaystyle\int F_{a}\tilde F_{a}$ $1$ ✓

So the correct pair is $\dfrac{g^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\displaystyle\int\operatorname{tr}$ and $\dfrac{g^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\displaystyle\int F_{a}\tilde F_{a}$, and the factor of two between them is the trace's — which is what the sentence "the factor of two is the trace's, not the group's" means to say. The article keeps the trace in both displays and drops the $g^{2}$ from the second, which is the one combination that fails.

The corpus already has the right form. The EDM article writes

$$ \theta\,\frac{g_s^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\,G_{a\imath\jmath}\tilde{G}_{a\imath\jmath}, $$

a sum over generators, no trace — correct. The instanton article is the deviation.

3.1 The periodicity is the casualty

With the article's coefficient the weight of one instanton is $e^{i\theta/2}$, whose period is $4\pi$. With the corrected coefficient it is $e^{i\theta}$, period $2\pi$. So the article's stated period is right and its stated coefficient is wrong, and the two cannot both be kept. The fix is one symbol in two places: $32\pi^{2}\to16\pi^{2}$ in the $\theta$ term and in the integrality display.

Everything numerical is unaffected. $S=8\pi^{2}/g^{2}$ does not use the $\theta$ normalisation, so $108.2$, $1.1\times10^{-47}$ and the whole moduli discussion stand.

4. Finding Three: the Review's Derivation of $4N-5$ Gives $8$

The article says "eleven parameters are left after the stabiliser is quotiented out. The arithmetic: $4N-5=11$."

The review offers the derivation: the stabiliser is $\mathrm{SU}(N-2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ of dimension $4N-9$, "leaving $4N-5$". That is not arithmetic, it is two different numbers.

$$ \dim\mathrm{SU}(4)=15,\qquad \dim\bigl[\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\bigr]=3+3+1=7, \qquad 15-7=8 . $$

The review's own displayed formula, $(N^{2}-1)-[(N-2)^{2}-1+3+1]$, evaluates to $8$ at $N=4$. It then reports the result as $11$. Applying the review's fix would replace a right number with a wrong derivation of it.

And the article's sentence has the same defect. Quotienting by the stabiliser of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ embedding gives $4N-8=8$, not $4N-5=11$. So "$4N-5$ after the stabiliser is quotiented out" cannot be a plain coset statement in either version.

What is true, and what the article now says. $4N$ is the collective-coordinate count (equivalently the ADHM count $4Nk$ at $k=1$), and $5$ of them are the four translations and the one dilatation. The other eleven split in two: the embedding orbit $\mathrm{SU}(4)/[\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)]$ has dimension $15-7=8$, and the three rotations of the instanton inside its own $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ complete it. $8+3=11$, and the check that it is right is that it reproduces $4N-5$ at $N=2$ ($0+3=3$) and at $N=3$ ($4+3=7$) as well as at $N=4$.

So both the article's sentence and the review's derivation are wrong, and the number in the table is right. The review's instinct — "the reader cannot see where the $5$ comes from" — is sound; its remedy is not.

5. Finding Four: "the Maximal Embedding" Is the Wrong Embedding

The article says "$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ has more than one conjugacy class of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ subgroups ... the standard one is the maximal embedding, and the others are not computed here."

The standard embedding is the block one, $4=2\oplus1\oplus1$, and it is the most reducible, not the maximal. The decompositions of the $\mathbf4$ under an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ are

$$ \mathbf4=\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf1,\qquad \mathbf4=\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1,\qquad \mathbf4=\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf2,\qquad \mathbf4=\mathbf4 . $$

The last is the principal (irreducible) embedding, and it is the one the word "maximal" describes. The BPST solution uses the first. So the article's sentence names the wrong class, and its moduli table is the block-embedding table.

The review's physics point here is right and worth keeping: the action is embedding-independent at unit winding — it is $8\pi^{2}|n|/g^{2}$ and knows nothing but $n$ — while the moduli count and the fluctuation spectrum are not. The article's "the article's claim does not depend on which is chosen" should become "the action and the topological charge do not depend on which is chosen; the table above is the block embedding's".

6. Finding Five: $S(M_C)$

The article quotes $S(M_C)=2\pi/0.0578=108.6$. Two problems, and the review found the first.

  • $2\pi/0.0578=108.71$, so the entry should be $108.7$, not $108.6$. The article's "half a unit larger" is then $0.55$, which the sentence's own arithmetic supports.
  • With the corrected running the coupling is $\alpha_4(M_C)=1/17.31=0.05776$, giving $S(M_C)=108.78$. So the value is $108.8$ and the difference is $0.62$ — "six tenths larger", not "half".

Both fixes are one digit each. The conclusion — the suppression is the same to two significant figures across the phase — is unaffected.

8. Suggested Strategy

One finding of physics, one of normalisation, three of arithmetic, and one of terminology. Nothing applied.

The order.

  1. §$\theta_4$ and the Questions It Raises. Replace the anomaly paragraph with the two-anomaly statement of §2.1, and the "physical rather than removable" conclusion with "redundant above $M_C$ because the fermions are massless, with $\bar\theta_4=\theta_4+\arg\det m$ and $m=0$".
  2. §The Topological Term and §The Checks. $g^{2}/32\pi^{2}\operatorname{tr}\to g^{2}/16\pi^{2}\operatorname{tr}$; the second display becomes $g^{2}/32\pi^{2}\int F_{a}\tilde F_{a}$; the checks row corrected.
  3. The introduction, the box and the summary. "a free parameter of the model" → "a parameter the phase renders redundant"; the summary's "physical because the content is anomaly-free" → the corrected statement.
  4. §The Moduli. $4N-5$ kept, the justification corrected per §4; "maximal embedding" → the block embedding $4=2\oplus1\oplus1$, with the scope clause of §5.
  5. The three minor numbers. $S(M_C)=108.8$ and "six tenths"; "the largest dimensionless number in the coupling block"; $e^{2\pi in}=1$.
  6. §The Action and Its Size. The gauge-choice qualifier on "three of the fifteen".

The decisions.

  • D1. The $\theta_4$ status. Accept that $\theta_4$ is redundant above $M_C$, or keep "physical" and defend it. The audit recommends acceptance: the content is massless by the model's own choice, the axial anomaly is nonzero, and the corpus's instanton article already lists "its relation to the $\theta$-angle" as untreated. This is the one change that alters the article's thesis, so it is the author's call and not a proofreading fix.
  • D2. The $\theta$ normalisation. Correct to $g^{2}/16\pi^{2}\operatorname{tr}$, or keep the displays and drop the $2\pi$ period. The audit recommends the correction: it restores the period the article already claims and matches the EDM article's convention.
  • D3. The $4N-5$ justification. Replace with the "standard count, five removed" form, or leave the sentence. The audit recommends replacing, because the current sentence is the only unsupported derivation in an article that is otherwise careful about what it derives.
  • D4. The minor items. Apply or defer.

What this audit does not do. It does not compute the fluctuation spectrum, does not settle which embedding the model should use, and does not prove that the block embedding is the one the corpus intends. It also does not claim that the correction to $\theta_4$ changes any number: the instanton's action, suppression and moduli are independent of it.

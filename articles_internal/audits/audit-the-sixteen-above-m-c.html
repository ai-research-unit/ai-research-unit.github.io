Audit: The Sixteen Above $M_C$

Verdict: one real numerical error, one real physics gap, one false positive, and one item where the review's arithmetic is wrong. The structural claims all survive.

1. Finding One: $g_*$ Was Double-Counted

The article said $g_*=\underbrace{30}_{\text{the fifteen}}\times2+\ldots=60+28=88$.

The label and the number disagree. Fifteen gauge fields with two polarisations each are thirty states, and thirty states contribute thirty to $g_*$ — the definition is $g_*=\sum_B g_i+\tfrac78\sum_F g_i$ with $g_i$ already counting helicities. Writing $30\times2$ counts the polarisations twice.

$$ 15\times2+32\times\tfrac78=30+28=58 . $$

The cross-check that settles it: the Standard Model gives $28+90\times\tfrac78=106.75$, with the ninety being 45 Weyl fields at two states each — the same convention in which sixteen Weyl fields are thirty-two states, which the article's own table uses. So the article's $32$ is the helicity count and its $30$ must be one too.

Fixed in three places: the display, the checks row, and the notation row. The review found this and then contradicted itself — its Strengths item 5 calls $60+28=88$ correct, and its issue 6 calls it wrong. The issue is right.

2. Finding Two: $\theta_4$ Was Missing From the $CP$ Argument

The article argued "no $CP$ violation above $M_C$" from $C$ and $P$ separately holding in the gauge sector.

The inference does not cover the one term that is $CP$-odd. $\theta_4\operatorname{tr}(F\tilde F)$ is $P$-odd and $C$-even, so $C\cdot P$ does not remove it: a product argument only kills what is odd under exactly one of the two.

What does remove it is the article's own first claim. The fermions are massless above $M_C$, so the axial rotation that shifts $\theta_4$ costs nothing and the angle is removable. The article's own premise therefore upgrades its conclusion, and the missing sentence was the bridge.

The review reached the right gap and the wrong fix. It proposed "no perturbative $CP$ violation; $\theta_4$ effects are $e^{-S}\sim10^{-47}$". That is the old instanton article's position, and the instanton article now says the opposite: the angle is redundant, not suppressed, so there is no $\theta_4$ dependence to weight. A caveat at $10^{-47}$ would have been weaker than the truth.

Added as a sixth bullet, with the sign of the argument — $P$-odd, $C$-even — and a pointer to the instanton article.

3. Finding Three: The Mass Matrix Count

The article said "the group does not forbid it; the model sets $m=0$".

True, and the count of tunings was missing. $m_{ij}$ has four complex entries; the copy index is ungauged, so rotations among the two $\mathbf4$ copies against the two $\bar{\mathbf4}$ copies are symmetries, and the invariants of $m$ are its two singular values. So $m=0$ is two conditions, not one.

The review says four real tunings, or two complex. Both are wrong: $2\times2$ complex is four complex entries, and the physical count is two, not four, because of the field redefinitions. The review's sentence would have introduced a new error while fixing a real gap.

Written as: four complex entries, two singular values, two masses set to zero.

4. Finding Four: The $C$ Bullet Is Correct

The review calls "the content is closed under $C$ as a permutation of the field set — $C$ carries $2\cdot\mathbf4$ to $2\cdot\bar{\mathbf4}$" a category error.

It is the standard statement and it is the corpus's own language. Classification of the Fermions "writes the conjugation as a permutation"; for a vector-like content, $C$ maps a field in $\mathbf4$ to a field in $\bar{\mathbf4}$ and the conjugate fields are already in the list, which is precisely what vector-like means. The review's own replacement says the same thing in different words — "the conjugate of a $\mathbf4$ is a $\bar{\mathbf4}$, which is already in the content" — and then calls the original misleading.

Rejected. The one clause added is the reason the map is available: the $\bar{\mathbf4}$ fields are already there.

Audit: Does Each Category Have Its Own Lagrangian?

Status of this file. A review, not an edit of any article, menu, plan or build file: only this audit is changed. The earlier long form is kept at articles_internal/archives/audit-lagrangian-existence-archive.md.

1. The question, and the only two answers

The question is: in this category, at this spin, does a genuine action exist in the theory? One question. Whether the corpus has printed it, which article holds it, which menu heading it sits under — none of that is part of the question, and none of it appears in the ledger below.

answer meaning
yes there is a field of this spin in this category, and a functional on it whose variation is that field's equation
no there is not, and a reason is named

The word "spin" in this file means one of the corpus's three carriers. Spin $0$ is the line $\mathbb{R}e_t$, spin $\tfrac12$ the plane $\Sigma(\vec u)$, spin $1$ the vector part $\mathfrak{k}$ — dimensions $1$, $2$, $3$. It does not mean "any field of that Lorentz spin". A colour-triplet scalar is an ordinary field with a perfectly good Lagrangian; it is simply not one of these three carriers, so it is not a line of this ledger. The same holds above spin $1$: the Rarita–Schwinger field of spin $\tfrac32$ and the Fronsdal and Vasiliev fields of higher spin are ordinary fields with functionals of their own, and none of them is a carrier in this algebra's roster — their absence here is a fact about the roster, not a claim that they are impossible. Every row below is read within that scheme, and a "no" row is a statement about the scheme and never about field theory at large.

The answer. Every sector has a Lagrangian at every spin that has a carrier, and the Action category supplies the generic density they all specialise. Thirty-seven lines, thirty-four occupied, three empty — and all three empty lines fail for a reason outside the category: one has no carrier in the roster, one has no density written in the algebra's own elements, and one has no commuting classical limit. No category is without a Lagrangian.

One correction, recorded because it is the error this audit exists to prevent. An earlier version of this file marked Electromagnetism empty at all three spins, on the ground that no article under that heading is called a Lagrangian. That is wrong. Maxwell's functional is $S=\int\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde F\tilde F)$, its variation is $\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde F=-Z\tilde J_\Phi$, and the category has a spin-$\tfrac12$ and a spin-$0$ action as well. Filing was mistaken for existence. A category's menu cannot tell you what exists in its physics.

A second correction, and a worse one, because it asserted a falsehood about physics. An earlier version of §5.1 said no scalar couples to a gluon, and the ledger called the strong spin-$0$ exclusion a fact about $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. Both are wrong. The Faddeev–Popov ghost $c_a$ in the QCD display is a spin-$0$ colour octet and it couples to the gluon through $\mathcal{L}_{cG}$ — it is unphysical, but it is there. The axion line couples a colour-singlet scalar to gluons through $G\tilde G$. And a coloured scalar is a perfectly ordinary field — a triplet or octet scalar, a squark, a GUT scalar — coupling through the covariant derivative. What is true is much narrower: in this corpus's carrier scheme the spin-$0$ carrier is the one-dimensional line, whose $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ representation is the singlet, so no fundamental coloured scalar is a carrier here. Colour and spin are independent, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ does not have Lagrangians classified by spin.

2. What a line is

A line is a spin in a category. Where a category has one functional carrying several spins, the action appears on several lines and its name repeats; where several functionals share one spin in one category, they share the line.

A line is not a separate theory, and the ledger must not be read as one. Several lines can be terms of one Lagrangian: QCD occupies the strong spin-$1$ line and the strong spin-$\tfrac12$ line because the ledger's rows are spins, not actions. Nothing in a row asserts that the corresponding term could stand alone — and in QCD it cannot, since the covariant derivative carries the gluon and the gluon's kinetic term is what makes it dynamical.

Two functionals are different actions when they differ in the fields, the terms, or the order of the equation. The polar substitution, momentum space, the frequency domain and the fluid variables change the chart and not the action; adding a connection, adding a term, and a limit that drops a term each produce a new one.

2.1 Lagrangian and action are one formula, used two ways

A Lagrangian is a density; an action is that same density added up. The Lagrangian is a function of the fields and their derivatives at one point of space-time. The action is the number you get by integrating it over a region:

$$ S[\phi]=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\;\mathcal{L}\bigl(\phi,\partial\phi\bigr) $$

Three things are done with the one expression, and each has a name.

what you do what you call the result
write it down at a point the Lagrangian, $\mathcal{L}$
integrate it over a region the action, $S$
vary the action and set $\delta S=0$ the equation of motion

On the Proca line this is three lines of arithmetic, not three theories. $\mathcal{L}=\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde F\tilde F)-\tfrac12k_m^{2}\langle\tilde A_\Phi\rangle^{\natural}$ is the Lagrangian. $S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}r\,\mathcal{L}$ is the action of the same field. $\delta S/\delta\tilde A_\Phi=0$ is the Proca equation. Nothing was added between the three steps, and nothing was chosen.

So "does a Lagrangian exist here" and "does an action exist here" is one question asked twice. Every density in this corpus is local: it depends on the fields and finitely many derivatives at a point. A local density can always be integrated, and a functional that is an integral of a local density always hands its integrand back. The two are one piece of information, so the ledger's exists column answers both at once; the Lagrangian (density) column is the first of the two, written at a point, and the action is its integral.

The one relation that is genuinely a relation, and can fail, is the next one. From the Lagrangian you may ask for the canonical momenta and a Hamiltonian, and that step can fail while the Lagrangian is perfectly good: Maxwell's Hessian is degenerate, so the Maxwell line has a Lagrangian and no Hamiltonian, and pays for it with a constraint and the Lorenz condition. Lagrangian without Hamiltonian is ordinary; Hamiltonian without Lagrangian is not. Nothing in this ledger is of the second kind, because a Hamiltonian presupposes a Lagrangian to be built from.

Where an action can exist with no Lagrangian at all — not a case here. A functional that is not the integral of any local density has no integrand to point at: a one-loop effective action carrying $\operatorname{Tr}\log$ of an operator, Hamilton's principal function, an action defined only as a lattice limit. Those are actions without Lagrangians. No line of this ledger is of that kind, so for this corpus the two words are interchangeable and only one question needs answering.

And one case where both fail together. Helmholtz's condition is asked of the equation, and it decides whether any density exists that has that equation as its variation (physics.md:487). A non-variational equation therefore has neither a Lagrangian nor an action. That is the viscous line, and it is the only entry in the ledger where the failure is of this kind.

3. The ledger, one line per spin

Ordered as the corpus orders its categories. The category is on every line, so each line stands alone. The group column names the gauged group, the Lagrangian (density) column writes the density, or the several densities, on which the line's verdict rests. The last column states, for an occupied line, what is not the case — the term the spin does not admit — and for an empty line, what is not the case and why.

The first column names the group whose connection the line's density carries, and the four force categories give one nested chain. The four groups are the corpus's own symmetry-group articles, quoted there with their generator counts and ranks:

The chain is $\mathrm{SU}(1)\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)\subset\mathrm{SU}(4)$, counted $0,3,8,15$, and the electroweak rung sits off it at four. The extra generator is the hypercharge factor, which is an index on the condensate rather than a member of the chain: the strong rung is the stabiliser of the line, and the weak factors arrive as the index that breaks the rung above rather than as debris of it.

The electroweak entry is the one to read with care, because two different groups share one algebra. The Standard Model's group is the product $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ — four generators, rank two, and the ledger writes this one, because the electroweak lines are the Standard Model's. The algebra's own compact group is $\mathrm{U}(2)=(\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$, of the same algebra and the same dimension and a different group: the quotient identifies the pair $(-1,-1)$ with the identity and the product does not (articles_physics/the-electroweak-pair-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-quotient-and-the-parity-lock.md:35). Both statements are needed and neither replaces the other. After the breaking the unbroken subgroup is $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$, one generator and rank one, which is the electromagnetism entry below; the corpus writes it as an intersection of three stabilisers (articles_physics/the-photon-as-the-unbroken-mixture.md:42).

Three $\mathrm{U}(1)$s are in play and they are not the same group, which is the one place this column can be misread:

the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ what it is where it appears
the centre the algebra's central phase, $\tilde\Xi\mapsto e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}}\tilde\Xi$; the structural reason a charge can be defined at all every coupled line, and the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ inside $\mathrm{U}(2)$ on the electroweak rung
$\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ hypercharge, the abelian factor of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ the electroweak and Standard Model rows only

The middle two are different subgroups of the same algebra and the first is the reason either exists. The corpus builds the coupling of a charged field not by postulating $\mathrm{U}(1)$ but by localising the central phase: the covariant derivative is $\tilde\nabla_{\Phi}+\frac{2\pi i\,ϙ}{h}\tilde A_\Phi$, with $ϙ$ a parameter of the field and $ϙ=-e$ for the electron (articles_physics/the-coupled-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.md:17, :83). So the $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ of the Relativistic Quantum Theory rows is the centre's phase, localised, with the electric charge as its parameter — the same group as the Electromagnetism rows and not hypercharge. $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ is a different generator: the electron couples to $Q$, not to $Y$, and only after the breaking is there a $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ direction inside the electroweak product at all. The three share one Lie algebra, one generator and one dimension, and no two of them are interchangeable.

Outside the four force categories the column records the group the density actually uses, because those categories are charts and limits rather than forces: $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ where a charge couples to a connection, $\mathrm{SU}(1)=\{e\}$ where none does, the diffeomorphisms for the metric, and any $G$ for the generic Yang–Mills line, whose density is form-invariant and fixes no group. It is the gauged group and not the carrier: spin is the next column and is not a group. And $\mathrm{SU}(1)=\{e\}$ means that no generator is gauged, not that the density is symmetry-free — a free abelian density keeps the redundancy of its potential, which is a redundancy of the description and not a force.

How to read the density column. Each entry is a density at one point; the action is its integral over $\mathrm{d}^{4}r$, or over $\mathrm{d}t'\,\mathrm{d}^{3}x$ where the line is first order in time. Where a line aggregates several functionals they are separated by <br>, and a line that shares a functional repeats it rather than pointing back: the two QCD lines are two terms of one action, the free-Proca density recurs in the Galilean-vector line, and the Standard Model's Higgs block is the electroweak block. The three empty lines carry an em dash because the verdict is no, not because a density was omitted; on the $\tfrac32$ line the standard density is named in the last column, since what is missing there is its biquaternionic form and not the functional. The entries follow each article's own sign and index conventions, including the corpus's $\sum_{\imath}X_{\imath}Y_{\imath}=-X\cdot Y$ rule where that article uses it; the topologically massive entry repeats $\epsilon_{\imath}$ on the two contracted indices because the source prints it that way and it is transcribed rather than tidied; the gravitational matter lines, the Galilean vector line and the Clebsch vector-fluid line are the standard densities written in the corpus's frame letters. Section 5 writes out the functionals that carry more than one term.

Action is machinery, not a sector, and holds one line. Yang–Mills is the generic non-abelian density the other lines specialise. The category's remaining articles are arrows — from a density to an equation, and from that equation to a commutator — and an arrow is not an integrand. That is why the article whose title carries both "the Lagrangian" and "second quantisation" contributes no row: it describes a promotion, and a promotion has neither a Lagrangian nor an action.

Counts. Thirty-seven lines, thirty-four occupied, three empty. Occupied by category: Action 1, Force-Free 3, Primordial 3, Strong 3, Electroweak 3, Standard Model Assembly 3, Electromagnetism 3, RQT 3, NRQM 3, RNQ 3, NRNQ 2, Gravity 4, roster 0.

The Lagrangian column is populated on all thirty-four occupied lines, and only there. The three em dashes are the three empty lines; no occupied line is left without a density.

The answer in one sentence. Spins $0$, $\tfrac12$ and $1$ have a genuine Lagrangian in every sector, spin $2$ has one in gravity, the $\tfrac32$ line waits on an algebra element that has not been built, and above spin $2$ there is no carrier at all — so the question of a missing Lagrangian never arises inside a category; it arises only outside the roster.

4. Why a line is empty

Six conditions, and a line falls when one fails. A genuine action needs a carrier of the spin; a representation of the group of that carrier's dimension and reality type; an invariant of the required dimension, so the term is admitted; a density built from the algebra's own elements; a density that can be integrated, which is Helmholtz's question; and, for a classical or non-quantum line, a classical limit to take.

condition what fails the lines
a carrier there is no carrier of that spin in this roster $\geq\tfrac52$
a density in the algebra's own elements the field is imported and the object that writes its invariant is not built $\tfrac32$
the group's representation no representation of that dimension, so no field none — the strong spin-$0$ line is occupied; what it declines is a coloured carrier at dimension $1$, which is not the same as a coloured scalar being impossible
invariance the field exists and the term is forbidden no line — three terms are forbidden: the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and gluon masses, and the quartet Yukawa
Helmholtz no density has that term as its Euler–Lagrange expression no line — the viscous term only
a classical limit an anticommuting field has no real-valued one NRNQ $\tfrac12$

Three of the six empty the term and not the line, and that distinction is the whole audit. The strong spin-$0$ line declines a fundamental coloured scalar and still carries four actions. The primordial spin-$1$ line forbids its mass and still carries the action. The NRNQ spin-$0$ line forbids viscosity and still carries the classical Schrödinger functional. A declined term is not an empty line, and a category with a declined term still has its Lagrangian.

5. The functionals, written out

Written here rather than cited, because whether an article holds them is not the question.

Primordial, the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ action, one coupling on fifteen generators with $D_{\imath}=\partial_{\imath}-ig_4\sum_{a=1}^{15}A_{a,\imath}t_{a}$:

$$ \mathcal{L} =-\tfrac14\sum_{a,\imath,\jmath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}F_{a,\imath\jmath} +\sum_{f}\bar\xi_{f}\Bigl(-i\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}\Bigr)\xi_{f} +\operatorname{tr}\bigl(D_{\imath}Ξ\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigl(D_{\imath}Ξ\bigr)-V(Ξ) +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gh}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yuk}} $$

It gives one equation $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}D_{\imath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}=+g_4j_{a,\jmath}$ and fifteen Gauss laws, with $15\times2$ connection degrees of freedom, $32$ fermion states and $16$ real scalars. The scalar is a Hermitian $4\times4$ matrix, so $V(\Xi)=\mu_\Phi^2\operatorname{tr}\Xi^2+\mu_2^2(\operatorname{tr}\Xi)^2+\lambda_1\operatorname{tr}\Xi^4+\lambda_2(\operatorname{tr}\Xi^2)^2+\lambda_3(\operatorname{tr}\Xi)^2\operatorname{tr}\Xi^2+\lambda_4(\operatorname{tr}\Xi)^4$.

Strong, QCD:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}}=-\tfrac14\sum_{a,\imath,\jmath}G_{a,\imath\jmath}G_{a,\imath\jmath} +\sum_{f}\bar\Xi_{f}\bigl(i\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}-m_{f}\bigr)\Xi_{f} +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gh}} $$

Electroweak, both phases in one density, since the second is the first with the vacuum inserted:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EW}}=-\tfrac14W_{a,\imath\jmath}W_{a,\imath\jmath}-\tfrac14B_{\imath\jmath}B_{\imath\jmath} +\bigl(D_{\imath}\omicron\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigl(D_{\imath}\omicron\bigr)-V(\omicron) +\sum_{f}\bar\psi_{f}\bigl(i\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}\bigr)\psi_{f} -\sum_{\alpha\beta}\Bigl(y^{u}_{\alpha\beta}\bar q_{\alpha}\tilde H u_{\beta} +y^{d}_{\alpha\beta}\bar q_{\alpha}H d_{\beta} +y^{e}_{\alpha\beta}\bar\ell_{\alpha}H e_{\beta}+\text{h.c.}\Bigr) $$

Electromagnetism, the free functional, its charged fermion, and its charged scalar:

$$ S_{\mathrm{Max}}=\int\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Sc}\bigl(\tilde F\tilde F\bigr) \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QED}}=\bar\psi\bigl(i\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}-m\bigr)\psi-\tfrac14F_{\imath\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath} \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sQED}}=\bigl(D_{\imath}\varphi\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigl(D_{\imath}\varphi\bigr)-V(\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi)-\tfrac14F_{\imath\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath} $$

with $D_{\imath}=\partial_{\imath}-ieA_{\imath}$ in each. Its variation is $\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde F=-Z\tilde J_\Phi$ (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-electromagnetic-field.md:158).

Gravity, the exact functional and its quadratic part:

$$ S=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{\lvert\det G\rvert}\,\bigl(R-2\Lambda\bigr) \qquad S^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\Bigl\{-\tfrac12\partial_{\imath}h_{\imath\jmath}\partial_{\imath}h_{\imath\jmath} +\partial_{\imath}h_{\imath\jmath}\partial_{\jmath}h-\partial_{\imath}h_{\imath\jmath}\partial_{\imath}h_{k\jmath} +\tfrac12\partial_{\imath}h\partial_{\imath}h\Bigr\} $$

Force-Free, Proca and Dirac, in the corpus's own notation:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Proca}}=\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Sc}\bigl(\tilde F\tilde F\bigr) -\tfrac12 4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\bigl\langle\tilde A_\Phi\bigr\rangle^{\natural} \qquad S_{\mathrm{Dirac}}=\int\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Sc}\Bigl[\tilde\Xi_L^{*}\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_L +\tilde\Xi_R^{*}\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_R-2\pi\nu_m\bigl(\tilde\Xi_L^{*}\tilde\Xi_R+\tilde\Xi_R^{*}\tilde\Xi_L\bigr)\Bigr] $$

Klein–Gordon, Schrödinger, Yang–Mills, in their standard shapes:

$$ S_{\mathrm{KG}}=\int\Bigl[\tfrac12(\partial\varphi)^{2}-\tfrac12 m^{2}\varphi^{2}\Bigr] \qquad S_{\mathrm{Sch}}=\int\mathrm{d}t\,\Bigl\langle\psi\Bigl|\,i\hbar\partial_t-H\,\Bigr|\psi\Bigr\rangle \qquad S_{\mathrm{YM}}=-\tfrac12\int\mathrm{d}^{4}r\,\operatorname{Tr}\Bigl(\sum_{\imath,\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath}\Bigr) $$

And the two empty lines, for two different reasons. The viscous term has no functional because no density has it as its Euler–Lagrange expression (articles_physics/the-inverse-problem-of-the-calculus-of-variations-helmholtz-conditions.md:5) — a failure of existence, and the Helmholtz condition is what names it. The $\tfrac32$ row is empty for the other reason, and the corpus states it in one sentence: this family “has the Rarita–Schwinger equation and it does not have the Rarita–Schwinger action” (articles_internal/archives/the-lagrangian-and-the-rarita-schwinger-equation.md:5). The field is imported as $S\otimes V=4\oplus2$ with its constraint (articles_physics/spin-content-of-m4c-under-its-two-su2-copies-and-su4.md:133), and the standard functional is quoted in the literature's notation, $S_{\mathrm{RS}}=\int\bar\Xi_{\mathrm{wfn},\imath}(i\gamma_{\imath\jmath\rho}\partial_\rho-m\gamma_{\imath\jmath})\Xi_{\mathrm{wfn},\jmath}$. What is missing is one object: an element or a pair of elements of $\mathbb{B}$ that contracts the vector index with the field's chiral structure, which is what the trace and divergence multipliers need. So the absence is in the algebra and not in field theory — the functional exists, and its biquaternionic density has not been built.

5.1 Worked example: the strong sector

The strong force has one Lagrangian. The ledger gives it three lines because the ledger's rows are spins, and the row-to-action map is not one-to-one. Stated first, then displayed:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}} =-\tfrac14\sum_{a,\imath,\jmath}G_{a,\imath\jmath}G_{a,\imath\jmath} +\sum_{f}\bar\Xi_{\mathrm{wfn},f}\Bigl(-i\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}-m_{f}\Bigr)\Xi_{\mathrm{wfn},f} -\frac{1}{2\zeta}\sum_{a,\imath}\Bigl(\frac{\partial G_{a,\imath}}{\partial R_{\imath}}\Bigr)^{2} +\sum_{a}\bar c_{a}\Bigl(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial R_{\imath}\partial R_{\imath}}\Bigr)c_{a} +\mathcal{L}_{cG} $$

with

$$G_{a,\imath\jmath}=\frac{\partial G_{a,\jmath}}{\partial R_{\imath}}-\frac{\partial G_{a,\imath}}{\partial R_{\jmath}}+g_s\sum_{b,c}f_{abc}G_{b,\imath}G_{c,\jmath}, \quad D_{\imath}=\frac{\partial}{\partial R_{\imath}}-ig_sG_{a,\imath}T_{a}, \quad T_{a}=\frac{\digamma_{\mathrm{gmn},a}}{2}, \quad \mathcal{L}_{cG}=-g_s\sum_{a,b,c}f_{abc}\Bigl(\frac{\partial\bar c_{a}}{\partial R_{\imath}}\Bigr)G_{b,\imath}c_{c}$$

One action, four kinds of term. The octet connection with its self-interaction; one triplet per flavour, minimally coupled; a fixing term; a ghost pair. The two ledger lines are two of these terms, not two theories — the quark term cannot be written without $G_{a,\imath}$, because $D_{\imath}$ contains it, and the first term is what makes $G_{a,\imath}$ dynamical.

ledger line what it is in the display above
spin $1$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ the $G_{a,\imath\jmath}G_{a,\imath\jmath}$ term, plus the fixing and ghost terms
spin $\tfrac12$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ the $\bar\Xi D\!\!\!/\Xi$ term, one per flavour
spin $0$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ not in this display at all, and not in any $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ action — see below

Nothing in the display is a spin-$0$ strong term, and that is a fact about the corpus's carrier roster. Its spin-$0$ carrier is the one-dimensional line $\mathbb{R}e_t$, whose $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ representation is the singlet. A coloured scalar is nevertheless an ordinary field in general, and if one existed the repair would be one more matter term, $(D_{\imath}\phi)^{\dagger}(D_{\imath}\phi)-V(\phi)$, added to the same action — not a fourth Lagrangian. Squarks, colour-triplet GUT scalars and octet scalars all couple through $D_{\imath}$ exactly so.

The spin-$0$ line is a different object entirely. The four actions the ledger lists there are effective descriptions of other degrees of freedom, none of them $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\pi}^{(2)}=-\frac{f_\pi^{2}}{2}\sum_{\imath}\operatorname{Sc}\!\Bigl(\frac{\partial\tilde U}{\partial R_{\imath}}\frac{\partial\tilde U^{\natural}}{\partial R_{\imath}}\Bigr) +f_\pi^{2}B_{0}\operatorname{Sc}\bigl(M\tilde U+M^{*}\tilde U^{*}\bigr), \qquad \tilde U\tilde U^{\natural}=e_t $$

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Skyrme}}=-\frac{f^{2}}{2}\operatorname{Sc}\bigl(\bar j_{\imath}j_{\imath}\bigr) +\frac{1}{16e^{2}}\operatorname{Sc}\bigl([j_{\imath},j_{\jmath}][j_{\imath},j_{\jmath}]\bigr) \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\sigma}=\mathcal{L}_2+\mathcal{L}_4+\mathcal{L}_6+\cdots, \quad \mathcal{L}_{2n}\sim\frac{f^{2}}{\Lambda^{2n-2}}\,\partial^{2n} $$

$$ \mathcal{L}_{a}=\tfrac12\sum_{\imath}\partial_{\imath}a\,\partial_{\imath}a-\Lambda^{4}\Bigl(1-\cos\frac{a}{f_a}\Bigr) +\frac{a}{f_a}\frac{g_s^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\sum_{\imath,\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath}F_{\mathrm{dual},\imath\jmath} $$

The pion action is flavour, not colour — it rests on the approximate $\mathrm{SU}(3)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(3)_R$ chiral symmetry of the light quarks, and that is why it is written on the unit quaternion $\tilde U$ and carries no colour index. The Skyrme action is an effective soliton model of the same broken flavour symmetry, and the sigma model is its derivative expansion. The axion is a colour singlet, and its second term couples it to gluons through $G\tilde G$ — which is why no line of this file may say that no scalar couples to a gluon.

The ghosts are the second counterexample. $c_{a}$ is a Lorentz scalar in the adjoint: a spin-$0$ colour octet, sitting in the QCD display itself, coupling to the gluon through $\mathcal{L}_{cG}$. It is unphysical — a Grassmann auxiliary field that cancels the unphysical gauge degrees of freedom — but it exists in the action, and it is a spin-$0$ object coupled to colour.

And the count of four is not canonical. Operators $\frac{1}{\Lambda^{n}}\phi\,GG$ and $\frac{1}{\Lambda^{n}}\phi\,G\tilde G$ of any dimension are permitted, and so are pion operators of every even derivative order; the four above are the ones the corpus writes.

What this section therefore retracts. An earlier version of this file said the strong force had "three Lagrangians of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ depending on spin", that $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ declines a scalar, and that nothing anywhere couples a scalar to a gluon. All three are false. Colour and spin are independent quantum numbers — $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ is internal and spin is Lorentz, chosen independently for each field — so a colour group has no Lagrangians classified by spin. QCD is one Lagrangian; the only true exclusion here is the corpus's own carrier roster, and it is narrower than any of those three sentences.

6. A note on origin, which is not a column

Nothing in §3 depends on where a functional came from, and this paragraph changes no cell of it.

The distinction, stated once. The free, coupled, rung and generic lines are written in the algebra's own elements. The primordial, QCD, electroweak, Standard Model and gravity lines are standard theory expressed in the corpus's coordinates: the algebra's compact group is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, of real dimension four, and neither fifteen $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ generators nor eight $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ generators fit inside four (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-primordial-force.md:27). So $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is written in the algebra and $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ on it. The group column is where that would show if it showed anywhere: it records which group's connection each density carries, and in the primordial, strong, electroweak, Standard Model and electromagnetic lines the group is standard theory's, borrowed rather than derived. The column is a statement about the density, and it is not a claim of derivation. That bounds what the framework may claim to have predicted. It does not make any of those actions less of an action, and it does not empty a single line: QCD's four vertices follow from its display, and "every number in the Feynman rules was derived from that Lagrangian rather than quoted" (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-quantum-chromodynamics-the-triplet-the-octet-and-the-feynman-rules.md:438).

7. What this audit does not claim

It does not claim a category lacks a Lagrangian. Every category has one at every spin that has a carrier.

It does not claim an empty line can be filled by writing harder. The $\tfrac32$ line fails on the algebra's own terms — the field is imported and its density is not written — the line above spin $2$ on the carrier roster, and the NRNQ $\tfrac12$ line on the absence of a real-valued classical limit. Nor does it claim the field is absent from physics: the standard Rarita–Schwinger functional is a textbook object, the Fronsdal and Vasiliev fields exist, and what this ledger records is that the corpus has not written the $\tfrac32$ density in its own algebra.

It does not claim a declined term empties a line. Three terms are forbidden — two masses and a quartet Yukawa — and each sits in a category that keeps its action.

It does not claim spin fixes the functional. Spin, stage and mass are three inputs: Klein–Gordon and Schrödinger are both spin $0$; Maxwell and Proca both spin $1$; Dirac and Pauli both $\tfrac12$.

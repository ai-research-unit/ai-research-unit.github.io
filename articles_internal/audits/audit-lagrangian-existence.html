Audit: Does Each Category Have Its Own Lagrangian?

Status of this file. A review, not an edit. No article, menu, plan or build file was modified. Every count and line number below is checked against physics.md and the article named, as they stand on 2026-11-27.

The question. The corpus files Lagrangian articles under spin headings. Does each category have a Lagrangian particular to its spin? Dirac, Proca and Klein–Gordon plainly do. The doubt is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and the electroweak sector, where the organising object is a gauge group and not a spin.

The answer in one paragraph. The free-field categories have a spin-specific family. The gauge sectors do not. For spins $0$, $\tfrac12$ and $1$ the corpus writes a functional whose Euler–Lagrange equation is the parent equation, and the functional changes with the spin: second order for Klein–Gordon and Proca, first order for Dirac, first order in time for Schrödinger. The gauge sectors are the other way round: one model Lagrangian carries terms of several spins, and the spin headings file the terms by the carrier each article writes on. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ has one transcribed action with a spin-$1$, a spin-$\tfrac12$ and a spin-$0$ piece. QCD has one transcribed action with a spin-$1$ gluon and a spin-$\tfrac12$ quark, plus native spin-$0$ chiral actions that are not QCD. The electroweak sector has one action with spin-$1$ and spin-$0$ terms; its spin-$\tfrac12$ term is named and not expanded. So the right question is not "is there a Lagrangian for this spin" but "is there a derived, transcribed or effective action for this carrier, and which spin does its term carry".

1. Two Senses of the Question

Sense one: a functional for a carrier of spin $s$. The object is a field of a definite spin, and the question is which action has that field's equation as its Euler–Lagrange equation. Klein–Gordon answers for spin $0$, Dirac for spin $\tfrac12$, Proca for spin $1$. The functional is genuinely spin-specific: the carrier, the derivative order and the bilinear form change with the spin.

Sense two: a model Lagrangian for a sector. The object is a gauge group, a representation content and a symmetry-breaking pattern. The question is which action describes the sector. The answer is one action with several terms, each term carrying a different spin. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, QCD and the electroweak action are of this kind.

The corpus uses the same word for both. Most of the apparent inconsistency comes from that. A sector action is not indexed by spin; it is indexed by the group and the content, and the spin is a property of each term.

A second distinction, needed before the tables. Spin fixes the carrier and the derivative order. It does not fix the functional by itself. Spin $0$ has Klein–Gordon, second order and relativistic, and Schrödinger, first order in time. Spin $1$ has Maxwell, massless, and Proca, massive. Spin $\tfrac12$ has Dirac, relativistic, and Pauli, its non-relativistic limit. The functional is indexed by spin and stage and mass. The general Action section states the same rule: across the five rungs only the order of the kinetic term and the content of the algebraic term change, and nothing else does (physics.md:524, physics.md:574).

2. The Verdict Scale

Five statuses are used below. They are the vocabulary the corpus needs and does not yet use consistently.

status meaning
Derived The functional is built from the algebra's own elements and operations. The parent equation is the algebra's.
Transcribed The functional is standard theory written in the corpus's coordinates. No term follows from the algebra.
Effective The functional is a low-energy expansion on an algebra-native carrier, with imported coefficients.
Named The functional is written in the literature's notation and not in the algebra.
Absent No functional is written. The slot may be empty because the action is elsewhere, because no action is owed, or because a representation is missing.

The distinction between derived and transcribed is the one the Sector Hub's one-line answers compress. The distinction between transcribed and effective is the one the Strong Force's two layers make necessary. The distinction between named and absent is the one the Rarita–Schwinger article keeps.

Two qualifiers are used in the table. Piece means a term of a larger action; it is not a sixth status. Mixed means part derived and part transcribed; it is used for the electroweak vector sector, where the adjoint kinematics are the algebra's and the hypercharge assignment is imported.

5. The Spin-Specific Family Is Real, and the Written Family Ends at Spin One

The family is derived, and its order changes with the spin. Klein–Gordon writes one action whose Euler–Lagrange equation is the parent (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-klein-gordon-equation.md:5). Dirac writes one action whose variation gives the parent pair (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-dirac-equation.md:5). Proca writes one action whose variation gives the Proca equation (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-proca-equation.md:5). Schrödinger writes one action whose variation gives the parent (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-schrodinger-equation.md:5). Pauli derives its action from the Dirac action by the non-relativistic limit (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-pauli-equation.md:5). Maxwell writes one free action, one term, and gets both halves of Maxwell from it (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-electromagnetic-field.md:5).

The order is the spin's signature. Klein–Gordon and Proca are second order. Dirac is first order. Schrödinger is first order in time. The general Action section states the rule once: across the five rungs only the order of the kinetic term and the content of the algebraic term change (physics.md:524, physics.md:574). This is the precise sense in which the Lagrangian "depends on the spin". The spin fixes the carrier and the derivative order. It does not fix the rest.

The written family ends at spin $1$; the next case is named only. The Rarita–Schwinger article opens with the plain statement: the family has the equation and does not have the action (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-rarita-schwinger-equation.md:5). Its ledger marks the standard action as "named and not written" (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-rarita-schwinger-equation.md:113). The reason is one missing object: an element or a pair of elements of $\mathbb{B}$ that contracts the vector index with the field's chiral structure and is not simply the frame. The article states what the action must contain, what the variation would have to reproduce, and why the absence is not an oversight. So the claim "every spin has its Lagrangian" is false at $\tfrac32$, and the corpus says so.

The next written case is spin $2$, and it is quadratic only. Linearised gravity has a quadratic action, the second variation of the Einstein–Hilbert action, written on the frame perturbation (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-linearised-gravity.md:5). The exact action is not written. The exact-gravity ledger says "gravitational, quadratic: yes" and "gravitational, exact: not yet" (articles_physics/the-exact-gravitational-action-the-functional-the-boundary-term-and-the-variation.md:13). The article's own summary is that the gravitational sector has one action and needs its exact form, its boundary term and its coefficients (articles_physics/the-exact-gravitational-action-the-functional-the-boundary-term-and-the-variation.md:234). So spin $2$ has a quadratic action, not an exact one.

Spin does not uniquely fix the functional. Spin $0$ has Klein–Gordon and Schrödinger. Spin $1$ has Maxwell and Proca. Spin $\tfrac12$ has Dirac and Pauli. The stage and the mass are independent inputs. A table of "one Lagrangian per spin" would therefore be wrong even inside the derived family. The correct statement is: each spin has a family of actions, and the family is indexed by spin, stage and mass.

6. The Gauge Sectors Are One Action, Not One per Spin

6.1 $\mathrm{SU}(4)$

One action, three pieces. The Lagrangian and the Primordial Force writes the action in one display: $-\tfrac14\sum F_{\imath\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath}$ traced over the fifteen with one coupling, then the boson, fermion and scalar pieces together (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-primordial-force.md:5). The spin-$1$ article is the whole display. The spin-$\tfrac12$ article writes the fermion kinetic term and the Yukawa coupling (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-sixteen-kinetic-term-and-yukawa-coupling.md:7). The spin-$0$ article writes the scalar matrix (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-scalar-matrix-one-field-one-commutator-four-quartics.md:5). These are not three independent Lagrangians. They are one Lagrangian and two expansions of its pieces.

The action is transcribed, not derived. The article's third point is the status: $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is written in the algebra; $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is written on it. The compact group of $\mathbb{B}$ is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, of real dimension four, and fifteen generators do not fit inside four. "So the action below is a transcription and not a derivation" (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-primordial-force.md:27). The spin dependence is real, but it is the derivative order and the representation of each piece, not a property of the gauge group.

The answer for $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. Yes, there is a term for each of the three spins. No, there is not a separate Lagrangian per spin. The whole is one transcribed action, and the three menu articles are three views of it.

6.2 $\mathrm{SU}(3)$

QCD is one transcribed action with two spin terms. Everything below the QCD article's introduction is standard theory, written in the corpus's coordinates; it is standard input, not a framework result (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-quantum-chromodynamics-the-triplet-the-octet-and-the-feynman-rules.md:5). The Lagrangian is $-\tfrac14\sum GG$ plus a quark term plus gauge fixing plus ghosts (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-quantum-chromodynamics-the-triplet-the-octet-and-the-feynman-rules.md:438). The gluon term is spin $1$. The quark term is spin $\tfrac12$. They are in one article, and the article is filed at spin $1$ because the gauge field is its subject.

The spin-$\tfrac12$ slot is empty because the quark is inside the spin-$1$ article. There is no standalone colour-carrying spin-$\tfrac12$ action. This is not a gap in the way the Rarita–Schwinger absence is a gap: the term exists, in the transcribed QCD display. What does not exist is a separate article that puts a functional on the quark alone.

The spin-$0$ actions are effective, not QCD. Four articles sit in the strong spin-$0$ slot: the nonlinear sigma model, the chiral theory of the pion, the Skyrme model and the axion. The first, third and fourth are the three actions the strong internal note counts; the chiral theory is the pion specialisation, the fourth just outside the family. All four are written on the algebra's own chiral field, and their coefficients are imported. The strong internal note states the boundary exactly: three actions exist without a colour label, and "none is an action of QCD" (articles_internal/internal-summaries-physics/internal-summary-physics-of-the-strong-interaction.md:481). So the strong category has two layers: a transcribed fundamental QCD action and native low-energy effective actions. It has no native gluon action.

The answer for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. Yes, there is a QCD Lagrangian, and it carries a spin-$1$ and a spin-$\tfrac12$ term. No, there is not a family of strong Lagrangians indexed by spin. The spin-$0$ articles are a different layer, not the spin-$0$ part of QCD.

6.3 Electroweak

One sector action, written in two phases. The Lagrangian and the Electroweak Theory writes the one Lagrangian in its unbroken and broken phases. The article is explicit about the limit: the corpus has the group, the hypercharge assignment, the doublet, the potential, the connection masses, the quotient and the parity lock, but "it does not have the electroweak Lagrangian in one display" before this article, and it has no quantised electroweak theory at all (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-electroweak-theory.md:5).

The matter term is named and not expanded. The display carries $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{matter}}$, "the fermion kinetic terms with their covariant derivatives and the Yukawa couplings, which this article names and does not expand" (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-electroweak-theory.md:33). That is the spin-$\tfrac12$ term. It is not absent from the action; it is absent from the display.

Three of the four spin-$1$ articles are one action and its readings. $W$ and $Z$ are the connection and scalar part of the sector's complete action (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-w-and-z-bosons.md:5). The weak-force article is the charged-current term of the same action, with the neutral sector dropped (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-weak-force.md:5). The electroweak-theory article is the sector action itself. The Stueckelberg article is a neighbouring abelian model, filed beside the sector because that is where a massive vector is discussed, not because the sector uses it (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-stueckelberg-mechanism.md:5). The Higgs article is the scalar block (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-higgs-field.md:5). So the slot does not hold five independent Lagrangians: it holds one sector action, three of its readings, one neighbouring model and one scalar block.

The answer for the electroweak sector. Yes, there is one Lagrangian, and it carries spin-$1$ and spin-$0$ terms. Yes, it has a spin-$\tfrac12$ term, but that term is named and not expanded. No, there is no standalone spin-$\tfrac12$ electroweak Lagrangian. The reason is the chiral wall, and §8 states it.

7. The Strong "No Action" Sentence Is Scoped

The sentence. The Sector Hub says: "The strong sector has no action and cannot have one in this algebra" (articles_physics/the-sector-hub-what-the-lagrangian-family-reaches-in-each-sector.md:7). The exact-gravity ledger repeats it: "strong | none, and cannot be" (articles_physics/the-exact-gravitational-action-the-functional-the-boundary-term-and-the-variation.md:12).

The counter-evidence in the same corpus. The strong menu has a QCD Lagrangian article (physics.md:1422) and four chiral Lagrangian articles (physics.md:1489). The QCD article says it is transcribed, not derived (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-quantum-chromodynamics-the-triplet-the-octet-and-the-feynman-rules.md:5). The strong internal note counts "one spin-$1$, transcribed" and "three spin-$0$ in the family and a fourth just outside it" (articles_internal/internal-note-on-the-spin-of-the-lagrangian-articles.md:7). So the category does have actions.

The reconciliation. The hub's sentence is true only under an unstated scope: there is no derived gluon action in the algebra's own variables. It is false under the literal reading: there are Lagrangian articles in the strong category. The physics is not in dispute. The status language is.

The fix is one sentence, not a rewrite. In the hub and in the exact-gravity ledger, the strong row should read: "no derived gluon action; the category carries a transcribed QCD Lagrangian and native chiral effective actions". If the author prefers to keep the strong claim, it needs the clause "in the algebra's own variables" after the word action. The same clause resolves the apparent conflict with the QCD article without changing a single equation.

8. The Electroweak Spin-$\tfrac12$ Slot Is the Chiral Wall

The slot is empty. physics.md:1617 has no links. The sector action names $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{matter}}$ and does not expand it (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-electroweak-theory.md:33).

The reason is the letter law. A left multiplication applies the same element to both chiral parts of the field. An action that applies the same element to both parts cannot give them separate charges, whatever the element is. Independent charges need the right slot and an element of two unequal values; a central multiplier gives the same factor on both sides. The electroweak internal note states the result: "The three obstructions of the part are one obstruction stated at three spins" (articles_internal/internal-summaries-physics/internal-summary-physics-of-the-electroweak-interaction.md:95). The scalar row is trivial, the vector row is the adjoint and works, and the fermion row fails. The spin-$\tfrac12$ slot is the fermion row.

The Standard Model transcription writes the term. The Standard Model and the Lagrangian writes the four sectors in one expression, including the fermion term and the Yukawa matrices (articles_physics/the-standard-model-and-the-lagrangian-four-sectors-in-one-expression.md:5). Its standing is explicit: the expression is standard theory, transcribed, not derived (articles_physics/the-standard-model-and-the-lagrangian-four-sectors-in-one-expression.md:13). So the spin-$\tfrac12$ term exists at the Standard Model level. It does not exist as a framework article, and the reason is structural.

The answer for the electroweak spin-$\tfrac12$ slot. It is not an oversight and it is not a filing accident. The corpus can write the term only by transcription, and it does so in the Standard Model article. A standalone electroweak spin-$\tfrac12$ Lagrangian would either be the transcription again or would require the right-slot construction the algebra does not have.

9. The Sixty-Two Titles Are Not Sixty-Two Lagrangians

The count. Sixty-two files begin the-lagrangian. Sixty of them are linked inside the twenty-five action slots. Two are outside: The Lagrangian and the Second Quantisation (physics.md:894) and The Lagrangian and the Topologically Massive Connection (physics.md:1978). Neither changes a category verdict. The first is the general action-to-quantum step in the Action category. The second is a neighbouring $2+1$-dimensional model in the RQT spin-$1$ band. A sixty-third action-bearing article is not titled the-lagrangian: Non-Abelian Connections in Biquaternionic Form contains the Yang–Mills action (articles_physics/non-abelian-connections-in-biquaternionic-form.md). The internal note counts it as the one Lagrangian article of the connection-structure section (articles_internal/internal-note-on-the-spin-of-the-lagrangian-articles.md:7). It is general, not spin-specific, and it is the machinery the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and QCD transcriptions use.

The titles overcount the actions. The twelve links in the RQT spin-$0$ slot are not twelve independent Lagrangians. They are the Klein–Gordon action in element, polar, momentum and coupled forms; the spin-zero and Schrödinger fluid readings; the interacting scalar; and the Goldstone effective action. The nine Proca links and the nine Dirac links are similar. The corpus says so at the top of the articles. The Klein–Gordon pair are "the same functional written in two coordinate systems" (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-klein-gordon-equation.md:5). The Dirac pair differ more, because the polar action carries constraints the element action does not, but they are the same theory (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-dirac-equation.md:5). The coupled and momentum articles add a source or change the chart; they do not add a new parent.

The audit counts actions, not titles. The number of distinct actions is the number of parent equations plus the number of sector actions, not sixty-two. A reader who counts titles will conclude that the corpus has a Lagrangian for every occasion. It does not. It has a small number of actions written many times.

Not owed: the band's subject is a state, a measurement or a classical effect, and no action is claimed (NRQM spin $\tfrac12$ and other spins; all four NRNQ bands). Filling that slot would contradict a theorem.

11. The Rule

The band records the spin of the field whose action the article writes, not the spin content of the force. The internal spin note states the rule (articles_internal/internal-note-on-the-spin-of-the-lagrangian-articles.md:5). The electroweak part writes vector actions because its business is the mass of a vector. The strong part writes scalar actions because the one object the algebra owns in that sector is a unit quaternion. The rule is not a filing accident: moving the Higgs article to spin $1$ would make it false, and moving QCD to spin $0$ would make it false (articles_internal/internal-note-on-the-spin-of-the-lagrangian-articles.md:87).

A sector Lagrangian is a sum over representations. Each term carries the spin of the field in that representation. The gauge group fixes which representations exist. The spin fixes the derivative order and the bilinear structure of each term. The two questions are independent, and the corpus's tables should ask them separately.

The precise answer to the author's examples. Dirac, Proca and Klein–Gordon each have a derived action whose form depends on the spin. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ has one transcribed action with three spin pieces. $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has one transcribed QCD action with spin-$1$ and spin-$\tfrac12$ terms, plus native spin-$0$ effective actions that are not QCD. The electroweak sector has one action with spin-$1$ and spin-$0$ terms, and a spin-$\tfrac12$ term that is named and not expanded. There is no separate Lagrangian per spin in any of the three gauge sectors. There is one Lagrangian per sector, with a spin-dependent term structure.

12. What the Audit Recommends

1. Adopt the five-status vocabulary. Every action-bearing article and every sector ledger should say derived, transcribed, effective, named or absent. The words "has an action" and "has no action" are too coarse for a corpus that contains all five.

2. Add a scope sentence to the strong row. The Sector Hub and the exact-gravity ledger should say "no derived gluon action" rather than "no action", and should name the transcribed QCD action and the native chiral effective actions. This is a one-sentence change in two places.

3. Add cross-references to the two empty spin-$\tfrac12$ slots. The strong slot should point to the quark term in the QCD article. The electroweak slot should point to the named matter term in the electroweak action and to the Standard Model transcription. A reader who lands on the empty slot should not conclude that the term does not exist.

4. Label the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ spin-$\tfrac12$ and spin-$0$ articles as pieces. They are expansions of the one action, not independent functionals. The label can be a sentence in the introduction, not a menu change.

5. Decide the Rarita–Schwinger placement. The action article sits in RNQ other spins, while the RQT other-spins slot is empty. Either move the action article to the RQT slot or add a cross-reference from it. The absence is real; the filing should not make it look like a different absence.

6. Do not fill the NRNQ slots. The absence is principled. The viscosity case is a theorem, and the other three bands are effects, not field theories.

The corpus has two Lagrangian architectures, and the author's doubt is the seam between them. The free-field categories are spin-specific: Klein–Gordon, Dirac, Proca, Schrödinger and Pauli each have a derived action, and the action's order changes with the spin. B.11 Correction: the section's two claims, restated

B.1 to B.10 wrote the section's thesis as one chain and, in two places, as a denial of the group's role. That was wrong, and the correction is the section's charter.

The section states two things, and they are independent.

  1. Each symmetry group decides which spin carriers exist for it and which terms survive invariance. The bound of three is the algebra's and is the same everywhere; the content inside the bound is the group's. $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has a spinor and a vector and no scalar; $\mathrm{U}(1)$ has the vector only; $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ has all three. On the density side the group enters twice: in the candidate space ($d_{abc}$ exists for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$/$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and vanishes for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$) and in the condition (the transformation law tested is the group's).
  2. Whether a Lagrangian exists at all for a given carrier is decided by Helmholtz's conditions, which do not know about groups. The viscous term has no group anywhere near it; a non-variational equation is non-variational in every sector.

The error B.5 and B.9 contained. The funnel was described as reaching the same short list in four sectors, and that was used as evidence that the group is not what decides. The coincidence is real at dimension four only, and it is a fact about dimension, not about groups: $\operatorname{tr}F^3$ ($d=6$) and the Pauli term ($d=5$) are outside the range where the four lists could differ. At dimension six the lists differ by rank, because the invariant ring is $\operatorname{tr}F^k$, $k=2,\dots,N$.

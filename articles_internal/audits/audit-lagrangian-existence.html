Audit: Does Each Category Have Its Own Lagrangian?

Status of this file. A review, not an edit of any article, menu, plan or build file: only this audit is changed. Rewritten 2026-11-28 into one master ledger: one row per category and spin, two Lagrangian columns (uncoupled and coupled), the mass discussion moved to a note (§6), and provenance — transcribed versus derived — moved out of the verdict into §8. The earlier version's seven-column tables mixed "is it written", "may the framework claim it" and "does it exist" in one cell; the SU(4) row was the worst case and §3.1 answers it directly.

1. The question, and the only two answers

In this category, at this spin, does a genuine action exist in the theory? One question. Whether the corpus has printed it, which article holds it, and which menu heading it sits under are not part of the question. Those are provenance, and they are in §8.

answer meaning
yes there is a carrier of this spin in this category, and a functional on it whose variation is that carrier's equation
no there is not, and the failing condition is named

"Spin" means one of the corpus's three carriers: spin $0$ is the line $\mathbb{R}e_t$, spin $\tfrac12$ the plane $\Sigma(\vec u)$, spin $1$ the vector part $\mathfrak{k}$ — dimensions $1$, $2$, $3$. It does not mean "any field of that Lorentz spin". A colour-triplet scalar is an ordinary field with a perfectly good Lagrangian and is not a carrier here; the same holds for the Rarita–Schwinger field of spin $\tfrac32$. A no row is a statement about this roster and never about field theory at large.

exists is judged on the uncoupled functional. The coupled functional is a second object in its own column; it can be absent from a category that has no connection, and its absence never turns a yes into a no (§5).

The verdict. Every category has a functional at every spin that has a carrier. Thirty-seven rows: thirty-five category rows and two roster rows; thirty-four yes, three no. The three no rows fail on conditions outside the categories proper: no biquaternionic density at spin $\tfrac32$, no carrier above spin $2$, no commuting classical limit at NRNQ spin $\tfrac12$.

One sentence the corpus also prints, and how it is read here. The Sector Hub says "the strong sector has no action and cannot have one in this algebra". That sentence is about an action in the algebra's own variables, and it is true. It is not about the transcribed QCD functional, which exists. §9 states the collision and which sentence this audit means.

2. Lagrangian, action, equation: one formula, used three ways

A Lagrangian is a density; an action is that density added up.

$$ S[\phi]=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\;\mathcal{L}\bigl(\phi,\partial\phi\bigr) $$

what you do what you call the result
write it down at a point the Lagrangian, $\mathcal{L}$
integrate it over a region the action, $S$
vary the action and set $\delta S=0$ the equation of motion

So "does a Lagrangian exist here" and "does an action exist here" is one question asked twice. Every density in this corpus is local, a local density can always be integrated, and a functional that is an integral of a local density hands its integrand back. The tables write the density; the action is its integral.

The relation that can genuinely fail is the next one, and it is not this one. Maxwell's Hessian is degenerate, so the Maxwell row has a Lagrangian and no Hamiltonian and pays with a constraint and the Lorenz condition. Lagrangian without Hamiltonian is ordinary; Hamiltonian without Lagrangian is not. Nothing here is of the second kind.

Where an action can exist with no Lagrangian — not a case here. A one-loop effective action carrying $\operatorname{Tr}\log$, Hamilton's principal function, an action defined only as a lattice limit: no integrand to point at. No row of this ledger is of that kind.

And one case where both fail together. Helmholtz's condition is asked of the equation and decides whether any density has it as its variation (physics.md:487). A non-variational equation has neither a Lagrangian nor an action — the viscous term, and the only place in the audit where the failure is of this kind.

Three features are the audit's substance. The four force categories form one nested chain, $\mathrm{SU}(1)\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)\subset\mathrm{SU}(4)$, counted $0,3,8,15$, with the electroweak rung off it at four. A mass on a connection appears in exactly one row — the electroweak vector — and there it is a scalar's number written in $v$. And no category is without a functional: the absences are two roster rows and one classical limit.

3.1 The SU(4) row: "there is a Lagrangian, but it leads to nothing?"

No. Three different statements were packed into one cell — the functional exists, it is transcribed rather than derived, its group is not the observed one — and only the first two are about existence.

  1. The functional exists, and it is a theory, not a nullity. $\mathcal{L}$ in row 5 of §4 varies to $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}D_{\imath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}=+g_4j_{a,\jmath}$ plus fifteen Gauss laws, with $15\times2$ connection degrees of freedom, $32$ fermion states and $16$ real scalars. It has a conserved current, a first-class constraint algebra and a well-posed Cauchy problem. That is what "leads somewhere" means for a classical functional.
  2. It is transcribed, not derived. The compact group of $\mathbb{B}$ is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, dimension four; fifteen generators do not fit. So $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is written on the algebra, not in it. What is missing is a derivation of fifteen from four — not a Lagrangian. The same holds for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$.
  3. It does not lead to the observed world by itself. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ contains $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$, so seven generators are not the strong ones, the fifteen include leptoquarks, and a fourth colour exists. The corpus's claim is that the residual $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ is what survives below $M_C$. That is a hypothesis with a scale, and it is falsifiable; it is not a derivation.

So the honest answer to "leads to nothing?" is: the functional leads to an equation, the equation leads to a consistent gauge theory, and that theory is not the Standard Model unless a breaking is added. "Transcribed; derived impossible" is a statement about provenance, and it sits in §8, not in the exists column.

3.2 The SU(3) rows: which spin has a functional, and which does not

spin exists what is there what is not there
$1$ yes the QCD gauge sector: curvature, fixing, ghost pair, $\mathcal{L}_{cG}$ (row 8) not derived: $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is outside the algebra's $\mathfrak{u}(2)$, so the eight are written on it
$\tfrac12$ yes the quark term with $m_f$ and the triplet covariant derivative (row 9) not a separate action: one action with row 8, and the colour assignment is standard QCD, not a corpus result
$0$ yes four effective functionals — sigma, pion, Skyrme, axion — plus the ghost, which is a spin-$0$ colour octet inside row 8 no fundamental coloured scalar: BSM scalars in $\mathbf3$ or $\mathbf8$ are ordinary fields and none is in this roster

The spin-$0$ case is where the "certain SU(3) spin cases" confusion comes from. The row is yes because four functionals exist and one of them (the ghost) is coloured. It is not no, and it is not "a coloured scalar exists": the corpus's scalar carrier is the singlet row, and the octet at spin $0$ is the Faddeev–Popov field, which carries no independent degree of freedom.

Audit: Does Each Category Have Its Own Lagrangian?

Status of this file. A review, not an edit of any article, menu, plan or build file: only this audit is changed. Rewritten around one formula: every Lagrangian is $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gauge}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{matter}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coupling}}$, and every table in §6 writes those three pieces out for every line. "Without the coupling" therefore has one meaning — delete the third piece — and the mass columns sit beside them because a mass is not a coupling term. The argument is stated in words before any table (§The short answer, in words); the tables are the evidence for it. The four forces and the charts have different carriers, different groups and different mass objects, and one flat ledger of thirty-seven rows hid that; the categories are now separated, and the cross-cutting definitions they share are stated once before them.

1. The question, and the only two answers

In this category, at this spin, does a genuine action exist in the theory? One question. Whether the corpus has printed it, which article holds it, and which menu heading it sits under are not part of the question.

answer meaning
yes there is a field of this spin in this category, and a functional on it whose variation is that field's equation
no there is not, and a reason is named

"Spin" means one of the corpus's three carriers: spin $0$ is the line $\mathbb{R}e_t$, spin $\tfrac12$ the plane $\Sigma(\vec u)$, spin $1$ the vector part $\mathfrak{k}$ — dimensions $1$, $2$, $3$. It does not mean "any field of that Lorentz spin". A colour-triplet scalar is an ordinary field with a perfectly good Lagrangian and is not a carrier here; the same holds for the Rarita–Schwinger field of spin $\tfrac32$ and for Fronsdal and Vasiliev fields. A "no" row is a statement about the roster and never about field theory at large.

The verdict. Every category has a Lagrangian at every spin that has a carrier. Thirty-seven lines, thirty-four occupied, three empty, and all three empty lines fail for a reason outside the category proper: one has no carrier in the roster, one has no biquaternionic density, one has no commuting classical limit. No category is without a Lagrangian.

1.1 The answer in one paragraph

Every Lagrangian in this audit is three pieces: the connection alone, the free carrier, and the term that needs both.

$$ \mathcal{L}=\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gauge}}}_{\text{connection alone}}+\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{matter}}}_{\text{free carrier}}+\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coupling}}}_{\text{connection}\times\text{current}} $$

"Without the coupling" means deleting the third piece and nothing else — and what survives is a self-interacting gauge field plus a free, still-massive carrier, which is why that phrase caused so much trouble (§4). No line here is unchanged by its coupling. What the coupling generally does not do is create a mass: mass lives in the first two pieces, and only two mechanisms move it into the third — a vev multiplying $g^{2}A^{2}\lvert\phi\rvert^{2}$, and a vev multiplying a Yukawa.

2. Lagrangian, action, equation: one formula, used three ways

A Lagrangian is a density; an action is that density added up.

$$ S[\phi]=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\;\mathcal{L}\bigl(\phi,\partial\phi\bigr) $$

what you do what you call the result
write it down at a point the Lagrangian, $\mathcal{L}$
integrate it over a region the action, $S$
vary the action and set $\delta S=0$ the equation of motion

So "does a Lagrangian exist here" and "does an action exist here" is one question asked twice. Every density in this corpus is local, a local density can always be integrated, and a functional that is an integral of a local density hands its integrand back. The two words are interchangeable in this audit, and the tables write the density — the object at a point — with the action being its integral.

The relation that can genuinely fail is the next one, and it is not this one. From a Lagrangian you may ask for canonical momenta and a Hamiltonian; Maxwell's Hessian is degenerate, so the Maxwell line has a Lagrangian and no Hamiltonian and pays with a constraint and the Lorenz condition. Lagrangian without Hamiltonian is ordinary; Hamiltonian without Lagrangian is not. Nothing here is of the second kind.

Where an action can exist with no Lagrangian — not a case here. A functional that is not the integral of any local density has no integrand to point at: a one-loop effective action carrying $\operatorname{Tr}\log$, Hamilton's principal function, an action defined only as a lattice limit. No line of this ledger is of that kind.

And one case where both fail together. Helmholtz's condition is asked of the equation and decides whether any density has it as its variation (physics.md:487). A non-variational equation has neither a Lagrangian nor an action — the viscous term, and the only entry in the audit where the failure is of this kind.

4. The three pieces

One formula, and every table in §6 is a row of it. The split is not decoration: it is the only way to say what a coupling does without the sentence collapsing into "it depends".

the piece what it is what it contains what it depends on

4.1 "Without the coupling" means: delete the third piece

And what survives depends on which piece you are looking at. That is the whole of the confusion, and it dissolves once the three are separated:

the piece survives? so the coupling-off theory is
the connection yes, entirely — including the commutator if the group is non-abelian pure Yang–Mills: self-interacting, not a free photon
the carrier yes, entirely — including its mass a free massive field, which is a perfectly good theory
the interaction no — this is the piece deleted nothing left of it

Three consequences, and they are the audit's answer to its own earlier mistakes.

  1. The coupling always changes the Lagrangian. Deleting the third piece removes real terms on every line where it is not empty. No row in §6 shows a density that the coupling leaves alone.
  2. The coupling is not a choice to be made per line. Once the group and the carrier are fixed, the third piece is fixed with them: the carrier says which generator acts, the group says whether there is one $g$ for all representations or a charge per field. The one genuine variable is whether the group is gauged — local, needing a connection — or left global, which needs none.
  3. The mass lives in the first two pieces. This is the fact that wrecked the earlier column designs: a mass term is not a coupling term. Nearly every mass in this corpus is written with the connection switched off, and the two exceptions are vev effects.

4.3 The two mass columns, and the one-line diagnostic

Each row carries two mass columns because the answer changes when the third piece is added, and it changes in only three ways:

agree — "the same", same symbol a parameter the category cannot explain electromagnetism, the quark, every free row

The diagnostic is one line: if the two mass cells agree, the mass is a parameter; if they differ, the coupling is its author. That is the whole content of the second pair of columns — one column can record a number but cannot say who wrote it.

5. Why a line is empty

Six conditions, and a line falls when one fails. A genuine action needs a carrier of the spin; a representation of the group of that carrier's dimension and reality type; an invariant of the required dimension; a density built from the algebra's own elements; an integrable density (Helmholtz); and, for a classical or non-quantum line, a classical limit to take.

condition what fails the lines
a carrier there is no carrier of that spin in this roster $\geq\tfrac52$
a density in the algebra's own elements the field is imported and the object that writes its invariant is not built $\tfrac32$
the group's representation no representation of that dimension, so no field none
invariance the field exists and the term is forbidden no line — three terms are forbidden
Helmholtz no density has that term as its Euler–Lagrange expression no line — the viscous term only
a classical limit an anticommuting field has no real-valued one NRNQ $\tfrac12$

Three of the six empty the term and not the line, and that distinction is the whole audit. The strong spin-$0$ line declines a fundamental coloured scalar and still carries four actions. The primordial spin-$1$ line forbids its mass and still carries the action. The NRNQ spin-$1$ line forbids viscosity and still carries the classical fluid functional. A declined term is not an empty line.

6. The categories, one section at a time

Ordered as the corpus orders them. Each section states the group, the carrier situation and the table for that category alone. The tables carry the same nine columns; the category is the heading rather than a column, since it would otherwise repeat thirty-seven times.

Every row is one carrier in one category, split three ways. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ is the connection's density with no matter in it; $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{matter}}$ is the carrier's density with $\partial_{\imath}$ everywhere and its own mass; $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coupling}}$ is what needs both. A — in the first column on a matter row is not an omission: the gauge piece of that sector is the vector row of the same section, and the cell says so.

Three reasons a $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coupling}}$ cell is empty, and they are different statements. Nothing is gauged — $\mathrm{SU}(1)=\{e\}$ has no generator (§6.2, §6.9 vector, §6.11). Nothing has a charge — the carrier is uncharged but the group is not trivial; this happens only to the photon (§6.7 vector), because the photon is the connection. The current is on another row — the connection's own row cannot carry its own current (§6.3, §6.4 vector rows).

The two mass columns then ask one question of the same row, twice: what mass does the free density carry, and what mass is there once the third piece is in. They agree, differ by "yes", or differ by "re-described" — §4.3, and the three cases exhaust the ledger.

Read one row at a time and the audit's claim becomes checkable in a single line. Take the quark: a free massive fermion, plus one term $g_{s}\bar\Xi\gamma A t\Xi$. The coupling changed the Lagrangian and left the mass where it was. Take the $W$: a massless vector, plus $g^{2}A^{2}\lvert\phi\rvert^{2}$, which at the vev is the mass. Same structure, different answer, and both visible without prose.

6.2 Force-Free

The group: $\mathrm{SU}(1)=\{e\}$. $\dim=0$, rank $0$. Lines: 3 (3 occupied, 0 empty). No generator is gauged, so nothing is a connection.

No force means nothing is gauged. $\mathrm{SU}(1)=\{e\}$ is not a small group; it is the absence of a group, and its consequences are exact: no generator, no connection, no field strength built from a shift, and no redundancy in the potential. What the category carries is a medium, not a potential — the Proca field read as $A_{\imath}=r\,u_{\imath}$, a density and a velocity (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-spin-one-fluid.md:17). The density is one field term and one mass term, with $\tilde F=\mathrm{Vect}\bigl((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi\bigr)$ a pure vector (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-proca-equation.md:12).

This is where an earlier version of this file was wrong, twice. It listed the spin-$1$ line as "free Maxwell; free Proca" and described the two as differing by the mass term alone. Both are wrong here. Maxwell is a gauge theory and belongs to Electromagnetism; at $\nu_m\to0$ the Proca equation acquires the shift symmetry, loses a mode, and its potential becomes a connection — the object changes category rather than staying here at zero mass. The corpus says it in as many words: "In the massless theory $S=0$ can be reached from any potential by a local transformation, so it is a choice of representative; here no local transformation exists" (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-proca-equation.md:115), and "No pure-gradient solution" (:248).

The mass is therefore not decoration on this line; it is the term that makes the field a field. The polarisation count is the check: three for Proca, two for Maxwell (articles_physics/the-proca-equation-massive-spin-1-in-biquaternionic-form.md:5).

6.3 Primordial

The group: $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. $\dim=15$, rank $3$. Lines: 3 (3 occupied, 0 empty). The rung above the strong force; one coupling on fifteen generators.

One coupling, one connection, one scalar matrix. The sector is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ with a single $g_4$ in the representative, fermion and scalar kinetic terms, and the scalar is a Hermitian $4\times4$ matrix — sixteen real scalars — and not a quartet.

Two structural facts decide the rows. No mass term is written for the connection: a bare mass breaks the invariance the curvature term rests on. And the sixteen Weyl fields carry no mass in $\mathcal{L}_\xi$; the mass is in the Yukawa, and above the condensation the minimum is symmetric, $\langle\Xi\rangle=0$, so the group is exact and the fermions are massless. That is why the scalar is the matrix and not the quartet: $\mathbf{4}\otimes\mathbf{4}=\mathbf{6}\oplus\mathbf{10}$ has no $\bar{\mathbf{4}}$, so a quartet scalar could not give the sixteen a mass even after condensing.

The scalar's own quadratic terms are a mass, and they break nothing. $\mu_\Phi^2$ and $\mu_2^2$ are both positive — the corpus is explicit that the origin is the minimum and the group is exact (articles_physics/the-lagrangian-and-the-primordial-force.md:121). A mass term with the sign that does not condense.

6.4 Strong

The group: $\mathrm{SU}(3)=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(\mathbb{C}e_t)$. $\dim=8$, rank $2$. Lines: 3 (3 occupied, 0 empty). The stabiliser of the line inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$.

One Lagrangian, three lines. The rows are spins and the row-to-action map is not one-to-one: the gauge sector and the quark term are two terms of one QCD action, and the quark term cannot be written without $G_{a,\imath}$ because $D_{\imath}$ carries it.

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}} =-\tfrac14\sum_{a,\imath,\jmath}G_{a,\imath\jmath}G_{a,\imath\jmath} +\sum_{f}\bar\Xi_{\mathrm{wfn},f}\Bigl(-i\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}-m_{f}\Bigr)\Xi_{\mathrm{wfn},f} -\frac{1}{2\zeta}\sum_{a,\imath}\Bigl(\frac{\partial G_{a,\imath}}{\partial R_{\imath}}\Bigr)^{2} +\sum_{a}\bar c_{a}\Bigl(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial R_{\imath}\partial R_{\imath}}\Bigr)c_{a} +\mathcal{L}_{cG} $$

In the action the gluon carries no mass and the quark carries one, and the asymmetry is the sector's whole mass content: $m_f$ is a free parameter of the triplet, while a local bare gluon mass is forbidden by gauge invariance. That is a statement about the density, not about the spectrum: QCD confines, the physical states are colour singlets with a mass gap, and "massless in the action" must not be read as "a massless gluon propagates".

The spin-$0$ line is not colour at all. It holds four effective functionals — sigma, chiral pion, Skyrme, axion — and only the axion carries a colour coupling, through the CP-odd $G\tilde G$. 9. What this audit does not claim

It does not claim a category lacks a Lagrangian. Every category has one at every spin that has a carrier.

It does not claim an empty line can be filled by writing harder. The $\tfrac32$ line fails on the algebra's own terms, the line above spin $2$ on the roster, the NRNQ $\tfrac12$ line on the absence of a real-valued classical limit.

It does not claim a declined term empties a line. Three terms are forbidden — two connection masses and a quartet Yukawa — and each sits in a category that keeps its action.

It does not claim spin fixes the functional. Spin, stage and mass are three inputs: Klein–Gordon and Schrödinger are both spin $0$; Maxwell and Proca both spin $1$; Dirac and Pauli both $\tfrac12$. And it does not claim the term count is invariant: a written count is a presentation, and the invariant count is modulo total derivatives, field redefinitions and the equations of motion — sixty parameters to nineteen in the Standard Model.

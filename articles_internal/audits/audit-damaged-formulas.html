Repairing the Formulas: Six Silent Faults and Four Mechanical Passes

Status of this file. Executed twice, and closed on 23 November 2026: the corpus renders $0$ failing snippets. The first pass cleared $498$ failing formulas. The corpus drifted back to $4\,498$ under a later migration, and the second pass — four mechanical steps and one hand list, recorded in §The Third Census — brings the served site to $0$ in $4\,371$ pages and $890\,619$ math regions. The two build defects that manufactured a third of those records are named in §The Build Defects That Look Like Article Faults.

What the file is for. It is the procedure of the last two passes that cleared them: the four mechanical repairs of a parse failure, the second pass over the class that never raises one, and the checks that accept a folder as repaired.

The reader's contract, in five lines.

  1. A formula is checked by rendering it with throwOnError: true, never by reading it. The build reports nothing: throwOnError: false and errorColor: '#c00' are the template's settings, so a broken formula is dark red on a page that exits zero.
  2. The rescan is the only certificate, and its number is zero. A pass that ends at a lower count is an unfinished pass.
  3. The pass restores the notation the article already intended. It does not rewrite mathematics, and where it cannot decide it reports to the author instead of choosing.
  4. Delimiter parity comes before formulas, and it is repeated after every structural edit, because one stray display delimiter derails every later block of the file.
  5. The row count is a second instrument. A matrix whose rows are separated by one backslash parses without complaint and renders as one row, so a scan that reaches zero is not yet a pass.

Introduction

The verdict. A broken formula in this corpus is invisible to the build. The page is written, the article is served, and the reader sees the offending source in dark red where the mathematics should be. Nothing reports it, so nothing gets fixed until someone opens the page.

The scale. The corpus holds about eight hundred thousand math regions. The census at the time of the pass is this one.

articles_physics $628$ $169\,377$ $14\,093$ $376$ in $55$ files
articles_maths $2\,464$ $610\,118$ $31\,549$ $122$ with the internal notes
articles_internal $53$ $25\,809$ $1\,263$ counted with the mathematics

The pass cleared them. The final scan of the three folders reports $0$ failing snippets in the physics articles, $0$ in the mathematics articles, and $1$ in the internal notes. That last record is a mention of the display delimiter inside a code span. It is not a formula and it renders as text.

Most of the work is one fault repeated. Of the $376$ physics records, $366$ are one of four mechanical faults: a script written twice, a command fused to its argument, an operator used as a script without braces, or a control character standing where a command begins. The remaining $10$ need a reading of the formula. §The Four Faults That Need Reading names them.

A sixth class produces no record at all. Every count above is a count of formulas KaTeX refused to parse. A formula it parses into the wrong picture leaves no record, and the corpus carried one such class in number: $90$ lines in $52$ display blocks of $33$ articles, each a matrix whose rows were separated by a single backslash. The matrix rendered, in dark type and without a complaint, as one long row. §The Separator That Lost a Backslash gives the detector and the repair. The class is why a scan that ends at zero is not yet a pass.

What the pass does not do. It does not rewrite mathematics. It restores the notation the article already intended, and it says where it cannot decide. The distinction is the subject of §The Objection, and the Four Parts of the Answer.

The organisation. §What the Reader Sees When a Formula Fails gives the renderer facts a repairer must reproduce. §The Delimiters, and the Four Ways a Formula Escapes gives the four ways a formula escapes the renderer entirely. §The Scanner builds the detector. §The Census tabulates the faults. §The Merge Rule: A Script Written Twice, §The Lost Space: Two Tokens Fused into One Command, §The Superscript on Nothing and §The Control Character: A Command Stored as Bytes give the four mechanical repairs of the first pass. §The Separator That Lost a Backslash gives the fifth, which belongs to a second pass because its fault never reaches the parser as an error. §The Four Faults That Need Reading lists what a script must not touch. §The Order of the Passes and §The Checks give the procedure and its acceptance test. §The Objection, and the Four Parts of the Answer answers the one serious objection. §Summary closes.

1. What the Reader Sees When a Formula Fails

The renderer is KaTeX 0.16.9, loaded from a CDN. article_template.html loads the stylesheet, the library and the auto-render extension, all three at that version, and calls renderMathInElement on the body.

The failure is silent by construction. The options are throwOnError: false and errorColor: '#c00'. KaTeX prints the source it could not parse in dark red, in place of the formula, and carries on with the rest of the page. There is no console message, no build warning, and no exit code. A repairer cannot find a fault by building the site.

Three macros are declared, and one of them is not KaTeX's. \slashed is defined as \not{#1}. \boxed and \fbox are redefined through \fcolorbox, with a black frame and a transparent background, so that a boxed equation gets a border and no fill. The redefinition passes its argument in display style, and that detail is what makes a boxed fraction keep its size. A scanner that omits these declarations reports a false failure on every slashed symbol. A scanner that keeps \boxed native reports no failure, because KaTeX has its own; the difference is cosmetic and not a fault.

A fourth patch is a string replacement, and it moves the error positions. fixStigmaTilde() rewrites every \tilde{Ϛ} into \tilde{\text{Ϛ}\vphantom{H}} before rendering. On version 0.16.9 the plain form renders without the patch, so a scanner can leave it out. If a scanner applies it, every position reported after the first stigma indexes the patched string and not the source. §The Scanner records what that costs.

The one sentence to carry away. A formula is checked by rendering it with throwOnError: true, never by reading it.

2. The Delimiters, and the Four Ways a Formula Escapes

Two delimiters are enabled, and only two. Auto-render is configured with the display pair and the inline pair. Nothing else is a delimiter. The LaTeX openers written with a backslash and a bracket, and the ones written with a backslash and a parenthesis, are not enabled: $648$ openers of the second kind stand in $7$ mathematics articles, and none of them is ever typeset. A repairer who meets them cannot fix them with the scanner of §The Scanner, because the scanner sees no math there at all. Converting them is a content pass of its own, not a repair.

The build protects math before Markdown runs, with one regular expression. build.py replaces every region with a placeholder, converts the Markdown, and restores the regions verbatim. A region is a run bounded by the display delimiter, or two single dollar signs on one line with no dollar sign and no line break between them. Four consequences follow, and each one has been observed in the corpus.

First, inline math cannot span a line break. The inline pattern excludes a newline. A region opened on one line and closed on the next is left unprotected, Markdown then eats its underscores and braces, and the reader sees fragments. A repairer must check the dollar-sign parity line by line, not file by file.

Second, display pairing is file-global and blind. The display pattern is non-greedy but unbounded: a stray display delimiter pairs with the next one anywhere below it, and everything between becomes one display block. Prose included. exercise-plane-wave-propagation-in-a-medium.md carried three prose sentences inside display blocks, and every later pairing in that file was shifted by the same three.

Third, nothing protects a code fence. Protection runs before Markdown, so a fence is not a shield. Two display delimiters inside a fence are taken as one display block, and the placeholder then swallows the fence. Verified on a four-line sample: the fence disappears. This article writes the display delimiter in words for that reason.

Fourth, a table cell is safe. Because the region is replaced before Markdown, a vertical bar inside a formula in a table cell does not split the cell. That is why the conventions allow a bare bar in a formula and never outside one.

3. The Scanner

The detector is a render with throwOnError: true and the template's macros. Four steps suffice. Read the file as UTF-8. Split it on the display delimiter: the odd pieces are display math, the even pieces are text. In each text piece, pair the single dollar signs in order, and report a piece with an odd count as an unbalanced delimiter at that line. Render every snippet with katex.renderToString and report the file, the line, the message and $60$ characters of context on each side of the reported position.

Export the failing snippet, not just its message. The repair is written by reading the snippet. A table of messages alone cannot be acted on, because the same message names a merge in one place and a brace in another.

Read the reported position against the string you rendered. During this pass the scanner patched the stigma before rendering, so every position after the first stigma in a snippet was off by the length of the patch. Thirty-five records reported a correct message at a position that pointed at the wrong brace, and one automated merge landed on the wrong subscript. The fix is to render the string as written. If a stigma genuinely fails in some future version, patch a copy and translate the positions back before editing anything.

Merge, re-render, repeat. One snippet may hold three faults. After each repair the snippet is rendered again: either it passes, or it fails with the next fault, or it fails with a different class, which is the signal to stop and read. The loop stops on the first of the three.

The frequencies are the argument for a script. A fault that occurs $320$ times is not a lapse of attention; it is what an editor produces when it treats a label as two scripts. The repair is therefore also a rule: one object carries one subscript, and its parts are separated by a comma.

5. The Merge Rule: A Script Written Twice

A script written twice is the corpus's dominant fault. The notation writes a label and a component as two subscripts on one symbol, as in \tilde E_{\Phi}_\mu, and KaTeX stops at the second one with Double subscript. The repair joins them.

The join is a comma by default. $$ \tilde E_{\Phi,\mu}=\partial_\mu\tilde E_\Phi,\qquad \tilde S_{\Psi,\pm}^{\natural}=-\tilde S_{\Psi,\pm},\qquad \tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b,y}\in\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}. $$ The first form names the element and the index that reads it, the second names the sector and the sign, the third names the sector, the factor and the axis. In each the two scripts are two labels of one object, and the comma is the separator the corpus already uses between them.

One case nests instead of joining. When the first script is a differential operator, the second is its argument and the pair is one object: $$ G_{\Box_\Phi},\qquad \sigma_{\Delta_\Phi},\qquad \tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}},\qquad \partial_\Phi . $$ A comma here would read as a list of two subscripts and would be wrong: $\Box_\Phi$ is an operator, and $G$ is its Green function. The discriminator is mechanical. The operators are \Box, \Delta, \nabla and \partial; when the first script is one of them, nest.

The script may be a superscript. \tilde S_{\Psi}^{\natural}_\pm carries a superscript and two subscripts. Only the two subscripts merge; the decoration stays where it is. The general statement is that a symbol takes at most one subscript and at most one superscript, and the merge is applied to the repeated one alone.

One merge is a brace, not a comma. \bar\mathrm{Sd} fails with Unexpected end of input in a macro argument, because \bar took one token and \mathrm is looking for its group. The repair is \bar{\mathrm{Sd}}. The signal is the message: a double script means a comma, a missing argument means a brace, and the two are never interchangeable.

The merge is checked by re-rendering and by reading one neighbour. A merge that renders is not yet a merge that is right. The check on meaning is one sentence in the same article that writes the same object in full. If the article nowhere writes it in full, the merge is reported to the author instead of applied.

6. The Lost Space: Two Tokens Fused into One Command

A space that disappeared fuses two tokens into one command. \lVertE reads as a single control word, KaTeX looks it up, and reports Undefined control sequence. The repair is the space: \lVert E.

The split is chosen, not guessed. The rule that works is to take the longest prefix that is a defined command and check that the remainder begins a new token. \lVertE splits at \lVert, leaving E, a vector. \Box_\PhiG splits at \Box_\Phi, leaving G, the Green function. \wedgeA splits at \wedge, leaving A, a form.

Three splits need the sentence. \rVerte is \rVert followed by the exponential that carries the phase. \lambdaiR is \lambda followed by the mode label iR. \cdoti is \cdot followed by the imaginary unit. Each is confirmed by the surrounding formula and not by the split alone.

The census of the class. Eleven sites of \lVertE, six of \lVertH, three of \rVerte, two each of \lambdaiR and \PhiG, one each of \lVertv, \lVertA, \intk, \Phif and \cdoti.

A space before a command matters where a name ends in a letter. \lVert and \rVert are the common carriers here, because the norm bar is written next to the object it measures and the two are easy to run together. The same fault appears once in the mathematics on \bar: \barA for \bar A.

7. The Superscript on Nothing

A space cannot take a superscript, and KaTeX 0.16 says so obscurely. \;^{*} fails with Got group of unknown type: 'internal', which names an internal node and not the space. The repair is an empty group: {}^{*} takes the script and is invisible.

Two sites in the corpus, one in each form. The involution of the tensor square was written \sigma_1=\;^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}; the transpose was written \operatorname{adj}=\,^{\mathsf T}. Both are the same fault and both take the same repair.

A prime is a superscript, so a prime followed by an exponent collides. e^{i\theta}'^{2} fails with Double superscript. The convention forbids q'_0^2 for the same reason. The repair separates the two scripts with an empty group: $$ e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}{}^{2},\qquad e^{i\theta}{}'{}^{2}. $$ Both forms render, and the second keeps the prime visibly on the group. Five sites in the mathematics articles took this repair.

Do not delete the prime. The prime distinguishes the second root from the first in the polar decomposition, and dropping it to make the formula render destroys the statement. The empty group is the repair; removal is not.

8. The Control Character: A Command Stored as Bytes

Some commands were stored as bytes, not as letters. A tab followed by ilde is \tilde with its backslash and its t replaced by one character. The same happened to \frac (form feed), \varrho (vertical tab), \breve and \begin (backspace).

The cause is a string escape. In a replacement string, \t, \f, \v and \b are control characters and not letters. Any script that builds TeX through such a string corrupts every formula it touches. The writer of the script sees a correct formula on screen and a broken one in the file.

The detector is a byte scan. Read each file as bytes and report every byte below $32$ other than $9$, $10$ and $13$. The scan is one line, it costs nothing, and it catches a fault that defeats every rule of this article, because the text looks right until the bytes are read.

The repair is the letter. Tab becomes \t, form feed becomes \f, vertical tab becomes \v, backspace becomes \b. Eight physics records come from one article, the Lorentz rotor, where $20$ bytes were corrupted into tabs, vertical tabs and one form feed. Five records in two files on the mathematics side come from a backspace, in \breve and in \begin.

Scan first, repair second. The byte scan comes before every other pass, because control characters break the pairing of braces and the splitting of scripts, and a scanner that reads them as whitespace reports one fault five times.

9. The Separator That Lost a Backslash

This fault is silent twice over: it raises no error and it turns nothing red. A matrix whose rows are separated by one backslash parses. The backslash standing before the line break is the command for a space, the environment receives no separator, and the entries meant for two rows arrive as one. The page is served, the matrix is drawn as a single row, and the scan of §The Scanner reports nothing, because throwOnError has no complaint to make. Every instrument of the first pass is blind to this class by construction.

The two forms differ by one character. The first row is written Q_t-Q_z & -Q_x-iQ_y\ in the broken form and Q_t-Q_z & -Q_x-iQ_y\\ in the repaired one, and the second is what the display below carries.

$$ \begin{pmatrix} Q_t-Q_z & -Q_x-iQ_y\\ -Q_x+iQ_y & Q_t+Q_z \end{pmatrix} $$

Three facts combine to hide it. A backslash before a line break is a legal command, so the parse succeeds. An environment accepts any number of entries per row and neither pads nor complains, so the structure does not object. And the damage is a wide row rather than a wrong glyph, so no character in the page is unfamiliar and the eye that has not been told what shape the matrix should have reads past it.

The cause is a string that halves a backslash. This is the sibling of §The Control Character: A Command Stored as Bytes, and the two faults come from one habit. A replacement string is not raw, so \\ in the script's source is one backslash and \\\\ is two; a script written with the first where it needed the second halves every separator it writes, exactly as \t written where \\t was meant turns a command into a tab. The control character changes a glyph; this one removes a character and leaves everything else in place, which is why it survives a build, a scan and a reading of the source.

The census, on the state before the pass. Thirty-three articles carried the fault, in $52$ display blocks and $90$ lines. The environments were aligned in $36$ blocks, pmatrix in $14$, cases in $1$ and array in $1$. Matrix Representations and Conventions carried $13$ of the lines in $4$ blocks, and the exercise on quantum channels and the article on left-right symmetric fermions carried $6$ each. Two of the blocks, holding $4$ lines, stood in The Newman–Penrose Formalism in Biquaternionic Form and were read and repaired by hand while that article was under review; the remaining $86$ lines in $50$ blocks of $32$ articles are the mechanical pass.

The detector is a row count, and it is not a parse. For each display block that contains an environment, count the separators the source declares — every \\, with or without a bracket argument — and count the rows the renderer produces, which is the number of <mtr elements in the returned HTML. A block whose rendered count is below its declared count is fused. A count above is not a fault: a block holding two environments, or a matrix inside an aligned block, legitimately produces more rows than it declares separators, and the pass reports the deficit alone.

The one false positive to expect. A \\ at the top level of a display, before or after an environment, is a line break and is legal. It is counted as a declared separator, the renderer honours it without a <mtr, and the block is flagged although it is correct. One record survived the corpus pass: \tilde Q\ \longmapsto\\ standing immediately before a matrix in The Lorentz Group as Biquaternion Norm Automorphisms. The repair is to read the block and dismiss the record. Counting per environment would remove the false positive at the cost of a parser for the environment tree, which one record does not justify.

The repair is mechanical. On every line of a display block that contains an environment and whose last character is a backslash not preceded by another, append one backslash. Nothing else moves: no entry, no brace, no bracket argument. The block is re-rendered at once and its row count is recomputed; after the pass the counts must be equal or above, and the deficit must be zero.

The guard is the length of the trailing run, not the last character. The pass acts on a run of exactly one backslash. A run of two is already a separator and is left alone, which makes the pass idempotent: a second run changes nothing. A longer odd run is a separator followed by a space and is reported for reading rather than repaired, because the author's intent there is not mechanical. There were none in the corpus.

The pass leaves two classes alone on purpose. A display block with no environment is not read, because a trailing backslash there is a space and the author may have wanted one: sixteen such lines stand in the physics articles, all chains and conjunctions written across source lines, and all of them render as intended. And a line outside a display block is not read at all, since a lone backslash in prose or in an inline formula is a space, not a row.

The pass was verified on the whole corpus. The $14\,145$ display blocks of the physics folder were re-rendered under throwOnError: true, $354$ of them carrying at least one environment, and no block was left with a deficit; the mathematics and internal folders were scanned for the class and carried none. The rescan of §The Order of the Passes is unchanged at zero, which is the expected outcome and not the check: this class never had a nonzero count there to begin with.

10. The Four Faults That Need Reading

A delimiter pair with one side missing. \exp\left(i\theta_{\mathrm{cph}}), fails, and the message asks for \right. The repair is the closing delimiter. The scanner reports the failure at end of input, so the position is useless and the reading is the whole job.

Matrix rows separated by a space instead of a double backslash. \begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4\ 5&6&7&8… puts the whole matrix on one row, and the failure appears at the first backslash. The repair is \\ between the rows. The scanner cannot see the intent; the four-by-four shape of the entries gives it.

Prose inside a display block. Three display blocks in one exercise contained a sentence each, and the blocks around them were closed in the wrong places. The repair is to close the display before the sentence and reopen it after. The repairer must count the delimiters again after the edit, because every later block in the file shifted.

A LaTeX array inside a display, used as a table. One article built a four-column table with \begin{array} and \hline inside a row. It fails, and the message says that \hline is valid only inside an array environment; moved out of the row, it breaks in another way. The conventions are explicit: tables are Markdown, never LaTeX array. The repair is a Markdown table in place of the display, with the cells in math.

The common feature. In all four the scanner localises the fault and not the repair. Each one is written out by hand and re-rendered on the spot.

11. The Order of the Passes

Pass zero is the byte scan. It runs on the three folders and repairs the control characters. Nothing else is attempted until it reports zero.

Pass one is the delimiter census. For each file, count the display delimiters and check the parity, then check the single-dollar parity line by line. A file with an odd count is inspected at the last unpaired site, and the structural repair is made before any formula is touched. A file repaired for structure is re-censused, because the repair moves every later block.

Pass two is the scanner. It produces one record per failing snippet with its file, line, message and source.

Pass three is the automated repair, in four steps. Scripts first, because they are $320$ of the $376$ and the rule is the safest. Spaces second, because the split test is local. Superscripts on nothing third. Braces fourth. Each step re-renders its own snippet and stops on a change of class.

Pass four is by hand. The records that remain after pass three are the ten of the delimiter and structure class, listed in §The Four Faults That Need Reading, and the handful of splits that need the sentence.

Pass five is the rescan. Its count must be zero. A pass is not finished at a lower count.

The separator pass stands outside that chain. It cannot join pass three, because its fault never reaches the scanner's output. It runs over every display block of whatever folder it is given, failing or not, and its acceptance is a deficit count rather than an error count. Because it is guarded by the length of the trailing run it is idempotent, so it may be rerun after any structural edit that changes the shape of a display block.

The chain of the second campaign is the one to run again, and it is slightly different. Pass zero repairs the build and the scanner, not the articles, because a pipeline that eats a < or a fence manufactures records faster than a repair removes them. Then braces and fused words, curated splits, line joins, and the second script as an empty group, each followed by a full rescan. §The Recipe for the Next Run gives the order, the counts and the acceptance numbers.

12. The Checks

The scan is the acceptance test, and its number is zero. The one record that survives is named and dismissed: a mention of the display delimiter inside a code span is not a formula, and trace-migration.md carries the only one.

The row count is the second acceptance test, and its number is a zero deficit. Render every display block that carries an environment and compare the rows produced with the separators declared. The check is one pass over the corpus, and it finds the class that §The Scanner cannot see. It reports one expected false positive, named in §The Separator That Lost a Backslash, and a reader dismisses it once and for good.

The delimiter audit uses the site's own expression. Protect each file with build.py's pattern and count the delimiters that survive. Expect none, apart from mentions in code spans. This check catches the file that renders today and breaks tomorrow, which the KaTeX scan alone does not.

The proof-end grep must return zero. The plan states the command, and this pass stripped three marks that survived it: two in the tensor product article and one in the entanglement article.

Three sites are read in the rendered page, one per class. A merge, a split and a structural repair. The scan certifies syntax; the eye certifies that the formula now says what the sentence says it says.

No scratch file stays in the repository. The scanner, the repair script and the test samples live in a temporary directory outside the repository and are deleted when the pass ends. The repository holds the repaired articles and this note.

13. The Objection, and the Four Parts of the Answer

The objection is that an automated substitution changes mathematics. A script that merges \tilde E_{\Phi}_\mu into \tilde E_{\Phi,\mu} decides that the sector label and the index are two labels of one object. In another formula the same characters could mean the component of a frame element, and the merge would then be wrong.

The answer has four parts. The comma is the separator the corpus already writes between a sector and an index, and $320$ sites use that convention. The one case where the two scripts are not labels, the operator, is excluded by a stated discriminator. The merge is checked against one neighbouring sentence that writes the object in full. And the sites that carry meaning rather than punctuation are repaired by hand: \lambdaiR, the split of \Box_\Phi G, the brace of \bar{\mathrm{Sd}}.

The objection is right about one thing. The rescan certifies syntax and nothing else. A formula can render and be false. That is why the pass is followed by the reading of three sites per class, and why the census is reviewed before the automated step runs rather than after.

14. The Second Census: The Command Taken as a Script

A rescan on 23 November 2026 finds $4\,481$ failing snippets in $517$ files. The certificate of §The Checks does not hold today. The physics articles carry $4\,015$ records in $469$ files, the mathematics articles $448$ in $43$, and the internal notes $18$ in $5$. Eleven further records sit in audit-bad-metric.md, where a backslash and the #1 of a macro body stand inside an indented code block; they are code, not formulas, and they are counted apart.

The scan repeats §The Scanner, with one addition. Every region is rendered by KaTeX 0.16.9 under throwOnError: true with the template's three macros, and code fences and code spans are masked first, because auto-render skips them. The instrument rendered $857\,422$ regions: $178\,054$ in the physics articles, $633\,962$ in the mathematics articles and $45\,406$ in the internal notes.

The census is one table.

class records source before source after
a function command taken as a script $3\,741$ q_\imath q_{\imath}
an inline formula broken across a line break $360$ a dollar, a line break, the rest one line
a command fused to its argument $247$ \epsilon_{\imath\jmathk} \epsilon_{\imath\jmath k}
a script written twice $128$ k_\upsilon_{\mathrm{crd}} k_{\upsilon_{\mathrm{crd}}}

The dominant class is new, and its repair is one pair of braces. KaTeX defines \imath as a function, so a script that holds it alone is refused with Got function \html@mathml with no arguments as subscript. The same symbol inside braces renders. The corpus writes the generic spatial index that way in $3\,741$ places, and $3\,730$ of them are in the physics articles.

The rule is already written in this corpus. audit-index.md states it in its contract: a dotless letter in a sub- or superscript is always braced, and Q_\imath raises Got function as subscript. The migration that introduced the two dotless letters reports its failures against a baseline of 14, not against zero.

The message names a macro of the library, not the article's source. It says \html@mathml, which appears nowhere in the corpus. A scanner that classifies a record by the command it prints therefore files the commonest fault of the corpus under a name the author cannot search for.

The other classes are old, and they have returned. The fused command is §The Lost Space's fault, and the context gives the reading: \epsilon_{\imath\jmathk} is the Levi-Civita symbol with three indices, so the repair is \epsilon_{\imath\jmath k}. The double subscript is §The Merge Rule's fault, and the same comma or nesting applies. The line-broken inline formula is §The Delimiters' first consequence: the inline pattern excludes a newline, the pair is never protected, and $24$ mathematics files carry the $360$ records between them. The two \max take braces. The prime followed by an exponent takes the order the conventions state, q_t'^{2}, which renders.

The repairs above were applied to one article only. The introduction to the biquaternion universe carried six sites in five formulas; it now returns zero on the same scan. The rest of the corpus was left as the table records it, and the pass of §The Order of the Passes then ran over it. §The Third Census gives that run.

15. The Third Census: The Pass Executed

The scan of 23 November 2026 found $4\,498$ failing snippets, and the same scan finds $0$ today. The instrument is the one of §14: KaTeX 0.16.9, throwOnError: true, the template's three macros, code fences, code spans, HTML comments and the page head masked first. Two corpora are scanned and both reach zero: the markdown sources, $874\,981$ regions in $4\,317$ files, and the deployed pages, $890\,619$ regions in $4\,371$ HTML files, which is the text auto-render actually meets in a browser.

The counts fall in five steps. Each step is a separate script confined to the math spans, and each is followed by a full rescan.

The dominant class is the one the scan reports last, not first. KaTeX stops at the first fault of a region, so a region that carries a fused word and a second script reports only the fused word. The $4\,498$ baseline therefore under-counted the second script by an order of magnitude: the census of the $128$ records of §14 became $1\,148$ insertions once the earlier faults were cleared and the scan could see past them. A count of records is a count of first faults, and a pass that trusts it will stop early.

The last twelve records are not formulas. They sit in two audit notes, inside snippets that the build served as text: eleven in audit-bad-metric.md, where an unindented node -e script and a macro table reached KaTeX as prose and carried dollars, \displaystyle #1 and a lone backslash; and one in audit-potent.md, a regular expression written between dollars. Both are fenced or put in a code span, which is the repair of a code block, not of a formula.

The folders that are not pages are out of the count, and they carry theirs. _reserve/ and PLAN.md are scratch material; the build never reads them. The same scan finds $15$ records there, all pre-existing, and they are left alone on purpose: a note that quotes a broken command, or a \mbox, must be able to write it. The acceptance is the site, and the site is the four article folders.

16. The Build Defects That Look Like Article Faults

Two faults of the pipeline produced a large share of the records, and neither is a fault of an article. A pass that repairs them one by one in the markdown would destroy content while reporting success, so they are repaired in build.py.

The first is unescaped math on restore. protect_math lifts each formula out of the markdown and stores it; restore_math puts it back. The restore inserted the LaTeX unescaped, so a formula holding a literal <, as in $a<b$, became an HTML tag on the way out, and the tag swallowed every character up to the next >, dollars included. The page then showed a formula cut in half and an unbalanced dollar where the text used to be. The repair is one call: the restored text is HTML-escaped, so < reaches the browser as &lt; and the math is text again. This is why so many records of the baseline were unbalanced single dollar rather than a KaTeX message.

The second is code scanned as math. protect_math ran over the whole file, so a display delimiter inside a fenced block paired with the next one and swallowed the fence. The block was then served as prose, the fence never reached Markdown, and the page lost its <pre>. The repair reorders the pass: code is protected first and only its body is masked, so Markdown still sees the fence and builds the block, and an empty fence is left alone. The check is the count of <pre> against the count of fences, article by article, and it now agrees on every page of the corpus.

A third defect lives in the scanner, not in the site. Three kinds of text are not rendered by auto-render and must be masked before a verdict: HTML comments, which carry hundreds of formulas in this corpus and are never drawn; the document head, where <title> holds the page title and an escaped apostrophe arrives as &#x27;; and code spans, which hold a dollar that is text. A scanner that skips these three reports faults that no reader can see, and its count can never reach zero. The rule is the one of §The Scanner with three more masks, applied in this order: comments, head, pre, code, tags, entities.

17. The Second Script: An Empty Group, Not a Comma

A symbol takes one subscript and one superscript, and this corpus writes a second one anyway. The form is a projector carrying its label and then its matrix index, as in $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},++Q}$ followed by the index ${2,21}$, or a projector carrying a sign after its label.

The repair of §The Merge Rule does not apply, and the difference is the level of the index. A merge joins two labels of one object into one list. Here the second group indexes the object the first group has already named: $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},++Q}$ is one object and ${2,21}$ is one of its entries. Joining them into $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},++Q,2,21}$ would state that four labels sit at one level. The corpus instead keeps the levels apart, and an empty group carries the second script: $$ \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},++Q}{}_{2,21},\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_{1},\qquad k_\upsilon{}_{\mathrm{crd}},\qquad \digamma_{\mathrm{gmn}}{}_{8}. $$ Each renders as the author wrote it: the two scripts stand side by side at the same size, and nothing is reordered, joined or dropped. The bare form fails with Double subscript; the emended form parses.

The repair is one insertion, and the decision is mechanical. A scanner walks the region, remembers whether the current base already carries a subscript and whether it already carries a superscript, and inserts the empty group when a script of a kind already taken arrives, or when a prime follows a full script run and a subscript follows the prime. Any other token resets the memory, because it opens a new base. The pass changes nothing else: it inserts {} and stops, and 1 148 sites across 264 files take it.

The rule that no comma is needed here is stated by the count. Of the $1\,148$ sites, the second group is a numeric index, a sign, or a short token in every case, and the neighbouring sentence always names the index on the object. A comma would have produced a formula that parses and says something else, which §The Objection calls the one failure a rescan cannot catch.

18. The External Conversions

The external folder is a second corpus with its own build, and it carried its own records. build_external.py writes articles_external/**/*.md to *-conversion.html and touches nothing else; 891 pages come out of it. The baseline scan of the main corpus missed them because the census listed the three article folders only. On the deployed site they held $20$ records, and every one of them was a consequence of the restore defect of §16: the pages had been built before the repair, and the rebuild alone cleared them.

The last five sites are conversion damage, and each is read. They are the OCR-shaped faults that a bulk conversion leaves behind, and none of them is a second script or a delimiter: the command \mathbf with its brace lost, (\mathbf3},\mathbf1,\mathbf1) for $(\mathbf{3},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$; a formula joined by a slash and wrapped in a code span at the wrong end, which left one dollar inside the span and one outside; two formulas cut by a carriage return inside a command, \rangle stored as the return and angle, and \nabla stored as a newline and abla, with \to stored as a tab and o; one command stored as a byte, the form feed of \frac; and two stray dollars in a page of two-column OCR text, removed because no formula survives around them.

The rule the external folder teaches is the one about the scanner. A conversion is text until it is rendered, and a rescan of the sources alone would have called this folder clean while the site showed twenty red lines. The deployed pages are the corpus that matters, and §The Checks scans them.

19. The Recipe for the Next Run

The instrument comes first, and it is three files. A renderer harness, a chunker that reproduces auto-render's boundaries, and a list of markdown files. The chunker masks, in this order, HTML comments, the head, pre, code, then strips tags and decodes entities; it pairs display delimiters inside a chunk, then single dollars line by line, and renders every pair with the template's macros. Its output is one row per failing snippet: file, message, source.

Then the four passes, in this order, each followed by a full rescan. Braces and fused words; the curated splits that definedness allows; the joins of a formula broken across a newline; the second script as an empty group. Each pass edits the math spans only, and each is idempotent, so a rerun of the whole chain on a repaired corpus reports no edit.

The acceptance is three numbers, all zero. Failing snippets on the deployed pages; unbalanced single dollars over the same pages; pages whose <pre> count differs from their fence count. The first is the certificate, the second catches the file that renders today and breaks tomorrow, and the third catches the block the math pass swallowed.

The index is a copy, and it is checked as one. index.md is written by sync_index() from the introduction to the biquaternion universe, with only the first heading replaced by the landing title. The check is the deployed pair: the <article> body of index.html equals the body of the introduction page, character for character, and the two H1 differ. It holds today at $44\,093$ characters and one line of difference.

A sample is read in the page, one per class. A merge, a split, a join, a second script and a structural repair. The scan certifies syntax; the eye certifies that the formula says what the sentence says it says.

Summary

The pass of this note cleared $498$ failing formulas; the migration that followed raised the count to $4\,498$, and the second pass of 23 November 2026 clears it to $0$. The $498$ were $376$ in the physics articles, measured on the first scan, and $122$ in the mathematics articles and the internal notes. The $4\,498$ were $4\,498$ records of first faults over $857\,423$ regions; five steps took them to zero, and the deployed site — $4\,371$ pages, $890\,619$ regions, external conversions included — renders with no red line.

Four classes hold $366$ of the $376$, and four rules clear them. A script written twice takes a comma, or a nesting when the first script is a differential operator. A command fused to its argument takes a space, chosen by the longest defined prefix. An operator that carries a script without braces takes them. A control character takes its letter back. The fifth class, delimiter and structure, holds the last $10$ and is repaired by reading. Two rules serve the mathematics articles: an empty group carries a script that a space cannot carry, and the same empty group separates a prime from an exponent that follows it.

The detector is a render, not a reading. KaTeX at 0.16.9 with throwOnError: true and the template's three macros. The build never reports a fault, so a scanner is the only instrument.

One class is invisible to the render check and needs a second instrument. A matrix whose rows are separated by a single backslash parses as one row: no message, no red type, and a wrong picture. $90$ lines in $52$ blocks of $33$ articles carried it. The detector compares the rows a block declares with the rows it renders, and the repair appends one backslash where the trailing run is exactly one.

The delimiters fail before the formulas do. One stray display delimiter derails the rest of a file, and a code fence does not protect one. The parity check comes before the formula check, and it is repeated after every structural edit.

Four faults cannot be automated. A missing \right, a matrix with its rows fused, prose inside a display, and a LaTeX array used as a table. Each is read, repaired and re-rendered on the spot.

The last pass is a rescan, and its number is zero. Anything else is an unfinished pass.

The number is a state, not a verdict. The third census found $4\,498$ records in $857\,423$ regions, and $3\,741$ of them were one class: a function command taken as a script. The procedure above cleared them in five steps and the deployed site now reports zero; a later migration can raise the count again, and §The Recipe for the Next Run is the way back.

Two of the faults were the build's, and they are fixed where they started. Restored math is now HTML-escaped, so a literal < stays text, and code is protected before math, so a fence is never swallowed. A third fault was the scanner's own: comments, page head and code spans are masked before a verdict. Without those three repairs a rescan can never reach zero, and a pass would spend itself on text that no reader sees.

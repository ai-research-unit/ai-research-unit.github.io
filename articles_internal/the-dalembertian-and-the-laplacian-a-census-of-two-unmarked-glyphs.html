The D'Alembertian and the Laplacian: A Census of Two Unmarked Glyphs

Introduction

Two glyphs, each with one definition, and both written without a sector label almost everywhere in the corpus:

$$ \Box=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2},\qquad \Delta=\sum_{\eta=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\eta^2}. $$

The first is the d'Alembertian and stands 1510 times, 1346 of them with no mark at all. The second stands 1870 times: 215 carry a sector mark, 88 more are bare in a formula whose chart can be read from its coordinates, 430 print no coordinate at all, and the rest are not the Laplacian — differences, increments, powers, the modular operator, and 144 names of other objects.

What this article establishes. What each glyph names, and why the two need the same treatment: a formula that prints sector coordinates carries the sector subscript on both, and a formula that prints none carries neither. The census, by meaning and by chart. The rule that decides the subscript, and the check that the rule is the corpus's own. The 135 sites that carried no subscript and now do, with the physics that justifies each class. The 518 bare glyphs that stay bare, and why. And the 910 occurrences of $\Delta$ that are not the Laplacian at all.

The answer, stated first. The subscript follows the coordinates. A formula that prints $r'_t$ or $r_\eta$ is in the material chart, and its two glyphs are $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$. A formula that prints $h_t$ or $h'_\eta$ is in the informational chart, and its glyphs are $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$. A formula that prints $R_\mu$, or prints no coordinate at all, names the operator in the algebra, and its glyphs are $\Box$ and $\Delta_Q$ — written bare, because there is nothing to distinguish. The subscript states which chart the derivatives were taken in. It never states a different operator: $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Box_\Psi$ are the same operator, and $\Delta_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$ differ by a sign.

The Two Glyphs and Their One Definition

One definition each, in the conventions. Operator and Calculus Conventions fixes both, and fixes the identity between them:

$$ \Box=\sum_{\mu}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2} =\frac{\partial^2}{\partial R_t^2}+\Delta_Q,\qquad \Delta_Q=\sum_\eta\frac{\partial^2}{\partial R_\eta^2}. $$

The identity holds in the algebra, before any coordinate is named: the four partial derivatives are taken in the complex coefficients $Q_\mu$, and $\Delta_Q$ is the three of them that carry a space unit.

On a chart the same operator reads differently. The substitution is the chain rule, and the corpus writes it once:

chart free parameters $\Box$ $\Delta$
$\mathbb{B}$ eight real $\partial^2/\partial R_t^2+\Delta_Q$ $\Delta_Q$
material $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ $r'_t$, $r_\eta$ $-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2+\Delta_\Phi$ $\Delta_\Phi=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial r_\eta^2$
informational $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ $h_t$, $h'_\eta$ $\partial^2/\partial h_t^2-\Delta_\Psi$ $\Delta_\Psi=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial h'^2_\eta$

Three facts follow, and they are the whole of the sign bookkeeping. The material Laplacian is the chart-free one, $\Delta_\Phi=\Delta_Q$, because the material space coordinates are real. The informational Laplacian is its negative, $\Delta_\Psi=-\Delta_Q$, because the informational space coordinates are imaginary and each second derivative carries $i^2=-1$. The d'Alembertian is the same number on both charts, which is why it needs no mark.

Verified at residual zero. On a complex test field of four real parameters, with the material substitution $R_t=ir'_t$ and the informational one $R_\eta=ih'_\eta$, the two chart readings agree with the chart-free $\Box$ to machine zero, and $\Delta_\Psi+\Delta_Q=0$ exactly. The identities in the table are not a convention to be remembered. They are the chain rule.

Why Both Glyphs Carry the Subscript

The two Laplacians are not the same operator. $\Delta_\Phi=+\Delta_Q$ and $\Delta_\Psi=-\Delta_Q$. A formula that prints $r'_t$ and a bare $\Delta$ leaves the reader unable to tell which of the two is meant, and the difference is the sign of the entire spatial part.

The two d'Alembertians are the same operator, and the corpus mistook that for a licence to leave the glyph bare. $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$ and $\Box_\Psi=\partial^2/\partial h_t^2-\Delta_\Psi$ are one operator read on two charts; they are equal, and the sign of the time term simply moves from one side to the other. So a bare $\Box$ is never wrong in value. It is only silent about which chart the sign pattern belongs to — and that silence is what let a formula print $\Delta_\Phi$ beside a bare $\Box$, marked on one glyph and unmarked on the other. The pass below closes exactly that gap.

The mark is a statement about the chart, not about the operator. The same sentence in Operator and Calculus Conventions prints both at once: $\Delta_Q=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial r_\eta^2=\Delta_\Phi$, and the two displays

$$ \Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial {r'_t}^2},\qquad \Box_\Psi=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial h_t^2}-\Delta_\Psi, $$

are headed the special case of the material sector and the special case of the informational sector. The name with the subscript is the one that says where the derivatives were taken.

A formula that prints only $R_\mu$ is in the algebra, and its glyphs are $\Box$ and $\Delta_Q$. The corpus writes both bare there, and writes $\Delta_Q$ when it wants the name spelled. That is the one place where bare is not a lapse but the correct reading.

The five $\Box_\Psi$ and five of the $\Box_\Phi$ are the conventions displays. They are the teaching pair above, and they are the only place in the corpus where the two sector readings stand side by side. The other 58 $\Box_\Phi$ are the second pass.

The last four rows are the trap. A search for \Delta without a subscript returns the whole of the table. 910 of the 1870 occurrences are not the Laplacian: they are differences, increments, powers, and the Tomita modular operator. A patch applied by search and replace would mark them, and that is why the patch below was applied by context and not by pattern.

The Rule

The subscript follows the coordinates of the formula, on both glyphs. If the formula prints $r'_t$ or $r_\eta$, write $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$. If it prints $h_t$ or $h'_\eta$, write $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$. If it prints $R_\mu$, or prints no coordinate at all, write $\Box$ and the bare Laplacian, or $\Delta_Q$ when the name is wanted.

Two consequences are worth stating separately. A formula that prints $\Delta_Q$ beside $r'_t$ is not wrong in its identity — the two Laplacians are equal — but it names the operator in the chart it is not working in; that is the only class of edit below that changes a formula already correct in value. And a formula that prints a bare $\Box$ beside $\Delta_\Phi$ is the mixed case the second pass removes: the glyphs of one formula are marked alike or not at all.

What Was Patched

Two passes, one rule. The first marked $\Delta$ where the formula prints sector coordinates: 77 sites in 34 articles. The second marked $\Box$ in the same formulas, which the first pass had left bare: 58 sites in 26 articles. Three suffixes that named the sector in a long way — $\Box_{\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}}$ once and $\Box\big|_{\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}}$ twice — were shortened to $\Box_\Phi$ in the same pass.

Four shapes account for most of the $\Delta$ marks, and each is a display of the same identity:

$$ \Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial {r'_t}^2},\qquad -\frac{\partial^2}{\partial {r'_t}^2}+\Delta_\Phi=\Box_\Phi,\qquad \Delta_\Phi-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial {r'_t}^2}=-\Box_\Phi,\qquad \frac{\partial^2}{\partial {r'_t}^2}-\Delta_\Phi=-\Box_g . $$

The first two are the material d'Alembertian in its two orders. The third is the same operator with the source's overall sign, as in the parabolic Dirac operator. The fourth is the curved-space version, where the sign of the flat part is fixed by the same chain rule.

The sign-critical class was mislabelled rather than unmarked. Seven sites printed $\Box=\Delta_Q-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$, in Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Dirac Field, The Foldy–Wouthuysen Transformation, The Reflection and the Rotation, and The Spin–Statistics Theorem. The identity is right: $\Delta_Q=\Delta_\Phi$. The name is the wrong one, because the display is in $r'_t$ and the operator beside it is the material Laplacian. Those seven now read $\Delta_\Phi$.

One article was deliberately left alone. Scattering for the Quaternionic Dirac Operator quotes a source's notation throughout, with $\Delta$ for that source's three-dimensional Laplacian and $D_3^2=-\Delta$ among its identities. Marking part of it would leave the article inconsistent with itself, and marking all of it would rewrite a source's notation. It is left bare, and this note is the reason.

What Stays Bare

518 bare glyphs remain, and they are correct. 430 of the $\Delta$ and 1346 of the $\Box$ print no coordinate; 68 $\Delta$ and the rest of the $\Box$ print $R_\mu$.

68 bare Laplacians print $R_\mu$. These are the chart-free $\Delta_Q$, and they include the chained displays $\Box=\partial^2/\partial R_t^2+\Delta=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$, which are the clearest statement in the corpus of the two names for one operator: the first $\Delta$ is the chart-free one, the second is the same operator in $r'_t$. Marking the first would hide the identity.

17 bare Laplacians print $r'_t$, and nine of them are that chain. The other eight are not the Laplacian in that formula: an energy shift $E_0+\Delta$, a component $\vec\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}$, a separation $\Delta x^2-\Delta{r'_t}^2$, the modular operator $\Delta=1$ of two Tomita–Takesaki articles, and the two factorization identities of the scattering article, which are quoted in its source's notation.

3 print $h_t$. One is defined in its own display, $\Delta=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial x_\eta^2$; one is the Helmholtz $\Delta+k_\Phi^2$ of a display in which the exponential carries $h_t$ and the operator acts on $r_x$; one is the Euclidean Laplacian of the Wick rotation, which is on no sector.

The bare $\Box$ falls into four classes, and none of them needs a mark. It prints $R_\mu$, or it is one of the chained displays whose left side is the chart-free operator, or it names no coordinate at all — $(\Box-k_m^2)\tilde\Xi=0$ is written that way in a dozen articles and the reader knows which operator is meant — or it carries a suffix that names something else: $\Box_A$ the gauge-covariant one, $\Box_g$ the curved one, $\Box_{\mathrm s}$ the Euclidean one, $\Box_{\tilde Q}$ and $\Box_{\tilde R_\Phi}$ the variable of differentiation, and $G_\Box$ the Green's function named after its operator.

A third chart is Euclidean and keeps its own name. Three displays print $\Box_{\mathrm s}=\partial^2/\partial\tau^2-\Delta_\Phi=-\Box$ or its bare equivalent, in Bispinor Fields and the Fundamental Solution, The Biquaternion D'Alembertian, and The Electro-Gravimagnetic Field. The Laplacian there is $\Delta_\Phi$ and is marked, because the space coordinates are still $r_\eta$; the time coordinate is $\tau$, so the operator is not $\Box_\Phi$ and carries no sector subscript.

Five $\Box$ sites in a linearized-gravity article are on Cartesian coordinates and belong to the spin-two wave operator $\Box h_{\mu\nu}$, not to the biquaternionic d'Alembertian. They are left untouched.

What Remains Open

The 430 chart-free occurrences of $\Delta$ need a reading each. They include the chart-free $\Delta_Q$ written in formulas that print no coordinate, and they include the discriminant $\Delta=4-12$, the modular operator, and the increments. A pattern cannot separate them.

Three Laplacians are still spelled as sums. The chiral-condensate article writes $\Box_\Phi=-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2+\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial x_\eta^2$, and two exercises spell the three space derivatives out in full. Every one of them is now marked, but the Laplacian inside them is a sum and not $\Delta_\Phi$. Whether to fold such sums into the glyph is an editorial choice, because the explicit sum is sometimes the point of the display.

The $1346$ bare $\Box$ are not audited one by one. The fourth class above — a formula that prints no coordinate — is the largest and the least examined. A formula that carries $r'_t$ only in the surrounding sentence now reads bare, and that is the right call, but the boundary between "prints a coordinate" and "is about a coordinate" is a judgement, not a rule.

$\Delta_\Psi$ stands at fourteen occurrences in two articles and $\Box_\Psi$ at five in one. The informational chart is almost never the working one. If a Ψ display ever becomes the working chart, the mark is what prevents the sign error, so such a display should carry it from its first line.

Summary

Two glyphs, one rule: the subscript follows the coordinates of the formula. A material formula carries $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$; an informational one carries $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$; a formula in the algebra, or one that prints no coordinate, carries neither. The rule changed 77 Laplacians and 58 d'Alembertians, and shortened three long sector suffixes to $\Box_\Phi$. 1346 d'Alembertians and 518 Laplacians stay bare, and each of them either prints no chart or is not the operator at all. The trap is the glyph itself: 910 of the 1870 occurrences of $\Delta$ are differences, increments, powers and the modular operator, and no search-and-replace can tell them apart.

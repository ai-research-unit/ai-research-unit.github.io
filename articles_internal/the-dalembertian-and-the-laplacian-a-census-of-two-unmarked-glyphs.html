The D'Alembertian and the Laplacian: A Census of Two Unmarked Glyphs

Introduction

Two glyphs, and after this pass one meaning each. The d'Alembertian:

$$ \Box=\sum_{\mu}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2}, $$

and the Laplacian:

$$ \Delta=\sum_{\eta}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\eta^2}. $$

The first stands 1510 times in the corpus, the second 606; both totals count a formula once, and every one of the second is the Laplacian.

A glyph that carries two meanings is not a notation, it is a hazard. Until this pass $\Delta$ was doing five jobs at once: the Laplacian; a difference of two values, $\Delta u$ for a spin fraction and $\Delta S$ for an action; a spinor module, $\Delta=S\oplus\bar S$; a conformal weight; and a detuning, $\Delta=\omega_0-\omega$. A reader who meets $\Delta r'_t$ cannot tell from the glyph whether the Laplacian is being applied or a coordinate is being incremented, and the sign of the entire spatial part turns on the answer.

This pass removes the collision, and the removal is counted. 261 $\Delta$ glyphs were rewritten. 34 became $\delta$, because they were differences. 227 became a calligraphic letter with a subscript that spells the object: $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rep}}$ for the spinor module, $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{dim}}$ for a weight, $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{gap}}$ for a gap or a detuning, and fourteen more. $\Delta$ is now the Laplacian and nothing else.

What this article establishes. What each glyph names. The rule that decides the subscript, and the second rule that decides between $\Delta$, $\delta$ and a letter. The census, by class, with every article named, linked and quoted: 115 rows, no class summarised and left unlisted, and a second article, The D'Alembertian and the Laplacian: The Patched Articles, which lists every article whose glyph carries a $\Phi$, with the count on each glyph. The renaming, symbol by symbol. And the sites that stay bare, each with its reason.

The answer, stated first. The subscript follows the coordinates. A formula that prints $r'_t$ or $r_\eta$ is in the material chart, and its Laplacian is $\Delta_\Phi$. A formula that prints $h_t$ or $h'_\eta$ is in the informational chart, and its Laplacian is $\Delta_\Psi$. A formula that prints $R_\mu$, or prints no coordinate, names the operator in the algebra: $\Delta_Q$, or the bare $\Delta$. A formula whose space coordinates are $\tau$ or $w$ takes $\Delta_E$ or $\Delta_{w\bar w}$. The subscript states which chart the derivatives were taken in. It never states a different operator: $\Delta_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$ are the same operator up to a sign.

The rule is written into the conventions, not only into this article. Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Laplacian Is the Only Delta, states it for the series: a symbol that is not a Laplacian does not wear the letter, a difference wears $\delta$, and a named object wears a letter of its own. This article is the audit of that sentence: it counts every place the letter stands.

The Two Glyphs and Their One Definition

One definition each, in the conventions. Operator and Calculus Conventions fixes both, and fixes the identity between them:

$$ \Box=\sum_{\mu}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2} =\frac{\partial^2}{\partial R_t^2}+\Delta_Q,\qquad \Delta_Q=\sum_\eta\frac{\partial^2}{\partial R_\eta^2}. $$

The identity holds in the algebra, before any coordinate is named: the four partial derivatives are taken in the complex coefficients $Q_\mu$, and $\Delta_Q$ is the three of them that carry a space unit.

On a chart the same operator reads differently. The substitution is the chain rule, and the corpus writes it once:

chart free parameters $\Box$ $\Delta$
$\mathbb{B}$ eight real $\partial^2/\partial R_t^2+\Delta_Q$ $\Delta_Q$
material $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ $r'_t$, $r_\eta$ $-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2+\Delta_\Phi$ $\Delta_\Phi=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial r_\eta^2$
informational $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ $h_t$, $h'_\eta$ $\partial^2/\partial h_t^2-\Delta_\Psi$ $\Delta_\Psi=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial h'^2_\eta$

Three facts follow, and they are the whole of the sign bookkeeping. The material Laplacian is the chart-free one, $\Delta_\Phi=\Delta_Q$, because the material space coordinates are real. The informational Laplacian is its negative, $\Delta_\Psi=-\Delta_Q$, because the informational space coordinates are imaginary and each second derivative carries $i^2=-1$. The d'Alembertian is the same number on both charts, which is why it needs no mark.

Verified at residual zero. On a complex test field of four real parameters, with the material substitution $R_t=ir'_t$ and the informational one $R_\eta=ih'_\eta$, the two chart readings agree with the chart-free $\Box$ to machine zero, and $\Delta_\Psi+\Delta_Q=0$ exactly. The identities in the table are not a convention to be remembered. They are the chain rule.

Why Both Glyphs Carry the Subscript

The two Laplacians are not the same operator. $\Delta_\Phi=+\Delta_Q$ and $\Delta_\Psi=-\Delta_Q$. A formula that prints $r'_t$ and a bare $\Delta$ leaves the reader unable to tell which of the two is meant, and the difference is the sign of the entire spatial part.

The two d'Alembertians are the same operator, and the corpus mistook that for a licence to leave the glyph bare. $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$ and $\Box_\Psi=\partial^2/\partial h_t^2-\Delta_\Psi$ are one operator read on two charts; they are equal, and the sign of the time term simply moves from one side to the other. So a bare $\Box$ is never wrong in value. It is only silent about which chart the sign pattern belongs to — and that silence is what let a formula print $\Delta_\Phi$ beside a bare $\Box$, marked on one glyph and unmarked on the other.

The mark is a statement about the chart, not about the operator. The same sentence in Operator and Calculus Conventions prints both at once: $\Delta_Q=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial r_\eta^2=\Delta_\Phi$, and the two displays

$$ \Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial {r'_t}^2},\qquad \Box_\Psi=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial h_t^2}-\Delta_\Psi, $$

are headed the special case of the material sector and the special case of the informational sector. The name with the subscript is the one that says where the derivatives were taken.

A formula that prints only $R_\mu$ is in the algebra, and its glyphs are $\Box$ and $\Delta_Q$. The corpus writes both bare there, and writes $\Delta_Q$ when it wants the name spelled. That is the one place where bare is not a lapse but the correct reading.

The Rule

The glyph follows the object, and the subscript follows the coordinates. Two decisions, in this order, and the first one decides whether the glyph is a $\Delta$ at all.

First, is the object the Laplacian? If the symbol acts on a field, it is $\Delta$ and takes a subscript that names the chart. If it names a difference of two values of one quantity, it is $\delta$: $\delta u$, $\delta S$, $\delta E$, $\delta r'_t$, $\delta\omega$. If it names anything else, it takes a letter of its own, listed in the next section. There is no third use of $\Delta$. The letter is reserved: in this corpus, and in every article added to it, a $\Delta$ is the Laplacian.

Second, which chart? If the formula prints $r'_t$ or $r_\eta$, write $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$. If it prints $h_t$ or $h'_\eta$, write $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$. If it prints $R_\mu$, or no coordinate at all, write $\Box$ and $\Delta_Q$. If the space coordinates are $\tau$, write $\Delta_E$; if they are $w$, write $\Delta_{w\bar w}$.

The first decision is a filter, and it is the one that was missing. A symbol that has two readings has no reading: the eye cannot separate the operator from the increment, and the subscript cannot help, because it is attached to a letter that has already lost its meaning. The subscript rule of the earlier passes was correct and useless while $\Delta$ still meant five things.

One consequence is worth stating separately. A formula that prints $\Delta_Q$ beside $r'_t$ is not wrong in its identity — the Laplacians are equal — but it names the operator in the chart it is not working in. That is the only class of edit here that changes a formula already correct in value.

How the rule is checked. Count the glyphs, then read the ones that are not written out. A $\Delta$ followed by a subscript is a Laplacian in a named chart; a $\Delta$ written as a sum of second derivatives is a definition of one; everything else must be read, and the census below reads every one of the 163 bare occurrences against the coordinates printed beside it.

The order of the table is the order of the argument. The two largest rows are the ones that made the letter unreadable: a difference, which every reader meets in the first equation of a paper, and the spinor module, which seventeen articles wrote as $\Delta$ while also writing $\Delta$ for the Laplacian. Neither is a small confusion. $\delta$ and $\Delta$ differ by one stroke at the size of a subscript, and a module and an operator differ by everything.

The sweep has two passes. The first rewrote the glyph where it stood as a symbol, and the second read every definition in which the letter named an object in the act of being defined: a module, a weight, a discriminant, a determinant, a horizon function. A pattern alone would have missed the second pass; the second pass alone would have missed the first.

No rename was made by pattern alone. Each row was read site by site, and the count is the number of $\Delta$ glyphs removed, not the number of symbols the corpus now holds: several of these letters were already in use for the same object in one or two articles, and those occurrences are not counted as work done here.

Three letters were left to the reader. $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{dec}}$, $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{NP}}$ and $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hor}}$ are not names the literature uses, and any of them can be changed without touching a formula elsewhere.

What Was Patched

One rule, applied in six passes. The rule is the one stated above. The subscripts were settled in earlier passes; this pass settles the letter.

The differences were the first class, and they are the ones a reader meets most. 34 sites in 12 articles: the spin fractions $\Delta u$ and $\Delta d$ of Hadron Structure from QCD, the free-energy change $\Delta F_{\mathrm{gas}}$ of Maxwell's Demon, the action difference $\Delta S$ of The Path Integral, the increments $\Delta\vec r$ and $\Delta h_t$, the wave-number uncertainty, the energy uncertainty, the probability difference of The PMNS Matrix, the angular impulses of The Einstein–de Haas and Barnett Effects. All 34 are now $\delta$.

The spinor module was the second class, and the largest single one. 50 sites in 17 articles wrote $\Delta$ for the Dirac module $S\oplus\bar S$: "the module $\Delta$", "a direct sum of $N_g$ copies of $\Delta$", "$\Delta=S\oplus\bar S$", "the determinant $\Delta$". The module is not an operator and never was one; it is now $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rep}}$.

The conformal weight was the third class. 48 sites in two articles, Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit and Bosonization: the weight $\Delta$ of a field, its power in the transformation law, $\Omega^{\Delta}$, and the exponent of the inversion kernel. It is now $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{dim}}$. Two $\Delta$ are left in the conformal article, and both are the Laplacian read beside $\Box$.

The named objects were the fourth class. 129 further sites: a gap or a detuning, a level shift, a pairing term, a tuning fraction, a variance, a jet fraction, a Faddeev–Popov determinant or a Jacobian, a Pauli–Jordan function, a spacetime separation, a constraint matrix, a discriminant, a perihelion advance, a decuplet baryon, the Tomita modular operator, the Newman–Penrose directional derivative, and the horizon function of a black-hole metric. Each carries a calligraphic letter and a subscript that spells the object, and each is listed with its count in The Renaming.

One chart-consistency site was the fifth class. Maxwell's Equations in the Biquaternionic Formulation printed $c\nabla^2=\Delta I$ beside a display that already carried $\Delta_\Phi$; one operator was being written under two names in one line. It is now $\Delta_\Phi I$.

One Laplacian was left bare on purpose. Matter Makes Space writes $\Delta=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial x_\eta^2$ in a Cartesian chart, and prints $h_t$ beside it. It is a Laplacian, and the bare letter is right: the chart is Cartesian, and no sector subscript names it.

Three articles printed the material Laplacian without its mark, and the mark was added. In these articles the glyph is read in the material chart: it acts on a field written in $r$, or it stands in a display that prints $x_\eta=r_\eta$, or it states the material form of the operator itself. Thirteen $\Delta$ gained their subscript: the ten of the Helmholtz pair in Harmonic Elementary Particles and the Periodic System of Atoms, where $\Delta\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}+\omega^2\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$ is read on the $r_\eta$; the two of The Nonlinear Dirac Equation, the second equality of $\Box=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial x_\eta^2-\partial^2/\partial x_t^2$ and the spatial square $\sum_\eta\partial_k^2$, where $x_\eta=r_\eta$; and the material form of the d'Alembertian in The Nodal Set and Quantized Circulation. The nodal-set display also carried its two terms in the wrong order, $\Box=\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2-\Delta$, which is $-\Box_\Phi$; it now reads $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$, and the amplitude equation that follows it is unchanged. These three articles have moved to the companion list.

The field-equation pass read the forty-three articles the census left, and twenty-seven of them moved. The question put to each article was not whether it printed the mark but whether the physics changed if the mark was added. In these twenty-seven it does not: the display is a material one, its operator acts on a field in $r$, on an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, or on a kernel whose argument is $\tilde R_\Phi$, and the mark on it states the chart the formula already takes. They are Bhabha Multi-Mass Wave Equations; The Electron; The Proton; The Majorana Equation; The Theta Parameter; The W and Z Bosons; Quantum Chromodynamics; The LSZ Reduction Formula; The Scalar Field Path Integral; Scalar Pair Creation; The Scalar Field in the Centre; The Null Four-Momentum; Wave–Particle Duality and the Three Positions of One Zero; Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave; The Functional Determinant; The Quantized Scalar Field; The Poincaré Group and the Biquaternion Frame; The Neutron; Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation; The Dirac–Hestenes Equation; The Biquaternion Basis of a Unified Field Fibre Bundle; The Rarita–Schwinger Equation; Electroweak Theory; and the four articles of the quantum potential, The Equation of State, The Wigner Function, The Relativistic Wigner Function and The Thermodynamics of a Quantum Fluid.

Four of these agree with the polar form because of this pass. The quantum potential $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Box\lVert\tilde{\Xi}\rVert/\lVert\tilde{\Xi}\rVert$ was written bare in four articles, while The Polar Form of the Klein–Gordon Equation writes it with $\Box_\Phi$. One formula had two spellings, and the four now carry the one the source article uses.

Two definitions were rewritten, not marked. In Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave the sentence $\Box=\partial^{2}/\partial R_{t}^{2}+\Delta$, which with $R_{t}=ict$ is $-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}+\nabla^{2}$, became $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^{2}/\partial {r'_{t}}^{2}$, which with $r'_{t}=ct$ is $-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}+\nabla^{2}$: the same number, named in the chart the field is written in. In The Scalar Field Path Integral the notation row $\tilde{\nabla}=e_t\partial/\partial R_t+\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\,\partial/\partial R_\eta$, $\Box=\partial^2/\partial R_t^2+\Delta$ became $\tilde\nabla_\Phi=-i\,e_t\partial/\partial r'_t+\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\,\partial/\partial r_\eta$, $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$. A notation row that names a chart the article never uses is the same fault as a display that names the wrong one.

The sixteen articles that stayed are listed in the census with a reason each. Their glyph sits in $R_\mu$, in $h$, in the null chart, or over both sectors at once, where a single subscript would be a false claim about the chart. Two of them are not the operator at all: $U_\Box$ is the plaquette of the lattice article and $\Box_{L\times T}$ the Wilson rectangle, and a loop takes no chart.

What Remains Open

No $\Delta$ in the corpus now means anything but a Laplacian. The count is recheckable by one search: a bare $\Delta$ must be a Laplacian or a definition of one, and in the 470 articles it is. The five classes of the previous section are closed.

The rule is enforced by reading, not by a parser. A new article can reintroduce $\Delta$ for a difference, and nothing in the build will stop it. The census is the check: it quotes every occurrence, so a reader can see each glyph beside the coordinates that decide it. Re-running the count is a minute's work, and it is the honest way to keep the zero.

$\Delta_\Psi$ stands at 15 occurrences in 2 articles and $\Box_\Psi$ at 5 in 1. The informational chart is almost never the working one, and most of them sit in a single conventions article. If a $\Psi$ display ever becomes the working chart, the mark is what prevents the sign error, so such a display should carry it from its first line.

361 math spans of 142723 in the corpus do not render, almost all of them double subscripts, where a symbol that already carries a subscript is given a second one. They are quoted here as the articles write them; repairing the source is a separate pass, and it is a rendering fault, not a notation fault.

The mark is on the display, not on the article, and the count that is left is stated rather than hidden. 163 $\Delta$ and 910 $\Box$ stand bare in the corpus: 20 and 88 of them in the sixteen articles that carry no $\Phi$ at all, and the rest inside articles that do. The companion article quotes every one of those lines, so the number is checkable rather than asserted.

One kind of bare glyph is a convention and not a chart. The equation that defines a kernel is written with the bare operator throughout the series: $(\Box-k_m^{2})G(\tilde R_\Phi)=-\delta^{(4)}$ in The Klein–Gordon Propagator, $(\Box-k_m^{2})G_F=-\delta^{(4)}$ in The LSZ Reduction Formula, $G_\Box$ in the retarded-potential exercise. The kernel is one object fixed once, and its defining line names the operator instead of reading it in a chart; the field equations in the same articles carry the mark. A pass that marks the kernel equations has to mark them in all four articles at once, or none, and it is the next thing to do here.

Three of the renamed letters are choices made here. $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{dec}}$ for the decuplet baryon, $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{NP}}$ for the Newman–Penrose derivative and $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hor}}$ for the horizon function are not names the literature uses; a reader who prefers another letter has only to change the table in The Renaming, and no formula elsewhere depends on the choice.

Summary

One glyph, one meaning: $\Delta$ is the Laplacian, $\delta$ is a difference, and a named object carries a calligraphic letter with a subscript that spells it. One rule for the subscript: it follows the coordinates of the formula. A material formula carries $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$; an informational one $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$; a formula in the algebra, or one that prints no coordinate, carries $\Box$ and $\Delta_Q$; a Euclidean or Kähler one carries $\Delta_E$ or $\Delta_{w\bar w}$.

The corpus prints 606 $\Delta$ in 112 articles and 1509 $\Box$ in 167, and not one $\Delta$ means anything other than a Laplacian. 317 of the $\Delta$ carry the material subscript, in 92 articles, and 481 of the $\Box$ do, in 143; those articles are listed one by one in the companion article. 261 glyphs that used to borrow the letter now carry their own: 34 $\delta$ and 227 calligraphic symbols in seventeen letter classes.

The rule is not a preference. A glyph with two readings has no reading, and no subscript can rescue a letter that has already lost its meaning. That is what this pass fixes, and the count is what keeps it fixed.

The D'Alembertian and the Laplacian: A Census of Two Unmarked Glyphs

Introduction

Two glyphs, each with one definition, and both written without a sector label almost everywhere in the corpus:

$$ \Box=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2},\qquad \Delta=\sum_{\eta=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\eta^2}. $$

The first stands 1510 times, 1019 of them with no suffix. The second stands 1906 times, 1404 with none.

What this article establishes. What each glyph names, and why the two need the same treatment: a formula that prints sector coordinates carries the sector subscript on both, and a formula that prints none carries neither. The rule that decides the subscript. The census, by class, with every article named, linked, and quoted: 729 rows, no class summarised and left unlisted. The residue that stays bare, six sites in two articles, each with its reason. And the 919 occurrences of $\Delta$ that are not a Laplacian at all.

The answer, stated first. The subscript follows the coordinates. A formula that prints $r'_t$ or $r_\eta$ is in the material chart, and its two glyphs are $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$. A formula that prints $h_t$ or $h'_\eta$ is in the informational chart, and its glyphs are $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$. A formula that prints $R_\mu$, or prints no coordinate at all, names the operator in the algebra, and its glyphs are $\Box$ and $\Delta_Q$ — written bare, because there is nothing to distinguish. The subscript states which chart the derivatives were taken in. It never states a different operator: $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Box_\Psi$ are the same operator, and $\Delta_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$ differ by a sign.

The Two Glyphs and Their One Definition

One definition each, in the conventions. Operator and Calculus Conventions fixes both, and fixes the identity between them:

$$ \Box=\sum_{\mu}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2} =\frac{\partial^2}{\partial R_t^2}+\Delta_Q,\qquad \Delta_Q=\sum_\eta\frac{\partial^2}{\partial R_\eta^2}. $$

The identity holds in the algebra, before any coordinate is named: the four partial derivatives are taken in the complex coefficients $Q_\mu$, and $\Delta_Q$ is the three of them that carry a space unit.

On a chart the same operator reads differently. The substitution is the chain rule, and the corpus writes it once:

chart free parameters $\Box$ $\Delta$
$\mathbb{B}$ eight real $\partial^2/\partial R_t^2+\Delta_Q$ $\Delta_Q$
material $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ $r'_t$, $r_\eta$ $-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2+\Delta_\Phi$ $\Delta_\Phi=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial r_\eta^2$
informational $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ $h_t$, $h'_\eta$ $\partial^2/\partial h_t^2-\Delta_\Psi$ $\Delta_\Psi=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial h'^2_\eta$

Three facts follow, and they are the whole of the sign bookkeeping. The material Laplacian is the chart-free one, $\Delta_\Phi=\Delta_Q$, because the material space coordinates are real. The informational Laplacian is its negative, $\Delta_\Psi=-\Delta_Q$, because the informational space coordinates are imaginary and each second derivative carries $i^2=-1$. The d'Alembertian is the same number on both charts, which is why it needs no mark.

Verified at residual zero. On a complex test field of four real parameters, with the material substitution $R_t=ir'_t$ and the informational one $R_\eta=ih'_\eta$, the two chart readings agree with the chart-free $\Box$ to machine zero, and $\Delta_\Psi+\Delta_Q=0$ exactly. The identities in the table are not a convention to be remembered. They are the chain rule.

Why Both Glyphs Carry the Subscript

The two Laplacians are not the same operator. $\Delta_\Phi=+\Delta_Q$ and $\Delta_\Psi=-\Delta_Q$. A formula that prints $r'_t$ and a bare $\Delta$ leaves the reader unable to tell which of the two is meant, and the difference is the sign of the entire spatial part.

The two d'Alembertians are the same operator, and the corpus mistook that for a licence to leave the glyph bare. $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$ and $\Box_\Psi=\partial^2/\partial h_t^2-\Delta_\Psi$ are one operator read on two charts; they are equal, and the sign of the time term simply moves from one side to the other. So a bare $\Box$ is never wrong in value. It is only silent about which chart the sign pattern belongs to — and that silence is what let a formula print $\Delta_\Phi$ beside a bare $\Box$, marked on one glyph and unmarked on the other. The passes below close exactly that gap.

The mark is a statement about the chart, not about the operator. The same sentence in Operator and Calculus Conventions prints both at once: $\Delta_Q=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial r_\eta^2=\Delta_\Phi$, and the two displays

$$ \Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial {r'_t}^2},\qquad \Box_\Psi=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial h_t^2}-\Delta_\Psi, $$

are headed the special case of the material sector and the special case of the informational sector. The name with the subscript is the one that says where the derivatives were taken.

A formula that prints only $R_\mu$ is in the algebra, and its glyphs are $\Box$ and $\Delta_Q$. The corpus writes both bare there, and writes $\Delta_Q$ when it wants the name spelled. That is the one place where bare is not a lapse but the correct reading.

The Rule

The subscript follows the coordinates of the formula, on both glyphs. If the formula prints $r'_t$ or $r_\eta$, write $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$. If it prints $h_t$ or $h'_\eta$, write $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$. If it prints $R_\mu$, or prints no coordinate at all, write $\Box$ and the bare Laplacian, or $\Delta_Q$ when the name is wanted.

One consequence is worth stating separately. A formula that prints $\Delta_Q$ beside $r'_t$ is not wrong in its identity — the two Laplacians are equal — but it names the operator in the chart it is not working in. That is the only class of edit below that changes a formula already correct in value.

What Was Patched

Four passes, one rule. The first marked $\Delta$ where the formula prints sector coordinates: 77 sites in 34 articles. The second marked $\Box$ in the same formulas, which the first pass had left bare: 58 sites in 26 articles. The third came from a reading of the displays the first two had skipped: nineteen more $\Box_\Phi$ and thirty-two more $\Delta_\Phi$, in twenty articles, and thirteen displays that lost the bare $\Delta$ of a chained middle link. The fourth swept the corpus for the asymmetry the first three left behind: any bare $\Box$ standing in a formula that names $\nabla_\Phi$ or $\Delta_\Phi$ now reads $\Box_\Phi$ — 294 sites in 106 articles. That is the pass that makes the rule hold on both glyphs at once instead of one glyph at a time.

The chains were the first class. Five articles printed three equalities in a row,

$$ \Box=\partial^2/\partial R_t^2+\Delta=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2, $$

and the middle link asserts $\Delta=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$, which is false as printed: the chart-free $\Delta$ is $\Delta_\Phi$, and the time term does not belong to it. Each of those displays now carries one equality, the material one, with the box marked:

$$ \Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2 . $$

Two of them were worse than that, ending in the tautology $\partial^2/\partial R_t^2+\Delta=\Delta+\partial^2/\partial R_t^2$; those now read $\Box_\Phi=\tilde\nabla_\Phi(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$.

The static and Helmholtz Laplacians were the second class. The first two passes looked for a $\Delta$ standing beside a $\Box$ or beside $\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$. A three-dimensional Laplacian acting on a function of $r_\eta$ has neither neighbour: $\Delta\frac{1}{R}=-4\pi\delta^{(3)}$, $\Delta\xi+k^2\xi=\delta$, $(\Delta-k_m^2)G_\mu=-\delta^{(3)}$, $\Delta\vec A=-\mu\vec J$. These now carry $\Delta_\Phi$, in Exercise: The Retarded Potentials, Hamiltonian Field Theory, The Klein–Gordon Equation, The Parabolic Dirac Operator, The Central Scalar Field, The Time-Harmonic Maxwell Operator, Bispinor Fields, Scattering for the Quaternionic Dirac Operator, and Maxwell's Theory on Non-Commutative Spaces.

The mislabelled chart-free Laplacians were the third class. Seven sites printed $\Box=\Delta_Q-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$, in Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Dirac Field, The Foldy–Wouthuysen Transformation, The Reflection and the Rotation, and The Spin–Statistics Theorem. The identity is right, because $\Delta_Q=\Delta_\Phi$. The name is the wrong one, because the display is in $r'_t$ and the operator beside it is the material Laplacian. Those seven now read $\Delta_\Phi$.

The asymmetry was the fourth class, and it was the largest. The rule says the subscript follows the coordinates, on both glyphs. The corpus applied it to $\Delta$ and not to $\Box$: a display would print $\tilde\nabla_\Phi(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}=\Box$ with the material gradient on the left and an unmarked box on the right, and the same article would print $\Delta_\Phi$ in the next line. That is why the count of $\Box_\Phi$ stood at 82 while the count of $\Delta_\Phi$ stood at 239. The fourth pass marks the box in every formula that already names the material gradient or the material Laplacian: 294 sites, 106 articles, and the count of $\Box_\Phi$ now stands at 396 in 115 articles. No formula in the corpus now prints a material gradient beside an unmarked box.

Five long suffixes were shortened to $\Box_\Phi$: $\Box_{\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}}$ once, $\Box\big|_{\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}}$ twice, and $\Box_{\Phi}$ twice.

Every article's marks are in the census tables above, row by row, with the formula each one prints. No separate list is kept, because the tables are the list.

The Audit

The audit is the census, and it is article by article. Every glyph in the corpus is in one of the twenty-one tables above, each row carrying the article's title, a direct link, the number of occurrences, and the formula that article prints. Nothing is summarised in a count without its list.

Two summary statements, both derivable from the tables. 156 articles carry at least one $\Delta$ with no suffix and 138 at least one bare $\Box$; 151 carry at least one sector mark. The rest of the corpus carries neither: its glyphs are all of the $R_\mu$ or no-coordinate kind, or are not glyphs at all.

What a count cannot settle, a row can. The $\Delta$ column of any table mixes the Laplacian with the differences and the increments of the same article. The row quotes the formula, so the reader can see which is which in one glance. That is the reason the tables are long.

What Remains Open

The 558 bare $\Delta$ followed by a name need a reading each. They include the chart-free $\Delta_Q$ written in a formula that prints no coordinate, and they include the discriminant $\Delta=4-12$, the increment $\Delta r'_t$, and the detuning $\Delta\omega$. A pattern cannot separate them, and the census does not claim to have separated them: it lists them with their formulas and leaves the reading to the reader.

Some Laplacians are still spelled as sums. The Chiral Condensate writes $\Box_\Phi=-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2+\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial x_\eta^2$, and two exercises spell the three space derivatives out in full. Every one of them is marked on the box; the Laplacian inside them is a sum and not $\Delta_\Phi$. Folding such sums into the glyph is an editorial choice, because the explicit sum is sometimes the point of the display.

$\Delta_\Psi$ stands at fourteen occurrences in two articles and $\Box_\Psi$ at five in one. The informational chart is almost never the working one, and twelve of the fourteen sit in a single conventions article. If a $\Psi$ display ever becomes the working chart, the mark is what prevents the sign error, so such a display should carry it from its first line.

Two articles print a formula that the renderer cannot read. Null Electromagnetic Fields and the Shear-Free Congruence and The Chiral Condensate write $r'^2_t$, a prime followed by a power, which the renderer reads as two superscripts. Those two census rows quote the source in monospace. The defect is in the articles, not in the census, and one pair of braces would fix it.

Summary

Two glyphs, one rule: the subscript follows the coordinates of the formula. A material formula carries $\Box_\Phi$ and $\Delta_\Phi$; an informational one carries $\Box_\Psi$ and $\Delta_\Psi$; a formula in the algebra, or one that prints no coordinate, carries neither. The corpus now prints 240 $\Delta_\Phi$ in 60 articles and 396 $\Box_\Phi$ in 115, and it prints 1404 unmarked $\Delta$ and 1019 unmarked $\Box$. Of the second number, six stand in a material display and each has a stated reason; the rest print no chart. The trap is the glyph itself: 919 of the 1906 occurrences of $\Delta$ are differences, increments, powers, defined quantities or $\vec\Delta$, and no search-and-replace can tell them apart.

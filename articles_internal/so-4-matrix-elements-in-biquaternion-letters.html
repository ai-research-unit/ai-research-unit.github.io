$\mathrm{SO}(4)$ in Biquaternion Letters: Two Versors, Two Sides and One Null Element

Introduction

The claim, in one sentence. Every object of the matrix article has a name in the biquaternion algebra: the group is the pair of unit quaternions up to the common sign, the two $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ summands are the left and the right multiplication, the two ladders are the two sides of the null elements $\vec e_x\pm i\vec e_y$, and the Jacobi weight is the monomial $\lvert\vec q_V\rvert^{m-m'}q_t^{m+m'}$ in the two invariants of the versor.

The article this one rewrites. The $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ Matrix Elements as Two Jacobi Functions proves six things in matrices and Euler angles: the split $\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$, the covering $\mathrm{SO}(4)=(\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2))/\{\pm1\}$, the angular equation and its eigenvalue, the reduction to the Jacobi equation, the factorisation $\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}$, and the closed form of the reduced matrix element. This article takes the same six objects and writes each in the letters of Algebra and Element Conventions: the base $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, the four conjugations, the six subspaces, and the left and right operators $L_{\tilde Q}$ and $R_{\tilde Q}$. The second of the six is the one that changes shape most: in the letters it is the pair of unit quaternions modulo the common sign, and the name $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is not used again in this article.

What translates exactly. Four things. The algebra and its split: the left multiplications by the three real vector units, the right multiplications by the same three, and the two triples commute. The group: the two-sided action $\tilde q_3\mapsto\tilde q\,\tilde q_3\,\tilde p^{\natural}$ with $\tilde q,\tilde p$ unit quaternions, and the kernel $\{\pm(e_t,e_t)\}$. The ladders: left and right multiplication by a null element. And the reduced matrix element: a weight times a Jacobi polynomial, where the weight is a monomial in $q_t$ and $\lvert\vec q_V\rvert$ and the Jacobi variable is $x=2q_t^{2}-1$.

What does not translate, and why. Two things. The general spin: the algebra carries the spinor and the vector and nothing higher, because the spin-$j$ module has dimension $2j+1$ and the algebra has four. The de Sitter group: $\mathrm{SO}(1,4)$ is ten-dimensional and the versor group of the algebra is six-dimensional, so the algebra carries the compact factor and not the group.

Three traps, stated before the argument. Each of the three is a statement that reads well and is false.

the trap the correction
the right multiplication is a representation like the left it is a representation of the opposite algebra, $[R_a,R_b]=-2\varepsilon_{abc}R_c$
the left multiplication is the self-dual triple it is minus the triple, $L_{\vec e_a}=-\omega_a$
$J_+J_-=J^{2}-J_3^{2}+J_3$, as in the textbooks $J_+J_-=J^{2}-J_3^{2}+iJ_3$ for the anti-Hermitian generators

The status of the three. The first two are signs inside the dictionary and are settled in §The Two Copies Are the Two Sides of the Algebra. The third is a normalisation and is settled in §The Product of the Two Steps. None of the three is a defect of the algebra; each is the price of a chosen dictionary, and the price is exact.

The Dictionary and the Two Sides

The Base, the Star and the Real Half

What is taken as known. The base, the six subspaces and the four conjugations are Algebra and Element Conventions. The $2\times2$ image and its forcing are $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Pauli Representation. The two-sided operator and its kernel are The Hermitian Pair of Operators Acting on Two Sides. The identification of the left and the right families with $\mathbb{B}_L$ and its commutant is Families of Matrix Representations, §The Two Sides and §What the Laws Give the Two Families. The identity $[\vec e_j,\tilde Q]=2\,\vec e_j\times\vec Q$ is Left and Right Actions of Operators, §The Commutator of a Unit with an Element. None of these is reproved here.

The one fact used throughout. The star is the dagger, $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q^{*})=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)^{\dagger}$, so the anti-Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ is the algebra name of $\mathfrak{u}(2)$, and the real pure quaternions $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}\cap\mathbb{B}_{V}$ are the algebra name of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ ($\mathfrak{su}(2)$, $\mathfrak{su}(n)$ and the Anti-Hermitian Question in Biquaternion Language, §$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ as a Subspace of the Algebra).

The carrier of the rotations is the real half. A rotation of $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ is a real-linear map, so its carrier is $\mathbb{B}_{H}$, the four real dimensions of the algebra, and the unit quaternions acting on it are

$$ S^{3}=\bigl\{\tilde u\in\mathbb{B}_{H}:\tilde u\,\tilde u^{\natural}=e_t\bigr\}. $$

The complex coefficients of $\mathbb{B}$ are not used by the rotation at all; they are used by the ladder, which needs $i$ to be null.

The Two Multiplications

One element, two operators. An element generates the left multiplication $L_{\tilde Q}\tilde\Xi=\tilde Q\tilde\Xi$ and the right multiplication $R_{\tilde Q}\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi\tilde Q$ (Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Operators). On $\mathbb{B}_{H}$ the six operators

$$ \mathcal{L}_a=L_{\vec e_a},\qquad \mathcal{R}_a=R_{\vec e_a},\qquad a=x,y,z, $$

are real and antisymmetric, so they lie in $\mathfrak{so}(4)$.

The six are a basis of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$. They are independent — the $16\times6$ array of their entries has rank $6$ — and every real antisymmetric $4\times4$ matrix lies in their span, checked on a generic antisymmetric matrix with residual zero. So

$$ \mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathbb{R}\{\mathcal{L}_x,\mathcal{L}_y,\mathcal{L}_z\}\oplus\mathbb{R}\{\mathcal{R}_x,\mathcal{R}_y,\mathcal{R}_z\}, \qquad 6=3+3 . $$

This is the biquaternion form of the paper's $3+3=6$: the two summands are the left side and the right side, and not two abstract labels.

The Two Copies Are the Two Sides of the Algebra

The Bracket, and the Sign of the Right Side

The left side is a representation. The map $\tilde Q\mapsto L_{\tilde Q}$ is multiplicative, $L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}=L_{\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2}$, so it is a homomorphism of algebras and therefore of Lie algebras. With $[\vec e_a,\vec e_b]=2\varepsilon_{abc}\vec e_c$ on the base,

$$ [\mathcal{L}_a,\mathcal{L}_b]=2\varepsilon_{abc}\,\mathcal{L}_c . $$

The right side is an anti-representation. The map $\tilde Q\mapsto R_{\tilde Q}$ reverses the order, $R_{\tilde Q_1}R_{\tilde Q_2}=R_{\tilde Q_2\tilde Q_1}$, because a product is associative, so

$$ [\mathcal{R}_a,\mathcal{R}_b]=\mathcal{R}_{[\vec e_b,\vec e_a]}=-2\varepsilon_{abc}\,\mathcal{R}_c . $$

Two representations of one algebra on two sides are two different things. The left copy is a subalgebra of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ isomorphic to $\mathbb{B}$, the right copy is a subalgebra isomorphic to the opposite algebra $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$ (Families of Matrix Representations, §What the Laws Give the Two Families), and the opposite algebra is the algebra with the bracket reversed. The minus sign is therefore structural and not a slip, and it is the first place where a translation made in a hurry invents one.

The cross brackets vanish. $[\mathcal{L}_a,\mathcal{R}_b]=0$ for all nine pairs, because multiplication is associative: $\vec e_a(\tilde\Xi\vec e_b)=(\vec e_a\tilde\Xi)\vec e_b$. This is the algebra statement behind the commutativity of the two isoclinic spheres.

The cure for the sign is one minus. Put $\mathcal{R}'_a=-\mathcal{R}_a$. Then $[\mathcal{R}'_a,\mathcal{R}'_b]=2\varepsilon_{abc}\mathcal{R}'_c$, the two triples bracket with the same constants, and the algebra splits as two ideals. The cure is the choice $\mathcal{R}'_a=-R_{\vec e_a}=\tau_a$, and not a rescaling.

The Dictionary with the Self-Dual Triples

The computation, on the six matrices. In the base $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$, with the self-dual and anti-self-dual triples of The Lie Algebra $\operatorname{Skew}_4\cong\mathfrak{so}(4)$, §The Self-Dual Split,

$$ L_{\vec e_a}=-\omega_a,\qquad R_{\vec e_a}=-\tau_a,\qquad a=x,y,z, $$

and the six identities are exact.

What this says in words. The self-dual triple is minus the left multiplication by the three real vector units; the anti-self-dual triple is minus the right multiplication by the same three. The paper's two ideals therefore have a one-word description each, and the description names the object the ideal acts on: the left ideal rotates the factor written before the field, the right ideal the factor written after it.

The two dictionaries name the generator with opposite signs. The other article writes $J_a^{+}=-\tfrac12\omega_a$ and $J_a^{-}=\tfrac12\tau_a$, with $[J_a^{+},J_b^{+}]=\varepsilon_{abc}J_c^{+}$. In the letters $J_a^{+}=L_{\vec e_a/2}$, the left multiplication by $\tfrac12\vec e_a$, which is $L_{-J_a}$ for the abstract generator $J_a=-\tfrac12\vec e_a$ of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, $\mathfrak{su}(n)$ and the Anti-Hermitian Question in Biquaternion Language. So the two articles name the same generator with opposite signs, and the brackets follow the naming: $[J_a^{+},J_b^{+}]=+\varepsilon_{abc}J_c^{+}$ against $[J_a,J_b]=-\varepsilon_{abc}J_c$. Both are consistent, because $L$ is a homomorphism and carries whichever sign the generator carries, and a computation that mixes the two namings reverses every bracket it touches.

The Casimir of Each Copy

The sum, from one line. Since $\vec e_a^{2}=-e_t$ for each $a$,

$$ \sum_a\mathcal{L}_a^{2}=\Bigl(\sum_a\vec e_a^{2}\Bigr)L_{e_t}=-3\,\mathrm I_4 , \qquad \sum_a\mathcal{R}_a^{2}=-3\,\mathrm I_4 . $$

The value at spin one half. With the generators halved, $\sum_a\bigl(\tfrac12\mathcal{L}_a\bigr)^{2}=-\tfrac34$, which is $-j(j+1)$ at $j=\tfrac12$. The two copies together give $-\tfrac34-\tfrac34=-\tfrac32$ on $\mathbb{R}^{4}$, which is the Casimir of the pair $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ and agrees with the $-(\tfrac34+\tfrac34)$ of the other article.

Why the number is worth stating. It is the one number that ties the two descriptions: the matrix article computes $-\tfrac34$ from the generators $\omega_a$, and the letters compute it from $\vec e_a^{2}=-e_t$. The agreement is exact, and it does not depend on the chart.

The Group Element Is a Pair of Versors

The Two-Sided Action

The action and its law. For unit quaternions $\tilde q,\tilde p$ the map

$$ \Phi_{(\tilde q,\tilde p)}(x)=\tilde q\,x\,\tilde p^{\natural} $$

is a rotation of $\mathbb{B}_{H}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}$. It is orthogonal of determinant one, to $8.9\times10^{-16}$ on one hundred random unit pairs, and it is a homomorphism from $S^{3}\times S^{3}$ onto $\mathrm{SO}(4)$.

Bilinear, of one degree in each versor. Each entry of the matrix of $\Phi_{(\tilde q,\tilde p)}$ is quadratic in the eight components, of degree one in the four components of $\tilde q$ and one in the four of $\tilde p$. This is the letters' form of the statement that the pair is a tensor product of two defining representations: the left versor acts on one slot and the right versor on the other, every entry is a sum of products that take one coefficient from each, and no entry is a function of one versor alone.

The kernel. The map is unchanged by $(\tilde q,\tilde p)\mapsto(-\tilde q,-\tilde p)$, exactly, on one hundred pairs, and this is the whole kernel: the equation $\Phi e_t=e_t$ reads $\tilde q=\tilde p$, and the map is then the conjugation by $\tilde q$, which is the identity only for the two central units. So

$$ \mathrm{SO}(4)\cong\bigl(S^{3}\times S^{3}\bigr)/\{\pm(e_t,e_t)\}, $$

which is the other article's quotient in letters. The sign is the sign of a quaternion and not of a rotation, and that is the whole content of the double cover at $n=4$.

The Kernel and the Diagonal

The diagonal is the rotor. Put $\tilde q=\tilde p=\tilde u$. Then $\Phi_{(\tilde u,\tilde u)}(x)=\tilde u\,x\,\tilde u^{-1}$, the conjugation, which fixes $e_t$ and acts on $\mathbb{B}_{V}$ by the Rodrigues matrix

$$ \tilde u\,x\,\tilde u^{-1}=x\ \ (x\in\mathbb{B}_{C}),\qquad \bigl[\tilde u\,\vec x\,\tilde u^{-1}\bigr]_{\mathbb{B}_{V}} =\bigl(q_t^{2}-\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert^{2}\bigr)\vec x+2(\vec q_{V}\cdot\vec x)\,\vec q_{V}+2q_t\,\vec q_{V}\times\vec x . $$

Both statements are symbolic identities, exact. This is the corpus's rotor, and it is the diagonal copy of $S^{3}$ inside $S^{3}\times S^{3}$.

The diagonal is the stabiliser of a point. The equation $\Phi_{(\tilde q,\tilde p)}e_t=e_t$ reads $\tilde q\tilde p^{\natural}=e_t$, that is $\tilde q=\tilde p$. So the stabiliser of the point $e_t$ of $S^{3}$ is exactly the diagonal, and

$$ \mathrm{SO}(4)/S^{3}_{\mathrm{diag}}\cong S^{3},\qquad 6-3=3 . $$

The homogeneous space is the sphere of the versor, which is the other article's $\mathrm{SO}(4)/\mathrm{SO}(3)$ written without the word $\mathrm{SO}(3)$.

What the diagonal is not. It is not the set of pairs with $\tilde q=\tilde p^{\natural}$, and it is not the set with $\tilde p=e_t$. The first set is the other isoclinic sphere, read with a conjugation, and the second is a single copy of the left multiplications.

The Two Angles and the Chart

The polar form. Every unit quaternion is $\tilde u=\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,e_t+\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\vec n$ with $0\le\theta\le\pi$ and $\vec n$ a unit vector of $\mathbb{B}_{V}\cap\mathbb{B}_{H}$. The angle of the versor is $\theta$ and the angle of the rotation it generates is $2\theta$; the half is the double cover.

The two angles of a rotation. For the pair, the two angles of the rotation $\Phi_{(\tilde q,\tilde p)}$ are the sum and the difference of the two half-angles of the two versors (Four-Dimensional Rotations, §The Angles of a General Rotation). So the paper's "two angles" are the two polar angles of the two versors, and the biquaternion description needs no more than that.

The six Euler angles are a chart. The other article writes each factor in three Euler angles, six in all, and calls them the double Euler angles. In letters this is a chart on $S^{3}\times S^{3}$: six coordinates for six dimensions, carrying the coordinate singularities that every Euler chart carries. The two phases of the matrix element, $e^{-im\varphi}$ and $e^{-im'\psi}$, live on the chart; the reduced element does not, because it is a function of the angle alone.

What the Matrix Element Becomes

Spin One Half: the Versor Itself

The defining representation is the left multiplication. The spinor is the algebra read as a left module, two complex dimensions at a time, and the left multiplication by $\tilde u$ on it is the defining representation. In the $2\times2$ image, the matrix element of spin $\tfrac12$ is a single entry of $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde u)$,

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde u)=\begin{pmatrix}Q_t-iQ_z & -iQ_x-Q_y\\ -iQ_x+Q_y & Q_t+iQ_z\end{pmatrix}, $$

which is linear in the four components: degree one, and nothing else.

Spin One: the Conjugation Action

The vector representation is the conjugation. The action $\tilde u\,x\,\tilde u^{-1}$ on the three real dimensions of $\mathbb{B}_{V}\cap\mathbb{B}_{H}$ is the spin-one representation, and its matrix is the Rodrigues matrix above: degree two in the components.

The corpus already has this matrix element at spin one. The trace of the vector rotation is $1+2\cos\theta=1+2(2q_t^{2}-1)$, that is $\sum_m d^{1}_{mm}(\theta)$, and the corpus computes it in The Rotation Factor in the Complexified Defining Representation. The letters give the same number from the Rodrigues form by summing the diagonal.

General Spin: a Polynomial the Algebra Does Not Contain

The degree, in general. The reduced element is a weight times a Jacobi polynomial, and both factors are monomials in the two invariants $q_t$ and $\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert$. Its degree is

$$ (m-m')+(m+m')+2(j-m)=2j , $$

so the matrix element of spin $j$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $2j$ in the four components of the versor, for every $j$.

Where the algebra stops. The algebra is four complex dimensions, and the modules it carries are the spinor (twice, as a left module) and the vector (under conjugation). The symmetric power $\mathrm{Sym}^{2j}$ of the spinor has dimension $2j+1$, which is $\le4$ only for $j\le\tfrac32$, and at $j=\tfrac32$ the four dimensions coincide with those of the left module without being that module: the left module is two spinors, and $\mathrm{Sym}^{3}$ is not a left module of the algebra. So the letters carry $j=\tfrac12$ and $j=1$ exactly, and $j\ge\tfrac32$ needs tensor powers, which live outside $\mathbb{B}$.

The consequence for the corpus. The Jacobi family of the other article is not an object of the biquaternion algebra. It is a function of the scalar component and of the norm of the vector part, and the algebra contains no element whose matrix element it is beyond spin one. The two articles are therefore complements and not duplicates: the matrix one supplies the general spin, the letters supply the exact description of the two smallest ones and of the group they generate.

The Weight in the Components

The three substitutions. With $\tilde u=q_te_t+\vec q_{V}$ a unit quaternion,

$$ \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}=q_t,\qquad \sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}=\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert,\qquad x=\cos\theta=2q_t^{2}-1 . $$

So the weight of the other article, $(\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2})^{m-m'}(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2})^{m+m'}$, is the monomial

$$ w=\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert^{\,m-m'}\,q_t^{\,m+m'}, $$

and the reduced element reads

$$ d^{j}_{mm'}=N_{jm}\,\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert^{\,m-m'}\,q_t^{\,m+m'}\, P^{(m-m',\,m+m')}_{j-m}\bigl(2q_t^{2}-1\bigr), \qquad N_{jm}=\sqrt{\frac{(j+m)!(j-m)!}{(j+m')!(j-m')!}} . $$

The check. The form was compared with the matrix exponential of the spin-$j$ representation on five spins up to $j=\tfrac52$, on every pair with $m-m'\ge0$ and $m+m'\ge0$, at one angle, and the residual is $4.7\times10^{-125}$, which is zero in double precision.

What the monomial says. The reduced element depends on the versor only through its scalar component and the norm of its vector part, so it is a function of the rotation angle and of nothing else. The axis and the two chart angles enter only through the two phases. In letters this is the statement that the reduced element is a function of $q_t$ and of $\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert$, the two invariants of the polar form.

The Ladders Are the Two Sides of One Null Element

The Null Element

Two null elements. The corpus's ladder operators are nilpotent elements on the norm cone (Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form, §Ladder Operators). The two of this article are

$$ N_{\pm}=\vec e_x\pm i\vec e_y,\qquad N_{\pm}^{2}=0, $$

and the nilpotency is one line: $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z=-\vec e_y\vec e_x$ makes the two cross terms cancel and $\vec e_x^{2}=\vec e_y^{2}=-e_t$ makes the two squares cancel. So $N_{\pm}$ are zero divisors, and their squares vanish identically.

What the pair produces. The product of the two conjugates is twice an idempotent,

$$ N_{+}N_{-}=-2\,(e_t+i\vec e_z),\qquad (e_t+i\vec e_z)^{2}=2\,(e_t+i\vec e_z), $$

so $(e_t+i\vec e_z)/2$ is idempotent. As an operator the product acts as $-4$ on the two states of $m_1=-\tfrac12$ and as $0$ on the two states of $m_1=+\tfrac12$, so the two steps compose to a projection of rank two: the ladder that starts at the top is annihilated at its first step. The composition is neither the identity nor zero, and both facts are the nilpotency read once on each side.

The Four States and the Two Ladders

The bimodule is the pair $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. The two Cartan operators $\mathcal{L}_z$ and $\mathcal{R}_z$ commute, and their joint eigenvectors are four, one for each pair of eigenvalues:

state vector $\mathcal{L}_z$ $\mathcal{R}_z$
$\lvert++\rangle$ $i\,e_t+\vec e_z$ $+i$ $+i$
$\lvert+-\rangle$ $i\,\vec e_x+\vec e_y$ $+i$ $-i$
$\lvert-+\rangle$ $-i\,\vec e_x+\vec e_y$ $-i$ $+i$
$\lvert--\rangle$ $-i\,e_t+\vec e_z$ $-i$ $-i$

Each pair occurs once, so the algebra is the tensor product of two two-dimensional spaces, one for each side. This is the letters' form of the product of the two Jacobi factors.

One element, two sides, two directions. The action of the null elements on the four states, with the coefficients as computed:

operator $\lvert++\rangle$ $\lvert+-\rangle$ $\lvert-+\rangle$ $\lvert--\rangle$
$L_{N_{+}}$ $-2\lvert-+\rangle$ $2\lvert--\rangle$ $0$ $0$
$L_{N_{-}}$ $0$ $0$ $2\lvert++\rangle$ $-2\lvert+-\rangle$
$R_{N_{+}}$ $0$ $-2\lvert++\rangle$ $0$ $2\lvert-+\rangle$
$R_{N_{-}}$ $2\lvert+-\rangle$ $0$ $-2\lvert--\rangle$ $0$

So $L_{N_{-}}$ raises the first label, $L_{N_{+}}$ lowers it, $R_{N_{+}}$ raises the second and $R_{N_{-}}$ lowers it: each null element raises on one side and lowers on the other. The two raising operators of $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ are therefore $L_{\vec e_x-i\vec e_y}$ and $R_{\vec e_x+i\vec e_y}$, and the two lowering operators are their partners. One element, two sides, opposite directions.

Why the directions are opposite. The right factor of the pair acts on the contragredient slot, so a raising in that slot is a lowering in the element. The opposition is a property of the bimodule and not a choice of sign, and it is what makes the second Jacobi factor of the other article a lowering in the reversed index.

The Product of the Two Steps

The composition is the Casimir plus a Cartan term. In the anti-Hermitian normalisation, with $J_a=-\tfrac12\vec e_a$ and $J_{\pm}=J_1\pm iJ_2$,

$$ J_+J_-=J^{2}-J_3^{2}+iJ_3 , $$

checked on the $2\times2$ with residual zero, and $\sum_a J_a^{2}=-\tfrac34$. The textbook identity $J_+J_-=J^{2}-J_3^{2}+J_3$ is written for generators with $[J_1,J_2]=iJ_3$. The convention here has $[J_1,J_2]=-J_3$, so the generators are $i$ times Hermitian ones and the $i$ lands on the Cartan term. It is the third trap of the introduction, and it is a normalisation and not an error.

The reading in the letters. The product of the two null elements is $-2(e_t+i\vec e_z)$, and $e_t+i\vec e_z$ is the Cartan direction plus the centre. So the composition of the two half-steps is the Casimir plus a term along the Cartan line, and in the letters the two terms are visible as the two pieces of the product. This is the biquaternion form of the factorisation $\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}$ of the other article: the second-order operator is the product of the two first-order steps, and the eigenvalue is what the product does.

Read Against the Factorisation

The two articles agree on the structure. The other article factorises a second-order operator into $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ and reads the result as two ladders in $m$ and $m'$. The letters factorise the same operator as the left and right multiplication by one null element, and the two ladders are the two sides. The agreement is not a coincidence of notation: both are the statement that the algebra is a bimodule and the two slots are independent.

What each form makes visible. The matrix form makes the coefficients $\sqrt{(j-m)(j+m+1)}$ explicit and the eigenvalues readable at any $j$. The letters make visible why the two steps commute and why each is nilpotent, and they name the objects: two null elements of the norm cone, two conjugates, one algebra.

The de Sitter Reading, in Letters

The count, first. The algebra is four complex dimensions, eight real. Its versor group is six-dimensional. The de Sitter group $\mathrm{SO}(1,4)$ is ten-dimensional, and its maximal compact factor is the $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ of this article. So the letters carry the factor and not the group; a de Sitter expansion needs a five-dimensional carrier, and no rewriting inside $\mathbb{B}$ supplies one.

What the factor is in letters. It is $S^{3}\times S^{3}$ modulo the common sign, with the diagonal as the little group of a point. The static patch of the other article is therefore the diagonal in the letters: the observer's little group is the set of pairs $(\tilde u,\tilde u)$, and the angular variables of a static-patch mode are the two variables of the versor, $q_t$ and $\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert$, beside the chart angles.

What is claimed and what is not. The claim is that the compact factor of a de Sitter expansion has a biquaternion name, exact and complete. It is not claimed that the algebra carries the non-compact directions; it does not, and the other article's §The de Sitter Reading, and the Strongest Objection records the same limit in matrix language.

What the Letters Give and What They Cost

What They Give

Five statements that the matrix form hides.

the statement in the letters
the kernel of the two-to-one cover $(\tilde q,\tilde p)\mapsto(-\tilde q,-\tilde p)$, the sign of a quaternion
the two ideals the left and the right multiplication, and their commutativity is associativity
the stabiliser of a point the diagonal, $\mathrm{SO}(4)/S^{3}_{\mathrm{diag}}\cong S^{3}$
the ladders two null elements, one per side, opposite directions
the weight the monomial $\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert^{m-m'}q_t^{m+m'}$

The economy. Each row is one line of algebra in the letters and a computation of several lines in the matrices. The kernel is the clearest case: in matrices it is a computation on a $4\times4$ image, in letters it is the statement that a quaternion and its negative are two names for one rotation.

What They Cost

The general spin. The reduced element at $j\ge\tfrac32$ is a polynomial that the algebra does not contain, so the letters cannot write the closed form of the other article. They can read it — the weight is a monomial, the Jacobi polynomial is a polynomial in $q_t^{2}$ — but not house it.

The chart. The two phases of the matrix element are a chart on $S^{3}$ and not an object of the algebra. A statement that uses them is a statement about the chart, and the letters make that visible by having no place to put them.

The Objection from Translation

The objection. A dictionary that renames every object adds nothing. The six objects are the same six objects, and the article is a glossary.

The answer, in two parts. First, three of the statements are not renamings but computations that the matrix form does not perform: the sign of the right side, the nullity of the ladder, and the monomial weight. Each is an identity in the algebra, verified exactly, and each changes what a reader can see at a glance. Second, the article states the boundary: the letters carry the compact factor and the two smallest spins, and they stop where the symmetric powers begin. A glossary that says where it stops is a map, and a map is what the other article needs in order to say what its Jacobi family is a function of.

Summary

The algebra. $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ is the span of the left and the right multiplications by the three real vector units, $L_{\vec e_a}=-\omega_a$ and $R_{\vec e_a}=-\tau_a$, six operators that span a six-dimensional space, which is every antisymmetric matrix. The left side is a representation and the right side an anti-representation, $[\mathcal{L}_a,\mathcal{L}_b]=2\varepsilon_{abc}\mathcal{L}_c$ against $[\mathcal{R}_a,\mathcal{R}_b]=-2\varepsilon_{abc}\mathcal{R}_c$, and the cross brackets vanish. Each copy has Casimir $-3$ on the four real dimensions, that is $-\tfrac34$ at spin one half.

The group. $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ is $S^{3}\times S^{3}$ modulo $\{\pm(e_t,e_t)\}$, acting by $\tilde q\,x\,\tilde p^{\natural}$. The action is bilinear, of degree one in each versor, orthogonal of determinant one. The diagonal is the rotor, it fixes $e_t$, and it is the stabiliser of a point, so $\mathrm{SO}(4)/S^{3}_{\mathrm{diag}}\cong S^{3}$.

The matrix element. At spin one half it is an entry of $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde u)$, linear. At spin one it is the Rodrigues matrix of the conjugation, quadratic. In general it is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $2j$, and the algebra houses only the first two, because the symmetric powers are outside it.

The weight. The Jacobi weight of the other article is the monomial $\lvert\vec q_{V}\rvert^{m-m'}q_t^{m+m'}$ in the two invariants of the versor, and the Jacobi variable is $x=2q_t^{2}-1$. The reduced element is therefore a function of the rotation angle alone, and the two phases are the chart.

The ladders. The two ladders are the two sides of the null elements $\vec e_x\pm i\vec e_y$: nilpotent, zero divisors, on the norm cone. One element raises on one side and lowers on the other, the two raising operators are conjugate, and the product of the two steps is the Casimir plus a Cartan term, $J_+J_-=J^{2}-J_3^{2}+iJ_3$.

The boundary. The letters carry the compact factor of the de Sitter group and not the group, and they carry spins up to one and not beyond. The matrix article meets both limits at the same place, and that is why the two articles are complements.

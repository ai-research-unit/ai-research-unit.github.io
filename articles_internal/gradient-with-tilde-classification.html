The Gradient with the Tilde: Where the Subscript Stands

Introduction

What this is. A census of every gradient the physics corpus writes with a tilde, and of the sector subscript the corpus puts on it or leaves off. The problems come first. The articles that carry none come last.

The headline. $201$ of the $470$ articles write the tilde, and they print it $4191$ times. $2957$ glyphs carry a sector subscript: $2907$ the material $\Phi$, $50$ the informational $\Psi$. $449$ are labelled variants, $108$ are powers, $192$ are definitions. The remaining $485$ carry no mark, in $42$ articles.

The partition of the $201$. $201=159+42$. The $159$ leave nothing unmarked and are listed in §V. The $42$ carry at least one unmarked glyph. Of those $42$, $6$ print the subscript somewhere and still leave glyphs bare (§I), and $36$ print no subscript at all (§II).

The order of the document. §I and §II are the problems, each article with the form of the offending expression. §III records what this pass changed. §IV is the companion half of the corpus. §V is the $159$ articles with no problem.

The rule. The subscript is printed where the operator is applied at the material parameters. It is left off for a power ($\tilde\nabla^{2}$), a labelled variant ($\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}$, $\tilde\nabla_\kappa$, the kernel label $\tilde G_{\Phi,\tilde\nabla}$), a definition ($\tilde\nabla=e_t\partial_{R_t}+\dots$), and an article that fixes no chart at all and works at the eight coordinates $R_\mu$.

The Glyph

The operator. The gradient is defined on the eight coordinates, with the derivative on the complex coefficient:

$$ \tilde\nabla=e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial R_t}+\vec e_x\frac{\partial}{\partial R_x}+\vec e_y\frac{\partial}{\partial R_y}+\vec e_z\frac{\partial}{\partial R_z}, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial R_\mu}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial r_\mu}-i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_\mu}. $$

The two parts.

$$ \tilde\nabla=\tilde\nabla_\Phi+\tilde\nabla_\Psi, \qquad \tilde\nabla_\Phi=-i\,e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_t}+\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial r_\eta}, \qquad \tilde\nabla_\Psi=e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial r_t}-i\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_\eta}. $$

The subscript names the chart: $\Phi$ the material chart, the imaginary time $r'_t$ and the real space numbers $r_\eta$; $\Psi$ the informational chart. Three spellings count as one mark, $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$, $\tilde\nabla_{\Phi}$ and $(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}$.

Twelve glyphs in eight articles. No glyph was added where the low slot already holds a label, and none was added in the $36$ articles of §II. The spelling of the conjugate. Where the conjugate of a sector-marked gradient stands on one glyph, the sector goes low on the operator and $\natural$ goes outside, $(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}$, rather than the other order, $(\tilde\nabla^{\natural})_\Phi$. The corpus carried the other order in $1198$ glyphs across $138$ articles, and every one of them was respelled in this pass. The two writings are one element: $\natural$ negates the vector part and leaves the scalar imaginary alone, so it carries each sector to itself, and both spellings equal $-i\,e_t\partial_{r'_t}-\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\partial_{r_\eta}$ on the material part. This is a change of spelling and not of algebra, and the census is unchanged by it: the marks counted above are the same marks.

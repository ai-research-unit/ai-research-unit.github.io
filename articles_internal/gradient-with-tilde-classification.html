The Gradient with the Tilde: A Classification of the Physics Articles by Reading

Introduction

What this is. An inventory of the articles in articles_physics/ that write the gradient with its tilde, $\tilde\nabla$, sorted by the reading each article gives the operator rather than by the glyph it prints. The companion article, The Gradient without the Tilde, carries the other half, where the arrow was missing and has now been supplied.

The count. $200$ of the $470$ physics articles write $\tilde\nabla$ or its d'Alembertian $\Box$. $35$ write a gradient with no tilde, and $235$ write no gradient at all. The split of this article is $175$ material, $20$ ordinary, $2$ informational and $3$ carrying both readings.

The three readings. The ordinary gradient is the operator with no reading fixed: the eight-coordinate derivative, written before any sector is named. The material gradient is the same operator read at the material parameters, the imaginary time $r'_t$ and the three real space numbers $r_\eta$. The informational gradient is the operator read at the informational parameters, the real time $r_t$ and the three imaginary space numbers $r'_\eta$.

The caution. The sector subscript is optional. Three research articles write $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$; no research article writes $\tilde\nabla_\Psi$; and $175$ articles are in the material reading. A reader who sorts by the glyph files $172$ material gradients under the ordinary heading. The classification below sorts by the reading, taken from what the article writes and from the element the operator meets, never from the presence of a subscript.

The Object

The operator. The gradient is the element

$$ \tilde\nabla=e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial R_t}+\vec e_x\frac{\partial}{\partial R_x}+\vec e_y\frac{\partial}{\partial R_y}+\vec e_z\frac{\partial}{\partial R_z}, \qquad \Box=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla\bigr), $$

with $\partial/\partial R_\mu$ the formal derivative on the complex coefficient, $\partial/\partial R_\mu:=\partial/\partial r_\mu-i\,\partial/\partial r'_\mu$ (Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Gradient).

The two parts. The gradient splits unit by unit into its anti-Hermitian and Hermitian halves:

$$ \tilde\nabla_\Phi:=-i\,e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_t}+\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial r_\eta}, \qquad \tilde\nabla_\Psi:=e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial r_t}-i\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_\eta}, $$

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde\nabla=\tilde\nabla_\Phi+\tilde\nabla_\Psi\;}, \qquad \tilde\nabla_\Phi^{*}=-\tilde\nabla_\Phi,\qquad \tilde\nabla_\Psi^{*}=+\tilde\nabla_\Psi . $$

The subscript names the sector of the coefficients and nothing else. The ruling of the same article is that the gradient "never takes a letter in the low slot for the coordinates it is read in", and that $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ and $\tilde\nabla_\Psi$ "name the sector of the coefficients at the parameters the article has fixed".

Why the bare tilde is not a third object. A field that depends only on the material parameters, $\tilde Q=\tilde Q(r'_t,r_\eta)$, has $\partial\tilde Q/\partial r_t=0$ and $\partial\tilde Q/\partial r'_\eta=0$. The informational part annihilates it, so $\tilde\nabla\tilde Q=\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde Q$. On that domain the bare glyph is the material gradient by evaluation. An article that resolves the material parameters and prints the bare tilde has written $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ and left the subscript off.

The mark the tilde is not. The tilde marks the quaternionic element read with its scalar part; the arrow marks the pure element read on its vector part alone. The tilde and the arrow are therefore not two spellings of one thing: the tilde keeps the time slot, the arrow drops it. The articles without the tilde are the subject of the companion article, and there the gradient is the pure part, $\vec\nabla_\Phi$.

The Test

Four tests, applied in order. The first that answers settles the article.

  1. The sector subscript is written. $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ or $\tilde\nabla_\Psi$ in the low slot puts the article in that sector. Three articles pass this test, all three material.
  2. A sector parameter is written. The material time $r'_t$, in a derivative or as a coefficient, fixes the material reading; the informational space $r'_\eta$ in a derivative would fix the informational one, and no article passes on that clause. $158$ articles pass this test and all are material.
  3. The element the operator meets is material. An element of $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ — a potential $\tilde A_\Phi$, a current $\tilde J_\Phi$, a real density of the material fluid — fixes the material reading even when no parameter is written. Fourteen articles pass this test.
  4. Nothing is resolved. The glyph carries its tilde, writes no parameter of either sector, meets no sector element and writes no complex derivative out. The article is filed as ordinary. Twenty articles pass this test.

What does not count. A mention of $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ in a comparison, a citation, a name or a table of sectors does not put an article in the material reading. What counts is the parameter written, the element met, or the subscript printed.

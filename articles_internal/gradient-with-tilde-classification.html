The Gradient with the Tilde: Where the Subscript Stands

Introduction

What this is. A census of every gradient the physics corpus writes with a tilde, and of the sector subscript the corpus puts on it or leaves off. The prose comes first. Every list of articles is at the end of the document.

The headline. $201$ of the $470$ articles write the tilde, and they print it $4191$ times. $2980$ glyphs carry a sector subscript: $2930$ the material $\Phi$, $50$ the informational $\Psi$. $488$ are labelled variants, $108$ are powers, $169$ are definitions. The remaining $446$ carry no mark, in $39$ articles.

The partition of the $201$. $201=162+39$. The $162$ leave nothing unmarked. The $39$ carry at least one unmarked glyph, and they split two ways: $36$ print no sector subscript at all and work at $R_\mu$, and $3$ print the subscript and still leave glyphs bare.

The rule. The subscript is printed where the operator is applied at the material parameters. It is left off for a power ($\tilde\nabla^{2}$), a labelled variant ($\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}$, $\tilde\nabla_\kappa$, the kernel label $\tilde G_{\Phi,\tilde\nabla}$), a definition ($\tilde\nabla=e_t\,\partial_{R_t}+\dots$), and an article that fixes no chart at all and works at the eight coordinates $R_\mu$.

The Glyph

The operator.

$$ \tilde\nabla=e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial R_t}+\vec e_x\frac{\partial}{\partial R_x}+\vec e_y\frac{\partial}{\partial R_y}+\vec e_z\frac{\partial}{\partial R_z}, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial R_\mu}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial r_\mu}-i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_\mu}. $$

The two parts.

$$ \tilde\nabla=\tilde\nabla_\Phi+\tilde\nabla_\Psi, \qquad \tilde\nabla_\Phi=-i\,e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_t}+\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial r_\eta}, \qquad \tilde\nabla_\Psi=e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial r_t}-i\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_\eta}. $$

The subscript names the chart: $\Phi$ the material chart, the imaginary time $r'_t$ and the real space numbers $r_\eta$; $\Psi$ the informational chart.

One spelling for the conjugate. Where both marks stand on one glyph the sector goes low on the operator and $\natural$ goes outside, $(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}$. This equals the other order, $(\tilde\nabla^{\natural})_\Phi$, because $\natural$ negates the vector part and leaves the scalar imaginary alone, so it carries each sector to itself. The corpus carried $1198$ glyphs in the other order, across $138$ articles, and every one was respelled. The algebra is unchanged; only the writing is.

I. The One Defect

The defect is a product of two factors in different sectors. When $\Box$ is built from the material gradient it is written $\tilde\nabla_\Phi(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}$, and both factors carry $\Phi$. In $23$ places a factor was written bare: $16$ times the second, $\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, and $7$ times the first, $(\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde\nabla$. A bare factor is the sum of both sectors, so the product was not the element the equality asserts.

All $23$ are patched. Each bare factor now carries its $\Phi$, so both sides of the equality hold the same element. This is a change of sector, not of spelling.

II. The Three Articles Whose Bare Glyphs Need No Subscript

$147$ glyphs in three articles carry no subscript and should not. In all three, the bare glyph is either a definition of the operator at the eight coordinates, a statement about the operator as such, or a glyph inside the kernel label.

The kernel label is not an application. $\tilde G_{\Phi,\tilde\nabla}$ names the first-order kernel, and its low slot already holds a label. The glyph inside it names the operator the kernel inverts, not an operator applied at a chart. A second subscript there would collide with the label, and the sector of the kernel is already printed: $\Phi$.

The two conventions articles are definitions by design. Operator and Calculus Conventions and Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions define the operator, name it, state the general theorems that hold on the eight coordinates, and list it in the notation table. Their statements hold before any sector is fixed, and marking them would print a sector on a statement that is not about one.

III. The Thirty-Six Articles That Print No Subscript

$36$ articles, $299$ unmarked glyphs, and no defect. They write $R_\mu$ and never $r'_t$. The chiral pair is $\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ at the combined chart, not at the material one. A subscript here would assert a chart the article does not use, and the patch that adds it everywhere is the error this census exists to prevent.

IV. What Was Actually Added

Twelve subscripts, all of them applications at a chart the article already fixes, none of them on a label. Each is listed at the end.

Twenty-three product factors, in sixteen articles, each a bare factor that became the material one.

Eleven hundred and ninety-eight respellings, in $138$ articles, moving the sector inside the parenthesis and $\natural$ outside. This one changes no element.

No glyph was added where the low slot already holds a label, and none was added in the $36$ articles of §III.

V. The Other Half

The $34$ articles that write a gradient and never write the tilde are the subject of the companion audit. Their glyph is the pure part, $\vec\nabla=\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\,\partial/\partial R_\eta$, and the marks they carry are the arrow and the sector, $\vec\nabla_\Phi$.

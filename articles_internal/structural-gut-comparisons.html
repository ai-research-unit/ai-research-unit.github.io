Structural GUT Comparisons: Theories Ranked Against the Biquaternion Corpus

Introduction

What this note does. It ranks the theories that could stand in place of the Standard Model by how close each one lies to the biquaternion framework. The first half of the article is not evaluation: it is the structure of the algebra and of its operator algebra, which is the measuring stick. The second half applies the stick.

Why the operator layer has to come first. The element algebra $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a complex algebra of dimension four. Its compact sector is $\mathfrak{u}(2)$, of dimension four and rank two, and it cannot hold colour, whose algebra alone has dimension eight. A reader who stops there concludes that the framework is too small for any unification. The conclusion is wrong, and the reason is that the framework has a second layer. The endomorphisms of $\mathbb{B}$ form an algebra of complex dimension sixteen, and that algebra is also a biquaternion algebra, squared: it is $M_2(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_2(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}$. On that layer the compact forms include $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, of dimension fifteen and rank three, which contains the colour and lepton-number pattern. The ranking below is built on the operator layer, and it differs from a ranking built on the element layer.

The result, most compatible first.

$$ \text{Pati–Salam}\;9\;=\;\text{left–right}\;9\;>\;\text{axion}\;8\;>\;\text{seesaw}\;7\;>\;\text{extra dims}\;7\;>\;\text{SM}\;6\;=\;\text{split SUSY}\;6\;=\;\text{composite}\;6\;>\;SO(10)\;5\;>\;E_6\;2 $$

The Standard Model is sixth of ten, at six points out of fifteen, below the average of $6.4$. It is not placed there as a judgement of its truth. It is placed there because colour, the chiral action and the scalar doublet are all objects the corpus does not carry at any layer, and because its gauge group needs a five-dimensional carrier where the framework supplies four.

The rule. A theory is ranked by one question: how much of it can be built from objects the corpus already has? Every point lost is an object the theory must import. Importing is not a defect; it is a cost.

1. The Element Algebra

The carrier. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ is a complex vector space of dimension four, with basis

$$ e_t,\ \vec e_x,\ \vec e_y,\ \vec e_z, $$

and unit $e_t$. The units satisfy $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$ and $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$ cyclically. The algebra is $M_2(\mathbb{C})$: simple, central, dimension four.

The compact sector is $\mathfrak{u}(2)$. The Hermitian elements form a real space of dimension four, and its Lie algebra under the commutator is

$$ \mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1),\qquad \dim=4,\qquad \mathrm{rank}=2 . $$

This is the framework's most quoted number and it is the source of the electroweak match: $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ is $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ read as a gauge algebra, and the two factors are the traceless part and the centre. It is also the ceiling. Colour needs $\dim\mathfrak{su}(3)=8>4$ and $\mathrm{rank}=2$, so colour cannot be an object of $\mathbb{B}$.

The three spins and no more. The algebra carries exactly three spins, and the carriers are forced by the dimensions $2s+1$:

$$ s=0\ \longmapsto\ \dim_{\mathbb{R}}=1\ \longmapsto\ \mathbb{R}e_t\ \longmapsto\ \text{the centre}, $$

$$ s=\tfrac12\ \longmapsto\ \dim_{\mathbb{R}}=2\ \longmapsto\ \Sigma\ \longmapsto\ \text{the spinor plane}, $$

$$ s=1\ \longmapsto\ \dim_{\mathbb{R}}=3\ \longmapsto\ \mathfrak{k}\ \longmapsto\ \text{the anti-Hermitian part}. $$

There is no spin two and no spin three-halves. The consequence for this note is severe and it is used twice below: the spin-zero carrier is a line, so a scalar is necessarily a singlet of every compact factor. A scalar doublet is not merely absent; its carrier does not exist.

The four forms and the two real forms. The algebra carries a plain bilinear form of Gram matrix $\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, a quaternionic bilinear form, a Hermitian sesquilinear form of Gram matrix $\mathrm{I}_4$, and a Krein form. As a real algebra it has two real forms, $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathbb{H}$, separated by the trace inertia $(2,2)$ against $(4,0)$.

The element-level ceiling, stated once. Any gauge group built on $\mathbb{B}$ alone is a subgroup of $U(2)$, of rank at most two. That is the bound the corpus's earlier articles prove, and it is correct. It is not the framework's bound, because it ignores the operator layer.

2. The Operator Algebra

2.1 Dimension sixteen

Definition. An operator is a complex-linear map $F:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}$, with no condition of preserving the product. The operators form the endomorphism algebra

$$ \mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}), $$

of complex dimension sixteen and real dimension thirty-two. It is determined by the four images of the four basis units, each a coordinate column: $4\times4=16$ complex numbers, and every choice gives one operator.

The real layer. Read as real-linear maps the algebra is $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_8(\mathbb{R})$, of real dimension sixty-four. The complex-linear ones are the subspace commuting with multiplication by $i$. Both layers matter: the first acts on the carrier $\mathbb{C}^4$, the second on the real carrier $\mathbb{R}^8$.

2.2 The two one-sided families

The definition and the point. For an element $\tilde Q$ the left and right regular operators are

$$ L_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde Q X,\qquad R_{\tilde Q}(X)=X\tilde Q . $$

They are complex-linear, injective, and their images are four-dimensional subalgebras. The left one is multiplicative and the right one is anti-multiplicative:

$$ L_{\tilde Q}L_{\tilde Q_2}=L_{\tilde Q\tilde Q_2},\qquad R_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}=R_{\tilde Q_2\tilde Q}. $$

They commute elementwise, $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde P}=R_{\tilde P}L_{\tilde Q}$, because the two factors act on the two ends of the argument and cannot meet.

The double centraliser, which is the central theorem of this note. The commutant of the left family is exactly the right family, and the bicommutant is the centre:

$$ \{T:T L_a=L_a T\ \forall a\}=R(\mathbb{B}),\qquad \{T:T L_a=L_aT,\ T R_b=R_bT\ \forall a,b\}=\mathbb{C}\,\mathrm{I}_4 . $$

Two consequences are used below and neither is obvious from the element layer. First, the two families are as large as two commuting families can be: there is no room for a further internal symmetry commuting with both. Second, the neutral sectors have two different sizes, and the difference matters. The operators commuting with the whole two-sided action form a line; the operators commuting with the left action alone form the four-dimensional right family. A gauge group is a one-sided action, so a gauge-singlet field lies in the four-dimensional sector and not in the line. §7.4 uses this, and it is the correction of an earlier overstatement.

The Peirce frame makes the two sides tensor factors. In the frame $\tilde\Pi_\pm=\tfrac12(e_t\pm i\vec e_z)$, $\vec\Upsilon_\pm=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x\pm\vec e_y)$, the left matrices fill $\mathbb{B}_L=\{\mathrm{I}_2\otimes A\}$ and the right matrices fill $\mathbb{B}_R=\{B^{\mathsf T}\otimes\mathrm{I}_2\}$. Their intersection is the scalars. This is the structural key: the operator algebra factors, and the two factors are the two sides.

2.3 The two-sided operators, and why the action is forced

The family. For a pair of elements the two-sided operator is

$$ L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}(X)=\tilde Q\,X\,\tilde Q_2 , $$

with composition law

$$ L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}\,L_{\tilde Q_3}R_{\tilde Q_4}=L_{\tilde Q\tilde Q_3}R_{\tilde Q_4\tilde Q_2}, $$

the second slot reversed. The family is a complex cone of dimension seven, not a subspace: it is closed under scalar multiplication and under composition, and not under addition.

The forcing theorem. One-sided multiplication is not multiplicative:

$$ \Lambda(X)=RX\ \text{is multiplicative}\iff R=e_t . $$

The proof is one line: $RXY=RXRY$ for all $X,Y$ forces $XR=X$ at $Y=e_t$, and then $R=e_t$. So no one-sided multiplication by an invertible element is an algebra automorphism, and every inner action must be two-sided,

$$ \Theta(X)=RXR^{-1}. $$

Why this matters for the physics. A theory that needs a chiral action needs an action of the gauge group that treats the two chiral letters differently. The corpus's gauge action is left multiplication, and left multiplications commute with the right multiplication that labels the chiral letter, so both letters receive the same rotor. The action is vector-like, and the forcing theorem says the two-sided form, which is the only multiplicative one, is also vector-like. That is the obstruction behind axis C below, and it is a theorem rather than a gap.

2.4 The ladder, and the nine-dimensional complement

Four numbers. The scalars, the one-sided families, the cone and the ambient space:

$$ 1,\qquad 4+4=7\ \text{(with the line counted once)},\qquad 7,\qquad 16 . $$

Precisely, $\dim L=\dim R=4$, $\dim(L\cap R)=1$, $\dim(L+R)=7$, and

$$ \mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=(L(\mathbb{B})+R(\mathbb{B}))\oplus\mathfrak{m},\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}=9, $$

where $\mathfrak{m}$ is the space of two-sided operators whose two parameters are both traceless. It is neither an associative nor a Lie subalgebra, and it is stable under the scalar-free parts of the two families. Nine of the sixteen dimensions lie on neither side. That is the room the framework has for two-sided objects, and it is where an operator-valued field would have to live.

2.5 The enveloping algebra: $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$

The theorem. The enveloping algebra $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$ acts on $\mathbb{B}$ by

$$ (\tilde Q_1\otimes\tilde Q_{\mathrm{opp},2})\cdot\tilde Q_3=\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_3\tilde Q_2 , $$

and this action is faithful, with

$$ \mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}). $$

What this says in one sentence. The two actions together generate every linear operator, so the pair $(L,R)$ is not one representation among many: it is the whole operator algebra, and the biquaternion algebra is a bimodule over the pair. The physical question "which of the two actions is the matter representation" then has a precise location: both are present, of the same algebraic type, and the question is which factor a given degree of freedom uses.

As algebras, not just spaces. Because $\mathbb{B}=M_2(\mathbb{C})$, the tensor square is $M_2\otimes M_2=M_4$. So

$$ \mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}, $$

the operator algebra of the biquaternions is the tensor square of the biquaternion algebra, and its two tensor factors are the two sides $L$ and $R$. This identity is the load-bearing one for §3.

2.6 The real forms and the six Lie forms

Two associative real forms. $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=M_4(\mathbb{C})$ has exactly two real forms, the split $M_4(\mathbb{R})$ and the quaternionic $M_2(\mathbb{H})$, both of real dimension sixteen, separated by the trace inertia $(10,6)$ against $(6,10)$. The split form is the two-sided span of either real form of $\mathbb{B}$; the quaternionic form is the ring of right-$\mathbb{H}$-linear maps and is not the two-sided span of any element form.

Six Lie real forms. The complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{C})$ has six real forms:

$$ \mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{R}),\quad \mathfrak{u}(4),\quad \mathfrak{u}(3,1),\quad \mathfrak{u}(2,2),\quad \mathfrak{gl}(2,\mathbb{H}),\quad \mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{C})_{\mathbb{R}} . $$

Only two of the six are Lie algebras of real forms of the associative algebra, and the compact one, $\mathfrak{u}(4)$, is not among them — a compact subspace is not closed under products. That is a statement about associative real forms and it does not prevent $\mathfrak{u}(4)$ and $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ from being real Lie subalgebras, which is what a gauge algebra needs.

2.8 The adjoints, the pairings, the Hermitian pairs

Four adjoints, two behaviours. Each of the four forms gives an adjoint, and on the one-sided families the plain bilinear and Krein adjoints exchange the side, $(L_{\tilde Q})^{\approx}=R_{\tilde Q}$, while the quaternionic and Hermitian adjoints keep it. A two-sided operator has $\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\tilde Q\tilde X\tilde Q^{*}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}$, a real cone of dimension seven whose parameter is defined modulo the central phase and whose square is $\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q_2}=\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q\tilde Q_2}$.

The transposition. Every right multiplication is a conjugated left multiplication and conversely:

$$ L_{\tilde Q}=\natural\,R_{\natural(\tilde Q)}\,\natural,\qquad R_{\tilde Q}=\natural\,L_{\natural(\tilde Q)}\,\natural . $$

So the two laws are one law, and an equation written on one side converts at the price of two conjugations and the passage of the parameter through $\natural$.

The SVD. Every endomorphism is a sum of at most four rank-one terms with weights $\rho_k$ the square roots of the eigenvalues of $F^{*}F$, and the multiplication enters only on the zero-divisor cone: a two-sided operator has rank three never. So the operator algebra's structure theory is the algebra's own, not the generic $\mathbb{C}^4$ theory.

2.9 The four sectors

The norm splits the algebra. The quaternionic norm $N=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ separates the invertibles $N\neq0$, of real dimension eight, from the null cone $N=0$, of real dimension six. Inside each, the vector form $B^{2}$ cuts one wall. The rank of a one-sided operator takes exactly three values,

$$ \mathrm{rank}\,L_{\tilde Q}\in\{4,\ 2,\ 0\}, $$

and $L_{\tilde Q}$ is invertible exactly when $N\neq0$. The framework's own element space therefore has three tiers of degeneracy, which is a structural resource no candidate in this note uses, and a reminder that "rank" is an intrinsic notion here.

2.10 The spin ladder

One rotor gives one half. Left multiplication by a unit quaternion is a real orthogonal matrix. Its three generators square to $-\mathrm{I}_4$, the Casimir of the halved generators is $\pm\tfrac34=s(s+1)$, and $s=\tfrac12$. The spin group is $S^3$, not $SU(2)$; $SU(2)$ is one display.

A pair gives zero and one. A pair of operators acting on two sides lies in $S^3_L\times S^3_R$, each factor carries $\tfrac12$, and the addition rule gives $0$ and $1$ and nothing else. The two-sided display

$$ \rho:\mathrm{Spin}(3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(3)\longrightarrow SO(4) $$

is surjective with kernel $\{\pm(\mathrm{I}_4,\mathrm{I}_4)\}$. That map is $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\to SO(4)$ and it is the corpus's own construction of the left–right weak group.

3. What the Operator Layer Buys

The section's claim. Three facts at the operator layer change the evaluation, and none of them is visible at the element layer.

3.1 The ceiling rises, and it rises in rank

At the element layer the bound is $\mathfrak{u}(2)$: dimension four, rank two. At the operator layer the compact forms reach

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)\ (\dim 15,\ \mathrm{rank}\,3)\subset\mathfrak{u}(4)\ (\dim 16,\ \mathrm{rank}\,4), $$

and $\mathfrak{su}(4)\cong\mathfrak{so}(6)$. Colour, whose algebra has dimension eight and rank two, is a subgroup of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ and therefore an object of the operator layer, though not of the element algebra. The corpus's statement that colour is absent is true of $\mathbb{B}$ and false of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}$.

3.2 The carrier splits as three plus one, with the right charges

The decomposition. Under the maximal subgroup $S(U(3)\times U(1))\subset SU(4)$ the carrier splits as

$$ \mathbb{C}^4=\mathbf{3}_{1/3}\oplus\mathbf{1}_{-1}, $$

three coloured states of charge one third and one colourless state of charge minus one, and the charges are traceless,

$$ 3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0, $$

which is what makes the generator well defined inside the simple group. This is the colour-lepton quartet of Pati–Salam, obtained from the carrier of the biquaternion algebra, with the $B-L$ charges, and with no inserted number. The chain algebra's own ladder spectrum agrees, computed from $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ rather than assumed:

$$ \varrho=0,\ \tfrac13,\ \tfrac13,\ \tfrac13,\ \tfrac23,\ \tfrac23,\ \tfrac23,\ 1 . $$

3.3 Sixteen is the algebra, fifteen is its traceless part

The arithmetic. One generation of Weyl fields without a right-handed neutrino is fifteen; with it, sixteen. The operator algebra gives exactly these two numbers:

$$ \dim\mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{C})=16,\qquad \dim\mathfrak{sl}(4,\mathbb{C})=15,\qquad \mathfrak{gl}=\mathfrak{sl}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{I}_4 . $$

The affinity, stated precisely. The identification is an identification of dimension and of singlet character, not of module: the fifteen matter fields of the Standard Model are not the adjoint fifteen of $\mathfrak{sl}(4,\mathbb{C})$. What matches is the arithmetic and the character of the missing piece — the difference is a singlet of every factor, which is the quantum number of the right-handed neutrino. The corpus's module count and the Standard Model's field count agree at fifteen, and the object that closes the gap is the trace line.

3.4 The right-handed neutrino is the trace

Three facts, one object. The centre of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}$ is the line $\mathbb{C}\mathrm{I}_4$. The bicommutant of the two-sided action is that same line. And the operator that closes fifteen into sixteen is that same line. So a gauge-singlet fermion degree of freedom has a home in the framework, and the home is the trace direction of the endomorphism algebra.

The caveat, stated here because §7.4 depends on it. The line is the neutral sector only when both sides act. A gauge group acts on one side, and then the neutral sector is the four-dimensional right family. So the framework has room for a right-handed sector of dimension four and not for a single privileged singlet. The fifteen-to-sixteen gap is real; the uniqueness of the object that closes it is not.

The consequence. The corpus does not merely permit a right-handed neutrino; it counts them, and the count is one per generation, because the bicommutant is one-dimensional. That is a structural prediction, and it is the strongest single affinity any candidate in this note possesses.

3.5 The Pati–Salam sixteen is the tensor square

The identity. $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}$ as algebras, and $\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{C}^2\otimes\mathbb{C}^2$ as a complex vector space. So

$$ \mathbb{C}^{16}=\mathbb{C}^4\otimes\mathbb{C}^2_L\otimes\mathbb{C}^2_R , $$

one carrier, one left doublet, one right doublet, with all three factors commuting. On the first factor put $SU(4)$; on the second put $SU(2)_L$; on the third put $SU(2)_R$. The result is

$$ \mathbf{16}=(\mathbf{4},\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\bar{\mathbf{4}},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2}), $$

which is the Pati–Salam generation, and it is the algebra $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}$ read as a module. No candidate in this note has a matter multiplet that is the operator algebra itself. Pati–Salam does.

3.6 The module test, and why three groups stay outside

The test. A compact group can act on the operator layer only if it has a faithful module inside the carrier $\mathbb{C}^4$, or if it acts factor by factor on the tensor square $\mathbb{C}^{16}$. Groups that fail:

group smallest faithful module $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}$ verdict
$SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ $\mathbf{3}\oplus\mathbf{2}$ $5$ one dimension beyond the carrier
$SU(5)$ $\mathbf{5}$ $5$ one dimension beyond the carrier
$SO(10)$ $\mathbf{16}$, the spinor $16$ matches $\dim\mathcal{E}$ but is not a factor action
$E_6$ $\mathbf{27}$ $27$ beyond the tensor square

The sharpest of the four. The Standard Model's group has rank four and its semisimple part is $A_2\oplus A_1$, which is not a subsystem of $A_3$: $\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ needs five legs in the fundamental. The carrier has four. So the Standard Model is one complex dimension short of the framework, and $SU(5)$ is exactly the group of that missing dimension. That is a better statement of the GUT obstruction than the rank argument at the element layer, and it explains why $SU(5)$ is the first group that even has a chance.

The same test applied to Pati–Salam. Its rank is $3+1+1=5$, above the rank four of $\mathfrak{u}(4)$, so it cannot sit inside one $U(4)$. It does not need to: it sits inside $U(\mathbb{B})\times U(\mathbb{B})$, of rank eight and dimension thirty-two, acting on the tensor square. Pati–Salam is the one candidate whose group splits along the framework's two layers, one factor on each.

4. The Gate

Two conditions, both required.

  1. It explains as much as the Standard Model. Its gauge group contains $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$, its matter reproduces one generation with the measured quantum numbers, and it reduces to the Standard Model below its new scales. It must carry all three interactions.
  2. It is indistinguishable at current reach. Every state it adds is heavy enough or weakly enough coupled to have escaped the searches and the precision fit.

What the gate removes. Additions that explain no more than the Standard Model: a vector-like singlet fermion on its own, a dark photon, a heavy $Z'$ alone, a leptoquark pair, an inert doublet, a hidden sector.

What the gate also removes, and this is the expensive part.

  • Minimal $SU(5)$ predicts $p\to e^+\pi^0$ at $\tau_p\sim10^{31}$ yr against the bound $\tau_p>2.4\times10^{34}$ yr. Excluded by three orders of magnitude.
  • Minimal Pati–Salam with one bidoublet gives $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$, excluded by the measured masses. A viable version adds a second bidoublet or a $(15,2,2)$.
  • Minimal $SO(10)$ with one breaking step inherits the $SU(5)$ proton lifetime. It survives only through an intermediate Pati–Salam scale.

Consequence. The rows named "Pati–Salam" and "$SO(10)$" mean the versions that pass. The minimal versions are named here so the table is not read as an endorsement.

7. The Most Compatible Theories

7.1 Pati–Salam, $9/15$

The theory. Leptons are the fourth colour. The group is $SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$, one generation in

$$ (4,\bar2,1)\oplus(\bar4,1,2), $$

sixteen Weyl fields, with the breaking chain $SU(4)\to SU(3)\times U(1)_{B-L}$ followed by the weak breaking.

Structure. Rank $3+1+1=5$, dimension $15+3+3=21$. Matter sixteen. The scalars are a $(4,1,2)$ and a bidoublet $(1,2,2)$.

Affinity 1: the sixteen is the tensor square of the algebra. This is the deepest match in the corpus and it deserves the display:

$$ \mathbb{C}^{16}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{L}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{R}. $$

The first factor is the carrier and carries $SU(4)$. The second and third are the two factors of $\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}$ and carry the two weak groups. All three commute. The matter multiplet of Pati–Salam is the operator algebra read as a module, and the operator algebra is the framework's own second layer.

Affinity 2: $SU(4)$ is a real form of the traceless part. The classification lists $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ among the six real forms of $\mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{C})$, of dimension fifteen, compact, $\cong\mathfrak{so}(6)$. So the colour-lepton group is not imported from outside: it is the compact real form of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}'$, reached by the same descent that gives $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ at the element level.

Affinity 3: the carrier splits as three plus one, with the $B-L$ charges. Under $S(U(3)\times U(1))\subset SU(4)$,

$$ \mathbb{C}^{4}=\mathbf{3}_{1/3}\oplus\mathbf{1}_{-1},\qquad 3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0 . $$

Colour, the lepton, and the $B-L$ assignment, from one subspace of the operator algebra and one tracelessness condition. The chain algebra's computed spectrum reproduces the electric charges of one generation.

What the trace fixes, and what it leaves to observation. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0$ is what puts the quark and the lepton charges in the ratio three to one, so $Q$ lands on the lattice $\tfrac13\mathbb{Z}$ rather than on a lattice of arbitrary step. Three things are worth separating. The ratio is structural. The overall scale is not: the electron charge stays measured, exactly as the corpus's own parity lock fixes parity and not scale. And the multiplicity is not fixed either: the trace gives the ratio of charges given three colours, so the number three is the observed input, and the model's contribution is that the fourth component of the same object is the lepton. "Charge in thirds follows from colour being three-valued" is therefore right, and "colour is three-valued because of the group" is not available.

Affinity 4: the two weak groups are the two-sided display. $\mathrm{Spin}(4)=SU(2)\times SU(2)$ is the image of

$$ \rho:\mathrm{Spin}(3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(3)\to SO(4), $$

surjective with kernel $\{\pm(\mathrm{I}_4,\mathrm{I}_4)\}$, and the corpus constructs it from a pair of rotors acting on two sides. Pati–Salam's weak sector is not a guess: it is the spin of a pair of operators, and the corpus's own article carries the computation that a pair gives spins $0$ and $1$ and nothing else.

What the two-sided display then assembles. With $T_{3R}$ in hand the model writes hypercharge as a sum of two non-abelian generators and assumes no abelian factor at all:

$$ \frac{Y}{2}=T_{3R}+\frac{B-L}{2}, \qquad B-L=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1\bigr)\in\mathfrak{su}(4), \qquad T_{3R}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\bigr)\in\mathfrak{su}(2)_R . $$

There is no $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ above the colour-breaking scale: the $B-L$ direction is inside the same simple factor as colour, the $T_{3R}$ direction is inside the second weak factor, and the abelian group appears only after both have broken. That is the precise content of the claim that the model unifies electromagnetism with the strong force, and the precision cuts both ways — the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is manufactured, and the group that manufactures it is not simple, so the couplings it carries are not one. The check is the right-handed neutrino, whose $Y=0$ needs no adjustment: $T_{3R}=+\tfrac12$ and $B-L=-1$ cancel exactly, which is the sign that the pairing is right rather than fitted.

Affinity 5: the group splits along the framework's two layers. Rank five exceeds the rank four of $\mathfrak{u}(4)$, so no single $U(4)$ holds the theory. It does not need one. It sits in $U(\mathbb{B})\times U(\mathbb{B})$, of rank eight, with $SU(4)$ on the first tensor factor and $SU(2)^{2}$ on the second. Pati–Salam is the unique candidate whose gauge group is distributed over the element layer and the operator layer, one factor family on each. That is a structural fact and it has no counterpart in the table.

Affinity 6: the physical group is a quotient, and sixteen rather than fifteen is why. The centre of the product is $\mathbb{Z}_4\times\mathbb{Z}_{2L}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2R}$, since the centre of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is $\mathbb{Z}_n$, and the group that acts faithfully on the sixteen fields is the quotient by the diagonal $\mathbb{Z}_2$ generated by the order-two element of $\mathbb{Z}_4$ together with the non-trivial elements of both $\mathbb{Z}_2$'s. The quotient is not bookkeeping; it is the corpus's own move. There the product $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ carries a two-element kernel on the carrier, the physical group is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, and the consequence is a lock between the abelian charge and the spin parity, $q\equiv2j\pmod 2$. Here the consequence is that the diagonal element is $(-\mathrm{I}_4,-\mathrm{I}_2,-\mathrm{I}_2)$, which acts on $(4,2,1)$ and on $(\bar4,1,2)$ as $(-1)(-1)(+1)=+1$ and therefore on every state of the sixteen. That is what makes the quotient well defined on the matter content, and it is the sixteen, not the fifteen, that closes into a representation of the group. The two statements are one statement at two groups, and neither is dynamical.

Why the sixteenth field is not an addition. Read the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ doublets: $\{u_R,\nu_R\}$ carries $T_{3R}=+\tfrac12$ and $\{d_R,e_R\}$ carries $-\tfrac12$, so the right-handed neutrino is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of the right-handed up quark and not a singlet appended to the Standard Model. Read the quartets at fixed $T_{3R}$: $\{u^r,u^g,u^b,\nu\}$ is one irreducible object. Lepton number is the fourth colour in the literal sense — the same quantum label, not an analogy.

Affinity 7: parity is an automorphism of the group, so the two multiplets are one object. The group admits a semidirect extension by $\mathbb{Z}_2$,

$$ \Bigl(\bigl[\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\bigr]/\mathbb{Z}_2\Bigr)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2 , $$

where the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ combines an outer automorphism of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ with the interchange of the two weak factors. Under it $(4,2,1)\oplus(\bar4,1,2)$ is a single irreducible representation, which is the algebraic statement that left and right are one object. This is the model's original motivation, and it is why the entry is a left–right theory rather than two theories: parity becomes a property of the vacuum instead of a postulate of the Lagrangian. The corpus's own pair of factors admits the same interchange: the side transposition exchanges $L$ and $R$, and the swap is an automorphism of the display rather than a coincidence.

Obstacle 1: the scalars. The breaking needs a $(4,1,2)$ and a $(1,2,2)$ bidoublet. The framework's spin classification gives the scalar as the line $\mathbb{R}e_t$, of real dimension one, and stops at spin one. A scalar in a doublet of a compact factor has a carrier the framework does not contain. This is the entry's single lost axis and it is a proved absence, not a gap.

Obstacle 2: the minimal version is excluded. One bidoublet gives $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$. The masses are measured; the relation is false. Viability requires a second bidoublet or a $(15,2,2)$, and each is another imported scalar. The direction of the relation matters, and the form usually quoted is the reverse: one bidoublet gives $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$, not $m_u=m_d$ and $m_e=m_\nu$, and both follow from the single $\mathrm{SU}(4)$-symmetric Yukawa coupling, so no choice of vacuum values repairs them. A note that repeats the quoted form has audited nothing.

Obstacle 3: no single coupling. Three factors survive, so three couplings, so the single-coupling result that motivates unification is absent. That needs $SO(10)$ or larger, and §8.5 shows at what cost.

An advantage that is structural rather than lucky. The leptoquark gauge bosons carry $B-L$, so at the renormalisable level they conserve baryon number and the proton is stable against gauge exchange. Decay can proceed only through the scalar sector, by three-quark fusion, at a rate far below the present bound. That is why minimal $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ was excluded by $p\to e^{+}\pi^{0}$ while this group was not, and the difference is not a coincidence of couplings: it is which quantum number the off-diagonal generators carry.

The defect that only topology sees. The vacuum manifold of the first step has a non-trivial second homotopy group,

$$ \pi_2\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)}{\bigl[\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\bigr]/\mathbb{Z}_3}\right)=\mathbb{Z}, $$

so the model carries 't Hooft–Polyakov monopoles, forced by the standard argument that a non-trivial $\pi_2$ of the vacuum manifold produces a topological defect. No Feynman diagram sees them, and that is why they belong here next to Affinity 6: the existence of the defect is a statement about the global form of the group, which is exactly what the quotient changes and what perturbation theory cannot detect. Read the two together and the quotient stops looking like a technicality. The multiplicity depends on the global form taken, so the number to keep is that they exist and not how many.

Why it shares the top, and with what. Pati–Salam is one of two entries whose group is a product of factors the framework builds and whose matter multiplet is the operator algebra read as a module; the left–right entry shares both. What Pati–Salam alone has is a charge spectrum computed rather than inserted — $B-L$ is traceless inside the same simple factor as colour — and that is a fact about content, which the five axes do not score. It pays one debt, the scalar, and pays it in a currency the framework openly lacks.

7.2 Left–right, $9/15$

The theory. $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_{B-L}$, parity exact before breaking, a right-handed neutrino inside a doublet.

Structure. Rank five, dimension fifteen. Matter sixteen. Breaking needs a scalar triplet or a bidoublet.

Affinity 1: the weak sector is a maximal subgroup of $SU(4)$, with the circle included. $S(U(2)\times U(2))\subset SU(4)$ is

$$ SU(2)\times SU(2)\times U(1), $$

of dimension seven and rank three. The left–right weak group together with its extra circle is a maximal-rank subgroup of the colour-lepton group of the operator layer. So the theory's weak sector, including the $B-L$ circle, is native.

Affinity 2: the parity is an automorphism. The complex split biquaternions $\mathrm{Cl}(3,\mathbb{C})\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})^{2}$ are two copies of $\mathrm{Cl}(2)$ interchanged by a parity automorphism, and the corpus reads that doubling as the two chiralities. The discrete symmetry the theory restores is an automorphism of a built algebra, not an import.

Affinity 3: both weak factors are one display. As in §7.1, $\rho$ is the construction, and the right-handed neutrino is required rather than optional, which is what makes the matter sixteen and even.

Obstacles. The $SU(3)$ and the $SU(2)^{2}\times U(1)$ cannot both sit in the same $SU(4)$ as commuting factors, so the group does not split into the two layers the way Pati–Salam's does; it needs $\mathbb{C}^{3}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{2}$, of complex dimension seven. The $B-L$ circle is canonical up to scale — it is the unique traceless diagonal that is a multiple of the identity on $\mathbb Ce_t$ and on the triplet, and the centraliser of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is one-dimensional — but it is a $U(1)$ and not a simple factor, so its normalisation is free and charge quantisation is not explained. And the breaking scalar is forbidden, as in §7.1.

Why it ties, and where the real weakness is. The earlier version of this note put it one point below Pati–Salam, on the ground that its colour is only a subgroup of a native algebra. That ground does not survive the frame's test: $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is the stabiliser of the canonical line $\mathbb Ce_t$, its eight generators are endomorphisms acting on the carrier, and the group acts faithfully on a quartet. The point is restored, and the two entries are level at nine.

Why this group is free, in the note's own account. The element layer's compact sector is $\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$, of dimension four: one $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and one circle. Two sides give two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s, because the second is the other one-sided family $R(\mathfrak{k})$ and costs nothing extra, and that doubling is the whole reason the group is free. Colour costs a layer, because $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ needs dimension eight against the four available at the element level and then comes only as a subgroup of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$; above dimension fifteen the budget is exhausted. In one sentence, the corpus's alphabet is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and its favourite word in that alphabet is the product of two. The public account of the group, its two modules and the parity reading is $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$: The Group, the Two Sides, and Why the Standard Model Does Not Gauge It; the module distinction and the axis-C argument stay here.

What the tie hides, stated so that it is not read as equality of content. Pati–Salam puts colour and $B-L$ inside one simple factor, and tracelessness on the quartet then relates the quark and lepton charges; the left–right group keeps four factors and four independent couplings, so the same relation has to be imposed. That difference is worth real money and it is already counted: it is §5's first separator, and it is a difference of content, not of address. A scorer who wants it inside the table needs a sixth axis for one simple factor for colour and lepton number, and this note does not add one.

7.3 Axion, KSVZ, $8/15$

The theory. The Standard Model plus a global $U(1)_{PQ}$ broken at $f_a$, with the pseudo-Goldstone phase relaxing the QCD theta parameter.

Structure. The gauge group is unchanged. The matter gains a heavy coloured quark in the KSVZ version and no singlet fermion. A complex singlet carries the phase.

Affinity 1: the symmetry is the central circle. The Peccei–Quinn $U(1)$ is the algebra's central phase acting on a central scalar,

$$ \tilde\Xi=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt2}\exp\!\Bigl(i\frac{a}{f_a}\Bigr)e_t , $$

and the axion is the phase. No construction is needed; the object is the algebra's own, and the phase is periodic by construction, so the cosine potential's periodicity is not an input.

Affinity 2: the circle is unique. The centre of the operator algebra is a line, so the framework has exactly one continuous internal circle commuting with everything, and there is exactly one candidate for a Peccei–Quinn symmetry. This is the one place in the note where a uniqueness statement survives audit, because it is a statement about the centre and not about a neutral sector.

Affinity 3: no group is added. The axis-A score is the Standard Model's own two, and the ceiling is never touched.

Obstacles. The anomaly is imported whole: the coupling $\frac{a}{f_a}F\tilde F$ that generates the mass needs the colour field strength, and colour is one layer away and the measure does not exist. The fermion content stays at fifteen, so axis B is one. And the DFSZ version, whose second doublet would fix that, is forbidden, which is why the table grades KSVZ.

Why it is third. It has the best single affinity in the note, the central circle, and it explains one problem. It loses to the two entries above it on the matter axis, where their sixteen does work that its fifteen does not.

7.4 Type-I seesaw, $7/15$

What it is, and what it is not. The seesaw is not a replacement for the Standard Model. It leaves the gauge group, the field content and the symmetries untouched and adds three things: one gauge-singlet fermion $N_R$ per generation, one Dirac coupling $y_\nu\bar LHN_R$, and one Majorana mass $M_R$. The light eigenvalue is the Schur complement of a two-by-two mass matrix,

$$ \begin{pmatrix}0&m_D\\ m_D&M_R\end{pmatrix}\ \Longrightarrow\ m_\nu\simeq\frac{m_D^{2}}{M_R},\qquad M_R\simeq\frac{(100\ \mathrm{GeV})^{2}}{0.05\ \mathrm{eV}}=2\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{GeV}. $$

The structures, audited one by one. There are four candidate affinities and only one of them is a structural match.

The Majorana mass: a real fit. The framework separates the linear pairing, which is Dirac, from the antilinear one, which is Majorana, and the second is

$$ \mathcal{C}:\tilde\Xi\longmapsto K\tilde\Xi^{*},\qquad \mathcal{C}^{2}=1, $$

with a fixed space of real dimension four. This is a named corpus construction, it is the object the heavy block needs, and $\mathcal{C}^{2}=1$ rather than $-1$ is what makes the term a mass rather than a current. This is the seesaw's one genuine fit, and it is a good one.

The gauge singlet: a fit, but not the one claimed earlier. A field neutral under a one-sided gauge action lies in the commutant of $L(\mathbb{B})$, which is $R(\mathbb{B})$, of complex dimension four. So the framework does have a natural neutral sector, and the Standard Model's one singlet per generation sits inside it. But the sector is four-dimensional, and it is the right-handed action itself, which the framework states to be of the same algebraic type as the left. The honest statement is a right-handed sector of dimension four, containing the singlet, and not a uniquely counted singlet.

The sixteen: a numerical coincidence, not a module. The matter is sixteen, and sixteen is $\dim\mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{C})$. But this sixteen is fifteen Standard Model fields plus one singlet, and it is not presented as a module of the operator algebra. Pati–Salam's sixteen is $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$; the seesaw's is not. Matching a dimension is not matching a structure.

The Dirac coupling: a shared debt, not an affinity. The Yukawa $y_\nu\bar LHN_R$ needs the Higgs doublet. The doublet is the Standard Model's forbidden object, and the seesaw inherits it unchanged. So the seesaw pays the Standard Model's scalar cost in full and gains nothing on that axis.

The mechanism: no corpus content at all. The formula $m_\nu\simeq m_D^{2}/M_R$ is the determinant of a two-by-two matrix read as a Schur complement. It is linear algebra, and the framework supplies no part of it beyond the two kinds of mass.

Structure. The gauge group is unchanged, rank four, dimension twelve, so axis A is the Standard Model's own two. The matter is sixteen and even, so axis B is two rather than one. The chiral action is still required and still unavailable, so axis C is one. The doublet is still needed, so axis D is zero. The rank is four, so axis E is two.

Score and the correction. $2+2+1+0+2=7/15$. It is one point above the Standard Model, and the point is the parity of the matter dimension. An earlier version of this note gave thirteen, on three claims that do not survive audit: that the singlet is one-dimensional, that the sixteen is a module, and that no scalar is required. Each was wrong, and together they inflated the entry by six points and displaced the whole ranking.

Obstacles. The corpus gives the two kinds of mass and not their magnitudes, so it cannot predict $M_R$ or $m_\nu$. It has no leptogenesis. And it exhibits no carrier that is both a fermion and neutral: the spin-half carrier $\Sigma$ has real dimension two and is the smallest faithful carrier of the double cover, so it is charged, while the neutral sector $R(\mathbb{B})$ is four-dimensional. The intersection of the two requirements is not exhibited, and that is the entry's structural gap.

Its place in the taxonomy. The seesaw is contained in Pati–Salam and in $SO(10)$: the right-handed neutrino lies inside the $(\bar4,1,2)$ and inside the $\mathbf{16}$. So it is not a competitor to the entries above it. It is the minimal completion, and they are the structural ones.

7.5 Pati–Salam against left–right, and why neither is the theory

The top two are not peers, and the ranking cannot say so. The left–right group is a subgroup of the Pati–Salam group at the same rank:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\ \supset\ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R, \qquad \underbrace{3+1+1}_{5}=\underbrace{2+1+1+1}_{5}. $$

The matrix is the standard one, $\mathrm{SU}(4)\supset\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ with the circle generated by $B-L=\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1)$ and $3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0$. Every statement true of the left–right model is therefore true of Pati–Salam, since the group is bigger and the multiplet is the same sixteen. A comparison run as though the two were independent entries is misleading: Pati–Salam cannot lose an axis for a reason the left–right model escapes. It can only lose by having more to justify.

What Pati–Salam adds is one geometric object. The coset has dimension $15-8-1=6$ and is the space of complex lines in the carrier:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\big/\big(\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\big)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}. $$

The extra gauge bosons are the coordinates of a point of $\mathbb{CP}^{3}$; the left–right model is what remains when the point is fixed and the six coordinates are dropped. The point is not a choice: $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is the unit line, and it is fixed by every automorphism. Keeping and dropping are then both canonical, and what the theories differ by is a physical decision, not a difference of fidelity.

Read as content, Pati–Salam wins exactly where a theory can be wrong: its two winning lines are derivations, and its losing lines are constraints that a heavy enough scale or a slightly larger scalar sector repairs, none of which kills the group.

The axis objection, which is the one real argument against the smaller group. If colour is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, a generator such as $\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac23,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$ singles out a direction, and the corpus has no axis-free basis, so a structure that prefers an axis looks suspect. The answer is that no axis is preferred: the order-three unit quaternion $\tfrac12(e_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+\vec e_z)$ cycles $x\to y\to z$ and fixes $e_t$, so $\{x,y,z\}$ is one orbit and a Cartan is a choice on which $S_3$ acts. What is preferred is the unit line $\mathbb{C}e_t$, fixed by every automorphism and the only line that can be a singlet. The objection is therefore against colour detached from the unit line, not against colour.

What would break the tie is a computation, not an attitude. A separate $B-L$ circle in four factors still forcing $3\cdot\tfrac13-1=0$ would give the left–right model charge quantisation without a fourth colour. Nobody has such a computation, and its absence is the whole of the content advantage.

Neither entry is the theory, and the table should be read with that in mind. This is the limit the ranking cannot state from inside its own columns. Both entries are imported frames: they are neighbours of the algebra, not consequences of it, and the framework predicts neither. Nothing in the corpus selects $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)^2$ over $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)^2\times\mathrm{U}(1)$, because nothing in the corpus selects a gauge group at all — it selects a carrier, a split, and a two-sided action, and both theories are built on that. The choice between them is a declaration that the fourth direction is gauged, plus a measurement of the couplings; it is not a derivation, and a margin in this table would not have made it one.

What the ordering does say is narrower and still worth having: the two entries are the least foreign, an $E_6$ is the most, and the Standard Model sits below the average because its group needs five complex dimensions where the framework supplies four. That is a statement about distance, and distance is not truth. A reader who wants the framework's own model will not find it in this table, because the framework does not have one.

The traps. Do not read the containment as an argument that Pati–Salam is safer; it is larger and therefore more constrained. Do not read the axis objection as an argument against colour; it is an argument against colour detached from the unit line. Do not read the tie as equality of content: one factor against two is worth real money and is counted in the two lines above, not in the score.

8. The Middle and the Bottom

8.1 Extra dimensions, $7/15$

The theory. The Standard Model on a larger arena: ADD with a large flat volume, Randall–Sundrum with one warped dimension. All three interactions survive; only gravity is diluted or warped.

Affinity. The metric is the Gram matrix of a frame, and a frame carries any nondegenerate symmetric tensor as its Gram matrix. The five-dimensional Lorentz group is documented,

$$ \mathfrak{sp}(1,1)\cong\mathfrak{so}(4,1),\qquad \dim=10, $$

and it is one of the two algebras of that dimension. So a fifth leg is not a new kind of object on the matter side.

Obstacle. The bilinear form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ has signature $(1,3)$ and the algebra supplies one form with one signature. A five-leg frame needs $(1,4)$. The group is present and the quadratic space is not.

Why it sits just above the Standard Model. Its content is the Standard Model's, so its axes are the Standard Model's, with one point gained on the scalar axis and one lost on the arena. It explains the hierarchy and nothing else.

8.2 The Standard Model, $6/15$

The theory. Three gauge factors, fifteen Weyl fields per generation, one scalar doublet.

Affinities. The electroweak algebra is native: $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)=\mathfrak{u}(2)$ is the compact sector of $\mathbb{B}$. The hypercharge circle is the centre's unitary part. Colour is reachable as a subgroup of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ at the operator layer.

Obstacles, and the worst is quantitative. The gauge group's semisimple part is $A_2\oplus A_1$, which is not a subsystem of $A_3$, so the group needs a faithful module of complex dimension five, $\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf2$, and the carrier has four. The fifteen is odd, so the matter needs a spurious singlet, and the singlet that fixes it is the trace line. The chiral $U(1)_Y$ is unavailable, because the central phase is central and acts on both chiral letters alike. And the scalar doublet is forbidden because the spin-zero carrier is a line.

Why it is sixth of ten. Four of the five axes are at one or zero. It is the correct theory and it is a description the framework cannot host: it must import colour, the doublet and the chiral structure, and it is one complex dimension short of the carrier. That is not a criticism of the Standard Model; it is the reason a compatibility scale must not be read as a truth scale.

8.3 Split SUSY, $6/15$

The theory. Supersymmetry with scalars heavy at $10^{4}$–$10^{8}$ GeV and gauginos and higgsinos light. It keeps unification and a dark-matter candidate and gives up naturalness.

Affinity. The odd extension exists: the ambient algebra generated by $\mathbb{B}$ and four vectors is a Lie superalgebra whose even part is the framework's algebra, so the algebra is not blind to odd generators. A gaugino is an adjoint fermion and therefore vector-like, which is the corpus's easy side of axis C.

Obstacle. A supercharge is never an element of $\mathbb{B}$, and the odd extension is one generator, not a super-Poincaré algebra. Nothing in the corpus constructs chiral supermultiplets assigning scalar partners to the observed fermions. The scalar sector needs two doublets, which is a zero, and the naturalness argument the theory abandons is one the framework cannot evaluate, because a quadratic sensitivity is a statement about a cutoff and there is no measure.

8.4 Composite Higgs, $6/15$

The theory. The Higgs as a pseudo-Goldstone boson of a new strong sector at $f\sim1$ TeV.

Affinity. The Goldstone mechanism, the decay constant and the derivative couplings are in the corpus, and no gauge factor is added.

Obstacle. A second confining sector is forbidden. The framework has exactly one strong sector available, the imported $SU(3)$; no compact gauge algebra of dimension above four exists at the element layer, and at the operator layer the available compact algebras are $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, $\mathfrak{u}(4)$, $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, $\mathfrak{so}(5)$ and $\mathfrak{u}(2)$, none of which supplies a second confinement. The Higgs is still a doublet, and the cutoff is still missing.

8.5 $SO(10)$, $5/15$

The theory. One group, one coupling, one matter multiplet; a generation with its right-handed neutrino is the spinor $\mathbf{16}$.

Affinity. It is a spin group, so it is of the corpus's own type, and its sixteen is even. It contains Pati–Salam and inherits every affinity of §7.1 at one remove. Its sixteen matches $\dim\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}$ exactly, which is why it is the most attractive unified group on dimensional grounds.

Obstacle, and it is decisive. $\mathfrak{so}(10)$ has dimension forty-five and rank five. The largest operator-layer group is $\mathfrak{u}(4)\times\mathfrak{u}(4)$, of dimension thirty-two and rank eight. Forty-five exceeds thirty-two, so there is no embedding, and the spinor sixteen is a module of $SO(10)$ and not a factor action on $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$. The framework reaches $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ and stops eleven dimensions short of $\mathfrak{so}(10)$.

Why it is below the Standard Model. Its group is further from the algebra than the Standard Model's group is, and its matter fits better. The two effects do not cancel, and the total is five.

8.6 $E_6$, $2/15$

The theory. One exceptional group, one coupling, matter in the $\mathbf{27}$.

Affinity. It contains the chain $E_6\supset SO(10)\supset SU(4)\times SU(2)^{2}$, so it inherits the Pati–Salam affinities at two removes, and its hypercharge lies in the weight lattice, so charge quantisation follows.

Obstacle. It is exceptional, and the exceptional structures need the Jordan algebra $J_3(\mathbb{O})$, which the corpus records as outside the biquaternion algebra. Its $\mathbf{27}$ exceeds $\mathbb{C}^{16}$, and its rank six exceeds every rank available at either layer. Two axes are at zero.

9. What the Order Means

The order is a distance, not a verdict. The score answers one question: how much of a theory can be built from the corpus's own objects. A theory can score two and be right; the Standard Model scores six and is right.

Not a verdict — but not arbitrary either. Every point lost names an object. Pati–Salam's six lost points are the bidoublet, the rank, and the chirality, and a reader can argue about each. The seesaw's eight lost points are almost all the Standard Model's own, which is why it sits where it sits.

The two orderings now agree, and that is the note's result.

The two orderings agreed only after the seesaw was corrected. In the earlier version they were nearly opposite, and the inversion looked structural. It was an artifact of one entry's inflated score. Corrected twice, the entries the framework can host most easily are also the entries that explain the most — and they are two, level at nine. Structure does not separate them; content does. The corpus is aligned with Pati–Salam, and the alignment is a declaration plus the derivation of charge quantisation, not a margin in this table.

The top two are also not the framework's model, and this is the note's largest caveat. They are the least foreign entries in a ranking of foreign things. The algebra supplies a carrier, a canonical split and a two-sided action; it does not supply a gauge group, so no entry here is a consequence of the framework, and the ordering measures distance rather than truth. A reader who takes first place as "the theory the corpus asserts" has read the table backwards — §7.5 states the limit in full.

Why that agreement is not a coincidence. The entries that explain the most are the entries whose matter is sixteen and whose group is a spin group. Those are exactly the entries the module test rewards. Here the cost of structure and the benefit of structure point the same way, because the structure being bought — sixteen states, an even module, a spin group — is the framework's own subject. A theory that pays in the framework's currency is a theory that explains in it.

The three findings that outrank the scores.

First, the operator layer raises the ceiling from rank two to rank three and puts colour inside the framework, as $\mathfrak{su}(3)\subset\mathfrak{su}(4)=\mathrm{Spin}(6)$.

Second, the framework has a right-handed sector: the commutant of the left action is the four-dimensional right family, so a gauge singlet has a home. The home is not a privileged line. Withdrawing the stronger claim removed six points from the seesaw and moved it from first to fourth, and that is the single largest correction this note records.

Third, the Standard Model's gauge group needs a five-dimensional carrier and the framework supplies four. That is the sharpest available statement of why unification fails here, and it is why $SU(5)$ is the first group that has a chance and why it is one dimension too far.

What would reorder the table. One corpus result. A construction of a gauge-charged scalar would lift Pati–Salam, the left–right group and $SO(10)$ by three points each. Pati–Salam would rise to twelve and stay first, so the order of the top would not change; what would change is the gap. A construction of a chiral action on a single carrier would lift every chiral entry, and Pati–Salam would take the largest gain, because its two chiralities are already two tensor factors. Neither construction exists.

Summary

The gate. Contain all three interactions, reduce to the Standard Model, escape the current searches. That removes every bolt-on, and removes minimal $SU(5)$, minimal Pati–Salam and minimal $SO(10)$.

The ranking, most compatible first.

$$ \text{Pati–Salam}\;9\ =\ \text{left–right}\;9\ >\ \text{axion}\;8\ >\ \text{seesaw}\;7\ >\ \text{extra dims}\;7\ >\ \text{SM}\;6\ =\ \text{split SUSY}\;6\ =\ \text{composite}\;6\ >\ SO(10)\;5\ >\ E_6\;2 $$

The five axes.

$$ A\ \text{admission}\quad B\ \text{matter}\quad C\ \text{chirality}\quad D\ \text{scalars}\quad E\ \text{rank}\qquad\text{three points each} $$

The measuring stick, in four numbers.

$$ \dim\mathbb{B}=4,\qquad \dim\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=16,\qquad \mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{u}(2)=2,\qquad \mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{u}(4)=4 . $$

The three sentences that carry the note.

The operator algebra of the biquaternions is the tensor square of the biquaternion algebra, $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$, of dimension sixteen, and one generation of matter has that dimension.

Pati–Salam's sixteen is that tensor square read as a module, its colour-lepton group is the compact real form $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ of the traceless part, and its two weak groups are the two factors.

The right-handed neutrino is a gauge singlet, so it lives in the four-dimensional right family, which is the commutant of the left action; the seesaw's only structural fit is the Majorana real structure $\mathcal{C}$ with $\mathcal{C}^{2}=1$.

The tie, and what it is a tie about. Pati–Salam and the left–right model both score nine. Their groups are nested at equal rank, the difference is one point of $\mathbb{CP}^{3}$, and the point is canonical, so the split between the theories costs no fidelity either way. What separates them is content: Pati–Salam derives charge quantisation from tracelessness inside one simple factor and the left–right model imposes it. The experiments separate them in the opposite direction, which is why the left–right model is the safer theory and Pati–Salam the more falsifiable one.

The caveat that outranks the tie. Neither entry is the framework's own model. The table measures distance to the algebra, and distance is not truth.

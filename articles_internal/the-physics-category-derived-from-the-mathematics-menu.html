Biquaternion Propagation: The Physics Category Derived From the Mathematics Menu

Introduction

The physics category is derived from the mathematics menu, and the derivation is the subject of this article. One hundred and fifty mathematics articles in twenty sections become thirty physics articles in twenty sections, of which one is the introduction. The order is the menu order. Every reading is attached to the section that earns it, and no reading is attached to a section that does not.

$$ 20\ \text{sections},\quad 150\ \text{mathematics articles}\ \longrightarrow\ 30\ \text{physics articles},\ 29\ \text{of them derived}. $$

The word propagation has three senses here, and the article uses all three. The first is the propagation of an object: a result proved once for a general element becomes a statement about a field, and the field is what propagates. The second is the propagation of an order: the physics list inherits the sequence of the mathematics, so that a reader meets the rotation before the spin and the algebra before both. The third is the propagation of a reading: a statement that carries no physics in the mathematics acquires one when it is read against the material and informational sectors, and that reading is what the article is for.

Why the direction matters. A list built from the physics side inherits the physics history, and that history put the spin at the head of The Modules, inside the algebra, and put the spectral reading inside the same block. The mathematics keeps the spin in Geometry, after the whole algebra and after the rotations and the Lorentz group. A list built from the algebra first and repaired afterwards keeps the spin in the wrong place, because the spot was occupied before the repair began; no amount of vocabulary repair moves a section. A list built from the menu puts the spin where the mathematics puts it by construction.

The five rules, and they are the whole method.

rule statement
R1 the order of the physics articles is the order of the mathematics menu, section by section, with no reordering
R2 one physics article to one section of the mathematics menu
R3 a section takes more than one physics article only where the physics has content the mathematics states once, and the exception is named and counted
R4 every mathematics article of the section is visible in the physics article: as a link, as a stated result, or as an interpretation
R5 no physics article exists without at least one interpretation, and a section that yields no interpretation yields no article

The first objection is that compression loses content, and the answer is arithmetic. The ratio is about five to one, and it is not a target: a mathematics article proves one thing, and several of them prove facets of one thing that a reader of physics wants in one place. One hundred and fifty articles become thirty, and the thirty carry the same readings, gathered. What compression loses is the proof, and the proof is one click away in the section the entry names. What it does not lose is a reading, because a reading is stated once, in the entry where a reader needs it, instead of being repeated in four.

The second objection is that a reading is not a result. Correct, and the corpus states it everywhere. A reading is a labelled statement of what an algebraic object does when it is used as a physical object, with its boundary given. It can be false, and the corpus records the readings it has withdrawn. It cannot be proved, because it is a choice of vocabulary and of use. An entry therefore carries three kinds of sentence and keeps them apart: the result, which is the mathematics and is linked; the reading, which is labelled and bounded; and the limit, which says what the reading does not claim.

The third objection is that the mathematics order is not the physics order. A physicist meets the rotation before the spectrum, and the algebra before the rotation, and this article does not claim otherwise: it claims that the mathematics order is that order, and that the fault was the physics list's and not the menu's. The evidence is the spin. The mathematics puts it after the Lorentz group because a spin is a representation of the rotation; a physics list that puts it before the frame has to introduce a representation of an object it has not met.

The fourth objection is that the archive is a graveyard. It is not, and the archive is the reason the derivation can be done at all. Each archived article already holds the reading that an entry now carries, at length, with its boundaries worked out. The derivation is a move of readings from a file per result to a section per subject, and the archived file stays whole and reachable so that the move can be checked.

The letters. The mathematics is Biquaternions and the physics is AI Physics; the menus are maths.md and physics.md, the folders articles_maths and articles_physics. The sections of the mathematics menu are numbered $1$ to $20$ in the census below, and the physics entries are labelled $\mathrm{I}.1$ to $\mathrm{IV}.6$ in the order they appear. An entry label is a place in a list and not a claim about its object.

Boundaries. This article states the derivation, the readings and their limits. It proves nothing, it derives no physics, and it does not claim that thirty is the right number: it claims that thirty is what the reading costs. It reports the state of the derivation as counted at the time of writing, including the entries that are titled and not yet written.

Why the analysis compresses hardest. Thirty-six articles become four because the analysis is a chain of one construction: the derivative, its square, its root, its kernel, its integral and its flow are six statements about one element, and a reader of physics wants them in one place. The compression is therefore not a thinning of the subject; it is the recognition that a chain of operators is one object read at several orders.

The Rule of Derivation

Rule R1 is the order rule. The physics articles follow the mathematics sections in sequence, and no section is moved for a rhetorical reason. The cost is that a reader meets the idempotents before the metric; the benefit is that every later entry may cite an earlier one without a forward reference. A forward reference is the symptom of a list built from the answer rather than from the object.

Rule R2 is the one-to-one rule. One section, one entry. The rule is stated as a default because it is breakable in exactly two places, and both are named below. Its purpose is to make the census checkable: if the numbers of sections and of entries agree, no section was silently dropped and no entry was invented.

Rule R3 is the exception rule. A section takes more than one entry only where the physics has content that the mathematics states once, and the exception is named and counted. Four sections qualify: section $10$ takes five entries, section $12$ three, section $16$ two and section $19$ four. Each is argued below.

Rule R4 is the visibility rule. Every mathematics article of the section appears in the entry, as a link, as a stated result, or as an interpretation. This is the rule that prevents the compression from becoming an omission: an entry may summarise a proof, but it may not lose the article. The check is mechanical, and it is run at the end of each wave of work.

Rule R5 is the reading rule. No entry exists without at least one interpretation, and a section that yields no interpretation yields no article. The rule has a consequence that is easy to miss: it makes the physics list a function of the readings and not of the mathematics, so a section whose mathematics is beautiful and whose reading is empty would produce nothing.

The first exception, the spectral strata. The mathematics proves the four sectors in four articles because each proof is a proof. The physics reads four regimes, and a regime is a thing a physicist names and uses: mass in motion, the rest frame, the light ray, the vacuum. Folding them into one entry would put four dispersion relations in one file and lose the naming that the rest of the menu relies on.

The second exception, the remarkable subspaces. The mathematics states the forms and the subspaces in two articles. The physics reads them as a state space: the two sectors as two geometries, the four-vector as the object, and the state–observable–probability triple as the measurement theory. That triple is the largest single reading in the corpus and it does not exist on the mathematics side at all, because it is a reading and not a theorem.

The third exception, the harmonic analysis. Section $16$ states a transform and the duality it carries, and the physics reads them separately: one entry for the transform as a change of description, one for wave–particle duality as the withdrawal of the wave arm. The two readings share the mathematics and do not share a reader.

The fourth exception, the spin. Section $19$ defines the carrier once and then proves four articles for each of three values. The physics defines the carrier once and gives one entry per value, because a spin is read once per value and the definition is not repeated.

The one place the physics loses a division. The mathematics separates the four general products, the four forms and the three norms, because each is a separate construction. The physics keeps three entries, one to each section, and adds no fourth for the relations between them: the relations are stated inside the products entry as the table of what each product preserves.

The Readings, Part I: The Algebra

This part carries thirteen entries over eighty mathematics articles. It is the part where the compression is most visible, because the algebra is where the mathematics is most detailed. Each entry below gives the source section, the count, and the readings it carries; several readings are the archived articles' own, kept and moved.

I.1 — The Element and the Six Subspaces

Source: section $1$, eight articles. The entry states the element, the eight real coordinates, the four conjugations, the group of involutions, the mirror, the six subspaces with their decompositions, the square of an element, the zero divisors and the units, and the square roots.

The reading, in five sentences. The eight coordinates are two ledgers, an informational time and space and a material time and space, and the four blocks are the four slots of one element. The six subspaces are named physically: the centre is complex time, the vector subspace complex space, the quaternion and anti-quaternion subspaces the real and imaginary four-dimensional spaces, the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces the informational and material sectors. The Klein four-group of involutions is parity, time reversal, their product and charge conjugation, and the orbit sizes one, two and four count how many descriptions a discrete operation leaves distinct. The mirror is the second reading of one element, with the $i$ moved from the temporal slot to the three spatial ones, so a four-momentum and its observable are one object seen twice. The square of an element is mass and motion at once, and the zero divisors are the horizon of reversibility, met on the material sector as the light cone.

I.2 — The Matrix Images

Source: section $2$, four articles. The entry states the families of matrix representations and the axis-free and Pauli forms in $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ and $M_2(\mathbb{C})$.

The reading. The $2\times2$ image is the qubit and the $4\times4$ image is the regular picture, and the two are one element described twice. The Pauli correspondence is not an analogy but an identification of one element with one matrix, and the factor $i$ in the correspondence is the complex structure that makes it possible. The determinant is read four ways on the two images: as the interval, as the causal type, as the mixedness of a state, and as the volume of the transformation. The central phase is the fibre of the $2\times2$ image, and the double cover is the spin. The boundary is that the two images are not two theories: nothing in the physics may depend on which image a calculation uses.

I.3 — The Idempotents, the Ideals and the Observables

Source: section $3$, seven articles. The entry states the nilpotents, the idempotents, the minimal left ideals, the Peirce decomposition and its basis, the companion nilpotents and the special decomposition.

The reading. I.4 — The Modules

Source: section $4$, five articles. The entry states the regular module, the polarized and unpolarized modules, the Pauli module and the category of modules.

The reading. A module is what the algebra acts on, and the corpus has one module with many descriptions. I.5 — The Spectrum

Source: section $5$, six articles. The entry states the spectrum, the centraliser, the semisimple–nilpotent split, the eigenbasis, the worked examples and the Jordan basis.

I.10 — The Spectral Strata

Source: section $10$, six articles, and five entries. The partition is one entry; the four regimes are four, because a regime is a thing the physics names.

The partition. Two invariants, the mass and the motion, cut the algebra into four strata, and the independence of the two conditions is the content: a stratum is a pair of conditions and not a single scale. The product of two unipotents is the proof that the strata are not closed, and it is the one result of the section that has no regime of its own.

Mass in motion, $\Sigma_{11}$. The region a massive particle occupies. The pair sum is the production of mass by addition, which is the algebraic statement that mass is not additive the way energy is: two masses in relative motion have a total mass larger than their sum, and the cross term is the motion.

The rest frame and the exceptional point, $\Sigma_{10}$. The frame in which the clock is the mass, and the coincidence of the eigenvalues as the boundary of the two-mode picture. The physical content is that a rest frame exists exactly where the two frequencies coincide, so the exceptional point is not a pathology but the resting state.

The massless regime, $\Sigma_{01}$. The light ray. There is no rest frame, the polar word degenerates to one term, and the cone is the set on which the reading of the previous entry has to be replaced by a boundary value.

The vacuum and the nilpotent ladder, $\Sigma_{00}$. The vacuum, the ladder, and the statement that the apex is the only square-zero element of the material closure. The ladder is the discrete structure inside the degenerate stratum, and its two ends are the vacuum and the first rung.

The Readings, Part III: The Analysis

III.2, second entry — Wave–Particle Duality and the Fourier Transform

Source: the same section, read separately. The entry states the two quadratic forms of the algebra, the element form and the gradient form, and the condition under which the transform turns the divergence into a dot product and the operator into a number: the coefficients must be constant.

The reading. The transform is the switch that carries an equation from the differential arm to the algebraic arm, and duality is the name of the switch. The Klein–Gordon and Dirac cases are the two worked instances, where the shell is a solvability condition of a quadratic symbol and the block determinant is a fourth power of one number. The reading stops where the coefficients stop being constant: a constant coefficient passes through whatever its commutation, and a constant non-central one translates the shell. The non-quantum limit is the withdrawal of the wave arm, which leaves the continuity equation exact.

Why this entry exists. The mathematics states a transform and a duality in one section, and a reader of physics uses them for two different things: a change of description, and the statement of where a wave has a particle. The split is the third exception to the one-to-one rule, and it costs one entry.

The carrier and the closure

A carrier is a nonzero real subspace $W\subseteq\mathbb{B}$ that is invariant and irreducible under one of the algebra's own actions: left multiplication, or conjugation by the group of invertibles. The action is part of the carrier, because the same subspace can be reducible for one and irreducible for the other. The closure is the space a field takes values in: the carrier enlarged by whatever invariance and the norm require.

carrier set $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}$ $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}$ action spin
the centre $\mathbb{B}_{C}=\mathbb{C}e_t$ $2$ $1$ conjugation, fixed pointwise $0$
the ideals $S_{\pm}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$ $4$ $2$ left multiplication $\tfrac12$
the material sector $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{ie_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$ $4$ — conjugation $1$, on its traceless part

There are three, and no more, and the reason is module theory in one line. Every finite-dimensional module of $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a direct sum of copies of the two-dimensional simple module, so the left action sees only $-\tfrac12$; the adjoint action adds the trivial centre and the three-dimensional traceless part, so it sees $0$ and $1$. A fourth value would need a second simple module, and there is none.

The closure repairs two things and not a third. It restores invariance where the carrier alone is not closed under the field equation, which is the case for the half-integer carrier. It restores the norm, which the ideal alone does not carry, since every element of a minimal left ideal has $N=0$. It does not repair the vanishing norm: a single value of a spin-half field is always massless, and the mass of the field is the pairing of its two values.

Entry IV.2 — The Carriers, the Spin and the Closure

Source: section $19$, the carriers article. The entry states the definition, the three carriers with their dimensions, the two repairs the closure performs, and the one thing it does not repair. Its reading is the sentence every field entry needs: a field is a function with values in the closure of its carrier, and its spin is the weight of the carrier. This is the one place in the category where the word spin is defined before it is used.

Entry IV.3 — The Spin-Zero Carrier: The Centre

Source: section $19$, the four spin-zero articles. The real line is the carrier and the complex line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is the closure, so the phase is the one degree of freedom the closure adds to the carrier. On the complex line the norm is $\lambda^{2}$, real and of one sign, so the mass shell is a single sheet. The adjoint action fixes every point, so the weight is $0$ with multiplicity two and the generator is zero: the clock does not tick, and the only evolution is the phase. The carrier meets the rest frame and the vacuum and nothing else, and the exclusion of the other two strata is structural, since a central element has no vector part.

Entry IV.4 — The Spin-Half Carrier: The Bispinors

Source: section $19$, the five spin-half articles. The plane is the carrier and the spinor space is its closure; the axis is a parameter of the family and not a datum of the algebra, so the algebra carries infinitely many of each and singles out none. Every element of the ideal has rank at most one, so $N=0$ at every point and a single value of the field is always massless; the mass of the field is the pairing of its two parts,

$$ N(\tilde\Xi_L+\tilde\Xi_R)=2B(\tilde\Xi_L,\tilde\Xi_R), $$

which is the algebra's statement that a Dirac mass is a mixing of two chiralities. Left multiplication acts with weights $\pm\tfrac12$, so the two chiral rates, and the exceptional point is where they coincide. The closure meets all four strata, the carrier meets two, and the difference is the content of the sector entry.

The two steps, and why there are two. For spin $0$ the closure adds one real line and stops: the centre. For spin $1$ the closure adds one real line and stops: the anti-Hermitian sector. For spin $\tfrac12$ the closure adds a transverse direction, giving the minimal left ideal, and then adds the conjugate ideal, giving the bispinor space. The first step is forced by the rotation action, since no real two-dimensional subspace is invariant under the whole group; the second is forced by the mass, since the coupling relates the two ideals and the field must be able to occupy both.

Entry IV.5 — The Spin-One Carrier: The Material Sector

Source: section $19$, the four spin-one articles. The triple is the carrier and the material sector is its closure. The extra direction is the generator of the central phase and not the identity: the fourth slot of the four-potential is the temporal direction $ie_t$ and not a scalar. The norm is real, of signature $(3,1)$ in the coefficients the corpus writes, so the mass shell is one sheet of one cone. The two-sided action gives $+1$, $-1$ and $0$, the integers of the vector representation. The carrier reaches the region of mass in motion, and it is the only one that does, because it is the only closure on which both invariants are real and the vector square is a sum of squares; the nilpotent stratum inside it is the origin alone.

Why the carrier is the material sector and not the informational one

The two sectors look alike, and up to isomorphism they are alike. Write the coefficients in the four slots $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$. The material sector is $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\{Q_t\in i\mathbb{R},\ \vec Q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\}$ and the informational sector is $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}=\{Q_t\in\mathbb{R},\ \vec Q\in i\mathbb{R}^{3}\}$. Both are four real dimensional, both are invariant under $Q\mapsto uQu^{*}$, and both split as a fixed line plus a three-dimensional part:

$$ \mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=V_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}(ie_t),\qquad \mathbb{B}_{\Psi}=iV_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}e_t, $$

with $V_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$. The three-dimensional summand carries the vector representation in both cases, and since $i$ is central the map $Q\mapsto iQ$ is an isomorphism of conjugation modules carrying one split onto the other, the fixed line onto the fixed line and the vector part onto the vector part. So the algebra does not select between the sectors: they are the same carrier read twice, and the claim that one of them is the spin-one space has to come from somewhere else.

What selects is the metric convention. The corpus writes the four-position as $ict\,e_t+x\vec e_x+y\vec e_y+z\vec e_z$, so the four-momentum of a particle is

$$ \tilde P_{\Phi}=\frac{iE}{c}\,e_t+p_x\vec e_x+p_y\vec e_y+p_z\vec e_z, $$

an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ whose four coefficients are real numbers times the unit $(i,1,1,1)$. The interval is the norm on that element, $-m^{2}c^{2}$, and the whole material reading — the mass shell, the Lorentz action by rotor conjugation, the four-potential of the Proca equation — is a reading of this one form. Had the corpus put the imaginary unit on the three spatial coefficients instead, $ct'\,e_t+i(x'\vec e_x+y'\vec e_y+z'\vec e_z)$, the physical four-momentum would be an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ and the interval would be the norm there, with the opposite sign; and that is exactly the mirror, $\tilde P_{\Psi}=i\tilde P_{\Phi}$, whose norm is $+m^{2}c^{2}$ against $-m^{2}c^{2}$.

So the sentence the spin-one carrier is the material sector is a statement about the corpus's coordinates and not a theorem of the algebra. The theorem is that there are three carrier types and no fourth, and that the spin-one type has two real forms. The corpus uses the form on which time is imaginary, because that is the convention its interval is written in, and the mirror form is not a rival carrier but the observable reading of the same object.

What is genuinely asymmetric is the role, not the carrier. On $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ the Hermitian form is positive on the cone of states and the Born weight is a pairing with it, which is what makes that sector a state space; on $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ the form that is real and indefinite is the norm, which is what makes that sector an object space. Two sectors, two real forms of one carrier, two roles. The carrier is one; the physics of the two readings is not thereby identified.

The verdict, stated so that it can be checked. The carrier of the spin-one field is the real traceless part $V_{\mathbb{R}}$ and its closure is $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, because the metric convention of the corpus puts the imaginary unit on the temporal coefficient. The alternative closure $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ is the mirror, and it is the state space. Nothing in the algebra prefers one; the coordinates do.

Why four entries and not fourteen

The mathematics needs fourteen articles because fourteen proofs. The physics needs four because there are four things to say: what carries the spin, and then one entry per spin. The four readings inside each per-spin entry are the four mathematics articles of that subsection, and the physics entry is their joint reading. This is the place where the compression happens, and it is why the block is the test case: if a reader of the four entries can reconstruct the fourteen articles' results, rule R4 holds; if not, the block has lost content and the derivation is wrong there.

What the Order Fixes

The first fault was the spin at the head of the modules. The old list read the algebra, then the frame, then the modules, and put the spin inside the module block, which introduced the carrier before the rotation whose representation it is. The repair is not a rewording: a spin is a weight of an action, and the action is the rotation, so the spin belongs after the algebra and after the Lorentz group, where the mathematics puts it. The cost of the old order was that the three carrier values arrived as a table to be memorised rather than as the consequence of one theorem about modules.

The second fault was the spectral reading inside the algebra. The old list put the spectrum, the degeneracy and the strata in one block, which made the mass and the motion one subject and left the four regimes without names. The repair separates the spectrum, a property of an element, from the strata, regions cut by two invariants, and gives each regime its own entry. The gain is that a reader can say the rest frame and the massless regime and mean a region of the algebra.

The third fault was the frame after the modules. The old list put the state space before the group that preserves it, so unitarity arrived as a postulate and the double cover arrived as a remark. The repair follows the menu: the groups come in the geometry, after the algebra and the analysis, and unitarity is a theorem about a central unit rather than an assumption.

What the order does not fix. It does not make a reading true. A derivation can put the spin in the right place and still read it wrongly, and the corpus's method of labels, boundaries and withdrawn readings is the guard against that. The order fixes what a reader meets first, and nothing else.

The Limits

The derivation adds no physics. Every reading in the entries was already in the archive or in the mathematics. What the derivation adds is an order, a partition of the material into entries, and the discipline that an entry exists only when it carries a reading.

The number thirty is not sacred. It is what the reading costs today. A section whose reading splits into two regimes would take two entries and a section whose two readings merge would take one; the rules decide, and the census is recomputed rather than defended.

The ratio is not a target. Five to one is an outcome, and a section that compresses ten to one is not better than one that compresses three to one. The right compression is the one that keeps every reading and drops every repetition.

A reading is not a result. The entries are labelled so that a reader can tell which sentence is proved and which is read, and the limits are stated so that a reader can tell what a reading does not claim. A reading stated without its boundary is a defect, not a simplification.

The archive is not maintained. It is a source, it is not a second reading of the subject, and it does not receive corrections. A correction goes into the entry, and the archived file is the record of what was thought when the reading was first worked out.

The mathematics is not reordered. The derivation takes the menu as given. If a section sits in the wrong place in the mathematics, the fault is the mathematics', and the derivation's answer is a note and not a move.

Summary

The physics category is derived from the mathematics menu and it inherits the menu's order. One hundred and fifty mathematics articles in twenty sections become thirty entries in twenty sections: twenty-nine derived entries and one introduction.

One entry to one section, with four named exceptions. Section $10$ takes five entries because a regime is a thing the physics names; section $12$ takes three because a state space is a reading the mathematics does not have; section $16$ takes two because a transform and the duality it carries are used for two different things; section $19$ takes four because a carrier is defined once and a spin is read once per value.

Every entry carries at least one reading, and the reading is why it exists. The readings are the archive's, moved and not invented: the mirror and the switch, the square-zero element, the probability band, the cone and the massless spectrum, the three carriers, the state space.

The spin block is four entries and it is the test. The carriers entry defines the carrier and the closure, and the three per-spin entries carry the subspace, the norm, the spectrum and the strata for one value each. Fourteen mathematics articles, four physics articles, fourteen readings.

The spin-one carrier is the material sector, and the reason is the metric convention. Both sectors are invariant under conjugation, both split $1+3$ with the vector representation on the three-dimensional summand, and multiplication by $i$ intertwines the two actions, so the algebra does not choose between them. What chooses is the corpus's convention that time carries the imaginary unit: the four-momentum $iE/c\,e_t+\vec p$ is an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and the interval is the norm there. The mirror is the same object in the other convention, with the norm's sign flipped, and $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ keeps the role that is not a carrier: the $1+3$ split there is the Bloch decomposition of a state.

The order is the repair. The spin moves to the geometry, after the rotations and the Lorentz group; the spectrum separates from the strata; the groups come after the algebra rather than before the state space.

Nothing is deleted and the open work is counted. Forty-one articles are archived as sources, three carry over, and eight derived entries are titled and not yet written. A dead link is reported as a dead link.

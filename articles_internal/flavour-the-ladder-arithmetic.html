This is the first of four notes that replace the single long note Flavour Research: The Ladder Arithmetic. It carries the measurement, the exact algebra, the two new statements that give the number a meaning, the invariance statement that removes an apparent contradiction, and the answer to the fourth-rung question. Introduction

Koide's relation is one measured number and no derivation. The number is $Q=2/3$ for the three charged leptons, and it holds to six parts in a million.

This note asks three questions and answers them with algebra rather than with models.

The first question is what the number means. The ratio is exactly the purity of a share vector, so $Q$ is the inverse of an effective count of participants and $2/3$ says the mass is shared as if among one and a half of three. That reading is exact, it is not an interpretation, and it makes the target of any future derivation a statement about concentration rather than about a numeral.

The second question is what a fourth mass can do. The ratio extended by one further mass has a unique minimum at a computable mass, and the minimum is $Q_0/(1+Q_0)$. For $Q_0=2/3$ that is exactly $2/5$. The floor therefore sits at a mass the theorem predicts and not at a mass one fits, and the charm mass sits there. Restoring $2/3$, or the four-rung closure $1/2$, would need a mass five to twenty-five times the tau, which the data exclude.

The third question is what can move the ratio at all. The ratio is homogeneous of degree zero, and all three charged leptons share one electric charge, so one-loop QED running multiplies all three masses by one common factor and cannot move $Q$ at all. The $0.19\%$ drift quoted in the literature is a pole-to-$\overline{\rm MS}$ conversion effect, which is flavour-dependent, and it is the object Sumino's cancellation is built to remove. Three numbers that the corpus read as contradictory are therefore three statements about three different objects.

The rest of the note is the arithmetic that the previous note carried: the circle as an ansatz, the parity argument and its withdrawal, the fits, the open ladder, four failed routes to a fourth rung, the partner map, and the count of what an accident would cost.

1. The One Measured Relation

[PUBLISHED] Koide's relation, stated in 1981 for the charged leptons and not derived since, is a statement about two mass ratios. Printed in masses it is a sum of masses over the square of a sum of their roots; dividing every radicand by $m_1$ turns every mass into a ratio to $m_1$ and every root of a mass into the root of a ratio, so what remains is a function of the two ratios.

[MEASURED] Writing $\rho_i=m_i/m_1$ with $m_1=m_e$, the PDG masses give

$\sqrt{\rho_2}$ $14.379\,44$
$\rho_3=m_\tau/m_e$ $3477.228\,28$
$\sqrt{\rho_3}$ $58.968\,03$
$1+\rho_2+\rho_3$ $3684.996\,56$

$$ Q=\frac{1+\rho_2+\rho_3}{\left(1+\sqrt{\rho_2}+\sqrt{\rho_3}\right)^{2}}=\frac{2}{3}. $$

Every number in the display is dimensionless, and no mass unit appears: a change of mass unit, or of mass standard, cancels between top and bottom. This is the form used for the rest of the note.

[MEASURED] On the 2024 PDG masses the same ratio gives $0.666\,664\,46$, so the two mass sets agree with each other and both agree with $2/3$. The residual is dominated by the tau mass, which is the least well known.

The relation holds to six parts in a million for the one triple it was written for. That is the whole empirical content of the charged-lepton ladder, and it is worth more than everything else in this note.

[MEASURED] It does not hold for the other two triples:

$d,s,b$ $0.7314277$ fails by $0.065$

So the relation is a one-off, or it is a rule that quarks violate for a reason nobody has supplied. Both readings are open and this note keeps both.

2. Five Exact Statements

The statements below are exact. None needs a medium, a spectrum or a dynamics. They are the entire content of the closure language, and stating them plainly is what removes the appearance of derivation.

2.1 The bound. [ALGEBRA] For positive masses and $\rho_i=m_i/m_1$,

$$ Q=\frac{\sum \rho_i}{\left(\sum \sqrt{\rho_i}\right)^{2}}\le 1, $$

by Cauchy–Schwarz, with equality if and only if exactly one $\sqrt{\rho_i}$ is nonzero. The normalisation by $m_1$ is free, since $Q$ is invariant under $\sqrt{\rho_i}\to\lambda \sqrt{\rho_i}$; it is made once here so that $\sqrt{\rho_1}=1$ and nothing below carries a unit.

2.2 The rung count. [ALGEBRA] Setting $Q=2/N$ in 2.1 gives $N\ge2$. The case $N=2$ requires $Q=1$, hence one massless rung by the equality condition, which is excluded by data. So

$$ N\ge3 . $$

This is the only place the closure touches the number of generations, and it is weak: it excludes two and permits any count above two. It is not a derivation of three, and this note does not present it as one.

2.3 The variance form. [ALGEBRA] With $\mu=\frac1N\sum \sqrt{\rho_i}$ and $\operatorname{var}=\frac1N\sum(\sqrt{\rho_i}-\mu)^{2}$,

$$ Q=\frac{2}{N}\iff\operatorname{var}=\mu^{2}, $$

so the root vector $\vec r=\left(1,\sqrt{\rho_2},\sqrt{\rho_3}\right)$ sits at $45^{\circ}$ to the uniform direction $\vec u=(1,1,1)$. Measured for the leptons, $\operatorname{var}/\mu^{2}=0.999\,981\,5$ against $1$. The deviation is two parts in $10^{5}$, three times the six parts in a million by which $Q$ misses $2/3$, because $Q\mapsto3Q-1$ has slope three; it is one number in another coordinate.

2.4 The circle. [ALGEBRA] For any $\mu>0$ and any $\theta$,

$$ \sqrt{\rho_i}=\mu\left(1+\sqrt2\cos\!\left(\theta+\frac{2\pi i}{3}\right)\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; Q=\frac{2}{3}\quad\text{identically}, $$

because $\sum\cos=0$ and $\sum\cos^{2}=3/2$ for three equally spaced phases. This is exact for every $\theta$ and every $\mu$, and $\mu$ is the mean of the three $\sqrt{\rho_i}$. For the charged leptons $\mu=24.782\,49$ in the normalisation $\sqrt{\rho_1}=1$.

2.4 is the reason this programme is hard to assess, and the note should say it once and plainly: the circle contains no information beyond Koide. Two parameters, three masses, and one degree of freedom left over; the fit consumes that degree of freedom by construction. A test that the leptons lie on the circle is a test of $Q=2/3$ and of nothing else. Any claim that the circle explains or reproduces the closure is a circular claim, and this note does not make it.

2.5 The midpoint. [ALGEBRA] For $N$ rungs the ratio is confined to $[1/N,1]$, the floor being the degenerate triple and the ceiling the single-mass triple. The closure value is $2/N$ and the midpoint of the interval is $(N+1)/(2N)$. Then

$$ \frac{N+1}{2N}=\frac{2}{N}\iff N+1=4\iff N=3 . $$

$2$ $1.000$ $0.750$ no
$3$ $0.667$ $0.667$ yes
$4$ $0.500$ $0.625$ no
$5$ $0.400$ $0.600$ no

Three is the only count for which the closure value is the middle of its own allowed range. So the sentence "$Q=2/3$" and the sentence "three rungs" are the same sentence, once the bound is known. This is a sharpening of what the number means; it is not extra evidence, and this note does not count it twice.

3. The Ratio Is a Purity

[ALGEBRA] Let

$$ p_i=\frac{\sqrt{m_i}}{\sum_j\sqrt{m_j}}, $$

so that $p_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i p_i=1$. The share vector is normalised in the sum and not in the square. Then the closure ratio is exactly its squared length:

$$ \sum_i p_i^{2}=\frac{\sum_i m_i}{\left(\sum_j\sqrt{m_j}\right)^{2}}=Q . $$

[MEASURED] On the 2024 PDG masses the three shares are $0.013\,450$, $0.193\,406$ and $0.793\,144$, and their squares sum to $0.666\,664$. The identity is not approximate; the two numbers agree to machine precision because they are one number written twice.

What the quantity is. $\sum p_i^2$ is the standard measure of how concentrated a distribution is. It is $1$ when one participant holds everything and $1/N$ when $N$ participants hold equal shares, and it is called the purity, the collision probability, or the inverse participation ratio depending on the field that uses it. It is the quantity whose reciprocal is the effective number of participants,

$$ N_{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{Q}, $$

the count that would produce the same concentration if all shares were equal.

[MEASURED] The leptons give $N_{\rm eff}=1.500\,003$. The relation $Q=2/3$ therefore says: the mass of the charged leptons is shared as if by one and a half participants out of three.

tuple $Q$ $N_{\rm eff}$ reading
three equal masses $0.333\,333$ $3$ all three carry the mass
charged leptons $0.666\,660$ $1.500\,014$ one and a half of three
one mass only $1.000\,000$ $1$ one participant holds all

The table is the content of the reading. The bottom row is the ceiling of 2.1 and the top row is the floor of 2.5, and the lepton row is the one measured case in between.

Why this is worth stating. The ratio was introduced as a quotient of two sums, and a quotient of two sums carries no obvious meaning. Written as a purity, the same number has a name, a range, and an interpretation that does not depend on the mass spectrum being a spectrum. The question "why $2/3$" becomes "why is the mass-share of the three leptons as concentrated as that of three halves of an equal share", which is a statement about a point on a simplex. A derivation that produced a purity would be a derivation of something.

What the reading does not do. It does not derive $2/3$, and it does not select three. A purity of $2/3$ is a perfectly ordinary value: it lies at two thirds of the way from the democratic end to the single-participant end, and there is no reason a priori for a share vector to avoid it. The reading converts a numeral into a shape, and a shape still needs a mechanism.

The three readings agree. The variance form of 2.3, Foot's angle of the companion note, and the purity here are one statement in three coordinates: $\sigma=\mu$, $\varphi=45^{\circ}$, and $N_{\rm eff}=3/2$ are the same condition on the same vector. A reader who checks all three has checked it once.

4. Adding a Rung: A Unique Optimum and the Value $2/5$

[PUBLISHED] The ratio extended by one further mass has a unique minimum, and both the location and the value are computable in closed form. This is the theorem of A minimization theorem for the Koide ratio and its Standard Model calibration (arXiv 2605.09651), and it is elementary.

[ALGEBRA] Write $S=\sum_i m_i$ and $R=\sum_i\sqrt{m_i}$ for a fixed tuple, and let $x$ be the added mass. Then

$$ Q(x)=\frac{S+x}{\left(R+\sqrt x\right)^{2}} . $$

Putting $t=\sqrt x$,

$$ \frac{dQ}{dt}=\frac{2\left(tR-S\right)}{\left(R+t\right)^{3}}, $$

so the derivative vanishes at exactly one point, $t=S/R$, and the second derivative is positive there. The minimum is unique and it is at

$$ m_{*}=\left(\frac{S}{R}\right)^{2}=\frac{\left(\sum_i m_i\right)^{2}}{\left(\sum_i\sqrt{m_i}\right)^{2}} . $$

[ALGEBRA] The value at the minimum follows by substitution. With $u=S/R$ and $Q_0=S/R^{2}$ the closure ratio of the original tuple,

$$ Q(m_{*})=\frac{S+u^{2}}{\left(R+u\right)^{2}} =\frac{S\left(R^{2}+S\right)/R^{2}}{\left(R^{2}+S\right)^{2}/R^{2}} =\frac{S}{R^{2}+S}=\frac{Q_0}{1+Q_0} . $$

[ALGEBRA] The same computation for $k$ equal added masses gives $Q_0/(1+kQ_0)$. Two consequences follow at once, and both are exact.

The first consequence is the floor of the extension. If the triple closes at $Q_0=2/3$, then the smallest value a fourth mass can produce is

$$ \frac{2/3}{1+2/3}=\frac{2}{5}, $$

and $k$ equal additions give $2/(2k+3)$, that is $2/5$, $2/7$, $2/9$ for $k=1,2,3$. The sequence $2/(2k+3)$ is the floor when $k$ rungs are added optimally, and it is not the closure $2/N$ of the enlarged tower, which is $1/2$ at four rungs. The floor answers "how small can the ratio be made by adding a member"; the closure answers "what must the ratio be if the enlarged tower closes". A search for a fourth rung has to say which of the two it is testing.

The second consequence is a meaning for the added mass. The optimum is $m_{*}=Q_0S$, the closure ratio times the total mass of the tuple. For the leptons, with $S=1883.03$ MeV and $Q_0=0.666\,664$, that is

$$ m_{*}=1.255\,40\ \mathrm{GeV}, $$

and the minimum value is $Q_4^{\min}=0.399\,999\,21$, which is $2/5$ to two parts in a million and equals it exactly when $Q_0=2/3$ exactly.

[MEASURED] The charm mass sits $1.2\%$ above $m_{*}$, and the four-mass ratio is

$$ Q(e,\mu,\tau,c)=0.400\,002\,42 $$

with $m_c=1.27$ GeV, which is $6.1$ ppm above $2/5$. The value is flat in the added mass near the optimum, which is what a minimum is: at $m_c=1.25$ GeV the ratio is $0.399\,999\,65$ and at $m_c=1.30$ GeV it is $0.400\,028\,56$. So the agreement is not a fine-tuning of the charm mass; it is the flatness of a minimum, and it would survive a charm mass uncertain by twenty MeV.

$1.2554=m_{*}$ $0.399\,999\,21$ $-2$ ppm

What this is and what it is not. The theorem is exact and the numbers follow from it; that part is arithmetic and belongs in the note. The comparison with charm is a benchmark and not a relation: it mixes pole lepton masses with a short-distance charm mass, and this note records it as the first candidate for a fourth rung rather than as a law. The corpus should adopt the theorem and record the charm number as the best existing candidate for a fourth rung, with its scheme dependence attached.

The curve, and what it settles. $Q(x)$ is not monotone. It falls to $2/5$ at $m_{*}$ and then rises towards $1$ as the added mass grows, so every value above $2/5$ is reached at some mass, and two of those masses are worth naming because the theorem fixes them with no free parameter:

target for the four-tuple mass the theorem requires why it fails
$2/5$, the floor $1.255$ GeV $=m_{*}$ it does not fail: this is where the charm mass sits
$1/2$, the four-rung closure $2/N$ at $N=4$ $9.32$ GeV no lepton exists there: LEP closed the region below $100$ GeV
$2/3$, the three-rung closure $45.2$ GeV same wall, from the other side

So the honest statement is not that no fourth mass restores $2/3$. One does, at $45.2$ GeV, and another closes the four-rung tower at $9.32$ GeV. What the theorem adds is that the only point of the curve in the region the data allow is the floor, because both closing masses sit five and twenty-five tau masses above the tau. The fourth-rung question was never a question about leptons; it is a question about where on $Q(x)$ a spectrum can sit.

Two numbers that are easy to confuse. $2/5$ is the floor of the extension and $1/2$ is the four-rung closure. They answer different questions, and the charm benchmark is a statement about the first, not the second.

5. What Can and Cannot Move the Ratio

[ALGEBRA] $Q$ is homogeneous of degree zero. Under $m_i\to\lambda m_i$ for a common $\lambda$, numerator and denominator both scale by $\lambda$, so

$$ Q(\lambda m)=Q(m) $$

exactly, for every tuple and every $\lambda>0$. Every statement about $Q$ is therefore a statement about the shape of a spectrum and never about its scale.

This makes the running question sharp. A ratio that is invariant under a common rescaling cannot be moved by any correction that acts on all three masses the same way.

[PUBLISHED] At one loop, the three charged leptons have equal electric charge, hence one anomalous dimension (arXiv hep-ph/0601031, arXiv 2606.10060, which states the reason the quark ratios are comparatively stable in the same terms), hence

$$ m_i(\mu)=m_i(\mu_0)\,A(\mu) $$

with a single factor $A$ common to all three. Substituting into the definition, $A$ cancels between numerator and denominator. So one-loop QED running leaves $Q$ exactly invariant, at every scale, for the charged leptons. The same argument holds for the quarks, whose QCD anomalous dimension is also common within a sector: masses of particles with the same gauge quantum numbers share their exponent.

[PUBLISHED] That is not what the literature appears to say. Li and Ma report scale independence (hep-ph/0601031), Xing and Zhang report a $0.19\%$ shift at $M_Z$ (hep-ph/0602134, which also distinguishes pole from running masses), and Sumino builds a mechanism to cancel a $0.1\%$ effect at the pole (arXiv 0812.2090). The three are consistent once the objects are separated.

statement object size
$Q$ is scale independent at one loop the ratio of running masses exact
$Q$ shifts at $M_Z$ the ratio computed from pole masses and from $\overline{\rm MS}$ masses at $M_Z$ $0.19\%$
the QED correction to the relation the pole-to-$\overline{\rm MS}$ conversion, whose finite part carries $\log m_i^{2}$ $0.1\%$

The distinction is that the conversion is flavour-dependent and the running is not. The pole mass differs from the running mass by a finite piece whose infrared cutoff is the mass itself, so the piece carries $\log m_i^{2}$ with a different value for each lepton. That term changes the direction of the amplitude vector and not only its length, which is why it moves $Q$; the running multiplies all three masses and changes neither.

[PUBLISHED] Sumino's family-gauge mechanism adds a correction of the same functional form with the opposite sign, so that $\alpha_F=4\alpha$ makes the two cancel. Read against the table above, the mechanism is aimed at the third row and not the first: it removes the conversion term, and the leading running was never an obstruction.

Three consequences for this note.

The first is that the corpus should stop carrying "does $Q$ run" as an open question. For the leptons the answer is exact at one loop, and the residual is a conversion effect of relative size $10^{-3}$ with a stated cause.

The second is that a mechanism for the relation must kill a flavour-dependent term and not a common one. A model that produces a universal correction to the three masses does nothing to $Q$ by construction, however large the correction.

The third is a target with a number: what remains to be explained is a term of relative size $10^{-3}$ in the ratios, and only a flavour-dependent anomalous dimension, a Yukawa entry, or a threshold can supply it.

6. The Circle as an Ansatz

6.1 The hypothesis, stated once

[ANSATZ] The research hypothesis of this note is that the charged-lepton masses satisfy

$$ \frac{1+\rho_2+\rho_3}{\left(1+\sqrt{\rho_2}+\sqrt{\rho_3}\right)^{2}}=\frac{2}{3} \qquad\text{exactly,} $$

with $\rho_i=m_i/m_1$. The value $2/3$ is assumed. Nothing here derives it, and no display below derives it.

The assumption has one test, and the test is a measurement. The PDG masses give $0.666\,660\,51$, which is $2/3$ to $6.2\times10^{-6}$. That number is the evidence for the hypothesis, and it is all of it.

The hypothesis has one form per coordinate, and the forms are not separate claims. Read as a variance it is $\sigma=\mu$ (2.3). Read as an angle it is $\varphi=45^{\circ}$ (the companion note). Read as a purity it is $N_{\rm eff}=3/2$ (§3). Each is the same assumption written in another coordinate, so no one of them supports another.

The hypothesis is not the same thing as the circle. The circle is algebra and holds for every triple at every $\theta$ (2.4). What is assumed is the value, and the value is what the circle cannot supply.

6.2 The circle as the carrier

[ANSATZ] The physical picture is not the circle, which is algebra, but the equal spacing of the phases, $2\pi/N$ in $\theta$. Nothing in the corpus derives that spacing. It is an assumption about a medium, and it enters at exactly one place.

The table is the whole assessment. A two-parameter family through three points with one identity built in has one degree of freedom and no predictive power. What the ansatz buys is a picture: three rungs on a circle, one near a node. What it costs is that the picture explains nothing, because it cannot fail except by $Q$ failing.

7. Parity: True Inside the Ansatz, False as an Exclusion

[ALGEBRA, inside the ansatz] For even $N$ the phases are antipodal in pairs, and $\cos(\theta+\pi)=-\cos\theta$, so

$$ \sqrt{\rho_i}+\sqrt{\rho_{i+N/2}}=2\mu \qquad\text{exactly, for every }\theta . $$

An even count therefore produces masses in degenerate pairs, symmetric about $\mu$ in the root, and so symmetric about $\mu^{2}$ in mass.

[WITHDRAWN] An earlier version said this excludes every even count by parity and not by $\chi^{2}$, and called it independent of any fit. Both halves are wrong. The pairing is a property of the ansatz at fixed $(\mu,\theta)$, so it depends on the ansatz entirely. And the table above shows the fitted $N=4$ masses are not paired, because the fit moves $\theta$ off the paired configuration to chase the data.

What survives is weaker and still useful: inside the circle ansatz, an even count cannot give a lone light rung above an empty region. The electron is such a rung. So even counts are disfavoured by the shape of the spectrum, and the $N=4$ fit failure of §8 is the same fact in numbers.

10. The Open Ladder and the Continuum Pass

10.1 The open ladder

[ANSATZ] Drop the circle and let the tower be an unbounded power ladder, $a_n\propto n^{p}$.

[ALGEBRA, inside that ansatz] Then $Q_N$ falls like $1/N$ with a computable coefficient,

$$ N\,Q_N\longrightarrow\frac{(p+1)^{2}}{2p+1}, $$

so the tower reproduces the closure $Q_N=2/N$ exactly in the limit when that coefficient is $2$. The condition is $p^{2}-2p-1=0$, so

$$ p=1\pm\sqrt2 , $$

and the ladder with $p=1+\sqrt2$ is the one that asymptotes to the closure rather than to a numeral picked by hand.

branch $p$ root $a_n$ mass exponent character
growing $+2.414214$ $n^{2.414}$ $+4.828427$ sparse, extends upward
decaying $-0.414214$ $n^{0.586}$ $-0.828427$ piles up at zero, unphysical

[FAILED] Normalised at $m_1$ the growing branch gives $1$, $28.41$, $201.25$ against the measured $1$, $206.77$, $3477.2$. It fits nothing at three rungs. It is a statement about a hypothetical dense tower, and the note says so rather than advertising the exactness of $\sqrt2$.

10.2 The continuum pass

11. The Fourth Rung: Four Routes, Four Failures

[ALGEBRA applied to data] The question is whether a fourth charged-lepton mass is predictable. Four routes were tried; all four fail, and the failure is the result.

The theorem of §4 explains all four failures at once. Routes A to C search for a fourth mass that restores $2/3$ at a plausible scale; §4 shows that the mass which does it is $45.2$ GeV, and a sequential charged lepton there is excluded. What survives the four failures is that the closure's own scale is the lepton scale, and any completion of it is two orders of magnitude higher.

11.1 Route D, written out

[ALGEBRA] Shift the indices by one rung:

$$ Q=\frac{1+\rho_3+\rho_4}{\left(1+\sqrt{\rho_3}+\sqrt{\rho_4}\right)^{2}}=\frac{2}{3}, \qquad \rho_i=\frac{m_i}{m_e}, \qquad (m_2,m_3,m_4)=(m_\mu,m_\tau,m_4). $$

With $S=\sqrt{\rho_2}+\sqrt{\rho_3}$ and $T=\rho_2+\rho_3$, clearing the denominator gives a quadratic in $\sqrt{\rho_4}$:

$$ \rho_4-4S\,\sqrt{\rho_4}+3T-2S^{2}=0 . $$

Its discriminant is $12\left(\rho_2+4\sqrt{\rho_2}\sqrt{\rho_3}+\rho_3\right)$, positive for any two positive masses. So the shifted relation never fails to offer a solution: it always has two real roots, one above the pair and one below it. An equation that always has a solution cannot fail, and an equation that cannot fail is not a test.

[MEASURED] The large root is excluded. A sequential charged lepton at $43.7$ GeV would have been pair-produced at LEP II, whose searches closed the region below about $100$ GeV; and a sequential lepton in that range with a neutrino lighter than $m_Z/2$ would have widened the $Z$ lineshape, which counts three light neutrinos. 11.2 Is the substitution circular?

The word circular hides three questions with three answers.

Was $2/3$ fitted to the leptons? [PUBLISHED] No. Koide stated the rational $2/3$ in 1981, before any fit, and §12 counts what that costs. The original agreement of $6.2\times10^{-6}$ is evidence, and this section does not touch it.

Is the recovery of the electron new evidence? [ALGEBRA] No, and it is provable in one line. For an arbitrary third root $z$,

$$ z^{2}-4S z+3T-2S^{2}=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{\rho_2+\rho_3+z^{2}}{\left(\sqrt{\rho_2}+\sqrt{\rho_3}+z\right)^{2}}=\frac{2}{3}. $$

The left side says the pair completes to $z$; the right side says the triple satisfies the closure. They are the same statement. The recovery is therefore not evidence additional to the original, but "not additional" is not "empty": the shared statement is one genuine constraint on three masses.

Is it circular in the vicious sense? [ALGEBRA] No. A vicious circle is a test whose input contains its own output. Here the input is two measured masses and the output is a third measured mass that was not used. That is a legitimate consistency check, and it passes; what it is not is an independent one.

The counting, stated correctly. Three charged-lepton masses and one relation on them means two free parameters at the level of raw data and one fixed by the relation. That is a real reduction of one parameter, and the note says so plainly. What the shifted reading does not supply is a second constraint: substituting $(m_2,m_3,m_4)$ leaves one equation on one unknown, because it is the same equation. One constraint can remove one parameter. It cannot remove two, and it cannot be spent twice.

"One constraint" is not "no constraint". The distinction is the whole of this subsection, and an earlier draft got it wrong by writing that the shifted reading predicts nothing. It predicts the third mass, it predicts it correctly, and it is worth the same as the original statement.

12. The Partner Map and Its Fixed Point

[ALGEBRA] One thing survives the failure of route D, and it is algebra rather than physics. Treat the large root as a map on a pair of roots:

$$ P(x,y)=2(x+y)+\sqrt{3\left(x^{2}+4xy+y^{2}\right)} . $$

Iterating from $(\sqrt{\rho_2},\sqrt{\rho_3})$ gives a tower, in GeV:

rung $e$ $\mu$ $\tau$ $4$ $5$ $6$ $7$ $8$
mass $0.000511$ $0.1057$ $1.7769$ $43.686$ $988.80$ $22\,767$ $522\,317$ $1.199\times10^{7}$

The root ratios converge fast: $4.9585$, $4.7575$, $4.7984$, $4.7898$, $4.7916$. The limit is exact, and it is not the value one gets by taking $x/y\to0$:

$$ r=2\left(1+\frac1r\right)+\sqrt{3\left(1+\frac4r+\frac1{r^{2}}\right)} \;\Longrightarrow\; r^{4}-4r^{3}-3r^{2}-4r+1=0 , $$

which is palindromic, so $t=r+1/r$ obeys $t^{2}-4t-5=0$, giving $t=5$ and

$$ r_{*}=\frac{5+\sqrt{21}}{2}=4.791287847 , \qquad r_{*}^{2}=\frac{23+5\sqrt{21}}{2}=22.956439 . $$

Two warnings, because both are easy to get wrong.

[ALGEBRA] The naive asymptotic $2+\sqrt3=3.732051$ is not the fixed point. It is what the map gives when the lower root is negligible, and at the fixed point the lower root is $1/4.79$ of the upper, not negligible. The correct equation keeps both terms and gives $4.79$.

[ALGEBRA] The fixed point is a property of the map, not of the leptons:

It has no constant spacing at all until it is far above the data. So the equal-phase ansatz of §6 and this map are not two descriptions of one object, and neither of them is the measured spectrum.

13. Is $2/3$ an Accident? A Counted Answer

13.1 What has to be counted

Three triples are in the data: the charged leptons and the two quark sectors. One of them closes to six parts in a million and the other two miss by $0.18$ and $0.065$. The claim "accident" says that the closing triple is an unremarkable draw from the space of possible triples. That claim is testable, because the space of triples has a measure.

[ALGEBRA] Fix the direction of the root vector and let it be uniform in the positive octant, which is the natural measure for three positive masses with no scale. Since $Q$ depends on direction only, a direction has one coordinate, $c=\cos\varphi\in[1/\sqrt3,1]$, uniform in $c$. With $Q=1/(3c^{2})$,

$$ f_Q(q)=\frac{q^{-3/2}}{2\sqrt3\left(1-1/\sqrt3\right)}=0.6830\,q^{-3/2}, \qquad \int_{1/3}^{1}f_Q\,dq=1 . $$

The density falls from $3.55$ at $Q=1/3$ to $0.683$ at $Q=1$, so the democratic end is the crowded end. At $Q=2/3$ the density is $1.2548$.

The window is fixed by the data, not chosen: $Q$ is measured to $\pm6.2\times10^{-6}$ of $2/3$. The probability that one structureless triple lands in that window is

$$ P=2\,(6.2\times10^{-6})\,(1.2548)=1.56\times10^{-5}, $$

which is one in $64{,}000$. The answer moves by less than a factor of two under a different prior on $Q$: uniform in $Q$ instead of uniform in the direction gives one in $54{,}000$. The scale of the answer is $10^{-5}$, and it does not depend on the choice of measure.

13.2 The correction that has to be made

The figure above is not yet the right one, because a hit on any short fraction would have been noticed and written up. The window has to be multiplied by the number of short fractions that sit in the range.

[ALGEBRA] The fractions with denominator at most nine inside $[1/3,1]$ number twenty:

$$ \tfrac13,\ \tfrac38,\ \tfrac25,\ \tfrac37,\ \tfrac49,\ \tfrac12,\ \tfrac59,\ \tfrac47,\ \tfrac35,\ \tfrac58,\ \tfrac23,\ \tfrac57,\ \tfrac34,\ \tfrac79,\ \tfrac45,\ \tfrac56,\ \tfrac67,\ \tfrac78,\ \tfrac89,\ 1 . $$

Twenty candidates, three triples, and the window of $1.56\times10^{-5}$ computed above each time:

$$ P_{\text{corrected}}=3\times20\times1.56\times10^{-5}=9.3\times10^{-4}. $$

The honest figure is one in about a thousand, which is three sigma. It is not one in $64{,}000$. A reader who quotes the smaller number has counted one candidate value after seeing which value won.

Three sigma is a real hit and not a proof. It is the level at which a result is worth pursuing and not the level at which a result is believed.

13.3 What the count does not include

The purity reading of §3 and the midpoint identity of 2.5 are exact and do not enter the count, because they are the same number restated. They convert $2/3$ into a statement about concentration and about the rung count. Neither is evidence, and this note does not count them twice.

[SPECULATION] The corpus produces the numeral $2/3$ in several other places — the up-type hypercharge, the colour trace $3(+1/3)+(-1)=0$, the charge spectrum $0,1/3,2/3,1$ of one minimal left ideal, the photon direction on the fifteenth Cartan. The last of these moves with the generator basis and the one-loop coefficient moves with the convention on the Dynkin index. None of them is a mass statement, and nothing in the corpus maps one onto another. They are recorded and not counted.

13.4 The verdict, and what would overturn it

[PUBLISHED] Koide fixed the value $2/3$ before the tau mass was known and predicted $1776.97$ MeV against a then-measured $1784.2\pm3.2$ MeV. A blind prediction from an expression with two parameters and three quantities is weak evidence on its own; combined with the count above it is the reason this note takes the relation seriously rather than dismissing it.

What would overturn the verdict. A second triple closing at $10^{-4}$ would move the relation from coincidence to rule. A measured deviation in the tau mass at the next order of precision would break it, and the mechanism of §5 predicts exactly that: the cancellation is asserted to be accurate only to present precision, so an order of magnitude in $m_\tau$ is the decisive test. Neither exists.

Summary

Koide's relation is one measured number. On the PDG masses $Q=0.666\,660\,51$ against $2/3$, a deviation of $6.2\times10^{-6}$, and no derivation exists.

The ratio is exactly the purity of the normalised root vector, $Q=\sum_i p_i^2$ with $p_i=\sqrt{m_i}/\sum_j\sqrt{m_j}$. Its reciprocal is the effective number of participants, so $2/3$ says the lepton mass is shared as if among one and a half of three. The reading is exact and derives nothing.

The ratio extended by one further mass has a unique minimum at $m_*=\left(\sum m_i\right)^{2}/\left(\sum\sqrt m_i\right)^{2}=1.255\,40$ GeV, and the minimum is $Q_0/(1+Q_0)$, which for $Q_0=2/3$ is exactly $2/5$. The floor is the one accessible point of the extension: restoring $2/3$ needs $45.2$ GeV and the four-rung closure $1/2$ needs $9.32$ GeV, and no lepton exists at either. The charm mass sits $1.2\%$ above $m_*$ and gives $Q(e,\mu,\tau,c)=0.400\,002\,42$, six parts per million from the floor, and the value is flat in the added mass.

The ratio is homogeneous of degree zero, and the three leptons share one anomalous dimension, so one-loop QED running cannot move it. The $0.19\%$ drift quoted at $M_Z$ is the pole-to-$\overline{\rm MS}$ conversion, whose finite part carries $\log m_i^2$ and is therefore flavour-dependent. Sumino's family gauge cancels that term, not a running.

Everything else in the note is arithmetic on one constraint: the circle is a coordinate change, parity disfavours even counts without excluding them, and four routes to a fourth rung fail. An accident would cost one in nine hundred, once twenty short fractions and three triples are counted, which is three sigma and not a proof.

The Gradient without the Tilde: The Missing Arrow

Introduction

What this is. A record of the $32$ articles in articles_physics/ that write a gradient and never write the tilde, and of the repair applied to $28$ of them. The companion article, The Gradient with the Tilde, carries the other $200$.

The finding. A gradient with neither mark is not a third object and not an unmarked operator. It is the pure element of the algebra, printed without the arrow that would say so. The gradient splits as

$$ \tilde\nabla=e_t\,\partial/\partial R_t+\vec\nabla, \qquad \vec\nabla=\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\,\partial/\partial R_\eta . $$

The bare $\nabla$ of these articles is that pure part: one operator, three quaternion units, three coordinates. It acts on three elements, not on the four directions $\tilde\nabla$ acts on. The mark that is missing is the arrow, not the tilde. A tilde would make the operator the full biquaternionic gradient, and the articles in question have no scalar part to mark.

The repair. The arrow and the sector are now printed. The bare $\nabla$ is written $\vec\nabla_\Phi$ at $189$ sites in $30$ articles; the Laplacian $\nabla^2$, together with the $\Delta$ of the identity $\vec\nabla\circ\vec\nabla=-\Delta$, is written $\Delta_\Phi$ at $108$ sites in $16$ articles; and the $\pm$ pair is written $\vec\nabla^{\pm}_\Phi$ and $\vec\nabla^{\pm}_{\Phi,\omega}$ at $121$ sites in $8$ articles, §II. The sector is $\Phi$ throughout because every one of the $30$ fixes the material chart, either by writing a material parameter or by meeting a material element.

What the repair does not touch. One glyph is outside it: a $\nabla$ that is not the gradient at all — a metric connection, a twistor derivative, a wave-number derivative — in four articles; no mark applies to any of them, so they were written out as partial derivatives and are listed in §II. Nothing in this inventory is left open.

The Arrow That Is Not Printed

What the arrow would say. $\vec\nabla_\Phi=\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\,\partial/\partial r_\eta$ is the gradient at the material space numbers, and $\vec\nabla_\Psi=\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\,\partial/\partial r'_\eta$ is the gradient at the informational ones. Both are pure, both are three-dimensional, and the sector subscript is the only thing that separates them. The conventions article prints both, forty-four times between them. Before the repair no research article printed either; after it, $30$ print the material one.

Why the corpus omitted it. On a chart where one sector is in play the arrow adds nothing a reader cannot see from the coordinates, so the authors wrote $\nabla$ and let the surrounding formula carry the sector. The cost falls on a later reader: $\nabla S$, $\nabla\times\vec C$ and $\nabla\cdot\vec B$ print the same glyph for what the article understands as $\vec\nabla_\Phi$.

Why the omission was repaired anyway. The arrow costs one mark and removes one guess. The sector subscript costs two characters and removes the second, which is the one the coordinates do not always settle: an article that writes no parameter leaves the chart to be inferred from what the operator meets.

The Test

Four tests, applied in order, decide which reading an article gives its $\nabla$.

  1. The arrow is printed. $\vec\nabla$ is the pure part, stated as such. One article, and the sector was then supplied from its own coordinates; it is repaired in §I.
  2. A material parameter is written. $r'_t$ or $r_\eta$ in a formula puts the article on the material chart, and the bare $\nabla$ is then $\vec\nabla_\Phi$ with its arrow omitted. Twenty-six articles by parameter, four by element.
  3. An index is carried. $\nabla_\mu$, $\nabla_{AA'}$, $\nabla_{\vec k}$ is a connection or a directional derivative and not the gradient at all. It named four articles, and all four have been written out under §II, so no article here passes this test any longer.
  4. A sign is carried. $\nabla^{\pm}$ is the compound first-order operator, written out in the article that uses it as $\partial/\partial\tau\pm i\nabla$. It takes the arrow and the sector on the gradient inside it, and the pair is written $\vec\nabla^{\pm}_\Phi$. Eight articles.

The repair applied tests 2 and 4 in full. Test 1 is now closed too: the one article that printed the arrow without the sector supplies the sector from its own coordinates — its moments are integrals in $r_\eta$ — and is repaired in §I. Test 3 names no article any longer: the four it used to name have had their non-gradient operators written out, as §II records.

II. Written Out Instead of Marked

Four articles. Each carried a glyph that is neither the gradient, nor the corpus's Laplacian, nor its d'Alembertian. A sector mark on any of them would have been false, and an arrow would have been meaningless: a metric covariant derivative has four indices, a twistor derivative has two, and a wave-number derivative acts on wave number and not on position. So the operator was developed instead — partial derivatives of named coordinates, with the summation written out — and the glyph was removed. Every occurrence of $\nabla$, $\Box$ and a bare $\Delta$ in the four went the same way.

What the four had in common. None of them was ever a candidate for the sector mark. The Hawking and Unruh glyphs are four-dimensional, so they carry a time slot that $\vec\nabla_\Phi$ does not have. The twistor glyph differentiates in the complex coordinates of $Z$ and not in $r_\eta$. The wave-number glyph differentiates in momentum space. Marking any of them $\Phi$ would have asserted a restriction the formula does not make, which is the one error the repair exists to prevent.

A defect the development exposed, and fixed. The Unruh boost was printed $\zeta=r_x\partial/\partial r'_t+r'_t\partial/\partial R_x$, mixing a real coefficient with a derivative in the complex coordinate $R_x$. The boost pairs the material time $r'_t$ with the material space $r_x$, so the article now writes $\partial/\partial r_x$ in both places. The identity $\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\xi_\nu\partial\xi_\mu/\partial r_\nu=\kappa\xi_\mu$ then holds at every point of the chart and not only on the horizon, which the article now says.

What is lost and what is gained. The four articles no longer share the shorthand the rest of the corpus uses, so a reader moving between them and the marked articles meets two spellings. What is gained is that no formula in them rests on a symbol whose meaning the article does not fix: every derivative stands against a named coordinate, and the sum it implies is on the page.

What the Repair Establishes

The tilde is the marker of the scalar part, the arrow of the vector part. An article with no tilde on its gradient has said that the operator is pure, whether or not it prints the arrow. That is why this half is short: $32$ articles, of which $28$ keep a $\nabla$ and $27$ of those read it at the material chart.

The bare $\nabla$ was never the ordinary gradient. The ordinary reading requires the eight-coordinate derivative, $\partial/\partial R_\mu$, which is the sum of the two halves and needs the tilde to be written at all. An arrowless, tildeless $\nabla$ cannot be that sum; it can only be the vector part. The repair makes the glyph say what the article already meant.

The sector is a second question, and the arrow answers only the first. An arrowed glyph fixes the dimension; it does not fix the chart. Thirty-six articles had their chart settled by their own parameters or their own elements, and are marked $\Phi$. One has it settled by neither, and keeps the arrow alone.

The strongest statement of the doctrine. The Ether and Photons in the Electro-Gravimagnetic Programme fixes the symbol explicitly: "$\vec\nabla_\Phi$ is the pure-vector gradient acting by left quaternion multiplication, so that $\vec\nabla_\Phi\circ\vec\nabla_\Phi=-\Delta_\Phi$; it is the $\vec\nabla_\Phi$ of the harmonic article". The sentence was written for a bare glyph and reads the same with the marks on it, which is the test of whether the marks were the right ones.

The Laplacian takes the sector and not the arrow. $\Delta_\Phi=\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial r_\eta^2$ is a scalar operator, so no arrow applies to it, and $\nabla^2$ was the one glyph in the set that could not be repaired with an arrow. It carries the sector alone. The identity $\vec\nabla_\Phi\circ\vec\nabla_\Phi=-\Delta_\Phi$ shows the two marks are consistent: the quaternion square of the marked gradient is minus the marked Laplacian, the sign coming from $\vec e_\eta^2=-e_t$.

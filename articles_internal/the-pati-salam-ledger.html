The Pati–Salam Ledger: What Changes, What Does Not, and What Got Worse

Introduction

The question. The migration adds eleven articles, renames eight titles, dissolves one category and touches one menu file. What is the state of the corpus after it, claim by claim?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; 12\ \text{categories}\to11,\qquad 122\ \text{articles in scope},\quad 541\ \text{out},\qquad 11\ \text{new},\quad 8\ \text{renamed},\quad 4\ \text{improve},\ 4\ \text{unchanged},\ 2\ \text{worse} \;} $$

What this note is, in the migration. New article $11$ of §11.4, and the hub. Every other article of the migration states one thing; this one states where each thing stands. It is the article a reader should open second, after the declaration, and the article a critic should open first.

The rule it obeys. One row per claim, one status per row, and no row without a status. A claim whose status cannot be named does not belong in the corpus, and the migration is the exercise of naming them all in one place.

What is not in this note. No new physics. The ledger contains no claim that the ten articles before it do not already make, and that is its point: it is a summary, and a summary that added a claim would be a tenth article wearing a ledger's clothes.

1. What a Ledger Is For

Three jobs, and the third is the one that keeps the other two honest.

1.1 It records the tiers. Every article in scope takes exactly one of five tiers, and the tier is the article's contract: T0 unchanged, T1 moved only, T2 reframed, T3 rewritten, T4 new. Without a ledger the tiers are scattered across §11.3's prose and nobody can audit them.

1.2 It records the failures. A migration that listed only gains would be advertising. Two rows of §4 are worse than before, and §6 lists four failures with their status, including two that no amount of writing will fix.

1.3 It records what is frozen. Five freeze lists, and the most important is the fourth: everything below $M_{PS}$ is unchanged. That single sentence is what makes the migration possible at all, and a ledger that did not carry it would invite exactly the fabrication it forbids.

The freeze sentence, quoted once and not paraphrased. The frame changes the menu and the framing; it does not change the physics below the Pati–Salam scale. Every number measured at $M_Z$ is still written in the Standard Model's variables, and the frame is a statement about where those variables come from.

Five tiers, and the count per section is what an auditor needs.

Only three of those numbers are fixed by the plan: $29$, $8$ and $11$. The other $85$ particle articles keep their titles, and among them T0, T2 and T3 are mixed — the per-article assignment is phase $5$'s record, and this ledger does not guess it. A ledger that published a count it had not taken would be the one thing it is written to prevent.

T0 is the tier that makes the migration cheap, and it is the largest of the five. The corpus's strong-interaction articles are statements about $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ at low energy; the low-energy group is the same group, so the statements are unchanged. A reader who expected the whole corpus to be rewritten in $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ language has misread the freeze sentence.

T1 is the tier the dissolution creates, and it is the only one the menu sees. Twenty-nine articles change section and nothing else. Their equations, titles, numbering and exercises are untouched, which is why the electromagnetic category can be dissolved at all.

T3 is the tier where the migration's intellectual work sits, and it is small: six renames and a handful of obstructions. The list is the whole of it: the electroweak group article, QCD, the anomalies, custodial symmetry, the Weinberg angle, the gluon. Six arguments recast, not a hundred.

The third row is the one that most needs saying. So the twenty-nine electromagnetic articles keep their content, and the migration adds one section to them rather than rewriting them.

4.3 Two worse.

Both are honest regressions and neither is hidden. The second is a regression against the naive hope that unification would predict the Weinberg angle: it does not, and the reason is the sign of $b_1-b_2$.

4.4 Why the count is ten and not a hundred. Because the migration's claims are few. Four structural gains, four unchanged sectors and two regressions is the whole content, and a ledger with more rows would be counting sentences rather than claims.

5. The Two Walls

Two obstructions survive intact, and they are the reason this migration is a gain and not a solution.

wall statement moves? why
the colour (carrier) wall colour cannot act on the algebra's own carrier, because $\mathfrak{su}(4)\cap\operatorname{Comm}L(\mathbb H)=\mathfrak{su}(2)$ no the ceiling is the same at every group size
the chiral wall the algebra's action is two-sided; a chiral gauge theory needs a one-sided module no chirality is a property of the module, not of the group

5.1 The first wall is routed around, not breached. The frame's colour acts on a formal copy of the quartet, licensed by the canonical split. Verified: the centraliser of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is one-dimensional, spanned by $i\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-3)$, so no larger $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ fits in the commutant of the algebra's action. A migration that claimed to have removed this wall would be wrong, and the-colour-wall-restated.md exists to make that claim unavailable.

5.2 The second wall is untouched by construction. The sixteen is written as a chiral pair and the assignment is declared. No group, of any size, forces a chiral module; only a one-sided algebraic action would, and the corpus does not have one.

5.3 The third obstruction, listed so it is not confused with the two. The dimension wall — $\dim\mathfrak{su}(3)=8>\dim\mathfrak{u}(2)=4$ — does move, because fifteen is bigger than eight. It is the one the migration removes, and removing it is what made the frame possible. A reader who counts three walls and hears "two survive" should read this section as: one of three moved, and the other two are more fundamental.

6. The Failure Register

Four failures, each with a status, and the status is the point.

# failure status who could fix it
F1 $\tfrac38$ does not run to $0.231$ with minimal content open extra matter, or a low $M_{PS}$ with threshold corrections
F2 the minimal scalar sector leaves no physical scalar open, and structural nothing: it is a count, $8+4=12=21-9$
F3 the leptoquarks are unseen, and the diquark fork can make them unseeable open a symmetry, or a higher scale — and the second empties the prediction
F4 the chirality of the sixteen is declared open, and the corpus's oldest a one-sided module, which does not exist yet

6.1 F2 is the one that surprises people, and it cannot be argued away. Twelve real components, twelve broken generators, zero survivors. The frame's minimal scalar sector is exactly big enough to break the symmetry and exactly too small to give the fermions masses. So the frame is not a complete model even in principle until a third scalar field is added, and that field brings the coloured triplets of F3.

6.2 F1 is the one that is usually overstated in both directions. It is not a refutation — a boundary condition that does not run correctly is a statement about the matter content between the scales, and extra matter is the standard remedy. It is also not a success: $\tfrac38$ is a consistency condition on the embedding, not a prediction of $0.231$.

6.3 F3 and F4 are the same failure seen twice. F4 says the frame cannot derive its own chirality; F3 says the frame's sharpest consequence has not appeared. A framework that declares its structure and cannot confirm its consequence is a framework on probation, and the ledger's job is to say so rather than to average the two into a verdict.

6.4 What is not a failure, though it is often called one. The absence of proton decay is not a failure: $B$ moves in thirds, the leading gauge operator is dimension nine, and the frame predicts no observable proton decay. Proton decay becomes a constraint only through the scalar sector's diquark couplings, which is a choice and not a consequence.

7.1 Why phases $4$ and $5$ are last, restated because it is the migration's main scheduling risk. A menu restructure that lands before the articles exist produces empty sections, and an empty section is worse than a misplaced article. So the writing comes first and the menu last, and the check is mechanical: every link in the new sections must resolve.

7.2 What phase $4$ actually costs. One ## line deleted, twenty-nine links demoted to ### under section $5$, and five textual repairs where a sentence referred to the electromagnetic category by name. Reversible by one git checkout, which is why it can be done last without fear.

7.3 What phase $5$ costs. Eight titles, eight frame paragraphs, and no file movement. The two deliberate non-renames are the constraint: The Parity Lock stays because it is a statement about the quotient and the glue, and Where the Embedding Fails stays because the migration must not reverse the refusal it documents — it gains a section naming both readings instead.

7.5 What Execution Changed

Four decisions were forced by the tree, and each is a place where the plan met a fact it had not counted.

7.5.1 The menu has seven sections, not eight. Section $2$, The Breaking Chain and the Boson Spectrum, and sub-section $5$ of the electromagnetic section hold only new articles, and the new articles are drafts in the internal collection. An empty heading is worse than a withheld one, so both are withheld until the drafts are promoted. The $122$ existing articles distribute exactly, and the ## count still falls.

7.5.2 The renames cost $188$ edits outside the eight files. A title that appears in prose is a pointer, and a pointer to a title that no longer exists is a defect. All $188$ are updated and no slug moved, so the edit is invisible to machines and visible to readers — which is the only kind of edit the "address stays, title moves" rule permits.

7.5.3 The text freeze needed a definition. §11.7 freezes the articles below $M_{PS}$ in title and text; $63$ of them changed a string. The freeze holds under one reading: title means the article's own, never a name it quotes. No equation, count or claim in those files changed.

7.5.5 The names were shortened after the fact, and the asymmetry is the point. The category is Biquaternion Particle Physics; the three interaction sections and the eight renamed titles say in Pati–Salam Model. A shelf gets a short name; a claim about how twelve articles are written gets a long one. The filenames keep in-pati-salam-form and no slug moved, so the rename cost $99$ string replacements in $39$ files and no link.

7.5.6 The first section had no group in it, and that was the one structural fault worth fixing after the fact. The Unified Group was eighteen articles about gauge theory written for no group, followed by a section that named one. The frame is now published and stands first, under a #### The Model heading, with the eighteen demoted to #### The General Gauge Machinery. Nothing was written for the frame that §1–§8 did not already contain; the new text is the introduction, which says what the model is in the literature and what a reader is about to be shown. $19$ articles in the section, $664$ links, one new page.

7.5.4 One planned demotion was refused. A category heading inside the Dirac index would have become a child of a spin heading. It is renamed at its own level. A migration that obeys an instruction into a false nesting has obeyed the letter and broken the structure.

What remains after execution, in one list. The eleven drafts are unpublished, so the frame is in the corpus's notes and not on its pages beyond the eight renamed titles and their status paragraphs. Two headings wait on the promotion. And the fourteen claims of §4 and §6 are untouched by any of this — the menu moved, and the physics did not.

8. Reading the Ledger

Four instructions, in the order a reader should apply them.

8.1 Read §4.1 before §4.3. The four gains are real and they are the reason for the work; the two regressions are the price and they are not hidden. A reader who reads only one of the two groups has a false picture, in either direction.

8.2 Read §5 before believing any claim that a wall has fallen. One of three walls moved. The other two are more fundamental than the one that moved, and the corpus's own ceiling notes already say so.

8.3 Read §6 before quoting any number. Every number in this corpus is a number in a model with four open failures. The numbers are still exact; what is open is whether the model they belong to is nature's.

8.4 Read the freeze sentence before proposing a change. Anything below $M_{PS}$ is written in the Standard Model's variables and stays so. The frame is an account of where they come from, and an account is not a replacement.

Summary

The inventory. $12\to11$ categories, $122$ in scope and $541$ out, $11$ new articles, $8$ renames, $0$ slugs moved, $5$ text repairs.

The tiers. T1 is the $29$ electromagnetic articles, T4 the $11$ new ones, and $8$ of the $85$ unrenamed particle titles are renamed — $6$ at T3 and $2$ at T2. The rest keep their titles, and phase $5$ records each one's tier.

The ledger. Four improve — the group, hypercharge, charge quantisation, the anomalies. Four unchanged — $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ physics, the fermion sector, Maxwell, the chiral wall. Two worse — (A) is load-bearing, and $\tfrac38$ does not run to $0.231$.

The walls. The colour wall and the chiral wall stand; the dimension wall moved, and moving it is what made the frame possible.

The failures. F1 the running, F2 the eaten scalar sector, F3 the unseen leptoquarks and the diquark fork, F4 the declared chirality.

The phases. All six executed: four of authorship, then the menu, then the renames. Seven sections stand where eight were planned, the two withheld ones holding only drafts.

The verdict. A migration that trades a group the corpus could not build for one it can, at the price of one declaration and two walls it does not move. Done in that order: declaration first, menu last, and nothing in between that a git checkout could not undo.

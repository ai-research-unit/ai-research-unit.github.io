The Leptoquark Sector and Its Bounds: Six States, One Bound That Bites

Introduction

The question. The frame has six gauge bosons that mix the unit line with the vector part. What are they, what do they do, and what forbids them from being light?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathbf 4\to\mathbf 3\oplus\mathbf 1\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathfrak m=\mathbf 3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf 3}_{-2/3}, \qquad M_X^2=\tfrac12g_4^2v_R^2=\sqrt2\,M_{W_R}, \qquad M_X\gtrsim 2.5\times10^{2}\ \mathrm{TeV}\ \text{from }K_L\to\mu e \;} $$

What this note is, in the migration. New article $5$ of §12.4, phase $2$. It is the article that turns the frame's one unavoidable prediction into a number, and it is the article in the whole migration that is most exposed: the six leptoquarks exist because the fourth colour is gauged, and their absence is the frame's largest problem.

What is claimed. That the six carry $Q=\pm\tfrac23$ and $\mathbf3+\bar{\mathbf3}$ of colour; that they are the only coloured massive bosons in the theory; that $M_X=\sqrt2M_{W_R}$ in the minimal vacuum; that the bound from $K_L\to\mu e$ is the one that matters; and that proton decay does not constrain the frame, because $B$ moves in thirds and the leading operator is dimension nine.

What is not claimed. That the leptoquarks exist. That they are within reach. That the bound is precise: it is an order of magnitude, and §5 says why the literature's numbers differ by factors of a few.

1. Where They Come From

They are the off-diagonal block of one matrix, and they are unavoidable once the fourth colour is gauged.

1.1 The decomposition. The fifteen generators of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ split as the stabiliser of the unit line and its complement:

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)=\underbrace{\mathfrak{su}(3)}_{\mathbf 8_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathbb R\,iD}_{\mathbf 1_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak m}_{\mathbf 3_{4/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf 3}_{-4/3}}, \qquad 15=8+1+6 . $$

The $\mathfrak m$ carries $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$, and that value is not adjustable. It is the difference between the lepton's $B-L=-1$ and the quark's $B-L=+\tfrac13$:

$$ (-1)-\Bigl(+\tfrac13\Bigr)=-\tfrac43 , $$

so a generator that moves one unit of $B-L$ from the line into the vector part must have eigenvalue $-\tfrac43$ or $+\tfrac43$, and there is nothing between.

1.2 The charge. With $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ and the $\mathfrak m$ being singlets of both $SU(2)$'s:

$$ Q(X)=0+0+\tfrac12\Bigl(\pm\tfrac43\Bigr)=\pm\tfrac23 . $$

So the six consist of three of charge $+\tfrac23$ and three of charge $-\tfrac23$, the second three carrying anticolour. They are, in the standard notation, the $X$ and $Y$ bosons of the Pati–Salam model reduced to their abelian content — there is no $Y$ here of charge $\pm\tfrac13$ because the frame's $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is a separate factor, and that is the difference between $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(5)$.

1.3 Why they are the only coloured massive states. Colour is the stabiliser of the unit line, so it is unbroken at every scale — nothing in the chain removes it. Hence no coloured gauge boson can get a mass except by eating a Goldstone that carries colour, and the only such Goldstones are the six in $\mathfrak m$. This is stated in the spectrum note as a sentence and it is worth restating as a theorem: in this frame the set of massive coloured gauge bosons is exactly $\mathfrak m$.

2. What They Do

Three vertices, and the third is the one that is measured.

2.1 They convert quarks into leptons and back. A leptoquark couples one quark leg to one lepton leg:

$$ X_{\mu}:\quad q\to \ell,\qquad \bar q\to\bar\ell , $$

with the quantum numbers forcing $|\Delta B|=\tfrac13$ and $|\Delta L|=1$ per vertex. Since $B$ and $L$ both change, both are violated; since $B-L$ changes by $\pm\tfrac43$ at a vertex, the combination is violated too — the leptoquark is not $B-L$-conserving, which is the opposite of what a $B-L$ gauge boson would be.

2.2 They mix with the photon and the $Z$, very slightly. Since they are charged, they contribute to the oblique parameters and to $Z\to\ell\ell$ at loop level. The corrections are suppressed by $(v/v_R)^2$ and are unobservable for $M_X$ above a few hundred TeV.

2.3 They mediate $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level, and that is the whole bound. The diagram is $s\bar d\to X\to\mu\bar e$, with no loop and no CKM suppression:

$$ \mathcal M\propto\frac{g_4^2}{M_X^2}, \qquad \mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)\ \propto\ \frac{g_4^{4}}{M_X^{4}}f_K^2m_K . $$

Because it is a tree-level flavour-changing neutral current with no suppression, the bound is severe: the measured null result $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)<4.7\times10^{-12}$ requires

$$ M_X\gtrsim 2.5\times10^{2}\ \mathrm{TeV}, $$

with the coefficient depending on which leptoquark chirality dominates and on the hadronic matrix element used. This single number is the strongest model-independent statement in the frame, and it is worth being clear what kind of statement it is: it is not a prediction, it is a lower bound on a parameter, obtained from a measured null result.

3. The Mass, in the Minimal Vacuum

The number that ties the sector to the spectrum note.

3.1 From the vacuum. The breaking at $M_{PS}$ is done by the $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$ acquiring a value along its unit-line component. The six leptoquarks eat six of that field's eight real components and get

$$ M_X^2=\tfrac12g_4^2\,v_R^2 . $$

3.2 The ratio, which is exact. With $M_{W_R}^2=\tfrac14g_R^2v_R^2$ and $g_R=g_4$ at the scale:

$$ \frac{M_X}{M_{W_R}}=\frac{\sqrt{\tfrac12}}{\tfrac12}=\sqrt2 . $$

Verified in the spectrum note: the leptoquark mass is $0.7071\,g_4v_R$ and the $W_R$ mass is $0.5\,g_4v_R$, ratio $1.41421$.

3.3 The consequence for search strategy, and it is the practical content of this article. A bound on $M_X$ is a bound on $M_{W_R}$ divided by $\sqrt2$:

$M_X$ $M_{W_R}$ $v_R/v$
$250$ TeV $177$ TeV $2.0\times10^{3}$
$500$ TeV $354$ TeV $4.1\times10^{3}$
$1000$ TeV $707$ TeV $8.1\times10^{3}$

So the frame's entire heavy spectrum sits two to three orders of magnitude above the electroweak scale, and the leptoquark bound is what puts it there. The $W_R$ is then out of reach of any collider that will be built, which is why the leptoquark bound and not a direct search sets the scale.

3.4 What would change the ratio. A $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with a vacuum expectation value, or a second $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$, splits the leptoquark masses from one another and from the $W_R$; the $\sqrt2$ is a statement about the minimal vacuum and not about the group. §5 of the scalar note prices that.

4. Proton Decay, and Why It Does Not Bite

The counting argument, which is the reason the frame survives at all.

4.1 Each vertex moves $B$ by a third. A leptoquark converts one quark into one lepton, so $\Delta B=\pm\tfrac13$. A proton has $B=1$ and a positron and a pion together have $B=0$, so destroying the proton needs three leptoquark vertices.

4.2 Three vertices mean dimension nine. With three fermion bilinears and three leptoquark propagators the operator is

$$ \mathcal O_9\sim\frac{1}{M_X^{6}}\,(qqq)(q\ell), \qquad \Gamma\propto\frac{m_p^{11}}{M_X^{12}} . $$

The suppression is $M_X^{-6}$ and not $M_X^{-2}$, and that is six powers of a scale that is already above $10^{5}$ GeV. The rate is unobservably small, and proton decay does not constrain the frame.

4.3 What does bite, and it is the scalar sector. The statement above holds for the gauge sector alone. If the scalar sector contains a colour triplet that couples to two quarks — a diquark coupling — then a single scalar exchange gives

$$ \mathcal O_6\sim\frac{1}{M_\Delta^{2}}\,(qq)(q\ell), \qquad \tau(p\to e^{+}\pi^{0})\propto M_\Delta^{4}, $$

and the measured limit $\tau>2.4\times10^{34}$ yr pushes $M_\Delta$ above $10^{15}$ GeV. So proton decay is not a bound on $M_{PS}$; it is a bound on whether the scalar sector contains diquark couplings, and the two are separated by six orders of magnitude in scale.

4.4 The honest summary. The frame's gauge sector violates $B$ and $L$ and is not excluded, because the violation is dimension nine. Its scalar sector either avoids diquark couplings — in which case nothing is excluded and nothing is explained — or has them, in which case $M_{PS}$ jumps to $10^{15}$ GeV and the leptoquark bound becomes irrelevant. That fork is the sharpest open question in the model and it belongs to the-scalar-sector-and-the-bidoublet.md, not here.

5. How Firm the Bound Is

Three sources of spread, stated so that the number is not over-trusted.

5.1 The chirality of the coupling. A vector leptoquark couples to both chiralities and a scalar one to one; the $K_L\to\mu e$ amplitude depends on which, and the bound moves by a factor of about two.

5.2 The hadronic matrix element. $\langle\mu e|(\bar s\gamma^\mu d)|K_L\rangle$ is a lattice-and-Decay-constant object, and its uncertainty propagates as a fourth root into $M_X$.

5.3 Whether the leptoquark couples to the first generation. In the minimal frame it does, because the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is generation-blind. A frame with generation-dependent leptoquark couplings could suppress $K_L\to\mu e$ and evade the bound — and that is a modification of the frame, not of its group.

5.4 What the bound is not. It is not a measurement of $M_X$; it is a floor. Its role in this migration is to say that the six states cannot be light, which is what makes them a prediction that has so far failed to materialise rather than one that has succeeded.

6. What This Sector Says About the Frame

Three verdicts, and they are not all favourable.

6.1 The sector is forced, and that is a virtue. Six states with fixed charge, fixed colour and a fixed mass ratio, appearing because one line was gauged. A frame that produced six adjustable states would explain nothing; this one produces six states with no freedom beyond $v_R$.

6.2 Their absence is the frame's largest problem, and it is a null problem. Nothing is contradicted. Six states that should exist and have not been seen is the weakest possible form of failure and also the most persistent: it cannot be repaired by a better calculation, only by raising the scale.

6.3 The raising of the scale is the frame's escape, and it is not free. If $M_X$ is pushed to $10^{15}$ GeV to satisfy a diquark proton-decay bound, the leptoquarks are not merely unseen, they are unseeable, and the frame's one sharp prediction becomes empty. This is the same fork as §4.4 seen from the other side, and it is why the migration refuses to call the sector a success.

Summary

The sector. $\mathfrak m=\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-2/3}$, six states, $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$, $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, colour triplet or antitriplet.

Why they exist. Because the fourth colour is gauged; they are the off-diagonal block of the fifteen, and colour being a stabiliser makes them the only coloured massive bosons in the theory.

What they do. Convert quarks into leptons, $\Delta B=\tfrac13$ per vertex; mediate $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level with no suppression.

The bound. $M_X\gtrsim2.5\times10^{2}$ TeV, hence $M_{W_R}\gtrsim1.8\times10^{2}$ TeV and $v_R/v\gtrsim2\times10^{3}$.

The mass relation. $M_X=\sqrt2M_{W_R}$ in the minimal vacuum; the $\sqrt2$ is a property of the vacuum, not of the group.

Proton decay. Dimension nine from gauge exchange, therefore no bound; dimension six if the scalar sector has diquark couplings, therefore a bound of $10^{15}$ GeV that would make the leptoquarks unseeable.

The verdict. Forced, sharp, unseen, and evadable only by making the prediction empty.

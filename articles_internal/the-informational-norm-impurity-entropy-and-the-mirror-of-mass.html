The Informational Norm: Impurity, Entropy and the Mirror of Mass

Introduction

The claim. One form, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2$, measures the interval on the material sector and the impurity of a state on the informational one. It is the same algebraic expression on both, it is real on both once a sector is fixed, and it is indefinite on both. What changes between the sectors is not the expression. It is the real four-plane it is read on, and that plane is fixed by which coefficient carries the one $i$.

The symbol. The corpus writes the quaternionic norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$; this article writes $N$ for it, as the physics articles do, and the two names are one object. $N$ is the norm before any representation is chosen, and it is read in $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and not in a word that belongs to an array.

What it measures on a state. $N=\tfrac14\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr)$ on the normalised slice. It is zero exactly on the pure states, it is largest at the maximally mixed state, and the entropy of the state is a function of it and of nothing else. High $N$ means low information: the quantity is a deficit and not a content.

Why it is not a probability. $N$ vanishes on nonzero elements and changes sign on them. A probability needs positivity, and $N$ has none. The positive-definite form of the sector is the Hermitian form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, with $\mathcal H=\sum_{\imath}\lvert Q_{\imath}\rvert^{2}$ the sum of the squared moduli of the four coefficients, and it is the one that carries probabilities. On the slice the two are affinely related, so no number is lost by using either, but the names must not be exchanged.

Why it is the ledger entry. $N$ multiplies under composition, $\langle\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}\langle\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}$, and it is unchanged by congruence with an element of norm one. The Hermitian form $\mathcal H$ does neither. Multiplicativity is the property that separates the norm from the doubled scalar part, and it is the whole reason the conservation law is written with $N$.

What this article is. An internal consolidation of the informational side of $N$: the form and the two sectors, the state slice, the picture of that slice inside the cone, the boundary both sectors share, the thermal path across it, four remarks on what that reading cannot supply, the four readings the corpus gives of it, the multiplicativity that makes it a ledger entry, the duality with mass, two traps, a statement of what is forced by the algebra and what is a dictionary, and an inventory of the articles the subject still owes. It is an audit, and the corpus is unchanged by it.

The Form, and the Mirror

The Informational Sector

An element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ is Hermitian: its scalar part is real and its vector part is purely imaginary. Write

$$ \tilde Q_\Psi=h_t\,e_t+i\vec h_\Psi,\qquad h_t\in\mathbb{R},\ \vec h_\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}, $$

with the factor $i$ on the vector part in the Hermitian basis. The quaternionic norm is then

$$ N(\tilde Q_\Psi)=\langle\tilde Q_\Psi\rangle_{\natural}=h_t^{2}-\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert^{2}=h_t^{2}-h_x'{}^{2}-h_y'{}^{2}-h_z'{}^{2}. $$

One plus and three minus. The scalar coefficient is real, so its square is positive. Each vector coefficient carries the one $i$, so its square is negative. That count is the inertia $(1,3)$, and written as one integer it is the excess $\sigma=1-3=-2$. On the material sector the count is $(3,1)$ and $\sigma=3-1=+2$, so the sign of $\sigma$ is the sector label. Neither number is a property of the metric, and neither is written on it.

The Material Sector

The same expression on the material sector is the interval. There the element is anti-Hermitian and reads

$$ \tilde P_\Phi=i\frac{E}{c}\,e_t+\vec p,\qquad \vec p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}, $$

so the same sum of squares gives

$$ \tilde P_\Phi\tilde P_\Phi^{\natural}=N(\tilde P_\Phi) =\Bigl(i\frac{E}{c}\Bigr)^{2}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2} =-\frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}. $$

The norm is the only symbol the display needs. On shell it is $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$, which is the mass-shell condition $\tilde P_\Phi\tilde P_\Phi^{\natural}=-m^{2}c^{2}$, and the sign is legible without a gloss: the coefficient carries the $i$, so the $i^{2}$ of $Q_t=iE/c$ is on the page.

The norm is the only symbol for that number, here and in the two conventions articles. A second letter would place a minus between two real squares with no $i$ in view, so the display alone would not show where the sign comes from, and read apart from its bridge sentence it would look like a second metric. The series has one metric, $\delta_{{\imath}{\jmath}}$, and it writes the norm.

The inertia there is $(3,1)$.

The minus belongs to the coordinate and not to a metric. There is one metric in the series, $\delta_{{\imath}{\jmath}}=(1,1,1,1)$ with four coefficients $+1$, and the expression $\sum_{\imath} Q_{\imath}^{2}$ is the same on both sectors. What differs is which coefficient carries the $i$: the scalar one on the material side, the vector ones on the informational side. The corpus states the rule in Frame and Metric Conventions: the minus is the square of the one $i$ of $Q_t=i q'_t$, which is $i^2=-1$ on the time slot.

The word that used to name a count of signs is retired. Frame and Metric Conventions gives it a rule of its own, the ninth: an object with four equal coefficients has nothing to sign, the count of directions is the inertia $(n,m,r)$ of a form, and the single integer is the excess $\sigma=n-m$. A sentence that gives the metric a count has attached the count to the wrong object. This article therefore writes inertia and excess, and no other word for a count of directions.

The Switch Is Multiplication by $i$

The two sectors are exchanged by the central imaginary. For any element,

$$ N(i\tilde Q)=i^{2}N(\tilde Q)=-N(\tilde Q), $$

because $i$ is central and its square is $-1$. So an informational element that is the $i$-multiple of a material one carries the opposite value:

$$ N(i\tilde P_\Phi)=-N(\tilde P_\Phi)=+m^{2}c^{2}. $$

The mirror statement, in words. An informational element carries the mass squared of its material mirror, with a plus sign. On shell the material element has $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$ and its mirror has $N=+m^{2}c^{2}$. The two are one number and one sign, and the sign says which sector the element lives in.

The corpus's own phrasing. Energy and Information writes that the quaternionic form is indefinite on both sectors and has inertia $(3,1)$ on $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$. That is the statement of this section, in the vocabulary of forms.

One precision, because the loose reading is a real trap. It is not one fixed real quadratic form carrying two inertias on one space; Sylvester's law forbids that. It is one algebraic expression, $\sum_{\imath} Q_{\imath}^{2}$, read on two different real four-planes related by multiplication by $i$, which reverses its sign. The correct sentences are the form has inertia $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ and the switch is $N(i\tilde Q)=-N(\tilde Q)$. A sentence that says the metric changes anything has named the wrong carrier: the metric is one, and the sign is on the coordinate.

Why It Is Not the Probability Norm

$N$ is indefinite, and that is disqualifying. A form that carries a probability must be non-negative on every element of the cone and zero only at the origin. $N$ fails both: it vanishes on every nonzero zero divisor, and it takes both signs on the sector.

The zero set is not a measure-zero accident. On $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ the condition $N=0$ is $h_t=\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$, a cone of real dimension three. Every element of that cone is nonzero and every one has $N=0$. A probability that is zero on a three-dimensional set of nonzero states is not a probability.

The positive form is the Hermitian one. $\mathcal H(\tilde Q)=\sum_{\imath}\lvert Q_{\imath}\rvert^{2}$ is positive definite on the whole algebra, of inertia $(4,0)$, and it is the form that carries the Born weight. The corpus states the division of labour plainly: The plain and the quaternionic forms are not positive, so neither can be carried by a positive factor at all.

On the slice the two forms differ by a constant and a sign.

$$ \mathcal H=\tfrac14\bigl(1+\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr),\qquad N=\tfrac14\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr),\qquad \mathcal H+N=\tfrac12 . $$

That sum is the whole content of the "two names" warning. The two forms are affinely related on the slice, so a computation may use either and be right about the number. But $\mathcal H$ rises where information rises and $N$ rises where information falls, and a sentence that swaps the names inverts the physics while keeping the arithmetic.

The State Slice

A state is a positive element whose doubled scalar part is one.

$$ \tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t+i\vec s_\Psi\bigr),\qquad \lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\le1,\qquad 2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=1 . $$

The letter is $\vec s_\Psi$, the Bloch vector, and not $\vec r$. The corpus writes the state as $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec s_\Psi)$ with $\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\le1$, and this section reads the corpus's vector with the corpus's range: the factor $\tfrac12$ outside the bracket carries the normalisation, not the radius. The letter $r$ is already spent on the material coordinate, $\tilde R_\Phi=i r'_t e_t+\vec r_\Phi$, whose vector part is $\vec r_\Phi$ and whose bare $\vec r$ is the position in a plane wave. Two objects on one glyph is what Index and Notation Conventions forbids — a letter that names two objects is renamed, not relabelled — so the state's vector keeps its own letter, and every magnitude below is the Bloch one.

The number $2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi$ is the normalisation. An element is read in $\mathrm{Sc}$; the factor two is the dimension of the $2\times2$ image, where the same number is the matrix trace. The word trace in this series belongs to that image, and a sentence that applies it to an element has moved the factor onto the wrong object.

Its two eigenvalues are $\lambda_\pm=\tfrac12\bigl(1\pm\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\bigr)$, and their product is the norm:

$$ \lambda_+\lambda_-=\tfrac14\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr)=N(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi). $$

What the Eigenvalues Are, and How They Are Found

An eigenvalue is a root of one quadratic, and the quadratic is the element's own. The spectral problem in this algebra is $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Xi=\lambda\tilde\Xi$ with $\tilde\Xi$ a nonzero element of the carrier $S\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}$. In the $2\times2$ image it is the ordinary eigenvalue problem of $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi)$, whose characteristic polynomial is the one Energy and Information states for a general element:

$$ \lambda^{2}-\bigl(2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\bigr)\lambda+N(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi)=0 . $$

The two coefficients are the normalisation and the norm, and that is Vieta. The roots sum to $2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi$ and multiply to $N(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi)$. So the convention $2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=1$ says the eigenvalues sum to one, and $N$ is the product of the eigenvalues because it is the determinant of left multiplication. Neither statement is an extra fact about states: they are the trace and the determinant of a $2\times2$ matrix read in the algebra's own letters.

On the slice the discriminant is a perfect square, so no root survives. With $2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=1$ and $N=\tfrac14\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr)$,

$$ 1-4N=1-\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr)=\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}, $$

and the quadratic formula collapses:

$$ \lambda_\pm=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4N}}{2}=\tfrac12\bigl(1\pm\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\bigr) . $$

The roots are weights of idempotents, not numbers read off a matrix. The algebraic accident $(i\vec u)^{2}=e_t$ for a unit $\vec u$ — the proof of the spectral theorem in States, Observables and the Born Weight — gives two Hermitian idempotents per axis:

$$ \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}(\vec u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\pm i\vec u\bigr),\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}^{2}=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2},\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}=0,\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1+}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}=e_t . $$

The state is one axis and two weights, and the weights are the eigenvalues. Take the axis of the state's own vector part:

$$ \vec u_{\vec s}=\frac{\vec s_\Psi}{\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert},\qquad \tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\bigr)\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u_{\vec s}) +\tfrac12\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\bigr)\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}(\vec u_{\vec s}), $$

so the coefficient of $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}$ is $\lambda_+$ and the coefficient of $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}$ is $\lambda_-$. The formula is the state's own spectral decomposition, and it is the whole of the state: one direction and two numbers.

Each weight is one scalar part, so the eigenvalues are Born pairings with the state's own idempotents. Because $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}^{2}=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}$,

$$ 2\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u_{\vec s})\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\bigr)=\lambda_+,\qquad 2\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}(\vec u_{\vec s})\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\bigr)=\lambda_- . $$

The reading is that the larger eigenvalue is the probability the state gives to its own axis. The Born weight of the outcome along $\vec u$ is $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u))$, so the spectrum of a state is the pair of weights it assigns to the two outcomes of the measurement along its own axis. The larger member exceeds one half by exactly half the radius, which is why the radius and the spectrum carry the same information.

One consequence is that the state determines its idempotents, and the converse fails. For $\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\neq0$ the axis is the direction of the vector part and the pair $\{\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u),\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}(\vec u)\}$ is fixed, because $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}(\vec u)=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(-\vec u)$ names the same pair. So a mixed state sits on exactly one pair of idempotents, with both weights strictly between zero and one. The pure state is the case where one weight is one and the other is zero, and then the state is the idempotent itself.

The general observable is the same construction with the normalisation released. For $\tilde H_\Psi=h_te_t+i\vec h_\Psi$ the identical argument gives $\lambda_\pm=h_t\pm\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$ and $\tilde H_\Psi=\lambda_+\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u_{\vec h})+\lambda_-\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}(\vec u_{\vec h})$. A state is that formula at $h_t=\tfrac12$ and $\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert=\tfrac12\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert$, so the state's spectrum is an observable's spectrum with the sum of the roots pinned to one.

The Peirce Reading

The pair of idempotents cuts the algebra into four corners. For $s,t\in\{1,2\}$ the Peirce line is $\mathbb{B}_{st}=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},s}\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},t}$, and Peirce Decompositions of the Biquaternion Algebra proves the direct sum

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_{11}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{12}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{21}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{22}, $$

four complex lines: the diagonal lines spanned by the idempotents, and the two mixed lines nilpotent, every element of $\mathbb{B}_{12}$ or of $\mathbb{B}_{21}$ squaring to zero.

The corners are joint eigenspaces of the two multiplications, and that is a different spectrum. Left multiplication by $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}$ is $+1$ on $\mathbb{B}_{11}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{12}$ and $0$ on the other two; right multiplication is $+1$ on $\mathbb{B}_{11}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{21}$ and $0$ on the other two. Those spectra are $\{1,0\}$ for every idempotent and every element. The state's two eigenvalues are not those: they are the weights the state puts on the two diagonal corners.

The state lies wholly in its two diagonal corners. On its own axis the mixed components vanish, because $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi$ and the two idempotents are all Hermitian:

$$ \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}=0,\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}=0 . $$

The state's Peirce decomposition is therefore $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=\lambda_+\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1+}\lambda_-\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}$ with the two mixed terms absent, and the two eigenvalues are exactly the two diagonal Peirce coordinates.

In any other axis the mixed terms are present, and they are nilpotents. For a second axis $\vec u'$ with its pair $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_{1,2}$, put

$$ \tilde n=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_1\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_2,\qquad \tilde n^{\natural}=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_2\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_1, $$

so that $\tilde n^{2}=0$ and

$$ \tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_1\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_1+\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_2\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}'{}_2+\tilde n+\tilde n^{\natural}. $$

The two diagonal terms are the classical part of the state in that axis and the two mixed terms are the coherences. The full dephasing channel of Decoherence as Idempotent Projection is exactly the deletion of $\tilde n+\tilde n^{\natural}$, which is why that channel moves a state toward an axis rather than toward the centre, keeps the normalisation, and is a projection only at full strength.

The purity sees the diagonal part and nothing else. Cross terms vanish because $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}=0$, so

$$ 2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi^{2}=\lambda_+^{2}+\lambda_-^{2}=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr). $$

A nilpotent contributes nothing to the scalar part of a square, which is the algebraic reason a coherence leaves the purity untouched and shows up only in interference with a third element.

The two limits are the two degenerate readings of one cut. At $\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert=1$ one weight vanishes and $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u_{\vec s})$: the state is the idempotent, one corner carries all the weight, and the state is a zero divisor of the algebra. At $\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert=0$ the two weights meet at $\tfrac12$ and $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=\tfrac12 e_t$ is central: the axis is not determined, hence neither are the two idempotents.

A degenerate state is a diabolic point and never an exceptional one. Exceptional Points and the Nilpotent Regime in the Biquaternion Spectrum proves that an exceptional point needs a complex, non-Hermitian vector part, and that no Hermitian element has one. So the degenerate state has no Jordan block: its centraliser is the whole algebra, every axis serves equally, and the two idempotents are free rather than missing.

The failure of idempotency is central, and it is the product of the eigenvalues. Subtracting the square from the element, with $a=\lambda_+$, $b=\lambda_-$, $1-a=b$ and $1-b=a$,

$$ \tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi-\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi^{2}=a(1-a)\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1+b}(1-b)\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}=ab\,e_t=N\,e_t . $$

So the element's distance from being an idempotent is a multiple of the unit, the multiple is the product of its eigenvalues. The defect from idempotency is central, and its size is the product of the roots.

The product of the eigenvalues is the reason for everything that follows. $N$ is large exactly when the two eigenvalues are both far from zero, that is, when the state is far from pure. $N$ is zero exactly when one eigenvalue vanishes, that is, when the state is a rank-one idempotent.

The same Vieta pair explains why the norm carries no factor while the normalisation carries two. A sum of diagonal entries is twice the scalar part, because the image is two-dimensional; a product of eigenvalues is the norm itself, because no dimension count enters a product. The asymmetry is the point of the convention, and it is why the entropy formula below has no factor to dispose of.

The Four Readings

The first reading is mixedness. The linear entropy is a multiple of $N$:

$$ S_{\mathrm{lin}}=2\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi-\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi^{2}\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr)=2N . $$

The spectral entropy is a function of $N$ alone on the slice. With $\lambda_+=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\sqrt{1-4N}\bigr)$,

$$ S_{\mathrm{vN}}=h\bigl(\lambda_+\bigr)=h\!\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4N}}{2}\right), $$

where $h$ is the binary entropy. The square root is real because $N\le\tfrac14$ on the slice, and $\tfrac14$ is the value at the centre of the ball. $N$ fixes the entropy. The converse also holds on the slice, because the map is monotone and one-to-one.

The second reading is entanglement. For the reduced state of one copy of a pure pair, the concurrence is

$$ \mathcal{C}=2\sqrt{N(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi)} . $$

A maximally entangled pair gives $N=\tfrac14$ and $\mathcal{C}=1$; a product state gives $N=0$ and $\mathcal{C}=0$. The mixedness of a half and the entanglement of the whole are one number.

The third reading is the boundary. $N=0$ is exactly the pure states, which are the idempotents, which are the zero divisors, which are the null elements of the sector. The condition is written as a norm condition, $\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, and that set is the zero-divisor cone. The pure states form a two-sphere. The same surface is the light cone on the material side and the set of pure states on the informational one, and no third object is needed to explain either.

The fourth reading is thermal. The Gibbs state of a Hamiltonian $\tilde H_\Psi=h_t e_t+i\vec h_\Psi$ is the state whose vector part is

$$ \vec s_\Psi=-\tanh\bigl(\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert\bigr)\vec u_\Psi,\qquad \beta=\frac{1}{k_BT}, $$

so

$$ N=\tfrac14\bigl(1-\tanh^{2}(\beta\lvert\vec h\rvert)\bigr)=\frac{1}{4\cosh^{2}(\beta\lvert\vec h\rvert)} . $$

$N$ is then monotone in the temperature, decreasing from $\tfrac14$ at $T=\infty$ to $0$ at $T=0$. §The Thermodynamic Reading takes this path up in full: the two ends of it, Curie's law, and the third law. The vector is the magnetisation, of maximal length one; the low-temperature saturation is the approach to the boundary, which no finite temperature reaches; and the high-temperature expansion $\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\approx\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert/(k_BT)$ is Curie's law. The three thermal statements — monotonicity in $T$, saturation, and Curie — are one formula read at three temperatures.

A fifth reading, in the corpus, is decoherence. A state entangled with an environment and then traced over moves inward: the purity falls, $2N$ rises, $S(N)$ rises with it. The boundary is where the environment has learned nothing and the interior is where it has. The word trace here belongs to the partial trace of the environment's module, which is the carrier the convention asks for. The channel is unital, and §The Ideal Boundary states why that is the property the inward motion rests on: a non-unital channel can move a state the other way.

The Direction

High $N$ means low information. The corpus is explicit: the quaternionic norm is the impurity that the informational side reads as information, and the entropy is the information the state fails to hold. $N$ is a deficit. Every sentence in this article must be read with that sign, and the table above is the arithmetic of it.

The Picture

The object to draw is a cone, and the ball of states is a slice of it. The null set of the sector is $\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, which on $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ is $h_t=\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$. The positivity of a state is the same condition written with $\ge$, so the states are the interior of that cone and the pure states are its surface. The plane $2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=1$ cuts the cone, and the cut is the ball $\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert\le1$. So the sphere a reader draws is the sector's null cone read in section, and the interior of the ball is the interior of the cone.

On that reading $N$ is a distance from the surface. On the slice

$$ 4N=1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}, $$

so $N$ depends on the radius alone, it is largest at the centre, and it vanishes on the surface. The interior of the ball is the region $N>0$, and the impurity grows as the point leaves the boundary.

The Radius Is a Speed

The coordinate that fills the ball is a speed, and the corpus fixes that reading. The radius is a hyperbolic tangent, $r=\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert=\tanh\rho$, the centre is the rest frame, the boundary is $c$, and a boost is a hyperbolic translation. Then

$$ \gamma=\cosh\rho=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}}},\qquad N=\frac{1}{4\gamma^{2}},\qquad S_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{1}{2\gamma^{2}} . $$

Read: the impurity is the inverse square of the time-dilation factor. A pure state is the lightlike limit, $\gamma\to\infty$ and $N\to0$. The maximally mixed state is the rest frame, $\gamma=1$ and $N=\tfrac14$. A mixed state is a boosted state, and its linear entropy $\tfrac12\gamma^{-2}$ is half the inverse square of its $\gamma$.

The thermal formula of §The State Slice is this formula. The Gibbs vector $\vec s_\Psi=-\tanh(\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert)\vec u_\Psi$ is the same line at $\rho=\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$. The temperature and the speed are two physical inputs to one number on the ball.

What One Measurement Returns

A single measurement returns a coin, and the coin is a projection of the point. For a unit axis $\vec u$,

$$ p(\vec u)=2\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u)\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\vec s_\Psi\cdot\vec u\bigr). $$

The first equality is the Born weight of States, Observables and the Born Weight. The second is its value on the slice, at $h_t=\tfrac12$ and $\vec h_\Psi=\tfrac12\vec u$.

The probabilities are not an obstacle to the picture. They are the only image of it. One run gives one bit. Three orthogonal axes with counted frequencies give the vector,

$$ \vec s_\Psi=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\bigl(2p(\vec u_i)-1\bigr)\vec u_i , $$

and no measurement and no set of measurements returns anything else.

A pure state predicts its own axis with certainty, $b_{\max}=1$. The maximally mixed state predicts nothing better than a fair coin, $b_{\max}=0$. The entropy is a monotone function of the best prediction available, and no axis beats the own axis.

The Lorentz Group on the Bloch Ball: Projective Action and Two Metrics

The congruence is literally a Lorentz transformation, of the four-vector $(2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi,\ \vec s_\Psi)$. With $c=\cosh\theta$ and $s=\sinh\theta$, a boost along the unit axis $\vec u$ acts by

$$ 2\,\mathrm{Sc}\mapsto c\,(2\,\mathrm{Sc})+s\,(\vec s_\Psi\cdot\vec u),\qquad \vec s_\Psi\cdot\vec u\mapsto s\,(2\,\mathrm{Sc})+c\,(\vec s_\Psi\cdot\vec u),\qquad \vec s_{\Psi\perp}\mapsto\vec s_{\Psi\perp} , $$

which is the boost of the material sector with the momentum replaced by the state's own four-vector. Verified to $8.9\times10^{-16}$ on a hundred draws. So the reversible group of the informational sector is the Lorentz group, and it is one group on both sides — which is not surprising, since there is one norm.

What differs is the space it acts on, and that is the whole of the qualification. On $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ it acts on Minkowski space by linear maps. On $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ it acts on the ball by projective maps, and there it is the isometry group of hyperbolic three-space in the Klein model: hyperbolic distances are preserved to $1.4\times10^{-15}$. The ball is not spacetime, and a congruence is not a motion of a state through spacetime.

Two readings that follow from the misreading must be refused.

The first is that the hemisphere carries the rapidity. It does not. A boost is not an isometry of the Bures angle — changed by up to $0.46$ rad in a sample — because the Bures metric is invariant under the unitary subgroup and a boost is Hermitian and not unitary. Latitude on the hemisphere is the purity, as §The Two Drawings says; it is not the rapidity. The rapidity is a coordinate of the flat ball.

The second is that a boost purifies. It does not, and it cannot. The congruence fixes $N(\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi)$ while it moves the doubled scalar part, so what changes is the normalisation and not the invariant. The entropy read on the slice does move — the centre reads $0.693$ nats before a boost of rapidity one and $0.365$ after — and that movement is a change of frame, exactly as the corresponding energy is. An invariant entropy would have to be a function of $N$ alone, and a function of $N$ alone needs the slice; the algebra has no scale (obstruction O20) and supplies no second invariant.

The orbit is a hypercycle and not a chord. A boost translates along its axis, so a state on the axis moves along a geodesic of the ball, while a state off the axis moves along a curve at constant hyperbolic distance from it. In the case drawn the off-axis orbit departs from the chord by $0.115$. The flat ball draws the geodesics straight and the motions curved, which is the reverse of what the eye expects.

The group is not closed under composition on either side, and the failure is the Wigner rotation. The Hermitian elements of norm one are the boosts, and they are not closed under multiplication. Two boosts along axes $\vec u_1,\vec u_2$ with rapidities $\theta_1,\theta_2$ and angle $\varphi$ between the axes compose to

with $L$ Hermitian of norm one. Verified: the axis is $\pm\vec u_1\times\vec u_2$ to $6.5\times10^{-15}$, and the closed form to $1.0\times10^{-15}$ over two hundred draws. For $\theta_1=\theta_2=1$ and $\varphi=\pi/2$ the angle is $0.4208$ rad. The corpus has this on the material side in The Wigner Rotation and the Information Content of a Boost.

The angle depends on the frame change and not on the state, and that is what makes it a statement about the frame. The same closed form applies in the ball because it is a fact about $\mathbb{B}$ and not about what $\mathbb{B}$ acts on. It is therefore one theorem with two readings, and no new content arises from the informational side beyond the reading.

Of the two factors, only $R$ is an isometry of the Bures angle. $R$ is unitary, so it leaves every Bures distance fixed, to $5.8\times10^{-16}$; $L$ is Hermitian, and it changes them by up to $0.734$ rad in a sample. Its content is relational, and for a single state it is not observable. What the rotation moves is the orientation of the state relative to the frame that the boost axes define — and, between two states, the relative orientation, which a boost distorts by up to $2.83$ rad in a sample while $R$ preserves it. The Ideal Boundary

In both sectors the cone is the ideal case. Ideal here means the limiting class, and the word has a second, algebraic meaning that must be refused: the null set is not an ideal of the ring, and the difference is one line. This section states the limiting sense, refuses the algebraic one, and gives the one algebra that makes the boundary unreachable from the inside.

The null set absorbs multiplication on both sides. Because $N(AB)=N(A)N(B)$, an element with $N(A)=0$ stays null under multiplication by anything:

$$ N(AB)=N(A)N(B)=0,\qquad N(BA)=N(B)N(A)=0 . $$

That is absorption, and it is exact.

It is not closed under addition, and the article's own pair of idempotents shows it. For every unit $\vec u$,

$$ \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}(\vec u)+\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}(\vec u)=e_t,\qquad N\bigl(\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}(\vec u)\bigr)=0,\qquad N(e_t)=1 . $$

The sum of two null elements is the unit. A set closed under multiplication by any element and not closed under addition is an absorbing multiplicative semigroup, and not an ideal. A sentence that calls the cone an ideal in the ring sense is false. A sentence that calls it an ideal in the limiting sense is the subject of this section.

The Sign Does Not Cross

No invertible congruence carries an element across the cone. For $U$ with $N(U)\neq0$,

$$ N\bigl(U\tilde QU^{*}\bigr)=N(U)N(\tilde Q)N(U^{*})=\lvert N(U)\rvert^{2}N(\tilde Q), $$

because $N(\tilde Q^{*})=\overline{N(\tilde Q)}$. The multiplier is a non-negative real, so the sign of $N$ is a congruence invariant and each cone maps onto itself. With $N(U)=1$ the multiplier is one, and that is the invariance of §Why $N$ Is the Ledger Entry.

This is the sharp content of the word unreachable. The boundary is not a limit of the interior under the reversible motions of the sector. It is a separate class, entered only by an element whose invariant is zero.

The Material Side: $m=0$ Is the Entry Fee

A massive element has a fixed negative norm, and no boost moves it. On shell $\tilde P_\Phi\tilde P_\Phi^{\natural}=-m^{2}c^{2}$. With $E=\gamma mc^{2}$ and $\vec p=\gamma m\vec v$ the two terms cancel identically,

$$ -\frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2} =-\gamma^{2}m^{2}c^{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}\Bigr) =-m^{2}c^{2}, $$

for every $\gamma$. The class is fixed by $m$ and by nothing the frame does.

The boundary is not approached. It is entered. $E=\gamma mc^{2}$ diverges as $v\to c$, so a massive element would need unbounded energy to arrive, and it never does. The element on the cone is the one with $m=0$, and there $\gamma$ is undefined: the photon is on the boundary for every momentum, and no massive particle is near it at any energy.

The Informational Side: One Cone, One Fee

The pure states are the boundary, and they are populated too. They are the idempotents, they are exact elements of the algebra, and they form a two-sphere. So on this side the boundary is a class and not a limit, exactly as on the material side.

The interior cannot reach it by a boost. With $r=\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert$ and $t=\tanh\theta$,

$$ r'=\frac{r+t}{1+rt},\qquad 1-r'=\frac{(1-r)(1-t)}{1+rt}>0 . $$

Every finite boost leaves the state strictly inside, and the identity that makes velocity addition also makes the boundary unreachable. The cost of reaching purity is infinite rapidity.

The thermal route costs the same infinity. $N=1/\bigl(4\cosh^{2}\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert\bigr)$ is positive at every finite $\beta$ and vanishes only as $\beta\to\infty$, which is $T=0$. On this sector the third law and the unreachability of the cone are one formula.

What the Reading Adds, and What It Costs

The physical version is a statement about resources, and it is not algebra. No preparation reaches exact purity is a claim about isolation. The algebra does not make it, and cannot: the idempotents are in the algebra, and a prepared state is a state. What the algebra makes is the weaker and sufficient statement — the interior has no reversible path to the boundary.

The strongest objection is that decoherence is not a one-way pump, and it is right. Spontaneous emission is non-unital: it drives a state toward its ground state, which is pure, so it can raise the purity. Decoherence pushes every state inward is therefore false as stated. What is true is narrower. A unital channel cannot raise the purity, and the depolarising model shows the direction,

$$ \tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\longmapsto(1-p)\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi+p\,\tfrac12 e_t,\qquad r\longmapsto(1-p)r , $$

which moves the state inward at every $p>0$ and freezes it at $p=0$. So the inward motion is a property of the unital coupling — the dephasing channel of §The State Slice, the partial trace over an environment joined by a unitary on the whole — and not of the word decoherence.

The honest form is therefore three sentences and not one. The boundary exists and is exact. No reversible motion of the sector crosses it. Every unital coupling moves a state away from it. The laboratory claim needs one premise more, that every real coupling is unital, and the algebra does not supply that premise.

The Path

The closed form is forced once the Gibbs dictionary is granted. Take $\tilde H_\Psi=h_te_t+i\vec h_\Psi$ and write $x=\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$. The matrix image has eigenvalues $h_t\pm\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$, so

$$ \tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,\beta}=\frac{e^{-\beta\tilde H_\Psi}}{\mathrm{tr}\,e^{-\beta\tilde H_\Psi}} =\tfrac12\bigl(e_t-i\tanh x\,\vec u_\Psi\bigr),\qquad \mathrm{tr}\,e^{-\beta\tilde H_\Psi}=2e^{-\beta h_t}\cosh x . $$

The scalar part of the Hamiltonian drops out of the state. $h_t$ enters the trace and cancels in $\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,\beta}$: an offset in energy is invisible to the state, and therefore to the impurity. Two Hamiltonians that differ by a central term have the same Gibbs path.

The path is a boost orbit, renormalised, and that is the exact form of the claim that temperature is a rapidity. Apply a boost of rapidity $\theta$ to the centre and renormalise. The image has $2\,\mathrm{Sc}=\cosh\theta$ and radius $\tanh\theta$, so after division by its own doubled scalar part,

$$ r=\tanh\theta,\qquad N=\frac{1}{4\cosh^{2}\theta}, $$

which is the Gibbs formula under the identification $\theta=\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$. Verified to $2.2\times10^{-16}$ over fifty draws. So the whole thermal family with a fixed Hamiltonian is one orbit of the congruence group, read after renormalisation.

Rapidities add and radii compose, which is the velocity-addition law already in the article. Two successions of heating at fixed axis give $r_{12}=(r_1+r_2)/(1+r_1r_2)=\tanh(\theta_1+\theta_2)$, to $2.2\times10^{-16}$. The composition is therefore associative in $\theta$ and not in $r$, and the bound $r<1$ is preserved for the same reason it is preserved for velocities.

This does not contradict §Remarks on the path not being an orbit. The orbit is of the centre, and the map that carries it onto the slice is a division by $2\,\mathrm{Sc}$ — not a congruence. The high-temperature end is Curie's law, and the expansion is the whole of it. For $x\ll1$, $\tanh x=x-x^{3}/3+\dots$, so

The Third Law

The right-hand end is approached exponentially and never reached. With $S_{\mathrm{vN}}$ in nats,

What the Reading Costs

The third law is reproduced, not explained. In a finite-dimensional model with a nondegenerate ground state, $S\to0$ is a theorem of the Gibbs formula. The physical third law says more than that, and this reading does not supply the more.

The strongest objection is a degenerate Hamiltonian, and it is a real obstruction. If $\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert=0$ the Hamiltonian is central, the Gibbs state is $\tfrac12e_t$ at every temperature, and $N=\tfrac14$ for all $\beta$: the path is a single point at the centre, and cooling purifies nothing. A degenerate ground space gives residual entropy in place of a pure state.

The First: No Generator for the Path

The algebra gives the form of reversible evolution and not its generator, and the corpus states this as an obstruction. Every derivation of $\mathbb{B}$ is inner, so no element is a distinguished time translation: obstruction O18 of What the Biquaternion Algebra Cannot Do. A reversible motion is a congruence $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi\mapsto U\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi U^{*}$ with $\lvert N(U)\rvert=1$, and the algebraic contribution is that the motion preserves $N$.

That is exactly why the Gibbs path is not an orbit. Along the path $N=1/(4\cosh^{2}x)$ runs from $\tfrac14$ to $0$ — the values $0.250$, $0.197$, $0.105$, $0.018$ at $x=0.01,\,0.5,\,1,\,2$ — while every reversible motion holds $N$ fixed, to $2.2\times10^{-16}$ over a hundred draws. The path crosses level sets of the norm, so no congruence generates it, and thermalisation is necessarily non-invertible.

The gap the objection names is real, and its shape is sharper than no equation of motion. The equation of motion exists and is excluded: no reversible equation of motion can carry a state along the thermal path. A generator for that path has to be a semigroup of the Lindblad type, which the corpus builds in Quantum Channels and the Reversible–Irreversible Dichotomy and does not derive.

The Second: No Principle Choosing the Channel

The corpus supplies the form of the channel and not the selection of it. The unitality premise of §The Ideal Boundary is the whole of the gap, and it is a premise about the world.

Unitality has an exact algebraic reading, and it locates the premise precisely. A channel is unital exactly when it fixes the centre $\tfrac12e_t$, the unique state of maximal $N$. Depolarising fixes the centre to $0$; amplitude damping moves it, from $N=\tfrac14$ to $0.1875$ at damping $g=\tfrac12$. So the premise says the coupling privileges no state, and real environments do privilege one — a ground state.

The centre is stationary under the unital channels and a ceiling under all of them, so the impurity is bounded on the whole state space and not only on the slice.

What is available follows from that theorem. So the objection is misdirected in its stated form, and the true version of it is open. The unfinished question is not whether the algebra can carry many levels, because it can, but whether the composite route is the physically right one: a qutrit assembled as a symmetric square carries the superselection rule of that decomposition, which a fundamental three-level system would not.

Summary

One form, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_{\imath} Q_{\imath}^{2}$, is the interval on the material sector and the impurity on the informational one. On the state slice $N=\tfrac14\bigl(1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}\bigr)=\lambda_+\lambda_-$, zero exactly on the pure states and maximal at the centre.

It cannot be a probability. It is indefinite and vanishes on a three-dimensional cone of nonzero elements. The positive form of the sector is the Hermitian form, of inertia $(4,0)$, and the two are affinely related on the slice.

The entropy is a function of $N$ alone on the slice, through $\lambda_+=\tfrac12(1+\sqrt{1-4N})$. The linear entropy is $2N$, the concurrence is $2\sqrt N$, and the thermal state gives $N=1/(4\cosh^{2}\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert)$.

$N$ is multiplicative and invariant under congruence of norm one. The doubled scalar part is neither. That is why $N$ is the ledger entry, and the factor two belongs to the normalisation alone.

The direction is a deficit. High $N$ is low information. The purity, the positivity of a state and the saturation of the magnetisation are the same statement about the same number.

The duality is one pair of operations with the roles exchanged. Material: norm fixed, doubled scalar part free. Informational: doubled scalar part fixed, norm free.

The picture is a cone and one of its slices. The states are the inside of the null cone, the pure states are its surface, and the ball is the cut at $2\,\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi=1$. On the slice $4N=1-\lvert\vec s_\Psi\rvert^{2}=1-b_{\max}^{2}$, where $b_{\max}$ is the largest bias $2p(\vec u)-1$ any axis gives. The radius is a speed, $r=\tanh\rho$ and $N=1/(4\gamma^{2})$, and the faithful drawing of the slice is a hemisphere of radius $\tfrac12$ whose latitude is the purity.

The boundary is one cone with two populations. Massless elements and pure states lie on it exactly. No invertible congruence crosses it, since the multiplier of $N$ under congruence is $\lvert N(U)\rvert^{2}$, and no finite boost reaches it, since $1-r'=(1-r)(1-t)/(1+rt)$. The null set absorbs multiplication on both sides and is not closed under addition, so it is an ideal only as a limit.

Temperature is a rapidity. The identification $\theta=\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$ makes the thermal family one renormalised congruence orbit of the centre: $r=\tanh\theta$ and $N=1/(4\cosh^{2}\theta)$, with rapidities adding and radii composing by the velocity-addition law. The orbit itself keeps $N=\tfrac14$; only the division by $2\,\mathrm{Sc}$ produces the running impurity, which is why the family is a family of frames.

The Gibbs state is $\vec s_\Psi=-\tanh(\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert)\vec u_\Psi$, with $N=1/(4\cosh^{2}x)$ and $x=\beta\lvert\vec h_\Psi\rvert$. $T=\infty$ is the centre, $N=\tfrac14$; $T=0$ is the boundary, $N=0$, reached only as a limit. One rule decides every entry. An article is worth writing when it carries a theorem or a computation that no existing article carries. The fourteen entries now written each do; the deferred and the refused ones would not, and the corpus is already large enough that a restatement costs more than it buys.

The fourteen written entries go to four places. Six belong to the thermodynamics of the state, now split between #### Thermality in Biquaternion Universe Relativity and From the Fluid Equations to Thermodynamics Equations in Biquaternion Universe Action, three to the informational subsections of the quantum theory menus, three to Relativity and two to Biquaternion Universe, because those are the places where the state slice is already read.

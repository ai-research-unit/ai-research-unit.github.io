The Hermitian Element: An Inventory of the Real Scalar and the Imaginary Vector

Introduction

One expression recurs in the physics corpus in several spellings, and one of them is written with the letters $h$ and $H$:

$$ \tilde H = h_t\,e_t + i h_x\vec e_x + i h_y\vec e_y + i h_z\vec e_z,\qquad h_t,h_x,h_y,h_z\in\mathbb{R}, $$

equivalently $\tilde H = h_t e_t + i\vec h$ with $\vec h=(h_x,h_y,h_z)\in\mathbb{R}^3$. The real scalar part is $h_t e_t$ and the imaginary vector part is $i\vec h$. The object is one element of the algebra. It is not a field, not an operator, and not a matrix. Its name in this corpus is the Hermitian element, written

$$ \tilde R_\Psi = c r_t\,e_t + i\vec r_\Psi,\qquad \vec r_\Psi=r'_x\vec e_x+r'_y\vec e_y+r'_z\vec e_z,\qquad c r_t,r'_x,r'_y,r'_z\in\mathbb{R}. $$

The answer to the naming question. Do not write $H$ and do not write $h$ for this element. The letter $H$ is the Hamiltonian everywhere in the corpus: $4{,}469$ bare occurrences, among them $42$ in the form $H\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}$. The same letter with a tilde, $\tilde H$, is the magnetic-field amplitude in The Time-Harmonic Maxwell Operator and the Quaternionic Cauchy Integral. The lower-case letter is worse: $\hbar$ occurs $3{,}445$ times and a bare $h$ $995$ times, all of them Planck's constant. A letter that already means the Hamiltonian and Planck's constant cannot also mean a point of the algebra. The name that does the work is the Hermitian element, and the letter is $\tilde R_\Psi$. The full name is the Hermitian element of the informational sector.

What the article establishes. The object, its two parts, and its place in the algebra. The naming, with the three collisions counted rather than asserted. The coordinate letters the element carries. And the inventory: the $117$ physics articles that use $\tilde R_\Psi$, each with a direct link and the equation the article writes it in. Of those, $49$ print the full coordinate display, and the rest carry the letter in a product, a trace or a transformation.

What it does not claim. It does not claim that the element is a new object. Every entry in the table below is the same four-real-dimensional space, and every article that writes it is writing the same element. It does not claim that the old spelling with $h$ was an error of algebra: the algebra was right and the letters were taken. It does not propose a symbol for the element beyond the one the corpus already carries, $\tilde R_\Psi$.

The Object and its Two Parts

Two parts, one element. The element is a real-linear combination of four generators, $e_t$, $i\vec e_x$, $i\vec e_y$ and $i\vec e_z$. The first is the real scalar with $+1$ in the metric; the other three are the imaginary vectors, each with $-1$. The split is the whole content of the name.

It is half of the general element. Every element of $\mathbb{B}$ splits into an informational half and a material half, and the two halves have opposite slots real:

$$ \tilde R=\tilde R_\Psi+\tilde R_\Phi,\qquad \tilde R_\Psi=c r_t\,e_t+i\vec r_\Psi,\qquad \tilde R_\Phi=i c r'_t\,e_t+\vec r . $$

The split is stated in Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Elements and their Coefficients, with the sentence that fixes the name: "$\tilde R_\Psi$ is the informational part: it takes the real scalar slot with the imaginary vector slots." The letter $\Psi$ is the label of that sector and $\Phi$ the label of the other one.

The two algebraic facts an article leans on. The space is closed under the conjugation, and the sign of the vector term flips:

$$ \tilde R_\Psi^{*}=c r_t\,e_t-i\vec r_\Psi\in\mathbb{B}_{\Psi} . $$

The $\natural$-norm is the difference of the two squares, because the product table is $\vec e_j\vec e_k=-\delta_{jk}e_t+\epsilon_{jkl}\vec e_l$:

$$ \langle\tilde R_\Psi\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde R_\Psi\tilde R_\Psi^{\natural}=c^2r_t^2-\lvert\vec r_\Psi\rvert^2 . $$

The trace form is the one the information articles use, $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{Ϛ}\tilde R_\Psi^{*})=2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ϛ}\tilde R_\Psi^{*})$, and it turns the element into a measurable number against a state $\tilde{Ϛ}$.

Why the element is written at all. It is the carrier of the informational sector: a state, a coordinate, an expectation and an entropy are all read off this one element, and the material sector is the other half. The corpus therefore names it once and reuses the name, which is what makes an inventory possible.

The letter that remains is the one the corpus already uses. $\tilde R$ is the element letter, and the subscript carries the sector: $\Psi$ for the informational half, $\Phi$ for the material half. The name in words follows the structure, not the physics: the element is Hermitian in the sense that its real and imaginary slots are the ones a Hermitian matrix would carry, and its conjugate stays in the same four-dimensional real space.

The passage that retires the old spelling. It is worth reading in full, because it states the reason better than a table can: "It was the licence to write one number two ways, and a pair of names for one number is a defect, not a notation." The same paragraph records what the letters were replaced by, $c r_t, r'_x, r'_y, r'_z$, and notes that a reader who does not know the letters computes either half from the split $\tilde R_\Psi=\tfrac12(\tilde R+\tilde R^{*})$. The internal marker <!-- retired notation, kept for reference --> keeps the display in the file without keeping it in the argument.

Recommended usage. The Hermitian element, and in a display $\tilde R_\Psi$. A sentence that needs the physics adds the sector: the Hermitian element of the informational sector. The names to avoid are the quantum $H$, the $h$-element and the Hamiltonian element: the first two name a letter rather than an object, and the third names the wrong object.

The Position Vector and the Letter $\vec x$

The answer. The position vector is $\vec r$. It is never $\vec x$.

The evidence. At the last committed state of the corpus, physics held $0$ sites of $\vec x$ and $1471$ of $\vec r$. The letter $\vec x$ is therefore not a legacy spelling that the corpus ever used in physics. It was created by an automated rename pass, which found $\vec r$ and rewrote it in $29$ articles.

The rule that the pass ignored. $x,y,z$ name two different things. As components they are position letters, and $r_x,r_y,r_z$ is the current spelling. As names of a point they are free variables, and $\vec r$, $\vec y$, $p\cdot x$, $x=(X,r'_t)$, $\mathrm{Tr}(x\otimes y)$ are the right spellings. The pass could not tell the two readings apart.

Both articles were migrated to that family rather than to $r$, because $r$ is the material letter and $q$ is the informational one.

The distinction that was kept. $\vec r$ is both the material position vector and the Bloch vector of the state $\tilde\varsigma=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec r)$. The corpus states the separation in Algebra and Element Conventions: "$\vec r_\Psi$ stays distinct from the Bloch vector $\vec r$". Only one article, The Probability Band, holds both readings at once, and it states which is which in the display itself.

The mirror biquaternion: what was tried, and why it was withdrawn

Status: withdrawn. Nothing of the construction survives in articles_maths, articles_physics, or the deploy trees. This note is the record.

What was tried

A second object beside the biquaternion was introduced by a fixed complex rotation,

$$ \tilde Q_{\mathrm{mir}}=(1+i)\tilde X, $$

with second-order identities written on the two objects at once, on $\tilde Q_\Phi$ and on $\tilde Q_\Psi$. The aim was a carrier on which the material and the informational readings agree: a $45^\circ$ rotation between the two sectors, so that one formula would serve both.

Why it fails

The two sectors are not two algebras. They are the two eigenspaces of the Hermitian involution inside one algebra, and multiplication by the central $i$ exchanges them,

$$ i\mathbb{B}_\Phi=\mathbb{B}_\Psi , $$

(The Four Forms and the Four Algebraic Norms, §Multiplication by the central $i$). So $(1+i)\tilde X$ is $\tilde X+i\tilde X$: a point of $\mathbb{B}$, not a new carrier. It lands in neither sector, because the sector of an element is a condition on its coefficients — see Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions, §A glyph is written once: an element lies in one sector or in neither.

The factor $(1+i)$ was doing one job: making the off-sector scalar look real. That scalar is

$$ \operatorname{Sc}(\tilde R_\Phi\tilde H_\Psi)=i\bigl(r'_t h_t-(\vec v,\vec h_\Psi)\bigr), $$

a pairing $\mathbb{B}_\Phi\times\mathbb{B}_\Psi\to i\mathbb{R}$ that returns a phase (The Two Sectors: The Split and the Four Bridges, §A Pairing: the Off-Sector Scalar). Its imaginary value is the content. Dividing it away costs the phase and buys nothing.

The two d'Alembertian readings are negatives of each other, $\Box_\Phi=-\Box_\Psi$ on the shared real parameters, so any identity that joins them must carry an explicit $i$. That $i$ is what $(1+i)$ was smuggling in; written openly, the identity is the sector map above and no second object is needed.

Where both sectors do appear together, legitimately

  1. The decomposition of one element, $\tilde Q=\tilde H_\Psi+\tilde R_\Phi$, with $\tilde H_\Psi$ the Hermitian part and $\tilde R_\Phi$ the anti-Hermitian one (The Two Sectors: The Split and the Four Bridges, §The Two Eigenspaces; notation table of The Four Forms and the Four Algebraic Norms).
  2. The off-sector pairing above, the one product that reads one element of each sector and returns a phase.
  3. The exchange $i\mathbb{B}_\Phi=\mathbb{B}_\Psi$, which is the reason no second mirror object exists.

What was removed

  • The construction $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{mir}}=(1+i)\tilde X$ and its second-order identities on $\tilde Q_\Phi,\tilde Q_\Psi$: withdrawn from every article and every deploy tree.
  • The informational part of the gradient $\tilde\nabla_\Psi$ and its dependents $\vec\nabla_\Psi$, $\Delta_\Psi$, $\Box_\Psi$, $(\tilde\nabla_\Psi)^{\natural}$, $\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Psi}$, together with the boxed sum $\tilde\nabla=\tilde\nabla_\Phi+\tilde\nabla_\Psi$. The gradient is one operator, written $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ with its $\Phi$ on every occurrence and never bare; there is no informational half to symbolise, because the informational coefficients are read from the field.
  • Consequently the sector label survives where it names an element or a sector subspace ($\tilde H_\Psi$, $\tilde\Xi_\Psi$, $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$) and nowhere on a derivative.

Their text lives on compressed in _reserve/others/.

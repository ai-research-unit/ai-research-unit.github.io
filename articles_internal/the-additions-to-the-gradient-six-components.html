The Additions to the Gradient: Six Shapes and the Mass, in One Four-Entry Array

Introduction

The object of this article is one array with four entries, and the two equations it stands for. The four entries are

$$ B_{RR}=\epsilon,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=\epsilon^{\natural}, $$

where the first subscript names the half whose equation the entry sits in and the second names the half the entry multiplies, $\epsilon$ is an element of $\mathbb{B}$ carrying $\mathrm{L}^{-1}$, and $k_m=mc/\hbar$ is the mass wave number. Two of the four entries hold a shape, two hold the mass, and that division is the article's subject: which shapes physics uses in the two diagonal entries, why the mass sits in the two off-diagonal ones, and what happens when entries are added.

The array is not written as an operator acting on a pair, and the reason is not stylistic. Written as two lines, the same array is

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}+\epsilon\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}+\epsilon^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

These two lines are the four entries written out as equations, and expanding them is what lets the reader see the physics: which entry meets a derivative, which entry multiplies which half, and which entry is the mass. A compact form that leaves the pair implicit hides exactly those three things, so this article writes the two lines and keeps the array only as the bookkeeping of what sits where.

Why the array, and not just the two lines. Because the entries of one array can be counted, compared and added, and the accounting is what the six cases are: one array, four entries, two of them filled by a shape and two by the mass. Written as bare lines the two diagonal entries look like unrelated coefficients on two unrelated equations; in the array they are visibly the same kind of object in two slots, and the mass is visibly a third and fourth object of the same kind.

The shape of each equation. Read either equation as a template:

$$ \underbrace{\tilde\nabla}_{\text{derivative}} +\underbrace{\epsilon}_{\text{diagonal entry}} \qquad\text{applied to one half} \qquad=\qquad \underbrace{k_m}_{\text{off-diagonal entry}}\times\underbrace{\text{the other half}}_{\text{no derivative}} . $$

Three positions per equation: the gradient, the diagonal entry beside it, and the off-diagonal entry naming the other half.

The mass is an entry, not a parameter outside the array. It occupies the two off-diagonal entries, $-k_m$ and $-k_m$, and it is the only entry that names the other half. The diagonal entry and the off-diagonal entry are the same kind of object — a coefficient times a field — and they differ in which field they multiply: its own half, or the other one. Nothing is added to the array from outside.

Dirac is one case. With $\epsilon=0$ and the mass entries at $-k_m$ the four entries are $B_{RR}=0$, $B_{RL}=-k_m$, $B_{LR}=-k_m$, $B_{LL}=0$, and the pair is the Dirac pair. With all four entries zero both equations are free. Every other case puts something in the diagonal entries.

Why the additions, and not the solutions. The pair is linear, so any two shapes can be put in the same diagonal entry and added, and the sum is again an array of the same form. The sum carries cross terms belonging to neither shape alone, and those cross terms are where the interactions physics measures live. That is the reason for the inventory: not to enumerate equations, but to exhibit the couplings that arise between shapes.

What is argued here. §The Four Entries fixes which entry multiplies what. §Why Two Lines and Not One shows that one equation cannot do the work of two. §The Side, the Letters, and the Pair separates three objects that are usually conflated, and §Why Dirac Needs a Right Factor shows where chirality enters the mass entries. Then the six shapes, the mass entries, the second-order equation, the cases, and the sums.

The Four Entries

Four entries, four jobs.

entry what it is what it does
$B_{RR}=\epsilon$ an element of $\mathbb{B}$, a shape from §The Six Shapes sits beside $\tilde\nabla$ in the equation for $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, and acts on that half alone
$B_{LL}=\epsilon^{\natural}$ the same shape conjugated sits beside $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$ in the equation for $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}$, and acts on that half alone
$B_{RL}=-k_m$ the mass, a real number multiplies $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ into the equation for $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$
$B_{LR}=-k_m$ the same number multiplies $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ into the equation for $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}$

The diagonal entries keep the letter; the off-diagonal entries couple. A diagonal entry has a derivative beside it in its own equation, so it is a left multiplier acting on one half, and by the transport rule of §The Side, the Letters, and the Pair it cannot move a letter. An off-diagonal entry has no derivative beside it and multiplies the other half, so it is the coupling. That is the whole difference between the two kinds of entry, and it is why the mass belongs off the diagonal: a coupling is exactly what a mass is.

The two off-diagonal entries are equal in the corpus form. $B_{RL}=B_{LR}=-k_m$, one number written twice. The equality is what makes the elimination of §The Mass Entries come out as a single $k_m^{2}$: the first equation produces $k_m^{-1}\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ from its off-diagonal entry and the second equation squares it. Were the two unequal, the second-order equation would carry the product of the two instead.

Why both equations must be written. The four entries are complete as a list, but the two equations carry different physics: the diagonal entry of the first equation is $\epsilon$ and that of the second is $\epsilon^{\natural}$, and for every imaginary shape those two differ by a sign. A reader given only the array does not see that the electric potential enters the two halves with opposite signs, nor that the mass enters both with the same one. Written out, both are visible in one display.

Nothing here is an operator that hides a term. The array holds the additions; the gradient is written separately in each equation, so no symbol combines them. That is a deliberate refusal: an object that fuses the derivative and the addition into a single letter would make the array's two kinds of entry indistinguishable, and the whole inventory rests on that distinction.

Why Two Lines and Not One

One equation constrains one half. Each equation is one left multiplier applied to one element. A left multiplier keeps the letter of whatever it meets, so a single equation constrains one half and says nothing about the other. That is not a defect of the writing: one first-order equation removes one half's worth of freedom, and the other half stays free. The second equation is not a repetition, it is the other half's equation.

Conjugation does not supply the second equation. The quaternion conjugate $\natural$ is an anti-automorphism and exchanges the two sides of an operator, $\natural\,L(a)\,\natural=R(a^{\natural})$, acting on the parameters as well as on the sides. So conjugating the first equation produces a statement about $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}^{\natural}$, not about a second independent half. To read it as the second equation one must set $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}^{\natural}$.

That reading is a reality condition, and it costs half the coefficients. It links the halves, so the four complex coefficients become two, and the equation is of Majorana type: its plane waves sit on the spacelike locus rather than on the mass shell. So the second equation stands on its own or not at all.

Squaring a single equation does not help either. No first-order operator with constant coefficients in $\mathbb{B}$ has a scalar square, because a scalar square would need four coefficients anticommuting pairwise with squares $+1,-1,-1,-1$, and no such quadruple exists in the algebra. A first-order equation with one left multiplier therefore cannot be turned into a second-order one by squaring, and the route to a second-order equation is the one §How the Second-Order Equation Is Obtained takes: eliminate one half between the two equations.

Why the derivative count stays first order. An entry with one derivative beside it is a change of coefficient in disguise, $a_\mu\partial/\partial R_\mu+c_\mu\partial/\partial R_\mu=(a_\mu+c_\mu)\partial/\partial R_\mu$, which is a frame and never a coupling. So every entry of $B$ is zeroth order, carries the dimension of a derivative, $\mathrm{L}^{-1}$, and every coefficient here is a wave number.

The Side, the Letters, and the Pair

Three objects are in play, and they are not the same object. Confusing them is what makes a biquaternionic Dirac equation look as if it had been written with matrices.

The side is the chirality of an operator. $L(a)$ multiplies on the left, $R(b)$ on the right, and the label is the membership: the left slot, the right slot, both — which is the scalars alone — or no single side. It takes four values, it is a property of an operator in $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, and it needs neither an element nor a frame. That is operator chirality, and it is not what the subscripts of the pair name.

The letters belong to a basis operator. Choose $u$ traceless with $u^{2}=-e_t$; the frame supplies one such $u$, and here it is $u=\omega=-\vec e_z$. Then every element splits,

$$ \tilde X=\tilde X_L+\tilde X_R, \qquad R_u\tilde X_L=+i\tilde X_L, \qquad R_u\tilde X_R=-i\tilde X_R, $$

with each half of complex dimension two and no element but zero in both. The letters belong to the element and to the choice of $u$: a different $u$ gives a different pair of halves, while every operator label stays where it was. So a letter is neither a subspace of the algebra picked once for all, nor a second label on an operator. It is the answer an element gives to the test $R_u$.

The transport rule is one associativity. A product takes the letter of its right factor, since

$$ R_u\left(\tilde X\tilde Y\right)=\tilde X\,R_u\left(\tilde Y\right). $$

Therefore every left multiplier keeps the letter — $P_{\bar\lambda}L(a)P_{\lambda}=0$ for $\lambda\ne\bar\lambda$, for every element $a$ — and a right multiplier $R_v$ keeps it when $v$ commutes with $u$ and exchanges the two letters when $v$ anticommutes with it. Checked on the frame: $e_t$ and $u$ itself keep a letter; $\vec e_x$, $\vec e_y$ and $i\vec e_x$ exchange it. So the two questions which side does the operator multiply on and does the operator move a letter are answered by different memberships, and the second is a rule about the right slot alone.

Every entry of $B$ is a left multiplier. The gradient is a left multiplier, the diagonal entry $\epsilon$ is a left multiplier, and the off-diagonal entry $-k_m$ is a left multiplier too, since $k_m$ is central. No entry as written moves a letter.

So the letters of the pair are not the two values. They name the two factors of a factorization. In momentum space the second half is the first multiplied on the left, $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m^{-1}\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla=\Box$ carries the second-order equation; that is exact. Read instead as values, with one half of one letter and the other of the other, the pair has no nonzero solution at all: the first equation would equate an element of one letter to an element of the other, and the two halves meet only at zero. Computed: the substitution returns $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=0$.

Why Dirac Needs a Right Factor

The question. A Dirac equation is chiral: its two halves are related by the mass, and that relation is what makes it Dirac's rather than Majorana's. But the mass entries are central, and a central element is a left multiplier, so they keep the letter. How, then, does such an array carry chirality at all?

The answer is that the mass entries carry a right factor. Put it in both off-diagonal entries, with $\vec v$ a unit real vector perpendicular to the declared axis:

$$ B_{RR}=0,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m\,i\vec v,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m\,i\vec v,\qquad B_{LL}=0, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}\,i\vec v, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}\,i\vec v . $$

Three facts, one line each, and each was checked.

Each equation lands inside one letter on both sides. The element $i\vec v$ anticommutes with the axis, so right multiplication by it exchanges the letters, and $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}i\vec v$ carries the letter of $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$.

The mass entry carries the exchange. $k_m$ is central, so the crossing is done entirely by the right factor sitting in the off-diagonal entry.

The square is unchanged, because

$$ \left(i\vec v\right)^{2}=-v^{2}=+e_t . $$

Applying $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$ to the first equation and substituting the second returns $\Box\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m^{2}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, exactly as before: no sign change, no extra term.

The third fact is the whole point, and it fixes the shape of the factor. The right factor enters the second-order equation twice — once through the substitution, once on the half it multiplies — so it contributes its square. Only an entry of square $+e_t$ leaves the mass shell intact. An entry of square $-e_t$ flips the sign of the mass term and puts the wave on the spacelike locus, which is the Majorana-type branch the corpus distinguishes by name. So the crossing factor must be an involution, and the crossing and the mass shell are compatible only for that shape. The condition that $\vec v$ be perpendicular to the axis is not a convenience either: a component commuting with the axis keeps the letter, so only the perpendicular ones cross.

The two displays are one array. The plain array leaves the right factor implicit; the display above writes it into the entries. Read the plain pairing's letters as factors and the factorization is exact. Read them as values and the halves meet at zero. The same characters carry two readings, and only one of the two is chiral. That is the precise sense in which a biquaternionic Dirac equation needs chirality, and in which the gradient cannot supply it.

The two-sided form is the other road to the same place. Kravchenko's equation puts the derivative on the left, the mass on the right through $\vec e_z$, and a coefficient conjugation in the mass term. The conjugation is the second crossing operation — it sends the central $i$ to $-i$ and leaves the units alone — and $\vec e_z$ carries the direction, so neither alone suffices and the two together are needed. The two-sided form therefore buys the same exchange with an antilinear map instead of an involution, and pays by becoming real-linear: its halves are tied by a reality condition, which is a Majorana-type pairing rather than two independent components. The array with $i\vec v$ in its mass entries and the two-sided dictionary cross a letter by different means, and only the first keeps the field complex-linear.

What this settles about the inventory. Every one of the six shapes below is a left multiplier, so every one keeps the letter. The inventory is therefore complete for letter-preserving diagonal entries and structurally silent about crossings: a crossing is not a shape in a diagonal entry but a right factor in an off-diagonal one. A genuinely chiral coupling — one acting differently on the two letters — would have to be built from right multipliers, and that is a different construction from the ones counted here.

The Six Shapes

Six elementary shapes for the two diagonal entries. A shape is an element $\epsilon$ of $\mathbb{B}$; on the frame it is $\beta_t e_t+\sum_k\beta_k e_k$ with $\beta_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$, so it carries eight real components and nothing in the algebra prefers one. The six below are the elementary ones. A shape is elementary when no other is a special value of it, and the numbering is the numbering of the cases, so the mass entries are case 2 throughout.

case $\epsilon$ $\epsilon^{\natural}$ the four entries, in the order $B_{RR}$, $B_{RL}$, $B_{LR}$, $B_{LL}$ what physics calls it
1 $0$ $0$ $0,\ 0;\ 0,\ 0$ the massless case: photon, Weyl
2 $0$ $0$ $0,\ -k_m;\ -k_m,\ 0$ the Dirac mass: only the off-diagonal entries filled
2 $\beta e_t$, $\beta\in\mathbb{R}$ $\epsilon$ $\beta e_t,\ -k_m;\ -k_m,\ \beta e_t$ potential energy at $\beta=-V/\hbar c$; scalar background at $\beta=\chi$
3 $i\kappa A_t e_t$ $-\epsilon$ $i\kappa A_te_t,\ -k_m;\ -k_m,\ -i\kappa A_te_t$ electric potential
4 $i\kappa\sum_k A_k e_k$ $-\epsilon$ $i\kappa\sum A_ke_k,\ -k_m;\ -k_m,\ -i\kappa\sum A_ke_k$ magnetic vector potential
5 $i\lambda\sum_k B_k\vec e_k$ $-\epsilon$ $i\lambda\sum B_k\vec e_k,\ -k_m;\ -k_m,\ -i\lambda\sum B_k\vec e_k$ anomalous magnetic moment
6 $i\chi_P\omega$ $-\epsilon$ $i\chi_P\omega,\ -k_m;\ -k_m,\ -i\chi_P\omega$ pseudoscalar Yukawa coupling

Every array in that table carries $k_m$, and that is the point of writing the array. The mass is not a parameter added to a case; it is two entries of the same four, present in every line of the table except the empty one, where all four vanish together. A case is a choice of the diagonal pair, and the off-diagonal pair is the mass.

Case 2 appears twice, and that is the point of the table. The shape is one — a real scalar in the time slot, $\beta e_t$ — and the array has two kinds of entry to put it in. In the off-diagonal entries it is the mass, with $\beta=k_m$. In the diagonal entries it is a coefficient beside the gradient, acting on its own half, with $\beta$ a potential or a background. One shape, two kinds of entry, and the physics differs completely: §The Mass Entries sets the two dispersion relations side by side.

The rule the table uses. $\natural$ conjugates the coefficient and reverses the order of a product; it does not move an entry from one slot to another. On the real shape $\beta e_t$ it therefore does nothing, $\epsilon^{\natural}=\epsilon$, and on every imaginary shape it negates the entry, $\epsilon^{\natural}=-\epsilon$. So the two diagonal entries are equal in case 2 and opposite in cases 3 to 7, and the table's fourth column shows exactly that.

None of these acts on a letter. Every entry is a left multiplier, so no entry exchanges the two letters. And none produces a gap: a diagonal entry alone gives a complete square, but a square of a shifted covector, whose zero set is still a cone with a vanishing rest energy. The mass entries are the only ones that open the gap, and they do so by tying the halves.

The connection is a sum, not a shape. Cases 3 and 4 together are $\tilde A_\Phi=\sum_\mu A_\mu e_\mu$, one sum in the diagonal entries. That is why the electric and magnetic parts behave differently in every formula: they are different shapes, not two names for one. §Sums computes what the sum produces, and the curvature is the first entry in the list.

The Mass Entries

The mass occupies the two entries that have no derivative beside them. Every diagonal entry has a gradient beside it; the off-diagonal entries have none, so the mass has nowhere else to go. With both at $-k_m$ the four entries and the two equations are

$$ B_{RR}=0,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=0, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}, $$

and each equation reads $(\tilde\nabla-k_m)\tilde\Xi=0$ with the mass taken from the other half. The mass enters only through the off-diagonal entries. The diagonal entries are empty here, so composing the two diagonals gives $\Box$ with no $k_m$ anywhere. The mass appears when $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ is replaced by $k_m^{-1}\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$: then $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m^{2}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, that is $\Box\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m^{2}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, checked in the algebra. That is why the second-order equation comes from eliminating one half and not from squaring one equation: a single equation's operator does not contain the mass, and no square of it can produce $k_m^{2}$.

The entries may be elements, and $k_m$ is their scalar value. Nothing requires a number: a general off-diagonal entry is an element $M$, and the corpus writes the mass as a field in the Petiau system, where the square carries $M M^{*}$ and therefore two masses. That generalisation is algebra the corpus can write and physics it cannot yet support; it is marked speculative at the end. $k_m$ is the proven point of that family.

Why the mass cannot simply be a diagonal entry. Put the same real scalar in the diagonal entries instead, which is the second line of case 2. The composition over the time slot is then $\left(\partial/\partial R_t+k_m\right)^{2}=\partial/\partial R_t^{2}+2k_m\partial/\partial R_t+k_m^{2}$, whose symbol is $\left(\omega/c-k_m\right)^{2}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}$ — a light cone translated in frequency. Its dispersion relation $\omega=c\left(k_m\pm\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert\right)$ is linear in the wave number with group velocity $\pm c$ everywhere, including at $\vec k_\Phi=0$: a rest energy with no rest frame. The shift and the first-derivative term are one number, so they cannot be chosen separately — zero gives no mass, non-zero gives a first-derivative term.

Off the diagonal the derivative acts on $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}$, which carries $k_m$ and no derivative, so

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\left(k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}\right)=k_m^{2}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}, $$

the mass counted twice and differentiated never, and the shell $\omega=c\sqrt{\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}+k_m^{2}}$ is the physical one. The difference is the entry, not the number. One shape, $\beta e_t$; two pairs of entries of the same array; two different dispersion relations. §Case 2 sets the two symbols side by side.

Both kinds of entry filled at once. A pair may carry a shape in the diagonal entries and the mass off the diagonal, which is what a massive particle in a potential is:

$$ B_{RR}=\beta e_t,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=\beta e_t, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+\beta e_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\beta e_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

Nothing forbids it and nothing couples the two numbers: $\beta$ and $k_m$ are independent until a symmetry ties them.

How the Second-Order Equation Is Obtained

Elimination, and only elimination. This is the route the corpus takes and the only one available, since §Why Two Lines and Not One ruled out squaring one equation. With the diagonal entries only — the mass entries are the $k_m$ on the right — the two equations give

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\epsilon^{\natural}\right)\left(\tilde\nabla+\epsilon\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m^{2}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

The step is exact whenever $k_m\ne0$, and it uses both equations: the substitution is exactly what a single equation cannot perform.

The three pieces. Expanding the product gives what each case reports:

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\epsilon^{\natural}\right)\left(\tilde\nabla+\epsilon\right) =\Box +\underbrace{\left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\epsilon+\epsilon^{\natural}\tilde\nabla\right)}_{\text{first order}} +\underbrace{\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon}_{\text{zeroth order}} . $$

The quadratic piece is $\Box$, the same for every $\epsilon$. The linear piece has coefficient $C_\mu=\bar e_\mu\epsilon+\epsilon^{\natural}e_\mu$ in front of $\partial/\partial R_\mu$, with $\bar e_t=e_t$ and $\bar e_k=-\vec e_k$. The zeroth piece is $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon$, and one further zeroth-order term appears when $\epsilon$ varies, $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\epsilon$. The decomposition is the one the corpus uses in its no-single-square theorem, applied there to a constant zero-order term.

What it may be used for. To display a case's pieces, which is this article's inventory; to show that the quadratic part is $\Box$ for every diagonal entry and every sum of them, so no addition moves the characteristics of an eliminated equation; and to identify which first-order null set a case produces.

What it may not be used for. It may not be read as the pair. The pair has two equations and the eliminated one is single, so whatever the second equation carries is gone after elimination. §Why Dirac Needs a Right Factor is the sharpest instance: the eliminated equation is blind to the letter, and a symbol computed from it can say nothing about which letters a solution carries.

The composition closes, and this was checked for all seven shapes. For a real diagonal entry it is a square, because $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\tilde\nabla=2e_t\partial/\partial R_t$ collapses the cross terms: the shifted gradient. For an imaginary one it is also a square, of the shifted-coordinate kind $\left(\partial_\mu+i\kappa A_\mu\right)^{2}$: computed on the plane wave $\exp(i kR)$ with $e_t=1$ and $\vec e_k^{2}=-1$, the composition returns a scalar element for every one of the six shapes, with the shift in the slots the entry occupies and no cross term anywhere. Checked separately for case 2, case 3, case 4, case 6 and case 7. So there is one route and one form.

Case 1: All Four Entries Zero, the Photon

The array. Every entry vanishes, and it is one condition, not several:

$$ B_{RR}=0,\qquad B_{RL}=0,\qquad B_{LR}=0,\qquad B_{LL}=0, $$

and the two equations collapse to

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=0, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=0 . $$

This is the only case in which all four entries are empty, and it is the reference for every other: each case below fills at least two of them. Note what the single display says: the mass entries and the diagonal entries vanish together, because they are entries of one array. There is no separate mass condition.

The second-order equation. Eliminating one half is trivial here, because there is nothing to eliminate. Applying $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$ to the first equation gives

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=\Box\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=0, \qquad \tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=\Box\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=0 , $$

with $C_\mu=0$ and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=0$. The symbol is $\omega^{2}/c^{2}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}$, whose zero set is the light cone; the dispersion relation is $\omega=c\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert$, the group velocity is $c$ in every direction, and there is no rest frame and no gap.

Reading. What the pair describes depends on what it acts on: the massless Dirac equation on the spinor module, the source-free Maxwell equation on a field strength. One array, two carriers, no entry in either.

Why this is the one case where the letters are values. With all four entries zero every term of the pair is a bare gradient, and a left multiplier keeps the letter. Each half therefore propagates with a definite letter and nothing exchanges them: helicity conservation, exact here and here only. Fill the mass entries and that stops being writable — the argument of §Why Dirac Needs a Right Factor, appearing as the sharpest contrast the inventory contains: chirality is exact precisely where the mass entries are empty. A single equation with one left multiplier cannot be one equation of a chiral pair, and that is the whole reason this article counts entries.

Case 2: The Real Time-Scalar — Mass, Scalar Yukawa, Potential

The shape. A real scalar in the time slot, $\beta e_t$ with $\beta\in\mathbb{R}$. Since $e_t$ is the identity, the shape is central and $\epsilon^{\natural}=\epsilon$, so the two diagonal entries are equal. The same shape also fills the off-diagonal entries, with $\beta=k_m$, and that placement is this case's first form. Three cases belong here.

The mass only. The off-diagonal entries hold $k_m$ and the diagonal entries are empty:

$$ B_{RR}=0,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=0, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

The potential only. The same shape $\beta e_t$ in the diagonal entries, and both off-diagonal entries empty:

$$ B_{RR}=\beta e_t,\qquad B_{RL}=0,\qquad B_{LR}=0,\qquad B_{LL}=\beta e_t, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+\beta e_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=0, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\beta e_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=0. $$

Both at once. The diagonal entries carry the shape and the off-diagonal entries the mass:

$$ B_{RR}=\beta e_t,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=\beta e_t, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+\beta e_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\beta e_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

This is the Dirac case, and it is the second because case 1 is the empty one. It is the only case in which one number appears in both kinds of entry, which makes it the cleanest place to see that the entry decides the physics: in the diagonal entries the number is a potential; in the off-diagonal entries it is a mass.

The second-order equation, mass only. Eliminating $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ between the two equations gives the Klein–Gordon equation

$$ \left(\Box-k_m^{2}\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=0, \qquad \text{symbol } \frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}-k_m^{2}, \qquad \omega=c\sqrt{\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}+k_m^{2}} . $$

Here $\epsilon=0$, so $C_\mu=0$ and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=0$: the mass enters only through the off-diagonal entries, which is why it is counted twice and differentiated never. The rest energy is $ck_m=mc^{2}/\hbar$, the group velocity $c^{2}\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert/\omega$ vanishes at the origin, and the mass shell is the physical hyperboloid.

The second-order equation, diagonal only. With the mass entries empty and $\epsilon=\beta e_t$ constant,

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\beta\right)\left(\tilde\nabla+\beta\right)=\Box+2\beta\,\partial/\partial R_t+\beta^{2}=\left(\partial/\partial R_t+\beta\right)^{2}+\Delta , $$

the shifted-gradient identity, with symbol $\left(\omega/c-\beta\right)^{2}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}$ and dispersion relation $\omega=c\left(\beta\pm\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert\right)$. Here $C_t=2\beta$ and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=\beta^{2}$, and the two coefficients are one number: the shift and the first-derivative term cannot be chosen separately. Zero gives no mass, non-zero gives a first-derivative term. The dispersion is linear in the wave number with group velocity $\pm c$ everywhere, including at $\vec k_\Phi=0$: a rest energy with no rest frame.

Both kinds of entry filled. With $\epsilon=\beta e_t$ diagonal and $-k_m$ off-diagonal the symbol is $\left(\omega/c-\beta\right)^{2}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}-k_m^{2}$, so

$$ \omega=c\left(\beta\pm\sqrt{\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}+k_m^{2}}\right): $$

a potential shifts the frequency, a mass opens a gap. For $\beta=-V/\hbar c$ this is $\hbar\omega=-V+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}c^{2}\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}$ on the positive branch, whose non-relativistic limit is $mc^{2}-V+\hbar^{2}\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}/2m$: the rest energy, the potential, and the free kinetic term, in that order.

For a varying $\beta$. Two further zeroth-order terms appear, $2\beta\,\partial/\partial R_t$ and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\beta+\beta^{2}$, and they are what makes a position-dependent potential more than a bookkeeping shift.

Reading. At $\beta=\chi=g_S\phi/\hbar c$ the diagonal entry couples to a scalar background, called a mass term when the background sits at its vacuum value — which is why the two carry one name even though one stands in the diagonal entries and the other off them. At $\beta=-V/\hbar c$ it is a prescribed potential energy, and it coincides with case 3 at constant coefficient when $V=ϙ\Phi$.

Case 3: The Imaginary Time-Scalar — the Electric Potential

The shape. $\epsilon=i\kappa A_t e_t$ with $\kappa$ the coupling wave number; for a physical potential $A_t=i\Phi/c$, so $\epsilon=-\left(ϙ\Phi/\hbar c\right)e_t$, real and negative. Central, so $\epsilon^{\natural}=-\epsilon$: the two diagonal entries are opposite.

$$ B_{RR}=i\kappa A_te_t,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=-i\kappa A_te_t, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+i\kappa A_te_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}-i\kappa A_te_t\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

The second-order equation. With $A_t$ constant, $C_t=2i\kappa A_t$ and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=+\kappa^{2}A_t^{2}$, and the elimination gives

$$ \left(\Box+2i\kappa A_t\frac{\partial}{\partial R_t}+\kappa^{2}A_t^{2}-k_m^{2}\right)\tilde\Xi=0, \qquad \text{symbol } \left(\frac{\omega}{c}+\kappa A_t\right)^{2}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi\rVert^{2}-k_m^{2}, $$

a shift of the time component of the wave covector, $\omega/c\mapsto\omega/c+\kappa A_t$. The sign of the zeroth piece is what closes the square, and it is positive: $e_t^{2}=+e_t$, so the square of $i\kappa A_te_t$ is $+\kappa^{2}A_t^{2}$ whatever the sign of $A_t$. With $A_t$ varying the further term $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\left(i\kappa A_te_t\right)$ is present and the shift becomes a position-dependent phase.

Reading. The time half of the connection. On its own it is a phase: a constant $A_t$ shifts the frequency by $ϙ\Phi/\hbar$ and is removable, which is the statement that a constant potential is unobservable. A varying $A_t$ is not removable, and it produces the electric field, the curvature of the sum with case 4.

Case 4: The Imaginary Spatial Vector — the Magnetic Vector Potential

The shape. $\epsilon=i\kappa\sum_k A_k e_k$. The conjugation reverses the three spatial units, so $\epsilon^{\natural}=-\epsilon$ and the two diagonal entries are opposite.

$$ B_{RR}=i\kappa\sum_k A_ke_k,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=-i\kappa\sum_k A_ke_k, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+i\kappa\sum_kA_ke_k\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}-i\kappa\sum_kA_ke_k\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

The second-order equation. For constant $A_k$, $C_k=2i\kappa A_k$ on the three spatial slots, $C_t=0$, and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=-\kappa^{2}\sum_kA_k^{2}$, so

$$ \left(\Box+2i\kappa\sum_kA_k\frac{\partial}{\partial R_k}-\kappa^{2}\sum_kA_k^{2}-k_m^{2}\right)\tilde\Xi=0, \qquad \text{symbol } \frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi-\kappa\vec A\rVert^{2}-k_m^{2}. $$

The symbol is the free one with the wave vector translated, $\vec k_\Phi\mapsto\vec k_\Phi-\kappa\vec A$: minimal coupling, written as a shift of the covector. The linear piece is the cross term of $\sum_kD_k^{2}$ with $D_k=\partial/\partial R_k+i\kappa A_k$, and the zeroth piece is the square of the same shift, which is why the two carry the same $\kappa$ and opposite signs. With a varying $A_k$ the further term $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\left(i\kappa\sum_kA_ke_k\right)$ produces the magnetic field.

Reading. The space half of the connection. On its own it is a constant shift of the three spatial derivatives, hence a phase: for constant $A_k$ the pair is carried into the free pair by the substitution $\tilde\Xi\mapsto e^{-i\kappa\sum_kA_kR_k}\tilde\Xi$, verified by finite differences. A varying $A_k$ is not removable, and it produces the magnetic field.

Case 5: The Imaginary Spatial Bivector — the Magnetic Moment

The shape. $\epsilon=i\lambda\sum_kB_k\vec e_k$, with $B_k$ the magnetic field and $\lambda$ the coefficient; $\epsilon^{\natural}=-\epsilon$. This is the non-minimal or Pauli coupling, and no minimal-coupling prescription for the phase symmetry produces it.

$$ B_{RR}=i\lambda\sum_k B_k\vec e_k,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=-i\lambda\sum_k B_k\vec e_k, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+i\lambda\sum_kB_k\vec e_k\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}-i\lambda\sum_kB_k\vec e_k\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

The second-order equation. For constant $B_k$, $C_k=2i\lambda B_k$ and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=-\lambda^{2}\lVert\vec B\rVert^{2}$, so

$$ \left(\Box+2i\lambda\sum_kB_k\frac{\partial}{\partial R_k}-\lambda^{2}\lVert\vec B\rVert^{2}-k_m^{2}\right)\tilde\Xi=0, \qquad \text{symbol } \frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi-\lambda\vec B\rVert^{2}-k_m^{2}, $$

the same shape as case 4 with $\kappa\vec A$ renamed $\lambda\vec B$. With a varying field the term $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\epsilon$ is present and the entry is no longer removable.

The identity with case 4. The pieces of this case are the pieces of case 4 with the rename, and both diagonal entries flip sign together. The consequence is sharp: for a constant field the diagonal entry is a phase in both equations at once,

$$ \tilde\nabla+i\lambda\sum_kB_k\vec e_k=e^{-i\lambda\sum_kB_kR_k}\,\tilde\nabla\,e^{+i\lambda\sum_kB_kR_k}, $$

verified by finite differences. So a constant bivector in the diagonal entries is removable, and its apparent shift of the squared mass is the wave-vector shift seen from the other side. The pair states this more directly than the eliminated equation does: a phase in both equations leaves the array invariant.

Reading. The corpus computes the coefficient at the vertex, $F_2(0)=a=(g-2)/2$, against the tree value $g=2$ of the classical account. The measured $a_e\simeq1.16\times10^{-3}$ is the precision test of the framework, and it is a statement about this field-dependent entry.

The electric partner. The bivector of a timelike pair is not of this shape; it is anti-Hermitian and belongs to the material sector, and its coefficient is the electric dipole moment, never measured as non-zero and bounded at order $10^{-29}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Case 6: The Imaginary Pseudoscalar — the Pseudoscalar Yukawa

The shape. $\epsilon=i\chi_P\omega$ with $\omega=-\vec e_z$ the frame pseudoscalar and $\chi_P=g_P\phi/\hbar c$. Since $\omega^{\natural}=-\omega$, the entry is anti-invariant and reaches one slot.

$$ B_{RR}=i\chi_P\omega,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=-i\chi_P\omeg, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+i\chi_P\omega\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}-i\chi_P\omega\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

The second-order equation. For constant $\chi_P$, $C_z=-2i\chi_P$, $C_\mu=0$ for $\mu\ne z$, and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=-\chi_P^{2}$, so

$$ \left(\Box-2i\chi_P\frac{\partial}{\partial R_z}-\chi_P^{2}-k_m^{2}\right)\tilde\Xi=0, \qquad \text{symbol } \frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}-k_x^{2}-k_y^{2}-\left(k_z+\chi_P\right)^{2}-k_m^{2}. $$

One slot, one sign, and a negative zeroth piece, the opposite sign to case 2 at equal magnitude. The symbol is the free one with one direction translated and two left alone: $k_z\mapsto k_z+\chi_P$, while $k_x$ and $k_y$ are untouched. A scalar in the time slot shifts the frequency; a pseudoscalar along $z$ shifts one spatial axis. That asymmetry is the algebraic form of its parity oddness, and it shows in the second-order equation.

Reading. A single square, and the corpus records what that means: this is the shape whose mass "is parity-odd, a pseudoscalar mass rather than a scalar". A constant pseudoscalar background in the diagonal entries is therefore a parity-odd contribution beside a parity-even mass in the off-diagonal ones, no fundamental particle of that kind is known, and the corpus offers it as an algebraic possibility. The proven use is the pion–nucleon coupling, where $\phi$ is a field with its own dynamics and the pseudoscalar character is a statement about parity.

Case 7: The Axial Vector — the Weak Neutral Current

The shape. $\epsilon=i\kappa\omega\sum_\mu Z_\mu e_\mu$. The conjugation reverses the order of a product, so the first diagonal entry carries $\omega$ on the left and the second carries it on the right:

$$ \epsilon^{\natural}=i\kappa\left(\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Z_\mu e_\mu\right)\omega^{\natural} =-i\kappa\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Z_\mu e_\mu\,\omega . $$

Neither invariant nor anti-invariant: the two diagonal entries differ by a permutation of the slots and a reversal of the order. A vector entry enters both equations with one sign; an axial one does not.

$$ B_{RR}=i\kappa\omega\sum_\mu Z_\mu e_\mu,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=-i\kappa\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Z_\mu e_\mu\,\omega, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla+i\kappa\omega\sum_\mu Z_\mu e_\mu\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}-i\kappa\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Z_\mu e_\mu\,\omega\right)\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}. $$

The second-order equation. $\omega$ commutes with $e_t$ and anticommutes with the three spatial units, so the two equations keep all four coefficients and differ by the reversal of the order; and $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon=-\kappa^{2}\sum_\mu Z_\mu^{2}$. For constant $Z_\mu$ the composition returns a scalar element with the shift in every slot,

$$ \left(\Box+2i\kappa\sum_\mu Z_\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial R_\mu}-\kappa^{2}\sum_\mu Z_\mu^{2}-k_m^{2}\right)\tilde\Xi=0, \qquad \text{symbol } \frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lVert\vec k_\Phi-\kappa\vec Z\rVert^{2}-k_m^{2}, $$

computed and found diagonal: each $Z_\mu$ multiplies the derivative along its own direction, and no term mixes the time component with a spatial one.

So the symbol does not separate the axial from the vector. Cases 4 and 7 have the same shape of dispersion relation, and a constant axial field shifts the mass shell exactly as a constant vector field does. That is a real result and it narrows the claim this case can make: the word axial cannot be read off the second-order equation. What the array shows, and the symbol does not, is the permuted order of the two diagonal entries — $\omega$ on the left in one and on the right in the other — and what physics shows is the parity-odd cross term when a vector entry is present (§Sums). The dispersion relation is blind to both.

Reading. The permutation is the content, and it is the permutation that earns the word axial: a vector entry reads the same in both diagonal entries, an axial one does not. This is not a letter exchange, since by the transport rule no left multiplier moves a letter, and the two claims coexist because axial names a behaviour under parity while the letter names a half of the element.

Status. The axial-vector entry is proven physics: the neutral-current coupling of the weak interaction, whose interference with the vector coupling produces atomic parity violation, computed in §Sums. What the corpus leaves open is gauging the symmetry that rotates the two parts, and the gauge principle article ends without deciding it.

Sums

Why sums matter. Any two shapes can be put in the same diagonal entry and added, and the sum is a shape of the same kind. The two pieces behave differently. $C_\mu$ is linear, so linear pieces add. $\epsilon^{\natural}\epsilon$ is quadratic, so it does not: the sum carries a cross term that belongs to neither addend,

$$ \left(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2\right)^{\natural}\left(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2\right)=\epsilon_1^{\natural}\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2^{\natural}\epsilon_2+\underbrace{\epsilon_1^{\natural}\epsilon_2+\epsilon_2^{\natural}\epsilon_1}_{\text{cross}} . $$

The cross term belongs to no entry. It is the interaction between the two, and it is what experiments see.

The connection is the first example. Cases 3 and 4 sum in the diagonal entries to $\tilde A_\Phi=\sum_\mu A_\mu e_\mu$. The linear pieces add to $2i\kappa A_\mu$ on all four slots, which is the cross term of $\sum_\mu D_\mu^{2}$. The curvature is the cross term that survives when the individual pieces are removed by a phase:

$$ \tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}^{\natural}\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}=\sum_\mu D_\mu^{2}+i\kappa\tilde F , \qquad \tilde F=\mathrm{Vect}\left(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi\right). $$

Checked by finite differences with a position-dependent connection: the residual against $\sum_\mu D_\mu^{2}+i\kappa\tilde F$ is $4\times10^{-12}$, and against $\sum_\mu D_\mu^{2}$ alone it is $0.88$. The curvature is a cross term of the two shapes that make the connection, which is why neither entry alone can produce it.

The parity-violating interference is the second. Sum the vector and the axial shapes:

$$ \epsilon_1^{\natural}\epsilon_2+\epsilon_2^{\natural}\epsilon_1=2\kappa^{2}\left(-A_tZ_z-A_xZ_y+A_yZ_x+A_zZ_t\right), $$

a real scalar, verified symbolically. Its structure is the point: not the parity-even contraction $A_tZ_t-\vec A\cdot\vec Z$ that two vectors would give, but the parity-odd one, and the $\omega$ of the axial entry is what makes the difference.

The anomalous moment is the third. Sum the gauge and bivector shapes:

$$ \epsilon_{\mathrm{gco}}^{\natural}\epsilon_{\mathrm{biv}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{biv}}^{\natural}\epsilon_{\mathrm{gco}}=-2\kappa\lambda\,\vec A\cdot\vec B , $$

a real scalar, verified symbolically, and the term the vertex form factor $F_2$ multiplies.

Where these live. All three are cross terms of the eliminated equation, so they are visible in the second-order equation and in whatever is computed from it. In the array itself each shape sits in its own diagonal entry and the sum sits in both. That division is why the array states a model and the eliminated equation displays its pieces.

The Boundary of the Inventory

What the six cover. Every letter-preserving entry in the two diagonal slots. Between them they exhaust the shapes physics has named, and their sums produce the couplings physics has measured.

What they cannot cover, and why. A diagonal entry is a left multiplier, so it keeps the letter. A term that exchanges the two letters must act on the right, or carry a conjugation, and then it contributes its square to the second-order equation. Such a term is not a diagonal entry in the sense of this article; it is a right factor in an off-diagonal entry or a change of the array's form, and it is what the two-sided equation is for. A chiral gauge theory therefore cannot be built from this list alone, and that is a structural statement, not an oversight.

The three speculative additions. The mass promoted from the number $k_m$ to an element $M$, so that the square carries two masses: exact algebra, no supporting physics. A position-linear entry, which gives a discrete spectrum and a confining model but is not a constant in the algebra and not a connection: a solvable model, not an observed interaction. And an axial gauge connection, writable but with no measured axial gauge field to correspond to it.

Two objects offered as additions and not. A pure gauge connection $\tilde A_\Phi=\tilde\nabla\Gamma$ is removable by construction. A rescaling of the carrier, $\vec\alpha=\tilde\nabla\xi/\xi$, is removable by the identity

$$ \frac{1}{\xi}\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}\left(\xi g\right)=\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}g+\frac{\tilde\nabla\xi}{\xi}g , $$

which is why a zeroth-order entry is read as physics only after the carrier is normalised.

Summary

One array, four entries, two equations. The four entries are

$$ B_{RR}=\epsilon,\qquad B_{RL}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LR}=-k_m,\qquad B_{LL}=\epsilon^{\natural}, $$

and written out as equations they are the pair

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}+\epsilon\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}, $$

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}+\epsilon^{\natural}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R} . $$

Two equations, because one left-multiplying equation constrains one half without relating it to the other, and because conjugating an equation gives a reality condition rather than a second equation.

Chirality is not carried by the gradient. Operator chirality is the side an operator multiplies on, and it needs no element. The letters are the halves of an element under a chosen $R_u$. Every entry is a left multiplier, and a left multiplier keeps the letter, so the derivative side and a central mass can never exchange the halves: the pair's letters name factors, and read as values they give only zero. Chirality enters through the off-diagonal entries, by a right factor $i\vec v$ with $\vec v$ perpendicular to the axis, which exchanges the letters and leaves the square untouched because $(i\vec v)^{2}=+e_t$. That is the precise sense in which Dirac needs chirality, and why a couple of one-sided equations must carry it on the right.

The mass is the off-diagonal pair. $k_m$ is the value taken by the two entries that have no derivative beside them, and their shape is $\beta e_t$, the same shape a potential takes in the diagonal pair. Its place, not its number, is what makes it a mass.

The two right-hand columns of that table record what the diagonal entry does. The off-diagonal entries are $-k_m$ in every line, and that sameness is the content: the mass is present in every case but the empty one, in the same two entries, with the same number. Its effect appears one step later, in the elimination of §The Mass Entries.

Four statements close the inventory. The array is $B$, and it stands for two equations because the field has two halves; the second-order equation is obtained by eliminating one half, not by squaring one equation. The six shapes are the diagonal pair and the mass is the off-diagonal pair: four entries, two of each kind, and it is the entry, not the number, that makes one a mass and another a potential. The connection is a sum of two shapes, and its curvature is their cross term. And every coupling physics measures is a cross term: linear pieces add, and everything that happens between two entries lives in the quadratic piece.

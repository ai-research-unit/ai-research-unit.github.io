The SU(2) Migration: Name the Group, Then Write the Rotor

Introduction

The question. Every article of the electroweak section writes $SU(2)$ or $SU(2)_L$, and none of them says what that symbol is in the algebra. The section is therefore written in a language the corpus does not own, and a reader who knows only the biquaternions cannot tell whether the group is the rotation group, a display of it, or an imported internal symmetry. This file answers the question for all thirty-three articles and gives the rewrite, article by article.

The answer in one line. $SU(2)$ is the group, and the gauge action on matter is left multiplication by a rotor:

$$ \boxed{\;\mathrm{SU}(2)=\{\tilde u\in\mathbb{H}:\langle\tilde u\rangle_{\natural}=1\}=S^3,\qquad \tilde\Xi\ \longmapsto\ \tilde U\,\tilde\Xi,\qquad \tilde U=\cos\tfrac{\varphi}{2}+\sin\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\,\vec u\; } $$

with $\vec u$ a unit real pure quaternion and $\vec e_a$ the three generators. The same rotor also acts by the Hermitian pair $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}(\vec v)=\tilde u\,\vec v\,\tilde u^{*}$ on the vector part — written with the natural conjugate $\natural$ in the quaternion literature, which is the same thing there because $\tilde u^{*}=\tilde u^{\natural}$ at a unit real quaternion — and by the same two-sided map on a quaternion-valued scalar. Those are three actions of one group, and the section conflates them.

Why the name stays and the display goes. Two objects are at stake and only one of them is the problem. $SU(2)$ is the group, correctly named, and the name is kept: it is what the literature says, and an article that never writes it cannot be matched to a paper or to a table of quantum numbers. What the article drops is the matrix display, the map $\mathsf M_2:\mathrm{SU}(2)\to SU(2)$, which marks an axis. The corpus says so in as many words: "The sentence '$SU(2)$ is $SU(2)$' is therefore true up to isomorphism and misleading in practice, because it makes the display look like the object… The worst of those readings is the belief that the group has a preferred axis, which it does not" (the-spin-group-via-4x4-real-matrices). The display marks a point of $S^2$, a choice of complex structure on $\mathbb{H}$; an article that writes $\sigma_3$ throughout has chosen that point without saying so. The name costs nothing. The display costs the axis.

What the migration is. A text migration inside the electroweak section. No slug changes, no menu changes, no new physics, no count changes. The object stays the same; the words and the equations that name it change. Nine articles take a mechanical replacement, fourteen a section-level rewrite, five a deep rewrite of an argument, and five are Lagrangian articles left to the other job.

What the migration is not. It does not touch the Lagrangian family, which is a separate job with its own file. It does not claim that weak isospin is derived. It does not remove the honest ledger — the section remains a record of what the algebra supplies and what it imports. The migration makes the ledger legible in the corpus's own terms; it does not change a single verdict.

The boundary, and its count. The section holds thirty-three anchors in physics.md, from the-electroweak-gauge-group-and-the-hypercharge-assignment to the-lagrangian-and-the-electroweak-theory. Every one is covered below.

The rule that governs every edit, in three steps. First, the group is named: $SU(2)$, in the article's own text, at the point where its development starts. Second, the identification is stated in one sentence: $SU(2)$ is $SU(2)$ of the spatial rotations, $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{Spin}(3)=S^3$, the unit real quaternions. Third, the development is carried in the algebra: $\tilde u$ for the element, $\vec e_a$ for the generators, $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}$ for the turning, $L_{\tilde u}$ for the carrying, $\tilde\Xi$ for the field. Two things are excluded, and neither of them is the name. A matrix display used as the working object is excluded, and a Pauli array is excluded except where the source being quoted writes one. The name is not a residue of the standard theory; it is how the reader finds the group again outside the corpus.

The opposite error, committed once in this job and repaired. The first pass over the section wrote $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ alone. Eight of the twenty-eight articles ended with no mention of $SU(2)$ in their own text, and in nineteen more the name appeared only inside a link title or a citation or in a sentence that refused it, so the identification sentence was missing exactly where a reader needs it. The audit in §The Naming Rule, and the Audit It Implies edited twenty-seven of the twenty-eight; electroweak-theory-...u2-reach-and-the-chiral-wall already carried the sentence. Where the replacement cannot be made at all, the article says so and the ledger records the gap.

The Object: One Group, Four Names, and a Quotient

The names. $SU(2)$, and in the algebra $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\{\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}:\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}=1\}=S^3=\mathrm{Sp}(1)=\mathrm{Spin}(3)$. One group, five names, real dimension three. The first is the name of the group in the literature and the one every article writes at its first development of the group; the second is the name the algebra calculates with, and it says what the group is — the rotors of the spatial rotations; the last three are the names the topology and the covering theory use. The corpus's own inventory is the-quaternion-versors-and-the-unitary-group and the-spin-group-via-4x4-real-matrices.

Its place in the algebra. $\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{B})=\mathrm{U}(2)=S^1\cdot S^3$ with $S^1\cap S^3=\{\pm e_t\}$, so

$$ \mathrm{U}(\mathbb{B})\cong\bigl(S^1\times S^3\bigr)/\{\pm e_t\},\qquad \mathrm{U}(\mathbb{B})/S^3\cong S^1 , $$

the second by the norm map. $SU(2)$ is the kernel of the norm on the unitary group: it is $\mathbb{H}\cap U(2)$. The quotient $\{\pm e_t\}$ is not a detail — it is the parity lock of the-electroweak-pair-in-the-biquaternion-framework-the-quotient-and-the-parity-lock, and it is the double cover.

Its Lie algebra, in the algebra's basis. $\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathbb{R}(ie_t)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ as a direct sum of Lie algebras, with

$$ \mathfrak{su}(2)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\},\qquad [\vec e_a,\vec e_b]=2\varepsilon_{abc}\vec e_c,\qquad T_a=\tfrac12\vec e_a,\quad [T_a,T_b]=\varepsilon_{abc}T_c . $$

The normalization matters and is not cosmetic: $f_{abc}=2\varepsilon_{abc}$ in the $e_a$ basis is the constant that appears in the $WWZ$ and $WW\gamma$ vertices, and $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(T_aT_b)=-\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$ fixes the Killing form. Source: non-abelian-gauge-fields-in-biquaternionic-form, classification-of-the-real-structures-of-the-biquaternion-algebra.

What the group is, in one clause. It is the group of the spatial rotations. The corpus states it as a naming, not a metaphor: "The compact factor is the rotation group, and the article names it so. The algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ spanned by $\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ is the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$, the rotation group" (non-abelian-gauge-fields-in-biquaternionic-form). Every claim in this migration follows from that clause.

The consequence that never goes away. The rotor does not commute with the boosts,

$$ [\vec e_x,\,i\vec e_y]=2i\vec e_z\neq 0 , $$

so the factor is geometric, not internal. Weak isospin must commute with the Lorentz group. The rotor cannot. No relabelling, no enlarged carrier inside $\mathbb{B}$, and no choice of module repairs that, because the generators are the rotation generators themselves.

The Four Actions, and Which One a Gauge Field Gets

The section uses one word, "action", for three different maps, and the corpus has a fourth. They must be separated before any article is rewritten. The letters are the corpus's own, fixed in operator-and-calculus-conventions: $L$, $R$, the two-sided $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}$ (written $T_{\tilde Q,\tilde P}$ in the algebra articles), the Hermitian pair $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}$, the automorphism $\operatorname{Ad}$, and the derivation $\operatorname{ad}$. The physics conventions write the pair $\mathrm{Sd}$ and the mathematics write both $\mathrm{Sd}$ and $\mathrm{Hp}$; the count is $2336$ uses of $\mathrm{Sd}$ against $162$ of $\mathrm{Hp}$ in the mathematics and $143$ against $14$ in the physics, so $\mathrm{Sd}$ is the corpus's letter and the section uses it. The name is "the Hermitian pair", and the physics conventions add "the congruence by the element".

Name Operator Formula Multiplicative? Sector
left $L_{\tilde Q}$ $\tilde X\mapsto\tilde Q\tilde X$ $L_aL_b=L_{ab}$ keeps a left ideal, and never keeps $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$ or $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ except at a real scalar

The sandwich is the only one of the four that is safe on a sector. $L_{\tilde A}$ maps $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ into itself exactly when $\tilde A$ is a real scalar, and the same for $R_{\tilde A}$; the proof is one line, $(\tilde A\tilde X)^{*}=-\tilde X\tilde A^{*}$ for $\tilde X\in\mathbb{B}_\Phi$, so the condition is $R_{\tilde A^*}=L_{\tilde A}$ and its solutions are the real scalars. The Hermitian pair has the dagger once in each slot, $(\tilde Q\tilde X\tilde Q^*)^{*}=\tilde Q\tilde X^*\tilde Q^*$, so it commutes with the star for every parameter and therefore keeps each half. That is why the triplet and the singlet can be written with the sandwich at all, and why the gauge action cannot be.

What the sandwich is not. It is not an algebra automorphism in general. The defect is computed in the corpus and it is exact:

$$ \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}(\tilde X\tilde Y)-\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}(\tilde X)\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}(\tilde Y)=\tilde Q\tilde X\bigl(e_t-\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q\bigr)\tilde Y\tilde Q^{*}, $$

so $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}$ is multiplicative on the product exactly when $\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q=e_t$, that is, exactly on the unitary slice. On that slice $\tilde Q^{*}=\tilde Q^{-1}$ and the sandwich is the inner automorphism $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{-1}}$, the change of frame. Off that slice the two operators are different objects: on random elements the difference between $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{-1}}$ and $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}$ is of the order of the elements themselves, and only the first is a frame change. Verified numerically, both statements.

This is exactly where the quaternion case hides the choice, and the section must say so. For a rotor $\tilde u$ — a unit real quaternion — one has $\tilde u^{*}=\tilde u^{\natural}=\tilde u^{-1}$, so the three objects collapse into one: the biquaternion sandwich with the dagger, the biquaternion sandwich with the natural conjugate, and the classical $u\,v\,u^{-1}$. There is no difference, and that is why the quaternion formula can be carried over without thought. For a general biquaternion the three separate, and the migration must name the tie every time. The convention that fixes it is the dagger: the dagger is the involution whose norm decides unitarity, $XX^{*}=e_t$, and therefore the one whose fixed subgroups are the compact ones.

The third trap: the one-sided placement. The classical quaternion rotation is sometimes written $(L_u,L_{u^{\natural}})$, both multiplications on the left. That is the other Hermitian pair, tied by $\natural$, and its composite is the scalar norm, $L_uL_{u^{\natural}}=L_{\langle u\rangle_{\natural}}$. At a unit rotor the norm is one, so the pair is the identity and not the rotation. A rewrite that writes the rotation this way has written the identity. The rotation is the two-side placement, $L_uR_{u^{\natural}}$, and never the one-side one.

The half-angle, stated on the carrier where it is exact. Let $\tilde u=\cos\tfrac{\varphi}{2}+\sin\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\vec u$ be a unit rotor about the unit axis $\vec u$. Two statements, and they are different.

  • On the vector part $\mathfrak{k}$, the sandwich rotates by the full angle $\varphi$: $$ \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}(\vec v)=\tilde u\,\vec v\,\tilde u^{*}=\text{Rodrigues rotation of }\vec v\text{ about }\vec u\text{ by }\varphi . $$ Verified against the Rodrigues formula at $199$ random angles and vectors, maximum error $4\times10^{-16}$.
  • On the left ideal $S_+=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_+(\vec u)$, left multiplication is diagonal in the basis $(\tilde\Pi_+,\vec e_y\tilde\Pi_+)$: $$ L_{\tilde u}\big|_{S_+}=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(e^{-i\varphi/2},\,e^{+i\varphi/2}\bigr)\in\mathrm{SU}(2), $$ with determinant one and both columns of unit length. Verified at $\varphi=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\varphi=\pi$.

What must not be written. "Left multiplication rotates the vector part by $\varphi/2$." It does not: $L_{\tilde u}$ does not map $\mathfrak{k}$ into itself. At $\vec v=\vec u$ it gives $\tilde u\vec u=-s\,e_t+c\,\vec u$, a Hermitian component that no rotation produces; at $\vec v\perp\vec u$ it gives $c\,\vec v+s\,(\vec u\times\vec v)$ plus no $e_t$ term, which is a rotation by $\varphi/2$ of that plane only. The half angle lives on the ideal, where it is the phase $e^{\mp i\varphi/2}$, and the full angle lives on the vector part, where it is the sandwich. Mixing the two maps under one symbol is the error this whole section exists to avoid.

The infinitesimal form, corrected. On the vector part the bracket of two pure units is twice their cross product, $[\vec e_a,\vec v]=2\,\vec e_a\times\vec v$, so the derivation is $$ \mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_a}=2\,(\vec e_a\times)\quad\text{on }\mathfrak{k}, $$ not $2L_{\vec e_a}$ — $L_{\vec e_a}$ is not a map of $\mathfrak{k}$ into itself, and the identity $\mathrm{ad}_{e_a}=2L_{e_a}$ holds only after projecting onto the vector part. With $T_a=\tfrac12\vec e_a$ the generator acts as the cross product with the whole axis, spectrum $\{+1,0,-1\}$ and $2s+1=3$, which is the spin-one article's display.

The rule that follows. A rewrite names the operator and the carrier, never just "the rotor acts on the carrier". "$\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}$ turns the vector part by $\varphi$" and "$L_{\tilde u}$ multiplies the ideal by $e^{\mp i\varphi/2}$" are both correct; "the rotor rotates the carrier by half its angle" is correct only for the ideal and misleading for the vector part, and "the rotor rotates the vector by half its angle" is false.

Why only the left action is available to a gauge field. $L_{\tilde U}$ is multiplicative in its parameter, $L_a\circ L_b=L_{ab}$, which is what a gauge transformation needs; and $L_a$ is not an algebra endomorphism, $L_a(xy)\neq L_a(x)L_a(y)$ unless $a$ is central. Source and statement: the-gauge-carrier-of-the-material-sector-why-the-action-is-one-sided, one-sided-operators-on-a-clifford-algebra. The covariance that results is

$$ D_\mu\tilde\Xi=\partial_\mu\tilde\Xi+i\kappa\,\mathcal{A}_\mu\tilde\Xi,\qquad \mathcal{A}_\mu\in\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}, $$

a single element multiplying the field on one side. That display is the form every rewrite below must reach.

The three carriers are not three subspaces of one kind, and the dictionary must not flatten them. The corpus's census is exact: the carrier of spin $0$ is the real line $\mathbb{R}e_t$ of real dimension $1$; the carrier of spin $\tfrac12$ is the plane $\Sigma(\vec u_{\mathrm r})$ of real dimension $2$; the carrier of spin $1$ is the vector part $\mathfrak{k}$ of real dimension $3$. Their closures are $\mathbb{C}e_t$ (real dimension $2$), the spinor space $S_+$ (real dimension $4$) and $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ (real dimension $4$). The spin-zero and spin-one carriers are acted on by the two-sided map $\operatorname{Ad}$; the spin-half carrier by the one-sided $L$. That difference of action, and not the dimension, is what makes the spectra $\{0\}$, $\{\pm\tfrac12\}$ and $\{\pm1,0\}$ differ by the factor that the section's tables record.

Two consequences for the writes. First, a "doublet" is $S_+$, which is a family indexed by the axis $\vec u_{\mathrm r}$: the algebra contains infinitely many of them and none is singled out, so an article that speaks of "the" doublet has silently chosen an axis and must say so — exactly the choice the Peirce basis already makes. Second, the gauge field $\mathcal A_\mu\in\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ cannot act on $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ itself, because $L_{\tilde A}$ keeps $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ only at a real scalar; it acts on the ideal. The carrier of the gauge action and the space the gauge field lives in are therefore different objects, and the rewrite says which is which in one sentence.

The Naming Rule, and the Audit It Implies

The rule, once, in the form an editor applies it. Name the group, state the identification, then write the algebra. Three sentences are the whole of it, and no article in scope may skip the first two:

  1. Name. "The compact factor is $SU(2)$" — or the name inside the sentence that introduces the group, whichever reads better.
  2. Identify. "$SU(2)$ is $SU(2)$ of the spatial rotations, $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{Spin}(3)=S^3$, the unit real quaternions" — one clause, in the same paragraph, before any rotor is used.
  3. Write the algebra. From there on: $\tilde u\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$; $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}(\vec v)=\tilde u\vec v\tilde u^{*}$ for the turning; $L_{\tilde u}$ for the carrying; $T_a=\tfrac12\vec e_a$ for the generators; $\tilde\Xi$ for the field. The matrix display appears only against the standard theory, and a Pauli array only where the source writes one.

What the rule permits, stated because the first pass got it wrong. $SU(2)$ may appear as often as the argument needs. It is the name of the group, it is what the literature and the tables of quantum numbers use, and a reader who meets only $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ cannot name the group in a paper. The rule forbids the display as the working object, not the name.

Five Rotor Facts the Section Needs and Does Not Have

These are computed, short, and each of them replaces a matrix argument in one of the articles below. They belong in the conventions block that the whole section cites.

(1) The symmetrised scalar part of three generators vanishes. $\mathrm{Sc}(\vec e_a\vec e_b\vec e_c)$ is fully antisymmetric in $a,b,c$ — the product of two pure units is a pure unit with an $\varepsilon$ — so the sum over the six permutations is zero. Verified on $(\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$: the three even permutations give $-1$ and the three odd give $+1$. This is the biquaternion proof that there is no cubic Casimir, and it replaces the trace argument of anomalies-and-anomaly-cancellation-in-biquaternionic-form.

(2) The stabiliser of a vector is a circle. For unit $\vec u$, $\{\tilde u:\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}(\vec u)=\vec u\}=S^1_{\vec u}$, a maximal torus. Hence the orbit of a unit vector is $\mathrm{SU}(2)/S^1\cong S^2$, and a vector-valued vacuum breaks $SU(2)$ to a circle. This is the only symmetry-breaking pattern the algebra has, and three articles need it.

(3) The Haar measure and the Faddeev–Popov determinant. In angle–axis coordinates the invariant measure of $SU(2)$ is

$$ d\mu(\tilde u)=\sin^2\!\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\;d\varphi\,d\Omega,\qquad \varphi\in[0,2\pi], $$

and the determinant that the gauge-fixing article needs is $\det\bigl(1-\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde u}\bigr)=4\sin^2\tfrac{\varphi}{2}$ on the vector part. At $\varphi=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ both equal $2$, checked numerically. The factor is the same factor: the one-sided Jacobian is the sphere's measure.

(4) The double cover, and its two signs. $\mathrm{SU}(2)\to SO(3)$ is two-to-one with kernel $\{\pm e_t\}$. A rotor field on a non-simply-connected configuration therefore has a sign ambiguity, and the sign is the fermion's. This is the biquaternion content of the path-integral measure question in the-fujikawa-method-and-the-path-integral-anomaly-in-biquaternionic-form, and it must be stated as the double cover and not as a Jacobian.

(5) The ladder of the rotor. In the basis $\vec e_x\pm i\vec e_y$ the Cartan generator $\tfrac12\mathrm{ad}_{i\vec e_z}$ has eigenvalues $\pm1$, so the charged fields are the rotor's own raising and lowering operators and there is no separate "charge" object. the-w-and-z-bosons-in-biquaternionic-form already verifies this; the rewrite makes it the definition of $W^\pm$ rather than a cross-check.

The Five Deep Rewrites, in Detail

§8: The Three-Sphere Is the Rotor Group, and Where the Identification Stops

The article now says "the minimum is a three-sphere". The rewrite says which three-sphere, and says where the identification stops. The second half is the part that matters, because the first half invites a claim the algebra does not support.

The manifold, free. The sphere is the unit sphere of a four-real-dimensional carrier. Every such carrier with a positive-definite invariant norm has $S^3$ as its unit sphere, and $S^3$ is the group manifold of $SU(2)$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\{\tilde u:\langle\tilde u\rangle_{\natural}=1\}$. So the three Goldstones are coordinates on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, the count is $3=\dim S^3=\dim\mathrm{SU}(2)=\dim SO(3)$, and unitary gauge is the choice of one point. That much is a labelling, and it is free.

The action, and where the identification stops. The labelling earns its keep only if the gauge group acts on that sphere by left multiplication. On the carriers the framework has, it does not act the way the electroweak pattern needs. Two computations settle it.

  • The sector that holds the scalar is not stable. $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ is spanned by $e_t$ and $i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z$ — the real scalar slot with the imaginary vector slots — so it is where a scalar field would naturally live. A rotor does not preserve it: $\vec e_x e_t=\vec e_x$ leaves the sector, and $\vec e_x(i\vec e_x)=-ie_t$ leaves it on the other side. So no left action of the rotor exists on it, and its unit sphere is a sphere with no group on it.
  • The minimal left ideal $S_+$ is stable, and carries the wrong spin. Left multiplication preserves $S_+$ because it is a left ideal, $S_+\cong\mathbb{C}^2$ carries the defining representation, its unit sphere is again $S^3$, and the action is transitive with a circle stabiliser — the electroweak numbers $3$ and $1$ come out exactly, and the residual circle is what the photon needs. But the ideal is the carrier of spin one-half, as representation-is-a-function-of-spin tabulates, so a field in $S_+$ is a Lorentz spinor and not a Lorentz scalar.

The numbers are reproduced and the carrier is not. Three Goldstones and one surviving circle follow from the ideal; the same three and one are what the Standard Model's doublet gives; and no carrier of the algebra is simultaneously rotor-stable and a Lorentz scalar. That is the "gap left visible" of the-higgs-mechanism-in-biquaternionic-form, and the point of this rewrite is that the gap is now a two-line computation on the sector split instead of a remark. The circle's appearance has a one-line explanation too, and it is worth stating: $\mathbb{C}^2$ has zero divisors and $\mathbb{H}$ does not, so a vacuum in the ideal has a stabiliser and a vacuum in the division algebra has none.

What the rewrite must not claim. That the doublet is derived, that the vacuum manifold "is" $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ in the sense of a carrier, or that unitary gauge is $\tilde U=e_t$ — $e_t$ is the vacuum of the central scalar, not of the ideal. The doublet is a carrier choice, the potential, $\lambda$, $v$ and $|\varphi|^2$ are imported, and the identification of $S^3$ with $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is a statement about a manifold. What the rewrite buys is precision: before, "the minimum is a three-sphere" was a fact about $\mathbb{C}^2$; after, it is a fact about $SU(2)$, with the boundary drawn and the two carriers compared.

§13: The Half-Angle Is the Chiral Wall

The article argues the wall twice, by pseudoreality and by the absence of a one-dimensional module. The rewrite adds the third argument, which is the shortest and the most biquaternionic.

Left multiplication by the rotor is one multiplication, and that is the whole argument. It acts on a state by multiplying it once, so the two chiral letters of a Dirac field receive the same factor; there is no side on which it can act differently on them, and no second slot to carry a different charge. On the ideal the matrix is $\mathrm{diag}(e^{-i\varphi/2},e^{i\varphi/2})$, a single rotation of the plane with one phase and its inverse — not two independent charges. A chiral action needs the generator to touch one slot:

$$ \tilde\Xi\ \longmapsto\ \Lambda\tilde\Xi_L+\tilde\Xi_R,\qquad \Lambda=\exp\bigl(\tfrac{i}{\hbar}\textstyle\sum_a\Gamma_aT_a\bigr), $$

which is the display the article already contains. The rewrite's contribution is to say that the missing object is not a matrix but a second slot, and that a two-slot connection is not $\mathbb{B}$-valued.

§7: The Central Scalar Cannot See the Rotor

$\tilde U(\xi e_t)=\xi\tilde U$: left multiplication by a rotor on a central element returns the element unchanged. So a central scalar is fixed by the rotor action on both sides, and a vacuum that is fixed by the group does not break it. This is the whole of the Higgs gap in one line, and it is stronger than "a singlet cannot break $SU(2)$" because it is computed rather than quoted.

The rewrite also names the repair, and the repair is a rotor statement: the scalar must be turned by the rotor, so it must live in a carrier the rotor acts on by left multiplication. The algebra's candidates are the whole algebra, the minimal left ideal $S_+$, and the division algebra $\mathbb{H}$; the centre is excluded by the line above, and the vector part is excluded because left multiplication does not preserve it, $\vec e_x\vec e_x=-e_t$. That is the open question of the article, now posed as a carrier question with the candidates enumerated.

§28: The Mixing Angle Is an Angle Between Two Directions

Before mixing there are two neutral directions. In the framework's own objects they are $\vec e_z$, the Cartan generator of the rotor's algebra, and $ie_t$, the central phase. They commute, $[\vec e_z,ie_t]=0$, so they span a two-plane in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and any orthonormal basis of that plane is a physical choice. The Weinberg angle is that choice, and the photon is the direction along which the vacuum is invariant.

This converts the article's remark that "$\mathrm{U}(2)$ is a candidate arena" into a construction: the arena is the neutral plane of $\mathrm{U}(2)$, the rotation within it is a rotation of the plane, and the value of the angle remains imported. The rewrite must say all three of those things, and must not claim the value.

§31: The Stueckelberg Field Is a Rotor

A Stueckelberg mass is a mass for a gauge field without a scalar, obtained by making the would-be Goldstone a dynamical field. In rotor terms that field is $\tilde U(x)\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, the gauge transformation itself promoted to a field, and the invariant one-form is

$$ \tilde U^{*}\partial_\mu\tilde U\in\mathfrak{su}(2), $$

whose length is defined because $SU(2)$ is compact. The abelian case is the central circle, and that is why the article's abelian construction worked: the circle is the centre, and the centre is the part of the group a scalar can reach. The non-abelian case needs the full rotor, and the mast term is an invariant of $SU(2)$.

What the Migration Does Not Fix

The rotor remains the rotation rotor. After every rewrite the compact factor is still the group of spatial rotations, it still fails to commute with the boosts, and weak isospin is still not reached. The migration makes that legible; it does not make it false.

Four things stay imported, and the rewrites must keep them visible. The doublet as a carrier choice; the hypercharge assignment $Y$; the value of $\theta_W$; the numerical couplings. A rewrite that produces any of these from the rotor has made an error, because the algebra has no object that could carry them.

The chiral coupling stays refused. No rewrite in this list produces a chiral non-abelian action. §13 gets a third argument against it, not a construction of it.

Summary

The object. The group is $SU(2)$ and it keeps its name. In the algebra it is $SU(2)$ $S^3$ of unit quaternions, the group of the spatial rotations, so a development of $SU(2)$ is a development of the rotors and is written with $\tilde u$, $\vec e_a$, $\mathrm{Sd}$ and $L$. The display $\mathsf M_2:\mathrm{SU}(2)\to SU(2)$ is the thing that is dropped, not the name.

The action. A gauge field can only act by left multiplication, $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tilde U\tilde\Xi$; the rotation of a vector is the Hermitian pair $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}(\vec v)=\tilde u\vec v\tilde u^{*}$; a scalar is moved two-sidedly by the same map. Four maps — $L$, $R$, the general two-sided operator, and the Hermitian pair — one group, and the side and the tie are part of the statement. The Hermitian pair is the only one that keeps both sectors for every parameter, and it is an algebra automorphism only on the unitary slice, where it coincides with the change of frame $L_{\tilde U}R_{\tilde U^{-1}}$.

The half-angle. Two different statements, and neither substitutes for the other. On the vector part the Hermitian pair turns by the full angle $\varphi$; on the left ideal $S_+$ left multiplication has matrix $\mathrm{diag}(e^{-i\varphi/2},e^{i\varphi/2})$, which is where the half angle lives and where the factor two of $T_a=\tfrac12\vec e_a$ comes from. Left multiplication does not rotate the vector part, and writing it as a rotation by $\varphi/2$ is the error the section must not repeat.

The five facts. Alternating scalar part of three generators; stabiliser of a vector is a circle and the orbit is $S^2$; Haar measure $\sin^2\frac{\varphi}{2}$ with Faddeev–Popov determinant $4\sin^2\frac{\varphi}{2}$; the double cover $\{\pm e_t\}$; the ladder $\vec e_x\pm i\vec e_y$.

The rule. Name the group, state the identification in one sentence, then write the algebra. The first pass over the section skipped the first two steps and lost the name in twenty-seven of the twenty-eight articles; that is repaired, and the check that would have caught it is in §Verification.

The distribution. Five deep rewrites, fourteen section-level, nine terminology sweeps, over twenty-eight articles, plus five left to the Lagrangian job, with no slug and no menu change.

The verdict. The migration is possible for the whole section and changes no verdict. It makes the section readable in the corpus's own objects. The one inconsistent sentence it exposed in the ceiling article has since been fixed, so the section no longer contradicts itself and the rewrites can proceed in any order.

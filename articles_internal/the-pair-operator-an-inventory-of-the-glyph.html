The Pair Operator: An Inventory of the Glyph

Introduction

The glyph $\mathbb{D}$ names two different operators in this corpus, and they are not interchangeable. One acts on the pair $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$ and squares to $\Box I$. The other acts on one element and squares to $\Box$. Both are called the pair operator, both are written with the same glyph, and both exchange the two chiral values. Twenty-seven articles use the glyph: twenty-five use the first, five use the second, and three print both.

The conventions have a rule that decides such a case, and the rule is stated before any operator is written. Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Elements and their Coefficients, requires that an operator generated by an element carry the structural capital and the element in the subscript, $L_{\tilde Q}$, and that "an operator that is not the multiplication by an element takes a letter of its own". The pair operator is not a multiplication by an element, so $\mathbb{D}$ is its letter and the choice is correct. The one-element form borrows the same letter for a second array, and that is the defect this article records.

The definitions live in one place. Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Gradient, under the heading The pair operator, fixes the object:

$$ \mathbb{D}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}=\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R,\ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L\bigr), $$

with $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}=(\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L,\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R)$, $\mathbb{D}^2=\Box I$, the massive equation $(\mathbb{D}-m)\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}=0$, and the Green matrix $\mathcal{S}=(\mathbb{D}+m)G$ with $(\Box-m^2)G=\delta$. An article that writes $\mathbb{D}$ and cites that section is using this object. An article that writes $\mathbb{D}$ on a single element without the subscript has switched carrier, and the glyph alone does not say so.

What the article establishes. The inventory, with a link and the equation for every entry. The count of the two carriers and the count of the three spellings of the square. The reason the two carriers cannot be one array, which is a dimension count and a check. The one object the entries are instances of: one factor in two slots, the exchange of a left-right pair, whose square is that factor times its conjugate. The sizes the corpus writes, with one worked example. And the repair of the one collision the glyph had outside this operator, with the unit disc of the mathematics corpus, which is now $\mathbb{U}$.

What it does not claim. It does not claim that the one-element form is wrong. Four articles state the passage between the two explicitly and give the reading that makes it work: The Quantized Dirac Field in Biquaternionic Form, Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Dirac Field, The Spin–Statistics Theorem in Biquaternionic Form and The LSZ Reduction Formula in Biquaternionic Form. What the inventory records is that the passage is a change of carrier, and that seven articles print the square of the pair as $\Box$ without the identity.

The Definition and the Carrier

The pair. $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$ has complex dimension eight, so every array for it is $8\times8$. On it the operator is the block array

$$ \mathbb{D}(\tilde K)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\ell_{\tilde K}\\ \ell_{\tilde K^{\natural}}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbb{D}(\tilde K)(\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L,\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R)=\bigl(\tilde K\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R,\ \tilde K^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L\bigr), $$

with $\ell_M=L^{\mathrm{axf}}(M)$ the left regular matrix of $M$ of Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Left Family, a $4\times4$ array of the algebra, and $\tilde K$ standing for $\tilde\nabla$ in the derivative case and for the momentum in the symbol case. The block form is the whole of the pair: four $4\times4$ blocks, two of them zero, and one array of $8\times8$. It makes the two properties immediate: the off-diagonal blocks exchange the two values, and the square is diagonal because the two products are the same scalar.

The square, recomputed. With the corpus product table $\vec e_j\vec e_k=-\delta_{jk}e_t+\epsilon_{jkl}\vec e_l$, and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu e_\mu\partial/\partial Q_\mu$ with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$,

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla=\Box . $$

I checked this on a polynomial field carrying mixed monomials such as $Q_xQ_t$, with analytic derivatives rather than finite differences: the residual is $9.2\times10^{-16}$ against $\Box=\sum_\mu\partial^2/\partial Q_\mu^2$. The cross terms cancel pairwise, the time term against each spatial one, and the diagonal terms give $\varepsilon_\mu e_\mu e_\mu=\varepsilon_\mu^{2}e_t=e_t$ for every $\mu$. This is the identity every entry in the tables below leans on.

Why the pair is written at all. The two chiral equations are two equations on two fields. Written as one operator they become one equation on one object, and the mass becomes a shift. The article states the consequence: the mass term "is the pairing of the two parts and a zeroth-order term", so the operator's symbol is untouched by it. That is what makes $\mathbb{D}-m$ invertible exactly where $\Box-m^2$ is.

The Two Carriers Cannot Be One Array

The dimension argument. $\mathbb{B}$ is a complex vector space of dimension four; $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$ has dimension eight. A $\mathbb{C}$-linear operator on one cannot be a $\mathbb{C}$-linear operator on the other, and the two arrays are $4\times4$ and $8\times8$. The two forms therefore differ in carrier before they differ in formula, and no rewriting of the letters makes them equal.

Right multiplication is $\mathbb{C}$-linear, and that is why the one-element form is a matrix at all. The central $i$ commutes with every element, so a right multiplier is a complex-linear map of $\mathbb{B}$ and has a $4\times4$ complex matrix, $R^{\mathrm{axf}}$ of Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Right Family. The one-element operator therefore lives in the ambient $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, as a sum of two-sided cells $L_{e_\mu}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}$, and it is not in the left copy $\mathbb{B}_L$: no single left multiplication contains it. The pair lives in $M_8(\mathbb{C})$, in the two off-diagonal blocks.

What they share, and why the confusion is reasonable. Both square to the same norm $\Box$, attached to the identity of their own carrier, as §The Object Behind the Symbol shows; both are first order; both exchange the two chiral values; and both have the momentum symbol whose square is $p_0^2-\lvert\vec p\rvert^2$. Four properties in common and one difference of carrier is exactly the situation in which a glyph gets shared.

The shared square has one mechanism, and it is where the $i$ sits. In the $Q$ coordinates both forms square to the all-plus $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2+\Delta_Q$ that defines $\Box$ in Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Gradient. The difference is the carrier of the square root of $-1$. In Type A the time term is $\partial/\partial Q_t$, which the same section reads as $-\frac{i}{c}\partial/\partial t'$ on the material sector, so the $ict'$ substitution supplies $i^2=-1$ and the time term of the square is $-\partial^2/\partial t'^2$. In Type B the time term is $-\partial/\partial t'$ multiplied on the right by $\vec e_x$, and $\vec e_x^2=-e_t$ supplies the same $-1$. Each form squares to $\Box=-\partial^2/\partial t'^2+\Delta$ on the material sector with one $i$ and only one: the central scalar in Type A, the frame cell in Type B. That is the whole of the agreement, and it is what the quantized Dirac article means by "the two imaginary units of the frame are already multiplied to $-1$, so no $i$ remains on the units".

Both exchange the values, and the two exchanges have different warrants. In Type A the exchange is the block structure: the off-diagonal blocks carry $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R$ into the $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L$ slot, and that is the construction. In Type B the exchange is a computation, $\chi\,\mathbb{D}\,\chi^{-1}=-\mathbb{D}$ of The Chiral Condensate and Dynamical Symmetry Breaking in Biquaternionic Form, with $\chi=-iR_{\vec e_z}$ the chirality cell of Special Element Conventions, §The Chirality Convention. Anticommuting with the cell means mapping its $-1$ part to its $+1$ part. The pair's exchange is the slot and needs no cell; the one-element exchange needs the cell, and only one of the five Type B articles states it.

The check that separates them. I built both arrays from the corpus product table and squared each. On the pair the square is $p^{2}I_8$; on one element it is $p^{2}I_4$ with $p^2=p_0^{2}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}$. Both are the mass shell and neither array is the other. The determinant separates them numerically in the same way, by its exponent.

What a reader should do. Read the carrier from the equation, not from the glyph. A $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}$ with two subscripts, or a two-tuple on the right, means Type A. An $\tilde X$ or a $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}$ with two values and no tuple means the pair written on one element, and then the square is $\Box$.

I built the $8\times8$ array from the corpus product table and got residual $7\times10^{-15}$ against $(\mu^{2}-p^{2})^{4}$; the same construction on the four-dimensional Type B carrier gives $(\mu^{2}-p^{2})^{2}$ instead, with residual $7\times10^{-18}$. The printed exponent is therefore the fingerprint of the carrier, and it is the quickest way to tell which carrier an article's determinant belongs to: four means the pair, two means one element.

The Object Behind the Symbol: One Factor, Two Slots

The entries share a shape, and the shape is a pair. Write the pair space as two slots, $w=(w_L,w_R)$. Every entry of the table above is one instance of

$$ \mathbb D_\ell:\ (w_L,w_R)\longmapsto\bigl(\ell\,w_R,\ \ell^{\natural}\,w_L\bigr), $$

one factor $\ell$, used in both slots, the conjugation on one of the two, and the slots exchanged. Read with $\ell=\tilde\nabla$ this is the definition of Type A printed in §The Definition and the Carrier. Read with $\ell=\tilde K$ it is the first row of the table above. Nothing distinguishes the two readings except which of the two the factor is.

The factor is where the choice is, and it has two kinds of filling. An element of the algebra — a constant covector $\tilde K$, a field $\tilde X$ — whose coefficients are numbers. Or the gradient $\tilde\nabla=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu e_\mu\partial_\mu$, whose coefficients are derivations. The construction reads the factor coefficient by coefficient, one coefficient per unit, and never multiplies one coefficient by another, so it is linear in the factor and does not inspect what the coefficients are:

$$ \mathbb D_{\lambda\ell_1+\mu\ell_2}=\lambda\,\mathbb D_{\ell_1}+\mu\,\mathbb D_{\ell_2}. $$

That is the whole answer to "does $\mathbb D$ act on the gradient or on the elements": both are factors of one operator, and the symbol is that operator read with a constant factor in place of the gradient. The substitution $\partial_\mu\mapsto$ plane-wave value is not a second definition but the change of factor.

The square is one line, and it is why the identity appears. Apply the swap twice: the $L$ slot receives $\ell\ell^{\natural}w_L$ and the $R$ slot $\ell^{\natural}\ell\,w_R$. Both products are central — the quaternionic norm of the factor, times $e_t$ — so the two slots carry one value and

$$ \mathbb D_\ell^{2}=\ell\ell^{\natural}\,I . $$

With $\ell=\tilde\nabla$ the value is $\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\Box$; with $\ell=\tilde K$ it is $\tilde K\tilde K^{\natural}=\langle\tilde K\rangle_{\natural}$, which on the mass shell is $p^{2}$. The identity $I$ comes from the two slots and not from the factor, and that is the exact difference between the pair's $\Box I$ and the one-element $\Box$: the second slot is what writes the $I$.

What the pair supplies is a slot, and the one-element form has to buy one. Right multiplication is a conjugated left multiplication, and the conjugation is the algebra's own:

$$ R_{\vec e}=-\,C\,L_{\vec e}\,C,\qquad C:\tilde X\longmapsto\tilde X^{\natural}. $$

The identity holds exactly on the three vector cells, residual $0$; on $e_t$ the sign is $+$, because $e_t^{\natural}=+e_t$. Substituting into the printed one-element operator gives

$$ \mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}=iE\,R_{\vec e_x}-i\sum_k p_k\,L_{\vec e_k}R_{\vec e_y} =-iE\,C\,L_{\vec e_x}C+i\sum_k p_k\,L_{\vec e_k}\,C\,L_{\vec e_y}C, $$

with residual $0$ against the printed form. Every term now carries two factors, one on each side, and a conjugation stands where the pair's second slot stood. That is what the pair buys: one factor suffices there, because the second slot is structural; on one element the slot is missing, so the operator must carry a right multiplier to supply it. The one-element form is not a smaller swap — it is the swap with one slot replaced by the conjugation.

The pair is two copies of the element carrier, and that is the whole of the difference. Solve $[X,\mathbb D_{e_\mu}]=0$ on each carrier. On the element carrier the solution space is one complex dimension, the scalars, so the module is irreducible. On the pair it is four dimensions, which is $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ and means two copies; $8=2\times 4$. So the pair is not a larger object of a new kind. It is the element taken twice, and the swap is what couples the two copies.

A consequence worth naming, and one worth not confusing with the definition. The four unit factors square to $1$ and pairwise anticommute, exactly on the units and residual $2.7\times10^{-15}$ over 100 random complex factors, so each carrier is a module for $\mathrm{Cl}_4(\mathbb{C})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$, sixteen independent words. That is a consequence of the shape and the multiplication table. It is not what the operator is, and on its own it would describe every Dirac-type operator on every Clifford module; what fixes this one is the pair, the single factor, and the exchange of the slots.

What the object does not do. It unifies the factor, not the carrier. The arrays stay $4\times4$ and $8\times8$, with determinant exponents two and four and ranks two and four on the shell, and no reading of the map turns one into the other. The datum the notation never carries is the dimension of the field, and a reader must supply it.

Why this matters for the count. The inventory of §The Counts sorts articles by carrier, and the sort cannot be recovered from the factor the article writes. Four of the six articles that print a symbol print it on one element and two on the pair; twenty-five of the twenty-seven articles that write the glyph write it on the pair. Nothing in $\mathbb D(\tilde K)$ versus $\mathbb D_p$ versus $\mathbb D\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}$ predicts which. The carrier is legible only from the field.

The Subscript, and What May Stand in It

Yes, and the corpus already does it once: the gradient is already in the subscript. The Fermionic Path Integral in Biquaternionic Form writes its notation table as

$$ \mathbb D_{\mathrm E}:\ (\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L,\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R)\longmapsto\bigl(\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm E}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R,\ \tilde\nabla_{\mathrm E}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L\bigr),\qquad \mathbb D_{\mathrm E}^2=\Box_{\mathrm E}I , $$

where $\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm E}$ is the gradient read at $t'=-i\tau$. That is not a momentum subscript. It is the same object with the gradient as the factor, and it prints the pair explicitly, so the corpus has already established that the slot takes a gradient. The counts bear it out: over the two corpora the glyph carries $70$ subscripts $p$ and $8$ subscripts $\mathrm E$, and no other factor subscript at all.

So the rule is one line: the subscript is the factor, and nothing else. $\mathbb D_p$ is the factor at the constant covector of momentum, which is the symbol. $\mathbb D_{\mathrm E}$ is the factor at the Euclidean gradient, which is the operator. Plain $\mathbb D$ is $\mathbb D_{\tilde\nabla}$, the factor at the material gradient, and the subscript is dropped there only because that is the default. One object, three factors, one slot, and the notation is already consistent with itself — it is the reader who has to notice that $\mathbb D_{\mathrm E}$ and $\mathbb D_p$ are the same slot filled two ways.

An element in the slot is allowed, and the corpus writes it in parentheses. $\mathbb D_{w}$ with $w$ a slot of the pair gives the exchange weighted by $w$, a degree-zero operator that squares to $\langle w\rangle_{\natural}I$, residual $1\times10^{-15}$; and $\mathbb D(\tilde K)$ of §The Object Behind the Symbol is the same construction with the factor written out. So the corpus has two spellings of one slot: a subscript for a factor whose name is a label, $p$ or $\mathrm E$, and parentheses for a factor that is an element, $\tilde K$ or $w$. The reason is not taste. A reader has also seen $\mathbb D\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}$, where the glyph carries no subscript and the letter on its right is the field acted on. $\mathbb D_w$ read aloud is either of the two, and after a rename spent on exactly that kind of ambiguity the corpus should not buy it back: an element belongs in the parentheses, and the subscript stays for the labels.

What a subscript cannot name is a product. Two factors compose to a block-diagonal array,

$$ \mathbb D_a\,\mathbb D_b=\begin{pmatrix}L(a b^{\natural})&0\\0&L(a^{\natural}b)\end{pmatrix}, $$

residual $2.5\times10^{-15}$, which is orthogonal to every $\mathbb D_\ell$ because those are off-diagonal: the projection of the product on the family is zero. So the products leave the family, the subscript names a degree-one factor only, and only the square returns, $\mathbb D_a^{2}=\langle a\rangle_{\natural}I$. The assignment $\ell\mapsto\mathbb D_\ell$ is linear and injective — rank four, so $\mathbb D_\ell$ determines $\ell$ — but it is not multiplicative, and there is no reading in which one composes two of these by multiplying in the subscript.

One subscript is already taken, and it is a block index. The Functional Determinant in Biquaternionic Form writes $\mathbb D_L$ and $\mathbb D_R$ for the two chiral blocks of the pair operator — scalars $0.9-0.2i$ and $0.7+0.4i$ in its own numerical check — three uses each. Those are not factors of the swap; they are the two off-diagonal blocks, read one at a time. An upright uppercase letter in the subscript is a block, an italic letter of the algebra is a factor, and the general subscript is safe only while that separation holds.

The rule is now in the conventions, and the subscript is not written everywhere. The count over the two corpora is $414$ occurrences of the glyph: $84$ subscripted, $27$ in parentheses, and $303$ bare. It would be mechanical to put $\tilde\nabla$ in all $303$, and it would be wrong in three ways. Sixty-two of the bare occurrences name the object rather than use it — "with $\mathbb{D}$ the pair operator of Operator and Calculus Conventions" — and a subscript there would be a category error. Twenty-two of the twenty-seven articles carry one factor only, so the subscript would resolve nothing. And the corpus already answers this question for the gradient: $\tilde\nabla$ is written bare and takes $\mathrm E$, $\natural$ or a sector label only where a second one is in play. What has been implemented instead is the statement, once, of what the bare glyph is: Operator and Calculus Conventions now names plain $\mathbb{D}$ as $\mathbb{D}_{\tilde\nabla}$ and gives the four spellings — $\mathbb{D}_p$, $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm E}$, $\mathbb{D}(\tilde K)$, $\mathbb{D}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}$ — and the two notation tables that carry two or three factors at once (The Fermionic Path Integral, The Quantized Dirac Field) now say in the row itself that the plain glyph is the material-gradient factor. Ten lines, and the default is no longer implicit.

The summary of the slot. A momentum covector, a gradient, a unit, an element, a field: all are factors, all give an operator of the same shape, and the difference between the symbol and the operator is which factor was written. A block is not a factor, and the field acted on is not a factor either — it is the argument.

The Sizes of the Arrays: One Worked Example

Three arrays, three sizes, three carriers. The corpus writes an element in a $2\times2$ array, an operator on the algebra in a $4\times4$ array, and the pair in an $8\times8$ array. The three sizes are not three conventions for one object. They are three carriers, and each size answers a question the others cannot. This section takes one element and computes all three, so that the sizes are visible and every number can be checked.

The element. Take $\tilde K=2e_t-i\vec e_x$, that is $k=(2,1,0,0)$ — the wave covector at which The First-Order Root of the Biquaternion Klein–Gordon Operator quotes its three exact values: the norm $\tilde K^{\natural}\tilde K=3$, the determinant $\det\ell_{\tilde K}=9$, and $\det\bigl(\mathbb D(\tilde K)-\mu\bigr)=16$ at $\mu=1$. All three are recomputed below, together with the sizes they come from. The coefficients are $Q_t=2$ and $Q_x=-i$, with $Q_y=Q_z=0$, so $\langle\tilde K\rangle_\natural=Q_t^2+Q_x^2=4-1=3$.

$2\times2$: the element itself. Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Isomorphism, reads the element as $\mathsf M_2(\tilde Q)$ with entries $Q_t-iQ_z$, $-iQ_x-Q_y$, $-iQ_x+Q_y$ and $Q_t+iQ_z$, which here is

$$ \mathsf M_2(\tilde K)=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}, $$

of determinant $3$, trace $4$, and eigenvalues $3$ and $1$. The determinant is the norm, $\det\mathsf M_2(\tilde Q)=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural$, so this size answers one question: is the element invertible. A vanishing determinant is a zero divisor, and that is the whole of the zero-divisor calculus. Every numerical check in the corpus is performed at this size, because it is the only size at which the array is the element.

$4\times4$: left multiplication, the operator on the algebra. Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Left Family, reads the same element as an operator on the four-dimensional space $\mathbb{B}$,

$$ L^{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde K)=\begin{pmatrix}2&i&0&0\\-i&2&0&0\\0&0&2&i\\0&0&-i&2\end{pmatrix}, $$

of determinant $9=\langle\tilde K\rangle_\natural^2$, trace $8=4Q_t$, and eigenvalues $3,3,1,1$. The spectrum is the spectrum of $\mathsf M_2$ with every value repeated twice. The repetition is the size: left multiplication is the element read on the algebra, and the algebra is the minimal left ideal taken twice — two copies of the spinor module $S$, a copy index and not a chirality, as Algebra and Element Conventions says — so each eigenvalue occurs twice and the determinant squares. This size answers what the $2\times2$ cannot: how much of $\mathbb{B}$ the element annihilates. At $\tilde K=e_t-i\vec e_x$ the norm vanishes, the determinant vanishes with it, and the rank drops from $4$ to $2$; that is the root article's "rank two" on the cone, in the language of the array.

$4\times4$ again, and a different operator. The one-element form of the pair has the same size and is a different operator. This article writes it $\mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}$ from here, a working label and not a corpus glyph, so that the two arrays can be named in one sentence:

$$ \mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}(\tilde K)=iE\,R^{\mathrm{axf}}_{\vec e_x}-i\sum_kp_k\,L^{\mathrm{axf}}_{\vec e_k}R^{\mathrm{axf}}_{\vec e_y}, $$

with $E=p_0$ and $R^{\mathrm{axf}}$ the right family of §The Right Family. At this element it reads

$$ \mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}(\tilde K)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-2i&0&-i\\2i&0&i&0\\0&i&0&2i\\-i&0&-2i&0\end{pmatrix}, $$

with ${\mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}}^2=3I_4$, trace $0$, determinant $9$, and $\det\bigl(\mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}-I_4\bigr)=4$. The determinant is the same as that of $L^{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde K)$ and the trace is not. That is the trap of this size: size and determinant together do not identify the operator, because both arrays have $\det=\langle\tilde K\rangle_\natural^2$ at the symbol. The trace separates them, $8$ against $0$, and so does the eigenvalue set, $\{3,3,1,1\}$ against $\{\sqrt3,\sqrt3,-\sqrt3,-\sqrt3\}$.

$8\times8$: the pair. With $\tilde K^\natural=2e_t+i\vec e_x$, the block form of §The Definition and the Carrier is

$$ \mathbb D(\tilde K)=\begin{pmatrix}0&L^{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde K)\\ L^{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde K^\natural)&0\end{pmatrix}, $$

of trace $0$, determinant $81=3^4$, and square $3I_8$; its eigenvalues are $\pm\sqrt3$, each four times. Then $\det\bigl(\mathbb D(\tilde K)-\mu\bigr)=(\mu^2-3)^4$, which is $16$ at $\mu=1$, the value the root article prints. The trace vanishes because the array is off-diagonal by construction, and that vanishing is the reason the eigenvalues come in opposite pairs and the determinant is a perfect fourth power instead of a general polynomial.

The determinants are the first, second and fourth powers of one number. They are $3$, $3^2$ and $3^4$, and the sizes are $2$, $4$ and $8$. Each doubling of the carrier repeats the whole spectrum, so each determinant is the square of the one before. At $\mu=1$ the one-element form gives $4$ and the pair gives $16$, and what separates the two numbers is the exponent, $2$ against $4$; the numerical ratio depends on $\mu$ and on $\kappa$ and means nothing by itself.

The rank on the mass shell is half the size, at every size. At $\tilde K=e_t-i\vec e_x$ the ranks are $1$, $2$ and $4$, and the kernels are one, two and four complex dimensions. The fraction is one half in all three rows: the first-order equation keeps half of what the second-order equation keeps, at $2$ as at $8$. The pair keeps four dimensions where one element keeps two, so the doubling of the carrier doubles the room for the field and leaves the fraction alone.

What each size is for. The $2\times2$ is for the norm, the inverse and the zero divisors, and it is the display every check uses. The $4\times4$ is for the operator algebra: the sixteen cells $L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$ span the ambient $M_4(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, the one-element operator is a combination of them and is not in the left copy $\mathbb{B}_L$, and the rank of an array of this size is the only place where "how much of the element survives" is read. The $8\times8$ is for the pair, and it is the size at which the determinant of the field operator, the Green matrix and the index are written.

One caution on the use. The $8\times8$ array is the symbol, one momentum at a time. The determinant of The Functional Determinant in Biquaternionic Form is the product of these arrays over the momenta, so the finite size is the size of the symbol and not of the operator whose determinant is taken. The fourth power counts the carrier at each momentum; the product over the momenta is the functional determinant and carries no fourth power of its own. The inverse is read off the block form, $(\mathbb{D}-m)^{-1}=(\mathbb{D}+m)(\Box-m^2)^{-1}$, whose denominator is a scalar: the determinant vanishes to order four on the mass shell and the propagator still has a simple pole, because the order of a zero of a determinant and the order of a pole of an inverse are two different counts.

The array of elements and the array of scalars are one array. The mathematics article prints the pair with element entries, $\begin{pmatrix}0&\tilde K\\ \tilde K^{\natural}&0\end{pmatrix}$. By §General Matrix Conventions an array of biquaternions carries no tilde and its entries do, and each element entry stands for its left regular matrix; read that way the $2\times2$ array of elements is the $8\times8$ array of scalars, written in four blocks. The size is the same in both writings and the entries are not.

Summary

The glyph $\mathbb{D}$ names two operators with one square. Type A acts on $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$ and squares to $\Box I$; Type B acts on $\mathbb{B}$ and squares to $\Box$. Both are first order, both exchange the chiral values — the first by the slot of the pair, the second by the chirality cell — and both have the momentum symbol whose square is $p_0^2-\lvert\vec p\rvert^2$. The squares agree because the time term carries one $i$ and only one in each form: the central scalar through the $ict'$ coordinate in Type A, the frame cell $\vec e_x$ in Type B.

The count is 27 articles: 25 Type A, 5 Type B, with 3 articles printing both. One article defines the operator, four state the passage between the carriers, ten print the square with the identity.

The square is spelled three ways, and the three denote one operator with three types. $\Box I_8$ is an identity on the pair; $\Box$ is a scalar; $\Box e_t$ is an element, and it acts as $\Box I_4$ because $\mathsf{M}_{4L}(e_t)=I_4$. The value agrees in all three and the type does not.

The glyph names an object, and every entry of the inventory is one value of it. With one factor $\ell$ used in both slots of a left-right pair and the slots exchanged, $\mathbb D_\ell(w_L,w_R)=(\ell w_R,\ell^{\natural}w_L)$, and $\ell$ may be an element or the gradient. The symbol is the case of a constant factor; the differential operator is the case of the gradient; the square in both cases is $\ell\ell^{\natural}$ times the identity of the pair, the identity coming from the two slots rather than from the factor. That is why the same $\Box$ appears attached to $I$ on the pair and bare on one element.

The one-element form does not have the second slot, and it buys one: right multiplication is a conjugated left multiplication, $R_{\vec e}=-CL_{\vec e}C$ on the vector cells, so the one-element operator carries two factors per term with the algebra's conjugation standing where the slot stood. The pair is two copies of the element carrier — the commutant of the four unit factors is one complex dimension on the element and four, that is $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, on the pair — and the swap is what couples the two copies. The carrier is still the only thing that separates the operators, and it is still not visible in the notation.

The subscript is the factor, and the corpus already fills it two ways: $p$ for the constant covector, so the symbol, and $\mathrm E$ for the Euclidean gradient, so the operator, in The Fermionic Path Integral. Plain $\mathbb D$ is the factor at the material gradient, which is why that subscript is dropped. An element belongs in parentheses, $\mathbb D(\tilde K)$, because a subscript on this glyph already means a factor and $\mathbb D\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}$ already means the field acted on; a subscript cannot name a product, since $\mathbb D_a\mathbb D_b$ is block-diagonal and orthogonal to every $\mathbb D_\ell$; and $\mathbb D_L$, $\mathbb D_R$ in The Functional Determinant are blocks and not factors.

The sizes carry the same story as the glyphs. The element is a $2\times2$ array whose determinant is the norm, left multiplication is a $4\times4$ array whose determinant is the norm squared, and the pair is an $8\times8$ array whose determinant is the norm to the fourth. Each doubling of the carrier repeats the spectrum and squares the determinant, and the rank on the mass shell is half the size: $1$, $2$, $4$ for $2$, $4$, $8$. The exponent of the determinant is half the carrier's dimension and not the dimension, and it is the fingerprint by which a reader tells which carrier an article's determinant belongs to.

The collision with the unit disc of the mathematics corpus is repaired: the disc is $\mathbb{U}$ in the sixteen mathematics articles that used to write $\mathbb{D}$, and the glyph is the operator's alone. The older internal note that calls the pair $\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{spl}}$ is out of date.

$\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}=(\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L,\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R)$ the pair, an element of $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$; the two subscripts are the chiral values
$\mathbb{D}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}=\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_R,\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{spn}}_L\bigr)$ the definition of Type A; the two members exchange the values
$\mathbb{D}(\tilde K)$ the block array $\begin{pmatrix}0&\ell_{\tilde K}\\ \ell_{\tilde K^{\natural}}&0\end{pmatrix}$, with $\ell_M=L^{\mathrm{axf}}(M)$ and the derivative replaced by $\tilde K$; $8\times8$
$\mathbb{D}_\ell$ the object behind every entry, $\mathbb D_\ell(w_L,w_R)=(\ell w_R,\ell^{\natural}w_L)$ on the pair, with one factor $\ell$; $\ell$ is an element of the algebra or the gradient; $\mathbb D_\ell^2=\ell\ell^{\natural}I$
$\mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}$ this article's working label for the one-element form, not a corpus glyph; $\mathbb D^{\mathrm{el}}(\tilde K)=iE R^{\mathrm{axf}}_{\vec e_x}-i\sum_kp_kL^{\mathrm{axf}}_{\vec e_k}R^{\mathrm{axf}}_{\vec e_y}$, a $4\times4$ array
$\mathsf M_2(\tilde Q)$, $L^{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde Q)$ the element as a $2\times2$ array, and the same element as left multiplication on $\mathbb{B}$, a $4\times4$ array; $\det\mathsf M_2=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural$ and $\det L^{\mathrm{axf}}=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural^2$
$I_4$, $I_8$ the identities of $\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{C}^4$ and of $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{C}^8$
$\mathbb{D}_p$ the momentum symbol; each derivative replaced by its plane-wave value; $\mathbb{D}_p^2=p_0^2-\lvert\vec p\rvert^2$
$\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm E}$ the same glyph with the Euclidean gradient as the factor, $\mathbb D_{\mathrm E}^2=\Box_{\mathrm E}I$; the subscript is a factor and not a momentum, The Fermionic Path Integral
$\mathbb D_L$, $\mathbb D_R$ the two chiral blocks of the pair operator, read one at a time in The Functional Determinant; a block index, not a factor
$\mathbb{U}$ the unit disc of $\mathbb{C}$ in the mathematics corpus, and the objects named after it, $K_{\mathbb{U}}$, $g_{\mathbb{U}}$, $H^p(\mathbb{U})$, $\mathbb{U}^{\mathrm{ad}}$, $\mathbb{U}^1$; a different object from $\mathbb{D}$, and the reason the collision is closed
$\Box$ the d'Alembertian, $\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla$
$\Box I$ the scalar $\Box$ times the identity on $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$; the exact square of Type A
$\Box e_t$ the same multiple of the algebra unit; an element, read as the operator $\Box I_4$ through $\mathsf{M}_{4L}(e_t)=I_4$; the spelling of The Spectral Triple
$\nu(\mu)$ the frame index of the right multiplier in the Type B form: $\vec e_x$ for $\mu=t$ and $\vec e_y$ for the three spatial values
$\chi$ the chirality element, $\chi\mathbb{D}\chi^{-1}=-\mathbb{D}$ in The Chiral Condensate
$C$ the conjugation of the algebra, $C\tilde X=\tilde X^{\natural}$, the matrix $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$; it supplies the second slot for the one-element form, since $R_{\vec e}=-CL_{\vec e}C$ on the vector cells
$\mathcal{S}$, $S_F$, $G$ the Green matrix, the Feynman propagator and the scalar Green function, $\mathcal{S}=(\mathbb{D}+m)G$
$\mathcal{D}=Q+Q^{\dagger}$ the Dirac operator of the supersymmetry article, pointing at the companion articles' $\mathbb{D}$

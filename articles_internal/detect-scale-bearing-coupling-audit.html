The Scale-Bearing Couplings of the Published Corpus: One Permitted Home, Twenty-Four Flagged Articles, Five Anomaly Types

Internal note, working document. It records the result of a scan of the $672$ published physics articles for scale-bearing couplings, names every article that carries one, and assigns each a treatment. It is a worklist, not a ruling on the physics, and it prints no strength value of its own.

Introduction

The result in one table.

count
published physics articles scanned $672$
articles that carry a scale-bearing coupling $25$
permitted $1$
flagged as problematic $24$
articles that merely point at the subject $3$
articles that state a $\beta$ function symbolically and are therefore clean $10$

The ruling applied here is the strict one. One article may carry a scale-bearing coupling, and it is the one that sits in the category built for it: The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model. Every other article that carries one is flagged, whatever its motive, including the articles whose printed strengths are measurements.

That last clause is a change from the rule now in force, and the change should be visible rather than quiet. The published boundary page, The Data Boundary, permits sixteen articles to print measured strengths as data, and a second article to state a claim. Under the ruling applied here, "measured" is a mitigation and not a permission: the flag stays, and what changes is the treatment.

The finding that makes the ruling cheap to apply. Read against the table, the corpus turns out to have almost no coupling claims of its own outside the permitted article. Twenty of the twenty-four are printed values, and eighteen of those twenty are either a measurement or arithmetic on a measurement. Two are values the article's own theory produces, and neither is a claim about this model. So the work is bookkeeping, not retraction.

1. What a Scale-Bearing Coupling Is

The test is one question: can the sentence drop the scale?

A value, a boundary condition at a scale, a running, a matching, and a ratio called exact are one object seen five ways. None can be written without naming an energy. That is what unites them, and it is why the algebra cannot adjudicate them.

  • A value needs a scale. $\alpha_s$ alone means nothing; $\alpha_s(M_Z)$ is a number.
  • A boundary condition is a scale statement. "$g_4=g_L$ at $M_{PS}$" carries the scale inside the sentence.
  • A running is two scales and the path between them.
  • A matching is two descriptions at one scale.
  • A ratio is exact only because two couplings were set equal at one scale.

The technical synonym is "renormalised coupling", and it is the worse name. A renormalisation condition is a boundary condition at a scale, so the phrase names the apparatus rather than the defect. Scale-bearing names the defect.

category test where it may live
structural coupling can drop the scale: a symbol multiplying a generator, a trace normalisation everywhere, untouched
scale-bearing coupling cannot drop the scale the permitted article only
measured coupling external data may stay where it is used, marked as data

Why the middle row is the dangerous one. A structural coupling is decided by the algebra, so an error in it is caught by a trace identity. A scale-bearing coupling is decided by nothing in the algebra — it is imposed or measured — so an error in it is invisible until an experiment, and it moves when the scale moves. That is why they are quarantined and not merely labelled.

The distinction that decides most of the cases below, and it is not the presence of a scale. A symbol with a scale argument is structural: $\lambda(\mu)$ is the notation of the renormalisation group, it carries no value, and it survives the deletion test. What is scale-bearing is a number, or an equation whose two sides are fixed at one scale. A $\beta$ function written with $b_0$ as a combination of Casimirs and $N_f$ is structural; the same $\beta$ function evaluated at a measured coupling is not. Ten articles in the corpus sit on the structural side of that line, and they are listed in §7 as clean.

2. The One Permitted Article

article what it carries why it is permitted
The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model all five forms: the matching at the first threshold, the boundary condition of the equal-coupling case, the running that fails it, the exact ratio, and the measured strengths reproduced it is the flagged article; the category exists for it

Nothing in this note proposes a second home. The permitted article is also the only place where the corpus's own coupling statement is made, and its status block says so.

3. How the List Was Made, and What the Method Cannot See

Six mechanical signatures were run over the folder, then every hit was read. The signatures were: a scale inside a coupling's argument; a scale in a predicate; an explicit energy unit; running language; an equality between coupling symbols; and exactness claimed for a ratio.

The signatures over-fire, and the reading is what produces the list. The raw scan returned $74$ files. Reading removed $45$ of them, and §8 names the kinds of false positive that caused most of that. A list produced by grep alone would be more than half wrong, and the article that a reader should trust is the read list, not the raw one.

The method cannot see three things, and they are worth stating because each one hides a real anomaly.

  • A hidden premise. $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ contains no scale. It is scale-bearing because it is true only where two couplings are equal, and that clause is often in the previous sentence.
  • A ratio that moves with the scale. $M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)M_{W_R}$ names no scale, but $g_4/g_R$ is fixed at the matching, so the coefficient is a scale-bearing number wearing a symbol.
  • A scale written in words. "at low energies", "in the infrared", "above the unification scale". No symbol and no unit.

So the counts below are a lower bound on the number of articles that contain a scale-bearing statement, and an exact count of those that write one down. That is the honest form of the claim, and it is the form the treatments in §6 assume.

4. Type 1 — A Printed Value: Twenty Articles

This is the bulk of the problem, and it is also the easiest to treat, because a printed value is either a measurement or arithmetic on one. The column "class" is assigned after reading: $M$ for a measured strength, $D$ for a value derived from measured strengths, $C$ for a value the article's own theory produces.

The two $C$ rows are the only real claims in the type, and neither claims anything about this model. The fixed point is a two-loop property of a gauge theory with a given fermion count; the chosen strong strength is an input to a consistency check. So the corpus's coupling assertions are, in fact, confined to the permitted article already, and the flag on the other twenty-two is a flag on data handling, not on doctrine.

The $C$ rows still need fixing, because a reader cannot tell $C$ from $M$ on the page. A number produced by the article's own machinery reads as a prediction, and a prediction outside the permitted article is the thing the rule forbids. The repair is one sentence of attribution or the deletion of the number, and the second is cheaper.

5. The Article That the Old Boundary Page Permitted

The Weinberg-angle article is the one place where this note and the published boundary page disagree, and the disagreement should be recorded rather than smoothed.

The boundary page lists two claim articles: the permitted one, and The Weinberg Angle and the Three Neutral States, on the grounds that it states "the mixing as a ratio of two strengths, the form the generators fix, and the rotation that makes the photon pure charge". The strict ruling does not admit a second claim article, for one reason: the mixing angle is a ratio of couplings, so a statement about it is a scale-bearing statement, and the category has one seat.

What the article actually contains decides it. Its denominators are the measured angle, the fine-structure value at the pole, and a table of widths compared with measurements. That is a data article about a ratio, not a structural derivation of one, and it belongs with the nineteen other value-bearing articles rather than beside the permitted one.

The repair is therefore not a rewrite. The article keeps its content and gains a status line: the angle is measured, the ratio of two strengths, and the model's one statement about it is in the permitted article. What it loses is the title's implicit claim — the $\tfrac38$ of its subtitle is conditional, and §T5 below says why a conditional $\tfrac38$ in a title is a scale-bearing statement.

6. Types 2 to 5, and the Treatments They Need

Four types remain, and each is small. Each table gives the article, the sentence that is the anomaly, and the repair.

Type 2 — a boundary condition: two articles.

article the sentence repair
The Four Colours Left–Right Model the equal-coupling condition, named with its scale, in the section that sends it away keep the classification sentence, drop the equation
The Breaking Chain and the Boson Spectrum §8's restatement of the same condition, "at one scale", and the $\tfrac38$ sentence of §"The Coupling Statements" keep the pointer, drop the equation and the number

The anomaly in both is one sentence too many. Each article does the right thing — it says the coupling statement lives elsewhere — and then prints the equation anyway, "for clarity". A pointer that prints the equation is not a pointer, and it is the commonest failure mode in the corpus's own repair pattern.

Type 3 — a running written with numbers: six articles, all inside type 1. The six are the running article, the Landau-pole article, the hadron-structure article, the parton article, the jets article, and the Yukawa article. No separate repair is needed: the treatment of a numerical trajectory is the treatment of a measured value, marked with its scheme and its endpoints.

The discrimination that keeps type 3 from swallowing the corpus. Sixteen articles mention a $\beta$ function and print no number for it. Ten of those print the coefficient as a combination of Casimirs, which is a count of states, and are clean; the rest point at another article. A $\beta$ function is structural. A trajectory is data. A coupling at one end of the trajectory is a value. The three are different sentences and only the last two are flagged.

Type 4 — a matching evaluated with a measured strength: two articles.

article the sentence repair
Lepton Flavour in the Four-Colours Model the matching relation used to replace the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ strength by the measured strong one, so that a bound becomes a number keep the bound; cite the permitted article for the replacement
The Scalar Sector and the Leptoquark Bounds the same replacement, and the mass ratio whose coefficient is $g_4/g_R$ keep the bound; carry the ratio as a parameter

This is the closest call in the corpus, and the reason is worth stating. The matching relation itself is structural — two couplings equal at a threshold is a group statement, and the permitted article derives it. What makes these two sentences scale-bearing is that they substitute a measured strength into the relation and then print the resulting mass. The substitution is what a bound needs. So the flag is real and the fix is minimal: one citation, no deletion.

Type 5 — an exact ratio: the same two articles as type 2. The $\tfrac38$ sentence appears in the Four Colours article and in the breaking-chain article, each time as a classification rather than a claim, and each time with the number printed.

repair why
keep "$\tfrac38$ is exact only where the two strengths are equal", drop the numeral and the measured comparison the conditional is the content; the numeral is the claim

Type 5 is the only type where the number can be removed without losing anything, because the sentence's content is the condition. That is why the subtype is worth separating: it has a free repair, and the other four do not.

7. The Clean Articles, and Why They Are Clean

Three articles point at the subject and print nothing.

  • Grand Unification in the Biquaternion Framework — a further-reading line to the running article.
  • The Nonlinear Sigma Model in Biquaternionic Form — "the $\beta$ function is that of the companion article".
  • The Standard Model in the Biquaternion Framework — points at the one-loop coefficient and at the article that derives it.

Ten articles state a $\beta$ function symbolically and stop there. The gluon article, the QCD dimension-ceiling article, the confinement article, the lattice article, the Yang–Mills path-integral article, the semiclassical-expansion article, the Euclidean dilute-gas article, the classical non-abelian article, the effective-potential article, and the instanton-gas article. Every one of them writes $\beta_g=-g^3b_0/16\pi^2$ with $b_0$ a combination of $C_2$ and $N_f$. That is a count of states and diagrams, it survives the deletion test, and it is the corpus's best structural work on the subject. They are the model for what the flagged articles should look like.

One article is the rule itself. The Data Boundary prints no strength and is the register that this note annotates. It is neither permitted nor flagged; it is the instrument.

8. The False Positives, So That the Next Scan Is Cheaper

Seven lookalikes produced most of the forty-five discarded hits. Each has a decidable test.

lookalike example the test that clears it
a group element called $g$ $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V=\{g_L=g_R\}$ in six articles does the symbol multiply a generator, or is it multiplied by one
a scale on a mass "$M_C\gtrsim$ a floor", "the boson at its pole" does the scale attach to a coupling or to a particle
"running" as a verb flows, condensates, indices is the subject a coupling
a logarithm in thermodynamics entropy and free energy is the argument an energy ratio
$\beta$ as a velocity or an angle $v/c$, the CKM angle is there a derivative with respect to $\ln\mu$
"coupling" for a structure self-coupling, minimal coupling, anomalous coupling is the word naming a strength or a vertex
a parenthesised index $N_{\mathrm{cont},\lambda({\imath}{\jmath})}$, $M_k(\mathbb{C})$ is the bracket an argument or an index

The last row is a regex artefact and it cost three files. A signature that matches a bracket after a symbol will match a tensor index, and the fix is a required scale token inside the bracket. The general lesson: a signature proposes, a reader disposes.

Energy units alone are not evidence. Fifty-seven articles print an energy unit and no coupling. The hierarchy article and the "what the Standard Model does not explain" article print dozens of them and no strength at all. A number in GeV is a scale; the question is whether a coupling is attached to it.

9. The Treatment Plan, in Order of Work

Four passes, cheapest first. The order is chosen so that each pass makes the next one smaller.

  1. Type 5, then type 2 — four sentences, two of them free. Drop the numerals, keep the condition and the pointer. No article changes its argument.
  2. Type 4 — two citations. Add the reference to the permitted article at the point where the measured strength is substituted. No number moves.
  3. Type 1, class $C$ — two numbers. Delete the fixed-point value and the chosen strong strength, or attribute them in one clause. These are the only sentences a reader could mistake for a prediction.
  4. Type 1, classes $M$ and $D$ — eighteen articles, the long pass. No deletions. Each article gains a status line naming what is measured, in which scheme, at which scale, and from which source; the data register in the boundary page is extended to cover the six articles it does not yet list.

What the plan deliberately does not do. It does not remove measured strengths from the articles whose subject is the measurement, because removing them would gut the article and hide the corpus's evidence. It does not create a second permitted article. And it does not rewrite any argument: in all four passes the physics stays and only the status of the numbers changes.

The measure of success. After the four passes, a reader who asks "what does this corpus claim about the strength of a coupling?" has one address, and a reader who asks "what does it know about the measured strengths?" has a register. At present the first question has one address and no guard rail, and the second has neither.

10. Purging the Twenty-Four Without Losing the Argument

The question this section answers is two questions, and they have different answers. Is there a method? Yes — five operations, each keyed to a type, and the discussion lane already uses the strongest of them. Do the articles keep their meaning? Twenty-two do, without qualification; two do, in a reduced form; none is emptied. The reduced cases are named below rather than averaged away.

10.1 The Two Lanes, Which Are Already Built

Nothing removed has to be invented, because the corpus already has one address for a statement and one for a discussion, and the second is the article you are thinking of.

lane address what it holds
statement The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model the equation, the number, the failure
discussion articles_internal/the-coupling-sector-of-the-four-colours-model.md §4 the fitted strength, §5 the equality that cannot be restored, §6 the reality condition, §7 Pati–Salam and what it cost, §8 why the patches do not repair, §11 the ledger

The discussion lane already holds every problem a purge would otherwise lose. It has the Pati–Salam case with its measured mass relations and their verdicts, the three open problems in order, the inventory of what the patches cost, and the ledger that separates what is derived from what is fitted. So a purged article does not need to keep its own argument — it needs to cite the one that has it.

10.2 The Method Is Already in the Corpus, Once

§7 of the discussion lane is the worked example, and it is worth reading as a template rather than as content. The mass relations arrive from the literature as a scale-bearing statement: two masses equal at a scale. The note converts them into a counting statement — one bidoublet puts down-type quarks and charged leptons in one Yukawa term, so one matrix serves two sectors, so the spectra are proportional — and sends the numerical comparison to the flagged article §6.

The conversion is what makes it work. "One matrix for two sectors" has no scale in it, survives every rescaling, and implies the same physical content as the equality it replaced. That single move is the strongest of the five operations, and it was executed once, correctly, in §7. The rest of this section generalises it.

10.3 The Five Operations

operation what it does where it applies
O1 — mark keep the number; print its status: measured, scheme, scale, source the ten measured articles
O2 — symbolise replace a numeric table by the symbolic relation plus one marked endpoint the six trajectories, and the eight derived articles where a table is the body
O3 — count replace a coupling equality by the counting statement it implies any equality whose content is a count; the template is §7 of the discussion lane
O4 — point keep the classification sentence, delete the equation and the numeral, cite the statement lane the two boundary conditions, the two exact ratios
O5 — delete remove the number; nothing depends on it the two computed values

The assignment over the twenty-four closes exactly.

operation articles which
O1 $10$ the measured articles of §4
O2, with O1 $8$ the derived articles; the six trajectories are the ones where a table is the argument
O4 $2$ the boundary conditions of §6
O1 with O4 $2$ the matchings based on a measured strength, where the substitution is cited and the bound is kept
O5 $2$ the two computed values
total $24$ no article needs two operations beyond O1, and none needs none

O3 is not in the assignment, and that is a finding. It applies to no article in this list, because the corpus's scale-bearing sentences are values and conditions rather than equalities with a counting content. It remains the operation to reach for first when a new case appears, and §7 shows it works.

10.4 Does the Meaning Survive? A Test, Then Five Answers

The test, in one question: with the number replaced by a symbol and one marked data point, does the article still make the point it existed to make?

class articles does it survive why
measured values $10$ fully the meaning of a data article is the measurement; the number stays and only its status is printed
derived values $8$ fully the derivation is arithmetic the reader can redo; the inputs are what carry the status
computed values $2$ fully the mechanism is the point; neither number is used again
boundary conditions $2$ fully the content is "this case departs, and here is why"; the equation is decoration
exact ratios $2$ fully, and for free the content is the condition under which the ratio is exact
matchings on a measured strength $2$ in reduced form the bound needs the substituted strength; the citation replaces the derivation, not the number

So the honest answer is: twenty-two keep their meaning without qualification, two keep it in a reduced form, and none is emptied. The reduction in the last row is real and should be stated in the repaired article: a reader there learns the bound and is sent for its derivation, instead of watching the derivation happen.

10.5 The Two Articles That Lose the Most, Named

A method that claims no cost is a method nobody trusts. Two articles are built out of numbers rather than decorated with them: Asymptotic Freedom and the Running of the Strong Force and The Landau Pole, Triviality and Vacuum Stability. In both, the coupling at each of many scales is the body of the article.

What survives the purge in each, stated in one sentence. The running article survives as one coefficient that is a count of states, one line that is exact, and one marked measurement — which is its own subtitle, and is what it says about itself already. The Landau article survives as three coefficients that are computable, and a meeting that is a measured fact rather than a theorem — which is its own conclusion.

What does not survive is the density of the numbers. The purge keeps the argument and thins the evidence, and for these two that is a real loss. The mitigation is O2 done properly: one marked endpoint carries the status for the whole trajectory, so a reader who wants the curve can follow the reference and a reader who wants the point is not made to audit a table.

10.6 What Cannot Be Purged

The hidden premise, which is not a number and therefore not removable by any of the five operations. $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ is scale-bearing because of a clause it does not print. Deleting the numeral leaves "the angle is conditional", which is not yet a statement; the condition has to be written out, and writing it out is a rewrite rather than a purge.

So the method has an edge, and the edge is: an operation removes a value, not a silence. Where the defect is an unstated condition, the repair is one added clause naming the scale at which the condition holds — and that clause then makes the sentence look like O4's output, which is why the two are easy to confuse in a plan and must not be confused in practice.

The other edge is a ratio that moves. $M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)M_{W_R}$ contains no numeral and no scale word, so no operation removes anything; what it needs is one sentence saying that the coefficient is evaluated at the matching, which is a status line and therefore O1.

10.7 The Acceptance Test for a Purged Article

Four conditions, and an article is done when all four hold.

  1. No numeral is attached to a coupling, except in the statement lane.
  2. Every numeral that remains carries a status: measured, scheme, scale, source — or the statement that scheme and scale do not apply.
  3. The article's own claim is still readable without the removed number; if it is not, the operation chosen was too strong and O3 should be tried.
  4. One link to each lane that the article needs: the statement lane for a value, the discussion lane for a problem.

The last condition is the one that keeps the purge from looking like a deletion campaign. A reader who lands on a purged article is sent somewhere with the missing content, not left with a hole.

Summary

One article may carry a scale-bearing coupling, and it is the one in the category built for it. Twenty-four others carry one and are flagged: twenty print a value, two print a boundary condition, two evaluate a matching with a measured strength, and the last two of those also print an exact ratio.

The test is one question: can the sentence drop the scale? A symbol with a scale argument can, and is structural. A number cannot, and is either measured or imposed. An equation with $=$ at one scale cannot, and is a boundary condition.

The flags are mostly about data handling, not doctrine. Eighteen of the twenty value-bearing articles are measurements or arithmetic on measurements; the two that are not are a two-loop fixed point and a chosen strength in a consistency check, and neither asserts anything about this model. The corpus's coupling claims are, in fact, already confined to the permitted article.

The repairs are four passes, and five techniques. Delete two numerals. Add two citations. Delete two computed numbers. Then mark eighteen articles as data — with the two heaviest of them symbolised rather than tabulated. The techniques are mark, symbolise, count, point and delete, and they sum to the twenty-four exactly. No argument changes in any of them.

The meaning survives, with two stated exceptions. Twenty-two articles keep their point without qualification. The two that evaluate a matching on a measured strength keep it in reduced form: the bound stays and the derivation is cited. The two that lose most are the running article and the Landau article, where numbers are the body — and each survives as its own subtitle, thinner in evidence but not in argument.

The clean articles are the model. Ten articles write a $\beta$ function as a count of Casimirs and stop, and three point without printing. That is what the flagged articles should look like when the passes are done.

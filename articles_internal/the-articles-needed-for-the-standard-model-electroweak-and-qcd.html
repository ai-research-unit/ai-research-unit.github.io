The Articles Needed for the Standard Model, Electroweak Theory and QCD

Scope

This file is an internal working list, not a published article and not an entry of a menu. It names the articles that must be written for the corpus to hold the Standard Model — its electroweak sector and its strong sector — in detail, in the corpus's own language and notation.

The subject is set by three existing articles, which are boundary maps and not expositions: The Standard Model under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda, Electroweak Theory under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda, and Quantum Chromodynamics under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda. Each states what the framework reaches and what it does not. None of the three carries the material at working length: the gauge group written out, the fermion table with its hypercharge column, the four pieces of the Lagrangian, the scalar doublet, the Yukawa matrices, the QCD vertices, the parton model. This list is the plan for that missing material.

The reference used to build the checklist is the extracted text of a twenty-page capture of the Wikipedia article "Standard Model", added to the project as Standard_Model.pdf: its particle table, its group, its four Lagrangians, its table of nineteen parameters, and its list of open problems. The page is a working checklist and not a citation; a stable review replaces it in any Further Reading.

Nineteen articles are proposed. They are staged, because the later stages quote the earlier ones.

The Standing Rule

Every article on this list transcribes standard theory. None derives it. Each therefore opens with a sentence that fixes its standing, in the manner already used by SU(3) Representations and the Gell-Mann–Okubo Mass Formula in Biquaternionic Form, which states at the head that its $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ "is not an algebra of $\mathbb{B}$" but external machinery.

The model sentence, adapted per article:

The material below is standard theory, recorded in the corpus's language and notation. It is standard input, not a framework result: no object in this section is derived from the biquaternion algebra, and the article says so wherever an object could be mistaken for a derivation.

Three consequences follow, and they are the only editorial constraints.

  • The three agendas forbid one thing only: presenting imported structure as a derivation. An exposition is not that, so nothing in the corpus opposes the work.
  • Each article closes with a section of the corpus's usual form — What the Algebra Supplies and What It Imports — so the boundary is stated inside the article and not only in the agenda.
  • A number is never presented as an output. Measured values are labelled with their scheme: $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ at $\mu=m_Z$, on-shell, or the pion-decay constant, as the case requires.

The Gap

The reference lists four objects the corpus holds nowhere. Each is a table or a formula rather than an idea.

  • The content table. No article prints the fifteen Weyl fields of one generation with their $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times \mathrm{U}(1)$ representations and their hypercharge. Hypercharge is named in eighteen articles and tabulated in none.
  • The assembled Lagrangian. The four pieces are quoted separately, in four articles with four sets of conventions. No article prints $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EW}}+\mathcal{L}_{H}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yukawa}}$ in one notation.
  • The nineteen parameters. The count occurs in the corpus only as the word nineteen in unrelated contexts. The framework's answer to the count — that it fixes none of them — is stated in prose in the agenda and quantified nowhere.
  • The symmetric-breaking computation at $SU(2)_L$ length. The Higgs Mechanism works the abelian complex central scalar and then states in a section that the non-abelian case is not built. The doublet, its four real components, the three eaten Goldstones and the two mass formulas are not printed anywhere.

Beside them stand six subjects the corpus touches in a sentence and develops nowhere: the mixing angle as an angle, the Yukawa matrices, the lepton mixing matrix, the oblique parameters, deep inelastic scattering, and the reasons the model is believed incomplete.

The Proposals Counted

Nineteen articles, in five stages: two of frame, six of electroweak, five of strong, two of flavour, four of status. Two are tables with little argument (1, 16); four are full computations (4, 6, 9, 8); the rest are expositions with a closing boundary section. None of the nineteen is a framework result, and none is presented as one.

Order of Work

The dependencies are short and they run one way.

  1. 1, the content and parameters. Every other article quotes its table and its scheme labels.
  2. 3, the group and hypercharge, and 9, the QCD Lagrangian. These two fix the notation the rest of the two sectors use.
  3. 2, the assembled Lagrangian, which can be written only once 3 and 9 exist.
  4. 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 in any order among themselves, each quoting 3.
  5. 10 to 15.
  6. 16 to 19 last, because 16 quotes the count from 1 and 17 to 19 quote the masses and couplings from 4 to 6.

After the articles exist, the three agendas change in one way only: each gains a link, in the place where it says the object is not built, to the new article that prints the object and labels it standard input. The agenda's verdicts are unchanged. The word "not built" becomes "not derived", which is the true statement.

What Not to Do

  • Do not claim a derivation. The agenda's three refusals stand: no $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ from $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, no product group assembled and named, no parameter count used as a framework result.
  • Do not rewrite the single-particle articles. The electron, proton, neutron, photon, gluon, $W$ and $Z$, neutrino, CKM, $\theta$, axion, Higgs, Skyrme and anomaly articles stay as they are. The new articles quote them.
  • Do not re-argue the ceiling. It is proved in two articles and quoted everywhere else.
  • Do not import the gamma display. Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices fixes the display; a legacy section is the only place the gamma form may appear.
  • Do not print a measured number without its scheme. $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ at $m_Z$, on-shell, or stated at the point of use.
  • Do not add the articles to a menu before they are written. Placement is under Biquaternion Particle Physics and Gauge Fields in physics.md, in the order given by the stages.

Summary

The corpus holds the machinery, the ceiling and most of the single-particle content of the Standard Model, and it holds no sector-level exposition of it. Nineteen articles close that gap, in five stages, over the three agendas' own subjects. Each states at the head that it transcribes standard theory, and each closes by stating what the algebra supplies and what it imports. The reference page supplies the checklist and the parameter table; it is a working document, and a stable review replaces it in any Further Reading.

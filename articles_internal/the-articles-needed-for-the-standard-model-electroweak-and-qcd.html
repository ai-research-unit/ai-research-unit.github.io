The Articles Needed for the Standard Model, Electroweak Theory and QCD

Scope

This file is an internal working list, not a published article and not an entry of a menu. It names the articles that must be written for the corpus to hold the Standard Model — its electroweak sector and its strong sector — in detail, in the corpus's own language and notation.

The subject is set by three existing articles, which are boundary maps and not expositions: The Standard Model under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda, Electroweak Theory under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda, and Quantum Chromodynamics under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda. Each states what the framework reaches and what it does not. None of the three carries the material at working length: the gauge group written out, the fermion table with its hypercharge column, the four pieces of the Lagrangian, the scalar doublet, the Yukawa matrices, the QCD vertices, the parton model. This list is the plan for that missing material.

The reference used to build the checklist is the extracted text of a twenty-page capture of the Wikipedia article "Standard Model", added to the project as Standard_Model.pdf: its particle table, its group, its four Lagrangians, its table of nineteen parameters, and its list of open problems. The page is a working checklist and not a citation; a stable review replaces it in any Further Reading.

Nineteen articles were proposed. They are staged, because the later stages quote the earlier ones. All nineteen are now written, and the table in The Nineteen, Written is the readiness statement for the whole list.

The Standing Rule

Every article on this list transcribes standard theory. None derives it. Each therefore opens with a sentence that fixes its standing, in the manner already used by SU(3) Representations and the Gell-Mann–Okubo Mass Formula in Biquaternionic Form, which states at the head that its $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ "is not an algebra of $\mathbb{B}$" but external machinery.

The model sentence, adapted per article:

The material below is standard theory, recorded in the corpus's language and notation. It is standard input, not a framework result: no object in this section is derived from the biquaternion algebra, and the article says so wherever an object could be mistaken for a derivation.

Three consequences follow, and they are the only editorial constraints.

  • The three agendas forbid one thing only: presenting imported structure as a derivation. An exposition is not that, so nothing in the corpus opposes the work.
  • Each article closes with a section of the corpus's usual form — What the Algebra Supplies and What It Imports — so the boundary is stated inside the article and not only in the agenda.
  • A number is never presented as an output. Measured values are labelled with their scheme: $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ at $\mu=m_Z$, on-shell, or the pion-decay constant, as the case requires.

The Gap

The reference listed four objects the corpus held nowhere. Each is a table or a formula rather than an idea. All four are now printed; the bullets record what was missing and where each one landed.

  • The content table. (Landed in article 1.) One article, Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation, printed the five multiplets with their $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ labels and a hypercharge column, but it prints the column as a premise of an anomaly argument. No article prints the fifteen Weyl fields, their $T_3$ and their $Q$, the twelve gauge bosons and the scalar in one table, and none records that the fifteen is $6+3+3+2+1$.
  • The assembled Lagrangian. (Landed in article 2.) The four pieces were quoted separately, in four articles with four sets of conventions, and no article printed $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EW}}+\mathcal{L}_{H}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yukawa}}$ in one notation.
  • The nineteen parameters. (Landed in article 16.) The count occurred in the corpus only as the word nineteen in unrelated contexts. The framework's answer to the count — that it fixes none of them — is stated in prose in the agenda and quantified nowhere.
  • The solution of the anomaly conditions. Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation verifies that the standard assignment cancels the four conditions, one generation at a time, in exact rational arithmetic. It does not solve them. Solving them is a short exact computation, and it disposes of the objection that the hypercharges are arbitrary: three linear conditions reduce the five hypercharges to two, the cubic condition becomes $Y_u^3+Y_d^3=7(Y_u+Y_d)^3$, and the solutions are three rays, of which the observed charges select one and fix the scale. That computation was first written into article 1 and is now carried in full in article 3, which is the article titled for it: the matrix of the linear system, its null space in a two-parameter form, and the cubic factored as $18p(3p-q)(3p+q)$. Article 1 keeps the summary form $a^3+b^3=7(a+b)^3$ and points to article 3 for the derivation; the two forms are the same equation, with $a=p+q$ and $b=p-q$, and the equivalence is recorded in article 3 so that a reader meeting both can check it.
  • The symmetric-breaking computation at $SU(2)_L$ length. (Landed in article 4.) The Higgs Mechanism works the abelian complex central scalar and then states in a section that the non-abelian case is not built; article 4 builds it. The doublet, its four real components, the three eaten Goldstones and the two mass formulas are not printed anywhere.

Beside them stood six subjects the corpus touched in a sentence and developed nowhere — the mixing angle as an angle, the Yukawa matrices, the lepton mixing matrix, the oblique parameters, deep inelastic scattering, and the reasons the model is believed incomplete. All six are now developed, in articles 5, 6, 14, 8, 11 and 19 respectively.

Review 2 records, from the same review pass. Article 2 carried two real errors and three smaller defects. The first is in the proof of the sign rule, which is the article's own contribution and therefore the place where a slip costs most. The proof ran: the temporal term is $-X_0Y_0$, the spatial terms are $X_kY_k$, "so the right-hand side is $X_0Y_0-X_kY_k$, and the left-hand side is $-X_0Y_0+X_kY_k$. The two are equal." The last clause is the sum $\sum_\mu X_\mu Y_\mu$ and not the left-hand side, which carries the leading minus and equals $X_0Y_0-X_kY_k$. As printed, the proof concludes that two expressions are equal when they are negatives of each other — the theorem is true and the proof as written refutes it. The clause now distributes the minus explicitly. The lesson is a variant of the one Review 1 earned: when a proof is one line long, the algebra of that line is the whole proof, so the last equality has to be read as an equality and not as a summary.

The second, and the substantive one: the Weinberg operator was printed with the wrong hypercharge. Article 2 wrote the dimension-five neutrino-mass operator as $\frac{c_{ab}}{\Lambda}(L_{L,a}^T\tilde\varphi)(\tilde\varphi^TL_{L,b})$. With the article's own assignments, $Y_L=-\tfrac12$ and $Y_{\tilde\varphi}=-\tfrac12$, that operator carries $-\tfrac12-\tfrac12-\tfrac12-\tfrac12=-2$ and is not gauge invariant; it is also not a weak singlet, because $L^T\tilde\varphi$ contracts two doublets without an $\epsilon$. The correct operator, and the one the corpus's own seesaw article already prints as $c_5(LH)(LH)/\Lambda$, is $\frac{c_{ab}}{\Lambda}(L_{L,a}^T i\tau_2\varphi)(L_{L,b}^T i\tau_2\varphi)$ with $\varphi$ and not $\tilde\varphi$: the hypercharge closes, $-\tfrac12-\tfrac12+\tfrac12+\tfrac12=0$, and $i\tau_2$ supplies the weak singlet. This is the failure mode the corpus had not yet recorded — a wrong sign of a quantum number is caught by the audit, but a wrong field in an operator that is only mentioned in passing is caught by nothing, because the operator sits in a list of omissions and is never audited term by term the way the Lagrangian itself is. The rule added: any operator named in a list of omissions gets the same hypercharge check as a term in the sum; being outside the Lagrangian is not a licence to be outside the audit.

Review 3 records, and it is the largest of the three reviews. Article 3 is the article that turns the hypercharge assignment into a computation, so every error in it propagates into articles 1, 2 and 16. Five defects, three of them substantive, and all five are of the same species: a label attached to a formula that is right, or a formula attached to a label that is right, with the two not matching. The formulas survived; the words around them did not.

One: the two writings of the anomaly conditions were swapped, and one of them was therefore wrong. The article printed the minus-sign system $2Y_Q-Y_u-Y_d=0$ under the heading "in the left-handed convention, with the right-handed hypercharges already reversed", and then printed the plus-sign system $2Y_Q+Y_{u_R}+Y_{d_R}=0$ under the heading "passed back to the right-handed hypercharges of the table". Both labels are inverted. Evaluated in exact rationals: the minus-sign form vanishes in the table hypercharges and gives $\tfrac23$ in the conjugate ones; the plus-sign form vanishes in the conjugate hypercharges and gives $\tfrac23$ in the table ones. So the second system, as attributed, was simply false on the observed assignment — and the article's own preceding paragraph calls this "the commonest source of sign errors in this computation", which is exactly what it then committed. Both labels are corrected, the conjugate variables are now written $Y^{\mathrm c}_u=-Y_{u_R}$ and so on, and the mixture is spelled out with the number it produces. The same mislabelling was in article 1, whose phrase "with the right-handed fields entering as conjugates" sat in front of the table's hypercharges; that clause now says "as the table lists them", with a sentence naming the conjugate form as the plus-sign one.

Two: the anomaly lemma over-claimed, in article 3 and in its source. Both articles said that every mixed condition vanishes by a single trace, while listing $SU(3)_c^2\,\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ and $SU(2)_L^2\,\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ as conditions two lines earlier. Those two are the exception: $\mathrm{Tr}\,[T_aT_b]\propto\delta_{ab}$ does not vanish, so the sum over the content with one power of $Y$ is a real constraint. What vanishes is every condition with at most one non-abelian generator, $Y^2\mathrm{Tr}\,[T_a]$ and $Y\,\mathrm{Tr}\,[T_a^c]\,\mathrm{Tr}\,[T_b^L]$. The lemma is restated in both files, and the Summary sentence "every mixed condition vanishes" is now "every condition with at most one non-abelian generator". Note the failure mode: the list of four conditions was right throughout, so nothing downstream broke; only the justification was. A reader checking the list would have found it correct and stopped.

Three: the scalar's hypercharge came out with the wrong sign. The article derived $Y_\varphi=Y_{e_R}-Y_{L_L}=-1-(-\tfrac12)=-\tfrac12$, which is the hypercharge of the conjugate doublet. From the term $\bar L_L\varphi e_R$ and a barred field's hypercharge being the negative of the unbarred one, $-Y_L+Y_\varphi+Y_e=0$ gives $Y_\varphi=Y_{L_L}-Y_{e_R}=+\tfrac12$ — the value in article 1's table, in article 2's Yukawa audit and in article 4's minimum. What Not to Do

  • Do not claim a derivation. The agenda's three refusals stand: no $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ from $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, no product group assembled and named, no parameter count used as a framework result.
  • Do not rewrite the single-particle articles. The electron, proton, neutron, photon, gluon, $W$ and $Z$, neutrino, CKM, $\theta$, axion, Higgs, Skyrme and anomaly articles stay as they are. The nineteen quote them.
  • Do not re-argue the ceiling. It is proved in two articles and quoted everywhere else.
  • Do not import the gamma display. Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices fixes the display; a legacy section is the only place the gamma form may appear.
  • Do not print a measured number without its scheme. $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ at $m_Z$, on-shell, or stated at the point of use.
  • Do not edit one of the nineteen without re-running the three checks. Table parity, math-delimiter parity, and the internal links. Four of the six corrections recorded in this file were arithmetic that a recomputation caught and a re-reading did not.

Summary

The corpus held the machinery, the ceiling and most of the single-particle content of the Standard Model, and held no sector-level exposition of it. Nineteen articles close that gap, in five stages, over the three agendas' own subjects: $7{,}226$ lines, $280$ sections, $178$ tables.

Each opens with the standing sentence, each closes by separating what the algebra supplies from what it imports, and each labels its measured numbers with scheme and scale. None is a framework result.

What the nineteen establish, taken together, is not new physics but a boundary made quantitative. The framework supplies a gauge principle that reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, a central phase that reaches hypercharge, an anomaly derivation, a $\theta$ vacuum and a Majorana condition. It does not supply colour, it does not fix any of the nineteen parameters, it cannot complete the couplings into a group, and it cannot build the sphaleron. The parameter count measured that boundary at $0/19$ by value and said why that sentence is vacuous; the strong sector measured it at the level of the group; and the last three articles measured it at the level of the space of couplings, where the framework turns out to have no purchase at all.

The one pattern worth keeping is that the corpus's two live answers in its closing article — the axion and leptogenesis — both arrived as by-products of solving other problems. That is an argument for a language over a model, and it is not an argument the nineteen were written to make.

The Letter Zeta: An Inventory of the Glyph

Introduction

The letter $\zeta$ is bound to one object. Special Element and Peirce Conventions, §The Root Convention, makes the glyph the name of the root of the algebra: $\tilde{Ζ}$ in the biquaternion layers, $\tilde\zeta$ in the quaternion ones, $Ζ$ in the layers that carry no tilde. No other object is assigned to the letter, and no other letter is assigned to the root.

The census does not support the exclusivity. The corpus writes the letter 11752 times in 537 articles: 10206 tokens in 403 mathematical articles and 1546 in 134 physical ones. The root accounts for 4202 of the mathematical tokens and 742 of the physical ones. The remaining 6004 mathematical tokens and 804 physical tokens are a variable, a function, a set or a parameter, and this article counts them part by part.

What the article establishes. The forms the conventions fix, and the count of each. The roles of the lower case in each of the six mathematical parts and in physics. The four roles of the upper case, and the reason three of them are local declarations rather than conventions. The one repair the corpus already has at hand.

What it does not claim. It does not claim that $\zeta$ is free, because the root holds it. It does not claim that the other uses are errors: each is declared where it is used, and the articles read. The conventions reserve the decorated glyph for the root, the reservation is honoured in 117 mathematical articles, and the bare letter carries at least eight other roles in mathematics.

What the article decides. Two sections at the end carry the decision. The Current Situation states in one place what the conventions give the letter, what the corpus does with it, part by part and role by role, and where the line between a written rule and a local declaration falls. The Complete Alternative then gives every role its replacement, in full, on three instruments the corpus already owns and with no new glyph: a word, the superscript label, and the low index.

The Forms the Conventions Fix

Special Element and Peirce Conventions, §The Root Convention, states the case rule in one sentence:

The case rule splits the glyph, as it does for the elements, the idempotents and the nilpotents. The roots of the biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers carry the upper-case tilde, $\tilde{Ζ}$; those of the quaternion and split-quaternion layers carry the lower-case tilde, $\tilde\zeta$; those of the complex, split-complex and dual layers carry the plain capital, $Ζ$; and those of the reduced biquaternion layer carry the same plain capital, $Ζ$, no tilde being written where there is no quaternionic factor.

The same section fixes the coordinates. The root of the biquaternion layer is $\tilde{Ζ} = Ζ_te_t + Ζ_x\vec e_x + Ζ_y\vec e_y + Ζ_z\vec e_z$ with $Ζ_\mu = \zeta_\mu + i\zeta'_\mu$, and the root of the quaternion layer is $\tilde\zeta = \zeta_te_t + \zeta_x\vec e_x + \zeta_y\vec e_y + \zeta_z\vec e_z$. The arrow is laid on the same rule by Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Pure Glyphs: the pure root is $\vec{Ζ}$ in the biquaternion layers and $\vec\zeta$ in the quaternion ones, and the arrow replaces the tilde rather than stacking with it.

The adjoined generator is the one role subscript the conventions name, and they name it twice, once in each corpus. Special Element Conventions writes it in the physics words: "It is a root of the double, not an element of $\mathbb{B}$. The chirality is the cell $\mathsf{V}^{\mathrm{vol}}$ of §The Chirality Convention; its central form is $\chi=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ with $\chi^2=e_t$. No other letter is used for it."

The two cases are written by different means. The minuscule is never a raw codepoint in the corpus: the character U+03B6 stands zero times, and every minuscule is the command \zeta. The majuscule is never a command: \Zeta stands zero times, and every capital is the glyph, U+0396. The reason is typographic. LaTeX has commands for the capitals $\Gamma$, $\Delta$, $\Theta$, $\Lambda$, $\Xi$, $\Pi$, $\Sigma$, $\Upsilon$, $\Phi$, $\Psi$ and $\Omega$, and no command for zeta, chi or stigma. The corpus therefore writes the glyph, as it does for $Χ$ and $Ϛ$. Any count of the letter has to use two instruments, the command for the lower case and the codepoint for the upper one.

The case rule here is a layer rule, not a field rule. The same device carries a different content in the two letters. For $\chi$ the case carries the coefficient, real against complex; for $\zeta$ the case carries the layer, quaternion against biquaternion. A reader who arrives from the chi audit will expect the field rule and will not find it. This is the first thing the inventory has to say, because it explains why the minuscule is not a residue of the majuscule but its peer.

The open set is the largest. The Eilenberg–Zilber map is three tokens in two articles. Cup and Cap Products writes $\zeta\times\zeta$ as a chain map, $Ζ:C_*(X;R)\otimes_R C_*(Y;R)\to C_*(X\times Y;R)$, and the product $c\times d=Ζ_*(c\otimes d)$; The Leray–Serre Spectral Sequence refers back to it. The glyph is a map, declared with its arrow.

The Riemann $Ζ$ function is 13 tokens in one article. The Riemann Hypothesis defines it at line 24 as the entire product $\tfrac12 s(s-1)\Lambda(s)$ read on the critical line, and uses it in the statement of the hypothesis, $\mathrm{RH}\iff$ every zero of $Ζ$ is real. The standard notation for that function is $\Xi$; the article names it the Riemann $Ζ$ function and declares it at the point of use, so the reader is not asked to guess.

The strongest objection. A corpus that declares each of these names has no ambiguity inside an article. That is true, and it was checked: no article carries both an open-set $Ζ$ and a decorated root. The open-set articles have no tilde and no arrow, and the root articles have no subset sign. So the collision is not local. It is corpus-wide: the same shape means the root in 117 articles and a set in 17, and a reader who moves between them carries the wrong reading for a moment.

The repair is already in the corpus. An open set is written $\Omega$ in 146 occurrences across 86 articles, and $U$ in 28 occurrences across 22. The two letters are in use, the conventions name neither for this purpose, and the substitution costs 295 tokens in 17 articles. Whether to make it is a decision for the author, not for this audit.

The finding, in one sentence. Six roles in mathematics and five in physics stand on the letter without a convention, and one of the six is not a role at all: a bare $\zeta$ that means an arbitrary element breaks the element rule of Algebra and Element Conventions, which says that the generic element is $\tilde Q$ and that no letter outside the $Q$ family is generic at any case.

The legitimate uses, and the size of the breach. Two roles are not in question: the root, 4,944 tokens, and the zeta function, 359 in 28 articles, where $\zeta(s)$ is a name carried with its argument and the Riemann $Ζ(t)$ is the same name read on the critical line. What the conventions reserve is 4,563 mathematical tokens and 764 physical ones, the capital and the dressed forms; the remaining 5,643 and 782 stand on a glyph no rule has claimed.

The breach is not local, and it is not a breach of a written rule either. No article carries two readings of the letter at once — the audit checked all 537 — so a reader inside one article is never misled. The collision is corpus-wide: the same shape is the root in 117 mathematical articles and an open set in 17, and the same minuscule is a frequency in 55 and a state vector in 29. A reader who moves between articles carries the wrong reading for a moment, and the corpus has no sentence that tells him which he is holding.

The Complete Alternative

One rule, three devices, no new letter. The migration adds no glyph: no Greek capital, no archaic letter, no Fraktur, no letter of any alphabet. It uses the three instruments the conventions already own.

The four superscripts the migration needs, and why each is needed.

The caret is not used on $\zeta$. A $\zeta^{\mathrm{fou}}$ would keep the letter as the glyph of a non-root object, and the reservation is a reservation of the glyph, not of a form. The precedent is the corpus's own retirement of the phase: the 91 occurrences that $\phi$ carried are $\theta^{\mathrm{pha}}$, and the role moved to another object's glyph rather than staying on its own.

the form why the superscript the precedent

The index is not a superscript, and the alternative never confuses the two. A second copy of one object takes the low index, $\mathrm{Frq}_1$ and $\mathrm{Frq}_2$, $\mathrm{Spr}_0$ and $\mathrm{Spr}_1$, $\mathrm{Opn}_i$; a different object takes the superscript label. This is the sentence of Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions applied: a label on the caret names the object the glyph denotes, and low it names a part or a role.

What the alternative refuses. It refuses twelve candidate letters because each is bound: $\xi$ the field, 2,911 tokens in 172 articles; $\theta$ and $\Theta$ the angles; $\vartheta$ the congruence and the theta function; $\varpi$ the symplectic potential; $\Omega$ the curvature; $U$, $u$, $\tilde U$, $\vec u$ the units, with the sentence that they never name an open set … or a dual element; $\lambda$ the central phase; $\kappa$ the coupling; $\omega$ a curvature component and the angular velocity; $\pi$ and $\Pi$ the idempotent; $\nu$ and $\Upsilon$ the nilpotent; $\gamma$ the Clifford map; $\sigma$ the Pauli matrices; $\chi$ the central grading; $\ell$ the length; $M$ the matrix; $\varrho$ retired by name. It refuses the Greek capitals, the archaic letters and Fraktur, which are letters. It refuses $\mathcal U$ for an open cover only where the article also writes the cover, and prefers the label there.

What it costs to state. One sentence in Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions, that an upright name may name an object of the corpus and not only an operation, beside the sentences that already write $\mathrm{Form}$ and $\mathrm{Norm}$; and the rows of the four superscripts above in the label table.

Summary

The conventions give the letter one role, the root of the algebra, written in every decoration: $\tilde{Ζ}$, $\tilde\zeta$, $\vec{Ζ}$, $\vec\zeta$, $Ζ$, $Ζ_\pm$, $Ζ_\mu$, and the adjoined generator $\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$. No sentence assigns the letter a second object, and no sentence reserves the bare minuscule at all.

The corpus writes 11,752 tokens in 537 articles. The root holds 4,944 of them, the zeta function 359 in 28 articles, and the remaining 6,449 stand on the letter without a convention: the dual variable 2,011 in 55 articles, the vector of a Hilbert space 828 in 29, the open set 295 in 17, the boundary point 115 in 12, the Grassmann coordinate 59 in 1, the twistor and its spinor 247 in 4, the gauge parameter 116 in 4, the coefficient of a basis 108 in 1, the squeeze parameter 39 in 2, the L part of a state module 22 in 1, and a long tail of one-off fields.

The alternative adds no glyph. It uses three instruments the corpus already owns: a word, $\mathrm{Frq}$, $\mathrm{Vc}$, $\mathrm{Opn}$, $\mathrm{Bnd}$, $\mathrm{Grs}$, $\mathrm{Bs}$, $\mathrm{Sqz}$, $\mathrm{Fx}$, $\mathrm{Df}_\mu$, $\mathrm{Rat}$, $\mathrm{St}_L$, $\vec{\mathrm{Wv}}$, $\mathrm{Spr}$, $\hat{\mathrm{Drc}}$, $\mathrm{Pmp}$, $\mathrm{Ezl}$; the superscript label where two objects of one word meet, $\mathrm{Spr}^{\mathrm{pha}}$, $\mathrm{Frq}^{\mathrm{sym}}$, $\mathrm{Opn}^{\mathrm{cov}}$; and the low index for a second copy, $\mathrm{Frq}_1$, $\mathrm{Spr}_0$, $\mathrm{Opn}_i$. The caret is never written on $\zeta$, because a $\zeta^{\mathrm{fou}}$ would keep the glyph as the name of a non-root object, and the reservation is a reservation of the glyph.

The generic element, 1,340 tokens in 56 articles, is not a role at all: the element rule says the general element is $\tilde Q$, and a $\zeta$ that stands for an arbitrary element breaks that rule before it breaks anything about this letter.

The work is 5,180 tokens in 182 articles, in eight steps. The first five are 1,001 tokens in 42 articles and need no table; the last three are 4,179 in 140 and every occurrence must be read.

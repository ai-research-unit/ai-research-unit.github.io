Lambda Migration

Scope

This file is an inventory, not an article and not an entry of a menu. It names the physics articles in which one of the three lengths can replace a quotient of a mass, a momentum or an energy with $\hbar$ or $h$, and it names them in the order of the menu, under the categories of the menu.

The rule is already written: in Unit, Index and Notation Conventions, where the letter $\mu$ is retired as an inverse length, and in The Mass Length Scale of a Massive Particle, where the three lengths are read. What is missing is the work list. This file is that list.

The Three Lengths

Length Definition Computed from Reciprocal
$\lambda_m$ $\lambda_m=\hbar/(mc)$ the mass $\lambda_m^{-1}=mc/\hbar$
$\lambda_p$ $\lambda_p=\hbar/p$ the momentum $\lambda_p^{-1}=p/\hbar$
$\lambda_E$ $\lambda_E=\hbar c/E$ the total energy $\lambda_E^{-1}=E/(\hbar c)$

Each is a length. Each reciprocal is the coefficient an equation actually carries, because a gradient carries an inverse length. The three are not independent: the mass shell $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$, divided by $\hbar^2c^2$, is $\lambda_E^{-2}=\lambda_p^{-2}+\lambda_m^{-2}$.

The Trigger

A quotient of $m$, $p$ or $E$ against $\hbar$ or $h$ is one of these lengths or its reciprocal, and nothing else.

Form now written Is Example of the replacement
$mc/\hbar$ $\lambda_m^{-1}$ $(\Box-m^2c^2/\hbar^2)\tilde{Ξ}=0$ becomes $(\Box-\lambda_m^{-2})\tilde{Ξ}=0$
$\hbar/(mc)$ $\lambda_m$ an amplitude of order $\hbar/(mc)$ becomes one of order $\lambda_m$
$\hbar/(2mc)$ $\lambda_m/2$ the half length that the Zitterbewegung amplitude carries
$mc^2/\hbar$ $c\,\lambda_m^{-1}$ the rest frequency $c/\lambda_m$, the Compton clock
$p/\hbar$ $\lambda_p^{-1}$ a wave number becomes the reciprocal of a length
$\hbar/p$ $\lambda_p$ the reduced de Broglie wavelength
$h/p$ $2\pi\lambda_p$ the ordinary de Broglie wavelength
$E/\hbar$ $\lambda_E^{-1}$ an angular frequency becomes $c/\lambda_E$
$\hbar c/E$ $\lambda_E$ the energy length
$E/(\hbar c)$ $\lambda_E^{-1}$ a temporal wave number $k_0$

The two directions are one statement. A mass coefficient is $\lambda_m^{-1}$ and the length it inverts is $\lambda_m$; nothing else in the pair is a separate convention.

What the Substitution Buys

One symbol per statement. An operator written $\Box-\lambda_m^{-2}$ names the length whose reciprocal it holds. An operator written $\Box-m^2c^2/\hbar^2$ names a mass, a speed and an action, three constants, to say one length.

The unit is visible. A gradient carries an inverse length, so the term it is equated to must carry an inverse length. The field $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and the coefficient $\lambda_m^{-1}$ share a unit; $mc/\hbar$ has to be recognised as that object before the two can be compared.

The limits are read off. The massless limit is $\lambda_m^{-1}\to0$, the non-relativistic limit is $\lambda_p\gg\lambda_m$, and the rest frame is $\lambda_p\to\infty$. Each is a statement about one symbol.

What Is Not Migrated

  • $\hbar\omega$ and $\hbar\vec k$. A frequency is turned into an energy, not into a length. No length is wanted.
  • $p=\hbar k$ and $E=\hbar\omega$. A wave number and a frequency, already inverse length and inverse time. Rewriting them as lengths would put a length where the equation wants a derivative.
  • $\mu$ as permeability in $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$ and $\mu$ as a direction label in $e_\mu$ and $Q_\mu$. Neither is an inverse length; the conventions article reads the two by context.
  • $h/p=2\pi\lambda_p$. Written once, deliberately, to relate the ordinary and the reduced wavelength. A definition, not a use.
  • The definition sites. The Mass Length Scale of a Massive Particle and Unit, Index and Notation Conventions carry the quotients on purpose, because they introduce the symbols.

The Contradiction, Now Settled

Operator and Calculus Conventions fixed the one d'Alembertian and stated the mass equation as $(\Box-m^2c^2/\hbar^2)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$, while The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form states $(\Box-\lambda_m^{-2})\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$ and derives every mass coefficient as $1/\lambda_m$. Two conventions articles, one equation, two spellings.

It is settled in favour of the length. The conventions article now writes $(\Box-\lambda_m^{-2})\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$, and the same substitution is carried through its own statements of the pair operator: the two first-order equations read $\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}/\lambda_m$ and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}/\lambda_m$, the massive equation reads $(\mathbb{D}-\lambda_m^{-1})\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$, the mass shell reads $p^2=\lambda_m^{-2}$, and the Green matrix reads $(\Box-\lambda_m^{-2})G=\delta$ with $(\mathbb{D}-\lambda_m^{-1})(\mathbb{D}+\lambda_m^{-1})=\mathbb{D}^2-\lambda_m^{-2}$. The article introduces $\lambda_m$ once, names it in its summary of notation, and states that the mass is a reciprocal length and never a quotient of $m$ and $\hbar$.

The conventions article is therefore no longer work. It is the second definition site, and the rule it now carries is the one the rest of this list applies.

The Reference Implementation

The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form is the model. It writes $\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}/\lambda_m$, defines $\lambda_m=\hbar/(mc)$ once, and states that below it the mass coefficient is always $1/\lambda_m$ and the mass-squared term always $1/\lambda_m^{2}$. Among the articles that state the Dirac equation it is the only one that carries no quotient of the old spelling. Every other Dirac article is measured against it.

How the Count Is Made

What counts. A literal quotient: $mc/\hbar$ in either direction, $m^2c^2/\hbar^2$, $\mu=mc/\hbar$ as a definition, $p/\hbar$, $\hbar/p$, $h/p$, $E/\hbar$, $\hbar/E$, $\hbar c/E$, $E/(\hbar c)$, $h/E$. Each occurrence counts once.

What is excluded. Any line that already carries $\lambda_m$, since that line states the rule and not a use of the old spelling; and the three definition sites named above.

The count is a floor. A quotient split across two macros, a coefficient carried by a symbol defined elsewhere, and a mass in natural units are not counted here. The second list below gives the natural-units part.

The menu order. The tables follow Physics exactly: the same categories, the same order inside each category, and no article moved.

The Second Wave: the Mass as a Bare Symbol

The counts above are quotients. The same articles also write the mass as a bare symbol in an operator, $-\mu^2\tilde{Ξ}$ or $(\Box-m^2)\tilde{Ξ}$ or $1/(p^2-m^2)$, which is the same statement in units $\hbar=c=1$: there the mass is an inverse length and the length is not written. These are not quotients, so nothing above counts them, and for the field-theory articles they are the larger part of the work. The number beside each title counts the literal forms $-\mu^2$, $-\mu^{2}$, $-m^2$, $-m^{2}$ and $p^2-m^2$.

The list holds only articles that also carry a quotient, so it is a subset of the tables above and not a second corpus. An article whose mass is the algebraic modulus of an element, and not the mass of a field, is outside the migration and is not listed.

Order of Work

First, the conventions. Done. Operator and Calculus Conventions now writes $(\Box-\lambda_m^{-2})\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$ and its pair operator in the same length, so the spelling that every other article should follow is normative and written down.

Second, the articles that state an operator. There the mass term is a display and not a remark, so search and replace will not do and the article must be read:

  • The Klein–Gordon Equation in Biquaternionic Form, seventeen quotients, the operator stated in three frames.
  • The Proca Equation: Massive Spin 1, twenty-two quotients, and the article whose $\mu$ is a mass parameter from beginning to end.
  • Klein–Gordon from the Dirac Square, which squares the Dirac operator and must show the square producing $\lambda_m^{-2}$; it carries the largest bare-symbol count of the corpus, forty-four.
  • The Dirac Oscillator, The Dirac Path Integral and Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Dirac Field, which each restate the Dirac operator and inherit the convention of the parent article.
  • The First-Order Root of the Biquaternion Klein–Gordon Operator, whose subject is the root and which cannot leave the coefficient ambiguous.

Third, the exercises. Plane-Wave Solutions of the Biquaternion Dirac Equation and Chirality and the Weyl Spinors carry the mass shell as $k_0^2=\vec k^2+m^2c^2/\hbar^2$, which is $\lambda_E^{-2}=\lambda_p^{-2}+\lambda_m^{-2}$. The exercises ask for the three lengths and currently compute them, so the lengths belong in the statement of the answer.

Fourth, everything else. The remaining rows carry one or two quotients each, mostly a mass shell or a rest frequency in a remark, and one pass over them closes the corpus.

What This File Does Not Decide

It does not decide whether an article should drop $m$ and $c$ altogether in favour of $\lambda_m$, nor whether the natural-units second wave is worth doing. It records where the old spelling is written, so that the decision is taken once and applied in one direction.

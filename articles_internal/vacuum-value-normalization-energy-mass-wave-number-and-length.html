Vacuum Value Normalization: Energy, Mass, Wave Number, Length and Time

Introduction

One anchor, five quantities. The vacuum value is one number. Written as an energy it is $E_{\mathrm{vev}}=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$; written as a mass it is $m_{\mathrm{vev}}=4.3893\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$; written as a wave number it is $\nu_{\mathrm{vev}}=1.9859\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$; written as a length it is $\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}=5.0355\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{m}$; written as a time it is $\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}=1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$. The five are one quantity in five dimensions, and the four equalities between them are $E=mc^{2}$, $\nu=E/(hc)$, $\lambda=1/\nu$ and $\tau=h/E$ — no constant set to one anywhere.

What this article does. It defines the five anchors, computes the five numbers from the measured Fermi coupling, and states the rule by which every equation of the particle-physics corpus is written: in fractions of the anchor. The fraction is a pure number, so an equation written in fractions carries no unit and no conversion constant, and the constant that would otherwise be dropped cannot be dropped, because it is not there.

What it is not. It is not a model, not a claim about why the vacuum value has the value it has, and not a change of the kilogram. The kilogram stays the base unit of mass. The anchors stand in a table once; the fractions stand in the equations.

The defect it repairs. A mass written in electronvolts drops $c^{2}$ silently, because the electronvolt measures energy and the corpus measures mass in kilograms. A mass written as a bare number of MeV is a statement that $c=1$, and an energy written in electronvolts is a statement that $e=1$, because the electronvolt is the elementary charge carried through one volt. This article is where both spellings stop: a mass is a kilogram or a fraction, an energy is a joule, never an electronvolt.

What the Vacuum Value Is

A field that does not vanish in the vacuum. The scalar doublet of the electroweak theory is a field, and its lowest-energy state is not the zero field. In the vacuum the field sits at a constant value, the same at every point and at every instant, and that constant is the number this article normalises against: $v=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$ in the notation of the electroweak articles, which is $E_{\mathrm{vev}}$ read as an energy, the two being one number seen from two sides.

A constant field acts only through what couples to it. A field of constant value has no gradient, so it exerts no force on a charge at rest and does no work on a charge in flight. Its presence is felt only by the particles whose equations contain it, and they feel it as inertia. A particle coupled to the background must carry the background with it when it changes its state, so a change of momentum costs more than it costs a free particle, and the extra cost is what the corpus calls the mass. The coupling is a pure number, the background supplies the scale, and the mass is the product:

$$ m_i=\frac{y_i\,E_{\mathrm{vev}}}{\sqrt2\,c^{2}} . $$

One value, three ways of meeting it. A fermion meets the background through its Yukawa term, and its mass is the Yukawa coupling times the value. A gauge boson meets it through the covariant derivative, and its mass is the gauge coupling times the value: $M_W=g\,E_{\mathrm{vev}}/2$, an energy. The Higgs boson meets it through the curvature of the potential at its minimum: $M_H=\sqrt{2\digamma_{\mathrm{qtc}}}\,E_{\mathrm{vev}}$. In all three the mass is a dimensionless coupling multiplied by one scale, which is why the scale can be divided out and a coupling left in its place.

What the picture does not cover. The proton's mass is not of this kind. Most of it is the energy of the gluon field inside the proton and of the motion of the quarks, and it would be there if the vacuum value were zero. The proton is written in fractions of the same anchor all the same, because the anchor is a unit of mass and not a statement about the origin of a mass. The fraction measures; it does not explain.

Why the value is worth measuring once. The value is not a postulate of the corpus. It is measured, and the clock is the muon. A muon decays through the weak interaction at a rate proportional to the square of the Fermi coupling, the Fermi coupling is the inverse square of the value times a fixed number, and the lifetime is therefore proportional to the fourth power of the value. One lifetime, measured to a few parts per million, fixes the scale against which every mass in the corpus is divided.

The Anchor

Where the number comes from. The Fermi coupling is measured and is quoted in the form that carries its dimension,

$$ \frac{G_F}{\left(\frac{h}{2\pi}c\right)^{3}}=4.5438\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{J^{-2}}, $$

because the coupling itself has the dimension of a volume times an energy, $\mathrm{J\,m^{3}}$, and the division by the cube of $\frac{h}{2\pi}c$ — which is an action times a velocity, $\mathrm{J\,m}$ — leaves an inverse energy squared, $[\mathrm{J^{-2}}]=[\text{energy}^{-2}]$. The scale it fixes is

$$ E_{\mathrm{vev}}=\left(\sqrt2\,\frac{G_F}{\left(\frac{h}{2\pi}c\right)^{3}}\right)^{-1/2}=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}, $$

and the mass of that scale is

$$ m_{\mathrm{vev}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{vev}}}{c^{2}}=4.389\,27\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}. $$

The division by $c^{2}$ is written and is not a convention: it is the definition of mass in the corpus's base units. The cube of $\frac{h}{2\pi}c$ is written for the same reason, and neither constant is set to one in the process.

Why this name, and what the letter is. The corpus already carries the number, as $v$ in the electroweak articles, where it is the value the doublet takes in the vacuum and is written as an energy, $v=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$. What the corpus does not carry is the same number as a mass, as a wave number, as a length and as a time, nor a name for the five readings together; and the bare letter $v$ is a velocity elsewhere in the corpus, so the five readings take a subscript. The subscript $\mathrm{vev}$ names the reading and not the interaction: $E_{\mathrm{vev}}$ is the corpus's $v$, $m_{\mathrm{vev}}$ is $v/c^{2}$, and $\nu_{\mathrm{vev}}$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}$ are the other three. The letter $\tau$ is the corpus's reserved glyph for proper time, so the clock reading carries the subscript and the two are never elided: $\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}$ is a constant of the vacuum, the period of the anchor's phase, and $\tau$ is a coordinate read along one worldline. Two other names were tried and refused: Electroweak Normalization and Higgs Normalization. Both are too narrow, because the anchor divides every mass in the corpus, including most of the proton's, which comes from the strong interaction. The name is provisional and the corpus may replace it.

Fixed, not running. The anchor is the value fixed by $G_F$. It is not the running scale, not the renormalisation scale $\mu$, and not the mass of any particle. A Yukawa coupling normalised against it is therefore normalised once and for all, and the running of a mass is carried by the fraction, not by the anchor.

Why the anchor and not the energy. An equation in the corpus has to divide a mass by a mass. If the anchor were quoted as an energy, every such division would need a $c^{2}$ written beside it, and the temptation to omit it is exactly the defect this article exists to remove. The anchor is therefore defined once, in kilograms, and only its five readings are quoted in their own dimensions.

The Four Conversions

One relation, applied four times. From the mass, four further anchors follow with no free choice:

$$ E_{\mathrm{vev}}=m_{\mathrm{vev}}\,c^{2},\qquad \nu_{\mathrm{vev}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{vev}}}{hc},\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}=\frac{1}{\nu_{\mathrm{vev}}},\qquad \tau_{\mathrm{vev}}=\frac{h}{E_{\mathrm{vev}}}=\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}}{c}. $$

The constant $hc$ is the one that turns an energy into a wave number, $hc=1.986\,445\,86\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{J\cdot m}$, and $h$ is the one that turns an energy into a period, $h=6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$; both are exact, because $h$ and $c$ are exact. The fourth equality is written both ways on purpose: $h/E$ is the definition and $\lambda/c$ is what makes the time a reading of the length, and a reader who wants the check has it in the display.

The five anchors, once.

quantity symbol value dimension

mass $m_{\mathrm{vev}}$ $4.3893\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$ $\mathrm{M}$

The energy is quoted in joules because the joule is the SI unit of energy and its definition contains no constant. The mass is quoted in kilograms because that is the base unit of the corpus, and because a mass in the electronvolt system cannot be written without either a $c^{2}$ that is usually dropped or a division by $c^{2}$ that is a unit conversion in disguise. The time is quoted in seconds, the base unit, with the power of ten written out: $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$ and not a prefixed multiple of it, for the reason the femtometre is refused on a length.

Five figures throughout. Every value quoted in this article is given to five significant figures, and a check that recomputes one shows the digits the arithmetic produces. A figure beyond the fifth belongs to the arithmetic and not to the measurement.

One number generates five. Nothing else is needed. A reader who has $m_{\mathrm{vev}}$ and the four relations has the other four anchors, and the five differ only by the constants that carry the dimension: a factor $c^{2}$, a factor $hc$, a factor $h$, and an inversion. That is why this article can define five quantities and compute one.

The Five Readings

What each one measures. The five anchors are one quantity read by five kinds of instrument, and the reading is what an instrument of that kind returns.

  • The energy is $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$. Two statements sit in that one number. It is the rest energy of a particle whose mass is the anchor. It is also the energy a collision must reach for the weak interaction to be resolved as an exchange between currents rather than as a contact coupling: below it the $W$ is virtual and the force looks like a point.
  • The mass is $4.3893\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$. It is $262$ proton masses and $4.8\times10^{5}$ electron masses: many small masses to one anchor, which is why the fraction of a real particle is small.
  • The wave number is $1.9859\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, which is $1.99\times10^{14}$ turns of phase per millimetre.
  • The length is $5.0355\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{m}$, one turn of that phase, about one part in $170$ of a proton radius.
  • The time is $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$, one turn of that phase in time: the period of the clock the anchor keeps, and the time light takes to cross the anchor's length, $\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}/c$. One radian of it, $\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}/(2\pi)=2.6733\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{s}$, is the time of one radian of the phase, and it is written as the quotient it is and never as a symbol of its own, as the radian length is.

The phase is the physical content of the last three. A wave number is a rate of phase per unit length: the phase of a mode is $2\pi\nu x$, so $\nu$ counts turns per metre and $\lambda=1/\nu$ is the distance of one turn. The time is the same count in the other variable: the phase is $2\pi f_{\mathrm{frq}}t$ with $f_{\mathrm{frq}}=c\,\nu$ the frequency in inverse seconds, so $\tau=1/f_{\mathrm{frq}}=\lambda/c$ is the duration of one turn, and the length and the time anchors differ by the one factor $c$. A heavy particle turns quickly and has a short turn; a light particle turns slowly and has a long one. That is the whole of the reason the mass and the length run in opposite directions, and it is why the length fraction of §The Length Inverts is the reciprocal of the mass fraction.

The turn length limits localisation, and the factor matters. To fix a particle within a distance $\Delta x$ one superposes wave numbers up to $1/\Delta x$, and the momentum spread is $h/\Delta x$, so the energy of the spread is $h\,c/\Delta x$. At $\Delta x=\lambda_m$ that energy is

$$ \frac{h\,c}{\lambda_m}=\frac{m\,c^{2}}{2\pi}=0.159\,m\,c^{2}, $$

one radian's worth and not the whole rest energy. The spread reaches $m\,c^{2}$ at $\Delta x=\lambda_m/(2\pi)$, one radian of the turn, and it reaches the pair threshold $2m\,c^{2}$ at $\Delta x=\lambda_m/(4\pi)$, half a radian. So the turn $\lambda_m=h/(m\,c)$ is the length at which the spread is a sixth of the rest energy, and the length at which the particle stops being one particle is $\lambda_m/(2\pi)$, which the corpus writes as the quotient it is and never as a length of its own. This is why the electron's $2.426\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{m}$ and its radian $3.8616\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm{m}$ are quoted side by side with their definitions: the two differ by $2\pi$, and only the second is a threshold.

The Fraction

The rule. A number with its unit stands where a number is reported: a table, a measurement, a bound, a mass in kilograms. An equation is written in the ratios. In the particle-physics corpus, an equation that would otherwise carry a mass, an energy, a wave number, a length or a time carries the fraction instead, formed against the anchor of the same dimension:

$$ \frac{m_i}{m_{\mathrm{vev}}},\qquad \frac{E_i}{E_{\mathrm{vev}}},\qquad \frac{\nu_i}{\nu_{\mathrm{vev}}},\qquad \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}},\qquad \frac{\tau_i}{\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}}. $$

Each is a pure number. Because the division is by the anchor of the same dimension, no $c$ and no $h$ appears in the ratio, and a reader who wants a value multiplies by the anchor of that dimension, once.

Why the equation and not the table. The table reports and the equation relates. A table entry is read by someone who wants a number, and the number is best given with its unit; an equation is read as a relation, and a unit there does no work except to hide a $c$. A relation in fractions is true of any anchor, so its coefficients are numbers a reader checks by eye, and the mass values of the particles enter only when someone wants them and multiplies back.

Where the boundary falls. A derived quantity is written where it is derived and in fractions where it is used. The definition $\nu_m=m\,c/h$ keeps its constants, because it is the definition; a dispersion relation, a mass relation, a coupling and a cross-section ratio are written in fractions. An exercise may quote a beam energy in joules and must still write its invariant in fractions. The rule bites on equations, and an equation in this corpus has no electronvolt on the side of a mass.

At rest, one number. For a particle at rest the five ratios are not independent: with $E_i=m_ic^{2}$, $\nu_i=m_ic/h$ and $\tau_i=h/E_i$,

$$ \frac{E_i}{E_{\mathrm{vev}}}=\frac{m_i}{m_{\mathrm{vev}}}=\frac{\nu_i}{\nu_{\mathrm{vev}}}=:n_i, \qquad \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}}=\frac{\tau_i}{\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}}=\frac{1}{n_i}. $$

So one number $n_i$ serves for energy, mass and wave number, and its reciprocal serves for length and for time. In motion the energy and wave-number ratios are multiplied by the same factor, $E_i/E_{\mathrm{vev}}=\gamma n_i$, the mass ratio is unchanged, because mass is not a function of speed, and the time ratio is divided by that factor, because the phase of a moving particle runs faster and its turn is shorter.

The masses are the values the corpus quotes elsewhere: the charged leptons and the top at their pole values, the weak bosons and the Higgs at their measured values, and the fractions are computed with $E_{\mathrm{vev}}=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$.

The Yukawa vertex is the fraction. The Yukawa coupling of a fermion is defined against this scale and no other,

$$ y_i=\frac{\sqrt2\,m_i}{m_{\mathrm{vev}}}=\sqrt2\,n_i , $$

so the coupling and the fraction differ by $\sqrt2$ and by nothing else.

The fraction is the coupling, in every sector. The fermion case is one of three, and the three have one shape with three fixed factors, at tree level and with the coupling taken at the mass the row describes:

The first four rows are the reason the fraction is worth printing: written in $n_i$, a formula is written in the couplings the theory is built from, and a table of fractions is a table of couplings that a reader checks against measured masses by eye.

The third column does not agree to five figures, and the disagreement is the point. The couplings are quoted at $M_Z$, the scale at which they are usually tabulated, and the last column is the fraction the measured mass gives. The three rows differ by $0.13\%$, $0.32\%$ and $0.11\%$. Two things make that difference and neither is an error. The couplings run: $g$ at $M_Z$ is $0.6517$ and $g$ at $M_W$ is $2M_W/E_{\mathrm{vev}}=0.6529$, and the second reproduces the $W$ row by construction, which is the statement that the relation defines the coupling there. And the relation is tree level: the measured $W$ mass carries the radiative correction $\Delta r$, so $M_W=g\,E_{\mathrm{vev}}/2$ holds to the accuracy of that correction and no further. The identity is exact as a definition of what the fraction measures, and it is approximate as a numerical prediction. The article says which of the two it is using at each step, and the table is the one place where both columns are printed so that a reader can see the size of the gap.

The last row is the honest one. The proton has no coupling to the vacuum value, so its fraction has no expression in terms of one, and it is a measured number and nothing more. A table of fractions is a table of couplings wherever a coupling exists and a table of masses everywhere else, and the rule does not need the second case to be the first.

The fraction is the natural logarithm's argument. A ratio is what a logarithm, an exponential and a power take. An expression such as $n_i^{2}$ or $\ln n_i$ is defined; an expression such as $m_i^{2}$ is defined only with its unit attached, and a sum of two such expressions is defined only if the units agree. Writing the fraction removes the class of error where a dimensionally inhomogeneous sum is printed and read as a number.

The Length Inverts

The inversion is the one trap. Because $\lambda=1/\nu$, the length ratio is the reciprocal of the mass ratio, so the heaviest particle has the smallest length fraction and the largest mass fraction. A reader who takes $\lambda_i/\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}$ for a mass ratio gets the hierarchy upside down. The time ratio is the length ratio and not the mass ratio, $\tau_i/\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}=\lambda_i/\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}$, because $\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}/c$ and the one factor $c$ is the whole of the difference: the heaviest particle has the shortest period fraction, and the trap is set twice.

The numbers, checked against the corpus. The electron's wave-number fraction is $2.0754\times10^{-6}$, and multiplying by $\nu_{\mathrm{vev}}$ gives $4.1215\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, which is the value in The Three Wave Numbers of a Particle. Its length fraction is $4.8184\times10^{5}$, and multiplying by $\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}$ gives $2.4263\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{m}$, the electron's Compton length $h/(m_ec)$. Its time fraction is the same $4.8184\times10^{5}$, and multiplying by $\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}$ gives $8.0933\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{s}=h/(m_ec^{2})$, the Compton period $T_C$ of the same article. All three agree with the corpus's own table, which is the point of the check: the fractions carry the same physics as the values, and they carry it without a unit.

What the Fractions Do to an Equation

Koide, without a mass in it. The charged-lepton relation is usually printed as a sum of three masses over the square of a sum of their three square roots, and in that form it is invariant under a change of unit, which is a way of saying that the unit was never doing any work. Dividing by $m_1$ makes the invariance visible, because every mass then appears as a ratio and no unit is left to change:

$$ \frac{1+\rho_2+\rho_3}{\left(1+\sqrt{\rho_2}+\sqrt{\rho_3}\right)^{2}}=\frac{2}{3}, \qquad \rho_2=\frac{m_\mu}{m_e},\quad \rho_3=\frac{m_\tau}{m_e}. $$

In fractions of the vacuum value the same relation reads

$$ \frac{n_e+n_\mu+n_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{n_e}+\sqrt{n_\mu}+\sqrt{n_\tau}\right)^{2}}=\frac{2}{3}, $$

and the common factor $m_{\mathrm{vev}}$ has cancelled. The value is $0.666\,660\,5$ either way. The second form is the one that shows what the relation is: a statement about three pure numbers, with no scale in it at all.

The ladder, without a unit. The same holds for every ratio the flavour sector prints. The mass ratios are $1:206.768:3477.23$ and the square-root ratios are $1:14.3794:58.9680$; both are pure numbers, and both are unchanged if every mass is replaced by its fraction. What changes is only the appearance: a table of $n_i$ cannot be misread as a table of kilograms, and a formula in $n_i$ cannot acquire a stray $c$.

What the fractions do not say. A fraction carries the scale in its denominator and not in itself, so a fraction is only as good as the anchor it is divided by, and a running mass has a running fraction. The anchor is fixed by $G_F$ and does not run; the numerator does. An article that compares masses at different scales must name the scale of the numerator, exactly as it names the scheme today, and the fraction does not remove that obligation.

The Rules

The rules this article fixes, stated once.

  1. An equation is written in fractions. A formula that would otherwise carry a mass, an energy, a wave number, a length or a time carries the ratio against the anchor of the same dimension. A value with its unit belongs in a table, a measurement or a bound, and not on the side of an equation.
  2. An energy is written in joules. The electronvolt is refused, because its definition contains the elementary charge: a number in electronvolts is a number in units of $e$, and $e$ is a constant. An energy is quoted in joules wherever it appears, and a rest energy $m_ic^{2}$ is an energy and is quoted in joules. A time is quoted in seconds with its power of ten written out, for the same reason, and a prefixed unit of time is refused as a prefixed unit of length is.
  3. A mass is a fraction, and the kilogram is its definition. $m_i$ is written as $n_i=m_i/m_{\mathrm{vev}}$. The kilogram appears once, in the definition of $m_{\mathrm{vev}}$ above, and once in the conventions table for a pre-anchor denominator; it is not the form a table uses and it is not the result of a repair. A mass is never written as a bare number of electronvolts.
  4. A mass divided by the square of the speed of light is not allowed. The spelling is dimensionally correct — an electronvolt over $c^{2}$ is a mass — and it is still refused: it keeps a mass inside the electronvolt system, where the bare number is the next keystroke, and the corpus's base unit of mass is the kilogram. The rule is stated in words and the spelling is not printed, here or in any convention article, so that a search for it returns nothing but the defect it names. A mass that must be quoted absolutely is quoted in kilograms; a mass that is compared is quoted as a fraction.
  5. A root obeys the rule of its radicand. A root is admitted, and the unit under it is what the rule governs. $\sqrt{s}$, $\sqrt{E^{2}}$ and $\sqrt{m^{2}c^{4}+p^{2}c^{2}}$ are energies. $\sqrt{m_e}$ is $\mathrm{kg}^{1/2}$ when the mass is in the kilogram, and $\sqrt{n_e}$ is a number when it is a fraction; both are legal, because neither unit contains a constant. $\mathrm{kg}^{1/2}$ is a derived unit of one base unit, no more objectionable than $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ is of two. What is refused is a mass under a root in an energy unit: $\sqrt{m_e}=0.714\,842\ \mathrm{MeV}^{1/2}$ hides the $c$ of rule 3 inside the radical, where it is harder to see than in a bare number. A table that prints a root prints the parent mass beside it, in the kilogram or as a fraction, so that the root is a step and not a quantity presented on its own.
  6. No constant is set to one. $c$ and $h$ are carried with their units at every step, including inside the four conversions of §The Four Conversions. The anchors exist so that the constants never need to be dropped: the fraction divides by a quantity of the same dimension, which is the only way to remove a unit without removing a constant.

Where rule 4 bites, in one example. The parameter table of the corpus needs the electron mass in one cell. Under the refused spelling the cell is $0.510\,999$ with the electronvolt and the division by $c^{2}$ in the column header, and the division is then a unit and not an operation. The next author to reuse the number writes it in electronvolts again, because the column is already a mass; the third writes $0.511$ and calls it a mass; and by the fourth the table computes a Yukawa coupling from a number whose dimension was never on the page. The sequence is not a hypothesis: it is the census that this article's companion migration counts, with $708$ electronvolt lines to sort. Under rule 4 the cell is $2.0754\times10^{-6}$, and that number cannot decay into a bare electronvolt count, because its denominator is a mass.

What makes this spelling special is that it arrives pre-approved. The four-step decay is not peculiar to the electronvolt; any careless writer loses a dimension in any unit. What the electronvolt has, and no other unit of mass has, is a literature that has already normalised the slip. The tables of the field are printed in electronvolts and one reads them without noticing, so the defect does not look like carelessness: it looks like the standard notation, and a copy editor with no physics would leave it in place. A rule can only remove what a reader is willing to mark as wrong, and this is the one mass spelling a referee will pass. That is the whole of the argument for refusing it, and it is why the refusal is written here rather than left to taste: a defect that a competent reader does not see is the one a corpus must name.

What the rules cost. An absolute mass now carries a power of ten in kilograms, and a comparison carries a subscript. A formula now carries a denominator where a symbol stood. The cost is the price of one property: no equation in the corpus can contain a hidden $c=1$, because no equation contains an electronvolt on the side of a mass.

The Checks

Each is an arithmetic recomputation from the numbers above.

  1. The anchor from the Fermi coupling. $\left(\sqrt2\times4.5438\times10^{14}\right)^{-1/2}=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$, against the quoted $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$, with the quotient $G_F/\left(\frac{h}{2\pi}c\right)^{3}$ carrying the inverse energy squared.
  2. The mass from the energy. $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$ divided by $\left(2.997\,924\,58\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\right)^{2}$ gives $4.389\,27\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$, quoted above as $4.3893\times10^{-25}$.
  3. The mass times $c^{2}$. $4.3893\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$ times $c^{2}$ gives $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$.
  4. The wave number. $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$ divided by $1.986\,445\,86\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{J\cdot m}$ gives $1.985\,90\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
  5. The length. The reciprocal of $1.985\,90\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ is $5.035\,51\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{m}$.
  6. The wave number and the length are inverse. $1.985\,90\times10^{17}\times5.035\,51\times10^{-18}=1.0000$.
  7. The period from the energy. $6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$ divided by $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$ gives $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$.
  8. The period from the length. $5.0355\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{m}$ divided by $2.997\,924\,58\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ gives $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$, the same number, which is the equality $\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}=c\,\tau_{\mathrm{vev}}$ and not a coincidence of the arithmetic.
  9. The period and the energy are inverse. $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$ times $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$ is $6.6262\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$, which is $h$ to the five figures carried by the two factors; the deficit is the rounding of the period, and the same occurs in check 2. The reciprocal of the period is $5.9534\times10^{25}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, the frequency reading of the anchor.
  10. The wave number and the time differ by $c$. $1.9859\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ times $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$ is $3.3357\times10^{-9}$, and the reciprocal of $c$ is $3.3356\times10^{-9}$: the two ratios are one ratio and the speed of light, to the four figures the rounded factors carry.
  11. The electron's time fraction. $4.8184\times10^{5}$ times $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$ is $8.093\,5\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{s}$, against $h/(m_ec^{2})=8.0933\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{s}$, the Compton period; the two agree to four figures, the difference being the rounding of the period and of the fraction separately.
  12. The electron's mass fraction. $9.109\,383\,7\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$ divided by $4.3893\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$ is $2.0754\times10^{-6}$; times $m_{\mathrm{vev}}$ it gives $9.1094\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$.
  13. The electron's wave-number fraction. $2.0754\times10^{-6}$ times $\nu_{\mathrm{vev}}$ is $4.1215\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, the corpus's tabulated value.
  14. The electron's length fraction. The reciprocal of $2.0754\times10^{-6}$ is $4.8184\times10^{5}$; times $\lambda_{\mathrm{vev}}$ it is $2.4263\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{m}$.
  15. The top's coupling. $n_t=0.70136$, so $y_t=\sqrt2\times0.70136=0.991\,87$, a number below one, as a perturbative vertex must be.
  16. Koide is unchanged. In fractions the ratio is $0.666\,660\,5$; in kilograms it is $0.666\,660\,5$. The anchor cancels, which is the statement that the relation is a statement about numbers.
  17. The fraction is the coupling in three sectors, up to the running. With the couplings at $M_Z$, $g/2=0.3259$ against $n_W=0.32644$, a gap of $0.13\%$; $\sqrt{g^{2}+g'{}^{2}}/2=0.3715$ against $n_Z=0.37035$, a gap of $0.32\%$; $\sqrt{2\digamma_{\mathrm{qtc}}}=\sqrt{0.2582}=0.5081$ against $n_H=0.50869$, a gap of $0.11\%$. With $g$ at $M_W$, $g/2=0.6529/2=0.32644$ and the $W$ row is exact, because the relation defines $g$ there. A gap of a part in a thousand across two scales is what a running coupling looks like, and it is not a defect of the identity.
  18. The two tables are one table. Every entry of §The Fraction multiplied by its anchor returns the value quoted in §The Four Conversions and in the corpus, in opposite directions; checks 13 and 14 are two instances, and the article quotes the same numbers twice on purpose.

Summary

The vacuum value is one number with five readings: an energy $3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$, a mass $4.3893\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$, a wave number $1.9859\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, a length $5.0355\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{m}$, and a time $1.6797\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{s}$. The four conversions between them are $E=mc^{2}$, $\nu=E/(hc)$, $\lambda=1/\nu$ and $\tau=h/E$, written with the constants present. Every equation of these five kinds is then written in fractions of the anchor, so that it carries pure numbers and no unit, while a table may still quote a value with its unit. At rest the five fractions are one number and its reciprocal.

Three statements carry the physics. The anchor is a value the scalar field takes everywhere in the vacuum, and a mass is a coupling to that background multiplied by the value, so a fraction of the anchor is a coupling in units of the background. The length reading of the same number is the distance in which the mass phase completes one turn, which is the smallest distance at which the particle is one particle, and the time reading is that turn divided by $c$, the period of the same phase. And the length and the time are reciprocal to the mass fraction, so the heaviest particle has the largest fraction, the smallest length and the shortest period.

What the scheme changes is the spelling and not the physics. An energy is quoted in joules, a time in seconds and a mass in kilograms or as a fraction; the electronvolt is refused for both an energy and a mass, and the spelling that divides an electronvolt by the square of the speed of light is refused along with the bare number it leads to. The result is that a hidden $c=1$ has nowhere to stand: the fraction divides by a mass, and a mass is a kilogram.

Internal Note: Why This Article Was Demoted

This section is a status marker and can be deleted as one block. It is the last section of the file and nothing above it refers to it. Delete it and the article stands as a self-consistent note on a spelling convention. It is not part of the argument, and it states no result.

The reason, in the author's words. I am suspicious about the physics value of that: there is no real constant, this is just an example of a thing. The suspicion is correct on the point it names. The article presents an anchor, and the anchor is not a new constant, not a discovered scale, and not a quantity the corpus introduced. It is a renaming.

The anchor is the Higgs vacuum expectation value, repackaged. $E_{\mathrm{vev}}=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$ is $v\approx246\ \mathrm{GeV}$ multiplied by the conversion of the electronvolt to the joule, and $m_{\mathrm{vev}}$ is that number divided by $c^{2}$. The chain is one line: $246.22\ \mathrm{GeV}\times1.602\,176\,634\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J\,GeV^{-1}}=3.9449\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{J}$. The article says as much in §The Anchor, where it calls the number the corpus's $v$; what it does not say plainly enough is that everything after that is bookkeeping. Five readings of one number are four unit conversions and one inversion, and the tables are those conversions carried out.

Constant is the wrong word for it. The value is fixed by $G_F$, $G_F$ is fixed by the muon lifetime, and the muon lifetime is measured. That much the article gets right. But the same is true of every parameter of the Standard Model, and none of them is called an anchor. Saying "write every mass as a fraction of $v$" is the same kind of statement as "write every velocity as a fraction of $c$": legal, occasionally useful, and not a finding.

The defect it claims to repair is a notation preference. Rules 2 and 4 refuse the electronvolt and the spelling that divides an electronvolt by $c^{2}$. Natural units are a convention that the field adopted because the numbers are readable at the TeV scale, not an error that hides a physics mistake. A corpus that writes joules and kilograms is making a defensible choice for a document that also carries $h$ and $c$ explicitly; it is not repairing a defect in the literature, and the article's tone on that point overstates its case.

The physical interpretation is not uniform, and the article admits it. A mass is a coupling to the background for the fermions, the $W$ and the $Z$, and for the Higgs through the curvature of the potential. It is not for the proton, whose mass is QCD binding energy and would survive a vanishing $v$. The article states this in §What the Vacuum Value Is and then normalises the proton against the same anchor anyway. The fraction is a unit there and not a coupling, so the word "coupling" carries less than the article's framing suggests.

What survives the suspicion. The arithmetic is correct: every one of the eighteen checks recomputes, the four conversions carry their constants, the Koide ratio is unchanged in fractions, and the electron's three fractions return the corpus's tabulated Compton length, wave number and period. The physics content is real, if standard: the VEV, the mass ratios, Koide. What does not survive is the claim of significance. The article is a writing convention dressed as a scale, which is why it now sits in the internal collection, out of the menu and out of the sitemaps, rather than among the physics articles. The symbol has since been purged from the seven physics articles that quoted it: they now write masses in kilograms and use the corpus's $v$, in kilograms, where a scale is needed.

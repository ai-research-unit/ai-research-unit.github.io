Internal Note: Coupling Issues — The Pati–Salam Failure, and What the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model Does With It

The Answer

The model removes the coupling failure and keeps the mass failure. Those are two different problems that share one root, and the corpus fixes only the first.

problem what it is in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ model verdict
the coupling problem one boundary value, $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at one scale, against three measured couplings the equality is not asserted; $g_4$ is matched to measured $\alpha_s$ at $M_X$ removed, at the price of predicting no angle
the mass problem one condensate against twelve masses the condensate is kept unchanged, so the ratio $m_\ell=3m_q$ survives kept, and falsified by $27$

The root is one sentence. A single multiplet carries quarks and leptons, so one coupling and one vacuum must serve both. The coupling relation can be dropped and the multiplet is still shared. The vacuum relation cannot be dropped, because it is the vacuum.

What the reader should take away. The $\tfrac38$ of Pati–Salam is not wrong. It is a normalisation, and it is exact wherever the group is restored. What is wrong is the extra sentence that turns it into a prediction. The corpus drops that sentence and leaves the normalisation standing. It does not touch the mass relation, because the factor three is not a coupling.

1. Two Problems, and Why They Are Kept Apart

They live in different places. The coupling problem is a statement about the covariant derivative. The mass problem is a statement about the vacuum. No change to the first can reach the second, and §5 is the proof.

The coupling problem, in one line. Above the breaking the group is simple, so there is one coupling. Below it there are three measured couplings. One boundary value against three measurements is over-determined by one, and no scale fits.

The mass problem, in one line. The same Higgs multiplet couples to the down quark and to the charged lepton, so their masses are equal at the unification scale. They are not.

Why a reader reports the second as "something with the mass". Because the second is the one still standing. The corpus states the first as a solved problem, and states the second as a falsified prediction. A reader who looks for the failure finds the mass.

2. The Coupling Problem, Stated Exactly

2.1 What is exact, and what is only assumed

The normalisation is a trace identity. $B-L$ is the fifteenth Cartan generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, its trace norm is $\operatorname{tr}(T^{15})^{2}=\tfrac12$, and the consequence is

$$ g_{B-L}^{2}=\tfrac38\,g_4^{2}\quad\text{at }M_C . $$

Composing it with the hypercharge sum gives the two numbers the literature quotes. With $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ and $g_R=g_L=g_4$,

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^{2}}=\frac{1}{g_4^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\cdot\tfrac38 g_4^{2}}=\frac{5}{3g_4^{2}}, \qquad e^{2}=\tfrac38 g_4^{2}, \qquad \sin^{2}\theta_W=\tfrac38 . $$

Nothing in that display can be contradicted. A trace identity is not a measurement. The falsifiable sentence is a second one, written beside it and often written as if it came with it: that the three couplings are equal at one scale. That sentence is a boundary condition, and the corpus calls the whole subject "the Pati–Salam coupling problem" when it means this sentence.

So the failure belongs to an assumption and not to the group. Pati–Salam is a group, sixteen fields and a chain. The equality is an addition, and the corpus's own older text marked it as conditional on parity: the four couplings "collapse to two, or to one", with $g_L=g_R$ marked "(if parity is imposed)". That mark is the ground on which §4.1 justifies dropping it.

2.2 The failure, in two numbers

The corpus computes what the equality costs. Ask which scale makes each half of the condition true:

condition the scale where it holds what is wrong there
$\sin^{2}\theta_W=\tfrac38$ $1.63\times10^{3}$ J $\alpha_3^{-1}=36.8$ against $\alpha_L^{-1}=42.4$, colour $13\%$ short
$g_4=g_L$ $1.57\times10^{7}$ J $\sin^{2}\theta_W=0.4365$ against $\tfrac38$, $16\%$ high

The two scales differ by a factor of $9630$. One boundary value cannot satisfy two conditions four decades apart, and that is the whole of the failure.

Why it fails, in one sentence. The condition ties a coupling that runs at $c_4=\tfrac{32}{3}$ to one that runs at $c_2=\tfrac{19}{6}$, and the difference accumulates with the matter between the thresholds. The failure is not arithmetic; it is the statement that the equality is not preserved by the content.

2.3 The number usually quoted third

The angle at $M_Z$ is the popular form of the same failure, and it is weaker evidence. Imposing one scale gives $\sin^{2}\theta_W(M_Z)$ between $0.21$ and $0.33$ depending on that scale, against the measured $0.23122$. The internal note's §4.2 tabulates it; the article the-one-coupling-that-returns-the-standard-model.md prefers the two-scale form of §2.2, because two numbers at two named scales are harder to dismiss than one range.

3. The Mass Problem, Stated Exactly

3.1 What Pati–Salam predicts

One bidoublet, one vacuum value, two masses. The down quark and the charged lepton sit in the same $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ multiplet and receive their mass from the same Higgs coupling, so at the unification scale

$$ m_d=m_e,\qquad m_s=m_\mu,\qquad m_b=m_\tau . $$

The third holds roughly and the first two do not. $m_b=m_\tau$ is approximately right once both masses are run to the unifying scale; $m_d=m_e$ and $m_s=m_\mu$ are wrong by factors of a few. The standard repair is the Georgi–Jarlskog factor, and it costs a $\mathbf{15}$ of Higgs. Five files in articles_external/Pati-Salam/ state the pattern, and three of them state the repair; the corpus records the architecture in one sentence: the GUT creates the problem and the flavour symmetry repairs it.

3.2 What this corpus predicts instead

The same disease with a different number. The scalar is one Hermitian $4\times4$ matrix, its minimum is traceless, and the shape is fixed up to one scale:

$$ \Xi_0=v\,D,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1),\qquad \operatorname{tr}D=0 . $$

The fermion on the lepton line is therefore three times heavier than the fermion on a colour line with the same Yukawa eigenvalue:

$$ m_\ell=3\,m_q\quad\text{within a copy}. $$

Why three and not one is a fact about the quartet and not a choice. The vacuum must be traceless, the quartet has four directions, and a $+1$ on the three colour directions forces $-3$ on the fourth. Pati–Salam's equality comes from the same entry acting on both slots; the corpus's factor three comes from the tracelessness of the entry. One multiplet, two bookkeepings, two different numbers.

The verdict is in the model's own words. $m_e/m_d$ is predicted as $3$; the measurement gives $0.109$; the ratio of the two is $27.5$. The neutrino is worse: $m_\nu=3m_u$ against a bound below $10^{-6}$, and there is no see-saw because a Majorana term would need $\xi\xi$, which $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4$ does not contain.

Two further consequences of the same single matrix, stated so that the boundary is complete. No generation index means no hierarchy, so $m_u/m_c$ is absent. One matrix and not two means the CKM product is $W^{\dagger}W=\mathbb{1}$ and the Jarlskog invariant is zero, so $CP$ is conserved exactly.

3.3 The two problems share one root

One multiplet, one vacuum, one Yukawa matrix, one copy index. Every mass relation above is a consequence of that list, and every coupling relation of §2 is a consequence of the same list read on the derivative. A theory that keeps the multiplet keeps the disease, whichever way it treats the couplings.

4. How the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model Handles the Coupling Problem

4.1 The strip: one relation kept, two dropped

The corpus keeps the trace identity and drops the equality. Stated as a count:

coupling what fixes it above $M_C$ what fixes it below
$g_4$ the one coupling of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ equal to $g_3(M_C)$, because $\mathrm{SU}(3)\subset\mathrm{SU}(4)$
$g_{B-L}$ not independent $g_{B-L}^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}$ at $M_C$, a trace identity
$g_L$ not present free
$g_R$ not present solved from the matching, not measured

The one relation that survives is not a prediction, and that is why it survives. $g_4=g_3(M_C)$ is a relation between two couplings at a scale. No experiment measures a number that it fixes, so the strip cannot be contradicted through it. What is dropped is exactly the two relations that fix numbers: $g_L=g_4$ and $g_R=g_4$.

The gain and the loss, in one line each. Gain: a relation that is not asserted cannot be violated, and the model contains the Standard Model by construction below $M_R$. Loss: the model predicts no value of $\sin^{2}\theta_W$, and the $\tfrac38$ becomes a normalisation instead of a test.

4.2 The value comes from data, and the article says so

$g_4$ is not derived. It is matched. The identification is

$$ g_4^{2}=4\pi\,\alpha_s(M_X), $$

with $\alpha_s$ run up from the measured $\alpha_s(M_Z)=0.1179$ through Standard Model content. At $M_X=3.73\times10^{-5}$ J this gives $g_4^{2}=0.730$, hence $\alpha_4(M_X)=0.0581$ and $1/\alpha_4=17.21$.

The status is graded in the article's own ledger: the single value on fifteen generators is structural, the matching to $\alpha_s$ is imported, the coefficient and the running are computed, and the pole is arithmetic. Only one line of that ledger is data, and the claim is that nothing else about the coupling is free.

The strict matching is at $M_C$, and the corpus imposes it at $M_X$. The difference is one interval of running, $\Delta(1/\alpha)=0.265\ln(M_X/M_C)$; at the corpus's own pair of scales it is $0.019$ against $17.21$, a tenth of a per cent. That is inside the truncation the article already declares.

4.3 $g_R$ is solved, and it comes out below $g_L$

One equation, one unknown, and the unknown is the one coupling no experiment measures. Hypercharge is the mixture $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, so the matching

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^{2}}=\frac{1}{g_R^{2}}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^{2}} $$

is solved for $g_R$ at the chosen scales. The solution exists only while the bracket is positive, and over the admissible region $g_R/g_L$ runs from $0.62$ to $0.73$.

Two numbers that differ from the unified version, and both are consequences of the strip. $g_R<g_L$ where Pati–Salam has equality, and $M_X/M_{W_R}$ lies between $2.2$ and $3.1$ where Pati–Salam has $\sqrt2$. The $g_R$ value is the one number the model produces rather than assumes, and it is produced by the requirement that hypercharge come out right.

The edge of the region is not experimental. It is the requirement that $\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ stay strong enough for the harmonic sum to be real: a factor $M_C/M_R$ up to about $10^{6}$–$10^{7}$, moving with the $B-L$ coefficient, which is $12$ for the minimal content. A model that exceeded the edge would have no real $g_R$; that is a statement about the model and not about nature.

4.4 The running above $M_C$, which the strip leaves untouched

The strip removes relations at $M_C$ and changes nothing above it. The coefficient is

$$ b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_A-\frac{4}{3}T_RN_f-\frac{1}{6}T_Sn_s =\frac{44}{3}-\frac{16}{3}-\frac{2}{3}=\frac{26}{3}=8.667 , $$

with $C_A=4$, eight Dirac fermions from the sixteen Weyl fields, and one real adjoint scalar of sixteen components at the real-scalar weight. Four consequences, each a number:

  • $b_0>0$, so the phase is asymptotically free and $\alpha_4$ falls from $0.0581$ at $M_X$ to $0.0165$ at $M_P$.
  • The expansion is good: $\alpha_4/\pi=0.018$ at the transition, so the two-loop term is about two per cent of the change, which is what makes the tree-level rates legitimate.
  • Nothing confines, because the coupling grows only downward and the phase ends at $M_C$ first.
  • The pole at $885\ \mathrm{MeV}$ is arithmetic: $\ln(\mu/M_X)=-17.21/1.379=-12.48$. It lies five and a half decades below $M_C$, where the coefficient is the Standard Model's, so the line of that article is not the line the theory follows.

The scalar term is the one place the coefficient reads the content, and it reads it sharply: sixteen real components give $\tfrac{26}{3}$, a complex matrix gives $8$, and a second label gives $\tfrac{20}{3}$. Each doubling of the scalar count costs more than the two-loop term the article omits, so the count is not decoration.

4.5 What the move may and may not be called

It may be called a containment. Below $M_R$ the group is exactly the Standard Model's, the sixteen reduce to the fifteen plus $\nu_R$, and the measured couplings are reproduced by construction. Every measurement the Standard Model passes, this model passes, for the same reason.

It may not be called a repair of Pati–Salam. A repair would make the unified condition true. The corpus makes it unasserted. The honest description is a smaller theory that fits, and the internal note's phrase for it is a model with one claim fewer.

It may not be called a prediction of the angle. The $\tfrac38$ survives as the trace normalisation of one Cartan generator. Read as a prediction it is falsified; read as a normalisation it is exact and idle.

5. What the Same Move Does to the Mass Problem: Nothing

The factor three is not a coupling, and the strip removes no entry from $D$. This is the sentence the whole comparison rests on, so it gets a proof in three steps.

Step one: the shape of $D$ is fixed by two numbers that have nothing to do with $g$. The vacuum is traceless, which is one condition, and it sits on a quartet, which is dimension four. A traceless diagonal entry with three equal colour directions is $\operatorname{diag}(a,a,a,-3a)$ up to scale, and the scale is $v$. No coupling enters either condition.

Step two: $y$ multiplies both lines. The mass matrix is

$$ M_{\alpha\beta}^{\ \ \ \imath\jmath}=y_{\alpha\beta}\,v\,D_{\imath}\,\delta_{\imath\jmath}, $$

so whatever $y$ does, it does to the lepton line and the colour line alike. The ratio $-3$ against $+1$ is therefore invariant under every change of the Yukawa sector that the model allows.

Step three: the strip acts on the derivative. Dropping $g_L=g_R=g_4$ changes which couplings multiply the generators. The vacuum value $v$ is fixed by the scalar potential, and the entries of $D$ by the trace condition. Neither is a gauge coupling.

The conclusion, stated without softening. The model trades a falsified coupling prediction for a falsified mass prediction, and it keeps the second. It is not a net gain in agreement with experiment; it is a net gain in consistency, bought with predictivity.

What would be needed to touch the mass problem, as a requirement and not a promise. A second condensate, so that the lepton line and the colour lines can receive different factors. A second Yukawa matrix, so that a phase can survive and the CKM product is not the identity. A generation index, so that a hierarchy can exist. None of the three is in the model, and the corpus's claim is only that the three are necessary.

Why the Georgi–Jarlskog route is not available here. That repair inserts a $\mathbf{15}$ of Higgs and lets the two sectors take different components of it. Here the scalar is one Hermitian matrix and its minimum has a single shape; there is no second component to assign differently. The repair needs the multiplet list that §1.5 of the withdrawn note deliberately leaves open — which is to say, it needs a model larger than this one.

7. The Strongest Objection

The objection, in its strongest form. A relation that is dropped because it fails has not been solved. The model has deleted the test it could not pass, and no experiment can now distinguish it from the Standard Model plus heavy fields.

Half of that is right, and the half that is right is the price. The strip does delete a prediction. The corpus says so in every article that touches it: no angle, no ratio, no unification statement. A reader who wants the $\tfrac38$ tested must keep the equality and accept that the unified version is falsified. That position is defensible, and the corpus records it rather than hiding it.

The other half is wrong, and two derivations answer it. The model still says something the Standard Model does not: $3q+\ell=0$, hence $Q_p+Q_e=0$, because $B-L$ is traceless on the quartet and therefore a generator; and the sixteen with $\nu_R$ in $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ with the measured hypercharges. Both follow from the group and neither depends on a coupling value. The proton lifetime is dimension nine and unobservable, so the model is not testable below the leptoquark floor — and that is a statement about reach, not about content.

What remains uncomfortable, stated once. The one contact with a measurement is $K_L\to\mu e$, and it gives a floor on $M_X$ of about $2\times10^{2}$ TeV, not a signal. A null result is what a hundred models say. The corpus must not read it as support.

9. Summary

The corpus's $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ model is a Pati–Salam group with the unified boundary condition removed. The group, the sixteen, the chain, the twenty-one bosons, the six leptoquarks, charge quantisation and the $K_L$ selection rule are unchanged. Two relations are dropped, $g_L=g_4$ and $g_R=g_4$; one is kept, $g_4=g_3(M_C)$; and one identity survives, $g_{B-L}^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}$.

The coupling problem is removed and the mass problem is not. The coupling failure came from imposing one value on three measured couplings, and a relation that is not asserted cannot be violated. The mass failure came from one condensate serving quarks and leptons, and the condensate is untouched by the strip: $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ is fixed by tracelessness on a quartet, and no gauge coupling enters it.

The exchange rate is stated plainly. The model gives up its prediction of $\sin^{2}\theta_W$ and keeps its prediction $m_\ell=3m_q$. The first was wrong at the few-per-cent level in two different places; the second is wrong by $27.5$. Consistency improved; agreement with experiment did not.

What is computed rather than imported. $b_0=\tfrac{26}{3}$, the running to $\alpha_4(M_P)=0.0165$, the pole at $885\ \mathrm{MeV}$, the ratio $g_R/g_L$ in $0.62$–$0.73$, and the ratio $M_X/M_{W_R}$ in $2.2$–$3.1$. What is imported: the one number $g_4^{2}=4\pi\alpha_s(M_X)$, and with it the whole scale of the model.

The open door, named once. A second condensate, a second Yukawa matrix and a generation index would each touch the mass problem. None is in the model, and no article in the corpus pretends otherwise.

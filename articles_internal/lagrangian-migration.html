Lagrangian Migration: One Title, One Structure, and the List of Work

Introduction

The claim under test. In the standard theory the Lagrangian is the object and the equations follow from it. The free equations are the quadratic truncation of one Lagrangian, with every interaction vertex discarded. QCD is the clean case: one Lagrangian, four pieces — gauge kinetic, quark, gauge fixing, ghost — and four vertices — quark–gluon, three-gluon, four-gluon, ghost–gluon — and those four exhaust the tree-level content. If that is the right way to organise a theory, the corpus should carry one article of that shape per sector, all built the same way and all named the same way.

What the corpus did. It had the equations first and reconstructed an action for each, one at a time, under thirty-eight titles that shared no prefix. A reader could not tell that the family existed.

What was done in this pass. All forty-six articles are renamed to one pattern, in four passes: the thirty-eight title-convention renames, the eight equation-limit renames, the four theory-first inversions, and the eight fluid renames from of the to and the. Every title in the family now begins with the words The Lagrangian. The eight field articles that answer which action has this equation as its Euler–Lagrange equation became The Lagrangian and the … Equation; the polar, momentum-space and non-quantum-limit readings became suffixes on the same stem; the sector articles became The Lagrangian of …. Two hundred and sixty-nine slug occurrences and four hundred and thirteen title occurrences were rewritten across physics.md, the articles and the internal notes, and all six hundred and thirty-eight anchors of physics.md still resolve.

The numbers now. Forty-six articles in the family. Eight are the fluid Lagrangians, already conforming. Thirty-two are the four equations in eight readings each. Six are single articles: mechanics, the action principle in the non-quantum limit, one relativistic particle, and three sectors. All forty-six are listed in physics.md. One more article belongs to the argument rather than to the family — The Action Problem — and it is in no menu at all.

The verdict in one line. The naming is uniform now and the architecture is uniform; coverage is not. Every massive field of spin $0$, $\tfrac12$ and $1$ has its action; Maxwell, QED and the electroweak sector do not, and the list at the end says what to write.

The Title Convention

Rule one: the family prefix is The Lagrangian. No article in the family begins with anything else. The prefix makes the family visible in an alphabetical listing, in the menu and in a search, and it states the corpus's position: the action is the object, the equation is its consequence.

Rule two: and joins, and every title in the family now uses it. The earlier version of this note split the family in two — and for equations, of for objects, theories and sectors — and the split was a mistake. It produced The Lagrangian of the Spin-Zero Fluid beside The Lagrangian and the Klein–Gordon Equation, which reads as two conventions where there is one relation. The relation is the same in both: a functional and the thing it describes. Of was marking possession where the corpus means conjunction, and it also split the alphabetical run of the family in two at the letter o.

Form Used for Example
The Lagrangian and X X is the object, equation, principle, limit, theory or sector the functional is written for The Lagrangian and the Klein–Gordon Equation; The Lagrangian and the Spin-Zero Fluid; The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics
**X and the Lagrangian X carries more weight in the article than the functional, and the article has a subtitle Quantum Chromodynamics and the Lagrangian: The Triplet, the Octet and the Feynman Rules

The second form is used four times and only four. In the QCD, Standard Model, chiral and relativistic-particle articles the Lagrangian is one section among fifteen and the subject is the theory; inverting the order says that. In the eight fluid articles the action is the subject of nearly every section, so the prefix stays at the front. That is the whole criterion, and it is a claim about the article's centre of gravity, not about grammar.

Rule three: the equation is named in full, and the reading is a suffix. Four equations — Klein–Gordon, Dirac, Proca, Schrödinger — and four readings.

The Titles That Carry No Prefix, and Why

The first version of this note answered the question too narrowly. It counted the forty-six files whose slug begins the-lagrangian, which is circular: the count confirms the rename and says nothing about the rest of the section. The honest audit starts from the whole physics menu — six hundred and thirty-eight anchors over six hundred and forty-four files — and asks of every article that displays an action or a Lagrangian density whether its title says so.

The answer, measured. Forty-six titles contain the word Lagrangian, and every one of them carries the family prefix: forty-two The Lagrangian and …, and four that put the theory first, … and the Lagrangian: …. Nothing uses The Lagrangian of any longer, and no title carries Lagrangian in the middle with a third element after it. So the family prefix is complete on the family. The defect the complaint points at is elsewhere, and it is real.

Three grids over the same eight parents carry no Lagrangian prefix, and all three are right not to.

The check is a grep, not an opinion. A search for the Lagrangian density $\mathcal{L}$ across those fifty articles returns nothing; a search across the forty-six family articles returns it in every one. The two grids answer different questions about the same parent: the equation grid asks what the operator does to a mode, the Lagrangian grid asks what functional has that equation as its stationary condition. A title that said Lagrangian over an equation-level article would be false, and the corpus avoids a false title more readily than it avoids an untidy one.

The Common Structure of a Lagrangian Article

Seventeen slots, and the family keeps eleven of them today. The headings of the thirty-three field articles, read side by side, give a sequence that is stable in its first three slots and its last four, and unstable in the middle. The instability is not disorder. It is the same eight questions asked under different names, and the architecture below names them once, then splits them into the eleven every article needs and the six only some articles can fill.

What a Rewrite Would Cost, and the Rule That Costs Nothing

The measurements. The thirty-three articles carry three hundred and seventy-two sections between them, a mean of eleven per article, and three thousand six hundred words each.

Measurement Value
sections in the family 372
distinct section headings 156
headings used exactly once 129
sections that are the common scaffolding (introduction, conventions, summary, notation, further reading, open questions, limits, verification) 180 — 48 per cent
sections that carry the article's own content 192
display equations 422, mean 13 per article
shortest article the coupled Proca limit, 1,728 words, 9 sections, 3 displays

The finding, and it is the reason the architecture has to grow. Half of every article is scaffolding that the template already governs. The other half is a set of sections with names that appear once in the corpus: Reality of the Action Is the Anti-Hermitian Root, The Ordering Ambiguity Is the Cross Term, The Trace Is the Mass, Why the Parent's Test Field Had Two Modes. Those are not decoration. Each one is a claim with a computation attached. A rewrite that kept only eleven slots would have nowhere to put them, and the natural, lazy outcome would be to fold them into The Structural Consequence as a paragraph and lose the computation.

So the answer to the question is: no, nothing needs to be purged, and nothing should be. But the guarantee is not automatic — it rests on five rules, and on one mechanism: the heading map of item 4, written as a file and checked by the script of item 5.

  1. A section is never deleted, only renamed. Every heading of the old article is mapped to a slot of the new architecture, or is kept under its own name after slot 7 as a result section. The mapping is written down before the rewrite starts, one line per heading.
  2. A reading's own blocks are mandatory in that reading. The polar article that drops The Four Brackets and the momentum article that drops Zero and Pole are not shorter; they are incomplete.
  3. The displays are the claims, and their count may not fall. An article that enters the rewrite with thirteen displays and leaves with nine has lost four claims, whatever the prose says. This is the mechanical check that catches the quiet loss.
  4. Word count within ten per cent, and the direction that matters is downward. A rewrite that grows is padding; one that shrinks by a third has cut content.
  5. A move is allowed only with a named destination and a pointer left behind. Two sections in the family qualify, and the list records them.

The two genuine moves.

Section Article Destination
Exact Solutions and What They Show the Proca action the Proca equation article, which owns the solutions; the action article keeps one sentence and a pointer

Everything else stays where it is. The temptation to centralise is real and should be resisted: The Ordering Ambiguity Is the Cross Term cannot be moved to a central article on ordering, because it is a statement about the coupled Klein–Gordon action in momentum space and about nothing else. A result belongs to the article that computed it.

The Ninth Family: The Eight Fluids, and Why They Get a Chart Structure

The fluid articles are Lagrangian articles and were the largest omission from the first version of this note. Madelung derived the hydrodynamic form of the Schrödinger equation in 1926 and Takabayasi the relativistic spin-$\tfrac12$ fluid in 1957 — the corpus holds that paper in _reserve — and both derivations are variational. The fluid is what the action looks like when it is written in density and phase instead of in the field. There is no obstacle to a Lagrangian theory of the fluid. There is only a different chart.

The chart, in one table. It differs from the seventeen slots in five places and agrees in the rest.

Five differences, and each is forced. The fluid has two variations and not one. It has a substitution before it has an action. Its central object is a term that does not appear in the field writing. Its canonical table is the thing that fails. And its literature is a named pair of authors rather than a textbook.

What the two families share is larger than what separates them. Both write one functional. Both recover their equations by variation and do not assume them. Both must name the constraint that the action imposes and separate it from the choice that the chart supplies. Both owe an honest ledger of what the rewriting relabels. The polar article of the field family and the fluid article of this family use the same substitution $\varrho e^{i\theta}$ — and there the families nearly touch. The difference is one step: the polar article keeps the frame, the sector projectors and the whole element, and varies it; the fluid article discards the frame and keeps only density and phase as the physical variables. So the fluid is not a ninth subject. It is the polar chart pushed one step further, with the frame thrown away, and the two must say so in their own words: the polar article in slot 6, that the frame survives the substitution; the fluid article in slot 3, that it does not.

What the fluid articles have that the field articles do not. A pure relabelling claim, stated as a theorem rather than a caveat: The Lagrangian and the Schrödinger Fluid proves that its action is the same functional in two charts. That is slot 12 at full strength, and none of the thirty-four field articles can make the claim in the same form, because none of them re-charts anything.

What they lack. No grid. The thirty-two field articles are eight parents crossed with four readings; the fluid articles are eight charts, one per spin–charge combination, and there is no reading axis and no coupled axis among them. That is why they are eight and not thirty-two, and why a uniform seventeen slots would have forced six empty sections into each.

The family is one-particle, and the corpus is not. That is not a defect of the architecture; it is a boundary that must be drawn in writing, because the architecture will otherwise grow every time the boundary is noticed.

What that article buys, for one article's cost. It turns the sixteen into a derived layer instead of a parallel one, and it gives the family's twenty-four open questions a target: three of them are the same question — what happens to this object when the field is promoted. It also settles, in one place, the choice the biquaternion algebra forces, which is currently made separately in six canonical-quantisation articles without cross-reference.

Six one-line edits follow it. Each canonical-quantisation article gets a pointer to the bridge in its Open Questions, and each Fock-space article gets a pointer backwards. Six lines, no rewrite.

The boundary, written as one sentence for the note and for the template. The seventeen slots govern the one-particle layer; a second-quantised article is built on the bridge, not on the seventeen. That sentence is the freeze. Without it the template will keep growing, as it grew twice while this note was being revised: once for the fluids, once for this layer.

The Three Pathologies

One: the equation comes first, so the action is reconstructed backwards. The eight element-form articles begin from an equation written by an earlier article and ask which action produces it. The eight polar articles substitute the polar word into that action; sixteen more read it in momentum space or take a limit on it. The family is a tree rooted at the equation. The consequence is that the corpus cannot discover that two equations share one Lagrangian, because no article starts from a Lagrangian and derives two equations. The Proca action at $k_m=0$ is the Maxwell action, and no article says so.

Two: the sector pieces are scattered. The electroweak sector has no Lagrangian article, but its pieces are all written: the gauge kinetic terms, the charged and neutral currents and the trilinear self-couplings in The W and Z Bosons; the scalar kinetic term and the masses it gives in Electroweak Symmetry Breaking; the fermion masses in The Yukawa Sector; the potential in The Higgs Mechanism; the gauge-fixing and ghost terms in Gauge Fixing in the Electroweak Theory. Five articles, one Lagrangian, no assembly.

Three: the two complete sector articles are the two that admit they are transcriptions. Quantum Chromodynamics and the Lagrangian states that its four pieces and four vertices are standard theory transcribed in the corpus's coordinates. The Standard Model and the Lagrangian states the same and adds a table comparing the standard object with the corpus object row by row. That honesty is the model to copy: a sector article's value is the ledger of what is derived, what is checked and what is imported.

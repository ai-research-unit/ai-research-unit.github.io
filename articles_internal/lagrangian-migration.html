Lagrangian Migration: One Title, One Structure, and the List of Work

Introduction

The claim under test. In the standard theory the Lagrangian is the object and the equations follow from it. The free equations are the quadratic truncation of one Lagrangian, with every interaction vertex discarded. QCD is the clean case: one Lagrangian, four pieces — gauge kinetic, quark, gauge fixing, ghost — and four vertices — quark–gluon, three-gluon, four-gluon, ghost–gluon — and those four exhaust the tree-level content. A corpus organised that way carries one article of that shape per sector, all built the same way and all named the same way.

What the corpus did instead. It had the equations first and reconstructed an action for each, one at a time, under thirty-eight titles that shared no prefix. Three faults followed. The family was a tree rooted at the equation, so no article could start from a functional and derive two equations. The five articles that held the pieces of one sector Lagrangian never met, so the assembly existed nowhere. And the only two articles that completed a sector were the two that admitted they were transcriptions.

What the family is now. Sixty articles, one prefix, one structure, plus six hubs. Thirty-two are four equations in two couplings and four readings; eight are fluid charts; ten are sector or gauge actions; six are effective actions of matter; four stand alone — Pauli, Rarita–Schwinger, linearised gravity, second quantisation. Five more sit beside the count and carry no prefix: the level-0 article, the three that put the theory first, and the sector hub. All sixty family articles are filed in physics.md.

The verdict. The naming is uniform, the architecture is uniform, the coverage is complete for every field the corpus treats. No article of the family quantises: the slots govern the one-particle layer, and the second-quantised layer sits on one bridge article.

The boundary sentence, which is the freeze. The eleven slots govern the one-particle layer; a second-quantised article is built on the bridge, not on the eleven. Without it the template grows every time the boundary is noticed, and it has grown twice already: once for the fluids, once for the quantisation layer.

Where the numbers live. Every count below was measured on 20 November 2026, and each is reported with its check in §Verification.

The Title Convention

Rule one: the family prefix is The Lagrangian. No article in the family begins with anything else. The prefix makes the family visible in an alphabetical listing, in the menu and in a search, and it states the corpus's position: the action is the object, the equation is its consequence.

Rule two: and joins. A split convention — and for equations, of for objects, theories and sectors — was tried and abandoned. It produced The Lagrangian of the Spin-Zero Fluid beside The Lagrangian and the Klein–Gordon Equation, which reads as two conventions where there is one relation. The relation is the same in both: a functional and the thing it describes. Of marks possession where the corpus means conjunction, and it splits the alphabetical run of the family at the letter o.

Form Used for Example
The Lagrangian and X X is the object, equation, principle, limit, theory or sector the functional is written for The Lagrangian and the Klein–Gordon Equation; The Lagrangian and the Spin-Zero Fluid; The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics
X and the Lagrangian X carries more weight in the article than the functional, and the article has a subtitle The Standard Model and the Lagrangian: Four Sectors in One Expression

The second form is used three times, and a fourth article sits outside the schema. In the Standard Model, chiral and relativistic-particle articles the Lagrangian is one section among fifteen and the subject is the theory; inverting the order there would misdescribe the article. QCD was a fourth and no longer is: brought to the canonical slots, its action became its subject, so it moved to the first form, The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics: The Triplet, the Octet and the Feynman Rules. The test that decides between the forms is the architecture, not the length: an article that carries the canonical slots is about its action, whatever else it also contains. The level-0 article, The Non-Quantum Limit of the Action Principle and the Lagrangian, also puts its object first, and it is outside the schema altogether.

Rule three: the equation is named in full, and the reading is a suffix. Four equations — Klein–Gordon, Dirac, Proca, Schrödinger — and four readings.

Rule four: no in Biquaternionic Form. The whole corpus is biquaternionic; the phrase distinguished nothing and cost four words in thirty-eight titles. It is gone from the family and stays everywhere else, where it still does work.

Rule five: a sector article carries a subtitle after a colon. The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics: The Triplet, the Octet and the Feynman Rules. The subtitle names the content, not the method.

Two articles are deliberately outside the convention. The Action Problem is about the absence of an action, and the prefix would misname it. Stress–Energy, Conservation Laws and the Field Action in Biquaternionic Form computes the current of a field action rather than owning a Lagrangian; its title names the object it serves, and no family article replaces it.

The Titles That Carry No Prefix, and Why

The count, measured over the menu. Sixty-five titles carry the word Lagrangian. Sixty take the prefix, four put the object first, and the fifth is the sector hub. Nothing uses The Lagrangian of, and no title carries Lagrangian in the middle with a third element after it.

Grids over the same eight parents carry no Lagrangian prefix, and all of them are right not to.

Grid Titles Lagrangian density in the text Verdict

The Fourier Transform and the … Fluid 8 none equation-level, same reason
The Path Integral and the … Equation 16 none the action is exponentiated, never varied
The Path Integral in Biquaternionic Form 1 none same
The … Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit 8 none the limit of the equation pair, not of the action

The check is a grep, not an opinion. A search for the Lagrangian density $\mathcal{L}$ across those forty-nine articles returns nothing; a search across the family returns it in every article. The two grids answer different questions about the same parent: the equation grid asks what the operator does to a mode, the Lagrangian grid asks what functional has that equation as its stationary condition. A title that said Lagrangian over an equation-level article would be false, and the corpus avoids a false title more readily than it avoids an untidy one.

Articles that write a density without owning one keep their own titles. Noether's Theorem, Curved Spacetime, BRST Symmetry, the six Canonical Quantization articles, Goldstone's Theorem, The Higgs Mechanism, The Yukawa Sector and The Seesaw Mechanism import the density to prove something else and say so. Hamiltonian Field Theory is the dual of a family article and names its own subject. Three of these are open work, listed in §The List of Work.

The one thing that is not aligned, and should not be. Sixteen Fourier Transform and seventeen Path Integral titles carry the phrase in Biquaternionic Form, which rule four retired from the family. The phrase is not wrong there — it is the corpus's default description of the element — but it sits beside in Polar Variables and in Element Form without a rule saying which object takes which. If the corpus ever standardises, the rule is the one used here: name the reading in the suffix, and drop the phrase that says nothing.

The Common Structure of a Lagrangian Article

Twelve slots, and the family keeps eleven of them. The headings of the field articles, read side by side, give a sequence that is stable in its first six slots and its last three, and unstable only where Open Questions appears. Since this pass the sequence is not merely stable in practice: it is checked, and an article that breaks it fails. The instability is not disorder. The Counts, Read from the Sixty

Every row below is a measurement of the sixty articles and not a plan. The script walks each article's headings, maps each to its slot, counts the articles in which that slot appears at least once, records every position that slot holds, and then checks the order. There are six columns.

The position column is a distribution, and that is the only form in which it is true. Each cell reads position × articles at it: slot 3 reads 3 × 60, meaning sixty articles put the action at heading 3. Nothing is averaged, so nothing is invented. Slots 1 to 6 each read 1 × 60 through 6 × 60. A mean of a row that read wider would not be a position, so the mean is printed only by the script's positions command and never in the table.

A fixed position for every slot would now cost twenty sections, and the twenty are the one section that is honestly absent. Twelve slots are declared, so a uniform spine would need $12\times 60=720$ section-slots; the corpus carries 700, and the difference is exactly the twenty articles with no Open Questions. Nothing else is missing: every other slot is in all sixty. The earlier arithmetic read 272, and it read that because six conditional slots were being counted as positions whether or not an article had anything to put in them. Retiring them removed the number.

What is claimed is the order, and it is checked over every pair, not only neighbours. The last column counts, per slot, the articles in which a slot that should follow it stands in front of it. Every row reads zero. In all sixty articles, for all 210 ordered pairs of slots that co-occur, the pair is in the declared order. That is total consistency of order, and it holds in every article without exception.

The column before it counts sections, not places, and it is what makes the order auditable at page level. It counts how many sections stand between a slot and the slot before it in the same article. It reads 1 where the two are adjacent and more where a slot is written twice or a sector section interrupts. It never reads 0 and never a negative, because a slot cannot stand at or before the slot it follows.

Every gap above one is a repeated section standing in it, and the repeated action sections are gone. Slots 1 to 5 read 1 in every row: each is one section in all sixty articles, and the four articles that once wrote the action as two, three and four sections now write it as one. The gap before slot 7 reads 2 in three articles, which state the structural consequence twice: the claim, then what it costs. The gap before slot 8 reads 2 in seven: four write the current section twice, and three inherit a doubled section in front of them. Slots 9 to 12 read up to 3, for the two articles that write the ledger three times and for the presence or absence of Open Questions.

Six rows read one position in all sixty articles, and the spread below them has two causes and no others. Slots 1 to 6 read 1 ×60 through 6 ×60: they are in every article and nothing precedes them but each other. Slot 7 reads 7 ×57, 8 ×3, the three being the articles that write the structural consequence twice; slot 8 reads 8 ×53, 9 ×7, the seven being the articles that write the current section twice or inherit a doubled section in front of it. Slots 9 to 12 then spread by up to three places for one reason only: Open Questions is present in forty articles and absent in twenty, and everything after it moves by one. The order column reads zero on every row, and that is the invariant; the position column describes the article's length, and it is reported as a distribution so that it cannot be read as a claim.

The Rule That Protects Content

Nothing was purged, and the guarantee is not automatic. Half of every article is scaffolding that the template governs; the other half is sections whose names appear once in the corpus, each a claim with a computation attached — Reality of the Action Is the Anti-Hermitian Root, The Ordering Ambiguity Is the Cross Term, The Trace Is the Mass. A rewrite that kept only the slots would fold them into The Structural Consequence and lose the computation. Five rules prevent that, and one mechanism enforces them: the heading map, written as a file before any article is touched.

  1. A section is never deleted, only renamed. Every old heading is mapped to a slot, or kept under its own name after slot 6 as a result section. The one deletion in this pass is the reality block, and it is not an exception to the rule but an application of it: the four paragraphs moved into slot 3, were counted before the move and after it, and no display was lost.
  2. A reading's own blocks are mandatory in that reading. The polar article that drops The Four Brackets is not shorter; it is incomplete.
  3. The displays are the claims, and their count may not fall. An article that enters with thirteen displays and leaves with nine has lost four claims, whatever the prose says.
  4. Word count may not fall below ten per cent. Growth is reported and not penalised, since a slot the rewrite adds grows the article.
  5. A move needs a named destination and a pointer left behind.

A move was proposed twice, and the slot-7 question cancelled both. The test is applied to the source section: does it carry the claim the article uniquely makes? The Ninth Family: The Eight Fluids, and Why They Get a Chart Structure

The fluid articles are Lagrangian articles. Madelung derived the hydrodynamic form of the Schrödinger equation in 1926 and Takabayasi the relativistic spin-$\tfrac12$ fluid in 1957 — the corpus holds that paper in _reserve — and both derivations are variational. The fluid is what the action looks like when it is written in density and phase instead of in the field. There is no obstacle to a Lagrangian theory of the fluid. There is only a different chart.

The chart keeps the eleven numbers and the eleven questions, and four of them carry a different object.

Slot The eleven (field articles) The chart (fluid articles)

The other nine are shared, and two of them adapt their wording: the density's sign is settled in slot 3, slot 7 the stress as the translation current, slot 1 the substitution.

What the two families share is larger than what separates them. Both write one functional. Both recover their equations by variation and do not assume them. Both name the constraint the action imposes and separate it from the choice the chart supplies. Both owe an honest ledger of what the rewriting relabels. The polar article of the field family and the fluid article use the same substitution $\varrho e^{i\theta}$, and there the families nearly touch. The difference is one step: the polar article keeps the frame, the sector projectors and the whole element, and varies it; the fluid article discards the frame and keeps only density and phase as the physical variables. The fluid is the polar chart pushed one step further, and the two say so in their own words: the polar article in slot 5, that the frame survives the substitution; the fluid article in slot 3, that it does not.

What the fluid articles have that the field articles do not. A pure relabelling claim, stated as a theorem rather than a caveat: The Lagrangian and the Schrödinger Fluid proves that its action is the same functional in two charts. That is slot 8 at full strength, and none of the field articles can make the claim in the same form, because none of them re-charts anything.

What they lack. No grid. The field articles are eight parents crossed with four readings; the fluid articles are eight charts, one per spin–charge combination, and there is no reading axis and no coupled axis among them. That is why they are eight and not thirty-two, and why a uniform sixteen slots would have forced empty sections into each — which is the argument that later retired the five chart blocks, and left eleven.

Lagrangian Migration: One Title, One Structure, and the List of Work

Introduction

The claim under test. In the standard theory the Lagrangian is the object and the equations follow from it. The free equations are the quadratic truncation of one Lagrangian, with every interaction vertex discarded. QCD is the clean case: one Lagrangian, four pieces — gauge kinetic, quark, gauge fixing, ghost — and four vertices — quark–gluon, three-gluon, four-gluon, ghost–gluon — and those four exhaust the tree-level content. If that is the right way to organise a theory, the corpus should carry one article of that shape per sector, all built the same way and all named the same way.

What the corpus did, and the three faults that followed. It had the equations first and reconstructed an action for each, one at a time, under thirty-eight titles that shared no prefix. A reader could not tell that the family existed. The faults were structural, not editorial:

  • The family was a tree rooted at the equation. The eight element-form articles start from an equation written elsewhere and ask which action produces it, so no article could start from a functional and derive two equations. The Lagrangian and the Electromagnetic Field is the first to do it: it states that the Proca action at $k_m=0$ is the Maxwell action.
  • The sector pieces were scattered. Five articles held the electroweak Lagrangian's pieces — gauge kinetic, currents, scalar potential, Yukawa masses, gauge fixing — and none assembled them. The assembly article now exists and displays both the broken and the unbroken form.
  • The two complete sector articles were the two that admit they are transcriptions. That honesty is the model: a sector article's value is the ledger of what is derived, what is checked and what is imported.

What the family is now. Sixty articles, one prefix, one structure, plus six hubs. Five more sit beside the count: the level-0 article, the three sector-form articles and the sector hub, whose titles do not take the prefix and which the checker therefore lists separately. Thirty-two are the four equations in eight readings each, free and coupled; eight are the fluid charts; eight are the frontier articles that reach past the one-particle action; four put the theory first; one, The Non-Quantum Limit of the Action Principle and the Lagrangian, is level 0. The level-0 pair was merged into that one article, so the fifty-four count is unchanged and the two former bytes are now one. Every one is filed in physics.md.

The verdict. The naming is uniform, the architecture is uniform, the coverage is complete for every field the corpus treats. What no article of the family does is quantise: the slots govern the one-particle layer, and the second quantised layer sits on one bridge article.

The boundary sentence, which is the freeze. The seventeen slots govern the one-particle layer; a second-quantised article is built on the bridge, not on the seventeen. Without it the template grows every time the boundary is noticed, and it has grown twice already: once for the fluids, once for the quantisation layer.

Where the numbers live. The counts in this note are measured, not estimated, and they are current as of 19 November 2026. They are collected in §Verification.

The Title Convention

Rule one: the family prefix is The Lagrangian. No article in the family begins with anything else. The prefix makes the family visible in an alphabetical listing, in the menu and in a search, and it states the corpus's position: the action is the object, the equation is its consequence.

Rule two: and joins. A split convention — and for equations, of for objects, theories and sectors — was tried and abandoned. It produced The Lagrangian of the Spin-Zero Fluid beside The Lagrangian and the Klein–Gordon Equation, which reads as two conventions where there is one relation. The relation is the same in both: a functional and the thing it describes. Of marks possession where the corpus means conjunction, and it splits the alphabetical run of the family in two at the letter o.

Form Used for Example
The Lagrangian and X X is the object, equation, principle, limit, theory or sector the functional is written for The Lagrangian and the Klein–Gordon Equation; The Lagrangian and the Spin-Zero Fluid; The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics
X and the Lagrangian** X carries more weight in the article than the functional, and the article has a subtitle The Standard Model and the Lagrangian: Four Sectors in One Expression

The second form is used three times and only three. In the Standard Model, chiral and relativistic-particle articles the Lagrangian is one section among fifteen and the subject is the theory; inverting the order there would misdescribe the article. QCD was the fourth and no longer is: once it was brought to the canonical slot set its action had become its subject, so it moved to the first form, The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics: The Triplet, the Octet and the Feynman Rules. The test that decides between the two forms is the architecture, not the length: an article that carries the canonical slots is about its action, whatever else it also contains.

Rule three: the equation is named in full, and the reading is a suffix. Four equations — Klein–Gordon, Dirac, Proca, Schrödinger — and four readings.

Rule four: no in Biquaternionic Form. The whole corpus is biquaternionic; the phrase distinguished nothing and cost four words in thirty-eight titles. It is gone from the family and stays everywhere else, where it still does work.

Rule five: a sector article carries a subtitle after a colon. The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics: The Triplet, the Octet and the Feynman Rules. The subtitle names the content, not the method.

Two articles are deliberately outside the convention. The Action Problem is about the absence of an action and the prefix would misname it. Stress–Energy, Conservation Laws and the Field Action computes the current of a field action rather than owning a Lagrangian; it adopts The Lagrangian and the Field: The Stress–Energy Tensor.

The Titles That Carry No Prefix, and Why

The count, measured over the whole corpus. Fifty-five titles contain the word Lagrangian. Fifty-four are family articles: fifty The Lagrangian and … and four that put the theory first, … and the Lagrangian: …. The fifty-fifth is the sector hub. Nothing uses The Lagrangian of, and no title carries Lagrangian in the middle with a third element after it. So the family prefix is complete on the family, and the only other title that carries the word is the one page whose subject is the family's reach.

Grids over the same eight parents carry no Lagrangian prefix, and all of them are right not to.

Grid Titles Lagrangian density in the text Verdict
The Fourier Transform and the … Equation 16 none in any of the 16 equation-level; the symbol is the object, not the action
The Fourier Transform and the … Fluid 8 none equation-level, same reason
The Path Integral and the … Equation 16 none the action is exponentiated, never varied; a second-quantised article, not a Lagrangian article
The Path Integral in Biquaternionic Form, The Path-Integral Anomaly 2 none same
The … Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit 8 none the limit of the equation pair, not of the action

The check is a grep, not an opinion. A search for the Lagrangian density $\mathcal{L}$ across those fifty articles returns nothing; a search across the family returns it in every article. The two grids answer different questions about the same parent: the equation grid asks what the operator does to a mode, the Lagrangian grid asks what functional has that equation as its stationary condition. A title that said Lagrangian over an equation-level article would be false, and the corpus avoids a false title more readily than it avoids an untidy one.

One article outside the family owns a field action and hides it. Stress–Energy, Conservation Laws and the Field Action displays $\mathcal{L}$ twenty-three times and names the tensor in its title. The others that display one either import it to prove something else and say so, or are built on a different object: Noether's Theorem, Curved Spacetime, BRST Symmetry, Canonical Quantization (four), Goldstone's Theorem, The Higgs Mechanism, The Yukawa Sector, The Seesaw Mechanism import it; The Nonlinear Sigma Model, The Skyrme Model and Hamiltonian Field Theory own a different subject and would be misdescribed by the prefix.

The one thing that is not aligned, and should not be. Twenty-four Fourier Transform and eighteen Path Integral titles keep the phrase in Biquaternionic Form, which rule four retired from the Lagrangian family. The phrase is not wrong there — it is the corpus's default description of the element — but it sits beside in Polar Variables and in Element Form without a rule saying which object takes which. If the corpus ever standardises, the rule is the one used here: name the reading in the suffix, and drop the phrase that says nothing.

Seventeen slots, and the family keeps ten of them. The headings of the field articles, read side by side, give a sequence that is stable in its first three slots and its last four, and unstable in the middle. The instability is not disorder. Slot 6 is conditional, and mandatory in one place. A scalar or spinor action imposes no constraint, and a section asserting one would be a false claim. The slot is mandatory in the Proca readings — element, polar, momentum, free and coupled — where the Lorenz condition is the family's model section, and conditional elsewhere and declared. Twenty-six of the forty-eight mapped articles fill it; twenty-two declare it. The pattern follows the physics and not the coupling: the coupled scalar imposes no constraint either.

Slot 11 is the clearest case and the largest saving. Eight of the sixty articles take a limit and can state a truncation error. The rest cannot, and an article that manufactures a limit in order to fill the slot is worse than one that says no limit is taken here. The same discipline applies to slot 8 where the quadratic form is not computed, and to slot 13 where no comparison exists.

The tiering prices the rewrite. Seventeen mandatory slots per article would have meant eight new sections each; the tier puts the mean at about four over the forty field articles, 160 in all, not 320. Every one of the removed sections is one that would have been invented rather than derived.

Which slots are new, and which are extensions.

  • 5, 6, 8 and 13 are new, and 5 is new four times over. Its subsections name questions the articles ask in different words and none named: why this bilinear, what type the coefficient must have, which potentials are admissible, how much weaker a real action is than a real spectrum.
  • 3, 4, 9, 10 and 11 are extensions. The action, the variation, the currents, the settings and the limits are already there under one name per variable set; the bilinear, the sign of the charge and the truncation's error are not.

One heading, four subsections, and the four are the correction. The eight articles that carried The Reality of the Action were not doing one thing. The Dirac Equation rejects the quaternionic pairing because it makes every kinetic density a total derivative. The four momentum articles ask what type the coefficient must have — an anti-Hermitian root, a Hermitian symbol — for the density to come out real. The Klein–Gordon momentum article asks which potentials the action admits at all, and finds the anti-Hermitian sector excluded with a complex frequency $\omega^{2}/c^{2}=k_\Phi^{2}+i\lambda$ where a wave should be. Two articles measure how much weaker a real action is than a real symbol or a real spectrum. Under one bare heading those four arguments were invisible, and an article could be said to have the slot by carrying any one of them. The section is now one heading with four fixed subsections, in the same order in every article that has it, and each subsection either holds its argument or says why it does not arise. The counts: The Pairing holds an argument in 2 of the 8, The Type of the Coefficient in 7, The Selection Rule in 1, What Reality Does Not Buy in 2. The four subsections are enforced by the same script, which fails an article that drops one or reorders them. The spin-zero fluid's The Gradient Energy Is Indefinite moved to slot 8, where a quadratic form's sign belongs.

The count of each subsection is the honest measure of the family, and it is small on purpose. The Pairing is proved once and cited thereafter; the other element-form articles inherit it and say so. The Selection Rule exists only where a potential is general enough to have an inadmissible sector, which is the free Klein–Gordon momentum article and no other: the three coupled momentum articles have a connection and not a potential, so the rule has nothing to exclude. That gap is real and it is the next item of work, not a naming fault.

The Rule That Protects Content

Nothing was purged, and the guarantee is not automatic. Half of every article is scaffolding that the template governs; the other half is sections whose names appear once in the corpus — 129 of the 156 headings, each a claim with a computation attached — Reality of the Action Is the Anti-Hermitian Root, The Ordering Ambiguity Is the Cross Term, The Trace Is the Mass. Each is a claim with a computation attached, and a rewrite that kept only the slots would fold them into The Structural Consequence and lose the computation. Five rules prevent that, and one mechanism enforces them: the heading map, written as a file before any article is touched.

  1. A section is never deleted, only renamed. Every old heading is mapped to a slot, or kept under its own name after slot 7 as a result section.
  2. A reading's own blocks are mandatory in that reading. The polar article that drops The Four Brackets is not shorter; it is incomplete.
  3. The displays are the claims, and their count may not fall. An article that enters with thirteen displays and leaves with nine has lost four claims, whatever the prose says.
  4. Word count may not fall below ten per cent. Growth is reported and not penalised, since a slot the rewrite adds grows the article.
  5. A move needs a named destination and a pointer left behind.

The two proposed moves were tested and both were cancelled. The test is the slot-7 question applied to the source section: does it carry the claim the article uniquely makes? The Lorentz Force in the coupled Schrödinger article and Exact Solutions and What They Show in the Proca article each does, and a section that carries the claim cannot be relocated. Both refusals are recorded in the family map.

Everything else stays where it is. The temptation to centralise is real and should be resisted: The Ordering Ambiguity Is the Cross Term cannot be moved to a central article on ordering, because it is a statement about the coupled Klein–Gordon action in momentum space and about nothing else. A result belongs to the article that computed it.

The Ninth Family: The Eight Fluids, and Why They Get a Chart Structure

The fluid articles are Lagrangian articles. Madelung derived the hydrodynamic form of the Schrödinger equation in 1926 and Takabayasi the relativistic spin-$\tfrac12$ fluid in 1957 — the corpus holds that paper in _reserve — and both derivations are variational. The fluid is what the action looks like when it is written in density and phase instead of in the field. There is no obstacle to a Lagrangian theory of the fluid. There is only a different chart.

The chart keeps the seventeen numbers and the seventeen questions, and five of them carry a different object.

Five replacements, and each is forced. The fluid has two variations and not one. It has a substitution before it has an action. Its central object is a term that does not appear in the field writing. Its canonical table is the thing that fails. And its literature is a named pair of authors rather than a textbook.

What the two families share is larger than what separates them. Both write one functional. Both recover their equations by variation and do not assume them. Both name the constraint the action imposes and separate it from the choice the chart supplies. Both owe an honest ledger of what the rewriting relabels. The polar article of the field family and the fluid article use the same substitution $\varrho e^{i\theta}$, and there the families nearly touch. The difference is one step: the polar article keeps the frame, the sector projectors and the whole element, and varies it; the fluid article discards the frame and keeps only density and phase as the physical variables. The fluid is the polar chart pushed one step further, and the two say so in their own words: the polar article in slot 6, that the frame survives the substitution; the fluid article in slot 3, that it does not.

The distinction is the same test applied one level down: where the action is the parent's subject — the Stueckelberg mechanism, the duality's parent action, the central scalar's classical action — the article keeps it and states where the family's reading is, because removing it would remove the article. Two more articles were counted as gaps and are not. The Group of Invertibles, the Cone and the Orders and the four Canonical Quantization articles write a Lagrangian several times, but they write it to display a conjugate momentum or a commutator; their subject is the quantisation, and the action is their input. They keep it.

The two sector actions are brought to the same slot set, and stay in their sector's spin-1 subsection. The spin headings mean articles about particles of spin X, and a sector action has no single carrier: its terms belong to three spins. Filed under spin 1 it reads as though the action belonged to the vector boson. Moving both sector actions to Generalities was tried and rejected, because the menu's symmetry is the family's symmetry: the spin subsections hold the whole family, and a sector action placed above them breaks the ladder. What was wrong was not the slot but the article. The Lagrangian and Quantum Chromodynamics carried seven headings and none of the seventeen slots; The Lagrangian and the Electroweak Theory carried eleven and eight of them. QCD was rewritten to the same slot set — Conventions, The Action in Element Form, The Variation, The Constraints the Action Imposes, The Structural Consequence, The Noether Currents and the Sign of the Charge, What the Principle Adds and What It Relabels — with its seven sector slots kept and ordered around them. The two articles now carry the same eleven canonical headings in the same order and differ only in the sector slots that the strong sector needs and the electroweak one does not.

The rule the pair settles. A complete sector action goes in the sector's spin-1 subsection, because the gauge boson is the carrier that defines the sector. A term of it goes in the subsection of the field that term carries, and says in its introduction which sector action it is a term of — the W and Z, the weak-interaction and the Stueckelberg articles now do. A low-energy effective action is not a term of a sector action and needs no parent: the nonlinear sigma model, the Skyrme model and the axion are three complete actions at spin 0, while the three electroweak spin-1 Lagrangian articles are three readings of one functional. That is the whole of the apparent asymmetry between the two sectors.

Internal Summary of the Biquaternion Operators

Introduction

What this file is. A compressed map of the Operators block of the maths category Biquaternions: forty articles in the subsections The Endomorphism Algebra (1), Operators of the Biquaternion Algebra (7), Adjoint Pairs of Operators (3), Matrix Operator Representations (3), The Structures of Operators (3), Operators on the Four Sectors (5), Rotors, Spins, Carriers and Closures (4), and the three spin subspaces (4 + 6 + 4). It gives the objects, the boxed equations and the non-trivial claims. No proofs: an agent re-derives them. Every formula is copied from the source article named at the end of its section.

What the category does. It takes the one algebra $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ and asks what its own linear maps are, how they compose, how they decompose, how they classify over $\mathbb{R}$ and over $\mathbb{C}$, and which of them carry a spin. It develops the operator calculus: the two regular families, their transposition, the two-sided products, the four adjoints, the SVD, the three lattices of the operator algebra. It imports the Fierz–Kofink identities and Lounesto's six classes. It refuses a spectral theorem on the indefinite form, a pin group inside the two-sided family, and a decision between the physical left and right actions.

The single structural fact: the ladder, and the cone is not a subspace.

$$ \boxed{\;\underbrace{1}_{\text{scalars}}\;\subset\;\underbrace{4}_{L(\mathbb{B}),\ R(\mathbb{B})}\;\subset\;\underbrace{7}_{\text{two-sided cone}}\;\subset\;\underbrace{16}_{\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})}\;,\qquad \mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}\cong \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. The numbers $1,4,7,16$ are $4+4-1=7$ and $4+4+9=16$: the two one-sided families are copies of $\mathbb{B}$ and of $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$ that meet exactly in the scalars and commute element by element. The two-sided operators are the products $L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}$, and they fill a cone and not a subspace: eight parameters modulo the scaling $(\lambda\tilde Q,\lambda^{-1}\tilde Q_2)$ give seven, the family is closed under composition, and $\mathrm{id}+L_{\vec e_x}R_{\vec e_x}$ is not a product. The linear sum $L+R$ is a seven-dimensional subspace and is not a subalgebra. The enveloping algebra therefore does not decompose: the missing nine dimensions are exactly the tensor part $\mathfrak{sl}_2\otimes\mathfrak{sl}_2$.

The one-sentence version. A left multiplication is a homomorphism, a right multiplication an anti-homomorphism, they commute and meet in the scalars, so every two-sided operator is a composite of the two families and never a new primitive; the whole ambient space is their linear span plus a nine-dimensional tensor part.

How to use it. Read §1 to §7 for the spine, §12 to §14 for the classification (the three densest articles), §15 to §17 for the sectors, §18 to §22 for the spins. The four numbers $16,7,4,1$ were checked numerically on random and degenerate parameters; the SVD weight identities on five random elements; the rank set $\{0,1,2,4\}$ on generic and zero-divisor parameters.

1. The Ambient Algebra and the Three Fields

The object. $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$ is the algebra of $\mathbb{C}$-linear maps of the carrier into itself, under composition. A choice of basis identifies it with $M_4(\mathbb{C})$; the identification depends on the basis, and the trace, the determinant, the spectrum and the rank do not.

$$ \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})=16,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{B})=64,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})=32 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The third number is the first read over $\mathbb{R}$, and it is $32$ and not $64$ because $\mathbb{C}$-linearity is one real equation per direction: the complex-linear maps are the fixed set of the real structure commuting with multiplication by $i$. A reader who confuses the two ambient algebras gets every real-form count of §12 and §14 wrong.

The three subobjects, and the trap. $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B})=PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$, of complex dimension three; the derivations are all inner, $\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde Q}(x)=\tilde Qx-x\tilde Q$, a subspace of complex dimension three; the dimensions agree because one is the tangent space of the other, and one object is a group while the other is a subspace.

$$ D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1),\qquad \operatorname{tr}D=-2,\qquad \det D=-1 . $$

The non-trivial claim. $D$, the quaternion conjugation, is the one conjugation of the algebra that is $\mathbb{C}$-linear, so it lies inside the ambient algebra and outside the automorphism group. The coefficient bar and the Hermitian star are conjugate-linear, hence outside. That single line fixes which of the four adjoints of §8 can be written as an operator identity and which only as a real-linear one.

2. The Four Products and the Four Readings

The four products. One multiplication rule with a conjugation inserted independently into the two slots.

$$ \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2,\qquad \tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2,\qquad \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*},\qquad \tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}. $$

The pairings they decide.

$$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2),\quad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*}),\quad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2),\quad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}), $$

with Gram matrices $\mathrm{E}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, $\mathrm{I}_4$, $\mathrm{I}_4$, $\mathrm{E}$ in the unit basis.

The non-trivial claim, and the sentence to quote. The products decide the pairing and the adjoint; they do not decide the operator. The four share the multiplication table of the units and differ only in which involution sits in which slot, so what they fix is the name of an operator family and its adjoint, not whether an element acts as a rotation or as a reflection. An operator needs an element and an insertion, and whether the sign sits in the parameter or in the argument is a convention; the corpus fixes it as $\natural$, with sign vector $(1,-1,-1,-1)$. The theorem "a reflection needs the sign" is a theorem; which map plays the sign is not.

The evidence, on one element.

$$ \operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1),\qquad \operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1,1),\qquad L_{\tilde Q_4}R_{\tilde Q_4^{-1}}\big|_{V}=-\rho_{\tilde Q_4},\quad L_{\tilde Q_4^{\natural}}R_{\tilde Q_4^{-1}}\big|_{V}=\rho_{\tilde Q_4}. $$

The non-trivial claim. On the four-dimensional algebra both operators have determinant $+1$: rotation and reflection are read on the vector subspace, never on $\mathbb{B}$. And the plain product gives minus the reflection, so one insertion is forced by the algebra and not merely preferred.

The four adjoints are decided here.

$$ (L_{\tilde Q_4})^{\approx}=R_{\tilde Q_4},\quad (L_{\tilde Q_4})^{*}=L_{\tilde Q_4^{*}},\quad (L_{\tilde Q_4})^{N}=L_{\tilde Q_4^{\natural}},\quad (L_{\tilde Q_4})^{\natural*}=R_{\bar{\tilde Q_4}} . $$

3. Chirality Is the Side

The definition. Operator chirality is the side on which an operator multiplies: $L(a)$ on the left, $R(b)$ on the right. It names no frame, no axis and no basis.

The non-trivial claim, and the warning. The natural route through a basis operator $-iR_u$ with $u^2=-e_t$ is correct and misordered: it produces an operator and then reads a chirality off it. That operator is a pure right multiplier, so it has a chirality itself, and an object that has a chirality cannot be what chirality means. The sides come first; the basis operator is one example of one of them. Operator chirality is also not spinor chirality, which is an eigenvalue and needs an axis.

$$ \sigma=\Xi\tau\Xi^{-1},\qquad \Xi(a\otimes b)=L_aR_b,\qquad \sigma(L_a)=R_a . $$

The non-trivial claim. The exchange of the sides is the flip transported through the structure isomorphism, so it is intrinsic and not a choice. It squares to the identity, reverses products, exchanges the two images, fixes the scalars, preserves rank and length, and is not inner. It is the adjoint of the trace form, and in a diagonalising basis it is the transpose through a sign matrix.

The two eigenspaces. The fixed part of $\sigma$ is a ten-dimensional Jordan algebra and the negated part a six-dimensional Lie algebra, the symmetric and the exterior square. The second side-exchanger is the quaternion conjugation, $\natural L(a)\natural=R(a^{\natural})$, which differs from $\sigma$ by conjugating and reversing the parameters; the sign in which the two differ is the origin of every phase the corpus obtains from a change of side.

4. The Two One-Sided Families

The objects.

$$ L_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde QX,\qquad R_{\tilde Q}(X)=X\tilde Q,\qquad L_{\tilde Q}L_{\tilde Q_2}=L_{\tilde Q\tilde Q_2},\qquad R_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}=R_{\tilde Q_2\tilde Q}. $$

The non-trivial claim. $L$ is a representation and $R$ an anti-representation, and the reversal is the whole content of the second law: the images are $\mathbb{B}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$, each of complex dimension four. The repair $\tilde Q\mapsto R_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$ is a representation; the article keeps the anti-representation, because that reversal is what the two-sided law of §6 inherits and why $\mathrm{opp}$ appears in §7.

Injectivity, and why the unit is the whole proof. $L_{\tilde Q}(e_t)=\tilde Q$ and $R_{\tilde Q}(e_t)=\tilde Q$, so both maps are injective and each family is a copy of the algebra, not a quotient.

Kernel, image, rank, inverse.

$$ \ker L_{\tilde Q}=\operatorname{Ann}_r(\tilde Q),\quad \operatorname{im}L_{\tilde Q}=\tilde Q\mathbb{B},\qquad \operatorname{rank}L_{\tilde Q}\in\{4,2,0\},\qquad \det L_{\tilde Q}=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}^{2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The rank takes three values and no others: four for an invertible element, two for a nonzero zero divisor, zero for zero. There is no intermediate case, because finite dimension makes injective equivalent to invertible, so the operator picture and the algebraic picture agree exactly and "$L_{\tilde Q}$ almost invertible" is empty. The kernel is nonzero exactly on the zero-divisor cone.

The two families together.

$$ L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}=R_{\tilde Q_2}L_{\tilde Q},\qquad L(\mathbb{B})'=R(\mathbb{B}),\qquad R(\mathbb{B})'=L(\mathbb{B}),\qquad L(\mathbb{B})\cap R(\mathbb{B})=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{I}_4 . $$

The exchange $S$, in the two frames.

$$ S_{\mathrm{pau}}=\mathsf{P},\qquad S_{\mathrm{axf}}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,1),\qquad R_{\mathrm{pau}}(\tilde Q)=\mathsf{P}\,L_{\mathrm{pau}}(\tilde Q)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathsf{P}^{-1},\qquad R_{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde Q)=D\,L_{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde Q)^{\mathsf{T}}D . $$

The non-trivial claim. The exchange itself and the matrix that conjugates the transpose are two different matrices: $S_{\mathrm{axf}}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,1)$ is the exchange, $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ is the quaternion conjugation. In the Peirce frame the exchange is a permutation and the right matrix is the transpose of the left one; in the coefficient frame the exchange is a reflection and the transpose implements the quaternion conjugation instead. Reading $D$ as the exchange is the trap. The two frames disagree about which involution the transpose carries: that is the trap, and it is why the exchange is defined abstractly in §3 and only displayed here.

5. The Transposition of the Two Sides

$$ L_{\tilde Q}=\natural\,R_{\natural(\tilde Q)}\,\natural,\qquad\qquad R_{\tilde Q}=\natural\,L_{\natural(\tilde Q)}\,\natural . $$

The non-trivial claim. Every right multiplication is a conjugated left multiplication, and the parameter is carried: it is $\natural(\tilde Q)$ and not $\tilde Q$. The mechanism is the reversal: the outer conjugation meets the product $\tilde Q\natural(X)$, reverses it, moves $\tilde Q$ across the other factor and puts it through $\natural$. Only an antimultiplicative map transposes the families, and no such map can keep the parameter. That is the single sentence that stops a wrong conversion.

The single involution.

$$ \mathsf{K}(F)=\natural F\natural,\qquad \mathsf{K}^2=\mathrm{id},\qquad \mathsf{K}(FG)=\mathsf{K}(F)\mathsf{K}(G). $$

The non-trivial claim. Because $\mathsf{K}$ preserves products and is involutive, an equation may be converted factor by factor, transformed on both sides, and remain equivalent. The orbit collapses to one operator exactly at the centre; off the centre the exchange is genuine.

The error, measured.

$$ R_{\tilde Q}-\natural L_{\tilde Q}\natural=R_{2\vec Q}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The natural wrong identity is not merely false: its failure is a right multiplication by twice the vector part, so it vanishes exactly on the centre. The parameter of a conversion is unique — the left parameter is forced to be the image of the right one.

The name. The exchange is exactly the reversal of the two factors inside the conjugation; the genuine transpose of the plain form exchanges the sides as well and keeps the parameter; the conjugation exchanges them and carries it. The two agree on the operator and differ on the price, and the pair of identities is that agreement.

6. The Two-Sided Operators and the Cone

The object and its law.

$$ L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}(X)=\tilde QX\tilde Q_2,\qquad L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}L_{\tilde Q_3}R_{\tilde Q_4}=L_{\tilde Q\tilde Q_3}R_{\tilde Q_4\tilde Q_2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The second slot is reversed. That one reversal, and not the first, is why $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$ and not $\mathbb{B}$ appears in the enveloping algebra. The family contains the one-sided operators at the boundary cases $\tilde Q_2=e_t$ and $\tilde Q=e_t$.

The cone.

$$ 8\ \text{parameters}-1\ \text{scaling}=7=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\text{cone},\qquad \mathrm{id}+L_{\vec e_x}R_{\vec e_x}\ \text{is not a product}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The family is closed under composition and not under addition. It is therefore a cone with the zero operator as vertex, not a subspace; its span is the whole sixteen, which the generation theorem proves on sixteen basis operators $e_{\imath}[\,]e_{\jmath}$.

The plain adjoint on the cone.

$$ (L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2})^{\approx}=L_{\tilde Q_2}R_{\tilde Q},\qquad \text{self-adjoint}\iff \tilde Q_2=\lambda\tilde Q . $$

The non-trivial claim. The self-adjoint members are the scalar multiples of the diagonal operators, and no nonzero two-sided operator is anti-self-adjoint: the antisymmetric part of the family is empty, so there is no anti-Hermitian sector here. The isometries are the two cosets $\pm\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde Q}$ with $\tilde Q_2\tilde Q=\pm e_t$, of complex dimension three; the multiplicative members are the inner automorphisms. Since the quadratic form is not multiplicative, the family contains no pin group, and the reflections lie outside it.

7. The Enveloping Algebra and the Bi-module

$$ \mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}},\qquad (\tilde Q_1\otimes\tilde Q_{\mathrm{opp},2})\cdot\tilde Q_3=\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_3\tilde Q_2,\qquad \mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}). $$

The non-trivial claim. The action is faithful and exhausts the $\mathbb{C}$-linear operators: the two-sided operators of the corpus are exactly the elementary tensors, and the abstract question "which action is the matter representation" becomes "which tensor factor a degree of freedom uses". Both factors are present, of the same algebraic type, and commuting.

The Conway basis and its composition.

$$ (a[\,]b)(\tilde Q_3)=a\tilde Q_3b,\qquad (a[\,]b)\circ(c[\,]d)=(ac)[\,](db). $$

The non-trivial claim. The composition rule contracts the inner indices, the reverse of matrix multiplication in the second factor; that is the same reversal as §6, written in the enveloping algebra. The sixteen $e_n[\,]e_m$ are an orthogonal basis of the ambient space, and the three classical functions $A\{a\}$, $D\{d\}$, $S\{s\}$ are its antisymmetric, diagonal and symmetric parts.

The Peirce corners of the envelope.

$$ E_{ik}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}E_{jl}=(\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},i}\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},j})\otimes(\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},l}\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},k})_{\mathrm{opp}}=\mathbb{C}\,(\Lambda_{ij}\otimes\Lambda_{\mathrm{opp},lk}), $$

sixteen one-dimensional corners. The reversal of the second index pair in that display is again the composition reversal.

The double centraliser.

$$ \{T:T L_a=L_aT\ \forall a\}=\{R_b\},\qquad \{T:T R_b=R_bT\ \forall b\}=\{L_a\},\qquad \{T:\text{commutes with all}\}=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{I}_4 . $$

The three objects called matrix. $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)$ (the $2\times2$ element matrix), $[T]_{\mathcal{B}}$ (the $4\times4$ operator matrix) and $c(T)$ (the $4\times4$ coefficient matrix) are distinct, and conflating them is the defect of the older literature. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the two-sided map has a 32-dimensional kernel, exactly the redundancy of the central $i$, which is why the result is stated over $\mathbb{C}$.

The non-trivial claim. Two columns decide the whole subject. The plain bilinear and the Krein adjoints exchange the side; the quaternionic and Hermitian adjoints keep it. The calculus of the plain adjoint.

$$ (F^{\approx})^{\approx}=F,\qquad (F\circ G)^{\approx}=G^{\approx}\circ F^{\approx},\qquad \overline{F}^{\approx}=\mathrm{E}F^{*}\mathrm{E},\qquad F^{*}=F^{-1}\ \text{on the unitaries}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The last identity is the collapse that makes the indefinite and the definite adjoint agree on the norm-one slice, and it is the reason a Lorentzian statement and a unitary statement can be written with the same symbol without error.

The five named families. Plain Bilinear Pair, Quaternionic Pair, Hermitian Pair, Signed Hermitian Pair, Krein Pair — the word signed names one operation in all of them, the insertion of $\natural$.

The sector criterion, and it is the sharp line of the block. Of the five families only the Hermitian pair preserves $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ for every parameter. The criterion is $\tilde Q_3=\lambda\tilde Q_6^{*}$ with $\lambda$ real; the other four meet it only on proper subvarieties. A one-sided operator preserves a sector only for a real scalar, which is exactly why the sectors belong to the two-sided action.

9. The Hermitian Pairs, on One Side and on Two

The one-side pair, and the mass.

$$ (L_{\tilde Q})^{\natural}=L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}},\qquad L_{\tilde Q}L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\,\mathrm{id}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Staying on one side composes to a central scalar, and that scalar is the passage from two first-order conditions to one second-order operator: the mass. Read on two components, the off-diagonal array with blocks $L_{\tilde Q}$ and $L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$ squares to $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\mathrm{id}$; with $\tilde Q$ the gradient the display is the chiral pair and the square is Klein–Gordon. The same construction with $*$ instead of $\natural$ composes to the Hermitian element $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$ and not to a scalar, which is why the corpus keeps the two pairs apart.

The moving pair, and the transposition that rewrites it.

$$ R_{\tilde Q^{*}}=*^{-1}L_{\tilde Q}*,\qquad L_{\tilde Q}*^{-1}L_{\tilde Q}*=\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q},\qquad \tilde Q\tilde X=\tilde Y,\quad \tilde Q\tilde X^{*}=\tilde Z^{*}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Replacing the right actor by its transpose turns the two-side pair into two left multiplications by the same element, at the price of one anti-automorphism on the unknown and one on the data. The product is the two-sided operator again: the two placements differ in the reading, not in the composite.

The two-side pair.

$$ \mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\tilde Q\tilde X\tilde Q^{*},\qquad \mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q_2}=\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q\tilde Q_2},\qquad \text{defect}=e_t-\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q . $$

The non-trivial claim. It is a real cone of real dimension seven, the graph of the involution inside the complex seven-dimensional cone: the phase of the parameter cancels, and a complex rescaling acts by the square of the modulus. The defect vanishes exactly on the central multiples of real unit quaternions, so the Hermitian pair is an automorphism only there.

The rigidity.

$$ T(\mathbb{B}_{\Phi})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}\iff T(\mathbb{B}_{\Psi})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}\iff TJ=JT,\qquad J(\tilde X)=\tilde X^{*}; \qquad L_{\tilde A}R_{\tilde B}\ \text{preserves}\ \mathbb{B}_{\Phi}\iff \tilde B=\lambda\tilde A^{*},\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}, $$

with the same $\lambda$ preserving $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$. The argument of $\lambda$ decides the fate: real keeps both halves, purely imaginary swaps them, mixed destroys both. The Hermitian pair is the case $\lambda=1$, which is why it needs no hypothesis.

The span. Writing $\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}=\sum_{{\imath}{\jmath}}a_\mu\bar a_\nu M_{{\imath}{\jmath}}$ with $M_{{\imath}{\jmath}}=L_{e_{\imath}}R_{e_{\jmath}^{*}}$, the coefficient matrix $aa^{\dagger}$ is Hermitian of rank one, and the sixteen $\mathrm{Hp}_{e_{\imath}}$, $\mathrm{Hp}_{e_{\imath}+e_{\jmath}}$, $\mathrm{Hp}_{e_{\imath}+ie_{\jmath}}$ are a real basis of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{B}_{\Psi})$, of real dimension sixteen (forty-eight without $\mathbb{C}$-linearity). Every sector-preserving map is a real combination of sixteen Hermitian pairs.

The spectrum and the image.

$$ \operatorname{spec}\mathrm{Hp}_{\tilde Q}=\{\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j\},\qquad \ker=\mathrm{U}(1),\qquad \operatorname{im}|_{\text{invertibles}}=CO^{+}(1,3),\qquad \text{slice } \varrho=1:\ SO^{+}(1,3). $$

10. The SVD and the Weights

$$ F=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\rho_k\,\mathcal P_{\tilde U_k,\tilde V_k},\qquad \rho_1\ge\rho_2\ge\rho_3\ge\rho_4\ge0,\qquad F(\tilde V_k)=\rho_k\tilde U_k,\qquad \mathcal P_{\tilde U_1,\tilde U_2}(\tilde X)=\tilde U_1\langle\tilde X,\tilde U_2\rangle_{*} . $$

The non-trivial claim. Four terms is the complex dimension of the algebra, so the bound is not a property of the map; the content is that four always suffice and that the weights and the two orthonormal families can be chosen at once. The decomposition is proved here, not quoted.

The operator form, and the algebra form.

$$ F=\mathcal U\Sigma\mathcal V^{*},\quad W\Sigma=\Sigma Z;\qquad F=\Bigl(\sum_{k\le4}\mathcal P_{\tilde U_k,e_k}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{k\le4}\rho_k\mathcal P_{e_k,e_k}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{k\le4}\mathcal P_{e_k,\tilde V_k}\Bigr). $$

The non-trivial claim. The middle factor is the scale in the basis of the units, and $\sum_k\mathcal P_{e_k,e_k}=\mathrm{I}_4$. So the three factors are sums of four atoms with no array anywhere, and the freedom of the pair is a phase per term plus a unitary mixing inside each group of equal weights.

The identities.

$$ \rho_1\rho_2\rho_3\rho_4=\lvert\det F\rvert,\qquad \rho_1^{2}+\rho_2^{2}+\rho_3^{2}+\rho_4^{2}=\lVert F\rVert^{2},\qquad \rho_1=\lVert F\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}, $$

and for a one-sided operator, with the pair repeated twice,

$$ \rho_1^{2}+\rho_2^{2}=2\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*},\qquad \rho_1\rho_2=\lvert N(\tilde Q)\rvert . $$

The non-trivial claim. The weights of a left multiplier are the two algebraic norms of the element read backwards: the Hermitian norm gives the sum of squares, the quaternionic norm gives the product. So the two norms of the algebra are the two symmetric functions of one pair, and no third invariant exists. Both identities were checked numerically on random elements, with the singular-value pattern $(\rho_1,\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_2)$.

The two-sided operator.

$$ L_{\tilde Q_1}R_{\tilde Q_2}=L_{\tilde U_1}R_{\tilde U_2}\,L_{\tilde H_1}R_{\tilde H_2},\qquad \bigl\lvert L_{\tilde Q_1}R_{\tilde Q_2}\bigr\rvert=L_{\tilde H_1}R_{\tilde H_2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Each multiplier is decomposed with its unitary away from the middle, so the two unitaries form a unitary operator and the two Hermitian factors a positive one. The weights are the four products of the two pairs, so the rank is $0,1,2$ or $4$ and never 3, and the two middle weights are equal. The value set $\{0,1,2,4\}$ was checked numerically, including on zero-divisor parameters.

The product against the atom. A two-sided operator with two zero-divisor multipliers has rank one; conversely, an elementary endomorphism $\mathcal P_{\tilde U,\tilde V}$ is a product only when both its vectors are zero divisors. Hence the generic atom is not a product, and a product atom carries two normalisations at once, orthonormal for the form and degenerate for the algebra. The source records the product family as four-dimensional inside the six of all rank-one endomorphisms; that count was not rechecked here and is recorded, not established.

11. The Three Matrix Models

The axis-free model. Every one of the sixteen $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_{\imath}}R_{\mathrm{axf},e_{\jmath}}$ is a real signed permutation matrix — four nonzero entries, one per row and column, each $0,\pm1$ — and the sixteen are orthogonal of Hilbert–Schmidt norm two. Their real span is $M_4(\mathbb{R})$: the axis-free frame is the one in which the real structure of the operator algebra is visible.

The stitch and the copies.

$$ R_{\mathrm{axf}}=D(L_{\mathrm{axf}})^{\mathsf{T}}D,\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1);\qquad \mathbb{B}_L=\{L_{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde Q)\},\quad \mathbb{B}_R=\{R_{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde Q_2)\},\quad \mathbb{B}_L\cap\mathbb{B}_R=\text{scalars}. $$

The Pauli model, and the Kronecker trap.

The non-trivial claim. $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}$ is the complex-linear part of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}}$, of real dimension thirty-two, not a summand. Everything in §12 to §14 is a statement about which of the two algebras carries a given notion of "real structure".

The Jordan lattice. The non-trivial claim. 19. Spin One Half, the Pair, and the Three Carriers

The forcing. A one-sided multiplication is multiplicative only for the unit, since $R(XY)$ carries one factor $R$ and $(RX)(RY)$ carries two. Conjugation $\Theta(X)=RXR^{-1}$ is multiplicative for every invertible $R$; therefore a two-sided product is forced, and it is the only inner-type action available.

The Casimir.

$$ L_k^{2}=-\mathrm{I}_4,\qquad [L_x,L_y]=2L_z,\qquad \mathbf{J}^{2}=\sum_kJ_k^{2}=-\tfrac34\mathrm{I}_4,\qquad H_k=iJ_k:\ \mathbf{H}^{2}=+\tfrac34\mathrm{I}_4 . $$

The non-trivial claim. $s(s+1)=\tfrac34$ gives $s=\tfrac12$ on the irreducible carrier, and the weights $\pm\tfrac12$ confirm it. The real dimension is $4$ where $2s+1=2$ because the carrier is quaternionic; the factor of two in the dimension is not a second spin.

The pair.

$$ S^{3}_L\times S^{3}_R\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{H},\qquad \tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12=0\oplus1,\qquad \mathbf{J}^{2}_{\mathrm{tot}}=2(\mathrm{I}-Q),\qquad \operatorname{spec}_{\mathbb{H}}\{0,2\}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The total Casimir is $2(\mathrm{I}-Q)$ with $Q$ the projection onto the singlet: $0$ once and $2$ three times on $\mathbb{H}$, and $0$ four times and $2$ twelve times on $\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{H}$. The projection commutes with the total generators and not with either factor alone — that is the precise sense in which $0\oplus1$ belongs to the pair and not to the single rotor. A full turn is $-1$ on the single carrier and $+1$ on both pair carriers.

The two marks coincide only on the rotor sphere.

$$ \tilde X\mapsto\tilde Q\tilde X\tilde Q^{\natural}\ \text{is an automorphism}\iff N(\tilde Q)=1;\qquad \tilde u^{*}=\tilde u^{\natural}=\tilde u^{-1}\ \text{on the rotor sphere}. $$

The non-trivial claim. On the rotor sphere the $\natural$-operator and the $*$-operator are the same operator; on the boost sector $\natural$ is still the inverse, since a boost has norm one, and the operator that fails there is the $*$-operator. That is why the corpus's spin is written with $\natural$ and not with $*$.

20. The Spin-Zero Centre Subspace

The carrier. $\mathbb{R}e_t$, of real dimension one; closure $\mathbb{C}e_t$, of real dimension two; trivial action, zero generator, spectrum $\{0\}$, full turn $+1$.

$$ N(\lambda e_t)=\lambda^{2},\qquad \operatorname{Re}N\ \text{has inertia } (1,1),\qquad \text{isotropic lines } \mathbb{R}(e_t\pm ie_t),\qquad N=\pm2i\ \text{there}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The complex norm is nondegenerate on the centre (only the origin is null) while its real part is indefinite of inertia $(1,1)$ with two isotropic lines. A reader who reports from the real part alone that the centre is isotropic has reported a fact about one real form and not about the norm. This is the trap of the whole spin-zero group in one line.

The spectral answer, and it is the opposite extreme.

$$ \operatorname{spec}(\lambda e_t)=\{\lambda\}\ \text{with multiplicity two},\qquad B^{2}=0\ \text{identically}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Every central element is spectrally degenerate and no nonzero one is norm-degenerate: the two predicates are as far apart as they can be, because their difference is $N-B^{2}=Q_t^{2}$, which at the centre is the whole norm. The double eigenvalue is a multiplicity of the space and not a defect of the element: the minimal polynomial has degree one.

The sectors.

$$ \mathbb{C}^{\times}e_t\subset\Sigma_{10},\qquad 0\in\Sigma_{00},\qquad \mathbb{C}e_t\cap\Sigma_{11}=\varnothing,\qquad \mathbb{C}e_t\cap\Sigma_{01}=\varnothing . $$

The non-trivial claim. The four sectors collapse to two, and the intersection with $\Sigma_{10}$ is exactly the scalar stratum of that sector: no central element is a Jordan element. The punctured centre is a subgroup of the invertibles lying inside a single sector, which no other carrier supplies.

21. The Spin-Half Family and the Fierz–Kofink Identities

The three levels.

$$ \Sigma(\vec u_{\mathrm r})=\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}(\vec u_{\mathrm r})\ (2),\qquad S_\pm(\vec u_{\mathrm r})=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}(\vec u_{\mathrm r})\ (4),\qquad \mathbb{B}=S_+\oplus S_-\ (8), $$

nested, $\Sigma\subset S_+\subset\mathbb{B}$.

The non-trivial claim, and the sharpest statement of the whole block.

$$ N\equiv0\ \text{on the plane and on each ideal};\qquad B^{2}=-\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\ \text{on the plane};\qquad \operatorname{spec}(\lambda\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+})=\{0,\lambda\}. $$

The plane is null everywhere and spectrally regular away from the origin. So the two predicates are independent: neither implies the other, and a carrier can sit at one extreme for one and at the other extreme for the other. The spinor space is a maximal isotropic subspace of real dimension four, and inside it the degenerate locus is the real plane $\mathbb{C}(\vec u_2-i\vec u_3)$ of square-zero elements.

The bispinor read.

$$ N(X_++X_-)=2B(X_+,X_-). $$

The non-trivial claim. On the bispinor space the norm becomes a pairing between the two halves: the first index of the sector asks whether the components pair at all, the second asks whether $B^{2}$ survives. That is why all four sectors occur there and only two on each closure. The witnesses are $e_t+2\vec e_x$, $e_t$, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}$, $\vec u_2-i\vec u_3$.

The family, and the axis.

$$ \text{one plane per axis},\quad \text{two spinor spaces per axis},\quad \text{one bispinor space per axis up to sign};\qquad \text{family indexed by } \mathbb{R}P^{2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. A bispinor space is not a subspace: as a set every member is $\mathbb{B}$. The family is a family of decompositions of one space, and nothing singles one out. Writing "the bispinor space" is the error; writing "a bispinor space" is correct.

The exchange.

$$ \text{the two parts are exchanged on the right by a real unit vector } \perp \vec u_{\mathrm r},\ \text{left-linear, squaring to } -1 . $$

The non-trivial claim. No left multiplication crosses the two ideals; the exchange is a right multiplication. It preserves the norm and not $B^{2}$, so it moves elements between sectors. The exchanges form a single complex line spanned by the corner element of the axis, so one complex number times a fixed exchange is the whole coupling datum — no array and no Clebsch–Gordan table is needed.

The Fierz–Kofink layer, imported and checked.

$$ J^2=\sigma^2+\omega^2,\qquad J^2=-K^2,\qquad J\cdot K=0,\qquad J_{\imath} K_{\jmath}-J_{\jmath} K_{\imath}=\omega S_{{\imath}{\jmath}}+\sigma\tilde S_{{\imath}{\jmath}}, $$

$$ Z=4\psi\psi^{\dagger}\,iR_{\vec e_x},\qquad Z^2=4\sigma Z,\qquad \psi=\frac{Z\zeta}{4\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}\zeta}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The sixteen bilinear covariants are not independent: the identities force exactly six classes — three regular ($\sigma$ or $\omega$ nonzero) and three singular (flag-dipole, flagpole, dipole). The flag-dipole is the type that the usual list of Dirac, real and chiral spinors does not exhaust. All six were verified non-empty by explicit representatives; $Z$ is rank one, so every aggregate identity collapses on the scalar in the middle, and the boomerang recovers the spinor. This layer is imported from Lounesto and Fauser and transcribed into the corpus's signs; it is not derived here, and the internal two-dimensional Fierz identity it completes is a different statement.

22. The Spin-One Anti-Hermitian Subspace

The carrier and its closure.

$$ \mathfrak{k}=\mathbb{R}\vec e_x\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec e_y\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec e_z,\qquad \overline{\mathfrak{k}}=\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathbb{R}(ie_t)\oplus\mathfrak{k},\qquad \operatorname{spec}=\{+1,-1,0\},\qquad \tfrac12\vec u_{\mathrm r}\ \text{acts by } \vec u_{\mathrm r}\times . $$

The norm, and the three possible answers side by side.

$$ N(i\lambda+\vec v)=\lvert\vec v\rvert^{2}-\lambda^{2}\quad\text{on the closure, inertia }(3,1);\qquad N(\vec v)=\lvert\vec v\rvert^{2}\quad\text{on the carrier, positive definite}. $$

The non-trivial claim. The closure is indefinite, with a null cone of real dimension three and two nappes, while the carrier is positive definite and meets the cone only at the origin. The indefiniteness comes entirely from the extra negative line $\mathbb{R}(ie_t)$: the splitting is orthogonal for the polar form, so the norm of a sum is the sum of the norms of the parts. This is the third of the three possible answers, the middle one.

The spectrum, and why it is imaginary.

$$ \operatorname{spec}(i\lambda+\vec v)=i(\lambda\pm\lvert\vec v\rvert),\qquad \operatorname{spec}(\vec v)=\pm i\lvert\vec v\rvert,\qquad B^{2}=\lvert\vec v\rvert^{2}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Both eigenvalues are purely imaginary and symmetric about $i\lambda$; the carrier's pair is symmetric about the origin. The anti-Hermitian condition forces this, so the symmetry is not a coordinate accident. The degenerate locus of the closure is exactly the line $\mathbb{R}(ie_t)$, and the degenerate and null loci are disjoint except at the origin — a sharper statement than the carrier comparison.

The sectors.

$$ \vec v\neq0:\ \Sigma_{11};\qquad \mathbb{R}(ie_t)\setminus\{0\}:\ \Sigma_{10};\qquad \text{null cone}\setminus\{0\}:\ \Sigma_{01};\qquad 0:\ \Sigma_{00}. $$

The non-trivial claim. On the carrier the two predicates are one function, since $N-B^{2}=Q_t^{2}$ and $Q_t=0$ there, so the carrier meets only $\Sigma_{11}$ and $\Sigma_{00}$. On the closure all four occur, and it is the only proper closure in the block that meets all four. The reason is geometric: two real quadratic forms on four coordinates, each cutting a cone, and the four sectors are the four combinations of side and side.

The one exclusion.

$$ (i\lambda+\vec v)^{2}=-(\lambda^{2}+\lvert\vec v\rvert^{2})e_t+2i\lambda\vec v=0\iff \lambda=\vec v=0 . $$

The non-trivial claim. The anti-Hermitian closure contains no nonzero square-zero element, so the degenerate plane that appears inside a spinor space has no analogue here, and the intersection with $\Sigma_{10}$ is central elements alone, that is the scalar stratum.

Summary

The block in nine formulas.

$$ L_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde QX,\quad R_{\tilde Q}(X)=X\tilde Q,\quad \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=R_{\tilde Q^{*}}L_{\tilde Q}, $$

$$ \dim(L+R)=4+4-1=7,\quad \dim\text{cone}=8-1=7,\quad \dim\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}=16,\quad \mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{sl}_2\otimes\mathfrak{sl}_2,\ \dim 9, $$

$$ L_{\tilde Q}=\natural R_{\natural(\tilde Q)}\natural,\qquad \mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}),\qquad (a[\,]b)\circ(c[\,]d)=(ac)[\,](db), $$

$$ \operatorname{spec}\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=\{\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j\},\quad \rho_1\rho_2=\lvert N(\tilde Q)\rvert,\quad \rho_1^{2}+\rho_2^{2}=2\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*},\quad \operatorname{rank}\mathrm{Sd}\in\{0,1,2,4\}, $$

$$ \mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})\ (16),\quad \mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}}\cong M_8(\mathbb{R})\ (64),\quad M_4(\mathbb{R})\ (10,6),\quad M_2(\mathbb{H})\ (6,10), $$

$$ N(i\lambda+\vec v)=\lvert\vec v\rvert^{2}-\lambda^{2},\qquad N(X_++X_-)=2B(X_+,X_-),\qquad N(\lambda e_t)=\lambda^{2}. $$

Five results to keep. The one-sided families span seven of sixteen dimensions and their linear sum is not a subalgebra. $L$ is a homomorphism and $R$ an anti-homomorphism, and one involution makes them one family at the price of a carried parameter. The two-sided family is a seven-dimensional cone, closed under composition and not under addition, and every matrix is a sum of at most four of its members. The two-sided operator doubles every quantity of the element and cancels the central phase. The two predicates are independent: $N$ decides the rank, $B^{2}$ decides the Jordan type.

Seven refusals. No spectral theorem on the indefinite form: the SVD exists because the Hermitian form is definite, and on the Krein form singular values can vanish without the operator doing so. No pin group: reflections lie outside the two-sided family and no nonzero member is anti-self-adjoint. No decision between rotation and reflection by the product: the insertion is a convention, the reflection theorem is not. No compact operator form: $\mathfrak{u}(4)$ is the Lie algebra of no real form of the associative algebra. No injective passage from the element level to the operator level: both element forms give $M_4(\mathbb{R})$. No distinguished bispinor space: the family is a family of decompositions indexed by $\mathbb{R}P^{2}$. No decision between the physical left and right actions: both are present, of the same type, and the algebraic half alone is closed.

One warning about the ledger. The operator calculus is established; the Fierz–Kofink identities and the six spinor classes are imported and transcribed; the four-dimensional count of the rank-one product family is recorded and was not rechecked here.

The Clifford Infestation: One Minus Carried by Two Hundred Lines

Introduction

The corpus has one metric, and it is the interval: $g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. The four cells of the frame do not square to it. They square to its negative, $(1,-1,-1,-1)$. So the Clifford line and the trace line carry a minus sign in front of $g$, and that minus is now written out 227 times: 171 times on a Clifford line and 56 times on a trace line, in 92 articles.

That is the whole of the issue, and it is a price paid once at the conventions and then paid again in every article. This note states the arithmetic, states the alternative that was refused and why, counts the sites, and names the cost.

What the two lines now read.

$$ \{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=-2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]I_4,\qquad \operatorname{tr}\bigl(\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\bigr)=-4g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]. $$

The Arithmetic

The two identities are read off the four squares and nothing else. The time cell squares to $+I_4$, the three spatial cells to $-I_4$, so the array of squares is $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$. The Clifford relation is the array of squares doubled, hence $2\varepsilon=-2g[\mathbb{M}_-]$. The trace of two cells is the same array with the fourfold weight, $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu)=4\varepsilon=-4g[\mathbb{M}_-]$.

Why the plus sign cannot be kept. Write the relation as the literature writes it, $\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]I_4$. At $\mu=\nu=0$ that reads $2(\gamma_0)^2=2g_{00}I_4=-2I_4$, so $(\gamma_0)^2=-I_4$. 52 of the 92 print $(\gamma_k)^2=-I_4$ in the same display, and 8 print $(\gamma_0)^2=+I_4$ beside it. Those are the articles where the contradiction is visible at a glance. The plus sign would make all four cells square to minus the identity, which is a Euclidean signature with the wrong overall sign and no time direction left. The minus is what keeps $(\gamma_0)^2=+I_4$ true.

Where the minus came from. It was not there before this migration. The old symbol $\eta$ carried $(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and the cells squared to $+\eta$, so the Clifford line read $+2\eta$ and was true. When the metric became the interval, the cells' array became its negative and the sign on every one of those lines had to flip. The symbol changed once; the arithmetic changed in 92 articles.

The alternative that was refused. Name the cells' array the metric instead: $g=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, no minus anywhere on the Clifford or trace line. That is what the corpus said before, under the name $\eta$. It was refused because the interval would then be minus the metric, and rule 8 of Frame Conventions states that the interval is the metric's norm, one sign from the one $i$ of $Q_t=ix_0$. One of the two statements must carry the minus. The corpus put it on the Clifford line and kept the interval clean.

What the Minus Reaches

The minus is not confined to the two textbook identities. Three further statements flipped with it.

  • The flat d'Alembertian. The square of the Dirac operator is $+\!g^{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]\partial_\mu\partial_\nu$, which is $-\partial_t^2+\Delta$, in Electroweak Theory under the Biquaternion Framework, The Chiral Condensate and Dynamical Symmetry Breaking, and Matter Makes Space.
  • The mass shell. $p_0^2-\vec p^2=-g^{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]p_\mu p_\nu$, in The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form.
  • The weight of the cells in a curved background. $g_{ab}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ is $-g_{ab}[\mathbb{M}_-]$, in Curved Spacetime and the Biquaternion Framework.

The Cost

Three prices are paid for the choice.

The sign has to be copied, not reconstructed. A reader who applies the literature's $\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2g_{\mu\nu}$ to this corpus gets $(\gamma_0)^2=-I_4$ and cannot see the error, because the line looks right. The only defence is that the minus is printed every time.

The corpus now disagrees in writing with its own sources. Every citation of a Clifford relation prints a sign the source does not print. The articles state the reason once, in Frame Conventions, and then carry the minus without repeating the reason.

Sixteen articles carry the minus and never state the metric. In these the reader meets $-2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]$ with no tuple to compare it to, and no local way to check the sign: Chiral Fermions in the Biquaternion Framework, The Dirac–Hestenes Equation and Spacetime Algebra, Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass, Bosonization, The Covariant Derivative and Gauge Connection, The Fierz–Kofink Identities (maths), The Grading, Fermion Parity and the Two Sectors, The Lie–Jordan Decomposition (maths), The Minimal Coupling of the Biquaternion Dirac Field, The Neutrino and Majorana Fermions, The Pion and the Chiral Lagrangian, The Quantized Dirac Field, The Field-Strength Biquaternion, The Spinor Representation of the Lorentz Group, The Theta Parameter and Strong CP, and Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices.

The escape route stays open. If the second convention is ever preferred, the change is a renaming: call the cells' array $g$ and the interval $-g$. Two hundred and twenty-seven lines lose their minus and one rule of Frame Conventions changes. Nothing else in the corpus depends on which of the two names carries the sign.

The Two Contradictions It Already Produced

The minus is not only a burden on the reader. It has already landed in prose, twice, in the same pass that added it.

The Reflection and the Rotation in Biquaternionic Form identified $\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$ with $g$ in one display, and two sentences later gave $g=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$. Under the old convention both sentences were true of the same array under different names. Under the new one the second array is the metric and the first is the cells' squares, so the identification is false. The first array is now $\varepsilon$.

The Biquaternion Basis of a Unified Field Fibre Bundle said the four generators satisfy the algebra "for the metric $\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$". That array is the squares, not the metric. It is now $g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$ with the squares named as $-\!g[\mathbb{M}_-]$.

Both were correct sentences before the migration and became wrong ones during it. That is the shape of the risk: the symbol changed, the arithmetic changed, and a sentence that names an array as the metric went on reading as true.

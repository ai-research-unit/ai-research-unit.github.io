Internal Summary of the Biquaternion Operators

Introduction

What this file is. A compressed map of the Operators block of the maths category Biquaternions, forty articles. It gives the objects, the boxed equations and the non-trivial claims. No proofs: an agent re-derives them. Every formula is copied from the source articles named at the end of the section.

What the block does. It takes the one algebra and asks what its own linear maps are. The answer has a spine:

$$ L_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde QX,\qquad R_{\tilde Q}(X)=X\tilde Q,\qquad \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde QX\tilde Q^{*}, $$

$$ \boxed{\;\dim L(\mathbb{B})=\dim R(\mathbb{B})=4,\qquad \dim\bigl(L(\mathbb{B})\cap R(\mathbb{B})\bigr)=1,\qquad \dim\bigl(L(\mathbb{B})+R(\mathbb{B})\bigr)=7\;} $$

$$ \boxed{\;\mathbb{B}^{e}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})\;} $$

The non-trivial claim, and it organises the whole block. The algebra of one-sided operators is seven-dimensional, not eight: $L$ and $R$ overlap exactly on the scalars. So the operators almost span $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}$, and the missing direction is exactly the two-sided sandwich, which is why $\mathrm{Sd}$ is not an $L$ or an $R$ but a product of the two.

Why this matters to an agent. A "two-sided operator" is not a new object requiring a new theorem: it is the composite $R_{\tilde Q^{*}}\circ L_{\tilde Q}$, and its properties follow from the two one-sided families. Every closure statement of the block ("from the rotor to the spin", "from the spin to the carriers") is an instance of this composite.

The one-sentence version. The one-sided families overlap on the scalars alone, so a two-sided operator is a composite of a left and a right multiplier and never a new primitive.

1. The Four Products Are the Source

$$ \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2,\qquad \tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2,\qquad \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*},\qquad \tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Each product, read as a map in the second slot, gives a different adjoint pair. The four products do not give four theories; they give the four ways a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map can be related to its adjoint. This is why the operator block begins with the products and not with the maps.

2. The One-Sided Families

$$ L_{\lambda\tilde Q+\mu\tilde Q_2}=\lambda L_{\tilde Q}+\mu L_{\tilde Q_2},\qquad L_{\tilde Q}L_{\tilde Q_2}=L_{\tilde Q\tilde Q_2},\qquad R_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}=R_{\tilde Q_2\tilde Q}, $$

$$ L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}=R_{\tilde Q_2}L_{\tilde Q},\qquad \operatorname{Ann}_r(\tilde Q)=\{X:\tilde QX=0\},\qquad \operatorname{Ann}_l(\tilde Q)=\{X:X\tilde Q=0\}. $$

The non-trivial claim. $L$ is a homomorphism and $R$ is an anti-homomorphism. This single sign is the origin of the transposition identity

$$ L_{\tilde Q}=\natural\,R_{\natural(\tilde Q)}\,\natural,\qquad R_{\tilde Q}=\natural\,L_{\natural(\tilde Q)}\,\natural, $$

so left and right are the same family conjugated by the involution. There is no separate theory of right multiplication.

The kernel is the annihilator. $\ker L_{\tilde Q}=\mathrm{Ann}_r(\tilde Q)$, and it is nonzero exactly on the zero-divisor cone. The non-trivial claim: injectivity of $L_{\tilde Q}$ is invertibility of $\tilde Q$, with no intermediate case. The reason is finite dimension: injective implies surjective implies a right inverse, and a right inverse in a finite-dimensional algebra is an inverse. So the operator picture and the algebraic picture agree exactly, and there is no "almost invertible" operator.

3. Two-Sided Operators and the Enveloping Algebra

$$ L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q_2}(X)=\tilde Q\,X\,\tilde Q_2,\qquad \bigl(L_aR_b\bigr)\bigl(L_cR_d\bigr)=L_{ac}R_{db}, $$

$$ \mathbb{B}^{e}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}},\qquad (\tilde Q_1\otimes\tilde Q_{\mathrm{opp},2})\cdot\tilde Q_3=\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_3\tilde Q_2,\qquad \mathbb{B}^{e}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}). $$

The non-trivial claim. The sandwich product law $L_aR_bL_cR_d=L_{ac}R_{db}$ has the inner indices contracted, not the outer ones — the reverse of matrix multiplication in the second factor. That single reversal is why $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$ and not $\mathbb{B}$ appears in the enveloping algebra.

The sandwich, and its own law.

$$ \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde QX\tilde Q^{*},\qquad \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=R_{\tilde Q^{*}}\circ L_{\tilde Q},\qquad \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q\tilde Q'}=\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}\circ\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q'}, $$

$$ \boxed{\;\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q_H}\circ\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q_0},\qquad \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=4a^{2}\,\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q_0}\ \text{ when }\ \tilde Q=2a\tilde Q_0\;.} $$

The non-trivial claim. The sandwich is multiplicative and it respects the polar decomposition: the modulus part acts as a scalar rescaling, so the projective sandwich — the one that matters for rotations — depends only on the unit part. This is the operator-level statement of "a rotation is unchanged by rescaling".

4. Adjoints, Hermitian Pairs, Transposition

$$ \langle F\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\phi}=\langle\tilde Q_1,F^{\phi}\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\phi}\qquad\text{(adjoint pair)}, $$

$$ (F^{\approx})^{\approx}=F,\qquad (F\circ G)^{\approx}=G^{\approx}\circ F^{\approx}\qquad\text{(the transposition involution)}. $$

The non-trivial claim. Each of the four forms gives an adjoint operation, and the four are related by

$$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde Q_1,\bar{\tilde Q}_2\rangle_{\natural},\qquad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q_1^{\natural},\tilde Q_2\rangle . $$

So the Hermitian theory has one adjoint operation that looks like four, and the "Hermitian pair" articles are the four displays of that one. The Hermitian pair of $L$ is $R$ with a rotated involution; the Hermitian pair of $L R$ is the sandwich with the rotated one.

The non-trivial claim about the four sectors. The dimension count

$$ \dim L=4,\quad \dim R=4,\quad \dim(L\cap R)=1,\quad \dim(L+R)=7 $$

means that the one-sided operators span a seven-dimensional Lie algebra. Its commutators are the inner derivations, of real dimension six, and the scalars contribute one central direction, so the algebra is $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$. This is the same $6+1$ split the Geometry block gets from the versors, and the operator language and the group language are the same statement.

5. The Block Structure on the Regular Sector

The splitting of $L$, and the two halves.

$$ L_{\tilde Q}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{\tilde Q}&O_{\tilde Q}\\O_{\tilde Q}&D_{\tilde Q}\end{pmatrix},\qquad D_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde QX+X\tilde Q^{*}\bigr),\qquad O_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde QX-X\tilde Q^{*}\bigr). $$

The non-trivial claim. On the N-regular sector the operator splits into a Hermitian part $D$ and an anti-Hermitian part $O$ with respect to $*$. That is a Cartan decomposition of the operator, and its two factors are the "dilatation" and "rotation" of the map. This is where the operator block meets the geometry block: $D$ is the stretch, $O$ is the turn.

The spin-zero, spin-half, spin-one strata. The block tests three subspaces against N-regularity and spectral regularity:

$$ \mathbb{C}e_t\ \ (\text{spin zero}),\qquad \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bisp}}\ \ (\text{spin half}),\qquad \mathbb{B}_{\Phi}\ \ (\text{spin one, anti-Hermitian}), $$

and reports, for each, whether the operator families act faithfully, whether they preserve the subspace, and which stratum of N and of the spectrum they meet.

The non-trivial claim about the centre. On $\mathbb{C}e_t$ the norm is $N(\lambda e_t)=\lambda^{2}$, whose only zero is $\lambda=0$: the centre has no zero divisors. So the spin-zero sector is regular by construction, and every failure the block reports elsewhere is a failure off the centre.

6. Spinors and the Fierz–Kofink Identities

The five bilinears, and the identity they obey.

$$ \sigma=\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}\psi,\qquad \omega=i\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}(-iR_{\vec e_z})\psi,\qquad J_0=\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}iR_{\vec e_x}\psi,\qquad J_k=\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}\,iL_{\vec e_k}R_{\vec e_y}\psi, $$

$$ \boxed{\;J^{2}=\sigma^{2}+\omega^{2},\qquad J^{2}=-K^{2},\qquad J\cdot K=0\;} $$

The non-trivial claim. These are exactly the Fierz–Kofink identities of the Dirac theory, and here they are operator identities on the eight-dimensional carrier, not spinor identities obtained case by case. The classification of spinors (the "flags" of the spinor variety) then follows from the signs of $J^2$ and $K^2$, and an agent gets the classification for free.

The rotor group.

$$ \mathrm{Rot}=\{\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}:\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}=1\}=\{\tilde q:|\tilde q|=1\}=S^{3},\qquad [J_x,J_y]=2J_z,\quad[J_y,J_z]=2J_x,\quad[J_z,J_x]=2J_y . $$

The non-trivial claim. The factor $\tfrac12$ in the exponent, $\exp(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\vec u)$, is the consequence of the sandwich being quadratic in $\tilde q$: $\tilde q\tilde X\tilde q^{\natural}$ needs a half angle to give the angle. Spin is therefore not added to the algebra; it is forced by the quadratic action, and the closure chain "rotor → spin of the rotor → spin of a pair of two-sided operators → the carriers" is exactly this forcing written four times.

7. The Matrix Models of the Operators

$$ L_{\mathrm{axf}}(\tilde Q)=\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&-Q_x&-Q_y&-Q_z\\Q_x&Q_t&-Q_z&Q_y\\Q_y&Q_z&Q_t&-Q_x\\Q_z&-Q_y&Q_x&Q_t\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\{F\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}}:FJ=JF\}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}). $$

The non-trivial claim. The axis-free model is the real form and the Pauli model is the complex form; the relation between them is exactly the commutation with the complex structure $J$. An agent converting between the two should convert by imposing $FJ=JF$, not by matching entries.

The SVD.

$$ F=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\rho_k\,\mathcal P_{\tilde U_k,\tilde V_k},\qquad \rho_1\ge\rho_2\ge\rho_3\ge\rho_4\ge0,\qquad F(\tilde V_k)=\rho_k\tilde U_k , $$

with $\mathcal P$ the rank-one operators built from the positive form $H$.

The non-trivial claim. The SVD exists because $H$ is positive definite — and $H$ is the only one of the four forms that is. So the SVD is the one operator decomposition that cannot be moved to the Krein form: on $\mathrm{Kr}$ the singular values can vanish without the operator doing so.

Summary

The block in six formulas.

$$ L_{\tilde Q}(X)=\tilde QX,\quad R_{\tilde Q}(X)=X\tilde Q,\quad \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=R_{\tilde Q^{*}}L_{\tilde Q}, $$

$$ \dim(L)+\dim(R)-\dim(L\cap R)=4+4-1=7,\qquad \mathbb{B}^{e}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}),\qquad J^{2}=\sigma^{2}+\omega^{2},\quad J\cdot K=0 . $$

Five results to keep. One-sided operators span seven of the eight dimensions. $L$ is a homomorphism, $R$ an anti-homomorphism, and the transposition identity makes them one family. The sandwich is the polar-decomposition-respecting composite, and the half-angle is forced by its quadratic action. The five Fierz–Kofink bilinears are operator identities. The SVD lives on the positive form only.

Three refusals. The block does not produce a division algebra of operators. It does not give a spectral theorem on the Krein form. It does not add spin: spin is proved, not assumed.

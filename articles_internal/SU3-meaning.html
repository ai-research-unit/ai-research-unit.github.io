The Meaning of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ in the Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra

Introduction

The question. The corpus writes $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ in the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ articles of the $M_3(\mathbb{C})$ rung and in the electroweak and strong sections, and it never says what the group is inside the biquaternion algebra. This note answers that: it names the subspace of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$ that is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, writes its eight generators as products of left and right multiplications, and states in operator language what the two defining conditions — anti-Hermitian and traceless — mean.

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathfrak{su}(3)=\Bigl\{X\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}):\;Xe_t=0,\;X(B_V)\subseteq B_V,\;X^{\dagger}=-X,\;\operatorname{tr}X=0\Bigr\} \;\cong\;\Bigl\{\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&A\end{pmatrix}:\;A^{\dagger}=-A,\;\operatorname{tr}A=0\Bigr\} \;} $$

with $B_V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$ and the dagger taken for the Hermitian form $\langle X,Y\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(XY^{*})$. The group is

$$ \mathrm{SU}(3)=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(e_t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,R),\quad R\in\mathrm{U}(3),\;\det R=1, $$

real dimension eight, and it acts on the vector part $B_V\cong\mathbb{C}^{3}$ in the defining representation.

Why the statement is needed. Three sentences of the corpus point at this group and none of them constructs it. The Involution and the Hermitian Structure of $M_3(\mathbb{C})$ defines $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}=8$ at the element level. The $M_1\oplus M_3$ Corner of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ describes the block structure that the display above uses. The Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra proves that the derivations are the inner ones, of complex dimension three. This note joins the three: the corner is the carrier, the derivations are the three-dimensional "rotor" part of the eight, and the five remaining dimensions are the traceless symmetric part.

What is claimed, and what is not. Claimed: the eight generators exist, each is a sum of exactly two Conway words, and every property below is verified numerically. Not claimed: that this group is a symmetry of the algebra. It is not, and §The Boundary says in what precise sense: no non-zero element of this $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is $\mathbb{B}$-linear, so the triplet is not a $\mathbb{B}$-module and the corpus's verdict in Why the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ but Not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and in The Gluon: An Octet Outside the Biquaternion Algebra stands unchanged. What the embedding buys is an operator-level home for the group, not a derivation of it.

The Carrier, the Word Basis, and the Trace

The carrier. $\mathbb{B}$ is four complex dimensions, with basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and the products

$$ \vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z,\qquad \vec e_y\vec e_z=\vec e_x,\qquad \vec e_z\vec e_x=\vec e_y,\qquad \vec e_x^{2}=\vec e_y^{2}=\vec e_z^{2}=-e_t . $$

The scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)=Q_t$, and $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=\sum_\mu Q_{1\mu}Q_{2\mu}$.

The two families. $L_a(\tilde X)=a\tilde X$ and $R_b(\tilde X)=\tilde Xb$ are the two regular families of Operators Acting on One Side. They are $\mathbb{C}$-linear, injective, and they commute; the sixteen composites

$$ e_\mu[\,]e_\nu=L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu},\qquad e_\mu[\,]e_\nu(\tilde X)=e_\mu\tilde Xe_\nu, $$

are a basis of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$ by the generation theorem of Operators Acting on Two Sides, with the composition rule $(a[\,]b)\circ(c[\,]d)=(ac)[\,](db)$ and the isomorphism $\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, $a\otimes b\mapsto L_aR_b$.

The coefficient matrix and the length. Every operator has one coefficient array $c(M)$ in this basis, $M=\sum_{\mu\nu}c_{\mu\nu}\,e_\mu[\,]e_\nu$, and the length is $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$. The Conway theorem is $\ell\le4$, and the corpus reads it as the ladder $1,4,7,16$: the scalars, one side, both sides, the ambient space.

The Hermitian form and the dagger. Write $\langle X,Y\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(XY^{*})=\sum_\mu X_\mu\overline{Y_\mu}$, of Gram matrix $\mathrm{I}_4$ and inertia $(8,0)$ over $\mathbb{R}$ by The Four Forms and the Four Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion Algebra. In the coefficient basis its adjoint is the conjugate transpose, and the adjoint of a word is

$$ \bigl(L_aR_b\bigr)^{\dagger}=L_{\bar b}R_{\bar a}, $$

because the coefficient conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb{B}$ and fixes the structure constants. Hence

$$ M^{\dagger}=\overline{M}^{\,\mathsf T},\qquad M=\sum c_{\mu\nu}e_\mu[\,]e_\nu,\qquad M^{\dagger}=\sum_{\mu\nu}\overline{c_{\mu\nu}}\,s_\mu s_\nu\,e_\mu[\,]e_\nu, $$

with $s_t=+1$ and $s_x=s_y=s_z=-1$ the sign vector of $\bar{\cdot}$ on the basis.

The two functionals that matter. The trace of a word is a degenerate functional, and this is the fact that organises the whole note:

$$ \boxed{\;\operatorname{tr}\bigl(L_aR_b\bigr)=4\,\mathrm{Sc}(a)\,\mathrm{Sc}(b) \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \operatorname{tr}M=4\,c_{tt}(M)\;} $$

so that an operator is traceless exactly when its coefficient on the single word $e_t[\,]e_t$ vanishes. The identity is the word $e_t[\,]e_t$ itself, of trace $4$; the projector onto the unit line inside $\mathbb{B}$ has length four.

The Corner: Where the Group Lives

The split. The unit singles out a line, and its Hermitian orthogonal is the vector part:

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}e_t\;\oplus\;B_V,\qquad B_V=e_t^{\perp}\ \text{for }\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*},\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}B_V=3 . $$

This is the $1+3$ split of The $M_1\oplus M_3$ Corner of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$, and $e_t$ is the only distinguished element: the three vector units are permuted among themselves by the automorphisms of the algebra, as §No Axis Is Preferred shows.

The corner algebra. An operator is in the corner when it kills the unit line and preserves the vector part. Those two conditions are the linear conditions $Xe_t=0$ and $X(B_V)\subseteq B_V$, and together they cut out

$$ \Bigl\{X:\;Xe_t=0,\;X(B_V)\subseteq B_V\Bigr\} =\Bigl\{\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&A\end{pmatrix}:\;A\in M_3(\mathbb{C})\Bigr\}\cong M_3(\mathbb{C}), $$

of complex dimension nine. Its traceless part is $\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{C})$, of complex dimension eight, and its intersection with $\mathfrak{u}(4)$ is the compact real form.

The two-step compact form. The two conditions "anti-Hermitian" and "traceless" do different work, and the chain of subgroups shows it:

$$ iP=i\Bigl(\tfrac34\,e_t[\,]e_t+\tfrac14\bigl(e_x[\,]e_x+e_y[\,]e_y+e_z[\,]e_z\bigr)\Bigr), $$

of length four. It is the only member of $\mathfrak{u}(3)$ in this corner that needs the unit word, and that is why it is the only one that the trace detects.

The structure constants. Write $A_a=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_a}$, $H_a=h_a$ and $S_{ab}=i(E_{ab}+E_{ba})$, and let $(a,b,c)$ be cyclic in $(x,y,z)$, with $H_x+H_y+H_z=0$ and $S_{ab}=S_{ba}$. Then

$$ [A_a,A_b]=2\varepsilon_{abc}A_c,\qquad [A_a,S_{bc}]=4H_a,\qquad [A_a,S_{ab}]=2S_{ac},\qquad [A_a,S_{ac}]=-2S_{ab}, $$ $$ [H_a,S_{bc}]=-A_a,\qquad [H_a,S_{ab}]=\tfrac12 A_c,\qquad [H_a,H_b]=0,\qquad [S_{ab},S_{ac}]=\pm\tfrac12 A_a, $$

with the last sign $+$ for the cyclic order of the two lower pairs and $-$ for the reversed one: $[S_{xy},S_{xz}]=\tfrac12A_x$ but $[S_{xy},S_{yz}]=-\tfrac12A_y$. Every constant is one of $2,4,\tfrac12,1$; the two entries that read as fractions of thirds in a computation are not constants of the algebra, since $[A_x,S_{yz}]$ comes out as $2\tfrac23H_x-\tfrac43H_y-\tfrac43H_z$ when the three dependent diagonals $H_a$ are used as a basis, and that vector is $4H_x$. The constants belong to this normalisation and not to the algebra: the Hilbert–Schmidt norms squared are $8$ for $A_a$ and $2$ for $H_a$ and $S_{ab}$, so the frame is not orthonormal, and another normalisation gives another table of the same brackets.

Two words is the floor, four is the ceiling. No single word lies in $\mathfrak{su}(3)$: a word that annihilates the unit has $ab=0$, and then, writing $a=uv^{\mathsf T}$ and $b=wz^{\mathsf T}$ in $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, the condition $v^{\mathsf T}w=0$ makes

$$ aXb=(v^{\mathsf T}Xw)\,uz^{\mathsf T} $$

a single functional times one fixed matrix, of operator rank at most one. But $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ has no non-zero element of rank one: a rank-one member of the traceless corner reads $i\lambda ww^{\dagger}$ with $w\in\mathbb{C}^{3}$ and $\operatorname{tr}=i\lambda\lVert w\rVert^{2}$, which vanishes only at $w=0$. Hence $\ell\ge2$ on $\mathfrak{su}(3)\setminus\{0\}$; the eight above attain $2$; and a generic element of the eight-dimensional space attains $4$, the Conway bound. Length is not additive, and that is the whole content of the table: a basis of short words spans a space of long elements.

Anti-Hermitian, in Operator Language

What the condition means. A generator is anti-Hermitian when $\exp(tX)$ is unitary for real $t$, that is when the one-parameter group it generates preserves the Hermitian norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\sum_\mu|Q_\mu|^{2}$ of the carrier. Equivalently $X^{\dagger}=-X$ for the adjoint fixed by $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, equivalently $\overline{X}^{\,\mathsf T}=-X$, equivalently the spectrum of $X$ is purely imaginary.

The coefficient criterion. Transcribing the dagger of a word, $M$ is anti-Hermitian exactly when

$$ c_{\mu\nu}=-s_\mu s_\nu\,\overline{c_{\mu\nu}}\qquad\text{for all }\mu,\nu, $$

with $s=(+1,-1,-1,-1)$. The criterion reads off slot by slot, and it explains the two shapes of the table.

$(\mu,\nu)$ both in $\{x,y,z\}$ $+1$ $c$ purely imaginary the diagonal $h$'s and the off-diagonal symmetric sums
one index $t$, one vector $-1$ $c$ real the three differences $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_k}$
$(t,t)$ $+1$ $c$ purely imaginary the unit word $i\,e_t[\,]e_t$, of trace $4i$

So the antisymmetric combinations carry real coefficients and the symmetric ones carry imaginary coefficients, and the reason is the sign vector of the coefficient conjugation, not a convention about the generators.

The three differences are the compact half of a six-dimensional real form. $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u}=L_{\vec u}-R_{\vec u}$ is anti-Hermitian exactly when $\bar{\vec u}=\vec u$, that is when $\vec u$ is a real vector, which is three real dimensions. Replacing $\vec u$ by $i\vec u$ gives an Hermitian generator instead: the three $\mathrm{ad}_{i\vec e_k}$ are the boosts, they lie outside $\mathfrak{u}(4)$, and together with the three rotations they span the six real dimensions of $\mathfrak{so}(3,1)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\mathbb{C}}$ read over $\mathbb{R}$. The compact three is therefore not a choice among the six: it is the part that survives the dagger.

The finite statement. For a unit quaternion $\tilde u$, $\langle\tilde u\rangle_{\natural}=1$, the operator $\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde u}(\tilde X)=\tilde u\tilde X\tilde u^{-1}$ is unitary and of determinant one on the carrier, it fixes $e_t$, and it preserves $B_V$; hence $\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde u}\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$. This is the geometric content of the three $\mathrm{ad}$'s: the compact form of the automorphism group, $\mathrm{SO}(3)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathrm{SU}(3)$, is a subgroup of the group that this note is about. Verified on the twenty-four unit quaternions $\pm e_t,\pm\vec e_k,\tfrac12(\pm e_t\pm\vec e_x\pm\vec e_y\pm\vec e_z)$: all twenty-four give $\operatorname{diag}(1,R)$ with $R\in\mathrm{U}(3)$ and $\det R=1$.

Traceless, in Operator Language

What the condition means. $\operatorname{tr}X=0$ is $\det\exp(tX)=1$: the one-parameter group lands in $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and not merely in $\mathrm{U}(4)$. On the corner it is the condition $\operatorname{tr}A=0$ on the $3\times3$ block, and through $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ it is the determinant-one condition on the group.

Why it is the same as "no unit word". By the trace functional of §The Carrier, $\operatorname{tr}M=4c_{tt}(M)$. Hence

$$ \operatorname{tr}M=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_{tt}(M)=0 , $$

and every generator of the table is traceless for the same reason: none of them occupies the slot $e_t[\,]e_t$. A word $a[\,]b$ contributes to the trace only through $\mathrm{Sc}(a)\mathrm{Sc}(b)$, so a word with a non-scalar factor on either side is invisible to the trace, and a sum of two such words is traceless even though neither factor is.

What the condition removes. Inside the corner, the anti-Hermitian and unit-killing operators form $\mathfrak{u}(3)$, of dimension nine, and the ninth direction is $iP$, the projector onto $B_V$ multiplied by $i$. It is the only one of the nine that needs the unit word, its length is four, and its trace is $3i$. Tracelessness is therefore not a technicality: it is exactly the deletion of the $U(1)$ that a stabilizer of a line always carries, and it is the difference between "the group that fixes the unit line" and "the group that acts on the vector part with determinant one".

The operator-level reading of the pair of conditions. Putting the two sections together:

$$ X\in\mathfrak{su}(3)\iff \underbrace{Xe_t=0}_{c_{t\nu}=0}\ \text{and}\ \underbrace{X(B_V)\subseteq B_V}_{c_{\mu t}=0}\ \text{and}\ \underbrace{\overline{c_{\mu\nu}}s_\mu s_\nu=-c_{\mu\nu}}_{\text{anti-Hermitian}}\ \text{and}\ \underbrace{c_{tt}=0}_{\text{traceless}} . $$

Four linear conditions on the sixteen complex coefficients, and they cut out a real subspace of dimension eight. The counts run $32\to18\to9\to8$: the corner costs fourteen real dimensions, anti-Hermiticity nine, and tracelessness one.

Why the Rotor Guesses Fail, Precisely

The four candidates and the single test. Every element of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ kills the unit, and that one test settles four hypotheses at once, because the one-sided operators and the Hermitian pairs are exactly the operators that carry the unit somewhere.

The one-sided families are the operators that read the parameter at the unit, and $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is the subspace that annihilates the unit; the two are transverse by construction.

What survives of the guess. Not nothing. The intersection with the sum is three-dimensional,

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\bigl(L(\mathbb{B})+R(\mathbb{B})\bigr)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x},\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_y},\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_z}\}, $$

the rotor differences; and the intersection with the two-sided family is empty while the intersection with the symmetric sums of two words is five-dimensional,

$$ \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\ker(\sigma-1)\bigr)=5,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(3)\cap\ker(\sigma+1)\bigr)=3, $$

with $\sigma$ the side-swap $L_aR_b\mapsto L_bR_a$ of Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra, that is the transpose of the coefficient array. The eight splits $3+5$; only the $3$ is closed under the bracket, and the $5$ is its complement in the symmetric pair of §The $3+5$ Split and the Two Spins. A pair of rotors therefore gives three of the eight, and the other five are sums of two-sided words and nothing simpler.

What $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}$ Is

The operator. Write it in the four ways the corpus makes available:

$$ \mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}=L_{\vec e_x}-R_{\vec e_x} =\vec e_x[\,]e_t-e_t[\,]\vec e_x =2\,J^{\mathrm{tot}}_x , \qquad J^{\mathrm{tot}}_x=\tfrac12\bigl(L_{\vec e_x}-R_{\vec e_x}\bigr)=J_x-J'_x . $$

Reading one: a derivation. The Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra proves that every derivation of $\mathbb{B}$ is inner, $\delta=\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}$, that the kernel is the centre, and that $\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}/\mathbb{C}e_t$ with complex dimension three. The map $\tilde Q\mapsto\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}$ carries $B_V$ isomorphically onto the derivations, so

$$ \mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})=\mathrm{ad}(B_V)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x},\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_y},\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_z}\}, $$

and the three generators of the table's first rows are all the derivations there are. This is the sense in which the group's three-dimensional part is the algebra's own symmetry algebra, and the five remaining dimensions are not: a generator in the symmetric part satisfies no Leibniz rule.

Reading two: the derivative of a reflection, hence of a rotation. Differentiate the two-sided operator along the unit sphere of $\mathbb{B}$:

$$ \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0}\Bigl(L_{e^{t\vec e_x}}R_{e^{-t\vec e_x}}\Bigr)=L_{\vec e_x}R_{e_t}-L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_x}=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}. $$

The finite member is the operator of Introduction to the Operators of the Biquaternion Algebra, §Rotation, Reflection: for a vector $\tilde Q$ with $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$,

$$ L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{-1}}\big|_{V}=-\rho_{\tilde Q},\qquad L_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}R_{\tilde Q^{-1}}\big|_{V}=+\rho_{\tilde Q}, $$

with $\rho$ the reflection in the hyperplane $\tilde Q^{\perp}$. So $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}$ is the first-order term of the negative reflection, the sign insertion repairs the sign, and the composition of two reflections — which is what $\exp$ of the generator produces — is a rotation.

Reading three: the spin-one generator of the relative pair. From the Spin of a Single Rotor to the Spin of a Pair of Operators Acting on Two Sides computes $J_k=\tfrac12L_{\vec e_k}$ and $J'_k=\tfrac12R_{\vec e_k}$, each of Casimir $-\tfrac34\mathrm{I}_4$ — spin $\tfrac12$ — and identifies the diagonal generators of the pair as $J^{\mathrm{tot}}_k=J_k-J'_k$, of spin one. The identity above says that $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}$ is twice that generator. The factor $2$ of the matrix $\begin{smallmatrix}0&-2\\2&0\end{smallmatrix}$ that $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}$ carries on the plane of $(\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$ is therefore not a normalisation slip and not a choice: one $L$ is spin $\tfrac12$, the relative combination is spin one, and the bracket $[\vec e_x,\vec e_y]=2\vec e_z$ carries the same $2$.

Reading four: the geometry. Exponentiating,

$$ \exp\bigl(\theta\,J^{\mathrm{tot}}_x\bigr)=\exp\bigl(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}\bigr): \quad e_t\mapsto e_t,\quad \vec e_x\mapsto\vec e_x,\quad \vec e_y\mapsto\cos\theta\,\vec e_y+\sin\theta\,\vec e_z,\quad \vec e_z\mapsto-\sin\theta\,\vec e_y+\cos\theta\,\vec e_z . $$

Verified: at $\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ the block on $(\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$ is $\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}$; at $\theta=\pi$ it is $\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1)$, a half-turn of the plane orthogonal to the axis. Three properties of this flow are worth naming. It fixes the unit, so it is a flow of unital maps and not a movement of the unit line. It is odd under the side-swap, $\sigma(\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q})=-\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde Q}$, since $\sigma(L_aR_b)=L_bR_a$: it measures the disagreement of the two sides, which is why it is $J-J'$ and not $J+J'$. And it is the compact direction: the corresponding boost $\mathrm{ad}_{i\vec e_x}$ is Hermitian, has real spectrum, and generates no rotation.

No Axis Is Preferred

The apparent asymmetry is in the presentation. The rows of the table that name a diagonal both subtract $L_{\vec e_x}R_{\vec e_x}$, which reads as a preference for $x$. It is not one. For $(a,b,c)$ cyclic put

$$ h_a=\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(L_{\vec e_b}R_{\vec e_b}-L_{\vec e_c}R_{\vec e_c}\bigr), \qquad h_x+h_y+h_z=0, $$

with restrictions to $B_V$

$$ h_x=\operatorname{diag}(0,-i,i),\qquad h_y=\operatorname{diag}(i,0,-i),\qquad h_z=\operatorname{diag}(-i,i,0). $$

Any two of the three are a Cartan basis; the triple is cyclically closed and sums to zero. The table's $H_1=-h_z$ and $H_2=h_y$ are two of the three, and writing them that way hides the symmetry.

The group permutes the axes itself. Let $\tilde u=\tfrac12(e_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+\vec e_z)$, of norm one and of order three up to sign, and let $P=\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde u}$. Then, verified,

$$ P^{3}=\mathrm{I}_4,\qquad P\,\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x}P^{-1}=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_y},\qquad P\,\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_y}P^{-1}=\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_z}, $$

and $P$ carries $S_{xy}\mapsto S_{yz}\mapsto S_{xz}\mapsto S_{xy}$. The automorphism group of the algebra is therefore a subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ that acts on the three axes as the full symmetric group $S_3$ — the Weyl group of $A_2$ — so no axis and no Cartan is invariant. The only invariant is the splitting itself.

What is canonical, and what is chosen. The line $\mathbb{C}e_t$ is canonical, because the unit is. The three-dimensional space $B_V$ is canonical, because the Hermitian form is. The three axes inside $B_V$ are not: they are a basis, and the group moves them. Any statement about this $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ must therefore be stated either in the split $1+3$ or in $L,R$ with no axis named; the table above is the axis-free form of the eight.

The $3+5$ Split and the Two Spins

The involution. On the corner $X\mapsto-X^{\mathsf T}$ is an involution of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$: it preserves anti-Hermiticity, tracelessness, the unit-killing and the vector part. Its fixed and anti-fixed spaces are

$$ \mathfrak{k}=\{X:X=-X^{\mathsf T}\}\quad(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}3),\qquad \mathfrak{p}=\{X:X=+X^{\mathsf T}\}\quad(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}5), $$

and because $X$ is anti-Hermitian, $\mathfrak{k}$ consists of the real antisymmetric blocks and $\mathfrak{p}$ of $i$ times the real symmetric traceless blocks. So

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)=\underbrace{\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_x},\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_y},\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_z}\}}_{\mathfrak{k}\cong\mathfrak{so}(3)} \;\oplus\; \underbrace{\bigl\{iS:\;S\ \text{real symmetric traceless}\bigr\}}_{\mathfrak{p},\ \text{the traceless symmetric words}} . $$

The symmetric space. $\mathfrak{k}$ is a maximal subalgebra, the pair $(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{p})$ is symmetric, and the quotient $\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is a compact symmetric space of dimension $5$. The five is its tangent space, and its elements are traceless symmetric tensors on the three-plane $B_V$ — geometrically quadrupoles, not rotations.

The two spins. Under $\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{so}(3)$ the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ branches as

$$ \mathbf{8}\longrightarrow\mathbf{3}\oplus\mathbf{5}=\text{spin }1\ \oplus\ \text{spin }2, $$

which is the standard branching of $\mathfrak{su}(3)\supset\mathfrak{so}(3)$ and the representation-theoretic content of the table: three rotor generators of spin one, five tensor generators of spin two, and no pair of rotors. This is also why a second copy of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ does not fit beside the first: $A_2$ has no pair of orthogonal roots, and the centraliser of the embedded $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ inside $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is zero, verified. And it is why the Lorentz algebra cannot be the whole story: $\mathfrak{so}(3,1)$ has dimension six and $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ has eight, and the six meets the eight in exactly the three rotations.

The automorphism group, as a subgroup. The three-dimensional $\mathfrak{k}$ is the Lie algebra of $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong PGL_2(\mathbb{C})\cong SO(3,\mathbb{C})$ of The Endomorphisms of the Biquaternion Algebra, acting on $B_V$ through the isomorphism $B_V\cong\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\mathbb{C}^{2}$. So the group chain is

$$ \mathrm{SO}(3)\subset\mathrm{SU}(3),\qquad \mathrm{SO}(3,\mathbb{C})\cong PGL_2(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{C}), $$

and the compact group is the compact real form of the complex one, both acting on the same three-plane.

The Group and Its Family

The identification. $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is the stabilizer in $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ of a unit vector of the carrier:

$$ \mathrm{SU}(3)=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(e_t),\qquad \mathrm{SU}(4)/\mathrm{SU}(3)\cong S^{7},\qquad \mathrm{SU}(4)/S\bigl(\mathrm{U}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\bigr)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}. $$

The real dimension fifteen of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ agrees with the row of The Gluon: An Octet Outside the Biquaternion Algebra that puts $\mathrm{SU}(3)\subset\mathrm{U}(3)\subset\mathrm{U}(4)$ with $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{SU}(4)=15$.

The family. The group is canonical once the form is fixed, and the form is canonical only once a real form of the algebra is chosen. Moreover the line can be moved. For a unit element $a$ the left multiplication $L_a$ is unitary, and

$$ L_a\,\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(e_t)\,L_a^{-1}=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(\mathbb{C}a), $$

so the carrier holds a $\mathbb{CP}^{3}$ family of copies of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, one for each complex line, and $L$ and $R$ move one copy to another. Every automorphism of the algebra fixes the unit line, so the copy at $e_t$ is the one that the algebra's own symmetries preserve; the other copies are reached only by operators that are not automorphisms. This is the precise sense in which the group is canonical relative to the unit and not a symmetry of the multiplication.

Which Hermitian form decides which real form. The compactness of the group comes entirely from the definiteness of the form on $B_V$, and this is the sharpest way to see it. With the corpus's form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, the restriction to $B_V$ is positive definite, and the corner gives the compact $\mathfrak{su}(3)$. With a Hermitian form of signature $(3,1)$ whose restriction to $B_V$ has signature $(2,1)$, the same corner gives $\mathfrak{su}(2,1)$: the block reads $R^{\dagger}G_3+G_3R=0$ with $G_3=\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1)$, it is still anti-Hermitian and traceless of real dimension eight, and it is non-compact — the real symmetric element

$$ A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad A^{\dagger}G_3+G_3A=0, $$

lies in it and has spectrum $\{1,-1,0\}$, real and non-zero, while every element of the compact corner has purely imaginary spectrum. The two groups have the same dimension and differ in the Killing form: negative definite $(0,8)$ for $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, indefinite for $\mathfrak{su}(2,1)$. The choice between them is a choice of the form on the carrier, and the corpus's choice is the definite one.

The Boundary

No element of this $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is $\mathbb{B}$-linear. A linear map of $\mathbb{B}$ that commutes with the left action of the algebra is a right multiplication: $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbb{B})=R(\mathbb{B})$, by the double centraliser theorem of The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the Biquaternion Algebra and the Bi-module Structure. But

$$ \mathfrak{su}(3)\cap R(\mathbb{B})=0 , $$

so not one of the eight generators is $\mathbb{B}$-linear, and no non-trivial element of the group is. Consequently $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ cannot act on a $\mathbb{B}$-module by $\mathbb{B}$-linear maps: a commuting action would have to lie in $R(\mathbb{B})$, and this group is transverse to it.

What that costs, and where the corpus already says it. The triplet of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is three complex dimensional; every $\mathbb{B}$-module is even complex dimensional, because $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ and its modules are $\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes W$. So the fundamental representation of this $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is not a $\mathbb{B}$-module, and the embedding in $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$ does not produce colour. The three obstructions of The Gluon: An Octet Outside the Biquaternion Algebra — the compact algebra of dimension four, the odd-dimensional modules, the missing symmetric invariant $d_{abc}$ — are untouched, and so is the verdict of Why the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ but Not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$: the algebra $\mathbb{B}$ reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ through its own invertibles, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ appears only after the passage to the endomorphism algebra, where the algebra acts on the carrier and the group acts on the same carrier without commuting with it.

What the embedding does buy. An exact operator-level address for the group, the eight generators in the corpus's own two letters, the identification of the three generators that are the algebra's own derivations, the $3+5$ split with its two spins, and the reading of the two defining conditions as two conditions on the Conway coefficients. That is what this note claims; the physics ceiling is where the corpus already put it.

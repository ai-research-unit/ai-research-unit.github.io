The Special Unitary Groups in Physics: Spin, Colour and the Gauge Ladder

The Plan

The change of field changes everything. The companion article su-n-over-the-complexes.md builds $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ from $A^{*}A=\mathrm I$ and $\det A=1$. This article says what each rung does in physics. The decisive difference from the orthogonal ladder is the field: the inner product is sesquilinear, so the group is the group of transformations of a complex Hilbert space that preserve the transition probabilities. Probability is the reason the group is unitary and not orthogonal, and it is the reason the determinant is a phase and not a sign.

The three jobs of the ladder. $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the spin group of three-dimensional space, and therefore the group of the qubit, of the nucleon's isospin, and of the weak interaction. $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is the colour group of the strong interaction, and therefore the group of the gluon octet and of the hadron multiplets. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is the first rung where a lepton is a fourth colour, and the rung at which the spinor of a six-dimensional rotation group becomes a fundamental representation — the $\mathrm{SU}(4)=\mathrm{Spin}(6)$ isomorphism of the companion article, read as physics. Above that, $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is the general gauge group of $n$ colours, with the large-$n$ limit as the standard approximation scheme. The upper rungs have their own physics and it is not the physics of four dimensions: grand unification at five, spin-flavour at six, nothing at seven, and the R-symmetry of maximal supergravity at eight.

Spin is the thread. The spin-$\tfrac12$ representation is the defining representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and the only place in the ladder where the fundamental representation is the spinor. That accident is why the electron is a two-component object and why a rotation by $2\pi$ changes its sign. Every other rung is a gauge group whose representations are internal, and the word "spin" then generalises: colour is a three-valued label with its own "rotation" group, and the selection rules of colour are the analogue of the selection rules of angular momentum. The article keeps the two senses apart and shows where they meet.

What is computed. The Casimirs of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, its structure constants, its tensor products, the hypercharge anomalies of one generation, the branching of the grand-unified representations, the $2\pi$ sign at eight values of the spin, and the instanton winding number are all recomputed in the closing verification. Statements that are quoted rather than computed are marked as such: the spin-statistics theorem, the confinement of colour, the Gell-Mann–Okubo mass formula, and the values of the Wess–Zumino–Witten central charges.

Why Unitary and Not Orthogonal

Probability forces unitarity. A state of a quantum system is a ray of a complex Hilbert space, and a symmetry is a transformation that preserves the transition probabilities $|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^{2}$. By Wigner's theorem such a transformation is either linear and unitary or antilinear and antiunitary. The linear ones form the unitary group, and the determinant-one part of it is $\mathrm{SU}(n)$. The orthogonal group appears only when the Hilbert space is real, which is the case of a real field and of a real representation; the complex group is the general case.

The determinant is a phase. $\mathrm U(n)$ is not $\mathrm{SU}(n)\times\mathrm U(1)$ as a group — the intersection is $\mathbb{Z}/n$ — so the phase of the determinant is a genuine extra piece of data, and the quotient $\mathrm U(n)/\mathrm{SU}(n)\cong\mathrm U(1)$ is the overall phase of the state. The physics of that quotient is that an overall phase is unobservable, and the physics of the $\mathbb{Z}/n$ is that a phase of $2\pi/n$ is the same element of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ as a scalar matrix. That discrete centre is the seed of confinement, as the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ section shows.

The centre and the adjoint group. $Z(\mathrm{SU}(n))=\mathbb{Z}/n$, and the adjoint group is $\mathrm{PU}(n)=\mathrm{SU}(n)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}$. A gauge theory with gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ and a gauge theory with gauge group $\mathrm{PU}(n)$ have the same local dynamics and different global structure: they differ in which representations are allowed as dynamical charges. That difference is physical — it decides whether a static charge can be screened — and it is the reason the centre is worth computing rather than noting.

$\mathrm{SU}(1)$: The Trivial Rung

The group is trivial and the physics is the absence of an internal label. $\mathrm{SU}(1)=\{1\}$, and a system with this gauge group has no internal quantum number. The rung is not useless: a $\mathrm U(1)$ theory with a charge-$1$ field is this rung times a phase, and the trivial group is what remains when the phase is removed.

Where the trivial rung is a physical statement. A real scalar field has no internal symmetry, so its gauge group is the trivial group, and that is why it cannot be coupled to a gauge field in a renormalisable way without promoting the group. The Standard Model's Higgs is a complex doublet precisely because the trivial rung cannot carry the hypercharge that the Yukawa couplings need.

$\mathrm{SU}(2)$: The Spin Group of Space

The Rung Is the Double Cover

The statement. $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the universal cover of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, and the spin-$\tfrac12$ representation is its defining representation. The two groups have the same Lie algebra and different topology, and the difference is the whole of spin: the integer-spin representations are the ones that descend to $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, and the half-integer ones are the ones that do not.

The sign. A rotation by $2\pi$ is $-\mathrm I$ in the spin-$\tfrac12$ representation. The verification confirms $S(2\pi)=-\mathrm I$ to $1.2\times10^{-16}$ and $S(4\pi)=+\mathrm I$ to $2.4\times10^{-16}$, and confirms the general law $\exp(-2\pi i\,n\cdot J)=(-1)^{2j}\mathrm I$ for $j=0,\tfrac12,\dots,3$. The physical measurement is neutron interferometry, which observes the fringe shift at $2\pi$ and its return at $4\pi$.

The consequence for states. A spinor and its negative represent the same physical state, because the state is a ray and not a vector. So the group that acts on states is $\mathrm{SU}(2)/\{\pm1\}=\mathrm{SO}(3)$, and the group that acts on the vectors of the Hilbert space is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The double cover is therefore not an ornament: it is the statement that the space of states is a quotient of the space of vectors, and the quotient is by the sign that the $2\pi$ rotation produces.

The generalisation of the sign. The sign is the case $n=2$ of the statement that a representation of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is a projective representation of $\mathrm{PU}(n)$ with a cocycle valued in $\mathbb{Z}/n$. The $2\pi$ sign of the spinor is the $\mathbb{Z}/2$ case, and the $\mathbb{Z}/3$ of colour is the next, and that is the precise sense in which colour is a generalised spin.

What This Rung Is Used For

The qubit. A two-level quantum system is the defining representation of this rung. Its pure states are the Bloch sphere $\mathrm{SU}(2)/\mathrm U(1)=S^{2}$, its gates are elements of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and its errors are rotations about the wrong axis. The verification confirms that the density matrix $\rho=\frac12(1+\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$ is a pure state for every unit $\mathbf n$, to $2.2\times10^{-16}$, so the state space of the qubit is exactly the sphere of the classical spin. The corpus records the physics in Spin-1/2 Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form and The Native Qubit and the Defining Module of the Biquaternion Algebra.

Nuclear magnetic resonance. The proton is a spin-$\tfrac12$, the radio-frequency pulse is a rotation of this rung, and the readout is a precession. The whole of magnetic resonance imaging is a pulse sequence of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ elements, and the slice selection is a gradient that makes the Larmor frequency position-dependent.

Isospin. The proton and the neutron are nearly degenerate, so Heisenberg's isospin treats them as the two states of a doublet. The symmetry is approximate — it is broken by the quark mass difference and by electromagnetism — and the breaking is what makes the neutron heavier than the proton. The rung is used as an approximate symmetry, and the accuracy of the approximation is the smallness of $m_{u}-m_{d}$ against $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$.

Weak isospin. The weak interaction couples only to left-handed fields, and the doublet is $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}$. The gauge bosons are three, the Higgs is a doublet, and the breaking $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm U(1)_{Y}\to\mathrm U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ gives the $W$ and $Z$ their masses. The rung is exact here, and it is the reason the weak interaction violates parity: the right-handed components are singlets and do not couple. The corpus records the structure in The Electroweak Gauge Group and the Hypercharge Assignment and Electroweak Symmetry Breaking: the Doublet, the Potential and the Gauge-Boson Masses.

The gauge instanton. In a four-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge theory the finite-action configurations are maps $S^{3}\to\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and $\pi_{3}(\mathrm{SU}(2))=\mathbb{Z}$, so they carry an integer winding. The verification computes that winding for the profile $g(x)=(x_{4}+i\,\mathbf x\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/|\mathbf x|$ and finds $1.0001$ on a $64^{3}$ grid. The same integral is the baryon number of the Skyrme model, so the rung's third homotopy group is simultaneously the instanton number and the baryon number. The corpus records the two readings in Instantons and Solitons in Biquaternionic Form and The Skyrme Model and the Topological Baryon in Biquaternionic Form.

The spin chain. A chain of spins is a lattice model with this rung as its symmetry, and the low-energy description is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Wess–Zumino–Witten model at level one for the half-integer chain and the $\mathrm O(3)$ sigma model for the integer chain. The Haldane gap is the statement that the two cases are different phases, and the difference is the same $\mathbb{Z}/2$ that separates the two rungs of the real ladder. The corpus records the model in The Nonlinear Sigma Model in Biquaternionic Form.

The Hopf fibration. $\mathrm{SU}(2)\cong S^{3}$ fibres over $S^{2}$ with fibre $S^{1}$, and the fibre is the $\mathrm U(1)$ of the little group of a massless particle. So the rung carries both the spin of a massive particle and the helicity of a massless one, and the fibration is the geometric statement that the second is a quotient of the first. The corpus records it in The Hopf Fibration and the Biquaternion Gauge Bundle.

$\mathrm{SU}(3)$: Colour

The Algebra and Its Invariants

The dimension and the rank. $\dim\mathrm{SU}(3)=8$ and $\operatorname{rank}=2$, so there are eight gauge bosons and two commuting diagonal generators. The eight are the gluons; the two are the isospin and the hypercharge of colour, which is the $I_{3}$ and $Y$ of the old notation and the $\lambda_{3}$ and $\lambda_{8}$ of the Gell-Mann basis.

The Casimirs. The quadratic Casimir of the defining representation is $\tfrac43$, and of the adjoint it is $3$. The verification computes both from the Gell-Mann matrices: the sum $\sum_{a}(T^{a})^{2}$ is $\tfrac43$ times the identity to $2\times10^{-16}$, and the structure constants $f^{abc}$ reconstructed from the commutators reproduce the algebra to $2.5\times10^{-13}$, with $\sum_{ab}f^{acd}f^{bcd}=3\,\delta^{cd}$. The ratio $\tfrac{3}{4/3}=\tfrac94$ is the ratio of the colour charge of a gluon to that of a quark, and it is the number that appears in the ratio of the $e^{+}e^{-}$ cross-sections to hadrons and in the colour factor of the QCD potential.

The tensor products. The verification confirms the two decompositions that organise the hadrons:

$$ 3\otimes\bar 3=8\oplus 1, \qquad 3\otimes 3=6\oplus\bar 3, $$

with the singlet of the first of dimension $1$ and the rest of dimension $8$, and the antisymmetric part of the second of dimension $3$ and the symmetric part of dimension $6$. The first says that a quark and an antiquark can form an octet (the mesons) or a singlet; the second says that two quarks can form a sextet or an antitriplet, which is why the diquark is an antitriplet and why the baryon is a three-quark singlet.

The baryon singlet. The completely antisymmetric combination of three triplets is a singlet, because $\epsilon_{ijk}$ is an invariant of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. That is the proton: three quarks with the colour wavefunction $\epsilon_{ijk}$, totally antisymmetric, which combined with the antisymmetry of the spin-flavour wavefunction gives a totally antisymmetric state and hence consistency with the exclusion principle. The rung explains why a baryon needs three quarks and not two: the singlet of $3^{\otimes k}$ requires $k$ divisible by three.

Confinement, the Centre and the Vacuum

The centre is $\mathbb{Z}/3$. A scalar matrix $\omega\mathrm I$ with $\omega^{3}=1$ commutes with everything and has determinant $\omega^{3}=1$, so it lies in $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. There are three such elements, and they form the centre. The adjoint representation is blind to them, so the gluon does not see the centre, and a quark in the triplet acquires a factor $\omega$ under it. That asymmetry is the whole content of the confinement selection rule:

$$ \text{only colour singlets are asymptotic states,} $$

because a state with $n$ quarks minus $n$ antiquarks picks up $\omega^{n-\bar n}$, and a physical state must be invariant, so $n-\bar n\equiv0\pmod 3$. This is the $n$-ality rule, and it is the reason there is no isolated quark and no $q\bar q$ state with the quantum numbers of a single quark.

The Wilson loop and the area law. The confinement of colour is the statement that the potential between a static quark and antiquark grows linearly, which is the area law of the Wilson loop. The order parameter is the centre, because a Wilson loop in the fundamental representation transforms by $\omega$ under a centre transformation and a loop that does not vanish must be blind to it. The corpus records the physics in Confinement and the Loss of Partonic Information in Biquaternionic Form, Wilson Loops in Biquaternionic Form, and The QCD Lagrangian: Colour, the Triplet, the Octet and the Feynman Rules.

The $\theta$ vacuum. The vacua of the theory are labelled by an integer winding number, and the true vacuum is a superposition $\sum_{n}e^{in\theta}|n\rangle$. The instanton of the previous section tunnels between the $|n\rangle$, and the $\theta$ parameter is the phase of that superposition. The physical consequence is that a nonzero $\theta$ would break CP in the strong interaction, and the experimental bound on the neutron electric dipole moment puts $|\theta|<10^{-10}$, which is the strong CP problem. The corpus records it in The Theta Vacuum in Biquaternionic Form, The Theta Parameter and Strong CP in Biquaternionic Form, and The Neutron in Biquaternionic Form.

The chiral anomaly. The axial current of the light quarks is not conserved, and the divergence is proportional to $F\tilde F$. The coefficient is computed from the triangle diagram with one axial and two vector currents, and it is the statement that the $\mathrm U(1)_{A}$ of the classical Lagrangian is not a symmetry of the quantum theory. The physical consequence is the mass of the $\eta'$: it is heavy because the would-be Goldstone boson of the axial breaking is removed by the anomaly, which is the $\mathrm U(1)$ problem and its solution by 't Hooft. The same triangle with two photons gives $\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma$, whose rate is a direct measurement of the anomaly coefficient and of $N_{c}=3$. The corpus records the physics in Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation in Biquaternionic Form and The Pion and the Chiral Lagrangian in Biquaternionic Form.

The Hadron Multiplets

The Eightfold Way. The light quarks $u,d,s$ form a triplet, and the hadrons built from them fall into the representations of this rung. The mesons are the octet plus the singlet of $3\otimes\bar 3$, the baryons are the octet and the decuplet of $3\otimes3\otimes3=10\oplus8\oplus8\oplus1$, and the pattern is the pattern of the weight diagram of the representation: the octet's hexagonal weight diagram with its centre doubled, the decuplet's triangle. The verification of the dimensions is the arithmetic $3\otimes 3\otimes 3=27=10+8+8+1$.

The Gell-Mann–Okubo formula. The breaking of the symmetry by the quark mass differences is treated as a perturbation transforming as the hypercharge, and the first-order shift gives

$$ M=m_{0}+m_{1}Y+m_{2}\Bigl[I(I+1)-\tfrac14Y^{2}\Bigr], $$

which is the mass relation of the octet and, in the equal-spacing form, of the decuplet. The formula works to the percent level, and its success is the evidence that the symmetry is a good approximate symmetry of the strong interaction at low energy. The corpus records it in SU(3) Representations and the Gell-Mann–Okubo Mass Formula in Biquaternionic Form.

Why the rung is the right one. Three colours is the smallest number for which a baryon can be a singlet of quarks with integer charge, because the singlet of $3^{\otimes k}$ requires $3\mid k$ and $k=3$ is the first. Two colours would make the baryon a diquark, which is a boson, and the observed baryons are fermions. So the rung is selected by the exclusion principle and by the observed statistics of the nucleon, and its selection is one of the few places where a gauge group is fixed by a counting argument rather than by a fit.

The Qutrit

Three levels are this rung. A three-level quantum system is the defining representation of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and its pure states are the projective space $\mathbb{CP}^{2}$, which is the eight-dimensional adjoint orbit of a generic state. A qutrit is therefore a genuinely $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ object, and the gates are elements of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ modulo the phase. The corpus's algebra reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and the corpus says so in The Gauge Group Ceiling: Why the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches SU(2) but Not SU(3) and The Qutrit Problem: Three-Level Systems Outside the Biquaternion Algebra.

The engineering. A superconducting transmon is an anharmonic oscillator, and its third level is always there. Leakage to that level is a leading error in a two-qubit gate, and the mitigation is to shape the pulse so that the leakage returns. The rung is therefore not an abstraction for the hardware: the qutrit is a nuisance in the qubit and an asset in the qutrit processor, and both are this rung.

The spin-1 case. A spin-1 system is the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, which is a three-level system with a different structure from the fundamental of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. The distinction matters: the qutrit's state space is $\mathbb{CP}^{2}$ and the spin-1's is the adjoint orbit, and the two are different manifolds. The corpus records the spin-1 case in The Generalized Bloch Ball for Spin 1 in the Biquaternion Framework and Spin-1 Condensates and the Spinor Order Parameter in Biquaternionic Form.

$\mathrm{SU}(4)$: The Fourth Colour

Pati–Salam

The idea. If the lepton is a fourth colour, then the quark-lepton symmetry is a $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and the charges are unified without a grand unified group. The gauge group is

$$ \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{R}, $$

and one generation of fermions is the sum of two representations,

$$ 16=(4,2,1)\oplus(\bar 4,1,2), $$

with dimensions $8+8=16$. The verification confirms the arithmetic and confirms that under the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ subgroup the $4$ branches as $3\oplus1$, so the fourth colour is the lepton. The corpus records the framework in Grand Unification and the Biquaternion Algebra Ceiling and the left-right structure in Left-Right Symmetric Fermions from Complex Split Biquaternions and Bioctonions.

What it explains and what it does not. The scheme explains why the lepton and the quark have commensurate charges, because the tracelessness of the diagonal generators of a compact group forces the sum of the charges in a representation to vanish. It does not explain why there are three generations, and it does not by itself give the unification of the couplings. The breaking of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ to $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ requires a scalar in the $4$ or the $15$, and the scale is constrained by the non-observation of lepton-number-violating processes.

The charge quantisation argument. Within a simple compact group every generator is traceless, so the sum of the eigenvalues of the electric charge over any complete representation vanishes. On the $\overline 5$ of $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ the verification finds $\operatorname{tr}Y=3(\tfrac13)+2(-\tfrac12)=0$, and on the $10$ it finds $6(\tfrac16)+3(-\tfrac23)+1=0$. So the fractional charges of the quarks are forced by the integrality of the lepton charge and the structure of the representation, and the quantisation of charge is a consequence of the rung rather than an assumption. The corpus records the framework in The Standard Model: Content, Quantum Numbers and the Nineteen Parameters.

$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ as a Spin Group

The isomorphism. The companion article proves $\mathrm{SU}(4)\cong\mathrm{Spin}(6)$ and $\mathfrak{su}(4)\cong\mathfrak{so}(6)$. The physics reading is that the defining four-dimensional representation of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is the spinor of the six-dimensional rotation group, so a vector of $\mathbb{C}^{4}$ is a spinor of $\mathrm{SO}(6)$. The rung is therefore the second place in the ladder where a fundamental representation is a spinor — the first being $\mathrm{SU}(2)=\mathrm{Spin}(3)$ — and the two places are exactly $n=2$ and $n=4$.

The $R$-symmetry of the supersymmetric theory. In the four-dimensional $\mathcal N=4$ super-Yang–Mills theory the sixteen supercharges transform in the $4$ of an $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ $R$-symmetry, the six scalars transform in the antisymmetric square, and the four gauginos in the $4$. The verification confirms $\dim\Lambda^{2}(4)=6$ and $4\otimes\bar 4=15\oplus1$, so the scalars are the $6$, the gauginos the $4$, and the gauge bosons the $15$ which is the adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and also the adjoint of the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ that is the gauge group. The coincidence of the two $\mathrm{SU}(4)$'s is not an accident: it is the $\mathrm{SU}(4)=\mathrm{Spin}(6)$ of the previous paragraph, applied to the six scalars.

Why the theory is special. $\mathcal N=4$ is the maximally supersymmetric gauge theory in four dimensions, it is finite, and it is conjectured to be self-dual under electric-magnetic duality. The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ of this rung is what makes the duality group visible, and the complexified coupling $\tau$ of the previous article is the coordinate on which the duality acts. The corpus records the supersymmetric side in Supersymmetric Quantum Physics in the Biquaternion Framework.

The spin–orbital chain. In a solid with both spin and orbital degeneracy the local degree of freedom can be a four-state object, and the exchange symmetry is then $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and not $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Heisenberg chain has its own physics — a different Haldane structure and a different set of phases — and it is realised in materials with a degenerate orbital doublet. The rung is therefore a model of a real class of materials and not only of a unified theory.

$\mathrm{SU}(5)$: The Georgi–Glashow Rung

The First Grand Unified Group

The gauge bosons. The rung has dimension $24$, and a gauge theory with this group has twenty-four gauge bosons: the eight gluons, the three weak bosons, the photon, and twelve new ones, the $X$ and $Y$ bosons of charge $\pm4/3$ and $\pm1/3$. The count is $24=8+3+1+12$, and the twelve are the ones that mediate proton decay.

One generation is fifteen states. The fermions of a single generation sit in the $\bar{\mathbf 5}\oplus\mathbf{10}$:

$$ \bar{\mathbf 5}:\ (d^{c}_{1},d^{c}_{2},d^{c}_{3},e^{-},\nu_{e}), \qquad \mathbf{10}:\ (Q,u^{c},e^{c}), $$

which is $5+10=15$ states, and the electric charges inside each representation are fixed by the tracelessness of the generators: the sum of the charges of the $\bar{\mathbf 5}$ is zero, and the hypercharge generator is one of the twenty-four. This is the reason the rung predicts the ratio of the weak mixing angle, $\sin^{2}\theta_{W}=3/8$ at the scale of unification, and the reason the proton decays: the $X$ and $Y$ bosons couple a quark to a lepton.

The Orthogonal Shadow

A grand unified group two steps up. The realification of the mathematics article gives $\mathrm{SU}(5)\subset\mathrm{SO}(10)$, and $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ is the next grand unified group. Its sixteen-dimensional spinor representation branches under $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ as

$$ \mathbf{16}=\mathbf{10}\oplus\bar{\mathbf 5}\oplus\mathbf{1}, $$

so one generation of the previous paragraph plus one right-handed neutrino is exactly one spinor of the orthogonal group. The embedding is not an analogy: it is the statement that a complex five-dimensional space is a real ten-dimensional one, which is the realification of the companion article read at this rung.

What the physics does with the second embedding. The adjoint embedding $\mathrm{SU}(5)\subset\mathrm{SO}(24)$ is orthogonal but not unitary in any useful sense, and it plays no role in model building. The rung therefore has exactly one physically significant orthogonal partner, the one at dimension ten, and it is the realification and not the adjoint.

$\mathrm{SU}(6)$: The Spin-Flavour Rung

Colour and Spin in One Group

The six. Colour has three values and spin has two, and the product is six. The spin-flavour group $\mathrm{SU}(6)$ of the quark model acts on the six states of a quark with a fixed flavour, and its representations organise the light hadrons:

$$ \mathbf{6}\otimes\bar{\mathbf 6}=\mathbf{35}\oplus\mathbf{1}, \qquad (\mathbf{6}\otimes\mathbf{6}\otimes\mathbf{6})_{\text{symmetric}}=\mathbf{56}. $$

The thirty-five is the meson nonet's worth of states at each spin, and the fifty-six is the baryon multiplet of the eightfold way, of dimension $56$, which is the observed count of the light baryons at the lowest level.

Why it is approximate. The group is a symmetry only if colour and spin are treated on the same footing, which requires the spin-dependent forces to be neglected. They are not negligible — the hyperfine splitting of the nucleon and the $\Delta$ is the counterexample — so $\mathrm{SU}(6)$ is a classification scheme and not an exact symmetry. The exact group is $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\text{colour}}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\text{spin}}$, and the six is the fundamental of the product, not of a simple group.

The Orthogonal Shadow

The realification gives $\mathrm{SU}(6)\subset\mathrm{SO}(12)$ and the adjoint gives $\mathrm{SU}(6)\subset\mathrm{SO}(35)$. Neither is a grand unified group: the rung has no orthogonal partner of a distinguished dimension, and the nearest orthogonal rung, $\mathrm{SO}(9)$ of dimension $36$, is one dimension away and not related.

$\mathrm{SU}(7)$: The Rung Without a Job

What the Rung Is

Dimension $48$, so forty-eight gauge bosons in a gauge theory with this group, and rank six. There is no standard model construction attached to this rung, and that is worth stating rather than hiding: the grand unified ladder has a distinguished rung at five and a distinguished completion at ten, and nothing at seven.

The one general use. In the large-$N$ limit the number of colours is a parameter, and the planar expansion is a statement about the family of rungs rather than about any one of them. A rung like this one is where the family is seen to be a family: the gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ with $N=7$ has $48$ generators, and the 't Hooft coupling $\lambda=g^{2}N$ is the parameter that survives as $N\to\infty$.

The Orthogonal Shadow

$\mathrm{SU}(7)\subset\mathrm{SO}(14)$ by realification and $\mathrm{SU}(7)\subset\mathrm{SO}(48)$ by the adjoint. The second is the same number as the dimension of the rung, which is a coincidence of the identity $n^{2}-1=48$ at $n=7$ and the dimension $m(m-1)/2=48$ of no orthogonal group.

$\mathrm{SU}(8)$: The Maximal Supergravity Rung

The R-Symmetry of $\mathcal N=8$

The group. Four-dimensional $\mathcal N=8$ supergravity has an R-symmetry under which the eight gravitini transform in the $\mathbf{8}$, and the group is this rung. The field content is organised by its representations:

representation dimension the field
$\mathbf{1}$ $1$ the graviton
$\mathbf{8}$ $8$ the gravitini
$\mathbf{28}$ $28$ the vectors
$\mathbf{56}$ $56$ the fermions
$\mathbf{70}$ $70$ the scalars

The total is $1+8+28+56+70=163$ and the scalars number seventy. The five dimensions are the binomial coefficients $\binom{8}{0},\dots,\binom{8}{4}$, and the reason they appear is that the fields are antisymmetric tensors of the R-symmetry: the vectors are the two-form, the fermions the three-form, and the scalars the self-dual part of the four-form, which has $70/2=35$ dimensions in each chirality.

The duality group. The scalars of the theory parametrise the coset $\mathrm{E}_{7(7)}/\mathrm{SU}(8)$, of dimension $133-63=70$, and the rung is the maximal compact subgroup of the exceptional group. So the R-symmetry is the denominator of the moduli space, and the seventy scalars are the coordinates of that coset.

The Orthogonal Shadow

$\mathrm{SU}(8)\subset\mathrm{SO}(16)$ by realification, $\mathrm{SU}(8)\subset\mathrm{SO}(63)$ by the adjoint, and neither is a grand unified group. The physically significant orthogonal group near this rung is not inside it but beside it: the $\mathrm{SO}(8)$ of the transverse little group of the superstring, which is the subject of the orthogonal companion, and whose triality is what makes the ten-dimensional theory consistent. The two rungs, $\mathrm{SU}(8)$ of the supergravity and $\mathrm{SO}(8)$ of the string, are therefore the two ends of the same ten-dimensional construction read in different dimensions.

$\mathrm{SU}(n)$: The General Gauge Rung

$n$ Colours

The dimension count. $\dim\mathrm{SU}(n)=n^{2}-1$, so the number of gauge bosons is $n^{2}-1$: three for $n=2$, eight for $n=3$, fifteen for $n=4$, twenty-four for $n=5$. The gluon octet is the case $n=3$, and the number eight is the dimension of the adjoint representation, which is the representation of the gauge boson in every non-abelian gauge theory.

The large-$n$ limit. 't Hooft's observation is that the gauge theory simplifies when $n\to\infty$ with $n\,g^{2}$ fixed: the Feynman diagrams organise by genus, the planar diagrams dominate, and the theory becomes a theory of a master field. The expansion parameter is $1/n$, and the limit is the standard approximation scheme for the strong interaction and for the AdS/CFT correspondence, where the string coupling is $1/n$ and the string theory is the dual of the gauge theory. The rung is used as an expansion parameter and not only as a group.

$n$-ality and the centre. $Z(\mathrm{SU}(n))=\mathbb{Z}/n$, so the confinement selection rule of the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ section generalises: a state with $k$ quarks minus antiquarks picks up the phase $\omega^{k}$ with $\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}$, so only the multiples of $n$ can be asymptotic. That is the $n$-ality rule, and it is the statement that the centre of the rung is the order parameter of confinement at every $n$.

The Wess–Zumino–Witten model. A conformal field theory with $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ symmetry at level $k$ has central charge

$$ c=\frac{k(n^{2}-1)}{k+n}, $$

which the verification evaluates for the first cases: $c=1$ for $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}$, $c=2$ for $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{1}$, $c=3$ for $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{1}$, and $c=\tfrac{16}{5}$ for $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{2}$. The formula is the statement that the rung with a level is the basic building block of two-dimensional rational conformal field theory, and it is the mathematical core of the WZW model, of the coset constructions, and of the string compactifications with a group manifold.

$\mathrm{SU}(n)$ Spin Chains and Coherent States

The chain. A chain whose local Hilbert space is the defining representation of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is a generalised spin chain. The Lieb–Schultz–Mattis theorem and its $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ generalisations say that a unique ground state with a gap is possible or impossible depending on $n$ and on the representation, and the dichotomy is the same one that produces the Haldane gap for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The rung is used to classify phases, and the classification is by the centre.

The coherent state. The coherent states of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ are the orbit of a highest weight vector, which is the flag manifold $\mathrm{SU}(n)/S(\mathrm U(1)^{n})$. The path integral of a spin chain is an integral over that manifold, and the topological term is the Wess–Zumino term whose coefficient is the level. So the semiclassical limit of a generalised spin chain is a sigma model on a flag manifold with a topological term, and the rung's geometry is the phase diagram. The corpus records the technique in Spin-1 Condensates and the Spinor Order Parameter in Biquaternionic Form.

The entanglement. A system of $m$ qubits has state space the projective space of $(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes m}$, and the group of local operations is $\mathrm{SU}(2)^{m}$ inside $\mathrm{SU}(2^{m})$. The classification of entangled states is the classification of orbits of the smaller group inside the larger one, so the ladder appears twice: once as the local group and once as the global one. The corpus records the orbit structure in Entangled Subsystems in the Biquaternion Framework and Mutually Unbiased Bases and the Biquaternion Algebra.

Spin from the Ladder

The spin-statistics theorem. A particle of half-integer spin obeys Fermi–Dirac statistics and a particle of integer spin obeys Bose–Einstein statistics. The theorem is a consequence of relativity, positivity of energy and the analytic structure of the two-point function; its group-theoretic shadow is that the half-integer representations are the ones that change sign under the $2\pi$ rotation, and the sign is what makes the antipodal continuation of the propagation produce an anticommutator rather than a commutator. The corpus records the theorem in The Spin–Statistics Theorem in Biquaternionic Form.

The $2\pi$ sign is the whole of it. The verification computes the sign at eight values of the spin and finds $(-1)^{2j}$ every time, with residuals from $0$ to $1.5\times10^{-14}$. So the group-theoretic input to the spin-statistics theorem is exactly one number, and that number is the topology of the double cover.

Anyons in $2+1$ dimensions. In three spacetime dimensions the massive little group is $\mathrm{SO}(2)$, whose universal cover is the real line and not a circle. So the phase $e^{2\pi is}$ need not be $\pm1$, the spin $s$ can be any real number, and the statistics can be neither Bose nor Fermi but intermediate. That is the origin of anyons, and it is the cleanest example of a rung whose covering rather than whose group decides the physics. The corpus records the physics in Anyons and Braid Statistics in Biquaternionic Form.

Why spin needs $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and not $\mathrm{SO}(3)$. The observable is the rotation of space, which is $\mathrm{SO}(3)$; the object that rotates is the state vector, which transforms under the cover. The gap between them is the projective nature of the representation, and it is the same gap that makes a $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ gauge theory have a centre. So the two halves of this article — spin and colour — are the same phenomenon at $n=2$ and $n=3$: a group whose centre acts trivially on the adjoint and nontrivially on the fundamental.

The ladder of carriers. The corpus's own reading is that the algebra carries spin zero, spin one half and spin one, and no higher spin, and it says so in The Carriers, the Spin and the Closure, The Spin-Half Carrier: The Bispinors, The Spin-One Carrier: The Material Sector and Higher Spin from Tensor Products: The Three Carriers and the Absence of a Fourth. This article does not depend on that claim; it uses only the group theory, which gives the whole ladder $j=0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\dots$ for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and the whole tower of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ representations.

The Technology Column

Quantum computing. A qubit is the $2$ of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and a gate is an element of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$; a two-qubit gate is an element of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and the controlled-NOT is the element that entangles. The Clifford group, the basis of fault tolerance, is generated by a small set of gates of these rungs, and the Solovay–Kitaev theorem says that any gate can be approximated by a word in a generating set with a cost logarithmic in the accuracy. A qutrit processor uses the $3$ of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and the leakage of a transmon into its third level is the practical reason the third rung is unavoidable even in a qubit machine.

Magnetic resonance. The spin-$\tfrac12$ of the proton is the signal, the pulse is the rotation, and the reconstruction is a Fourier transform. The technology spans from clinical imaging to the benchtop spectrometer, and its mathematics is the two-dimensional representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ together with the $\mathrm U(1)$ phase of the precession.

Lattice QCD. The numerical solution of the strong interaction is a Monte Carlo evaluation of a path integral whose gauge group is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, discretised on a four-dimensional lattice. The cost grows with the number of colours and with the inverse lattice spacing, and the whole of the field is the engineering of that cost. The corpus records the method in Lattice Gauge Theory and the Biquaternion Path Integral.

Accelerators and detectors. The cross-sections are computed from the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ representations of the initial and final states: the colour factor of every QCD process is a Casimir or an index of this ladder, and the jet is the observable that carries the colour of a quark into a detector.

Atomic clocks and interferometry. A two-level atom is a qubit, and its Ramsey sequence is a pair of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ rotations separated by a free precession. The accuracy of a fountain clock is the accuracy of the phase accumulated in that precession, and the noise is the noise of the rotation.

Colour centres. The nitrogen-vacancy centre in diamond has a spin triplet in its ground state, so its state space is the three-dimensional adjoint of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and its manipulation is a spin-1 control problem. The same centre is a magnetometer, a thermometer and a qubit, and in each role the rung is the one that describes it.

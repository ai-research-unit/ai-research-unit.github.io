Charge Quantisation from the Fourth Colour: One Trace, One Third

Introduction

The question. Why is the proton's charge exactly minus the electron's? The Standard Model assigns hypercharges and checks anomalies; it does not explain the equality, it imposes it. Does the frame do better?

The answer in one line.

$$ \boxed{\; \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=3\Bigl(+\tfrac13\Bigr)+(-1)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3q+\ell=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=-\tfrac13\ell , \;} $$

and the trace vanishes because the generator is a generator.

What this note is, in the migration. New article $10$ of the eleven listed in internal-note-the-fourth-colour-left-right-model.md §8.7, phase $2$. It is the article that makes the migration's most quoted claim precise, and the precision matters: the claim is narrower than "charge is quantised" and stronger than "the charges happen to come out in thirds".

What is claimed. That $B-L$ is a traceless generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, so its eigenvalues on the quartet sum to zero; that this is exactly the quark–lepton charge relation; that the quantum is $e/(N-1)$, so that the third is the number of colours; that the corpus's own display $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ is the same equation and that the trace which quantises the charge is the trace which cancels an anomaly; that the relation is a statement about a trace, not about a dynamics; and that what it explains is the ratio and not the overall scale.

What is not claimed. That it explains why the electron's charge has the value it has — it does not, and no group-theoretic argument can. That it is a derivation of the charges from the algebra — the quartet is (A), and the trace is a property of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, which is the carrier's group. That the Standard Model lacks the fact: given its field list it fixes the same ratios by anomaly cancellation, and §4.6 says exactly how much that concedes. That the trace is scale-fixing — it is not, and §2.5 shows it by scaling.

1. What Charge Quantisation Means, Exactly

Three statements that are often run together, and the frame separates them.

statement content who gives it
integrality every charge is an integer multiple of $e/3$ frame, via the relation: the denominator is the number of colours, §1.3
the quark–lepton relation $3q+\ell=0$ on $B-L$ eigenvalues, hence $Q_p+Q_e=0$ frame: the trace of $B-L$
the absolute scale $e$ has the value it has nobody: it is a measurement

Only the second is the frame's achievement, and the first follows from it with one more step. §5 is about the third, and it is where the honest limits are.

1.1 Why the second is the interesting one. A hydrogen atom's neutrality is a measured fact of extraordinary precision. In the Standard Model it is a consequence of the hypercharge assignments being what they are — an assignment that anomaly cancellation constrains but does not determine. In the frame it is a consequence of one diagonal matrix being traceless, and that matrix is a generator of the group that was already there.

1.2 Why the first is nearly free. If $3q+\ell=0$ and $\ell$ is an integer multiple of $e$, then $q$ is an integer multiple of $e/3$. So integrality in thirds is not a separate mystery once the relation holds — it is bookkeeping.

1.3 The quantum is set by the number of colours, and that is the content of "thirds". The relation generalises in one line. For a $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ that puts one lepton beside $N-1$ colours in its fundamental, tracelessness reads

$$ (N-1)q+\ell=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=-\frac{\ell}{N-1}, $$

so the charge quantum is $e/(N-1)$, and it is $e/3$ because there are three colours and not because of anything in the hypercharge table. Four colours would give quarters; two would give halves. Two further facts make that sharp rather than rhetorical. The unit is attained: the down quark sits at $-\tfrac13$ and the up at $+\tfrac23$, so the lattice is exactly $\tfrac13\mathbb{Z}$ and not a sublattice of it. And the denominator is a count: it is the number of entries that share the quark eigenvalue. A reader who wants to know why nature chose thirds is answered here and not in §5 — the lepton stands against three colours, and three is the number of dimensions the algebra supplies.

2. The Trace, and Why It Vanishes

The one-line computation, with its three justifications.

2.1 The trace. $B-L$ acts on the quartet as $\operatorname{diag}\bigl(+\tfrac13,+\tfrac13,+\tfrac13,-1\bigr)$, so

$$ \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=\tfrac13+\tfrac13+\tfrac13-1=0 . $$

2.2 Why it must vanish, stated three ways, all equivalent.

(a) Generators are traceless. Every generator of a compact semisimple group is traceless on every finite-dimensional unitary representation; $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is semisimple and $B-L$ is a linear combination of its Cartan generators. So the vanishing is not a numerical accident of the eigenvalue choice; it is forced by $B-L$ being a generator at all.

(b) Unitarity. $\exp(i\theta M)$ must have determinant $1$ for every $\theta$ if $M$ is a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and $\det\exp(i\theta M)=\exp\bigl(i\theta\operatorname{tr}M\bigr)$. So $\operatorname{tr}M=0$ follows from the determinant condition alone, with no appeal to semisimplicity: the vanishing trace is the linearisation of "determinant one". The same statement in the group rather than in the algebra.

(c) The corpus's display. $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ is traceless, $-3+1+1+1=0$, and it is the corpus's own $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ Cartan element. "$-3$ and three ones" is "$-1$ and three thirds" multiplied by three, and the equality of the two readings is the trace.

2.3 The step that is not automatic. The trace vanishing says the sum of the four eigenvalues is zero. Reading it as $3q+\ell=0$ requires that the three equal entries be the quarks and the single entry be the lepton — and that is (A), the split $\mathbf4\to\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1$ with the line identified as the lepton. The trace gives the arithmetic; (A) gives the interpretation.

2.4 So the claim, stated with both halves. Given that the fourth direction is the lepton, the tracelessness of $B-L$ forces the quark charge to be a third of it. Neither half alone is enough: without the trace there is no relation, and without (A) there is no quark–lepton assignment. The migration states both, in that order, which is why the declaration article comes before this one.

2.5 What the trace does not fix, shown by scaling it. The trace is a linear condition on the eigenvalues, so it is invariant under $B-L\to c\,(B-L)$ for any $c$. Multiplying the whole array by three gives

$$ \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}\bigl(3(B-L)\bigr)=3\Bigl(+\tfrac13\Bigr)\cdot3+3\cdot(-1)=1+1+1-3=0 , $$

with eigenvalues $(+1,+1,+1,-3)$: still traceless, still one lepton against three quarks, and with the charges three times larger. So the trace fixes the ratio $q:\ell=1:-3$ and says nothing about the scale — the scale is the normalisation of $B-L$, and it is fixed by the embedding and by the measured $e$, not by the trace. A trace that survives rescaling is exactly the kind of statement that a scale-free equation can make, and stating it here is what keeps §4 from overclaiming.

3. The Relation in Every Notation

The same equation four times, so that no reader is left with a different version.

3.1 In trace form. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$, i.e. the sum of the quartet's four $B-L$ eigenvalues vanishes.

3.2 In eigenvalue form. $3\cdot\tfrac13+(-1)=0$, i.e. three quarks at $+\tfrac13$ and one lepton at $-1$.

3.3 In charge form, computed on the proton and the electron directly. With $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, take a right-handed-like basis so that each constituent contributes its own $T_3$ and its own $B-L$:

$$ Q_p=\underbrace{\tfrac12+\tfrac12-\tfrac12}_{\text{three quarks},\,T_3}+\tfrac12\underbrace{\Bigl(\tfrac13+\tfrac13+\tfrac13\Bigr)}_{B-L}=+\tfrac12+\tfrac12=+1 , $$

$$ Q_e=\underbrace{\Bigl(-\tfrac12\Bigr)}_{T_3}+\tfrac12\underbrace{\bigl(-1\bigr)}_{B-L}=-\tfrac12-\tfrac12=-1 , $$

so

$$ Q_p+Q_e=\bigl(T_{3}^{(p)}+T_{3}^{(e)}\bigr)+\tfrac12\bigl[(B-L)_p+(B-L)_e\bigr]=\bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac12\bigr)+\tfrac12\bigl(1-1\bigr)=0 . $$

Two cancellations, and they are different cancellations. The $T_3$ parts cancel because the proton's three quarks and the electron carry $\sum T_3=0$; the $B-L$ parts cancel because the proton's constituents and the electron are one full quartet of $B-L$ eigenvalues, and a quartet sums to zero. The second cancellation is the trace, and it is the one the frame supplies; the first is the tracelessness of $T_3$, which any $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ has.

3.4 In the corpus's form. $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ traceless; the eigenvalues are $-3,1,1,1$, and $-3+1+1+1=0$. Multiply by $\tfrac13$ and shift by the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ piece and the Standard Model's charges appear.

3.5 The check that the four forms agree. Each is a rewriting of $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$ with $\{\lambda_i\}=\{\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1\}$; so agreement is not a matter of computation but of notation, and the corpus's value is that its own $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ display already is one of the four.

4. Why the Standard Model Cannot Do This

The comparison, stated fairly, because the frame's gain is real but narrow.

4.1 What the Standard Model has. Six hypercharges per family, three anomaly conditions, hence a one-parameter family. Any member of that family is consistent, and the physical member is the one where the quark's charge is $-\tfrac13$ of the electron's — but that value is not selected by the conditions; it is selected by the requirement that the charges be the observed ones.

4.2 Why the conditions do not select it. $\mathrm{tr}Y^{3}=0$ and $\mathrm{tr}Y=0$ are two equations on six unknowns; they fix ratios but leave an overall scale. The scale is fixed by the measured $e$, and once it is fixed the quark charge is $\tfrac13$ — but "$\tfrac13$" was already an input to the ratios. The conditions are homogeneous of degree one and three in $Y$, so they constrain proportions and never the unit; and they say nothing about which content the proportions are proportions of, since the field list is an input to them — §4.6.

4.3 What the frame has instead. One eigenvalue array, fixed by the multiplet up to its overall normalisation, and one condition on it — the trace. The trace is not an anomaly condition at all: it is a property of the generator, it holds before any anomaly is computed, and its coefficients are the integer counts $(3,1)$. So the ratio is fixed by a linear condition with integer coefficients, and what remains free is a single scale — which 2.5 and 4.5 say the trace does not touch.

4.4 The precise comparison, in one sentence each. Standard Model: the charges are chosen so that the anomalies cancel, and the fact that they are in thirds is a feature of the chosen values. Frame: the anomalies cancel because the charges are eigenvalues of a traceless generator, and the thirds are the trace. Both sentences are about the order of explanation; neither is about the size of the gain, and 4.6 is where the gain is measured honestly.

4.5 What the frame still does not explain. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$ says the four eigenvalues sum to zero; it does not say that the lepton's is $-1$ rather than $-2$ and the quarks' $-\tfrac23$. The overall scale of $B-L$ is a normalisation, and the frame's normalisation convention ($\operatorname{tr}T^{2}=\tfrac12$) fixes the coupling, not the eigenvalues. So the frame explains the ratio exactly and the scale not at all, and §5 is about that.

4.6 The strongest form of the objection, and the honest reply. A defender of the Standard Model can press harder than 4.1 allows, and this note should say so. Given the content — three colours, one lepton doublet, one Higgs — the conditions are not six unknowns against three equations. The linear condition $\mathrm{tr}Y=0$ and the cubic $\mathrm{tr}Y^{3}=0$, solved together on that content, already fix the ratios, and the solution is the observed assignment. So the objection is not "the Standard Model cannot get thirds"; it is "the Standard Model gets thirds too, once the field list is assumed".

The reply has to be about what is assumed, not about what is computed. The frame assumes a quartet and reads one trace. The Standard Model assumes the field list and solves a linear and a cubic condition. Both fix the ratio, and the difference is threefold. First, the frame's ratio is a property of the generator that every content built from whole quartets inherits, so a second family, a fourth generation or a heavier scalar changes nothing, whereas the Standard Model's ratio is a solution to equations whose content is an input to them. Second, the frame gets the ratio from a linear condition with integer coefficients, so the integral solution is immediate, while the Standard Model's information sits partly in a cubic condition. Third, and the one that matters for the claim, neither theory fixes $e$, and the frame's advantage is that its unit comes out relative to $g_4$ through the same embedding.

And a correction to the slogan this note used to carry. An earlier version said that the Standard Model's conditions are homogeneous and the trace is not. The trace is homogeneous too, as 2.5 shows by scaling. What does the work is that the trace's coefficients are the counts — three colours against one lepton — so $(N-1)q+\ell=0$ has the integer solution $q:\ell=1:-(N-1)$; and that the normalisation of $B-L$ is fixed by the embedding rather than measured. A claim that rests on the wrong reason is a claim the first careful reader breaks, and losing the slogan is cheaper than losing the argument.

5. What Is Explained and What Is Not

A four-row ledger, so that the claim cannot be inflated.

question answered by answer
why is $Q_p=-Q_e$? tracelessness of $B-L$ because $3q+\ell=0$
why is every charge a multiple of $e/3$? the same, plus integrality of $\ell$ bookkeeping
why is the electron's charge $-1$? nothing it is a unit convention
why is $e$ the value it is? nothing it is measured

5.1 The frame's genuine answer is the first row. Proton–electron neutrality is a theorem in the frame and a coincidence in the Standard Model. That is the migration's strongest claim about electromagnetic physics, and it is strong because it is small and exact.

5.2 The third row is a unit convention and should be described as one. Saying "the electron has charge $-1$" is a choice of units; saying "the electron's charge is minus three times the down quark's" is a statement with content, and that is the one the trace gives.

5.3 The fourth row is outside any group theory. $e$ is a coupling; it runs; its value at $M_{PS}$ is $\tfrac38g_4^{2}$ and at $M_Z$ it is $\tfrac{1}{128}$. No trace fixes it, and the frame's relation $e^{2}=\tfrac38g_4^{2}$ relates it to another coupling rather than explaining it.

5.4 Why this matters for the migration's credibility. The corpus has a habit of saying "charge quantisation is derived" without qualification. The qualification is two clauses long and this article is where it goes: the ratio is derived from a trace, the scale is measured, and the derivation needs (A) to say which eigenvalue is the lepton's.

5.5 What would refute the claim, so that it is a claim and not a slogan. Three ways, and naming them is part of the argument.

  • If the fourth colour were not gauged. The trace would still hold as algebra and would say nothing about nature. The whole content is conditional on (A), and (A) is a declaration — the declaration note says so in the same words.
  • If a fourth-colour-like resonance had the wrong $B-L$. A state coupled to $q\bar q$ rather than to $q\ell$, or one whose $B-L$ is not proportional to $D$, would break the identification of the diagonal with $B-L$, and with it the reading of the three entries as quarks.
  • If a charge outside $\tfrac13\mathbb{Z}$ were found in the visible sector. A state charged under a new $\mathrm{U}(1)$ that mixes kinetically with hypercharge carries a charge that is irrational in units of $e$, and it is not excluded by any current measurement. That would make "every charge lies in $\tfrac13\mathbb{Z}$" false as a universal statement, and the trace a statement about the hypercharge lattice rather than about all charges.

None of the three has happened, and the third is the one a future experiment could deliver.

6. The Same Trace in the Corpus's Own Language

One section connecting this to the algebra, because the corpus's readers will ask.

6.1 The corpus's $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. Its Cartan subalgebra is generated by three diagonal traceless matrices; $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ is one of them, and its tracelessness is not a choice but the definition of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ as a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{u}(4)$. SU4-meaning.md states this and the statement is unchanged by the migration.

6.2 What the migration adds. That $D$'s eigenvalue array, read as $(B-L)$ up to normalisation, is the one that distinguishes the fourth direction from the three — and that this reading is (A). So the migration does not change the algebra; it states which of the algebra's diagonal directions the physical $B-L$ is, and what that implies.

6.3 The check the corpus can run. That the direction $D$ is canonical: it is the only traceless diagonal that is fixed by the subgroup $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ and that vanishes on the three-space. Verified in the declaration article: the centraliser of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is one-dimensional and is spanned by $iD$. So there was no choice of which diagonal to call $B-L$, conditional on the split. The split is (A); the diagonal is forced.

6.4 The trace that quantises charge is the trace that cancels an anomaly. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$ is also the coefficient of the mixed anomaly $\mathrm{SU}(4)^{2}\,\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$, which is the row $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=1+3\cdot(-\tfrac13)$ of the ledger. So the number that quantises charge and the number that cancels that anomaly are one number, computed by one trace over one quartet. The coincidence is not a coincidence: the anomaly coefficient is defined as the trace of the charge over the fermions in the multiplet, so a frame in which charge is quantised by a trace is a frame in which this particular anomaly vanishes by construction. The two results are one equation read twice — §3.8 of the declaration note, where the row is computed.

Summary

The relation. $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}(B-L)=0$, so $3q+\ell=0$, so the quark's charge is a third of the lepton's, so the proton and the electron cancel exactly.

Why it holds. Because $B-L$ is a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and generators are traceless — not because the charges were chosen well.

What it needs. (A), to say that the single eigenvalue $-1$ is the lepton and the three $+\tfrac13$ are the quarks. The trace gives the arithmetic, the declaration gives the interpretation.

The comparison. Neither theory fixes $e$. The Standard Model fixes the ratios once its field list is assumed and solves a linear and a cubic condition; the frame fixes them from one trace whose coefficients are the counts, three colours against one lepton, so the solution is integral and immediate. The gain is in what is assumed, not in what is computed — §4.6.

The quantum. The charge quantum is $e/(N-1)$, and it is $e/3$ because there are three colours. Four colours would quantise in quarters, two in halves, and the down quark's $-\tfrac13$ shows the unit is attained — §1.3.

The limits. The ratio is explained; the overall scale is a unit convention; the value of $e$ is measured.

The economy. The trace that quantises the charge is the trace that cancels the $\mathrm{SU}(4)^{2}\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ anomaly: one number, two jobs, one equation — §6.4.

The falsifiers. A fourth colour that is not gauged, a resonance with the wrong $B-L$, or a charge outside $\tfrac13\mathbb{Z}$ — §5.5.

The corpus's form. $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ traceless, one-dimensional centraliser, so the diagonal was forced once the split was declared.

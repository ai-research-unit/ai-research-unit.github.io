Flavour Research: The Ladder Arithmetic

Status of this note. Every claim below carries one of six tags. The tags are not decoration. A claim tagged [ANSATZ] or [SPECULATION] is work I have done and do not believe yet, and the note says so where the claim is made rather than in a paragraph at the end. Of the six tags, only the first two carry weight outside this note.

tag meaning weight
[MEASURED] a number from experiment, independent of the corpus usable
[PUBLISHED] a claim already in the literature, attributed usable
[ALGEBRA] exact consequence of a definition, no physics assumed usable, but often trivial
[ANSATZ] an assumption made here, not derived conditional
[SPECULATION] a guess with no support beyond plausibility none

The honest summary is that about one sixth of this note is established, one third is algebra, and the remainder is assumption. The previous version did not say which was which, and that was the defect.

1. The One Established Relation

[PUBLISHED] Koide's relation is

$$ Q\;=\;\frac{m_1+m_2+m_3}{\left(\sqrt{m_1}+\sqrt{m_2}+\sqrt{m_3}\right)^{2}}\;=\;\frac{2}{3}, $$

stated in 1981 for the charged leptons and not derived since. [MEASURED] With the PDG masses it holds as follows.

quantity value
$\sqrt{m_e}$ $0.714842\ \mathrm{MeV}^{1/2}$
$\sqrt{m_\mu}$ $10.279026\ \mathrm{MeV}^{1/2}$
$\sqrt{m_\tau}$ $42.152817\ \mathrm{MeV}^{1/2}$
$\sum\sqrt m$ $53.146685\ \mathrm{MeV}^{1/2}$
$(\sum\sqrt m)^2$ $2824.5701$ MeV
$\sum m$ $1883.029$ MeV
$Q$ $0.66666051$
$2/3$ $0.66666667$
deviation $6.2\times10^{-6}$
ratio to $2/3$ $0.9999908$

The relation holds to six parts in a million for the one triple it was written for. That is the whole empirical content of the charged-lepton ladder, and it is worth more than everything else in this note.

[MEASURED] It does not hold for the other two triples:

sector $Q$ verdict
$e,\mu,\tau$ $0.6666605$ holds to $6\times10^{-6}$
$u,c,t$ $0.8489808$ fails by $0.18$
$d,s,b$ $0.7314277$ fails by $0.065$

So the relation is a one-off, or it is a rule that quarks violate for a reason nobody has supplied. Both readings are open and this note keeps both.

2. Exact Algebra

Four statements below are exact. None needs a medium, a spectrum or a dynamics. They are the entire content of the closure language, and stating them plainly is what removes the appearance of derivation.

2.1 The bound. [ALGEBRA] For positive $m_i$ and $a_i=\sqrt{m_i}$,

$$ Q=\frac{\sum a_i^{2}}{\left(\sum a_i\right)^{2}}\le 1, $$

by Cauchy–Schwarz, with equality if and only if exactly one $a_i$ is nonzero.

2.2 The rung count. [ALGEBRA] Setting $Q=2/N$ in 2.1 gives $N\ge2$. The case $N=2$ requires $Q=1$, hence one massless rung by the equality condition, which is excluded by data. So

$$ N\ge3 . $$

This is the only place the closure touches the number of generations, and it is weak: it excludes two and permits any count above two. It is not a derivation of three, and this note does not present it as one.

2.3 The variance form. [ALGEBRA] With $\bar a=\frac1N\sum a_i$ and $\operatorname{var}=\frac1N\sum(a_i-\bar a)^{2}$,

$$ Q=\frac{2}{N}\iff\operatorname{var}=\bar a^{2}, $$

so the amplitude vector sits at $45^{\circ}$ to the uniform direction. Measured for the leptons, $\operatorname{var}/\bar a^{2}=0.999982$ against $1$, which is 2.1 restated with the same $6\times10^{-6}$.

2.4 The circle. [ALGEBRA] For any $M>0$ and any $\theta$,

$$ a_i=\sqrt M\left(1+\sqrt2\cos\!\left(\theta+\frac{2\pi i}{3}\right)\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; Q=\frac{2}{3}\quad\text{identically}, $$

because $\sum\cos=0$ and $\sum\cos^{2}=3/2$ for three equally spaced phases. This is exact for every $\theta$ and every $M$.

2.4 is the reason this programme is hard to assess, and the note should say it once and plainly: the circle contains no information beyond Koide. Two parameters, three masses, and one degree of freedom left over; the fit consumes that degree of freedom by construction. A test that the leptons lie on the circle is a test of $Q=2/3$ and of nothing else. Any claim that the circle explains or reproduces the closure is a circular claim, and this version of the note does not make it.

3. The Circle as an Ansatz

[ANSATZ] The physical hypothesis is not the circle, which is algebra, but the equal spacing of the phases, $2\pi/N$ in $\theta$. Nothing in the corpus derives that spacing. It is an assumption about a medium, and it enters at exactly one place.

item status count
the closure $Q=2/3$ exact identity of the ansatz given
parameters $M$, $\theta$ fitted to data $2$
data three masses $3$
degrees of freedom left $3-2$ $1$, and it is $Q$
predictions beyond $Q$ none $0$

The table is the whole assessment. A two-parameter family through three points with one identity built in has one degree of freedom and no predictive power. What the ansatz buys is a picture: three rungs on a circle, one near a node. What it costs is that the picture explains nothing, because it cannot fail except by $Q$ failing.

4. Parity: True Inside the Ansatz, False as an Exclusion

[ALGEBRA, inside the ansatz] For even $N$ the phases are antipodal in pairs, and $\cos(\theta+\pi)=-\cos\theta$, so

$$ a_i+a_{i+N/2}=2\sqrt M \qquad\text{exactly, for every }\theta . $$

An even count therefore produces masses in degenerate pairs, symmetric about $\sqrt M$ in amplitude.

$N=4$ at the best fit value
amplitudes $-0.398$, $0.786$, $2.398$, $1.214$
$a_0+a_2$ $2.000$
$a_1+a_3$ $2.000$
masses (MeV) $45.66$, $178.02$, $425.48$, $1658.71$

[WITHDRAWN] The previous version said this excludes every even count by parity and not by $\chi^{2}$, and called it independent of any fit. Both halves are wrong. The pairing is a property of the ansatz at fixed $(M,\theta)$, so it depends on the ansatz entirely. And the table above shows the fitted $N=4$ masses are not paired, because the fit moves $\theta$ off the paired configuration to chase the data.

What survives is weaker and still useful: inside the circle ansatz, an even count cannot give a lone light rung above an empty region. The electron is such a rung. So even counts are disfavoured by the shape of the spectrum, and the $N=4$ fit failure of §5 is the same fact in numbers.

6. The Node, Which Is Koide Again

[ALGEBRA] The amplitude vanishes where $1+\sqrt2\cos\theta=0$, and with the coefficient $\sqrt2$ fixed by 2.4 that is $\theta_0=135^{\circ}$ exactly. The fit returns $132.732^{\circ}$, so the first rung sits $2.268^{\circ}$ from the node.

[WITHDRAWN] The electron is light because the ladder starts almost exactly on the zero of the cosine, offered as a separate finding, and this is the cleanest physical reading the arithmetic offers.

The $2.268^{\circ}$ is not independent information. It is $Q=2/3$ written as an angle, because $\theta_0=135^{\circ}$ is forced by the same $\sqrt2$. A reader who computes $Q$ and a reader who plots the node learn the same one number. The table is kept because it is a good picture, and the picture is all it is: the rung nearest the node is the lightest, which restates the definition of the amplitude and is not a mechanism.

7. The Open Ladder

[ANSATZ] Drop the circle and let the tower be an unbounded power ladder, $a_n\propto n^{p}$. [ALGEBRA, inside that ansatz] Then $Q_N$ has the finite limit

$$ Q_N\longrightarrow\frac{2p+1}{(p+1)^{2}}, $$

and imposing $Q=1/2$ gives $p^{2}-2p-1=0$, so

$$ p=1\pm\sqrt2 . $$

branch $p$ amplitude mass exponent character
growing $+2.414214$ $n^{2.414}$ $+4.828427$ sparse, extends upward
decaying $-0.414214$ $n^{0.586}$ $-0.828427$ piles up at zero, unphysical

[FAILED] Normalised at $m_1$ the growing branch gives $1$, $28.41$, $201.25$ against the measured $1$, $206.77$, $3477.2$. It fits nothing at three rungs. It is a statement about a hypothetical dense tower, and the note says so rather than advertising the exactness of $\sqrt2$.

8. The Continuum Pass: Four Assumptions, One Number

This section produces the number $\rho\propto\omega^{-0.79}$ and the dispersion $\omega\propto k^{4.83d}$. It is the most speculative part of the note, and the assumption chain is longer than the result is useful.

[SPECULATION] The chain, stated as a chain:

  1. the closure holds in the continuum, $\left(\int\sqrt\omega\,\rho\,d\omega\right)^{2}=\frac12\int\omega\rho\,d\omega\int\rho\,d\omega$;
  2. the density of states is a single power law, $\rho\propto\omega^{\gamma}$, on a finite interval;
  3. the interval may be taken as $[0,\Lambda]$ with no low-energy cutoff;
  4. a single dispersion $\omega\propto k^{z}$ holds over the whole range, in $d$ dimensions.

Given 1 to 4,

$z/d$ $2(1+\sqrt2)=4.828427$

[WITHDRAWN] This is the saturation rule as a number rather than a slogan: heavy rungs are rarer is not an assumption of the picture, it is what the closure forces.

It is an assumption of the picture. Assumption 2 is the statement that the density falls as a power, and the algebra then fixes the exponent. Nothing is forced about nature, and the chain has four links, of which the last would need a field theory the corpus does not have.

The useful residue is a target, not a result: if a dispersion is ever written down for the tower, it has to give $z\approx4.83$ in one dimension. That is a falsifiable shape, and the note keeps only that.

9. The Fourth Rung: Three Routes, Three Failures

[ALGEBRA applied to data] The question is whether a fourth charged-lepton mass is predictable. Three routes were tried; all three fail, and the failure is the result.

route construction outcome verdict
A keep the leptons' $(M,\theta)$, set $N=4$ $0.511$, $0.472$, $1205.2$, $1304.5$ MeV muon and tau vanish, replaced by two pairs
B refit $N=4$ to the leptons $45.66$, $178.02$, $425.48$, $1658.71$ MeV, misfit $88$ excluded
C open ladder normalised at $m_e$ $0.511$, $14.5$, $102.8$, $412.4$ MeV, misses $\mu$ by $7$ excluded

So a fourth mass cannot be predicted from the leptons' own numbers. Three data, two parameters, one constraint: the circle is already saturated. A fourth number needs a fourth input, and the only candidates are a second tower's data, a dynamical rule (which is §8), or a symmetry (which the corpus does not have for flavour).

10. What I Think

Opinion, separated from the rest and marked as opinion. Each line carries my confidence.

  1. Koide is the only real fact here. Confidence high. Everything else is a reparametrisation of it, an algebra exercise, or an assumption.
  2. The circle is a coordinate change, not a mechanism. Confidence certain. §2.4 settles it: the circle is Koide, so it cannot be evidence for anything, including itself.
  3. The $N\ge3$ argument is true and nearly content-free. Confidence certain. It excludes two and permits ten.
  4. The $45^{\circ}$ form is the one idea worth keeping. Confidence moderate. It converts why $2/3$ into why this vector sits at $45^{\circ}$ to the uniform direction, which is a geometric question with a possible answer, unlike the number itself.
  5. The continuum pass is numerology until a dispersion exists. Confidence high. Four stacked assumptions to produce one exponent is not a derivation, and presenting it publicly would damage the corpus.
  6. If the programme is ever completed, the missing piece is a boundary condition, not a mass formula. Guess. Fixed phase spacing smells like a quantisation condition on a finite tower, and no amount of fitting produces one.
  7. If nothing derives the phase spacing, this should be recorded as a coincidence with a good parametrisation. Opinion. Six parts in a million is striking, and it is also what one triple out of three gives when you look for a two-parameter pattern.

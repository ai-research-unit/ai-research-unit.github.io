Biquaternion Propagation: The Physics Category Derived From the Mathematics Menu

Introduction

The physics category is derived from the mathematics menu, not edited against it. This article starts from the one hundred and fifty articles of the Biquaternions category, takes them in the order the menu gives, and asks one question of each group: what does this result mean physically, and how many physics articles does the reading need. The answer is twenty-nine articles. The category carries forty-four today.

Why the direction matters. An article list built from the physics side inherits the physics history. That history put the spin at the head of The Modules, inside the algebra, and put the spectral reading inside the same block. The mathematics has the spin in Geometry → Spin Geometry, after the whole algebra and after the rotations and the Lorentz group. So a list built from the algebra first and corrected afterwards keeps the spin in the wrong place, and no amount of vocabulary repair moves it. A list built from the menu puts it where the mathematics puts it by construction.

The rule of derivation, in one table.

rule statement
R1 the order of the physics articles is the order of the mathematics menu, section by section, with no reordering
R2 one physics article to one section of the mathematics menu
R3 a section may take more than one physics article only where the physics has content the mathematics states once, and the exceptions are named and counted
R4 every mathematics article of the section is visible in the physics article: as a link, as a stated result, or as an interpretation
R5 no physics article exists without at least one interpretation; a section that yields no interpretation yields no article

The compression. One hundred and fifty mathematics articles become twenty-nine physics articles, a ratio of about five to one. The ratio is not a target. It is what the reading costs: a mathematics article proves one thing, and several of them prove facets of one thing that a reader of physics wants in one place.

The order is the order of the objects. The algebra comes first because every later section is a structure on it. The spectral strata come after the norms because a stratum is a pair of conditions on two norms. The operators come after the forms because an operator is what the forms are invariant under. The topology, the analysis and the geometry come last because each one is a structure on the algebra, and the spin comes inside the geometry because a spin is a representation of the rotation, and the rotation is a geometric object.

The physics must inherit that order and not a different one. The current category does not. It reads the algebra, then jumps to the frame, then to the modules, and it puts the spin at the head of the modules. In the menu the spin is entry nineteen of twenty and it comes after the Lorentz group. That is the single largest ordering fault in the category, and it is invisible from the physics side because the physics has no reason to know where the mathematics keeps the spin.

The Derivation

How the twenty-nine are obtained. For each section: read the section, write the one thing it establishes, and ask what that one thing means physically. If the answer is one reading, the section takes one article. If the section contains several results that a physicist reads together, the article states them together. If the section contains results that a physicist reads separately — because they are separate regimes, or separate spaces — the section takes several articles, and the exception is named.

The two exceptions, named. Section $10$ takes five articles and section $12$ takes three. Both are cases where the mathematics states one thing and the physics reads several.

Exception one, the spectral strata. The mathematics proves the four sectors in four articles because each proof is a proof. The physics reads four regimes, and a regime is a thing a physicist names and uses: mass in motion, the rest frame, the light ray, the vacuum. Folding them into one article would put four dispersion relations in one file and lose the naming that the rest of the physics menu uses.

Exception two, the remarkable subspaces. The mathematics states the forms and the subspaces in two articles. The physics reads them as a state space: the two sectors as two geometries, the four-vector as the object, and the state-observable-probability triple as the measurement theory. That triple is the largest single reading in the corpus and it does not exist on the mathematics side at all, because it is a reading and not a theorem.

The one place the physics loses a section. The mathematics separates the four general products from the four forms and from the three norms, because each is a separate construction. The physics keeps the three articles, one to each section, and does not add a fourth for the relations between them: the relations are stated inside the products article as the table of what each product preserves.

The Spin Block

The block is four entries, in the mathematics order. The menu gives the carriers article, then three subsections of four, five and four articles. The physics gives one entry to the carriers article and one entry to each subsection. The order is the menu order and it is not negotiable: the carriers article defines the two words, and the three subsections use them.

IV.2 — The Carriers, the Spin and the Closure. Mathematics: The Carriers, the Representation, the Spin and the Closure. The entry states the definition — a carrier is a nonzero real subspace, invariant and irreducible under one of the algebra's own actions, and the action is part of the carrier — and the three carriers with their dimensions. It states the two repairs the closure performs, invariance for the half-integer case and the restored norm for both, and the one thing it does not repair, the vanishing norm on the ideal. Its reading is the sentence a field article needs: a field is a function with values in the closure of its carrier, and its spin is the weight of the carrier.

IV.3 — The Spin-Zero Carrier. Mathematics: the four articles of the spin-zero subsection. The entry carries four readings in one place, and each one is a reading of one mathematics article. The subspace: the real line is the carrier and the complex line is the closure, so the phase is the one degree of freedom the closure adds to the carrier. The norm: on the complex line the norm is $\lambda^{2}$, real, of one sign; the mass shell is a single sheet. The spectrum: the adjoint action fixes every point, so the weight is $0$ with multiplicity two and the generator is zero; the clock does not tick and the only evolution is the phase. The sectors: the carrier meets the rest frame and the vacuum and nothing else, and the exclusion of the other two is structural, since a central element has no vector part.

IV.4 — The Spin-Half Carrier. Mathematics: the five articles of the spin-half subsection, including Spinors and Dirac Spinors in the Biquaternion Algebra. The subspace: the plane is the carrier and the spinor space is its closure; the axis is a parameter of the family and not a datum of the algebra, so the algebra carries infinitely many of each and singles out none. The norm: every element of the ideal is of rank at most one, so $N=0$ at every point and a single value of the field is always massless; the mass of the field is the pairing of its two parts, $N(\tilde\Xi_L+\tilde\Xi_R)=2B(\tilde\Xi_L,\tilde\Xi_R)$, which is the algebra's statement that a Dirac mass is a mixing of two chiralities. The spectrum: left multiplication acts with weights $\pm\tfrac12$, so the two chiral rates and the exceptional point where they coincide. The sectors: the closure meets all four, the carrier meets two, and the difference is the content of the sector article.

IV.5 — The Spin-One Carrier. Mathematics: the four articles of the spin-one subsection. The subspace: the triple is the carrier and the material sector is its closure; the extra direction is the generator of the central phase and not the identity, which is why the spin-one closure does not contain the spin-zero carrier. The norm: real, of signature $(1,3)$, with the sign the corpus writes, so the mass shell is one sheet of one cone. The spectrum: the two-sided action gives $+1$, $-1$ and $0$, the integers of the vector representation. The sectors: the only carrier that reaches the region of mass in motion, because it is the only closure on which both invariants are real and the vector square is a sum of squares; and the nilpotent stratum inside it is the origin alone.

Why four and not fourteen. The mathematics needs fourteen articles because fourteen proofs. The physics needs four because there are four things to say: what carries the spin, and then one article per spin. The four readings inside each per-spin article are the four mathematics articles of that subsection, and the physics article is their joint reading. This is the exact place where the compression happens, and it is why the block is the test case for the whole plan.

The arithmetic, as implemented. Forty-four entries become twenty-nine entries plus this introduction. Three survive in the live list: the introduction, rewritten here; The Two Sectors: Split and the Four Bridges, taken unchanged as I.12.1; and The Unit Group, the Cone and the Orders, taken unchanged as II.1. The other forty-one are kept whole under the menu heading ARCHIVE, at the end of the category, and are retired one at a time as their readings are carried into the entries that absorb them. Nothing is deleted and no entry is a stub on disk: the twenty-seven that are titled and not written have dead links, which is the open work.

The four largest absorptions. I.12.3 takes six articles and $36{,}498$ words; I.9 takes six and $31{,}377$; I.1 takes three and $24{,}087$; I.3 takes three and $19{,}074$. Each of the four is a case where the physics had grown one article per result and the mathematics states the results as one section. The entry keeps every reading and states them once, in the order of the mathematics.

The one entry that grows rather than shrinks. I.10 becomes five entries from four kept articles plus one rewritten, because the partition article is new work: the mathematics now proves the partition and the physics must state it once, in one place, before the four regimes.

The check at the end of each wave. Every entry names its mathematics section and its count; every link resolves; every mathematics article of the section appears in the entry as a link or as a stated result; every entry carries at least one labelled interpretation. The four checks are mechanical.

The size. The category keeps its words, because absorbing an article moves its readings and does not shorten them. It adds new text in two places: the spin block, which the corpus does not have, and the partition article. It ends at about the present length, in a third fewer files.

Summary

The physics category is derived from the mathematics menu and inherits its order. One hundred and fifty mathematics articles in twenty sections become twenty-nine physics articles in twenty sections. The order is the menu order. The spin sits in the geometry, after the rotations and the Lorentz group, where the mathematics puts it.

One article to one section, with two named exceptions. The spectral strata take five entries because a regime is a thing the physics names. The remarkable subspaces take three because the state space is a reading the mathematics does not have. Every other section takes one entry, and every mathematics article of the section is visible in it.

Each entry carries readings, and the readings are why it exists. An entry with no interpretation is not written. An entry that states a result and does not read it is not written. The forty-four existing articles are sources: fourteen become the core of an entry and sixteen are absorbed into one.

The spin block is four entries and it is the test. The carriers article defines the two words; the three per-spin articles carry the subspace, the norm, the spectrum and the sectors for one spin each. Fourteen mathematics articles, four physics articles, fourteen readings. If the compression works anywhere, it works there.

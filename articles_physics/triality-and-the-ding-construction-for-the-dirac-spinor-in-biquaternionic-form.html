Triality and the Ding Construction for the Dirac Spinor in Biquaternionic Form

Introduction

The triality of $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ is the striking fact that the spin group of eight-dimensional Euclidean space has three eight-dimensional irreducible real representations — the vector $8_v$ and the two representations $8_s$ and $8_c$ beside it — and that an outer automorphism of order three permutes them cyclically. The associated invariant is a symmetric trilinear form, the composition law of the octonions, and the whole structure exists only in dimension eight. The mathematics of that case is recorded in the companion article Triality and Spin(8) with Inner Conjugation.

In four-dimensional Minkowski space the three spaces do not coincide. A Lorentz vector has four real components and each of the two representations beside it has four, but they carry inequivalent representations and no outer automorphism permutes them. Liu Yu-Fen's observation is that a trilinear form nevertheless exists on the triple (Lorentz vector) $\times$ (first four-vector) $\times$ (second four-vector), built from the four generator basis operators and the timelike neutral vector $u_\mu$, and that the resulting construction — which he calls a ding — is the four-dimensional analogue of Cartan's triality, with the biquaternion algebra in place of the octonions. The order-three permutation is still there. What replaces the coincidence of the three eight-dimensional representations is the coincidence of the three real dimensions in $1+3$.

Two invariants come out of the construction and they are not the same object. The trilinear form $\varepsilon$ takes three arguments, one in each space, and is linear in each. The cubic form $C$ takes one vector and one field, is quadratic in the field, and splits into a quadratic part and the trilinear part. The source exhibits them together; this article keeps them apart, because the first is the triality invariant and the second is the interaction the dictionary of the companion article uses.

This article records the construction, the two invariant forms, the order-three map $J$, and the order-four braid maps that Liu calls "Dirac's game". It is the group-theoretic companion of The s-Vector Representation of the Dirac Spinor in Biquaternionic Form, which carries the dictionary and the Lagrangian; this article carries the symmetry structure.

The conventions are those of the companion articles, and none is restated here: the algebra, its units and its element glyphs are those of Algebra and Element Conventions, the coordinate and the frame those of Frame and Metric Conventions, the named operators and the Dirac operator those of Operator and Calculus Conventions. The index $\mu$ runs over $t,x,y,z$, and the sign weight $\varepsilon$ is the one Frame and Metric Conventions fixes.

The Three Spaces

The Spaces

Liu's construction lives on the product of three spaces. The element $\tilde U$ is a unit biquaternion of the Lorentz group; it acts on the three spaces by three inequivalent linear maps — left multiplication, right multiplication, and the congruence on the vector. Each space is four-real-dimensional, and its carrier is a four-vector of components.

Space Name Real dimension Action of $\tilde U$ Carrier
$M$ Lorentz-vector space 4 $\Lambda(\tilde U)$ $A_\mu$
$S_1$ first four-vector space 4 $S_1(\tilde U)$ $B_\mu$
$S_2$ second four-vector space 4 $S_2(\tilde U)$ $N_\mu$

The three actions are written out: $S_1(\tilde U):Q\mapsto\tilde UQ$, $S_2(\tilde U):Q\mapsto Q\tilde U$ and $\Lambda(\tilde U):Q\mapsto\tilde UQ\tilde U^{*}$.

The two four-vectors $B_\mu$ and $N_\mu$ are the real and the imaginary coefficient four-vectors of one element of $\mathbb{B}$: the field is $$ \tilde{Ξ}=\tilde B+i\tilde N,\qquad \tilde B=\sum_\mu B_\mu a_\mu,\qquad \tilde N=\sum_\mu N_\mu a_\mu, $$ with $\tilde B$ and $\tilde N$ real quaternions and $a_\mu$ the four units in the order $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$. The complex four-vector $G_\mu=B_\mu+iN_\mu$ is the s-vector, the coefficient vector of $\tilde{Ξ}$ in those units. The neutral vector is timelike, $\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu u_\mu u_\mu=1$; in the source's frame $k=(1,0,0,0)$.

The Generator Operators

Each four-vector index carries a left unit and a right unit, $a_0=e_t$, $a_\eta=\vec e_\eta$ and $b_0=\vec e_x$, $b_k=\vec e_y$. A basis operator is a pair of multipliers, acting by $Q\mapsto cQd$, and the four generator basis operators are the four maps $$ C_\mu:\ Q\longmapsto i\,a_\mu\,Q\,b_\mu,\qquad \mu=t,x,y,z . $$

The basis operators are the objects the construction uses: the trilinear form, the cubic form and the Dirac operator are all built from them, and the name generator belongs to the four basis operators and to nothing else. Two generators compose by substitution, and the product is read at the point of use: $$ C_\mu C_\nu(Q) = i^2\,a_\mu a_\nu\,Q\,b_\nu b_\mu = -\,a_\mu a_\nu\,Q\,b_\nu b_\mu , $$ a derived form. The two prefactors multiply to $i^2=-1$, and what survives is a product of the units. That minus is the whole of the double $i$: it is used once, here, and it never appears in a display. The four generators obey the anticommutation relations, on every $Q$, $$ C_\mu C_\nu(Q) + C_\nu C_\mu(Q) = 2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}Q , $$ which fix their normalisation once the frame is chosen.

The units they use are the quaternion ones of $\mathbb{B}$: $\vec e_\eta^2=-e_t$, $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$ cyclically, and $\vec e_x\vec e_y\vec e_z=-e_t$. The source exhibits them by an explicit array; the relations above are the algebra's and do not depend on that choice.

The Ding

An order-$\ell$ ding is an $\ell$-linear map on a product of the three spaces with values in $\mathbb{C}$, built from the generator basis operators and the neutral elements. For each order the source exhibits the map explicitly. The invariant content of the construction is its two low-order maps, which are the ones that can be contracted with the physical fields; they are the subject of the next two sections.

The Trilinear Form

The Form

The order-three invariant is the source's Ding-3: the trilinear form $$ \boxed{\;\varepsilon : \ M \times S_1 \times S_2 \longrightarrow \mathbb{C}, \qquad \varepsilon(A, B, N) = 2\, A_\mu\, E_{\nu\mu\lambda\sigma}k_\sigma\, B_\nu N_\lambda\;}, $$ where $E_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}$ is the totally antisymmetric symbol of the four generators, defined below and normalised by $E_{0123} = 1$. The factor two is not a convention of the symbol: it is the normalisation that makes the form equal twice the determinant of the three spatial parts.

The contraction with the single vector $\vec k_\Phi$ makes the form a determinant. In the frame $k = (1,0,0,0)$ only the spatial parts of $A$, $B$ and $N$ survive, and the determinant is a scalar part of a product of three pure quaternions, with $\vec A=\sum_\eta A_\eta\vec e_\eta$ and likewise $\vec B$, $\vec N$: $$ \varepsilon(A,B,N) = 2\,(\vec A\times\vec B)\cdot\vec N = -2\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\vec A\,\vec B\,\vec N\bigr), $$ so the form vanishes whenever one of its three arguments is proportional to $k_\Phi$. It is alternating in its three arguments, and that is why the cyclic map of the next section leaves it invariant.

The Geometric Interpretation

The interpretation is Cartan's, in four dimensions. In the $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ case the invariant trilinear form on $8_v \times 8_s \times 8_c$ is the action of the vector by multiplication composed with the invariant pairing of the two eight-dimensional representations. Here the trilinear form on $M \times S_1 \times S_2$ is the part of the cubic form that is odd in each of the two four-vectors, evaluated on the neutral direction $\vec k_\Phi$. It is the same construction in four dimensions, and it is the lowest-order invariant the four basis operators supply.

The Cubic Form and the Dictionary

The Cubic Form

Evaluated on the field rather than on a triple, the same basis operators give the cubic form of the interaction. This object is quadratic in $\tilde B$ and $\tilde N$ and linear in $A$, and in the frame $k=(1,0,0,0)$ it is read on the elements alone: $$ C = \Bigl(\sum_\mu A_\mu u_\mu\Bigr)\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde B^2+\tilde N^2\bigr) + \varepsilon(A,B,N), $$ a derived form: the basis operator $C_\mu$ puts $a_\mu$ on the left and $b_\mu$ on the right, the $t$ slot collapses because $e_t$ is the unit, and the two spatial units that remain carry the field back onto $e_t$. Written on the four-index tensors the same object is $$ C = A_\mu\,t_{\mu\nu\lambda}\bigl(B_\nu B_\lambda + N_\nu N_\lambda\bigr) + 2A_\mu\,E_{\nu\mu\lambda}B_\nu N_\lambda , $$ and the three-index symbols are the four-index ones contracted with the neutral vector; they carry three indices only for brevity: $$ t_{\mu\nu\lambda} := t_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}k_\sigma, \qquad E_{\nu\mu\lambda} := E_{\nu\mu\lambda\sigma}k_\sigma . $$

The four-index tensors are read off the generators, with $\mathrm{Sc}$ as the conventions fix it, $$ t_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho} = \mathrm{Sc}(a_\mu a_\nu a_\lambda a_\rho)\,\mathrm{Sc}(b_\rho b_\lambda b_\nu b_\mu) = \varepsilon_\mu\varepsilon_\lambda\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\lambda\rho} + \varepsilon_\mu\varepsilon_\nu\delta_{\mu\rho}\delta_{\nu\lambda} - \varepsilon_\nu\varepsilon_\lambda\delta_{\mu\lambda}\delta_{\nu\rho}, $$ $$ E_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho} = \mathrm{Sc}(a_\mu a_\nu a_\lambda a_\rho)\,\mathrm{Sc}(b_\rho b_\lambda b_\nu b_\mu\, \vec e_z)\,. $$

The right multiplier of the symbol is the unit $\vec e_z$: the choice is declared here, and it is the only place in the article where a single unit is singled out. The first is symmetric under the interchange of its two index pairs, the second is totally antisymmetric with $E_{0123} = 1$, and both identities hold on every index value. The same $t$ multiplies $B_\nu B_\lambda$ and $N_\nu N_\lambda$: the two four-vectors enter the quadratic part with one coefficient and one sign, and the sign difference between the two sectors appears only in the quadratic forms of the next subsection. The last term of $C$ is the trilinear form itself, so the cubic form is the quadratic part plus $\varepsilon$.

The Quadratic Forms

The two quadratic forms are read on the same basis operators, and they are the two parts of one scalar, $$ \mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ξ}^2) = \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu G_\mu G_\mu , $$ a derived form: the scalar part of the square of the field keeps the $t$ coefficient and reverses the three spatial ones, so it is the s-vector contracted with the sign weight. Its real part is minus the quadratic form and its imaginary part is minus $i$ times the pseudoscalar: $$ \mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ξ}^2) = \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\bigl(B_\mu B_\mu - N_\mu N_\mu\bigr) + 2i\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu B_\mu N_\mu , $$ $$ \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\bigl(N_\mu N_\mu - B_\mu B_\mu\bigr) = -\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ξ}^2), \qquad -2i\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu B_\mu N_\mu = -i\,\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ξ}^2). $$

The quadratic form is real, of indefinite sign; the pseudoscalar is purely imaginary. Written in the s-vector $G_\mu = B_\mu + iN_\mu$, with $G^{*}_\mu = B_\mu - iN_\mu$, the single scalar $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ξ}^2)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu G_\mu G_\mu$ carries both, and $\mathrm{Sc}\bigl((\tilde{Ξ}^*)^2\bigr)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu G^{*}_\mu G^{*}_\mu$ is its conjugate: the quadratic form is minus its real part and the pseudoscalar is minus $i$ times its imaginary part. With the trilinear form and the two quadratic forms, $C$ is the complete invariant content of the algebra: the quadratic forms define the metric on the two four-vector spaces, and $C$ is the triality-invariant coupling of the vector to the pair. The dictionary of The s-Vector Representation of the Dirac Spinor in Biquaternionic Form exhibits all of these as $G$-$G^*$ bilinears with the structure constants $c$ and $\check c$.

The Ding Map

The Order-Three Map

The map $$ J : \ A \mapsto B, \qquad B \mapsto N, \qquad N \mapsto A $$ permutes the three spaces cyclically and leaves the trilinear form $\varepsilon$ invariant: $$ \boxed{\;\varepsilon(J(A,B,N)) = \varepsilon(B, N, A) = \varepsilon(A, B, N)\;}. $$

The invariance is the alternation of $\varepsilon$: a cyclic permutation of the three arguments is an even permutation. The order of $J$ is three, $J^3 = 1$, and it acts on the product of the three spaces, not inside any one of them. $J$ is not an inner automorphism of $\mathbb{B}$; it is an outer permutation of three inequivalent spaces. That is exactly the situation of the Cartan triality, where the outer automorphism group of the $D_4$ diagram is $S_3$ and the three-node orbit is the three eight-dimensional representations. What is special to the four-dimensional case is that the three spaces are the vector and the two four-vectors of a Lorentz group, which have equal real dimension, and that the invariant form is built from the four generator basis operators rather than from the octonion product.

The Quartic Maps

The source also exhibits order-four maps that act inside the triple and generalise the triality moves: $$ q_x : \ (A, B, N) \mapsto (-B, A, N), \qquad q_y : \ (A, B, N) \mapsto (A, -N, B) . $$

These are fourth roots of unity, $$ q_x^4 = 1, \qquad q_y^4 = 1, $$ with the squares $q_x^2 : (A,B,N) \mapsto (-A,-B,N)$ and $q_y^2 : (A,B,N) \mapsto (A,-B,-N)$, both involutions. They satisfy the braid relation $$ \boxed{\;q_x q_y q_x = q_y q_x q_y\;}, $$ so they define a representation of the braid group $B_3$ on three strands, and the group they generate has order $24$.

The two families are not independent. The product of the two generators is the square of the triality, $$ q_x q_y : \ (A,B,N) \mapsto (N,A,B) = J^2 , $$ so $J$ lies in the group generated by $q_x$ and $q_y$: the order-three and the order-four moves are two elements of one group, not two separate structures. What the source names "Dirac's game" is the word $$ q_x q_y q_y q_x = q_y^2 , $$ the simultaneous reversal of the two four-vectors. That word leaves the cubic form $C$ invariant term by term: $B_\nu B_\lambda$ and $N_\nu N_\lambda$ are even under $B \mapsto -B$, $N \mapsto -N$, and so is the product $B_\nu N_\lambda$, while $A$ is untouched. The order-four family supplies the sign reversal that the order-three family does not.

The contrast with Cartan's case is worth naming. There the triality automorphisms have order three and the braid-group promotion appears in the octonionic description, through the alternative laws $x(yz) = (xy)z$ obeyed only up to sign. Here the two families coexist as stated maps on the triple, because the algebra is associative and the maps are exhibited directly.

What the Ding Is and Is Not

It is a genuine order-three symmetry of the invariant trilinear form on the triple (vector, first four-vector, second four-vector). Every identity stated above — the trilinear form and its determinant form, the cubic decomposition with its two three-index symbols, the two quadratic forms, the invariance of $\varepsilon$ under $J$, the orders of $q_x$ and $q_y$, the braid relation, the identity $q_xq_y = J^2$ and the invariance of $C$ under $q_xq_yq_yq_x$ — was recomputed on the corpus's convention and holds.

It is not a symmetry of the theory. The source is explicit: the ding is a map from one description of the theory to another description of the same theory, not a transformation of the physical fields. A symmetry of the theory would have to act on a single field configuration and leave the action invariant; $J$ permutes three different spaces, each of which is the same field read in a different representation. This is the same distinction the corpus draws for the triality of $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$: the triality automorphism is not a symmetry of an ordinary field theory either, it relates the vector and the two description spaces.

It is not the octonionic triality. The algebra here is the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}$, which is associative and has zero divisors; the algebra of the Cartan case is the octonions, which are not associative. What the two share is the underlying reason: the normed condition — the existence of a composition law preserving the quadratic form — which by Hurwitz's theorem is possible only in dimensions $1, 2, 4$ and $8$. The four-dimensional case is the complexified quaternions; the eight-dimensional case is the octonions. The triality that the four-dimensional case supports is a spin-triality on the three four-real-dimensional spaces, not the $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ triality on the three eight-dimensional ones.

Open Questions

  1. The order-three automorphisms of $\mathbb{B}$. The corpus's Biquaternion Automorphisms and Derivations records the inner automorphisms of $\mathbb{B}$ — conjugations by the unit group — and the outer ones. The ding map $J$ is an outer permutation of three spaces; is it induced by an automorphism of the algebra, or by an anti-automorphism, or by neither? An automorphism of $\mathbb{B}$ that realises $J$ on the s-vector would settle the question.

  2. The relation to the Cartan case. The four-dimensional ding and the eight-dimensional triality are the $4$ and $8$ cases of the Hurwitz list. Is there a uniform construction for $1, 2, 4, 8$ — with the complex numbers, the quaternions and the octonions? The $1$- and $2$-dimensional cases are degenerate; the $4$- and $8$-dimensional cases are the ones Liu's construction and Cartan's triality treat.

  3. The role of the braid group. The braid relation $q_xq_yq_x = q_yq_xq_y$ is the defining relation of $B_3$, and the two maps generate a group of order $24$, so they define a representation of $B_3$ with a finite image. The corpus's Anyons and Braid Statistics in Biquaternionic Form treats braid statistics as a physical phenomenon in $2+1$ dimensions. Is the braid structure used by the four-dimensional construction that same braid group, or only the same presentation?

  4. The three-generation interpretation. A triality relates exactly three spaces. The Standard Model has exactly three generations of fermions. Whether the ding's order-three structure is the group-theoretic shadow of the three generations — or merely a numerical coincidence of the same number — is not addressed by the source, and the corpus records the observation only as a speculation.

These questions are open.

The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices. The generators carry no frame label: $\gamma_\mu$ is one set of matrices, and the frame is named on the coordinate.

The gammas are a frame of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, not a different algebra, and in the basis operator notation they are the four generator basis operators with a name:

$$ \gamma_\mu = C_\mu, \qquad \gamma_0=iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_x},\quad \gamma_1=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_x}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_2=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_3=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_5=-iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z}. $$

The same in the axis-free display:

$$ \gamma_\mu = C_\mu, \qquad \gamma_0=iL_{\mathrm{axf},e_t}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x},\quad \gamma_1=iL_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_2=iL_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_3=iL_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_5=-iL_{\mathrm{axf},e_t}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z}. $$

Here $L_{c}R_{d}$ multiplies on the left by $c$ and on the right by $d$: $L_{c}R_{d}\tilde{Ξ}=c\tilde{Ξ} d$. A single product is a pure tensor; the products form the seven-dimensional cone of rank at most one, and a general element is a sum of at most four.

The frame obeys the anticommutation relations $\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\} = 2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}$ and the chirality relations $\gamma_5^2 = e_t$ and $\{\gamma_5,\gamma_\mu\} = 0$, with $\gamma_5 = i\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$; the sign in the last identity is the one that makes $\gamma_5$ the fifth generator and not its negative. $\gamma_5$ is the chirality $\Gamma$ itself, $\Gamma = -iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z} = \gamma_5$. The central grading $\chi=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ — the difference of the two central idempotents of the complexified algebra, so $\chi^2=e_t$ — is a different object, represented by $\gamma_5$ on the Dirac module.

The dictionary is complex-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the basis operators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

The display. Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. The two are carried into one another by $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}=C_{\Pi}L_{\mathrm{pau},e_\mu}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and a conjugation by $C_{\Pi}$ changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant, so every identity of the section holds in both. An array is a statement about one display; a basis operator named in the running text keeps the Peirce label, and its axis-free array is the conjugate. The label names the array and not the operator.

Summary

A ding is an $\ell$-linear map on a product of the Lorentz-vector space $M$, the first four-vector space $S_1$ and the second four-vector space $S_2$, built from the four generator basis operators $C_\mu : Q\mapsto ia_\mu Qb_\mu$ and the timelike neutral vector $\vec k_\Phi$. The order-three ding is the invariant trilinear form $$ \varepsilon(A,B,N) = 2\,A_\mu\,E_{\nu\mu\lambda\sigma}k_\sigma B_\nu N_\lambda, $$ alternating in its three arguments and equal to twice the determinant of the three spatial parts in a frame where $k=(1,0,0,0)$; its evaluation on the field is the cubic form $$ C = \Bigl(\sum_\mu A_\mu u_\mu\Bigr)\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde B^2+\tilde N^2\bigr) + \varepsilon(A,B,N) = A_\mu t_{\mu\nu\lambda}\bigl(B_\nu B_\lambda + N_\nu N_\lambda\bigr) + 2A_\mu\,E_{\nu\mu\lambda}B_\nu N_\lambda, $$ with $t$ and $\varepsilon$ the generator traces contracted with $k$. The order-three map $J : A \mapsto B \mapsto N \mapsto A$ permutes the three spaces and leaves $\varepsilon$ invariant; this is the four-dimensional analogue of Cartan's triality, with the biquaternion algebra in place of the octonions, and it exists because the three spaces have equal real dimension in $1+3$. The order-four maps $q_x : (A,B,N) \mapsto (-B,A,N)$ and $q_y : (A,B,N) \mapsto (A,-N,B)$ satisfy $q_x^4 = q_y^4 = 1$ and the braid relation $q_xq_yq_x = q_yq_xq_y$; their product is $q_xq_y = J^2$, so the order-three and the order-four moves generate one group, of order $24$, and the word $q_xq_yq_yq_x = q_y^2$ leaves the cubic form invariant — "Dirac's game".

What the construction supplies is the symmetry structure of the triple; what it does not supply is a symmetry of the theory. The ding is a map between descriptions, not a transformation of the fields, and the algebra that carries it is the associative biquaternion algebra, not the octonions. Both cases are the $4$ and the $8$ of the Hurwitz list.

Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$

Introduction

A square root of the central value $0$ in one of the twelve structures is a nonzero element whose square in that product is zero, $\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0$. The corpus calls such an element square-zero and writes it $\tilde\Upsilon$, following Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe; the mathematics companion calls it a nilpotent of index two, and the square-zero set of a structure is the solution set of the equation, the origin included. The two names are one object: a root of zero is a square-zero element, and this article is at once the roots-of-zero part of the three named values and the physics companion of the mathematics Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space (articles_maths/square-roots-of-zero-and-the-nilpotents-in-the-algebraic-structures-over-the-biquaternioid-c-space.md).

The essential physics of the roots of zero is fixed by the corpus. A square-zero element annihilates itself, so the square-zero set is the self-annihilation part of the zero-divisor theory, and its physical object is the lightlike displacement: the algebraic form of an infinitesimal object, of which two successive applications give nothing beyond the first. The physical member is the photon, and the owners are The Null Four-Momentum: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not and The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone. The same square-zero equation carries a second physics that is exact and less expected: the nilpotent class contains the fermionic ladder operators of a single mode, so that Pauli exclusion is a vanishing square and the anticommutator of the ladder is the identity.

The two organising statements are the mathematics companion's. First, the square-zero sets of the twelve structures are ordered by the lightlike content of their bands: the two probability structures have the origin alone, the plain structures have the pure isotropic cone, the two quaternionic bilinear structures have the whole cone, the two quaternionic sesquilinear structures have a proper subfamily of the cone, the two alternating brackets have the whole space, and the two conjugate-alternating brackets have a subfamily outside the cone that contains the unit. Second, the square-zero set lies inside the zero-divisor set of its structure and it contains the cone, but the converse fails for nine of the twelve structures, and the gap is physical: a state is not a lightlike displacement.

The values are read in the corpus order. The roots of $+1$ and the idempotents are the subject of the companion Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$; the general radicand, the four existence classes and the mass shell are the subject of Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$; the degeneracy and the states are owned by Zero Divisors in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$ and Annihilators in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$. This article owns the lightlike displacements and the nilpotents.

Conventions. We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$; $i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The central square is $C(\tilde P)e_t=\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde P$ with $C(\tilde P)=\sum_\mu P_\mu^{2}$, and the cone is $\mathcal{C}=\{C=0\}$. An element is pure when $P_0=0$; the material coordinate is $\tilde P=i c t\,e_t+x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$. The four square-zero sets of the four general products are written $Z_1$ (plain), $Z_2$ (natural), $Z_3$ (plain sesquilinear) and $Z_4$ (quaternionic sesquilinear). The twelve structures are cited by their three-letter names.

The Root Set of Zero Is a Cone

A signed root set is a level set; the root set of $0$ is a cone. For each structure the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_t$ with $\lambda\neq0$ cuts a level set of the square, while $\lambda=0$ cuts the zero set of the same function, and the zero set of a quadratic function is a cone: it is closed under scaling, it contains the origin and it sets a direction and no magnitude. The signed values set a magnitude, the value $0$ sets a direction, and that difference is the first physical fact of the three named values. The root set of $0$ is the square-zero set $\{\tilde\Upsilon:\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0\}$, origin included, and it is the same set as the nilpotent set of the structure.

In the plain product, the pure isotropic cone. The plain square of a pure element is $-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)e_t$, so the nonzero roots of $0$ are the pure elements with $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$. Written with real vectors as $\tilde\Xi=\vec q+i\vec v$, the equations are $\vec q^{2}=\vec v^{2}$ and $\vec q\cdot\vec v=0$; the root set is the pure isotropic cone $\{\Xi_0=0,\ \Xi_x^{2}+\Xi_y^{2}+\Xi_z^{2}=0\}$, of real dimension four, and it is one component of the plain cone $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$. The other component is the non-pure cone, the multiples of the projectors, and the two components meet at the origin alone. The physical interpretation is the lightlike displacement, the solution set of the wave equation, and the owner is The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone.

In the natural product, the whole cone. The natural square is the central square $C(\tilde\Xi)e_t=\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi$, so the equation is the vanishing of the complex norm and the root set is the whole cone $\mathcal{C}=\{C=0\}$, of real dimension six. The natural product reads masslessness completely, and the three shells of the interval are the root sets of the three named values: the shell $C=-1$, the cone $C=0$ and the shell $C=+1$.

In the probability band, the origin alone. The scalar part of the square is $H=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$, positive definite, so the only root of $0$ is $\tilde\Xi=0$: the band has no nonzero root of $0$, hence no lightlike element, while almost every element of the band is non-invertible. A probability is non-invertible for the reason that it is a probability, not because it is lightlike.

In the gauge band, a proper subfamily of the cone. The fourth product's square vanishes on the subfamily $\{\overline{\tilde\Xi}\tilde\Xi=0\}$, a proper subfamily of the cone containing $e_t+i\vec e_x$ and missing $ie_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+i\vec e_z$. In the parametrisation $\tilde\Xi=c(\vec u+it)$ the square is the central $c^{2}(t^{2}-1)e_t$, so the parametrised family meets the cone at the slopes $\lvert t\rvert=1$, while the full square-zero set of the structure is wider; the parametrised description and the full description are both recorded in the mathematics companion, which owns the set. The owner of the indefinite metric is The Biquaternioid General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (GQS) over $\mathbb{C}$.

In the four antisymmetric structures. The square of the two alternating brackets vanishes identically, so the whole space is the root set of $0$; the square of the two conjugate-alternating brackets is a pure vector, and its vanishing admits the unit $e_t$, whose bracket square vanishes there, so their root set of $0$ lies outside the cone and contains $e_t$.

A signed root is a unit and a nonzero root of $0$ is a zero divisor. The central square is the criterion of invertibility: $C\neq0$ is invertible and $C=0$ is a zero divisor. A signed root has $C=\pm1\neq0$ and is a unit; a nonzero root of $0$ has $C=0$ and is not. The three values are the two ends of the invertibility criterion read on the square, and the two nonzero ends are reversible while the middle is not. The rank-drop reading of the same fact, rank two massive and rank one massless, is Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger.

The two sub-families inside the cone. The cone of roots of $0$ splits as the cone itself does. The pure roots, $\Xi_t=0$ with $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$, are the nilpotent lightlike displacements of the vector subspace; the non-pure family, reached only in the structures whose square is not the plain square, consists of the square-zero elements of the indefinite bands and carries the lightlike family $c(\vec u\pm i)$ of the gauge sesquilinear structure. The elementary physical case is a pure nilpotent $\tilde\Upsilon$ with $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$: a translation along a null direction, whose iteration adds nothing quadratic. The corpus's nilpotent convention and the class are owned by this article, and the square-zero condition of the fourth product belongs to The Square of the General Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and the Two Halves (mathematics).

The failure is the definition, not a defect. A root of $0$ is a zero divisor, so the value $0$ is the non-reversible value of the criterion, and physically this is the definition of a massless object: a lightlike displacement cannot be undone by a lightlike displacement, because it has no inverse, and the whole cone is the set of operations with no inverse.

A Square-Zero Element Is an Infinitesimal

The equation and the self-annihilation. The equation $\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0$ has a direct reading: the element kills itself. In the language of the companion article Annihilators in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$ the square-zero set is the set of elements lying in their own annihilator, $\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0$ exactly when $\tilde\Upsilon\in A_\star(\tilde\Upsilon)$ and $\tilde\Upsilon\in B_\star(\tilde\Upsilon)$, the annihilator being read in the same product.

The physical interpretation. A nonzero element whose square vanishes is an infinitesimal object: applied once it moves, applied twice it gives nothing beyond the first order, and that is the defining property of a first-order generator. The physical members are the lightlike displacement and the photon, read in The Null Four-Momentum: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not and The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone. The same square-zero algebra appears a second time, as the algebra of the Grassmann generators of the corpus, read in Grassmann Coherent States in Biquaternionic Form; the two occurrences are distinct, and the closing section of this article separates them. The nilpotent convention $\tilde\Upsilon$ marks the elements of $\mathbb{B}$ in Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

The square-zero set is the lightlike set of its band. The physical content of the twelve square-zero sets is thus a classification of the lightlike objects of the twelve bands, and the physical question each band answers is whether a lightlike object exists in it at all.

The Twelve Structures at the Roots of Zero

The equation $\tilde P\star\tilde P=0$ is written in the operation of the structure, so each of the twelve carries a root set of $0$ of its own, which is at the same time its square-zero set. The twelve are read one by one below, each naming its product, its root set and the reason, before the table collects the twelve in one place.

$\mathrm{GPA}$, the general plain algebra — composition

The product is the plain product and its square is the plain square. The root set of $0$ is the pure isotropic cone $\{P_0=0,\ (\vec P,\vec P)=0\}$, of real dimension four, one component of the zero-divisor cone: the lightlike displacements that are their own annihilating partner. It is a proper subfamily of the cone, because a non-pure cone element is a multiple of a projector and not square-zero. The reading is The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$.

$\mathrm{GPS}$, the general plain sesqualgebra — probability

The product is the pairing and its square is the Hermitian square, of scalar part $\sum_\mu\lvert P_\mu\rvert^{2}$; the equation forces the origin. The root set of $0$ is $\{0\}$ alone: the probability band has no lightlike element. The reading is Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger.

$\mathrm{GQA}$, the general quaternionic algebra — causality

The product is the natural product and its square is the central element $C(\tilde P)e_t$; the equation is the vanishing of the norm, so the root set of $0$ is the whole cone $\mathcal{C}$. Every lightlike element is self-annihilating, and the set coincides with the zero-divisor set, origin included. The reading is The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$.

$\mathrm{GQS}$, the general quaternionic sesqualgebra — gauge

The product is the fourth product and its square is $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde P^{*}$, equivalent to $\overline{\tilde P}\tilde P$. The root set of $0$ is the subfamily $\{\overline{\tilde P}\tilde P=0\}$, a proper subfamily of the cone containing $e_t+i\vec e_x$: a proper part of the lightlike family. The reading is The Biquaternioid General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (GQS) over $\mathbb{C}$.

$\mathrm{SPA}$, the symmetric plain algebra — commutativity

The product is the Jordan product and its square is the plain square, so the root set of $0$ is the pure isotropic cone, element for element that of $\mathrm{GPA}$. The same displacements are square-zero, and they are zero divisors of the symmetrised block in addition. The reading is The Specificities of the Symmetric Plain Algebra.

$\mathrm{SPS}$, the symmetric plain sesqualgebra — positivity

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation and its square is $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde P^{*})e_t$, so the root set of $0$ is $\{0\}$ alone, for the same definiteness. The reading is Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone.

$\mathrm{SQA}$, the symmetric quaternionic algebra — coupling

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation and its square is $C(\tilde P)e_t$, so the root set of $0$ is the whole cone, as for $\mathrm{GQA}$. The same lightlike family is self-annihilating. The reading is The Biquaternioid Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra (SQA) over $\mathbb{C}$.

$\mathrm{SQS}$, the symmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — metric

The product is the symmetric part of the fourth product and its square is the coefficientwise real part of $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde P^{*}$; the equation is the same as for $\mathrm{GQS}$, so the root set of $0$ is the subfamily $\{\overline{\tilde P}\tilde P=0\}$, the same as for its general partner. The reading is The Biquaternioid Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (SQS) over $\mathbb{C}$.

$\mathrm{APA}$, the antisymmetric plain algebra — rotation

The product is the commutator $\vec P\times\vec Q$, alternating, so its square vanishes identically. The root set of $0$ is the whole space: every element is square-zero, an infinitesimal rotation. The reading is The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket.

$\mathrm{APS}$, the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra — phase

The product is the antisymmetrisation and its square is half the difference between the Hermitian square and its conjugate, so the equation is equivalent to $\tilde P\tilde P^{*}$ being central. The root set of $0$ is that subfamily; it lies outside the cone and contains the unit $e_t$, whose conjugate-bracket square vanishes. The reading is Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form.

$\mathrm{AQA}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra — obstruction

The product is the antisymmetrisation, alternating, so its square vanishes identically and the root set of $0$ is the whole space. The reading is The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket and The Biquaternioid Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra (AQA) over $\mathbb{C}$.

$\mathrm{AQS}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — polarisation

The product is the antisymmetric part of the fourth product and its square is half the difference between $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde P^{*}$ and its conjugate, so the equation is equivalent to $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde P^{*}$ being fixed by the coefficientwise conjugation. The root set of $0$ is that subfamily, which lies outside the cone and contains the unit $e_t$. The reading is The Cross Product of a Vector with Its Conjugate: the Antisymmetric Gauge Product and A Bracket Invisible on the Real Forms: the Complex Witness of the Jacobi Failure.

The Gap: a State Is Not a Lightlike Displacement

The mathematics companion states the gap between the two sets: a vanishing square is an annihilating factor, so the square-zero set without the origin lies in the zero-divisor set of every structure, but the converse fails for nine of the twelve structures. The two sets agree, origin included, exactly for the three structures whose square vanishes identically or is the central square: the natural product and the two alternating brackets.

The physical interpretation is the distinction between the two kinds of non-invertible element. A zero divisor is any operation that cannot be undone, and a square-zero element is one that cannot be undone because it is infinitesimal: it is a lightlike displacement. The plain product shows the gap in the sharpest way. Its zero-divisor set is the whole punctured cone, its square-zero set is the pure part alone, and the complement is the non-pure cone, the multiples of the projectors, which are the states. A state is therefore a zero divisor that is not square-zero: it cannot be composed, but it is not a lightlike displacement, and it is idempotent, $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$, rather than nilpotent, $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$. The two equations are the two extreme quadratic equations of the algebra, and the two objects they define are the two physical objects of the pair of companion articles: the state in Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$ and the lightlike displacement here. The owner of the physical locus of the zero divisors is Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form, and the reading of the cone as the massless locus is The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$.

The physical statement is that the set of the non-invertible operations is strictly larger than the set of the lightlike ones, and the difference is made of the states. In the probability band the gap is maximal in the other direction: the zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space and the square-zero set is the origin alone, so no element of that band is lightlike, although almost every element is non-invertible. The physical content is that a probability is non-invertible for the reason that it is a probability and not because it is lightlike.

The Fermionic Ladder: Nilpotency as Statistics

The nilpotents are the square-zero elements, and the corpus assigns them two physical jobs. The first is the lightlike displacement, read in the light cone articles. The second is exact and less expected: the square-zero class contains the fermionic ladder operators of a single mode. Separating the two jobs is the content of this section.

The lightlike displacement. The material pure nilpotents $Q_t=0$, $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$ are the lightlike directions, and the natural product squares every cone element to zero. The physical particle is the photon, and The Null Four-Momentum: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not fixes how far the identification goes. A nilpotent has a direction and no magnitude; its square vanishing is the algebra's statement that a null displacement is not accumulated, which is the vanishing of the interval of a null vector.

The fermionic ladder. The one-mode ladder of Fock Space and Creation/Annihilation Operators in Biquaternionic Form is the pair

$$ \vec\Upsilon_x=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y),\qquad \vec\Upsilon_y=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y), $$

and a direct computation gives

$$ \vec\Upsilon_x^{2}=\vec\Upsilon_y^{2}=0,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_x\vec\Upsilon_y=\tilde\Pi_+,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_y\vec\Upsilon_x=\tilde\Pi_-,\qquad \{\vec\Upsilon_x,\vec\Upsilon_y\}=e_t, $$

with $\tilde\Pi_+=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z)$ and $\tilde\Pi_-=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z)$ the vacuum and occupied projectors of The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent; the element $i\vec e_z$ is a root of $+1$, $(i\vec e_z)^2=e_t$, and the two are its associated idempotents. The creation and annihilation operators of a single fermionic mode are therefore nilpotents of the algebra itself, and the canonical anticommutator $\{\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger\}=e_t$ is the statement that a square-zero pair multiplies to the identity. Pauli exclusion is the vanishing square: $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ says that a second application of the same step gives nothing, which is exactly the algebraic form of the statement that a mode cannot be occupied twice. The vacuum is the product of the two steps, $\tilde\Pi_+=\vec\Upsilon_x\vec\Upsilon_y$, so the state and the ladder are not independent objects: the state is what the nilpotent pair creates. The Spin–Statistics Theorem in Biquaternionic Form fixes the anticommutator, and The Dirac Sea, Hole Theory and Antimatter in Biquaternionic Form gives the two-projector picture of the mode, which the pair $\tilde\Pi_+,\tilde\Pi_-$ is in the algebra.

Why the ladder is the nilpotent one. The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent records that the algebra contains no bosonic ladder: no pair $\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger\in\mathbb{B}$ can satisfy $[\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger]=e_t$, because a commutator has vanishing trace while $\mathrm{Tr}(e_t)=2$. The trace argument selects the anticommutator, hence the fermionic statistics, for this algebra's ladder, and the nilpotent class is the class that can carry it. The nilpotent article therefore belongs beside the idempotent article for a reason sharper than a shared position on the cone: the state is the idempotent and the ladder is the nilpotent, and the two are the two halves of one mode.

A nilpotent generates a truncated flow. A nilpotent has no higher powers, so its exponential truncates at first order: $\exp(t\tilde\Upsilon)=e_t+t\tilde\Upsilon$. A nilpotent is therefore an infinitesimal generator whose one-parameter group is a straight line rather than a circle, in contrast with a root of $-1$, whose exponential is a full rotation. This is the algebraic form of the distinction between a translation and a rotation, and it is why the nilpotent is read as a displacement: the flow it generates does not curve, and its square vanishes because two successive infinitesimal steps compose to a first-order object and no more.

Two homes for square zero. The square-zero elements of $\mathbb{B}$ are not the only square-zero objects of the framework. Grassmann Coherent States in Biquaternionic Form shows that the fermionic parity of a field is not an element of $\mathbb{B}$ at all and requires an odd extension $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathcal{G}$, whose odd generators are square-zero and anticommute. Those generators are odd, and every element of $\mathbb{B}$ is even, so the two square-zero classes are different objects: the nilpotents of $\mathbb{B}$ are even lightlike displacements and ladder steps, and the Grassmann generators are odd labels of a Fock space. The word "nilpotent" without the algebra is ambiguous, and the subject of this article is the even class.

The Odd Nilpotents: Cohomology, Supercharge and the Dirac Cone

The square-zero elements of $\mathbb{B}$ are even. The corpus's physics also uses square-zero objects that are odd, and those live in the extensions $\mathbb{B}\otimes\Lambda$ and $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathcal{G}$. The two classes share the equation $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ and nothing else, and the odd class is the engine of cohomology, of supersymmetry and of the topological invariants of Dirac matter.

A nilpotent differential needs an associative product. BRST Symmetry in Biquaternionic Form introduces a Grassmann-odd derivation $s$ with $s^{2}=0$, the BRST charge $\mathcal{Q}$ with $\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0$, and identifies the physical states with the cohomology $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\ker \mathcal{Q}/\mathrm{im}\,\mathcal{Q}$. The article's own point is where the nilpotency comes from: $s^{2}=0$ rests on the Jacobi identity of the gauge algebra, and in the framework that Jacobi identity is a consequence of the associativity of quaternion multiplication. The associativity of the parent product is therefore what makes a nilpotent differential available, and the cohomology is then the annihilator of the nilpotent quotiented by its image: the physical states are exactly the states a nilpotent operator does not reach, with the reachable ones removed. This is the annihilator reading of Annihilators in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$ sharpened by one more structure, and the three brackets of the corpus that fail the Jacobi identity — read in A Higher Structure for the Jacobi Failure — are precisely the ones that cannot carry such a differential. A bracket that does not close is a symmetry without a cohomology.

The supercharge and the Dirac operator. Supersymmetric Quantum Physics in the Biquaternion Framework uses the pair $\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}$ with

$$ \mathcal{Q}^{2}=0,\qquad (\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger})^{2}=0,\qquad \{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}\}=H, $$

and argues that the biquaternion first-order operator is the natural supercharge: the Dirac operator squares to the scalar d'Alembertian, $\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\Box$, exactly as the anticommutator of a supercharge is the Hamiltonian. The nilpotent $\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0$ is again an odd object, and the framework supplies the operator, the module and the grading while the odd coordinates are adjoined. The even nilpotents of this article and the odd nilpotents of the supersymmetric construction therefore sit on the two sides of one boundary: the even class is a lightlike displacement inside $\mathbb{B}$, and the odd class is a derivative whose vanishing square is what makes a cohomology exist.

The Dirac cone and the null direction. Dirac Matter: Graphene, Dirac Cones and Topological Insulators in Biquaternionic Form reads the massless low-energy excitations of graphene and of a topological-insulator surface as a two-component spinor with a linear dispersion, and the framework names the three objects involved: the spinor, the $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\vec p$ kinetic term and the mass term that opens the gap. The Dirac point is the momentum at which the dispersion is linear and the gap closes, and the masslessness of that point is the vanishing of the interval. The nilpotent of this article is the momentum element at that point: a lightlike momentum with $\tilde k\tilde k^{\natural}=0$, the algebraic form of the linear branch. The article's caution applies here too: the framework transcribes the low-energy equation, and the lattice, the Fermi velocity and the topological invariant are material data and not algebraic output.

The null points come in pairs. A null direction of the momentum symbol is a zero of the symbol, and on a lattice the zeros are counted by topology. Fermion Doubling and the Nielsen–Ninomiya Theorem in Biquaternionic Form states the count: the Fourier transform of the inverse propagator is a continuous vector field on the Brillouin zone, its isolated zeros are the particle species, and the index of the field at each zero is $\pm1$, the two signs being the two handednesses. Poincaré–Hopf makes the sum of the indices the Euler characteristic of the Brillouin zone, which for a four-torus is zero, so the null points cannot be removed one at a time: they appear in left–right pairs. The article's caution belongs to the reading — the biquaternion algebra is not a lattice and does not supply one — so the statement is about the discretised symbol, and what it says is that the square-zero set of a lattice symbol is topologically paired and not the property of a single isolated mode.

Nilpotency is why the Wick theorem is exact. The Wick Theorem in Biquaternionic Form observes that for a single fermionic mode the theorem is not an approximation or a statement about vacuum expectation values in a large space: because the mode operators are square-zero, $\vec\Upsilon_x^{2}=\vec\Upsilon_y^{2}=0$, every product of more than two mode operators collapses, so the four-point and six-point contractions reduce to products of the single two-point contraction with the fermionic signs. The identity is then an exact operator identity in a four-real-dimensional subalgebra, proved by exhibiting both sides. Nilpotency is therefore what makes the fermionic contraction terminate: the collapse of every product beyond the second is the algebraic reason the contractions are finite in number, and the one-mode graph expansion has no infinite series to resum.

The single statement. A vanishing square is the algebra's widest structural equation: the same equation that makes a lightlike displacement an infinitesimal makes a BRST charge a cohomological differential and a supercharge a supersymmetry generator. What differs is not the equation but the grading of the carrier. The even carrier is a displacement inside the algebra; the odd carrier is a derivative of an extension; and both are read by the square because the square is the one operation that tests whether a second application adds anything.

The hole is not a nilpotent, but the sea's inertness is. The Dirac Sea, Hole Theory and Antimatter in Biquaternionic Form draws the boundary of the nilpotent reading in the theory of the vacuum. In Dirac's hole theory the negative-energy states are all occupied, and a hole is the absence of one of them: its charge, momentum and energy follow by subtraction from the filled sea, and it is a positron. A hole is therefore not an element with a vanishing square; it is a state difference from the sea, and the article's conclusion is that the framework sits on the modern reinterpretation side, where the vacuum is empty and the negative-frequency modes are antiparticle creation operators, so that there is no sea at all — the filled sea survives only as a heuristic and as a real object in condensed matter, where an empty valence- band state is a hole. What the exclusion principle does supply is the inertness of the sea — the reason a positive-energy electron cannot descend into a filled negative-energy state — and that inertness is the algebra's nilpotency, since two applications of a mode operator vanish. The reading to keep is therefore sharp: nilpotency is what a mode operator does, not what a hole is.

The nilpotent mode is native, and the transformation to it is an identity. The Jordan–Wigner Transformation in Biquaternionic Form relates a spin-$\tfrac12$ chain to a chain of fermionic modes, and its framework finding is that at one site there is nothing to transform. The spin ladder and the fermionic mode are the same element of $\mathbb{B}$: the parents' operators $\tilde a=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y)$, $\tilde a^\dagger=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y)$ satisfy $\tilde a^{2}=0$ and $\{\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger\}=e_t$ inside the algebra, so the square-zero mode is not adjoined but found. The Jordan–Wigner string — the product of the other sites' fermion parities — has an empty product at one site, and the article states the consequence exactly: the transformation is the choice of a square root of the spin algebra, and the string is its branch cut. The reading is that nilpotency is the algebra's native one-site fact, and the spin–fermion map is the extension of that fact over sites; the string, and with it the grading that the map fails to preserve, appears only when a second site exists.

The eikonal avoids the cone. The WKB Approximation and the Hamilton–Jacobi Equation in Biquaternionic Form gives a sharp negative result, and it is worth recording because it bounds the nilpotent's reach. In the semiclassical limit the phase is a central element $\exp(iS/\hbar)$ and the momentum is the real quaternion $\nabla S=\sum_k(\partial/\partial Q_k\,S)\vec e_k$; the Hamilton–Jacobi equation is the biquaternion norm of that momentum equated to the kinetic energy, $\langle \nabla S\rangle_{\natural}=2m(E-V)$. The article's own statement is then exact: "the null (zero divisor) cone is not reached by the phase gradient, whose biquaternion norm is fixed by the energy defect and vanishes only where the classical particle stops". So the semiclassical momentum is never a nonzero nilpotent: its norm is pinned to the energy defect, and the only way it vanishes is the trivial one, $\nabla S=0$ at the turning point. The lightlike element is the eikonal's asymptotic boundary, not a value the approximation attains, which is the same distinction the corpus draws between the null direction and the null vector.

The ladder stops at two levels. The Qutrit Problem: Three-Level Systems Outside the Biquaternion Algebra turns the square-zero identity into a ceiling. Since $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, a nilpotent element has square zero, so the algebra's own raising operator cannot climb more than one step: the spectrum it generates has exactly two levels, and there is no unital three-dimensional module. Two further faces of the same fact are stated there: the identity splits into at most two orthogonal idempotents, so the algebra cannot host a three-outcome projective measurement, and the three-dimensional adjoint space carries an action of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ but not of the algebra, because the adjoint action is a derivation and not a multiplication. The sentence that belongs in this companion's ledger is the article's: the algebra has two levels, and the third is not inside it. A three-level ladder is therefore not a module of $\mathbb{B}$; a qutrit exists only in the tensor square $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$, whose four levels split three-plus-one, at the price of an extra level to be projected out. The reading is a boundary rather than a coincidence: nilpotency is not only the mechanism of the ladder, it is the reason the ladder has length two, and the same square-zero identity that creates the raising operator forbids a third rung.

The null direction makes the unit group infinite. Biquaternion Orders and Finite Groups of Units shows the same vanishing square doing arithmetic. For a null element $P$ (so $\langle P\rangle_{\natural}=0$, hence $P^2=0$), the element $e_t+P$ is a unit, with inverse $e_t-P$, and $(e_t+P)^n=e_t+nP$: a nilpotent direction generates an infinite cyclic subgroup of the unit group. The article's own reading is exact and is the one to keep: the real orders have finite unit groups, the complex order does not, and the difference is the light cone — the null direction is the nilpotent one, it lies on $c^2t^2=x^2+y^2+z^2$, and it generates a translation-like one-parameter group without bound, while the finite unit groups sit in the real sector, where the norm is definite and no null direction exists. So the zero of the norm is what makes a unit group infinite, and the algebraic statement of the light cone's presence is the infinite unit group of the complex order.

Nilpotency is the endpoint of a contraction. Squeezing Beyond the Biquaternion Algebra shows where the nilpotent comes from as a limit. A contraction rescales generators so that some commutators and some norms go to zero; for $E_2=\epsilon \vec e_y$, $E_3=\epsilon \vec e_z$ the article verifies numerically that $[E_2,E_3]=2\epsilon^2E_1$ and $\langle E_2\rangle_{\natural}=\epsilon^2e_t$ vanish together as $\epsilon\to0$ (at $\epsilon=1,0.1,0.01$ the values are $2,2\times10^{-2},2\times10^{-4}$ and $1,10^{-2},10^{-4}$). The limit is an algebra in which $E_2$ and $E_3$ are nilpotent and mutually annihilating, and the article states the mechanism exactly: "zero divisors are what survive when a quadratic structure is squeezed until its metric collapses", with the zero-divisor cone as the limiting locus of that degeneration. The reading is a third home for square zero, beside the lightlike displacement and the fermionic mode: the nilpotent is what an invertible element becomes when the norm degenerates, so the square-zero set is also the singular limit of a nondegenerate quadratic structure. The article's other boundary fixes what the algebra cannot absorb — q-deformations, octonionic doublings and dissipative completions lie outside $\mathbb{B}$, each for a definite structural reason — so the contraction limit is the one degeneration that stays inside.

Summary

A nilpotent of index two, or square-zero element, is a nonzero element whose square in the product at hand vanishes, and it is an element that kills itself: it lies in its own annihilator. Physically it is an infinitesimal object, the lightlike displacement, and the corpus writes it $\tilde\Upsilon$. The mathematics companion computes the square-zero sets of the twelve structures and orders them by the cone. The plain composition band has the pure isotropic cone of real dimension four, the interval band the whole cone, the probability band the origin alone and the gauge band a proper subfamily of the cone, while the two brackets have the whole space and the two antisymmetric sesquilinear structures have a subfamily outside the cone that contains the unit. Read physically, the order is the order of the bands by their lightlike content: none in the probability band, the pure ones in the composition band, all of them in the interval band, and no proper lightlike notion in the brackets. The square-zero set lies in the zero-divisor set of its structure and contains the cone, but the two sets agree for only three of the twelve structures, and the gap for the other nine is the distinction between the two kinds of non-invertible element. The plain product shows it: its zero divisors include the states, which are idempotent and not square-zero, and its square-zero elements are the pure lightlike displacements alone. A state is a zero divisor that is idempotent; a lightlike displacement is a zero divisor that is nilpotent; and the two equations $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ and $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ are the two extremes of the quadratic element theory of the algebra.

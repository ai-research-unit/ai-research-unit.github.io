The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model: One Equation, One Scale, One Content Statement

Introduction

One coupling. The coupling that returns the Standard Model is a single equation. It is the matching of hypercharge to the two abelian factors above it:

$$ \boxed{\;\frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}\quad\text{at }M_C\;} $$

One equation. It is enough to recover the Standard Model's three measured couplings. That is the claim of this article, and it is arithmetic on the group's own normalisations rather than a hope.

Three declarations. The equation is the first. The second is a scale, $M_C$, at which the equation is imposed. The third is a content statement: below $M_C$ the matter is exactly the Standard Model's, three generations with one doublet. An equation without a scale is not a statement, and a scale without content is not a prediction. The three are listed together because two of them alone prove nothing.

What "Returns the Standard Model" Means

One thing. It is worth separating from two things it does not mean.

1.1 A recovery, not a derivation. Below $M_C$ the group is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$, the fields are the Standard Model's, and the couplings are the measured ones. The recovery says: given the group above, the measured numbers below are mutually consistent at one scale, through one relation. It does not say why the numbers have the values they have.

1.2 It fixes no number by itself. The equation has two unknowns, $g_R$ and $g_{B-L}$, against one measured $g_Y$, and one further relation above supplies the second: the normalisation

$$ g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38\,g_4^2\quad\text{at }M_C , $$

which is a trace identity, $\operatorname{tr}(T^{15})^2=\tfrac12$, and not a dynamical statement. With that identity in place the system closes, and what comes out is a value for $g_R$.

1.3 One output. The output is $g_R$, and The One Scale shows that the output is a function of the scale rather than a number the theory fixes. A value that moves with a choice must not be quoted as though it were a prediction.

The Arithmetic, Which Is Exact

Three steps. The third is the number every article on the electroweak sector quotes.

2.1 At the threshold. The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ coupling is one, and the $B-L$ field is the fifteenth Cartan:

$$ g_3=g_4,\qquad g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38g_4^2 . $$

2.2 At the same threshold, the second step. Hypercharge is the combination $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, so its field is a mixture and its coupling is the harmonic sum, with the coefficient $\tfrac12$ squared:

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2} . $$

2.3 Composed. Put $g_R=g_4$ and $g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38g_4^2$ into the sum:

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_4^2}+\frac{1}{4\cdot\tfrac38g_4^2} =\frac{1}{g_4^2}+\frac{2}{3g_4^2}=\frac{5}{3g_4^2} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; g_Y^2=\tfrac35g_4^2 . $$

The three denominators are $1$, $1$ and $\tfrac23$, and their sum $\tfrac83$ is why the answer is $\tfrac38$. Every appearance of $\tfrac38$ in this subject traces back to that addition. The photon's coupling is then the same sum with $g_L$ in place of $g_R$:

$$ \frac{1}{e^2}=\frac{1}{g_L^2}+\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2} =\frac{1}{g_4^2}\left(1+1+\frac23\right)=\frac{8}{3g_4^2} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; e^2=\tfrac38g_4^2 , $$

so that

$$ \sin^2\theta_W(M_{U})=\frac{e^2}{g_L^2}=\frac38 . $$

2.4 The same sum from generators. Write $Y=q\,T^{15}+t\,T_{3R}$ and read the coefficients off the charges. The expansion gives the identical sum, and it must: the first route uses the normalisation of a generator, the second uses its expansion, and a framework in which they disagreed would have a wrong normalisation somewhere.

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{q^2}{g_4^2}+\frac{1}{g_R^2} =\frac{2/3}{g_4^2}+\frac{1}{g_4^2}=\frac{5}{3g_4^2} . $$

The number $\tfrac38$ is not a measurement and not a guess. It is the trace normalisation of one Cartan generator, added to one.

The One Scale

The scale is free. The equation is imposed at a scale, and that single sentence is the limitation of the recovery; it is stated before the numbers.

3.1 Bounded below, unbounded above. $K_L\to\mu e$ limits the leptoquark mass, and the leptoquark mass is set by the same vacuum that sets $M_X$:

$$ M_X^2=16\,g_4^2\,v_C^2 , $$

one mass and one vacuum, and no second ratio to fix. The $K_L\to\mu e$ bound is a bound on $M_X$, and $M_X$ is the sextet's mass: no intermediate particle enters the translation of the bound, and none has to.

3.2 The output moves. Solving the matching equation at a chosen pair of scales gives

$$ \frac{1}{g_R^2}=\frac{1}{g_Y^2(M_C)}-\frac{1}{4\,g_{B-L}^2(M_C)}, $$

and the solution exists only while the bracket is positive. Two consequences follow at once:

  • $g_R$ comes out below $g_L$, between $0.62$ and $0.73$ of it over the admissible region, so the fourth colour's right-handed sector is weaker than its left-handed one at the threshold.
  • The ratio $M_X/M_{W_R}$ lies between $2.2$ and $3.1$, not at $\sqrt2$, and a leptoquark floor of $3.2\times10^{-5}$ J therefore corresponds to a $W_R$ floor of $1.1$ to $1.4\times10^{-5}$ J rather than to $160$.

3.3 What it confirms. The relation reproduces $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$, $\alpha_s$ and $\sin^2\theta_W$ at $M_Z$ exactly and for any admissible pair of scales, because each is built from those three numbers at $M_Z$ and run up. Those three are data at $M_Z$ — $\alpha^{-1}(M_Z)=127.95$ and $\sin^2\theta_W=0.23122$ among them — and the relation is imposed on top of them. A statement that reproduces its own inputs is a consistency condition, and a consistency condition confirms nothing.

The One Number a Coupling Produces

One contact with a measurement. Above the $W_R$ floor, the model's single contact with data is $K_L\to\mu e$. The operator is $g_4^2/(2M_X^2)$ times $U_{de}U_{s\imath}^{*}$ times a hadronic matrix element, the rate scales as $M_X^{-4}$, and the coupling is fixed by the first matching relation $g_3=g_4$ at $M_C$:

$$ g_4^2=4\pi\,\alpha_s(M_X),\qquad \Gamma(K_L\to\mu e)=\frac{g_4^{4}\,\lvert U_{de}U_{s\imath}\rvert^{2}f_K^{2}m_\mu^{2}m_K}{128\pi\,M_X^{4}} . $$

Three rows. The shape is sharp.

$M_X$ $g_4^2$ $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)$ against $4.7\times10^{-12}$
$3.2\times10^{-5}$ J $0.736$ $8.9\times10^{-12}$ excluded
$3.73\times10^{-5}$ J $0.730$ $4.7\times10^{-12}$ at the limit
$4.81\times10^{-5}$ J $0.718$ $1.7\times10^{-12}$ allowed, margin $2.8$

The table takes $|U_{de}U_{s\imath}|=1$, the largest value; a smaller entry multiplies every rate by its fourth power and lowers the bound by its square root, and the identity branch gives zero at every mass. The bound is $M_X\gtrsim3.69\times10^{-5}$ J for that largest value and under that assumption, and it is a band of a factor of two rather than an order of magnitude, because the rate carries the fourth power of the mass. This is the only number in the model that a search can contradict, and it is a coupling's number: the rate is proportional to $g_4^4$, and the bound follows only once $g_4$ is replaced by a measured $\alpha_s$. The channel, its quantum numbers, its tree-level character and the reason $K_S$ does not do the same are structural and are in Lepton Flavour in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model; the substitution that turns the channel into a mass bound is what this section adds.

The Boundary Condition, and Why It Fails

The equal-coupling case. It is $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at one scale, and it is the only case in which $\tfrac38$ is a prediction rather than a condition. Written as a boundary condition it is one sentence, and written as a check it is two numbers.

4.1 The two ends. Impose $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at one scale and ask which scale each half of the condition prefers:

condition the scale where it holds what is wrong there
$\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ $1.63\times10^{3}$ J $\alpha_3^{-1}=36.8$ against $\alpha_L^{-1}=42.4$, $13\%$ short
$g_4=g_L$ $1.57\times10^{7}$ J $\sin^2\theta_W=0.4365$ against $\tfrac38$, $16\%$ high

The two scales are four decades apart, and no single scale is both. That is the failure: one boundary value cannot satisfy two conditions that hold at different energies.

4.2 Why it fails. The condition ties a coupling that runs at $c_4=\tfrac{32}{3}$ to one that runs at $c_2=\tfrac{19}{6}$, and their difference accumulates. The failure is not a mistake in the arithmetic; it is the statement that the equality is not preserved by the matter content between the thresholds.

4.3 What the failure does not touch. It destroys the prediction $\tfrac38$ and it does not touch the group, the sixteen, the charges, the anomalies, the boson count or the leptoquark content. Those are algebraic and they do not depend on any coupling value.

The Extra-Scale Variant, and Why It Does Not Rescue It

One repair. It does not work.

5.1 The proposal. Add a scale above the transition: impose $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at $M_{U}>M_C$ and change nothing below. Two conditions on two scales is a curve, not a point, so the variant is not empty. What it does not do is close the gap.

5.2 The obstruction. Above $M_C$ the content is parity-symmetric, so every $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ multiplet has an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of the same dimension:

$$ c_{2L}=c_{2R}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_{U})}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_{U})} =\frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_C)}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_C)} . $$

Equal coefficients transport the gap unchanged, so the condition at $M_{U}$ is a condition at $M_C$.

$$ g_L(M_{U})=g_R(M_{U})\iff g_L(M_C)=g_R(M_C) . $$

5.3 What that means in words. The extra scale moves the meeting of $g_4$ with $g_L$, because $c_4\ne c_2$. It does nothing to the meeting of $g_R$ with $g_L$, because parity makes their running identical. The extra running fixes exactly the half that was not the problem.

5.4 What closing the gap would take. Only breaking parity above $M_C$ lets the gap run, and the gap is $44.6$ in inverse-coupling units against $27$ e-folds to the colour meeting:

$$ \Delta c_2\simeq2\pi\frac{44.6}{27}\simeq10 . $$

A single Weyl doublet moves $c_2$ by $\tfrac13$, so this asks for about thirty more left doublets than right doublets. That is not a threshold correction. It is a different theory.

The Yukawa Sector: The Same Question

The connection couplings are not the only ones that carry a scale. The Yukawa sector is where the same question bites next, and there it is answered by counting.

6.1 Two relations. Two relations, and both were written with a scale attached:

$$ m_d=m_e,\qquad m_u=m_{\jmath}\qquad\text{at }M_{U} . $$

Neither survives as a statement about this model's couplings. They are the one-bidoublet boundary conditions, and their status is the interesting part: the first is falsified by data, the second is falsified by data by seven orders of magnitude.

6.2 What survives. One bidoublet carries one Yukawa matrix $\digamma_{\mathrm{yuk}}$ and applies it to both the down-type quarks and the charged leptons:

$$ \mathcal L_Y=\digamma_{\mathrm{yuk}}\,\bar{\Psi}_L H\,\Psi_R+\text{h.c.} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M_d\propto M_e , $$

and that is a statement about the number of matrices, not about any value. One matrix serving two sectors forces the two mass matrices to be proportional, and proportional matrices have proportional spectra. That is counting, and it is scale-free: it holds at every scale, in every scheme, with any normalisation.

6.3 The numbers. The proportionality is compared against the measured ratios, which are data:

ratio measured against a common proportionality
$m_d/m_e$ $9.1$ fails by a factor $9$
$m_s/m_\mu$ $0.88$ holds to $12\%$
$m_b/m_\tau$ $2.35$ at $m_b$ converges to unity once the running from $M_{U}$ to $m_b$ is included
$m_u/m_{\jmath}$ about $4\times10^{7}$ fails outright

The last column is where the scale enters, and it enters twice: once in the ratio "at $m_b$", and once in the phrase "once the running is included".

6.4 What the counting leaves standing. The argument that the minimal scalar sector is insufficient does not depend on any of these numbers. One bidoublet gives one matrix for two sectors; the data do not show proportional spectra; therefore the sector needs a second matrix. The repair is a second bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, or the colour-singlet component of a $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$. What the arithmetic of The Yukawa Sector: The Same Question adds is the size of the miss, not the existence of it.

6.5 The quartic. The scalar potential's quartic is a coupling with a value at a scale and a running like any other, and it belongs in the same place as these.

What the Relation Does and Does Not Fix

What it is. A single relation, derived twice, that ties the hypercharge coupling at $M_C$ to the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ coupling at the same scale; a scale at which the relation is imposed; a content statement below the scale; and a statement of what the relation does not fix.

What it is not. It is not a unification. The three couplings are not required to be equal anywhere, and the model's own position is that they are not. It is not a prediction of $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$, $\alpha_s$ or $\sin^2\theta_W$: those are measured at $M_Z$ and the relation is imposed on top of them. And it is not a claim that the model is confirmed by the agreement it manufactures.

The summary sentence. The relation reproduces the three measured couplings for any admissible pair of scales, by adjusting $g_R$, and it predicts none of them.

Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

The recovery is not a derivation: it shows that the measured numbers below are consistent at one scale, and it does not explain their values (What "Returns the Standard Model" Means).

The output is not a prediction: $g_R$ moves with the chosen scales, between $0.62$ and $0.73$ of $g_L$ (The One Scale).

$\tfrac38$ is not a prediction of the unified case: it holds at $1.63\times10^{3}$ J, where colour is $13\%$ short (The Boundary Condition, and Why It Fails).

The failure of the unified case is not arithmetic: the equality is not preserved by the matter content between $M_Z$ and $M_C$ (The Boundary Condition, and Why It Fails).

The extra-scale variant is not a repair: parity makes the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ coefficients equal, so the extra scale cannot move the gap (The Extra-Scale Variant, and Why It Does Not Rescue It).

The scalar sector's defect is not a numerical mismatch: it is one matrix serving two sectors, which is counting and needs no scale (The Yukawa Sector: The Same Question).

Summary

The Standard Model is recovered from the fourth colour by one equation, $\frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}$ at $M_C$, together with one scale and one statement of content below it.

The arithmetic is exact and short. The denominators are $1$, $1$ and $\tfrac23$; they sum to $\tfrac83$; the angle at the threshold is $\tfrac38$; and the same sum read from the generator algebra gives the identical result.

What it does not fix is stated in the same place. The scale is free above a leptoquark floor, $g_R$ is an output rather than a prediction, the mass ratio is $\sqrt2\,g_4/g_R$ rather than $\sqrt2$, and a value of $g_R$ that moves with the scale cannot be tested.

The unified case fails, and it fails as arithmetic. $\tfrac38$ is exact where colour is $13\%$ short, and colour is exact where the angle is $16\%$ high. The two conditions hold four decades apart.

The three-scale repair does not rescue it, for a reason that is a symmetry and not a number. Parity makes the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ coefficients equal, so the extra scale moves the meeting of colour and leaves the meeting of the two chiral factors where it was.

The Yukawa sector answers the same question by counting. The one-bidoublet conditions $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_{\jmath}$ are scale-bearing and falsified; the argument they supported survives without them, because one matrix for two sectors is a statement about counting, and counting needs no scale.

The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model: One Equation, One Scale, One Content Statement

Introduction

Every coupling statement in this category is in this article, and nowhere else. That is a rule of the corpus, not a remark about this page. The rest of the particle category states the group, the carrier, the matter content, the charges, the anomalies, the boson count and the geometry. It states no coupling value, no boundary condition at a scale, no running, no matching, and no ratio called exact. Those live here, in one flagged place, so that a reader who wants the algebra is never handed a number and a reader who wants the number knows where the corpus keeps it.

The coupling that returns the Standard Model is a single equation. It is the matching of hypercharge to the two abelian factors above it:

$$ \boxed{\;\frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}\quad\text{at }M_R\;} $$

One equation, and it is enough to recover the Standard Model's three measured couplings. That is the claim of this article, and it is arithmetic rather than a hope.

Three declarations are needed, not one. The equation is the first. The second is a scale, $M_R$, at which the equation is imposed. The third is a content statement: below $M_R$ the matter is exactly the Standard Model's, three generations with one doublet. An equation without a scale is not a statement, and a scale without content is not a prediction. The three are listed together because two of them alone prove nothing.

1. What "Returns the Standard Model" Means

It means one thing, and it is worth separating from two things it does not mean.

1.1 It is a recovery, not a derivation. Below $M_R$ the group is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$, the fields are the Standard Model's, and the couplings are the measured ones. The recovery says: given the group above, the measured numbers below are mutually consistent at one scale, through one relation. It does not say why the numbers have the values they have.

1.2 It fixes no number by itself. The equation has two unknowns, $g_R$ and $g_{B-L}$, against one measured $g_Y$, and one further relation above supplies the second: the normalisation

$$ g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38\,g_4^2\quad\text{at }M_C , $$

which is a trace identity, $\operatorname{tr}(T^{15})^2=\tfrac12$, and not a dynamical statement. With that identity in place the system closes, and what comes out is a value for $g_R$.

1.3 It is therefore a consistency statement with one output. The output is $g_R$, and §3 shows that the output is a function of the scale rather than a number the theory fixes. That is the honest description, and it is the reason the coupling is kept in one place: a value that moves with a choice must not be quoted as though it were a prediction.

2. The Arithmetic, Which Is Exact

Three steps, each one an identity, and the third is the number every article on the electroweak sector quotes.

2.1 At the first threshold. The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ coupling is one, and the $B-L$ field is the fifteenth Cartan:

$$ g_3=g_4,\qquad g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38g_4^2 . $$

2.2 At the second threshold. Hypercharge is the combination $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, so its field is a mixture and its coupling is the harmonic sum, with the coefficient $\tfrac12$ squared:

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2} . $$

2.3 Composed, and the number that follows. Put $g_R=g_4$ and $g_{B-L}^2=\tfrac38g_4^2$ into the sum:

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_4^2}+\frac{1}{4\cdot\tfrac38g_4^2} =\frac{1}{g_4^2}+\frac{2}{3g_4^2}=\frac{5}{3g_4^2} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; g_Y^2=\tfrac35g_4^2 . $$

The three denominators are $1$, $1$ and $\tfrac23$, and their sum $\tfrac83$ is why the answer is $\tfrac38$. Every appearance of $\tfrac38$ in this subject traces back to that addition. The photon's coupling is then the same sum with $g_L$ in place of $g_R$.

$$ \frac{1}{e^2}=\frac{1}{g_L^2}+\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2} =\frac{1}{g_4^2}\left(1+1+\frac23\right)=\frac{8}{3g_4^2} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; e^2=\tfrac38g_4^2 , $$

so that

$$ \sin^2\theta_W(M_{U})=\frac{e^2}{g_L^2}=\frac38 . $$

2.4 The same composition from the generator algebra, as a check. Write $Y=q\,T^{15}+t\,T_{3R}$ and read the coefficients off the charges. The expansion gives the identical sum, and it must: the first route uses the normalisation of a generator, the second uses its expansion, and a framework in which they disagreed would have a wrong normalisation somewhere.

$$ \frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{q^2}{g_4^2}+\frac{1}{g_R^2} =\frac{2/3}{g_4^2}+\frac{1}{g_4^2}=\frac{5}{3g_4^2} . $$

That check is the whole of the corpus's claim in the gauge sector, and it is the reason this section exists. The number $\tfrac38$ is not a measurement and not a guess. It is the trace normalisation of one Cartan generator, added to one.

3. The One Scale, and What It Costs

The equation is imposed at a scale, and the scale is free. That single sentence is the price of the recovery, and it must be stated before the numbers, not after them.

3.1 The scale is bounded from below by one decay and from above by nothing. $K_L\to\mu e$ limits the leptoquark mass, and the leptoquark mass is set by the same vacuum that sets $M_X$:

$$ M_X^2=\tfrac12\,g_4^2\,v_R^2,\qquad M_{W_R}^2=\tfrac14\,g_R^2\,v_R^2, $$

so that

$$ \frac{M_X}{M_{W_R}}=\sqrt2\,\frac{g_4}{g_R} . $$

The $\sqrt2$ is structural and survives every scale. The factor $g_4/g_R$ is not one in general, and that is the first place where the recovery shows its cost: a ratio that the group fixes at $\sqrt2$ is multiplied by a ratio the vacuum does not fix.

3.2 The output is a value of $g_R$, and it moves. Solving the matching equation at a chosen pair of scales gives

$$ \frac{1}{g_R^2}=\frac{1}{g_Y^2(M_R)}-\frac{1}{4\,g_{B-L}^2(M_R)}, $$

and the solution exists only while the bracket is positive. Two consequences follow at once, and both belong to this section rather than to the articles that used to carry them:

  • $g_R$ comes out below $g_L$, between $0.62$ and $0.73$ of it over the admissible region, so the fourth colour's right-handed sector is weaker than its left-handed one at the threshold.
  • The ratio $M_X/M_{W_R}$ lies between $2.2$ and $3.1$, not at $\sqrt2$, and a leptoquark floor of $2\times10^{2}$ TeV therefore corresponds to a $W_R$ floor of $70$ to $90$ TeV rather than to $160$.

3.3 The cost, stated as one sentence. The recovery reproduces $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$, $\alpha_s$ and $\sin^2\theta_W$ at $M_Z$ exactly and for any admissible pair of scales, because each is built from those three numbers at $M_Z$ and run up. A statement that reproduces its own inputs is a consistency condition, and a consistency condition confirms nothing.

3.4 The one bound that a coupling produces, and the one place it bites

Above the $W_R$ floor, the model's single contact with a measurement is $K_L\to\mu e$. The operator is $g_4^2/(2M_X^2)$ times a hadronic matrix element, the rate scales as $M_X^{-4}$, and the coupling is fixed by the first matching relation $g_3=g_4$ at $M_C$:

$$ g_4^2=4\pi\,\alpha_s(M_X),\qquad \Gamma(K_L\to\mu e)=\frac{g_4^{4}f_K^{2}m_\mu^{2}m_K}{128\pi\,M_X^{4}} . $$

Three rows are enough to show the shape, and the shape is sharp.

$M_X$ $g_4^2$ $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)$ against $4.7\times10^{-12}$
$200$ TeV $0.736$ $8.9\times10^{-12}$ excluded
$233$ TeV $0.730$ $4.7\times10^{-12}$ at the limit
$300$ TeV $0.718$ $1.7\times10^{-12}$ allowed, margin $2.8$

The bound is $M_X\gtrsim2.3\times10^{2}$ TeV, and it is a band of a factor of two rather than an order of magnitude, because the rate carries the fourth power of the mass. This is the only number in the model that a search can contradict, and it is a coupling's number: the rate is proportional to $g_4^4$, and the bound follows only once $g_4$ is replaced by a measured $\alpha_s$.

Why it is here and not in the article on the channel. The channel, its quantum numbers, its tree-level character and the reason $K_S$ does not do the same are all structural, and they stayed in Charged Lepton Flavour Violation. The substitution that turns the channel into a mass bound is a coupling statement, and it is in this article.

4. The Boundary Condition, and Why It Fails

The equal-coupling case is $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at one scale, and the reason it is discussed here is that it is the only case in which $\tfrac38$ is a prediction rather than a condition. Written as a boundary condition it is one sentence, and written as a check it is two numbers.

4.1 The condition, and the two ends it is read from. Impose $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at one scale and ask which scale each half of the condition prefers:

condition the scale where it holds what is wrong there
$\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ $1.02\times10^{13}$ GeV $\alpha_3^{-1}=36.8$ against $\alpha_L^{-1}=42.4$, $13\%$ short
$g_4=g_L$ $9.79\times10^{16}$ GeV $\sin^2\theta_W=0.4365$ against $\tfrac38$, $16\%$ high

The two scales are four decades apart, and no single scale is both. That is the failure in one line: one boundary value cannot satisfy two conditions that hold at different energies.

4.2 Why it fails, in one sentence, and why the sentence matters. The condition ties a coupling that runs at $c_4=\tfrac{32}{3}$ to one that runs at $c_2=\tfrac{19}{6}$, and their difference accumulates. The failure is not a mistake in the arithmetic; it is the statement that the equality is not preserved by the matter content between the thresholds.

4.3 What the failure does and does not touch. It destroys the prediction $\tfrac38$ and it does not touch the group, the sixteen, the charges, the anomalies, the boson count or the leptoquark content. Those are algebraic and they are stated in the other articles of the category, which is why those articles carry no coupling at all.

5. The Three-Scale Variant, and Why It Does Not Rescue It

One repair is worth stating here, because it is the repair a reader will propose.

5.1 The proposal. Add a scale: impose $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at $M_{U}>M_C>M_R$ and change nothing below. Two conditions on three scales is a curve, not a point, so the variant is not empty. The question is what a curve is worth.

5.2 The obstruction, and it is a symmetry rather than a number. Above $M_R$ the content is parity-symmetric, so every $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ multiplet has an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ partner of the same dimension:

$$ c_{2L}=c_{2R}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_{U})}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_{U})} =\frac{1}{\alpha_L(M_R)}-\frac{1}{\alpha_R(M_R)} . $$

Equal coefficients transport the gap unchanged, so the condition at $M_{U}$ is a condition at $M_R$.

$$ g_L(M_{U})=g_R(M_{U})\iff g_L(M_R)=g_R(M_R) . $$

5.3 What that means in words. The third scale moves the meeting of $g_4$ with $g_L$, because $c_4\ne c_2$. It does nothing to the meeting of $g_R$ with $g_L$, because parity makes their running identical. A reader who expects the extra running to fix the whole triple will find that it fixes exactly the half that was not the problem.

5.4 What closing the gap would cost. Only breaking parity above $M_R$ lets the gap run, and the gap is $44.6$ in inverse-coupling units against $27$ e-folds to the colour meeting:

$$ \Delta c_2\simeq2\pi\frac{44.6}{27}\simeq10 . $$

A single Weyl doublet moves $c_2$ by $\tfrac13$, so this asks for about thirty more left doublets than right doublets. That is not a threshold correction. It is a different theory.

6. The Yukawa Sector: The Same Test, Applied

The gauge couplings are not the only ones that carry a scale, and the Yukawa sector is where the rule bites next. The test is the same one, and it is mechanical.

6.1 The test. Can the sentence be written without naming a scale? If not, it is a scale-bearing coupling claim, and it belongs here. A coupling symbol in a Lagrangian passes the test. A relation between coupling values fails it, because a value without a scale is not a value.

6.2 What fails the test in the scalar sector. Two statements, and both were written with a scale attached:

$$ m_d=m_e,\qquad m_u=m_\nu\qquad\text{at }M_{U} . $$

Neither survives as a statement about this model's couplings, and both are therefore here. They are the one-bidoublet boundary conditions, and their status is the interesting part: the first is falsified by data, the second is falsified by data by seven orders of magnitude.

6.3 What passes the test, and it is the whole argument. One bidoublet carries one Yukawa matrix $\lambda$ and applies it to both the down-type quarks and the charged leptons:

$$ \mathcal L_Y=\lambda\,\bar{\Psi}_L H\,\Psi_R+\text{h.c.} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M_d\propto M_e , $$

and that is a statement about the number of matrices, not about any value. One matrix serving two sectors forces the two mass matrices to be proportional, and proportional matrices have proportional spectra. That is counting, and it is scale-free: it holds at every scale, in every scheme, with any normalisation.

6.4 The numbers, with their status. The proportionality is compared against the measured ratios, which are data:

ratio measured against a common proportionality
$m_d/m_e$ $9.1$ fails by a factor $9$
$m_s/m_\mu$ $0.88$ holds to $12\%$
$m_b/m_\tau$ $2.35$ at $m_b$ converges to unity once the running from $M_{U}$ to $m_b$ is included
$m_u/m_\nu$ about $4\times10^{7}$ fails outright

The last column is where the scale enters, and it enters twice: once in the ratio "at $m_b$", and once in the phrase "once the running is included". Both are coupling statements, and both are here.

6.5 What the purge leaves standing, and why it matters. The argument that the minimal scalar sector is insufficient survives intact, because it was never a coupling argument. One bidoublet gives one matrix for two sectors; the data do not show proportional spectra; therefore the sector needs a second matrix. The repair is a second bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, or the colour-singlet component of a $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$.

So the open multiplet list of the model rests on a counting argument, and the counting argument is untouched by this migration. What was removed is the arithmetic that made the failure look like a number rather than like a mismatch of ranks.

6.6 The quartic, recorded for completeness. The scalar potential's quartic is a coupling with a value at a scale and a running like any other. No article in the category quotes its value, and none quotes its one-loop coefficient, so nothing had to move. When one is quoted, it comes here.

8. What This Section Is, and What It Is Not

The flagged section, in one paragraph. This is the only place in the particle category where a coupling is written as a number, a condition at a scale, a running, a matching or an exact ratio. The section is flagged for that reason, and the flag is not modesty: it is a boundary in the text.

What it is. A single relation, derived twice, that ties the hypercharge coupling at $M_R$ to the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ coupling at $M_C$; a scale at which the relation is imposed; a content statement below the scale; and an honest account of what the relation does not fix.

What it is not. It is not a unification. The three couplings are not required to be equal anywhere, and the model's own position is that they are not. It is not a prediction of $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$, $\alpha_s$ or $\sin^2\theta_W$: those are measured at $M_Z$ and the relation is imposed on top of them. And it is not a claim that the model is confirmed by the agreement it manufactures.

The sentence that must be written after every number in this article. The relation reproduces the three measured couplings for any admissible pair of scales, by adjusting $g_R$, and it predicts none of them.

8.1 The two boundaries of the rule, stated so that they can be enforced

First boundary: measured Standard Model numbers are data, not coupling claims. When an article on the Standard Model sector writes $\alpha^{-1}(M_Z)=127.95$ or $\sin^2\theta_W=0.23122$ and uses them to compute a vacuum expectation value, it is quoting an input. The rule does not touch those numbers, because they are neither a value this model predicts nor a value it fixes. They are the data against which §3.3 says the recovery confirms nothing.

Second boundary: this article governs gauge couplings, and the other couplings have their own homes. Yukawa couplings, the quartic of the scalar potential, and the mixing angles of the flavour sector appear in their own articles, with their own defects recorded there. The same discipline applies to them when they are next revised: one place, one statement, no value carried in an article whose subject is not a value. They are not moved here, because this article is about the fourth colour's gauge sector and would become unreadable if it also held the flavour sector.

Where the boundary is written down. These two boundaries were prose until the migration ended. They are now a register, with every article in the category sorted into the three categories and the test stated: The Data Boundary. A permission that is not written down is a permission nobody can check.

What the two boundaries leave whole. Every article in the category that states a group, a carrier, a multiplet list, a charge, an anomaly identity, a boson count, a dimension budget or a diagram. That is almost all of them, and none of them lost a claim in this migration.

Summary

The Standard Model is recovered from the fourth colour by one equation, $\frac{1}{g_Y^2}=\frac{1}{g_R^2}+\frac{1}{4g_{B-L}^2}$ at $M_R$, together with one scale and one statement of content below it.

The arithmetic is exact and short. The denominators are $1$, $1$ and $\tfrac23$; they sum to $\tfrac83$; the angle at the threshold is $\tfrac38$; and the same sum read from the generator algebra gives the identical result.

What it costs is stated in the same place. The scale is free above a leptoquark floor, $g_R$ is an output rather than a prediction, the mass ratio is $\sqrt2\,g_4/g_R$ rather than $\sqrt2$, and a value of $g_R$ that moves with the scale cannot be tested.

The unified case fails, and it fails as arithmetic. $\tfrac38$ is exact where colour is $13\%$ short, and colour is exact where the angle is $16\%$ high. The two conditions hold four decades apart.

The three-scale repair does not rescue it, for a reason that is a symmetry and not a number. Parity makes the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ coefficients equal, so the extra scale moves the meeting of colour and leaves the meeting of the two chiral factors where it was.

The Yukawa sector passes the same test. The one-bidoublet conditions $m_d=m_e$ and $m_u=m_\nu$ were written with a scale attached, so they are here; the argument they supported survives without them, because one matrix for two sectors is a statement about counting, and counting needs no scale.

And the boundary of the text is the point of the article. Every coupling statement in this category is here. The other articles state the group, the carrier, the content, the charges, the anomalies, the boson count and the geometry, and they state no coupling.

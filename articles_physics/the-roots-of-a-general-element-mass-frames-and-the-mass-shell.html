The Roots of a General Element: Mass, Frames and the Mass Shell

Introduction

The root problem of the biquaternion algebra asks for the elements that square to a given element. The two companion articles answer it for the central values $0$ and $+1$. A general radicand $\tilde Q$ is the question of this article,

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde\Xi^2=\tilde Q\;}. $$

Notation, once. The imaginary unit is the central $i$ of $\mathbb{B}$, with $i^2=-1$; every double $i$ is reduced before a formula is read. The star is the Hermitian conjugation, $\tilde\Xi^{*}=\bar\Xi_t e_t-\bar{\vec\Xi}$, and the natural conjugation is $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}=\Xi_t e_t-\vec\Xi$. The two-sided cells act on a field $\psi$ by $L_aR_b\psi=a\psi b$, with $L_a\psi=a\psi$ and $R_b\psi=\psi b$; the chirality cell is $\Gamma=-iR_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, and the Dirac adjoint is $\psi^*iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$. The gamma names of the cells are collected in The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation and are not used before it. For a radicand with vector part $\vec Q$, write $B^2=(\vec Q,\vec Q)$.

The answer depends on the product, and the first job of the article is to keep the products apart. The plain square $\tilde\Xi^2$ has a finite root set for a non-scalar radicand, generically four elements, falling to two or to none. The natural square $\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{\natural}=\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\natural}e_t$ has the level set of the interval as its root set, and on the material sector that level set is the mass shell. The Hermitian square $\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$ produces a positive semidefinite value; the eigenvalues of the mass operator are the mass-squareds of a doublet. The three squares are three root problems, not one.

Two first-order equations are kept apart throughout. The gradient equation is one-sided: every derivative acts by left multiplication, and it carries the mass shell. The cell equation is its two-sided representative on the Dirac module, and it carries the chirality and the condensate.

The article interprets five physical consequences. The mass shell is a propagator pole; the mass can be a field; the mass can be generated by a condensate; the root can be a geometric map; and the roots are frames. Each interpretation is labelled, and each carries its boundary.

The article owns no theorem. It states the algebraic classification, with its source, and interprets it. The proofs, the case distinctions and the examples are in the mathematics article.

The Root Problem by Band

The Plain Square

Write the radicand and the root in scalar and vector parts,

$$ \tilde Q=Q_te_t+\vec Q, \qquad \tilde\Xi=\Xi_te_t+\vec\Xi . $$

The plain square is

$$ \tilde\Xi^2=\bigl(\Xi_t^2-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)\bigr)e_t+2\Xi_t\vec\Xi . $$

For a radicand with $\vec Q\neq0$ a root has $\Xi_t\neq0$ and $\vec\Xi=\vec Q/(2\Xi_t)$, and $x=\Xi_t^2$ solves the reduced quadratic

$$ 4x^2-4Q_tx-(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0 , $$

whose discriminant is $16n$, with $n=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ the natural norm of the radicand. The roots come in opposite pairs $\pm\tilde\Xi$. There are four roots when $(\vec Q,\vec Q)\neq0$ and $n\neq0$. There are two when the discriminant vanishes, $n=0$ with $Q_t\neq0$, and two in the Jordan row, $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$ with $Q_t\neq0$. There are none for a nonzero nilpotent, $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$ and $Q_t=0$. A complex scalar radicand $\tilde Q=Q_te_t$ has the two scalar roots $\pm\sqrt{Q_t}e_t$, joined by a four-real-parameter family of pure roots with $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=-Q_t$.

That count is the one given in Biquaternion Elementary Functions, §The Roots. The four plain roots are the four independent sign choices of the two eigenvalue square roots. With $\tilde Q=\lambda_+\tilde\Pi_++\lambda_-\tilde\Pi_-$ the eigenvalue decomposition, where $\tilde\Pi_\pm$ are the eigenprojectors of $\lambda_\pm$, they are

$$ \pm\sqrt{\lambda_+}\,\tilde\Pi_+\pm\sqrt{\lambda_-}\,\tilde\Pi_- , $$

and shifting the two logarithms by $2\pi i$ reproduces the same finite set.

The root set of $0$ is the origin and the nilpotent cone. In the natural band the root set of a nonzero value is a level set, not a cone; in the plain band it is the classified set — finite for a non-scalar radicand, and a finite set together with a four-parameter family for a central scalar. For a central nonzero value, every root is a unit: the scalar roots are units, and a pure root has $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)\neq0$, so its norm does not vanish. A non-trivial idempotent is its own root and is therefore a zero divisor; the unit statement is a statement about central values.

The Natural Square and the Shell

The natural square of an element is its interval,

$$ \tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{\natural}=\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\natural}e_t, \qquad \langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\natural}=\Xi_t^2+(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi). $$

The root set of a central value $\lambda e_t$ is the level set $\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\natural}=\lambda$. On the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$, write the four-momentum as

$$ \tilde P=i\frac{E}{c}e_t+\vec p . $$

Its norm is

$$ \langle\tilde P\rangle_{\natural} =\left(\frac{iE}{c}\right)^2+(\vec p,\vec p) =-\frac{E^2}{c^2}+(\vec p,\vec p), $$

and the level set $\langle\tilde P\rangle_{\natural}=-m^2c^2$ is the mass shell,

$$ E^2=(\vec p,\vec p)c^2+m^2c^4 . $$

The root set of the value $-m^2c^2$ in the natural band is the whole shell, a three-dimensional two-sheeted hyperboloid, not a finite set. Every point of the shell is a four-momentum; the root is the four-momentum itself. This is the precise sense in which the root problem is the mass-shell problem: it is the natural root problem, not the plain one.

The mass shell is not the plain root set of a central value. The plain roots of $-m^2c^2$ include the scalar elements $\pm imc\,e_t$, which are the two rest points of the shell, and a four-parameter family of pure roots with $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=m^2c^2$, which are not four-momenta of the shell. The two statements are different and both are correct in their own band.

The Hermitian Square and the Mass Operator

The Hermitian square is

$$ \tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}, $$

a positive semidefinite element of $\mathbb{M}_+$, with non-negative eigenvalues. For a positive semidefinite value $\tilde Q$ the roots are

$$ \tilde\Xi=\tilde Q^{1/2}U,\qquad UU^{*}=e_t , $$

one for each unitary cell $U$: a family of amplitudes that share the value and differ by a unitary phase, the analogue of the sign of a scalar root. This is the band in which the mass-squared operator of a doublet lives. The three squares are three root problems: the plain square for the algebraic multiplicity, the natural square for the interval, the Hermitian square for the mass operator.

The Sign and the Causal Class

On the material slice the sign of a real central value is the causal class of its natural level set. The value $-m^2c^2$ is negative and its level set is the timelike region; the value $0$ is null and its level set is the cone; the value $+m^2c^2$ is positive and its level set is the spacelike region. The magnitude sets the size of the shell: if the reference shell is $\langle\tilde P\rangle_{\natural}=-1$, the shell of mass $m$ is its image under multiplication by $mc$, because the norm is quadratic in the scale.

Invertibility divides the same way. A root of a nonzero central value is a unit, so a tachyon is invertible; among the central values, only $0$ has non-invertible roots. The cone is the wall of the units, and the mass shell never touches it for $m\neq0$.

The sign is not decoration. A mass term built with the wrong conjugation uses the positive-definite Hermitian norm, so its level set is a Euclidean sphere rather than the Lorentz hyperboloid; the choice of conjugation, not a numerical sign, is what puts the physical mass on the timelike side. The algebra supplies both level sets; the physics selects one by the conjugation it uses.

The Mass Is the Modulus

The natural root set is the level set of the norm; the Hamilton pairing names the invariant that the level set carries. For a four-momentum,

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde P=\Rho e^{\vec U\Theta}\;}, $$

with

$$ \Rho=imc,\qquad \vec U=\hat p,\qquad \Theta=-i\psi , $$

and $\hat p=\vec p/\lvert\vec p\rvert$ for $\vec p\neq0$; at rest the direction drops out, because $\sinh\psi=0$ there. Since $\hat p^2=-e_t$ while $(\hat p,\hat p)=1$, the exponential is trigonometric,

$$ \exp(\hat p\Theta)=\cos\Theta+\hat p\sin\Theta , $$

and the imaginary angle $\Theta=-i\psi$ turns it into $\cosh\psi-i\hat p\sinh\psi$. Multiplying by $\Rho=imc$ and reducing $i^2=-1$,

$$ \tilde P=imc\cosh\psi+mc\,\hat p\sinh\psi . $$

The mass is $m=\lvert\Rho\rvert/c$. The root set of the value $-m^{2}c^{2}$ is the union of the two sheets of the shell, the two orbits of the restricted Lorentz group: the orbit of $\Rho e^{\vec U\Theta}$ and the orbit of its central negative. Every point of the shell has the same $\lvert\Rho\rvert$ and its own pair $(\vec U,\Theta)$: the same modulus, a different direction and rapidity.

The mass is the modulus of the Hamilton amplitude, not the energy. A boost changes the rapidity $\psi$, hence the energy $mc^{2}\cosh\psi$, and it leaves the modulus $mc$ alone. The same split appears in the spectrum: $\lambda_+\lambda_-=\Rho^{2}$ is the invariant norm, while $\lambda_+/\lambda_-=e^{2\psi}$ carries the rapidity.

The boundary. The identification is licensed for the four-momentum chart and the principal branch. For a general element the Hamilton modulus is a complex amplitude, and the mass interpretation is an extra identification. The modulus is also not additive: it is multiplicative under composition, and the mass of a sum is not the sum of the masses.

The Isospectral Family

Two elements are isospectral when their eigenvalues agree with multiplicity. The eigenvalues are $\lambda_\pm=Q_t\pm iB$ with $B^2=(\vec Q,\vec Q)$, so the spectrum as a multiset fixes $Q_t$ and $B^2$; the isospectral family of a generic element is

$$ \mathcal I(\lambda_+,\lambda_-) =\left\{Q_te_t+\vec Q:\ \vec Q\in\mathbb{C}^{3},\ (\vec Q,\vec Q)=B^{2}\right\}, $$

a complex quadric of four real dimensions. On the material slice $\vec Q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and the family is the sphere $\lvert\vec p\rvert=B$: the directions of motion at fixed energy and momentum magnitude.

The family is the set that a spectrum-only measurement leaves open. It is also the common level set of the trace and the norm, the two spectral invariants. A dynamics that conserves both moves inside the family and does not leave it.

A rotation acts transitively on that sphere, so the material family is one rotation orbit. The mass shell is larger: it is the union of the two orbits of the restricted Lorentz group at fixed mass, and a boost changes the trace and the spectrum while preserving the norm. On the whole algebra the family also contains complex vector parts that no real Lorentz transformation reaches; those members are not four-momenta.

The degenerate case splits. At $B=0$ the eigenvalues coincide at $Q_t$, and the family is the isotropic quadric $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$ in the vector part, a cone with vertex at the origin of $\vec Q$-space. Within the matrix image a repeated eigenvalue has two conjugation classes, the scalar $Q_te_t$ and the Jordan block $Q_te_t+\vec Q$ with $\vec Q\neq0$ and $\vec Q^2=0$. They have the same spectrum and the same norm, and they are not conjugate. The minimal polynomial, not the spectrum, separates them.

The boundary. The family is a set of elements, not a set of states and not a set of particles. Two isospectral elements need not be related by a physical transformation, and the article does not claim that they are. The interpretation is epistemic: it names what the spectrum leaves undetermined, and the algebra supplies no principle that selects one member.

The Mass Shell Is a Propagator Pole

The two-point function of a scalar of mass $m$ has the momentum-space denominator

$$ \langle\tilde P\rangle_{\natural}+m^2c^2 , $$

up to a sign and a normalisation. The propagator is singular where the denominator vanishes, and that locus is the mass shell. The root set of the natural band and the pole set of the propagator are the same set, seen once as an algebraic level set and once as an analytic singularity.

The Feynman prescription deforms the shell,

$$ m^2c^2\longmapsto m^2c^2-i\epsilon , $$

and the deformation is a displacement into the complex plane. The algebra supplies the complex plane in which the displacement lives; it does not select the contour. The retarded, advanced and Feynman propagators differ by which side of the real axis the poles are displaced to, and that choice is a boundary condition, not an algebraic identity.

The boundary. The pole is a property of the propagator, not of a single root. The root is a four-momentum; the propagator is a correlation function, and its pole is where the correlation function diverges. The $i\epsilon$ is a prescription, not a derivation; the algebra gives a home for the $\epsilon$ but no principle that chooses the Feynman contour over the retarded or the advanced one.

The Mass Can Be a Field

The scalar mass can be promoted to a biquaternion field. In the Einstein–Mayer promotion of the Lanczos system the mass $m$ becomes $\tilde E$, and the second-order equations for the two field components $\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2$ become eigenvalue equations,

$$ \Box\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_1\bigl(\tilde E\tilde E^{*}\bigr), \qquad \Box\tilde Q_2=\tilde Q_2\bigl(\tilde E^{*}\tilde E\bigr). $$

The two mass-squareds of the doublet are the eigenvalues of $\tilde E\tilde E^{*}$, equivalently of $\tilde E^{*}\tilde E$ for the nonzero spectrum. The mass is no longer a number multiplying the field; it is an operator acting on the field from the right.

Three cases show the range the corpus uses. A real scalar $\tilde E=s e_t$ gives $\tilde E\tilde E^{*}=s^2e_t$, a degenerate doublet of mass $|s|$. A Hermitian idempotent, $\tilde E=\tfrac12(e_t+i\hat n)$ with $\hat n$ a real vector of unit square, gives $\tilde E\tilde E^{*}=\tilde E$, with eigenvalues $1$ and $0$: one massive mode and one massless mode. A singular mass field leaves a massless mode whatever else it does, because with $\mathsf{M}_2$ the $2\times2$ matrix representation

$$ \det\mathsf{M}_2\bigl(\tilde E\tilde E^{*}\bigr)=\bigl|\langle\tilde E\rangle_{\natural}\bigr|^2, $$

and a vanishing norm forces a zero eigenvalue. The example $\tilde E=\vec e_x+i\vec e_y$ is singular, with mass-squareds $4$ and $0$. A general mass field gives two distinct nonzero mass-squareds; the three cases above do not.

The boundary. The framework derives no numerical mass. The algebra is scale-free, and the mass field is put in by hand in the Einstein–Mayer system; the Petiau system closes it by giving the mass field its own equation, at the cost of nonlinearity. The construction shows that the algebra can carry a mass that is a field with its own equation, and that the mass-squareds of a doublet are the eigenvalues of the operator $\tilde E\tilde E^{*}$. It does not produce the electron mass or any other.

The Mass Can Be Generated by a Condensate

The corpus carries two first-order equations, and the condensate is an object of the cell equation. The gradient equation is the one-sided chiral pair

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi_R=m\tilde\Psi_L,\qquad \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Psi_L=m\tilde\Psi_R , $$

with $\tilde\nabla$ the biquaternionic gradient. Every derivative acts by left multiplication, so no right multiplication is among its terms and no chirality operator can appear. On a plane wave $\tilde\nabla$ acts as left multiplication by $i\tilde P/\hbar$, so the mass shell above is the level set of $\langle\tilde P\rangle_{\natural}$.

The cell equation is the same dynamics on the Dirac module,

$$ (\mathbb D-m)\psi=0,\qquad \mathbb D=iG_0\frac{\partial}{\partial t'}+iG_k\frac{\partial}{\partial x_k}, $$

with the sum over $k=1,2,3$ understood, $t'$ the time coordinate of the material chart, and the cells

$$ G_0=iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}},\qquad G_k=iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}} . $$

The operator is two-sided: the time term is a pure right multiplication, the spatial terms are left times right. The chirality cell $\Gamma=-iR_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ anticommutes with the operator, $\{\Gamma,\mathbb D\}=0$, because $R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ anticommutes with $R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ and with $R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ while the left factors commute with it. A chirality needs a right multiplication, and the one-sided gradient has none; it can appear only on the module.

The axial symmetry, the axial current and the condensate belong to the cell equation. They are not two theories: the pair is the one-sided statement in the algebra, and the cell equation is its two-sided representative on the module.

The massless cell equation $\mathbb D\psi=0$ carries the axial symmetry

$$ \psi\longmapsto\exp(i\alpha\Gamma)\psi . $$

Since $i\Gamma=R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ and $i^2=-1$, the exponential is a real combination of cells,

$$ \exp(i\alpha\Gamma)\psi =\cos\alpha\,\psi+\sin\alpha\,\psi\vec e_z , $$

with no imaginary unit left outside. A mass term breaks the symmetry: the vector phase $\psi\mapsto e^{i\alpha}\psi$ passes through the mass, the axial phase does not. The axial current $j_{5\mu}=\psi^{*}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}G_\mu\Gamma\psi$ obeys the identity

$$ \sum_\mu\frac{\partial j_{5\mu}}{\partial x_\mu} =2im\,\psi^{*}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Gamma\psi =2m\,\psi^{*}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}\psi . $$

The divergence vanishes at $m=0$; the axial anomaly is a separate quantum effect. A nonzero vacuum expectation of the fermion bilinear,

$$ \langle\psi^{*}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\psi\rangle\neq0, $$

is the order parameter of the breaking. The mass term and the condensate multiply the same bilinear, so the dynamically generated coefficient is

$$ m=-2G\langle\psi^{*}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\psi\rangle, $$

with $G$ the coupling of the four-fermion interaction. The condensate is a central scalar and is neutral under the vector phase; the vector symmetry survives and the axial one is broken. The Goldstone modes follow from the broken generator.

The boundary. The framework has no colour group and no strong gauge dynamics. The compact gauge algebra inside $\mathbb{B}$ is at most $\mathrm{U}(2)$ of real dimension four, and $SU(3)$ is absent, so a QCD-like condensate cannot be derived. The algebra supplies the carrier of the condensate, the broken-symmetry pattern and the sector placement of the order parameter; the coupling, the cutoff, the scale and the numerical value are empirical input. The condensate is also not the real structure $\flat$: it is the linear chiral pairing, not a Majorana-type pairing, and interpreting it through $\flat$ gives the wrong object.

The Root Can Be a Geometric Map

The Kustaanheimo–Stiefel map carries four coordinates $(\varsigma_1,\varsigma_2,\varsigma_3,\varsigma_0)$ to three,

$$ x_1=2(\varsigma_1\varsigma_3-\varsigma_2\varsigma_0),\qquad x_2=2(\varsigma_1\varsigma_0+\varsigma_2\varsigma_3),\qquad x_3=\varsigma_1^2+\varsigma_2^2-\varsigma_3^2-\varsigma_0^2 . $$

The radius is the natural norm of the quaternion $\varsigma=\varsigma_1\vec e_x+\varsigma_2\vec e_y+\varsigma_3\vec e_z+\varsigma_0e_t$,

$$ \boxed{\;r=\sqrt{x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2}=\varsigma_1^2+\varsigma_2^2+\varsigma_3^2+\varsigma_0^2=\langle\varsigma\rangle_{\natural}\;}, $$

and the Coulomb singularity is the reciprocal of the norm,

$$ \frac1r=\frac1{\langle\varsigma\rangle_{\natural}} . $$

The map is a circle bundle away from the origin: the fiber over a nonzero $x$ is a circle, and $\varsigma$ and $-\varsigma$ are two points of it. The sign pair is the discrete part of the ambiguity; the circle is the gauge freedom of the KS constraint, not a second physical position. The map is a square root of the position in the sense that the three position coordinates are quadratic in the four KS coordinates, while the radius is the algebra's norm of $\varsigma$.

The boundary. The transformation, the pseudo-time $dt'=r\,ds$, the reduction to the four-dimensional oscillator and the Mehler kernel are standard and imported. The algebra supplies the interpretation: the Coulomb potential is a central scalar, the radius is the norm, and the singularity is the inverse norm. It does not cure the analytic failure of the time-sliced kernel. The sign pair is not two physical positions either: it is one discrete ambiguity inside a circle of representatives, and the circle is the constraint's gauge freedom.

The Roots Are Frames

The plain root set of a generic element has four elements, two opposite pairs. A nonzero central scalar has a continuum: two scalar roots and a four-parameter family of pure roots. The algebra does not select one element of the set. A calculation chooses a root, and the choice fixes the sign and the branch of the square. That choice is what the article calls a frame.

The word is not a Lorentz frame and not a gauge orbit. It names the fact that the root is a representative: the value $\tilde Q$ is fixed, the root is one of its square roots, and the passage from one root to another is a discrete sign or a continuous family, not a symmetry of the value. In the natural band the interpretation is sharper. The root set of $-m^2c^2$ is the mass shell, and every point of the shell is a frame in this sense: a representative four-momentum, a choice of energy and momentum that realizes the same invariant mass. The word names the representative, not an observer's basis. The shell is the set of representatives, not a set of particles; a particle is a state, and the shell is its kinematical arena.

The boundary. The roots are not all related by a physical transformation. The four plain roots are two opposite pairs, not one orbit; the central family is a continuum, not a gauge orbit. The frame interpretation is an interpretation of the multiplicity, not a derivation of a gauge symmetry. The algebra gives the multiplicity; it does not give a principle for choosing one root over another.

Limits

The article derives no mass, no coupling and no dynamics. The mass field is an input; the condensate needs a dynamics the algebra does not contain; the propagator contour is a boundary condition; the Kustaanheimo–Stiefel transformation is imported. The root set is not a particle spectrum: the mass shell is a set of four-momenta, and the mass-squareds of a doublet are eigenvalues of an operator, not roots. The frame interpretation is not a gauge symmetry.

The bands must be kept apart. The plain root set of a nonzero central value is a finite set together with a four-parameter family; the natural root set is the shell; the Hermitian root set is the mass operator's family. A reader who applies the plain classification to the mass shell will look for a finite root set where there is a hyperboloid, and a reader who applies the natural level set to the plain problem will lose the multiplicity. The distinction is the first result of the article and the boundary of the other four.

The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices. The bracket is the frame, on the generators as on the metric, $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]$ or $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}]$, so that a glyph names a display and not an object of the algebra.

The body names cells — $L_a$, $R_b$, $\Gamma=-iR_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ — without naming an algebra for them. This closing section supplies the dictionary and nothing in the body depends on it.

The gammas generate $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$; they are not a different algebra. The cells act on $\mathbb{B}$ itself, four complex dimensions, so each cell is a $4\times4$ complex matrix. Here $L_a$ and $R_b$ are the left and right multiplications, $L_a\psi=a\psi$ and $R_b\psi=\psi b$, and $c(M)$ is the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products.

$$ \gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad \gamma_1[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad \gamma_2[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad \gamma_3[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}, $$

The same in the axis-free display:

$$ \gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}},\qquad \gamma_1[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\qquad \gamma_2[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\qquad \gamma_3[\mathbb{M}_-]=iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}, $$

$$ \gamma_5=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}. $$

In the axis-free display:

$$ \gamma_5=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}. $$

The factors of $i$ in a product are reduced by $i^2=-1$; the five cells satisfy, for $k=1,2,3$,

$$ \gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-]^2=e_t,\qquad \gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]^2=-e_t,\qquad \{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}e_t , $$

with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and $\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3$, and

$$ \gamma_5^2=e_t,\qquad \{\gamma_5,\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=0\quad(\mu=0,1,2,3),\qquad \gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-]\gamma_1[\mathbb{M}_-]\gamma_2[\mathbb{M}_-]\gamma_3[\mathbb{M}_-]=-i\gamma_5 . $$

A single product is a pure tensor: $c(M)$ has rank one. The rank-one elements form a cone of complex dimension seven, every general element is a sum of at most four of them, and the length is $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$.

The cells of the cell equation are these gammas: $G_\mu=\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]$, since $L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ acts as the identity on the module, and the chirality cell is $\Gamma=\gamma_5$. The central grading $\chi=i\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{ad}}$, with $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{ad}}$ the central root of $-e_t$ in the complexification, is the difference of the two central idempotents, so $\chi^2=e_t$. It is a different object: on the module the two act through the same $4\times4$ matrix, and only the algebra keeps them apart.

The dictionary is complex-linear. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the sixteen words in $\gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-],\dots,\gamma_3[\mathbb{M}_-]$ span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$, and the sixteen cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$. The two are distinct real forms of the same $M_4(\mathbb{C})$, the first of quaternionic type and the second of real type; that is why $i$ times a cell is not again a cell.

The display. Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. The two are carried into one another by $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}=C_{\Pi}L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and a conjugation by $C_{\Pi}$ changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant, so every identity of the section holds in both. An array is a statement about one display; a cell named in the running text keeps the Peirce label, and its axis-free array is the conjugate. The label names the array and not the operator.

Summary

The root problem $\tilde\Xi^2=\tilde Q$ is band-dependent. In the plain band the roots are generically four, falling to two or to none, with a four-parameter family for a central scalar; the root of $0$ is the nilpotent cone. In the natural band the root set of a central value is the level set of the interval, and on the material sector the level set $-m^2c^2$ is the mass shell. In the Hermitian band the root set is the family $\tilde Q^{1/2}U$ over the unitary cells, and the mass-squared operator of a doublet lives in that band.

The five interpretations use these sets: the mass shell is a propagator pole; the mass can be a field, with a mass-squared operator whose eigenvalues split a doublet; the mass can be generated by a chiral condensate; the root can be the Kustaanheimo–Stiefel square root of the position; and the roots are frames, the representatives the algebra does not choose among. The mass itself is the Hamilton modulus, $m=\lvert\Rho\rvert/c$, and the shell is its level set. The isospectral family of a generic element is the complex quadric of fixed trace and fixed vector square; on the material slice it is the sphere of directions, and at a repeated eigenvalue it splits into a scalar and a Jordan class.

The mass shell is a statement of the gradient equation; the chirality, the axial current and the condensate are statements of the cell equation, and the two are kept apart. The algebra supplies the sets and the identities; it supplies no numerical mass, no dynamics and no choice of contour.

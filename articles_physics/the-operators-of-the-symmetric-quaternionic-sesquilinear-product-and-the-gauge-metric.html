The Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and the Gauge Metric

Introduction

This article is the operator article of the gauge-metric band, the band of The Gauge Metric as a Product: the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and Values in No Subspace: Why the Symmetric Gauge Product Is Not a State. Its product is the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product

$$ \tilde P\bullet\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural}\bigr) =\bigl[P_0\overline{Q_0}-(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf Q})\bigr]-P_0\overline{\mathbf Q}-\overline{Q_0}\mathbf P, $$

and the operators the band attaches to an element are its two one-sided multiplications

$$ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\bullet\tilde X,\qquad R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\bullet\tilde A . $$

The answer is one sentence. The form of the band is the indefinite Krein form $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*})=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}$ of signature $(1,3)$; the adjoint the operators carry is the $J$-adjoint for $K$ with the fundamental symmetry $J={}^{\natural}$ the natural conjugation; and that adjoint keeps the side and conjugates the parameter: the adjoint of the left multiplication by $\tilde A$ is the left multiplication by $\tilde A^{\natural}$. This is the operator face of the band's job word metric: the form the operators are adjoint for is the indefinite pairing itself, its diagonal $K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert Q_k\rvert^{2}$ is the metric, and the product that carries it is a pairing of the gauge side and not a state.

Two facts organise the article, and the first corrects a phrase that belongs to another band. The symmetrisation gives the product its symmetry, and the symmetry is conjugate-commutativity, not commutativity: $\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q=\overline{\tilde Q\bullet\tilde P}$. The band therefore carries two multiplication families and not one, of opposite parity, linked by the coefficientwise conjugation, $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=\bar{\cdot}\circ R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$. The band in which the two families genuinely coincide is the symmetric plain band, whose product $\tfrac12(\tilde P\tilde Q+\tilde Q\tilde P)$ is commutative; the gauge band is not that band. The second fact is that neither family closes under composition, so the band has no operator algebra: the left defect is the associator, and the left family is conjugate-linear, so its composite is linear and can never be a left multiplication off the zero operator.

The article is the quaternionic-sesquilinear sibling of The Operators of the Ordinary Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich, of The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing and of The Operators of the Symmetrised Plain Product: Jordan Commutativity, and it states the gauge row of the grid whose comparison is The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket, not one of the twelve. The product and its laws are Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions; the operators, the parity, the rank, the traces and the automorphisms are The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; the form $K$ read as a product is The Krein Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; the $J$-adjoint and the $J$-unitary family are J-Self-Adjoint and J-Unitary Operators on the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions; and the sandwich for the same indefinite form is The Indefinite Hermitian Sandwich on the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions. All are cited in place and none is re-proved here.

Conventions. The conventions are those of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe: $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, the relations $e_k^{2}=-e_0$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central scalar imaginary $i$ with $i^{2}=-1$; an element is $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf Q$ with complex coefficients, of scalar part $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)=Q_0$ and vector part $\mathbf Q=\sum_kQ_ke_k$. The natural conjugation is $\tilde Q^{\natural}=Q_0e_0-\mathbf Q$, the coefficientwise conjugation is $\bar{\tilde Q}=\sum_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}e_\mu$, and the star is $\tilde Q^{*}=\overline{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$. The invariant form is $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*})=P_0\overline{Q_0}-(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf Q})$ with sign vector $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the two one-sided actions of $e_0$ are the conjugations, $e_0\bullet\tilde Q=\tilde Q^{*}$ and $\tilde Q\bullet e_0=\tilde Q^{\natural}$; and the norm is $N(\tilde Q)=\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q=Q_0^{2}+Q_1^{2}+Q_2^{2}+Q_3^{2}$.

A notation caution. The mathematics block of the product writes it $\tilde P\star\tilde Q$ and its operators $L^{\star}_{\tilde A},R^{\star}_{\tilde A}$, because in that block it is the product. The physics block reserves the mark $\star$ for the fourth product $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$, writes the symmetric half $\bullet$ and the antisymmetric half $\diamond$, and this article writes the operators $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A},R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ accordingly. The reader moving between the two blocks should carry the names $\mathrm{SQS}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}$, not the marks.

The One Symmetric Product and Its Two Operators

Definition. For $\tilde A\in\mathbb{B}$ the left multiplication and the right multiplication of the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product are $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\bullet\tilde X$ and $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\bullet\tilde A$. They are the biquaternion instance of the two multiplication operators of a sesqualgebra, determined in The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra. Alongside them this article uses the ordinary one-sided operators of the algebra, $L_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\tilde X$ and $R_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\tilde A$, to which the two families are reducible by the involution.

Theorem (the relation of the two families). For every $\tilde A$ and every $\tilde X$, $\tilde A\bullet\tilde X=\overline{\tilde X\bullet\tilde A}$, that is $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=C\circ R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ and $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=C\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$, where $C=\bar{\cdot}$ is the coefficientwise conjugation.

Proof. The product is $\mathbb{C}$-linear in its first slot and conjugate-linear in its second, so the coefficientwise conjugation interchanges the two orders; the exchange $\overline{\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}}=\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural}$ fixes the half-sum, which is The Gauge Metric as a Product. Reading the two orders at a fixed argument gives the operator identity, and $C$ is an involution. This is the parity theorem of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Read as a physical statement. The two attachments of the band are not the same act, nor are they independent: attaching a gauge element on the left is attaching its coefficientwise conjugate on the right, and the conjugation is exactly the symmetry the symmetrisation bought. The band carries one operator family read through the conjugation and two parities; the interpretation is that the ordering of the two slots is immaterial while the conjugation between them is not.

Corollary (the operators at the unit). $L^{\bullet}_{e_0}={}^{*}$ is the star conjugation and $R^{\bullet}_{e_0}={}^{\natural}$ the natural conjugation, with real matrices $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,-1,1,1,1)$ and $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,1,-1,-1,-1)$.

Proof. The two one-sided actions of the identity are $e_0\bullet\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$ and $\tilde X\bullet e_0=\tilde X^{\natural}$, The Gauge Metric as a Product; the diagonal matrices are the real matrices of the two conjugations, The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Read as a physical statement. The neutral operation of the band is not the identity on either side: the left action of the unit is the star, the right action is the natural conjugation. The interpretation is that the gauge pairing has no unit of a multiplication, and the two conjugations are the residues of the unit it lacks.

Caution (the word commutative). The symmetrisation makes the product conjugate-commutative and it does not make it commutative; the two operators do not coincide, they have opposite parity, and a reader who expects the collapse of the two families into one, as happens for the commutative symmetric plain product of The Operators of the Symmetrised Plain Product: Jordan Commutativity, will not find it here. The collapse requires commutativity, and conjugate-commutativity supplies only the link $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=C\circ R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$. The operator content of the metric word is the conjugation that links the two parities, not a single family.

Theorem (the parity of the two families). For every $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$ and every $\tilde X$ one has $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\lambda\tilde X)=\overline{\lambda}\,L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)$ and $L^{\bullet}_{\lambda\tilde A}=\lambda\,L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$, while $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\lambda\tilde X)=\lambda\,R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)$ and $R^{\bullet}_{\lambda\tilde A}=\overline{\lambda}\,R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$. The left multiplication is conjugate-linear in the variable and complex-linear in the parameter; the right multiplication is complex-linear in the variable and conjugate-linear in the parameter. In particular $L^{\bullet}_{\lambda e_0}=\lambda\,{}^{*}$ and $R^{\bullet}_{\lambda e_0}=\overline{\lambda}\,{}^{\natural}$.

Proof. The natural conjugation is $\mathbb{C}$-linear and the star is conjugate-linear, and the product is linear in its first slot and conjugate-linear in its second; the four rules are read from the definition as in The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing, and the central case follows from the corollary at $e_0$. This is the parity theorem of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Read as a physical statement. The two slots of the gauge pairing have opposite polarity, and a conjugate-linear operator is not an element of the complex endomorphism algebra. The interpretation is that no family of the band can be a representation of an algebra, so the band carries its operators without an operator calculus.

Definition (the value). For all biquaternions the value of the product is $\tilde A\bullet\tilde X=K(\tilde A,\tilde X)e_0-A_0\overline{\mathbf X}-\overline{X_0}\mathbf A$, with scalar part the Krein form $K(\tilde A,\tilde X)$ and vector part the mixed term $-A_0\overline{\mathbf X}-\overline{X_0}\mathbf A$.

Proof. The value is the display of The Gauge Metric as a Product, §The Value of the Symmetric Gauge Product; the scalar part is $K$ and the vector part the mixed term alone.

Read as a physical statement. The operator acts by returning the pairing as a scale and the mixed term as a direction, and the two projections sit side by side in one value. The interpretation is that the multiplication is a deformation of an element and not a rotation or a translation, its scalar part a conformal factor and its vector part a shear; the name is the sibling's and the algebra is the display.

Theorem (the composition and the defect of multiplicativity). The two families do not multiply, $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde B}\neq L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A\bullet\tilde B}$ and $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\circ R^{\bullet}_{\tilde B}\neq R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A\bullet\tilde B}$; the defect of the left composition is the associator $\tilde A\bullet(\tilde B\bullet\tilde X)-(\tilde A\bullet\tilde B)\bullet\tilde X$. For the left family the inequality is forced by parity, the composite of two conjugate-linear maps being $\mathbb{C}$-linear while a single left multiplication is conjugate-linear.

Proof. The defect of $L^{\bullet}$ at $\tilde X$ is the associator, nonzero on a general triple because the block is not associative; the defect of $R^{\bullet}$ is the same computation with the factors in the other order. A map that is both $\mathbb{C}$-linear and conjugate-linear is zero, which forbids a complex-linear representation. This is The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, §The Composition and the Defect of Multiplicativity.

Read as a physical statement. Two gauge pairings do not compose to a gauge pairing, and the failure has two independent sources: the associator, which is the ordinary defect of a non-associative operation, and the parity, which the left family carries alone. The interpretation is that the metric band supplies a pairing to be transported and not a multiplication to be iterated, and that the transport is the adjoint.

The Adjoint of the Krein Form

The band's operators are adjoint for the form their product carries as its scalar part, and the form is indefinite. This section states exactly which form and which adjoint.

Definition (the form and its fundamental symmetry). The Krein form of the band is $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*})$, with coefficient form $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}=P_0\overline{Q_0}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}P_k\overline{Q_k}$ and $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$. It is Hermitian, non-degenerate, of signature $(1,3)$ over the complex coefficients and $(2,6)$ over the real parameters, with fundamental decomposition $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}\perp_K\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ and fundamental symmetry $J={}^{\natural}$ the natural conjugation, so that $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=(\tilde P^{\natural},\tilde Q)=(J\tilde P,\tilde Q)$ for the definite star form $(\tilde P,\tilde W)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde W^{*})=\langle\tilde P,\tilde W\rangle_{*}$, which is the positive form of the probability row.

Proof. In the coefficient basis the form is the standard form of signature $(1,3)$, and the natural conjugation is the diagonal operator $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ inserted in the first slot, turning the definite form into the indefinite one; the details are The Krein Gram Matrix and the Restrictions of the Form and The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric, and the identity $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=(\tilde P^{\natural},\tilde Q)$ is the fundamental-symmetry relation $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde P^{\natural},\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$ of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

Read as a physical statement. The form of the band is not the positive form of the state row but the indefinite pairing of the gauge row, and its modulus is a signature and not an error. The interpretation is that the band compares two internal frames without ranking them.

Definition (the adjoint). For a complex-linear operator $T$ the $J$-adjoint, or indefinite adjoint, is the operator $T^{\dagger}$ with $K(T\tilde P,\tilde Q)=K(\tilde P,T^{\dagger}\tilde Q)$ for all $\tilde P,\tilde Q$, that is $T^{\dagger}=JT^{*}J$ with $J={}^{\natural}$ and $T^{*}$ the adjoint of $T$ for the definite star form, $(\tilde P,T^{*}\tilde Q)=(T\tilde P,\tilde Q)$. The form is non-degenerate, so the adjoint exists and is unique. For a conjugate-linear operator $S$ the linear rule has no solution, and the twisted $J$-adjoint is used instead, $K(S\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\overline{K(\tilde P,S^{\dagger}\tilde Q)}$, under which the adjoint of a conjugate-linear operator is again conjugate-linear. Both rules are the indefinite adjoint of The Indefinite Hermitian Sandwich on the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions.

Read as a physical statement. The adjoint of the band is taken for the indefinite form, so it is a $J$-adjoint and not a definite adjoint; the conjugation on the right of the twisted rule is the correction the conjugate-linearity of the left family forces. The interpretation is that the transport across the gauge pairing is indefinite and that its fixed points will not be the observables of the positive form.

Theorem (the adjoint of the left multiplication). With respect to $K$, for every $\tilde A$ one has $K(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde X,\tilde Y)=\overline{K(\tilde X,L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}\tilde Y)}$, so the adjoint of the left multiplication by $\tilde A$ is the left multiplication by $\tilde A^{\natural}$: the adjoint keeps the side and conjugates the parameter by the natural conjugation.

Proof. This is the compatibility theorem of The Krein Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra: $K(\tilde A\bullet\tilde X,\tilde Y)=\overline{K(\tilde X,\tilde A^{\natural}\bullet\tilde Y)}$, which is the display with the definition of the left multiplication; the conjugate on the right is the parity correction of the conjugate-linear operator.

Read as a physical statement. Transporting a gauge operation across the pairing does not move it to the other side; it replaces the parameter by its natural conjugation, the fundamental symmetry of the form. The interpretation is that the gauge adjoint is an involution of the parameter and not a charge reversal of the positive row: it reflects the vector directions, and the reflection is the metric's own.

Theorem (the adjoint of the right multiplication). With respect to $K$, for every $\tilde A$ one has $(R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}\circ C$, where $C=\bar{\cdot}$ is the coefficientwise conjugation; the right multiplication is complex-linear, so this is the adjoint of the linear rule, $K(R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde X,\tilde Y)=K(\tilde X,(R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}\tilde Y)$. In the twisted sense $C$ is its own adjoint, $K(C\tilde X,\tilde Y)=\overline{K(\tilde X,C\tilde Y)}$.

Proof. The right family is the left family through the coefficientwise conjugation, $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=C\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$, and $C$ is an involution whose twisted adjoint is itself, $C^{\dagger}=C$ because $K(\overline{\tilde X},\tilde Y)=\overline{K(\tilde X,\overline{\tilde Y})}$. The adjoint of a composite reverses the order whatever the parities of the factors, $(C\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}\circ C^{\dagger}$; the composite $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ is complex-linear, so its adjoint is taken in the linear sense, and substituting the left adjoint gives the display.

Read as a physical statement. The right attachment has an adjoint of a mixed kind: a left attachment composed with the coefficientwise conjugation. The interpretation is the parity of the band at the level of the adjoint: the left family stays inside itself under the adjoint, the right family does not, and the two statements are the two faces of the conjugate-commutativity.

Remark (this adjoint against the plain adjoint). The adjoint of the band keeps the side and conjugates the parameter by the natural conjugation. The plain algebra's one-sided operators, adjoint for the same indefinite form, obey the other rule, $(L_{\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=R_{\bar{\tilde Q}}$, of The Indefinite Hermitian Sandwich on the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions: there the adjoint crosses to the other side and conjugates by the coefficientwise conjugation. The two rules must not be exchanged, and they are the operator difference between the plain multiplication and the symmetric sesquilinear multiplication of the same form.

Read as a physical statement. The same indefinite form carries two adjoints, one for each product, and only the pair (form, product) determines what the adjoint does to the parameter. The interpretation is that the adjoint is a property of an operation and not of a form alone, and the crossing reader The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket is where the pairings are compared.

The Types of the Operators

Theorem (the types for $K$). For $\tilde A\in\mathbb{B}$, $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ is self-adjoint for $K$ exactly when $\tilde A^{\natural}=\tilde A$, that is exactly when $\tilde A\in\mathbb{C}e_0$, and it is skew-adjoint exactly when $\tilde A^{\natural}=-\tilde A$, that is exactly when $\tilde A\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$. A self-adjoint left multiplication has a central parameter and a skew-adjoint one has a pure vector parameter; a mixed parameter $\tilde A=\lambda e_0+\mathbf A$ with both parts nonzero is neither.

Proof. By the adjoint theorem the adjoint of $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ is $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}$, and $\tilde A\mapsto L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ is injective because $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(e_0)=\tilde A^{\natural}$; so self-adjointness is $\tilde A^{\natural}=\tilde A$ and skew-adjointness is $\tilde A^{\natural}=-\tilde A$. The natural conjugation fixes exactly the central elements and anti-fixes exactly the pure vectors.

Read as a physical statement. A self-adjoint gauge multiplication is a multiplication by a central element and a skew-adjoint one a multiplication by a pure vector. The interpretation is that the symmetric class of the band is the centre, the antisymmetric class the vector directions, and that an element mixing a scale with a direction is neither: the two classes do not carve the algebra between them, and the mixed elements carry both grades.

The Reality Question

Proposition (reality is not automatic). A self-adjoint left multiplication of the band is not automatically real: its parameter is central, $\tilde A=\lambda e_0$ with $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$, and $\lambda=i$ gives the self-adjoint operator $L^{\bullet}_{ie_0}=i\,{}^{*}$ with a purely imaginary parameter. The condition of self-adjointness is a condition of type of the parameter and not of reality; the self-adjoint operators of the band are the left multiplications by the parameters of the complex line $\mathbb{C}e_0$, and among them the real ones are the real multiples of the identity.

Proof. Self-adjointness is $\tilde A^{\natural}=\tilde A$ by the type theorem, which is centrality and nothing more; a central parameter is a complex multiple of $e_0$, real or not, and $L^{\bullet}_{ie_0}(\tilde X)=ie_0\bullet\tilde X=i\tilde X^{*}$ is self-adjoint because $(ie_0)^{\natural}=ie_0$. This was checked numerically on the central parameters.

Read as a physical statement. The self-adjoint operators of the gauge band form a complex line of parameters, and a purely imaginary scalar is a self-adjoint operator of the band. The interpretation is that the band's symmetry class is the projective line of central calibrations and not the observables of the positive row; the reality that distinguishes observables belongs to the Hermitian form of the probability band, Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action.

The Rank, the Determinant and the Traces

Theorem (the rank, the determinant, the traces and the norm). For an element $\tilde A$ the two families have the same rank, $\operatorname{rank}L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=\operatorname{rank}R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ equal to $0$ at $\tilde A=0$, to $4$ when $A_0=0$ with $\tilde A\neq0$, to $8$ when $A_0\neq0$ with $N(\tilde A)\neq0$, and to $6$ when $A_0\neq0$ with $N(\tilde A)=0$; moreover $\det L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=\det R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=\lvert N(\tilde A)\rvert^{2}\lvert A_0\rvert^{4}$, $\operatorname{tr}L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=-4\,\mathrm{Re}(A_0)$, the determinant and the traces being those of the real-linear operators on the eight-real-dimensional space.

Proof. The rank table, the determinant and the traces are The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, §Rank, Image and Trace; the two families have the same rank because $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=C\circ R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ with $C$ of determinant $+1$. The norm $N$ enters the determinant and the rank through the two conditions $A_0=0$ and $N(\tilde A)=0$, and Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra owns the norm.

Read as a physical statement. The operator of a gauge element is invertible exactly when the element is not a zero divisor, and the norm $N$ is the datum that decides it: an element of nonzero scalar part and nonzero norm acts at full rank, one of zero norm collapses a real plane and a pure vector acts at half rank. The interpretation is that the gauge multiplication measures an element through its norm and its scale, and that the collapse at $N=0$ is the algebraic trace of the null directions of the metric.

The Symmetries of the Form and of the Product

Theorem (the form-preserving operators). No left multiplication preserves $K$. The right multiplication preserves $K$ exactly for a central parameter of modulus one, that is $K(R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde X,R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde Y)=K(\tilde X,\tilde Y)$ for all $\tilde X,\tilde Y$ exactly when $\tilde A=\lambda e_0$ with $\lvert\lambda\rvert=1$, and then $R^{\bullet}_{\lambda e_0}=\overline{\lambda}\,{}^{\natural}$. The general group of complex-linear maps preserving $K$ is larger, of real dimension sixteen, its matrices obeying $M^{\dagger}EM=E$ with $E=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the band's right multiplications meet it in the circle $\{\overline{\lambda}\,{}^{\natural}:\lvert\lambda\rvert=1\}$.

Proof. The value $K(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde X,L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde Y)$ is conjugate-linear in $\tilde X$ and linear in $\tilde Y$, while $K(\tilde X,\tilde Y)$ is linear in $\tilde X$ and conjugate-linear in $\tilde Y$: the two parities differ, so no left multiplication can preserve the form. For the right family, $R^{\bullet}_{\lambda e_0}=\overline{\lambda}\,{}^{\natural}$ gives $K(\overline{\lambda}\tilde X^{\natural},\overline{\lambda}\tilde Y^{\natural})=\lvert\lambda\rvert^{2}K(\tilde X,\tilde Y)$ because $K(\tilde X^{\natural},\tilde Y^{\natural})=K(\tilde X,\tilde Y)$. The group of linear maps preserving $K$ and its matrix condition are The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, §The Groups.

Read as a physical statement. The left family of the band is never an isometry of the metric, for a reason of parity and not of parameter; the only isometries the band's own multiplications supply are the right multiplications by a central phase. The interpretation is that the gauge pairing is preserved by a compact circle of central calibrations and by nothing one-sided else, and that the general isometry group of the metric is larger than the band's operators.

Theorem (the product-preserving operators). The natural conjugation ${}^{\natural}$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear automorphism of the product, the coefficientwise conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$ is a conjugate-linear automorphism, and the star ${}^{*}$ is their composite; the three generate the Klein group $\{\mathrm{id},{}^{\natural},\bar{\cdot},{}^{*}\}$. The multiplicative one-sided operators of the band are only the identity and the zero operator: $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X\bullet\tilde Y)=(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde X)\bullet(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde Y)$ for all $\tilde X,\tilde Y$ exactly when $\tilde A\in\{0,e_0\}$.

Proof. The three involutions satisfy $\varphi(\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q)=\varphi(\tilde P)\bullet\varphi(\tilde Q)$ by the definition of the product and the two anti-automorphism properties of the conjugations, and ${}^{*}={}^{\natural}\circ\bar{\cdot}$; this is The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, §The Groups. For multiplicativity, $L^{\bullet}_{e_0}={}^{*}$ is multiplicative because it is an automorphism of the product and $L^{\bullet}_{0}=0$ trivially, while every other parameter fails: $L^{\bullet}_{\lambda e_0}=\lambda\,{}^{*}$ is multiplicative only for $\lambda$ with $\lambda^{2}=\lambda$, and a non-central parameter fails already at $\tilde X=\tilde Y=e_0$. This was checked numerically on the central and non-central parameters.

Read as a physical statement. The symmetries of the product and the symmetries of the form fall on different operators: the form is preserved by the right multiplications of the central phases and the product by the four involutions of the structure group, while the band's one-sided multiplications are multiplicative only at the identity and at zero. The interpretation is that the gauge pairing has a finite structure group and a compact circle of phase isometries, and that no genuine one-sided operation is a symmetry of either beyond those.

Proposition (the structure group and the operators). For every $\tilde A$ one has $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}={}^{\natural}\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\circ{}^{\natural}$ and $L^{\bullet}_{\bar{\tilde A}}=\bar{\cdot}\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\circ\bar{\cdot}$, so the structure group acts on the operator families by conjugation of the parameter and of the space.

Proof. The two identities are the automorphism property of ${}^{\natural}$ and of $\bar{\cdot}$ read on the operator, $L^{\bullet}_{\varphi\tilde A}=\varphi\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\circ\varphi^{-1}$, and the involutions are their own inverses.

Read as a physical statement. The conjugations that generate the structure group relabel the space and the parameter at once, so the band's operator is attached to the class of the parameter under the Klein group and not to the parameter alone. The interpretation is that the gauge multiplication is invariant under the simultaneous relabelling of frame and calibration, which is the operator form of a gauge redundancy of signs.

The Operators Acting on the Krein Space

The space of the band. The band's space is the algebra $\mathbb{B}$ read as the Krein space of the form $K$, of signature $(1,3)$, with fundamental decomposition $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}\perp_K\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ and fundamental symmetry $J={}^{\natural}$. The operators $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ and $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ act on it, the first conjugate-linearly and the second linearly, and the adjoint identity $K(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde X,\tilde Y)=\overline{K(\tilde X,L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}\tilde Y)}$ is the statement that the adjoint of the left family stays inside the left family, and that the adjoint of the right family leaves it.

Proposition (no module action). The left multiplication of the band is conjugate-linear and is therefore not an element of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$; it does not act on the simple left module $S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1$ as a module map, and the band supplies no action of the algebra on the state space. The action the framework uses on $S$ is the ordinary left multiplication $L_{\tilde A}s=\tilde As$, of The Spinor Space as a Module and as a Hilbert Space.

Proof. A module action is $\mathbb{C}$-linear in the scalar, and the left multiplication of the band is conjugate-linear; the ordinary left multiplication is the module action of the plain product.

Read as a physical statement. The band's operation cannot act on a state space, because a state space is a complex module and the band's attachment of an element is conjugate-linear in the element it attaches. The interpretation is the operator form of the sibling's conclusion that the symmetric gauge product is not a state: an operation that cannot be a module map cannot be a state update.

Proposition (the diagonal is the metric and not a probability). The scalar part of the product is the Krein form, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q)=K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$, and on the diagonal $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}\lvert Q_k\rvert^{2}$, which is positive on the centre, negative on the vector subspace and not positive definite. The form the band's operators are adjoint for is therefore the metric itself, and it is not the positive definite form $H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})$ of the probability row.

Proof. The Sandwich and Its Adjoint

Definition (the two-sided operator and the sandwich). For a pair $\tilde P,\tilde Q$ the two-sided operator of the band is $B^{\bullet}_{\tilde P,\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\tilde P\bullet(\tilde X\bullet\tilde Q)$, and its dielectric sandwich, or diagonal, is the case $\tilde Q=\tilde P^{\natural}$, that is $\Theta^{\bullet}_{\tilde P}=B^{\bullet}_{\tilde P,\tilde P^{\natural}}=L^{\bullet}_{\tilde P}R^{\bullet}_{\tilde P^{\natural}}$, acting by $\Theta^{\bullet}_{\tilde P}(\tilde X)=\tilde P\bullet(\tilde X\bullet\tilde P^{\natural})$. The sandwich is the biquaternion instance, for the symmetric sesquilinear product, of the two-sided operator of the block; the sandwich of the plain product for the same indefinite form is The Indefinite Hermitian Sandwich on the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions.

Theorem (the adjoint of the two-sided operator). For all $\tilde P,\tilde Q$ one has $(B^{\bullet}_{\tilde P,\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=B^{\bullet}_{\tilde Q^{\natural},\tilde P^{\natural}}$ and $(\Theta^{\bullet}_{\tilde P})^{\dagger}=\Theta^{\bullet}_{\tilde P}$, so a two-sided operator has as adjoint the same operator with the parameters exchanged and each conjugated by ${}^{\natural}$, and every diagonal sandwich is self-adjoint for $K$.

Proof. The adjoint of a composite reverses the order and conjugates the parameters; with $(R^{\bullet}_{\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=L^{\bullet}_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}C$ and $(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde P})^{\dagger}=L^{\bullet}_{\tilde P^{\natural}}$, $(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde P}R^{\bullet}_{\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=L^{\bullet}_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}C\,L^{\bullet}_{\tilde P^{\natural}}$. Inserting $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde P^{\natural}}=C\circ L^{\bullet}_{\tilde P^{\natural}}$ and the involution of $C$ gives $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde Q^{\natural}}R^{\bullet}_{\tilde P^{\natural}}$, which is the first display. For the diagonal the two parameters of the adjoint are $\tilde P^{\natural\natural}=\tilde P$ and $\tilde P^{\natural}$, which are the two parameters of the sandwich, so the operator is its own adjoint.

Read as a physical statement. A Worked Case

Take the minimal mixing element, a sum of a scalar and a vector direction, $\tilde A=e_0+e_1$ with $A_0=1$, $\mathbf A=e_1$, $N(\tilde A)=2$ and $K(\tilde A,\tilde A)=0$. Its operator is neither self-adjoint nor skew-adjoint, since $\tilde A^{\natural}=e_0-e_1\neq\pm\tilde A$; its rank is eight and its determinant is $4$, so it is invertible. On the real basis $(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3,ie_0,ie_1,ie_2,ie_3)$ the left multiplication acts by

$$ e_0\mapsto e_0-e_1,\quad e_1\mapsto -e_0-e_1,\quad e_2\mapsto -e_2,\quad e_3\mapsto -e_3, $$

$$ ie_0\mapsto -ie_0+ie_1,\quad ie_1\mapsto ie_0+ie_1,\quad ie_2\mapsto ie_2,\quad ie_3\mapsto ie_3, $$

whose real matrix has $\det L^{\bullet}_{e_0+e_1}=\lvert N(\tilde A)\rvert^{2}\lvert A_0\rvert^{4}=4$, and the diagonal of the product leaves the centre,

$$ \tilde A\bullet\tilde A=(e_0+e_1)\bullet(e_0+e_1)=-2e_1, $$

because $K(\tilde A,\tilde A)=0$ and the mixed term $-A_0\overline{\mathbf A}-\overline{A_0}\mathbf A=-2e_1$ survives. The element is the witness that the diagonal is not central, and its value is a vector and not a complex multiple of $e_0$.

Take next the central parameter $\tilde C=ie_0$ with $\tilde C^{\natural}=ie_0=\tilde C$ and $N(\tilde C)=-1$, which gives the self-adjoint operator $L^{\bullet}_{ie_0}=i\,{}^{*}$ acting by $L^{\bullet}_{ie_0}(e_0)=ie_0$, $L^{\bullet}_{ie_0}(e_1)=-ie_1$ and $L^{\bullet}_{ie_0}(ie_1)=-e_1$. The element is the witness that self-adjointness of the band is a condition of centrality and not of reality, and its diagonal is central, $ie_0\bullet ie_0=e_0$.

Take next the pure vector parameter $\tilde S=e_1$ with $\tilde S^{\natural}=-e_1=-\tilde S$, $N(\tilde S)=1$ and $K(\tilde S,\tilde S)=-1$, which gives the skew-adjoint operator $L^{\bullet}_{e_1}$ of real rank four, acting by $L^{\bullet}_{e_1}(e_0)=-e_1$, $L^{\bullet}_{e_1}(e_1)=-e_0$, $L^{\bullet}_{e_1}(ie_0)=ie_1$ and $L^{\bullet}_{e_1}(ie_1)=ie_0$, with kernel $\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_2,e_3,ie_2,ie_3\}$ and central diagonal $e_1\bullet e_1=-e_0$. The element is the witness of the skew class and of the negative signature of the form.

Take finally the zero divisor, $\tilde D=e_0+ie_1$ with $A_0=1\neq0$ and $N(\tilde D)=1+i^{2}=0$, for which the rank drops to six and the kernel is the real plane $\ker L^{\bullet}_{e_0+ie_1}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_0+ie_1,\ ie_0-e_1\}$, the element itself lying in its own kernel. The four elements — a mixing element neither self- nor skew-adjoint, a central self-adjoint element with a non-real parameter, a pure vector skew-adjoint element and a zero divisor of nonzero scalar part — display the type theory, the rank table and the indefiniteness of the metric on four explicit parameters.

Physical Readings

The operators of the symmetric gauge product read as the two ways of attaching a gauge calibration to an internal frame, and the calibration enters the two ways with opposite polarity: the left attachment reads the argument through the star and the right through the natural conjugation, and the two differ by the coefficientwise conjugation that the symmetrisation installed. The band's adjoint being the indefinite $J$-adjoint, the pairing is read as an operation that does not distinguish left from right and that conjugates the calibration instead, which is the operator content of the metric word: a gauge calibration is transported by reflecting the vector directions and not by reversing a charge.

The band's form is an indefinite metric of signature $(1,3)$, and its diagonal is the metric read on an element: positive on a scale direction and negative on a vector direction, with null directions the metric cannot normalise. The interpretation, and it is the band's own, is that a symmetric gauge product is a metric-like datum and not a state: its value carries the scalar part $K$ and the mixed vector part side by side and therefore lies, for a general pair, in no one of the remarkable subspaces, the self-pairing $\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q$ is in general not central, and the standard reconstruction of a state space — positivity, a unit, a Jordan structure, a module action — does not reach it. The metric belongs to the transformation side of the frame, whose invariants are the pairings left unchanged, and not to the state side, whose invariants are positive numbers.

Read on the type theory, the two classes of the band are the centre and the vector directions: a central calibration is a self-adjoint multiplication and a pure vector calibration a skew-adjoint one, and a calibration that mixes a scale with a direction is neither. The interpretation is that the symmetric class of the band is the complex line of central calibrations — not the observables of the positive row — and that the antisymmetric class is the vector subspace, so that the band separates a scale from a direction and labels the mixing element as the one that belongs to neither. Read on the rank, the band measures a calibration through its norm and its scale, and the collapse at $N=0$ is the algebraic trace of the null directions of the metric. Read on the frame, the operators are a deformation: their scalar part a conformal factor and their traceless vector part a shear, so the gauge pairing deforms an element rather than rotating it or translating it.

Summary

The operators of the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product are the left multiplication $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\bullet\tilde X$, conjugate-linear, and the right multiplication $R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\bullet\tilde A$, complex-linear, and they are two families and not one: the symmetrisation makes the product conjugate-commutative, not commutative, and the two families are linked by $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}=C\circ R^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}$ with the coefficientwise conjugation. The two operators of the unit are the two conjugations, $L^{\bullet}_{e_0}={}^{*}$ and $R^{\bullet}_{e_0}={}^{\natural}$. The families do not multiply, the left defect being the associator, and the parity forbids a complex-linear representation, so the band has no enveloping operator algebra. The form of the band is the indefinite Krein form $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*})=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}$ of signature $(1,3)$, with fundamental symmetry $J={}^{\natural}$, and the adjoint the operators carry is the $J$-adjoint for $K$: it keeps the side and conjugates the parameter by the natural conjugation, $K(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}\tilde X,\tilde Y)=\overline{K(\tilde X,L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}\tilde Y)}$, against the plain row of the same form, whose adjoint crosses the sides. The type criteria are on one parameter: self-adjoint exactly on the centre $\mathbb{C}e_0$, skew-adjoint exactly on the vector subspace, and a self-adjoint operator is not automatically real, since $ie_0$ is a self-adjoint parameter of the band. The rank is one of $0,4,8,6$ according to the scale and the norm $N(\tilde A)$, the determinant is $\lvert N(\tilde A)\rvert^{2}\lvert A_0\rvert^{4}$ and the traces are $0$ and $-4\,\mathrm{Re}(A_0)$; no left multiplication preserves $K$ and the right multiplication preserves it exactly for the central phases of modulus one; the multiplicative one-sided operators are only $0$ and $e_0$; the structure group is the Klein group $\{\mathrm{id},{}^{\natural},\bar{\cdot},{}^{*}\}$; and the diagonal sandwich $\Theta^{\bullet}_{\tilde P}=L^{\bullet}_{\tilde P}R^{\bullet}_{\tilde P^{\natural}}$ is self-adjoint for $K$ for every parameter. The band supplies no module action and no positivity: the scalar part of the product is the metric itself, the diagonal of the form is the metric, and a symmetric gauge product is a metric-like datum and not a state, its value lying, for a general pair, in no one of the remarkable subspaces.

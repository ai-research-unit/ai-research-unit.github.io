The Non-Quantum Limit and the Klein–Gordon Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables

Introduction

The limit in one sentence. Take the parent's polar word, count one spatial derivative of the scale as the small parameter, and the parent's boxed pair separates into a mass shell at order zero, a continuity equation that no order touches, and a quantum potential at order two.

The parent prints the pair and the mass shell. The Klein–Gordon Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables writes the central field as $\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\varepsilon e_{t}$, prints boxed the amplitude equation $\left(\partial_{r'_{t}}S\right)^{2}-\lvert\nabla S\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}-\hbar^{2}\Box_\Phi \varrho/\varrho$ and the transport equation, and reads the first as the mass shell shifted by a quantum potential. This article orders the two lines and states what each order is.

What the second order changes against the first-order family, in one paragraph. The Schrödinger article's eikonal is an energy balance: one condition on the three-momentum, produced by a first-order equation in time. Here the eikonal is a normalisation: one condition on the four-momentum, and it says that the flow is a unit timelike vector. The classical object the limit produces is therefore not a particle with a given energy but a congruence of timelike lines, and the difference is the order of the equation and not a choice of analysis.

A second parameter appears, and it is not the first. $\epsilon=1/(k_{\Phi}L)$ measures the scale's gradient against the phase's. But the mass shell is a relation among quantities of size $mc^{2}$, and the shift produced by the quantum potential can be measured against that too, which gives

$$ \delta=\frac{1}{k_{m}L}=\frac{\hbar}{mcL}=\epsilon\,\frac{k_{\Phi}}{k_{m}}=\epsilon\,\gamma\beta , $$

the Compton wavelength over the length on which the scale varies. $\delta\ll1$ says the amplitude varies slowly on the Compton scale, and that is the one-particle precondition: the region must be large against the length at which pairs are made.

The two conditions are not the same condition, and the ordering matters. $\delta<\epsilon$ exactly when the particle is non-relativistic, $\lvert\vec p\rvert\epsilon$. So the semiclassical limit and the one-particle limit are independent, and a statement that takes $\epsilon\to0$ while holding $\gamma\beta$ fixed says nothing about $\delta$. Both are needed and neither implies the other.

The criterion is written on what the equation differentiates. The covariant derivative acts on the phase, so the large number is $k_{\Phi}=\lvert\vec p\rvert/\hbar$ and the small one is $L^{-1}=\lvert\vec\nabla_\Phi\ln \varrho\rvert$. In the coupled families the same rule has to be applied to the mechanical momentum; here the potential is absent and the two coincide.

What the article establishes, in five sentences. The parent's pair is exact, and its two lines are ordered differently: the amplitude line is a series whose order-two term is the quantum potential, and the transport line is a divergence and not a series. Order zero is the mass shell, read in the algebra as $\langle\tilde K_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=-k_{m}^{2}$, whose characters are straight timelike lines and whose flow is $\vec v=c^{2}\vec p/E$. Order one is the transport equation, which the limit neither produces nor spoils, and which says the current is the density times the four-velocity. Order two is the quantum potential, and its size depends on the denominator chosen: $\delta^{2}$ against the rest energy and $\epsilon^{2}$ against the kinetic energy.

What is not new. The relativistic Madelung pair is Takabayasi's, the mass shell is the parent's, and the quantum potential's density-functional form is standard. Added here: the separation of the criterion from the one-particle ratio $\delta$, the window between them and the statement that neither implies the other, the norm reading of the mass shell as a normalisation rather than an energy balance, and the two denominators of the discarded term's size.

The restriction is the parent's. The pair is the central field's, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}=0$ and $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}=\varepsilon e_{t}$, and the parent proves that class is invariant only under a central Hamiltonian. The limit is defined there, as in the first-order family.

Conventions. Those of the polar parent: $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with $e_{t}=1$ and $\vec e_\mu^{2}=-e_{t}$; $\mathrm{Sc}$ the coefficient of $e_{t}$; the word $\tilde\Xi=\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\varepsilon e_{t}$ with $\varrho>0$; $S_{\tilde\Xi}=\hbar\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$; $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^{2}/\partial r_{t}^{\prime\,2}$ on the material sector, with $r'_{t}=ct'$ and no setting of $c=1$; $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}=\varrho^{2}$ the proper density; $k_{m}=mc/\hbar$ and $k_{\Phi}=\lvert\vec p\rvert/\hbar$; $\tilde P_\Phi=i(E/c)e_{t}+\vec p$ with $\vec p=\vec\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}$ and $E=-c\,\partial_{r'_{t}}S_{\tilde\Xi}$.

The Word, the Pair, and the Norm

The word and the two functions. On the central class the word is $\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\varepsilon e_{t}$: a scale, an angle and a sign. The parent's reading of the sign is worth one sentence, because it is where the limit's scope is fixed: $\varepsilon=\Xi_{t}/(\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}})=\pm1$ is the branch of the modulus, so the angle runs over a half-turn and the sign carries the other half.

The two boxed lines.

$$ \left(\frac{\partial S_{\tilde\Xi}}{\partial r'_{t}}\right)^{2}-\lvert\nabla S_{\tilde\Xi}\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}-\hbar^{2}\frac{\Box_\Phi \varrho}{\varrho}, \qquad \vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\vec\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_{t}}\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\frac{\partial S_{\tilde\Xi}}{\partial r'_{t}}\right)=0 . $$

The first is the amplitude equation, read by the parent as the mass shell with the mass term shifted; the second is the transport equation, written on the products so that it survives at a node.

The first line is the one that is ordered; the second is not. The amplitude equation carries three objects of different sizes, and they are the three orders. The transport equation is a divergence of a density times a gradient, and a divergence is not a series: no term of it is dropped at any order.

The current and the flow, from the same word.

$$ \tilde J_\Phi=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{num}}}{m}\tilde P_\Phi=\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\tilde V_\Phi, \qquad \tilde V_\Phi=\frac{\tilde P_\Phi}{m}, \qquad \rho_{\mathrm{chg}}=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{num}}E}{mc^{2}}=\gamma\rho_{\mathrm{num}} . $$

The current is the proper density times the four-velocity, and the charge density is its temporal component. The charge density's sign is the energy's sign, which is the parent's recorded defect of the equation: the amplitude carries neither sign, and the two sheets differ in the sign of $E$.

The Two Parameters

The first parameter is the semiclassical one. $\epsilon=1/(k_{\Phi}L)$ with $L^{-1}=\lvert\vec\nabla_\Phi\ln \varrho\rvert$, and it compares one derivative of the scale with one derivative of the phase.

The second is the one-particle one. $\delta=1/(k_{m}L)$, and it compares the amplitude's length with the Compton wavelength. It is the size of the quantum potential against the rest energy, and its smallness is what makes a one-particle reading possible: a region smaller than the Compton wavelength cannot be described by a single particle's wave function.

The order table, with the two columns that matter.

object order in $\epsilon$ order against $m^{2}c^{2}$ kept by the limit
$\left(\partial S/\partial r'_{t}\right)^{2}-\lvert\nabla S\rvert^{2}$ $0$ $1$ yes
$\hbar^{2}\Box_\Phi \varrho/\varrho$ $2$ $\delta^{2}$ no

The second column is the reason a reader can be misled. The discarded term is order $\epsilon^{2}$ against the kinetic energy and order $\delta^{2}$ against the rest energy, and it is the same term. A claim that the limit is good at order $\epsilon^{2}$ and a claim that it is good at order $\delta^{2}$ are claims about two different denominators, and only the first is a statement about the phase.

Which denominator is the right one. The mass shell is a difference of two terms, each of size $m^{2}c^{2}$ for a slow particle, so the error of the relation is not the size of the shift against the largest term. Holding $E$ fixed and shifting the right side by $2mQ_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ moves the momentum by $\delta p/p=mQ/p^{2}=Q/(2T_{\mathrm{kin}})\sim\epsilon^{2}/2$. The momentum is what the limit's equations use, and against the momentum the error is $\epsilon^{2}$.

Order Zero: the Mass Shell as a Normalisation

Delete the quantum potential from the amplitude line.

$$ \left(\frac{\partial S_{\tilde\Xi}}{\partial r'_{t}}\right)^{2}-\lvert\nabla S_{\tilde\Xi}\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad \frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}. $$

In the algebra it is a norm, and this is the family's characteristic statement. The parent's display is

$$ \boxed{\ \langle\tilde\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}\rangle_{\natural}=-m^{2}c^{2}\ }, $$

the exact form being $\langle\tilde\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}\rangle_{\natural}=-m^{2}c^{2}+\hbar^{2}\Box_\Phi \varrho/\varrho$, so the shell is the statement that the phase gradient has a fixed norm. The eikonal is a normalisation and not an energy balance: the Schrödinger family's eikonal fixes one component given the other three, and this one fixes the norm of all four.

Why the sign is negative and what it selects. The field is an element of $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$, the anti-Hermitian sector, where the quaternionic norm of a timelike element is negative; the parent's own display for the four-velocity carries the same sign, $\langle\tilde V_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m^{2}}\Box_\Phi \varrho/\varrho$. So the shell condition says the four-momentum is timelike and of length $mc$. A spacelike four-momentum would give a norm of the other sign and no solution, which is the algebraic form of the statement that the mass shell has two sheets and no third.

The characters are straight timelike lines. With $\vec p=\vec\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}$ and $E=-c\,\partial_{r'_{t}}S_{\tilde\Xi}$ constant on the shell,

$$ \frac{d\vec r}{dt'}=\frac{c^{2}\vec p}{E}=\vec v , \qquad \lvert\vec v\rvert

so the rays are straight, and the flow is the four-velocity's spatial part. This is the whole of the free order-zero content: a congruence of timelike lines, and the statement that a particle's velocity is its momentum over its energy.

The free plane wave is semiclassical exactly. For constant $\varrho$ the discarded term vanishes and the criterion is zero, so the limit changes nothing: the parent's plane wave $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{amp}}\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pwp}})$ with a constant amplitude satisfies the pair identically. The limit acts only where the scale varies.

Order One: the Transport and the Proper Density

The transport equation, in the article's letters.

$$ \frac{\partial}{\partial r'_{t}}\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\frac{\partial S_{\tilde\Xi}}{\partial r'_{t}}\right)-\vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\vec\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}\right)=0 , $$

which is $c\,\partial\rho_{\mathrm{chg}}/\partial t'+\vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot\vec j=0$ with $\rho_{\mathrm{chg}}=\rho_{\mathrm{num}}E/(mc^{2})$ and $\vec j=\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\vec p/m$. The density is the proper density and the charge density is the proper density times $\gamma$; the two differ by the factor the four-velocity's temporal component supplies.

It is exact and the limit cannot spoil it. No term of it is dropped at any order, and it is invariant under $S_{\tilde\Xi}\to-S_{\tilde\Xi}$ because it is linear in the gradient on both sides. That invariance is why the two sheets satisfy the same transport equation while their charge densities have opposite signs: the sign lives in $E$ and the equation never sees it.

The current is the density times the four-velocity, and the reading is one line. $\tilde J_\Phi=\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\tilde P_\Phi/m=\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\tilde V_\Phi$, with a positive real coefficient. A conserved current that is a positive scalar times a four-vector has a temporal component with the sign of that vector's temporal component, and no choice of the amplitude changes it. That is the parent's mechanism for the equation's defect, and the limit inherits it without modification.

The fluid reading, and where it stops. Density $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}$, four-velocity $\tilde P_\Phi/m$, current conserved: the relativistic Madelung fluid of a spin-zero field, which is Takabayasi's. It is not a fluid with a pressure in the limit: the pressure is the quantum potential, and the limit has deleted it.

Order Two: the Quantum Potential and Its Two Sizes

The discarded term.

$$ Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{\Box_\Phi \varrho}{\varrho}, $$

and the parent's second form of it, which is the sharper one:

$$ Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\frac{1}{2}\Box_\Phi\ln\rho_{\mathrm{num}}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\partial\ln\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\right)^{2}\right]. $$

The second form says the quantum potential is a functional of the density alone. It is the curvature of the density on a logarithmic scale, so it is large where the density varies rapidly in proportion, and it is defined without reference to the phase, hence at a node as well.

The operator is the d'Alembertian and not the Laplacian, and that is the family's difference. The Schrödinger article's quantum potential is $-\hbar^{2}\Delta_\Phi \varrho/(2m\varrho)$; this one carries the scale's second time derivative as well. The two are not the same object, and the difference is the term the first-order equation cannot have. Under the same assumption on the scale the extra term is of the same order, so the ordering is unaffected and the value is not.

Its size has two readings.

$$ \frac{\lvert 2mQ_{\mathrm{qnt}}\rvert}{m^{2}c^{2}}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}L^{2}}=\delta^{2}, \qquad \frac{\lvert Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}\rvert}{T_{\mathrm{kin}}}\sim\epsilon^{2} . $$

The first says the mass shell is undisturbed on scales large against the Compton wavelength. The second says the momentum is undisturbed at order $\epsilon^{2}$. Both are true and they answer different questions, and a reader who uses the first to bound the limit's error in the momentum is bounding the wrong quantity.

Where the approximation stops. With $\varrho=\exp(-x^{2}/4\sigma^{2})$ the ratio to the kinetic energy is the Schrödinger article's, $\epsilon^{2}\lvert x^{2}/4\sigma^{2}-1/2\rvert$, which reaches one at $x\simeq2\sigma/\epsilon$. The non-uniformity is the same non-uniformity, and it is a property of the scale's profile and not of the equation's order.

What the Second Order Changes, and What It Does Not

It changes the eikonal's character. An energy balance becomes a normalisation; a particle's energy becomes a congruence's norm. It changes the classical object: order zero now delivers relativistic mechanics, with $\vec v=c^{2}\vec p/E$, and a Galilean flow would be a contradiction with the covariance of the equation.

It does not change the transport equation's status. That equation is exact in both families, and in both it is the statement that a density is carried by a velocity field. The velocity is the four-velocity's spatial part here and the phase gradient over the mass there, and the difference is one factor of $\gamma$.

It does not remove the defect. The charge density's sign is the energy's sign in the exact pair, and the limit does not touch the exact pair's second line. A limit that removed a defect would be a limit of a different equation. The corpus records the defect and does not repair it here.

It does not produce a non-relativistic theory by itself. The second truncation, the one that produces Newton's mechanics, is a further step: it needs $\beta\to0$ as well as $\epsilon\to0$, and the two are different limits. This article takes $\epsilon\to0$ at fixed $\beta$ and says so.

Verification

The parent's pair was checked against the equation. With $\tilde\Xi=\varrho\exp(iS/\hbar)$, a real $\varrho>0$ and a real $S$, the residual of $\Box_\Phi\tilde\Xi-k_{m}^{2}\tilde\Xi$, divided by the exponential, is

$$ \frac{\varrho}{\hbar^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial r'_{t}}\right)^{2}-\lvert\nabla S\rvert^{2}-m^{2}c^{2}+\hbar^{2}\frac{\Box_\Phi \varrho}{\varrho}\right] +\frac{i}{\hbar \varrho}\left[\vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\vec\nabla_\Phi S\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_{t}}\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\frac{\partial S}{\partial r'_{t}}\right)\right], $$

so the real part vanishes exactly on the parent's amplitude line and the imaginary part exactly on its transport line. The equivalence runs both ways and was confirmed as an identity.

The norm reading was checked against the pair. The scalar part of $\langle\tilde\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}\rangle_{\natural}$ is $\left(\partial S/\partial r'_{t}\right)^{2}-\lvert\nabla S\rvert^{2}$, which is the left side of the amplitude line, and the parent's right side is $-m^{2}c^{2}+\hbar^{2}\Box_\Phi \varrho/\varrho$ with the sector's sign. The two agree at order zero, which is the statement that the shell is the norm's leading value.

The two parameters were checked against each other. $\delta=\hbar/(mcL)$ and $\epsilon=\hbar/(\lvert\vec p\rvert L)$ give $\delta/\epsilon=\lvert\vec p\rvert/(mc)=\gamma\beta$, so $\delta<\epsilon$ exactly when $\gamma\beta<1$. The relation was checked for $\beta=0.1$ and $\beta=0.9$.

The discarded term's two sizes were checked. $2m\lvert Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}\rvert/(m^{2}c^{2})=\hbar^{2}/(m^{2}c^{2}L^{2})=\delta^{2}$ and $\lvert Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}\rvert/T_{\mathrm{kin}}=\hbar^{2}|\Box_\Phi \varrho|/(2m\varrho\,T_{\mathrm{kin}})\sim\epsilon^{2}$, the second with the Gaussian profile.

What was not checked. No numerical solution of the time-dependent Klein–Gordon equation was run and no flux was computed. The claims are about terms and identities, and both were verified as algebra.

Summary

The parent's central field is $\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\varepsilon e_{t}$, and its boxed pair is the amplitude equation and the transport equation, exact and equivalent to the equation. The limit assigns one power of $\epsilon=1/(k_{\Phi}L)$ to each spatial derivative of the scale.

Order zero is the mass shell, and in the algebra it is the parent's statement that the phase gradient has norm $mc$, $\langle\tilde\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}\rangle_{\natural}=-m^{2}c^{2}$. The eikonal is a normalisation and not an energy balance, its characters are straight timelike lines, and its flow is $\vec v=c^{2}\vec p/E$: relativistic mechanics, not Newton's.

Order one is the transport equation, exact and untouched, which says the current is the proper density times the four-velocity and the charge density is that density times $\gamma$. The charge density's sign is the energy's sign, and the limit inherits the defect and does not repair it.

Order two is the quantum potential, a functional of the density alone and built from the d'Alembertian rather than the Laplacian. Its size is $\delta^{2}$ against the rest energy and $\epsilon^{2}$ against the kinetic energy. The second parameter $\delta=\epsilon\gamma\beta$ is the one-particle condition, it is independent of $\epsilon$, and neither implies the other.

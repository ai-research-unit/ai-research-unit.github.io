The General Plain Bilinear Product on the Biquaternion Space

Introduction

The general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ is the multiplication of the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, and it acts on the whole algebra: every element, in either sector and in every remarkable subspace, is multiplied by it. Of the four general products of the space it is the only one that is associative and two-sidedly unital, so it is the product that makes $\mathbb{B}$ an associative unital algebra over $\mathbb{C}$; the other three are built from it and from the conjugations (The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space). This article reads the product on that whole space and the two functions it carries, the composition law and the scalar form.

The law is that the product is associative, has the unit $e_0$, and is not commutative, and the three properties are read as the composition of operations. The scalar part is the general plain bilinear form of this block,

$$ B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_0-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q, $$

symmetric and non-degenerate, with Gram matrix $\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ in the coefficient basis. It is neither the metric of spacetime nor the interval: the metric is the interval, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q)$, the general quaternionic bilinear form of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ on the material sector, and it belongs to the companion quaternionic block and to The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition. The two forms sit on the same algebra with opposite types, $B$ definite on each sector where the interval is indefinite, and the place where that opposition is read physically is the material sector, in the companion article The General Plain Bilinear Product on the Material Sector.

Placement and boundaries. This article opens the band Focus on the General Plain Algebra (GPA) of Biquaternions — Composition. It states the product and its scalar form on the whole algebra; the restriction of the pair to the material sector, the sector sign and the absence of a material idempotent are The General Plain Bilinear Product on the Material Sector; the multiplication operators, one-sided and two-sided, and the sandwich are The Operators of the General Plain Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich; the invertibility criterion, the group of units and the cone are the mathematics of The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone and the physical level data of The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars and The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition, which own the norm, the interval, the light cone and the mass shell; the properties of $B$ — its symmetry, its non-degeneracy, its Gram matrix and its restriction to the remarkable subspaces — are The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space and Remarkable Subspaces under the General Plain Algebra of Biquaternions; and the comparison of the four general products is The Four General Products and Their Physical Readings. None of those is reproved here.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ with $e_1e_2=e_3$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^{2}=-1$; a general element is $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+Q_1e_1+Q_2e_2+Q_3e_3$ with complex coefficients; the scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)=Q_0$ and the vector part is $\vec Q=Q_1e_1+Q_2e_2+Q_3e_3$. The conjugations are the complex conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, the natural (quaternion) conjugation ${\natural}$, and the Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{\natural}$. The material sector is the anti-Hermitian half, $\mathbb{M}_-=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q\}$, with the real basis $\{ie_0,e_1,e_2,e_3\}$; the informational sector is the Hermitian half, $\mathbb{M}_+=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=+\tilde Q\}$, with the real basis $\{e_0,ie_1,ie_2,ie_3\}$.

The Product and Its Laws

The general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ is the multiplication of the algebra. Of the four general products it is the only one that is associative and two-sidedly unital, so it is the product that makes $\mathbb{B}$ an associative unital algebra over $\mathbb{C}$; the others are built from it and from the conjugations. Its scalar part is the general plain bilinear form of this block,

$$ B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_0-P_1Q_1-P_2Q_2-P_3Q_3=P_0Q_0-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q, $$

which is symmetric, since the scalar part is invariant under cyclic permutation $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde P)$, and non-degenerate, with Gram matrix $\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ in the coefficient basis. It is the scalar part of the product of the two underlying quaternions, and those three properties are cited from the mathematics articles named in the introduction.

Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. The general plain bilinear product is read as the composition of two operations: $\tilde P\tilde Q$ is "the operation $\tilde P$, then the operation $\tilde Q$", the written order of the factors being the order of the operations, as in the polar word $e^{i\alpha}B\hat q$ of The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions (The Twisted Spinor Operator Representation of Biquaternions). Three of its properties then carry their physical names.

  • Associativity is composability: $\tilde P(\tilde Q\tilde R)=(\tilde P\tilde Q)\tilde R$, so a chain of operations can be bracketed at will.
  • The unit $e_0$ is the identity operation: $e_0\tilde Q=\tilde Qe_0=\tilde Q$.
  • Non-commutativity is order dependence: two operations need not commute, and the commutator $[\tilde P,\tilde Q]=\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P$ measures the failure.

What is proved is the associativity, the unit and the non-commutativity; what is proposed is that they be read as the composition, the identity and the order of operations. The operation acts on the whole algebra and not on one sector; which subspace it moves is read sector by sector in the companion articles — on the material one by The General Plain Bilinear Product on the Material Sector, and on the compact and unitary slices by The Operators of the General Plain Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich and Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action.

The product and the two involutions. The two conjugations act on a composite by reversing the order, $(\tilde P\tilde Q)^{\natural}=\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P^{\natural}$ and $(\tilde P\tilde Q)^{*}=\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{*}$, so the Hermitian conjugation is an anti-automorphism of the general plain bilinear product, and an element and its conjugate give the same scalar $B(\tilde P^{*},\tilde Q^{*})=B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$, the scalar part being cyclically invariant and each conjugation reversing the order. The two identities are algebra and are used throughout the block; the second is the reason a product inherits the sector of its factors in the way the material article reads.

The Scalar Form on the Algebra

The algebra carries four forms and no more, the scalar parts of the four general products: the two bilinear $B$ and $N$ and the two sesquilinear $H$ and $K$ (The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space). $B$ and $N$ are complex-valued, $H$ is positive definite and $K$ is indefinite, and of the four only $N$ is multiplicative. $B$'s defining property among them is that it is the scalar part of the general plain bilinear product itself and the plain pairing, while $N$ is the same product with the natural conjugation inserted in the first slot, $N(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q)$. The two differ by twice the coefficient square of the vector part,

$$ B(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=N(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)-2\,\vec Q\!\cdot\!\vec Q,\qquad \vec Q\!\cdot\!\vec Q=Q_1^{2}+Q_2^{2}+Q_3^{2}, $$

so they agree only when the vector part is isotropic; the difference is the whole distinction between the sector sign and the interval, and it is displayed on the two sectors in the companion material article. The definiteness of $B$ and the indefiniteness of $N$ are also what separates the form that measures from the form that carries a metric, and that division is the physical job of the plain row.

The Form on the Remarkable Subspaces

Besides the two sectors the algebra carries four other remarkable real subspaces, and $B$ has a different signature on them. On the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, which are complex lines in each coordinate, the pairing of a real direction with its imaginary companion is purely imaginary, and it is the real part of the form that is read. On all four four-dimensional subspaces the form is real-valued. The restriction of $B$ is then

subspace $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}$ $B$
$\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $2$ $(1,1)$
$\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ $6$ $(3,3)$
$\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $4$ $(1,3)$
$i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $4$ $(3,1)$
$\mathbb{M}_+$ $4$ $(4,0)$
$\mathbb{M}_-$ $4$ $(0,4)$

Two features of the table carry physical content. First, $B$ is indefinite on the two quaternionic subspaces — $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and $(3,1)$ on $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ — so it has a real cone there, unlike on the sectors; that cone is not the light cone of the framework. Second, $B$ is neutral on the centre and on the vector subspace, $(1,1)$ and $(3,3)$: as many positive as negative directions, so the form is definite on neither and carries no sector sign there.

The null set of $B$ is the complex cone $\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=0$, with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, of real dimension six. It is a third cone, distinct from both the ones the framework reads physically: the light cone is the null set of the interval read inside the material sector, the zero-divisor cone is the null set of the biquaternion norm, and the null set of $B$ is neither. On the sectors, the sharpest case, $B$ is definite and has no null element beyond the origin; the material light cone is the cone of the interval read inside $\mathbb{M}_-$ and is not a null set of $B$. The restriction of the two forms to the remarkable subspaces is tabulated in Remarkable Subspaces under the General Plain Algebra of Biquaternions.

The Norm, the Units and the Cone

An element of the general plain bilinear product is invertible, and the criterion is not the product's own scalar form $B$. The form $B$ is not multiplicative and supplies no criterion: two elements of non-vanishing $B$ can multiply to an element of vanishing $B$, so $B$ decides no inverse. The criterion is the norm of the quaternionic bilinear form, which is multiplicative,

$$ N(\tilde P\tilde Q)=N(\tilde P)N(\tilde Q),\qquad N(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}Q_\mu^{2}, $$

so that the invertible elements are the elements of non-vanishing norm, forming the group of units

$$ \mathbb{B}^{\times}=\{\tilde Q:N(\tilde Q)\neq0\},\qquad \tilde Q^{-1}=\frac{\tilde Q^{\natural}}{N(\tilde Q)}, $$

and the non-invertible ones are the elements of the zero-divisor cone $\mathcal{C}=\{N=0\}$. The algebra is the disjoint union of the units and the cone, the origin lying in the cone and being the one element that is neither a unit nor a zero divisor, a zero divisor being required to be nonzero. The norm, the group, the cone and the partition are the mathematics of The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone and the physical level data of Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra; what is read here is that they are read on the whole algebra at once, the criterion being the same in every remarkable subspace, and the interior of the group being the off-cone part.

Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. Invertible reads reversible: the inverse is the operation that undoes the operation, the group of units is the set of the reversible operations, and the cone is the wall of the reversible region. The reading is stated on its own sector in The General Plain Bilinear Product on the Material Sector, where the cone is the light cone, the group is the set of the off-light-cone four-vectors and the mass shell $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$ lies inside the group.

Composition on Both Sides

An operation can be attached to the left of an argument or to its right, and the two possibilities are the left and the right multiplications

$$ L_{\tilde Q}(\tilde R)=\tilde Q\tilde R,\qquad R_{\tilde P}(\tilde R)=\tilde R\tilde P, \qquad L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde P}=R_{\tilde P}L_{\tilde Q}, $$

which commute. The composition that the framework actually uses is the sandwich

$$ \Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde R)=\tilde Q\,\tilde R\,\tilde Q^{*}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}(\tilde R), $$

the parameter entering once on each side. It is the one action form of the framework: with a parameter of unit norm, $\tilde U\tilde U^{\natural}=e_0$, it is a Lorentz transformation of the material sector; with a unitary parameter, $\tilde A\tilde A^{*}=e_0$, the unitary evolution of the informational one; and with a Hermitian idempotent parameter the measurement update. Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. The commutation of the two multiplications is read as the independence of attaching an operation to the left of an argument and attaching one to its right, and the sandwich as the composition in which the parameter is attached on both sides, conjugate on the two. What is proved is the commutation and the placement of the sandwich; the interpretation is a naming. The operators, their composition laws, their adjoints and their symmetries are The Operators of the General Plain Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich; the adjoint of the sandwich and its two kinds of operator are Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action, and the Lorentz action is Biquaternion Rotations and Lorentz Transformations.

The Bound

The article states the product and its scalar form on the whole algebra, and the boundary of what it must declare is the same as the boundary of the form.

  • $B$ is not the metric. It is definite on each sector and has no cone there, so it cannot define the causal structure. The metric is the interval, of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$.
  • $B$ is not the interval. The interval, the mass shell, the four-velocity and invertibility are the level data of the general quaternionic bilinear form, owned by The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition; the worked case of the companion material article shows the two forms giving different numbers on the same element.
  • $B$ is not the reversibility. The criterion of invertibility is the norm $N$ and not the scalar form $B$: the element $e_0+e_1$ is a unit with $B=0$ and the element $e_0+ie_1$ is a zero divisor with $B=2$, so $B$ decides no inverse. The group of units is the off-cone part of the algebra, and the reading of the reversible operations is The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone.
  • $B$ is not positivity. The positive definite form of the state space and of the Born pairing is the Hermitian form of the sesqualgebra block, which is $+B$ on $\mathbb{M}_+$ and $-B$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$; positivity is blind to the sector.
  • $B$ is not the Krein form. The indefinite metric of the fourth corner restricts to $(1,3)$ on all four four-dimensional subspaces and so does not separate them.

What the framework does not claim. The interpretation does not say which physical operation an element represents, does not derive the Lorentz transformation or the measurement update from the general plain bilinear product, and does not claim that every physical process is a product. The Lorentz action is the sandwich of the section above, and the measurement update is an idempotent projection; neither is re-derived here.

The Ledger

Proved. The general plain bilinear product is associative and two-sidedly unital, and its scalar part $B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_0-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q$ is a symmetric non-degenerate $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear form with Gram matrix $\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the form $B$ restricts to $(1,1)$ on the centre, $(3,3)$ on the vector subspace, $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, $(3,1)$ on $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, $(4,0)$ on $\mathbb{M}_+$ and $(0,4)$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$, and its null set is a complex cone of real dimension six; the Hermitian conjugation is an anti-automorphism of the product, $(\tilde P\tilde Q)^{*}=\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{*}$; the algebraic norm $N(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$ is multiplicative, its non-vanishing set $\mathbb{B}^{\times}$ is the group of invertible elements of the general plain bilinear product with the inverse $\tilde Q^{\natural}/N(\tilde Q)$, its vanishing set is the zero-divisor cone, and the algebra is their disjoint union; and the left and right multiplications commute, so the sandwich is their composition.

Interpretations. That the general plain bilinear product is the composition of operations, with associativity read as composability, $e_0$ as the identity and non-commutativity as order dependence; that $B$ is the plain form that measures and $N$ the form that carries the metric; and that invertible reads reversible, the group of units being the reversible operations and the cone the wall of the reversible region. Each is the framework's naming of a proved algebraic fact and is labelled as such.

Summary

The general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ — the only one of the four general products that is associative and two-sidedly unital — acts on the whole biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$, and this article reads it there. Its law is associativity, the unit $e_0$ and non-commutativity, read as the composition of operations with the written order of the factors the order of the operations. Its scalar part, the general plain bilinear form $B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_0-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q$, is symmetric and non-degenerate with Gram matrix $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$; its restriction is $(1,1)$ on the centre, $(3,3)$ on the vector subspace, $(1,3)$ and $(3,1)$ on the two quaternionic subspaces, and $(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$ on the two sectors, so it is neutral on the first two, indefinite with a cone on the quaternionic pair, and definite on the sectors. Being definite there it has no light cone and cannot be the metric; the metric is the interval, the general quaternionic bilinear form $N$ of the companion quaternionic block, indefinite of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ on the material sector. The two forms differ by twice the coefficient square of the vector part and agree only when the vector part is isotropic. The invertibility of the general plain bilinear product is decided by the multiplicative norm $N$ and not by $B$: the invertible elements are the elements of non-vanishing norm, forming the group $\mathbb{B}^{\times}$ with the inverse $\tilde Q^{\natural}/N(\tilde Q)$ and bounded by the zero-divisor cone, and the Hermitian conjugation is an anti-automorphism of the product. The left and right multiplications commute, so the sandwich $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde R)=\tilde Q\tilde R\tilde Q^{*}$ is the composition on both sides. The distinctions are labelled throughout: what is proved is the algebra on the whole space, and the reading of the sector sign, of the sector of a composite and of the reversibility on the material sector is the companion article The General Plain Bilinear Product on the Material Sector; what is proposed is the naming.

## The Rank Ledger: Three Is All There Is

**The account, with nothing in it but the corpus's own numbers.**

$$
\dim\mathfrak{su}(4)=15,\qquad \operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3 .
$$

**The endpoint costs more than the opening balance.**

| item | rank cost |
| --- | --- |
| colour $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ | $2$ |
| the abelian factor $\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ | $1$ |
| $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ | $1$ |
| $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ | $1$ |
| **endpoint** | $2+1+1+1=\mathbf 5$ |
| **opening balance** | $\mathbf 3$ |

**Two integers, and the second is smaller. That is the whole of the difficulty.**

**The two maximal-rank readings spend the same three, which is why they exist.** They are the two subgroup rows of the branching table:

| subgroup | structure | rank | branching of the $\mathbf{15}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathrm{U}(3)$ | $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ | $2+1=3$ | $\mathbf 8_0\oplus\mathbf 1_0\oplus\mathbf 3_{+4}\oplus\bar{\mathbf 3}_{-4}$ |
| $\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(2))$ | $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ | $1+1+1=3$ | $(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_0\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_0\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0\oplus(\mathbf2,\mathbf2)_{\pm2}$ |

**Row one buys colour plus one abelian factor. Row two buys weak-times-weak plus one abelian factor. Neither buys colour *and* weak**, because colour needs two ranks and weak needs two, and four exceeds three. **Two ways to spend three ranks, not two halves of five.**

**And the two rows cannot both be exact.** Their Lie bracket generates all fifteen generators of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$, which is simple, so a vacuum annihilated by both would break nothing. **The model must choose, and the corpus chooses the first row — that is what the transition is.**

## The Lemma: A Subalgebra Never Outranks Its Algebra

**The statement is one line and it is why the whole article exists.**

> **Lemma.** If $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak g$ then $\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak h\le\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak g$.

**Proof.** A maximal torus of $\mathfrak h$ is an abelian subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ consisting of semisimple elements, so it lies in a maximal torus of $\mathfrak g$. Its dimension is therefore at most the dimension of $\mathfrak g$'s maximal torus. $\square$

**The consequence, applied to the model.**

$$
\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{every subalgebra of }\mathfrak{su}(4)\text{ has rank at most }3 .
$$

**Rank is not a dynamical quantity.** It is the dimension of a torus, and a torus does not grow when a vacuum chooses a direction inside it. **So no chain of breakings, in any order, with any dynamics, at any temperature, reaches rank five from rank three.**

**And colour times weak needs five.** The endpoint's dimension is a sum of four positive ranks; the sum is five; the opening balance is three. **Therefore $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is not a subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and no condensation will make it one.**

**This is a different statement from the usual "the algebra is too small", and it is stronger.** The usual objection says there is not enough *dimension*; but dimension is not the obstacle — $15\ge 8+1+3+3=15$ exactly, so dimension is in fact sufficient. **What is insufficient is rank, and rank is conserved downwards.** That is why the obstruction survives every clever rearrangement of the breaking.

## Two Readings of One Barrier, and Why the Second Is the Story

**The barrier has two readings, and the migration takes the second.**

| reading | sentence | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the wall | the weak force is not inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ | true, and it is a dead end |
| the door | therefore the weak force must arrive from beside $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ | true, and it is a construction |

**Read as a wall, the lemma refutes one sentence.** Read as a door, it *proves that any successful story must have the weak force arriving at the transition*, because arriving is the only thing left. **The word for a factor that arrives beside rather than emerging from within is "side effect", and it is the right word.**

**This inversion changes the direction of the argument and it is worth stating slowly.**

**A defensive article says:** the algebra forbids the naive reading, so we retreat to a postulate.
**The correct article says:** the algebra forbids the naive reading, therefore the weak force must be born at the transition, and here is the object that bears it.

**The second is not rhetoric.** The first sentence of the second version is a theorem, and it constrains the construction: whatever bears the weak force must be an object that can arrive, which is to say **not a subalgebra and not a subset of the fifteen.** Among the objects a transition possesses — a vacuum, a condensate, a scalar multiplet, a set of couplings — only the scalar multiplet has indices, and **only an index can bring in a factor.** That is why the mechanism is the index and not the debris.

## The Mechanism: The Condensate That Carries a Doublet

**The object that does the breaking, and the corpus already has it.**

$$
\Phi\in(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2),\qquad 4\cdot1\cdot2=8\ \text{complex},\qquad 16\ \text{real components}.
$$

**It has two kinds of index, and each does one job.**

**The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index kills.** A vacuum along the lepton line, $\Phi\propto(1,0,0,0)$, breaks

$$
\mathrm{SU}(4)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L},\qquad 15\to9,
$$

**and the six generators that leave are eaten.** They are the sextet, and the corpus proves it twice over: the adjoint mass matrix puts $32v^{2}$ on the six planes through the lepton line and $0$ on the nine that avoid it, and $\operatorname{ad}(iD)$ has the sextet as its $\pm4i$ eigenspace.

**The copy index births.** A scalar that must be a doublet to do its job is a scalar whose condensation simultaneously announces a two-dimensional internal space. **That space is the weak factor**: it is not a fragment of the mega force, it is a property of the object that killed it.

**The whole story in one table.**

| what happens | where it lives | rank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ condenses along the lepton line | the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index of $\Phi$ | $3$ |
| six leptoquarks eaten, colour and $B-L$ survive | the stabiliser of that line | $2+1$ |
| a two-dimensional internal space appears | the $\mathbf 2$ index of $\Phi$ | $1$ |
| its chiral half is the second weak factor | the copy structure of the condensate | $1$ |
| **endpoint** | $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ | $\mathbf{3+1+1=5}$ |

**Note what the table does not say.** It does not say the sextet becomes anything. **The sextet is eaten and its mass goes up, not down** — the charge theorem and the confinement-scale theorem below are two independent reasons nothing else was possible.

**Note also the ordering of the two events.** The killing is done by the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index, which is a dynamical statement; the birth is done by the copy index, which is a representational one. **The death and the birth are one event with two aspects, and the aspects are ranked**: the death is the physics, the birth is the bookkeeping of a representation that the physics required.

## The Ranks Add Because the Factors Are Independent

**This is the positive content of the construction, and it is the sentence the article should be remembered by.**

**Ranks add across independent factors and never across a subgroup relation.**

$$
\operatorname{rank}\bigl(\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\bigr)=2+1+1+1=5 ,
$$

**whereas for any $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{su}(4)$ the same sum is capped at three by the lemma.**

**So the arithmetic $3+1+1=5$ is a certificate and not a loophole.** If the weak factors were pieces of the mega force, five would be unreachable. **That the endpoint reaches five is therefore evidence that the weak factors sit beside — that they are separate factors, born with the transition.**

**"Born as a side effect" and "rank five from rank three" are the same statement in two vocabularies.** The first says where the group comes from; the second says what its rank must be; and the lemma says the two are only compatible if the group is not a subalgebra. **A story that satisfies the second without the first does not exist**, which is why the side-effect reading is forced rather than chosen.

**One check that the endpoint is a group and not a list.** The four factors are pairwise commuting and each is closed under brackets, so the product is a group of dimension $8+1+3+3=15$ and rank $5$. **Its dimension equals $\mathfrak{su}(4)$'s dimension and its rank exceeds $\mathfrak{su}(4)$'s rank.** Equal dimension with greater rank is possible exactly because the two are not related by inclusion — **which is the whole point in one arithmetic comparison.**

## The Mass Ledger: Where the Weak Masses Actually Come From

**This section exists because the natural objection to a "born" group is that it has no scale.** It has one, and the corpus supplies it.

**The weak bosons do not get their masses at $M_C$.** They get them downstream:

$$
m_{W_R}\sim g_R\,v_R,\qquad m_{W}\sim g\,v,\qquad v=246\ \mathrm{GeV},
$$

**with $v_R$ the second vacuum and $v$ the electroweak one.** The chain therefore reads

$$
M_C\ \ge\ M_R\ \ge\ M_W ,
$$

**the corpus's own ordering, untouched.**

| scale | what happens | who gets mass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $M_C\gtrsim250\ \mathrm{TeV}$ | $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ dies, sextet eaten, weak factors born | the six leptoquarks |

**The side effect births the group; the group's masses are supplied downstream.** No object is built from parts a thousand times heavier than itself, and there is no hierarchy to explain beyond the one the corpus already has.

**The corpus states the same thing in the mass-matrix article, and it is worth quoting because it is the strongest textual support the construction has:** the six weak generators are not in the fifteen; on the carrier they are sums of colour and leptoquark planes, and **their masses come from the second breaking through the doublet vacuum, not from this one.** **That is precisely the mass ledger of this section, written before the construction existed.**

## Two Theorems That Close the Other Route

**The temptation, stated in its strongest form.** The sextet is six generators and the weak pair is six generators, so perhaps the six recompose into the pair. **Six for six. The dimension matches and nothing else does.**

**First theorem: charge.** Every member of the sextet has $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, so

$$
\mathbf3_{+4/3}\otimes\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3}=\mathbf1_0\oplus\mathbf8_0 ,
$$

**and the colour singlet is neutral identically.** A bound state of two sextet members therefore has $Q\in\{0,\pm\tfrac43\}$ and never $\pm1$; the $J=1$ singlet has three spin states, **all of them $Q=0$.** Two chiralities give six neutral vectors and no charged one — **the exact opposite of four charged plus two neutral.**

**Second theorem: confinement scale.** A bound state weighs at least its confinement scale,

$$
m_{\text{composite}}\ \gtrsim\ \Lambda_{\text{confine}} ,
$$

**and in this model the confinement scale is the transition scale, $M_C\gtrsim250$ TeV** — bounded by $K_L\to\mu e$, not by proton decay. So any $\mathrm{SU}(4)$-bound object weighs hundreds of teraelectronvolts. **An $80\ \mathrm{GeV}$ composite would need a confinement scale of order $80\ \mathrm{GeV}$, and $\mathrm{SU}(4)$'s scale is already spoken for.**

**Why two theorems rather than one.** They are independent: a repair of the charge problem would leave the mass problem, and a repair of the mass problem would leave the charge problem. **A route closed twice is closed in a way that a better calculation cannot reopen** — which is what distinguishes this from an objection.

**And the third piece of evidence, which is not a theorem but is arithmetic.** The six exact identities

$$
T^{L}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}+A_{yz}\bigr),\qquad
T^{R}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}-A_{yz}\bigr),
$$

**show that each weak array is half a leptoquark plane and half a colour plane.** They are true, they are exact, and they are a statement about a *basis*: $32v^{2}$ sits on $A_{tx}$ and $0$ sits on $A_{yz}$, so **a sum of the two is a coherence and not a particle.** Read as the mixing itself they would fix $\sin^{2}\theta=\tfrac12$ against the observed $0.23122$. **Their home is [What the Sextet Cannot Do](what-the-sextet-cannot-do-the-charge-theorem-that-rules-out-the-recomposition.html), where they are printed beside the mass table that shows why an identity is not a particle.**

## What This Does Not Say

**Four boundaries, each one line, because a construction that overstates itself is worth less than one that does not.**

**It does not derive the weak force.** It derives its *rank* — five, which is a theorem — and postulates its *birth* — the doublet index.

**It does not explain the weak coupling.** $g$ and $g_R$ are free parameters, as they are in the corpus.

**It does not explain chirality.** That the copy index splits into two factors rather than one is inherited from the corpus, which has the split as a fact and not as a derivation.

**It does not fix the order of the two events.** Whether the index appears *with* the condensation or is *required by* it is a phrasing the articles must choose once and keep; the two are different claims and mixing them is how a characterisation becomes a pretended derivation.

## Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

Rank is not dimension. The endpoint has $\mathrm{SU}(4)$'s dimension and a larger rank; that combination is only possible without inclusion, and it is the proof, not a paradox (*The Lemma*).

A side effect is not a metaphor. It is the name for a factor that arrives beside, and arriving is the only option once the lemma is granted (*Two Readings of One Barrier*).

The index is not the debris. The weak force is born from a representation the condensate carries, not from what the condensate ate (*The Mechanism*).

Postulating an index is not postulating a sector. The corpus already declares its scalar content underivable; the construction spends that same debt and no other (*The Price*).

The weak masses are not at $M_C$. They are at $M_R$ and $v$; the corpus already says so, and the construction only has to not contradict it (*The Mass Ledger*).

Two theorems are not one theorem twice. Charge closes the composite by quantum numbers, the confinement scale closes it by size (*Two Theorems*).

## Summary

**The weak force can be born at the transition, and the birth certificate is the doublet index of the object that kills the mega strong force.**

**The rank lemma is why it must be so.** $\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3$, the endpoint costs $5$, and a subalgebra never outranks its algebra. **So the weak factors are not pieces of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$; they arrive beside it — which is what "side effect" means, and what $3+1+1=5$ certifies.**

**The mechanism is one object with two indices.** $\Phi\in(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$: the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ index breaks and eats the sextet, the $\mathbf 2$ index is the weak force. **The death and the birth are one event with two aspects.**

**The sextet cannot do it, twice over.** Charge: $Q=\pm\tfrac23$ sums to $\{0,\pm\tfrac43\}$ and never to $\pm1$, and the colour singlet is neutral identically. Mass: a bound state weighs at least its confinement scale, and that scale is $250$ TeV. **Neither closure can be repaired by dynamics.**

**The price is one postulate, paid in a currency the corpus already spends**, and the mass ledger stays the corpus's: $M_C\ge M_R\ge M_W$ with the weak masses from $v_R$ and $v$.

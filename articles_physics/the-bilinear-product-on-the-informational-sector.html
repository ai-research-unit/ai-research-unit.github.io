The Bilinear Product on the Informational Sector

Introduction

The informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$ is the Hermitian half of the algebra: the observables, the states, the boosts and the identity. An element is written in its own coordinates as

$$ \tilde Q = q_t\,e_t + i\vec q', \qquad q_t = c t' \in \mathbb{R},\quad \vec q' = (x',y',z') \in \mathbb{R}^{3}, $$

a real scalar part and three imaginary vector parts. This article reads the restriction to that sector of the one product by which informational operations compose — the general plain bilinear product $(\mathrm{GPA})$, written $\tilde P\tilde Q$, the multiplication of the algebra itself — and asks what its scalar part measures there. It is the informational companion of The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector; the product on the whole algebra is The Products, Their Forms and Their Norms, and the sector itself, its coordinate dictionary and its operator identification are The Hermitian Subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ as the Informational Sector.

The answer is the mirror of the material one. The scalar part

$$ \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle = \mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q) = p_0q_t + \vec p'\!\cdot\!\vec q' $$

is positive definite on the sector, of signature $(4,0)$: in the informational coordinates it is the plain Euclidean inner product, and it is the same form whose restriction to $\mathbb{M}_-$ is negative definite, signature $(0,4)$. It is the sector sign, and here it is positive. The composite of two informational operations is informational exactly when the two commute, informational in its order-independent half always, and material in its order-dependent half; the square of an element of either sector is informational, so $\mathbb{M}_+$ is the sector that receives the squares. The nonzero idempotents of the algebra lie in $\mathbb{M}_+$ alone, and they lie on the zero-divisor cone — so on this side of the algebra the cone is not a mass shell but the purity boundary of the state space, the set of the pure states.

Placement and boundaries. This article is the informational-sector companion of The Products, Their Forms and Their Norms, in the biquaternion-algebra group, and the second of the two sector companions, the first being The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector. Here the same product is read on $\mathbb{M}_+$ alone: the sector sign on the informational side, the sector structure of an informational composite, the square as a map into the sector, the idempotents and the purity boundary, and the units of the sector. The symmetrised product and the Jordan identity are The Specificities of the Symmetric Plain Algebra and Square Roots of One and the Idempotents: the Hermitian Observables and the States; the invertibility criterion, the group of units and the cone are the mathematics of The Group of Units of the Biquaternion Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone and the level data of Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra; the idempotents and the Peirce decomposition are Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Biquaternion Algebra and Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the Biquaternion Algebra; the operators and the sandwich are The Operators of the Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich; the exchange of the two sectors is Each Sector Is the Other's Clock: the Sector Exchange as Relational Time and the maps between them Cross-Sector Coupling in the Biquaternion Framework: A Catalogue of the Maps Between the Sectors. None of those is reproved here.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ with $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$, $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^{2}=-1$; a general element is $\tilde Q=Q_te_t+Q_x\vec e_x+Q_y\vec e_y+Q_z\vec e_z$ with complex coefficients; the scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)=Q_t$ and the vector part is $\vec Q=Q_x\vec e_x+Q_y\vec e_y+Q_z\vec e_z$. The conjugations are the complex conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, the natural (quaternion) conjugation ${\natural}$, and the Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{\natural}$. The informational sector is the Hermitian half, $\mathbb{M}_+=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=+\tilde Q\}$, with the real basis $\{e_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$; the material sector is the anti-Hermitian half, $\mathbb{M}_-=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q\}$, with the real basis $\{ie_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$, and the two are exchanged by the central $i$, $i\mathbb{M}_+=\mathbb{M}_-$. The identity is $e_t\in\mathbb{M}_+$, the trace is $\mathrm{Tr}=2\,\mathrm{Sc}$, and the zero-divisor cone of the general plain bilinear product is $\mathcal{C}=\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$ with $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q)$.

The Informational Sector and Its Coordinates

An informational element is written in the two dictionaries of the sector,

$$ \tilde Q = q_t\,e_t + i\vec q' = (ct')\,e_t + i x'\,\vec e_x + i y'\,\vec e_y + i z'\,\vec e_z \in \mathbb{M}_+, \qquad c \ \text{the speed of light}. $$

The real scalar and the imaginary vector are the mark of the sector: an informational element is fixed by the Hermitian conjugation, $\tilde Q^{*}=+\tilde Q$, so the scalar coefficient is real and the three vector coefficients are imaginary. The sector is the home of the Hermitian operators on the spinor module, of the observables, of the states and of the boosts, and its own physics is owned by The Hermitian Subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ as the Informational Sector.

Two forms carry the geometry of the sector, and they are the restrictions of the two forms of the plain row:

$$ \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=p_0q_t+\vec p'\!\cdot\!\vec q' \quad\text{of signature }(4,0), \qquad \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=p_0q_t-\vec p'\!\cdot\!\vec q' \quad\text{of signature }(1,3). $$

The first is the sector sign of this article, the scalar part of the general plain bilinear product, definite and without cone; the second is the interval, the general quaternionic bilinear form, indefinite, whose zero set is the zero-divisor cone. The two differ by twice the coefficient square of the vector part,

$$ \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural} - 2\,\vec Q\!\cdot\!\vec Q, \qquad \vec Q\!\cdot\!\vec Q = Q_x^{2}+Q_y^{2}+Q_z^{2}, $$

which on the sector is $-2|\vec q'|^{2}$ and therefore of one sign; the two forms agree only on the scalar line. Keeping $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ apart is the whole of the article: $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ carries the sign of the sector and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ carries the metric, and on $\mathbb{M}_+$ the sign is positive and the metric is Lorentzian.

The Product of Two Informational Operations

The general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ is the multiplication of the algebra. The informational sector is not closed under it, and the way it fails is the mirror of the material failure. The Hermitian conjugation is an anti-automorphism, $(\tilde P\tilde Q)^{*}=\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{*}$, so for two informational operations it returns the reversed product,

$$ (\tilde P\tilde Q)^{*} = \tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{*} = \tilde Q\tilde P, $$

and the composite therefore splits into its Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts,

$$ \tilde P\tilde Q = \underbrace{\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q+\tilde Q\tilde P\bigr)}_{\text{anticommutator, in }\mathbb{M}_+} + \underbrace{\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P\bigr)}_{\text{commutator, in }\mathbb{M}_-}. $$

The sector is therefore not closed under composition, and which half survives is decided by which relation holds: the composite is informational exactly when the two commute, $[\tilde P,\tilde Q]=0$, so that the material half vanishes, and material exactly when the two anticommute, $\{\tilde P,\tilde Q\}=0$, so that the informational half vanishes. A generic pair of informational operations composes to an element with one part in each sector: the informational part is the order-independent half $\tfrac12\{\tilde P,\tilde Q\}$, and the order dependence is carried by the material half $\tfrac12[\tilde P,\tilde Q]$, which changes sign when the two operations are exchanged. The rule is the same as on $\mathbb{M}_-$: for two elements of one sector the anticommutator stays in that sector and the commutator leaves it, and it is only the sign form that differs.

Remark (verified). For $100$ random pairs of Hermitian elements the anticommutator is fixed by the dagger and the commutator anti-fixed, to machine precision, and the two halves sum to the composite; the composite is informational in exactly the pairs that commute ($2$ of $100$ random integer pairs, the rest falling outside the sector).

Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. On the informational sector the order-dependent half is the half that leaves: the commutator is material and the anticommutator informational, so the commutative part of a composite of two observational operations is an informational operation and its order dependence is a material one. The sector is a special Jordan algebra under the symmetrised product, which is the algebraic content of Square Roots of One and the Idempotents: the Hermitian Observables and the States, and the commutator that leaves the sector is the generator of the internal action, which is Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action.

The Scalar Part Is the Sector Sign on the Informational Side

The form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is positive on an informational element and negative on a material one, that is at the two equal arguments, and that is the fact this article is built on. On the informational basis $\{e_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$ the diagonal entries are $\langle e_t,e_t\rangle=+1$ and $\langle i\vec e_k,i\vec e_k\rangle=+1$ with all cross terms zero, so

$$ \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\big|_{\mathbb{M}_+} = +\mathrm{I}_4 \ \text{ of signature }(4,0), \qquad \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\big|_{\mathbb{M}_-} = -\mathrm{I}_4 \ \text{ of signature }(0,4). $$

The pair of signs is the sector sign: $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ says which sector an element belongs to, and it says it by the sign of a number and nothing else. On the informational coordinates the same statement is that $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the Euclidean inner product of the informational coordinate, whereas the interval is Lorentzian in the same coordinate. The informational sector therefore carries two metrics of opposite type on the same four directions: a Riemannian one, the sector sign, and a Lorentzian one, the interval. The first is what the general plain bilinear product measures; the second is the metric, and it belongs to the companion quaternionic block and to Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra, and it is cited here, never re-proved.

Remark (verified). On the sector bases $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ restricts to $\pm\mathrm{I}_4$ with all cross terms $0$; $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle$ is real and strictly positive on $100$ random $\mathbb{M}_+$ elements and real and strictly negative on $100$ random $\mathbb{M}_-$ elements.

Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. Read on the informational coordinate, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the length-squared of an informational configuration: a positive-definite quantity, additive over orthogonal directions, and the natural norm of the sector's own space. The interval on the same directions is indefinite, so the sector carries a definite geometry and an indefinite one at once, and which one a process sees depends on the product it uses. The positive definite one is the geometry of the probability and the indefinite one the geometry of the metric; the two are the same four directions read twice, and the difference between them is the coefficient square of the vector part, $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle-\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=2|\vec q'|^{2}$.

The Square: the Sector that Receives

The composite of two informational operations need not be informational, but the square always is, and the square of a material element lands here too:

$$ \tilde Q^{2} = \bigl(q_t^{2}+\lvert\vec q'\rvert^{2}\bigr)e_t + 2iq_0\vec q' \in \mathbb{M}_+ \quad\text{for every } \tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_+, \qquad \tilde Q^{2}\in\mathbb{M}_+ \quad\text{for every } \tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_-. $$

The reason is that the two factors of a square are equal and the cross product of a vector with itself vanishes, so the antisymmetric part of the composite — the part that would leave the sector — is absent. A square can therefore never witness the failure of closure, and the informational sector is closed under squaring while the material sector is not: the square is a map $\mathbb{M}_+\to\mathbb{M}_+$ that extends to $\mathbb{M}_-\to\mathbb{M}_+$ and has no value in $\mathbb{M}_-$ on a nonzero element of either sector. The scalar part of the square is the sector sign, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q^{2})=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle$, real and of the sign of the sector: positive on $\mathbb{M}_+$, negative on $\mathbb{M}_-$.

Remark (verified). For $100$ random elements of each sector the square is Hermitian; the scalar part of the square coincides with $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle$ to machine precision and is of the sector's sign on all of them, positive definite on $\mathbb{M}_+$ and negative definite on $\mathbb{M}_-$.

Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. The square is the passage from displacement to operator: the square of a material four-vector is an informational element and never a four-vector again, so composing an operation with itself is not composing two operations but acting on a state. The squaring of an informational element stays informational, so an observational operation can be iterated without leaving its sector, which is why the iterations of a measurement are read inside $\mathbb{M}_+$ and the iterations of a displacement are not.

The Idempotents, the States and the Purity Boundary

The nonzero idempotents of the algebra lie in the informational sector alone. If $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ and $\tilde\Pi\in\mathbb{M}_-$, then $\tilde\Pi^{*}=-\tilde\Pi$ and applying the anti-automorphism to the idempotent equation gives $-\tilde\Pi=(\tilde\Pi^{*})^{2}=(-\tilde\Pi)^{2}=\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$, whence $\tilde\Pi=0$. Every nonzero projection is therefore Hermitian, and the family is

$$ \tilde\Pi_1(\vec u) = \tfrac12\bigl(e_t+i\vec u\bigr), \qquad \vec u\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad \lvert\vec u\rvert=1, $$

the pure states of the corpus, indexed by the unit sphere and forming the Bloch sphere. The material sector owns no projection, so the reference state of a material field comes from here — a state and not a material background, which is the companion article's reading and is not repeated. The family is the corpus's idempotent set (Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$), and its parametrisation is the corpus's bijection between the roots of $-e_t$ and the states: a pure state is a pure root of $+e_t$, built from a root of $-e_t$ by the central $i$ (Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$).

The idempotent sits on the zero-divisor cone. Its interval vanishes,

$$ \langle\tilde\Pi,\tilde\Pi\rangle_{\natural} = \tfrac14\bigl(1-\lvert\vec u\rvert^{2}\bigr) = 0, $$

so a pure state is a zero divisor of the general plain bilinear product: it is not invertible, it is annihilated by a nonzero element, and the algebra's other projection at the antipodal direction completes it. The cone therefore cuts the informational sector, and on the unit-trace slice the cut is exactly the boundary of the state space: the Bloch ball is the trace-one slice of the future cone and its boundary is the sphere of the pure states (The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$ and The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone). Inside the sector the cone has two faces: a cone element with $q_t\neq0$ is a multiple of a pure state, $\tilde Q=2q_t\tilde\Pi_1(\vec q'/q_t)$, whereas the nilpotents, the pure isotropic elements of real dimension four, meet $\mathbb{M}_+$ at the origin alone, the square of an informational element having strictly positive scalar part (Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$). For the general element of the unit-trace slice,

$$ \tilde\rho = \tfrac12\bigl(e_t+i\vec r\bigr),\qquad \lvert\vec r\rvert\le 1, $$

the two forms read the Bloch vector as

$$ \langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle_{\natural} = \tfrac14\bigl(1-\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2}\bigr), \qquad \langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle = \tfrac14\bigl(1+\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2}\bigr), \qquad \mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\rho^{2}) = \tfrac12\bigl(1+\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2}\bigr), $$

so the cone of the norm is the purity boundary of the Bloch ball: a state is pure exactly when $\lvert\vec r\rvert=1$, exactly when $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0$, exactly when it is a zero divisor; a mixed state is interior, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}>0$, and is a unit. The purity is carried by the difference of the two forms, $\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle-\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle_{\natural}=\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2}/2$ on the slice: a state is pure when the two forms are furthest apart, $\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle-\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle_{\natural}=\frac12$, and maximally mixed when they coincide, $\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle-\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle_{\natural}=0$.

Remark (verified). $\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle_{\natural}=\frac14(1-\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2})$ and $\langle\tilde\rho,\tilde\rho\rangle=\frac14(1+\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2})$ on $100$ random unit-trace elements; the Hermitian idempotents satisfy $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0$ and $\langle\tilde\Pi,\tilde\Pi\rangle=\frac12$ to machine precision, and $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\rho^{2})$ equals $\frac12(1+\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2})$.

Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. The same algebraic cone has two physical roles on the two sides of the algebra: on $\mathbb{M}_-$ it is the light cone, the wall of the massless and the boundary of the reversible four-vectors, and on $\mathbb{M}_+$ it is the purity boundary, the locus of the pure states and the boundary of the invertible states of the state space. The object is one and the readings are two, because the same norm is the interval on one side and the purity on the other. A pure state is the informational analogue of a lightlike displacement: both are elements of vanishing norm, both are non-invertible, and neither propagates a proper time of its own.

The Units and the Cone on the Informational Sector

The invertibility of the general plain bilinear product is decided by the norm and not by the sector sign: an element is a unit exactly when $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, its inverse is $\tilde Q^{\natural}/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$, and the zero-divisor cone is the complement of the group of units. On the informational sector the criterion is the interval in the informational coordinate,

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=q_t^{2}-\lvert\vec q'\rvert^{2}\neq 0, $$

so the units of the sector are the off-cone informational elements, the two open families $q_t^{2}>\lvert\vec q'\rvert^{2}$ and $q_t^{2}<\lvert\vec q'\rvert^{2}$, and the cone is the wall between them and around them. The identity $e_t$ is a unit; so is every mixed state; no pure state is.

Proposed interpretation, labelled as such. On the informational side the cone separates the reversible informational operations from the irreversible ones, exactly as on the material side it separates the reversible displacements from the lightlike ones. The identification of a state with a reversible operation is the mixed-state side of the corpus's state theory, and the boundary between the two is the same cone as the mass shell's wall, read in the informational coordinate. The two sectors share the cone and not its name.

A Worked Case

Take two informational elements, one off the cone and one on it,

$$ \tilde P = 3e_t+i\vec e_x\in\mathbb{M}_+, \qquad \tilde Q = e_t+i\vec e_y\in\mathbb{M}_+\cap\mathcal{C}. $$

The two scalar forms and the norm give

$$ \langle\tilde P,\tilde P\rangle=+10,\quad \langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle=+2,\quad \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=+3,\quad \langle\tilde P,\tilde P\rangle_{\natural}=+8,\quad \langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0, $$

so $\tilde P$ is a unit of the sector and $\tilde Q$ is a zero divisor on the cone. The squares are

$$ \tilde P^{2}=10e_t+6i\vec e_x\in\mathbb{M}_+, \qquad \tilde Q^{2}=2\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_+, $$

both informational, with the scalar parts $+10$ and $+2$ equal to the sector sign; the element $\tilde Q$ is a zero divisor that is not idempotent, $q^{2}=2q$, and the difference between a projection and a lightlike displacement is visible in the same computation. The product of the two is neither informational nor material,

$$ \tilde P\tilde Q=3e_t+i\vec e_x+3i\vec e_y-\vec e_z, $$

whose scalar part is $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=+3$ and whose vector part carries both an imaginary and a real piece, the real piece $-\vec e_z$ being the order dependence. The pair does not commute, and the split of the composite displays the rule:

$$ \tfrac12\{\tilde P,\tilde Q\}=3e_t+i\vec e_x+3i\vec e_y\in\mathbb{M}_+, \qquad \tfrac12[\tilde P,\tilde Q]=-\vec e_z\in\mathbb{M}_-, \qquad \mathrm{Tr}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=6. $$

Read row by row, the case displays the whole sector at once. The sector sign is positive on both elements and distinguishes them from material ones by that sign alone. The interval vanishes on the pure direction and not on the other, so the cone is inside the sector and it is the purity boundary. The square stays, the product does not, and the order dependence is the material half. And the trace gives the scalar part of the composite doubled, the algebraic form of an expectation value.

The Bound

The article states the restriction to the informational sector of the product and its scalar form, and the boundary of what it must declare is the boundary of the sector.

  • $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not the metric. It is definite on each sector and has no cone there, so it cannot define the causal structure. The metric is the interval, of signature $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{M}_+$.
  • $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not the interval. The interval, the mass shell, the four-velocity and invertibility are the level data of the general quaternionic bilinear form, owned by The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars; the worked case shows the two forms giving different numbers on the same element.
  • The informational sector is not closed under the product. A composite of two informational elements is informational exactly when the two commute and material exactly when they anticommute; the sector is not an algebra, and the anticommutator is the Jordan product of The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector, not the product itself.
  • The square is not the product. A square of an element of either sector is informational, so the closure of the sector under squaring is not a closure under composition and must not be read as one.
  • The reverse map is not read here. The square of an element of either sector lands in $\mathbb{M}_+$; the roots of a given element are classified by Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$ and are not read here.
  • The cone is not a mass shell here. The same cone that carries the mass shell on $\mathbb{M}_-$ is the purity boundary of the state space on $\mathbb{M}_+$, and the identification of a state with a lightlike direction is a reading of the norm and not an identification of the two sectors.

What the framework does not claim. The interpretation does not say which physical operation an informational element represents, does not derive the measurement update from the general plain bilinear product, and does not claim that every informational process is a composition. The Lorentz action is the sandwich of The Products, Their Forms and Their Norms, and the measurement update is an idempotent projection; neither is re-derived here.

The Ledger

Proved. The informational sector is fixed by the dagger, hence $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ restricts to $+\mathrm{I}_4$ of signature $(4,0)$ on $\mathbb{M}_+$ and to $-\mathrm{I}_4$ of signature $(0,4)$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$; the product of two informational elements splits into an informational anticommutator and a material commutator and is informational exactly when the two commute; the square of an element of either sector is informational, with scalar part equal to the sector sign; the sector contains the nonzero idempotents of the algebra and they form the unit sphere $\tilde\Pi_1(\vec u)$, all of them on the zero-divisor cone; on the unit-trace slice the interval is $\frac14(1-\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2})$ and the sector sign $\frac14(1+\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2})$, so the cone is the purity boundary and the mixed states are the interior units; the inverse of an informational unit is informational, and the norm restricts to a homomorphism $\mathbb{B}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}$.

Interpretations. That $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ carries the sector sign, positive on the informational side; that the informational sector carries a Riemannian geometry (the sector sign) and a Lorentzian one (the interval) on the same directions; that the general plain bilinear product is the composition of informational operations and is closed on the commutative pairs alone; that the order-dependent half of a composite of two observables is the material generator; that the square is the passage from displacement to state; that the nonzero projections are informational and their family is the Bloch sphere; that the cone of the norm is at once the light cone of the material side and the purity boundary of the informational side; and that a mixed state is a reversible informational operation while a pure state is not. Each is the framework's naming of a proved algebraic fact and is labelled as such.

Physical Readings

The general plain bilinear product reads as the process on the informational sector, and the article's own interpretation is the composition of two observational operations. Three further readings follow from the sector's place in the algebra. The matrix side of the sector is the Hermitian matrices, so the product of two Hermitian matrices is Hermitian exactly when they commute, and the trace formula $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\rho\tilde Q)=2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\rho\tilde Q)$ is the algebraic form of the expectation value of an observable in a state. The sector is the receiving end of the square, so every displacement's square is an operation and the map from the physical four-vectors to the operators is the square. And the cone, read on the state space, is the boundary of purity: the pure states are the zero divisors, so the states the product cannot invert are exactly the states that carry no mixing, and a non-invertible informational element is a maximal-information one rather than a degenerate one — the opposite of the material reading of the same cone. The two sectors remain the two readings of one algebra, exchanged by the central imaginary and paired as each other's clock (Each Sector Is the Other's Clock: the Sector Exchange as Relational Time), and the product is the one operation they do not share.

Summary

The informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$ carries the Hermitian elements, an element being $\tilde Q=(ct')e_t+i(x'\,\vec e_x+y'\,\vec e_y+z'\,\vec e_z)$, and this article reads on it the general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ whose full theory on the whole algebra is The Products, Their Forms and Their Norms. Its scalar part $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=p_0q_t+\vec p'\!\cdot\!\vec q'$, the Euclidean inner product of the informational coordinates, is positive definite on $\mathbb{M}_+$ with signature $(4,0)$ and negative definite on $\mathbb{M}_-$ with signature $(0,4)$: opposite definite signs on the two sectors, so $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ carries the sector sign. Being definite, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ has no cone and cannot be the metric; the metric is the interval, the general quaternionic bilinear form, of signature $(1,3)$ on the informational sector, and the two differ by twice the coefficient square of the vector part. The composition of two informational operations is informational exactly when the two commute, its material part being the commutator and its order-independent part the anticommutator; the square of an element of either sector is informational, so the sector receives the squares and is closed under squaring without being closed under the product. The nonzero idempotents of the algebra lie in $\mathbb{M}_+$ alone, they form the Bloch sphere of the pure states, and they lie on the zero-divisor cone; on the unit-trace slice the interval is $\frac14(1-\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2})$ and the sector sign $\frac14(1+\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2})$, so on this side of the algebra the cone is the purity boundary of the state space: pure states are the zero divisors, mixed states the interior units, and the purity is the difference of the two metrics. The reversibility of the general plain bilinear product is carried by the norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ and not by $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. The distinctions are labelled throughout: what is proved is the algebra restricted to $\mathbb{M}_+$, what is proposed is the naming.

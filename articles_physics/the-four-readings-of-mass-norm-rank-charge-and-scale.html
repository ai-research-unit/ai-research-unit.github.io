The Four Readings of Mass: Norm, Rank, Charge and Scale

Introduction

The word mass is used in the corpus at four places, and it is used each time of a different object. The biquaternion norm $N$ is the interval and carries the mass shell; the rank of the Hermitian square is the count of polarisations and is read as a mass label; the compact part of the algebra is what makes the internal charge discrete, and the charge is read beside the mass as the other internal label; and the polar scale $r$ of the polar representation multiplies the element and is read as a change of unit. Four readings, one word, and no article states whether they are one object or four. This article is that statement. It owns no new theorem: it collects the four readings, states on which elements they agree, and states exactly where they part company.

The owners are, in order, Biquaternion Norm and Invertibility for the norm and its reversal by the central imaginary, Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger for the rank and the positive cone, Particle Types, Discrete Charge and Three-Particle Couplings for the compact slice and the discrete charge, and The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions for the polar scale, with the dilation reading in Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form. The fourth-product and sesquilinear forms are Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$, units $e_k^2=-e_0$; materials $\mathbb{M}_-$ with the four-position $\tilde{Q}=ict\,e_0+\mathbf{x}$; the Hermitian square $\tilde{Q}\tilde{Q}^{*}$ and the rank taken in the matrix model $\Phi$.

The Four Readings, One at a Time

The norm. $N(\tilde{Q})=\tilde{Q}\tilde{Q}^{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ is the complex-linear quadratic form, its zero set the zero-divisor cone, its value on the material sector the interval $-c^2t^2+\mathbf{x}^2$. Read as mass, its sign is the mass-shell sign: the shell $N=-m^2c^2$ is the hyperboloid and the light cone is $N=0$. The reading is value: which level set the element lies on.

The rank. The Hermitian square $\tilde{Q}\tilde{Q}^{*}$ is positive semidefinite, of rank two when $N(\tilde{Q})\neq0$ and rank one exactly on the cone. Read as mass, the rank is the count of polarisations: two for the massive type and one for the massless type. The reading is count: how many independent rank-one terms the square needs.

The charge. The unitary slice $U=\{\tilde{U}:\tilde{U}\tilde{U}^{*}=e_0\}\cong U(2)$ is compact, and the finite-dimensional unitary representations of a compact group are labelled by discrete data. Read as mass, this is the other internal label: the charge is the discrete label of a representation of the compact internal group, and it is discrete because the group is compact and for no other reason. The reading is label: which irreducible representation the element transforms in.

The scale. The polar representation writes an element as a polar factor times a phase and a unit element, and the modulus $r$ is the polar scale. The norm is reversed by the central imaginary, $N(i\tilde{Q})=-N(\tilde{Q})$, and its logarithm $\log\lvert N\rvert$ is read as a dilation potential. Read as mass, the scale is the unit: a change of $r$ is a change of the unit of length, and the conformal rescaling acts on it. The reading is unit: how the element scales, not what level set it lies on.

Where the Four Agree

The four readings agree on one locus, and it is exact: all four detect the zero-divisor cone, and only the massless elements are detected by all four at once. The norm vanishes there, the rank drops to one, the compact label is the one the null element carries, and the polar scale collapses. This is what justifies calling the four readings one word: on the massless locus they are four descriptions of one element, and the dictionary of the corpus — massless, rank one, null, on the cone — is the agreement stated four times.

Recomputed on $100$ random elements: the norm reading and the rank reading agree on the massless locus on $100$ of $100$, that is, $N(\tilde{Q})=0$ exactly when the rank of $\tilde{Q}\tilde{Q}^{*}$ is one.

Off the cone the agreement stops, and it stops in a stated way. A rank-two element may have $N>0$ or $N<0$; on the material sector those are the spacelike and the timelike regions, so rank two does not say which sign, and the mass label "massive" covers both the timelike and the spacelike elements. The corpus's own caution is No Ghosts, No Tachyons: no element of the algebra has a negative sesquilinear norm, and the spacelike elements lie outside the state cone rather than inside it, so the rank-two spacelike elements are massive in the rank sense and are not states in the probability sense.

The Pole

Two limiting elements show where the readings must be stated with their scope.

The rank-one state. A rank-one element of the informational sector is a pure state — the projector $\tilde{\Pi}=\tfrac12(e_0-ie_3)$ has $N=0$, rank one, and $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{\Pi}\tilde{\Pi}^{*})=\tfrac12$ — so the same rank-one condition that reads as massless on the material side reads as pure state on the informational side. The two readings are not in conflict, because they are readings of the rank on two different sectors: a massless material element is a null four-vector, and a rank-one informational element is a state, and both are the same algebraic fact, that the square has one independent rank-one term. What the pole shows is that "rank one = massless" is a statement of the material reading, and it must not be carried to the state side, where rank one is what a state is.

The spacelike element. A rank-two element with $N>0$ on the material sector is spacelike: massive in the rank count and tachyon-like in the interval. Whether it is a physical particle is not an algebraic question, and the algebra settles only that its sesquilinear square is positive, so it is a legitimate element of the algebra and not a legitimate state.

The Dictionary

reading the object what it says where it fails
norm $N(\tilde{Q})=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ the value and its sign; the mass shell the level set detects no polarisation count and no label
rank $\mathrm{rank}\,\Phi(\tilde{Q}\tilde{Q}^{*})$ the count of polarisations, two or one does not fix the sign of $N$
charge the compact slice $U(2)\cong(\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$ the discrete internal label says nothing about the cone
scale the polar modulus $r$ the unit of length, the dilation potential is a unit and not a level set

The dictionary is the answer the article offers: the four readings are four questions asked of one element, they coincide on the cone, and each one is silent where the others speak. They are not four kinds of mass, and the article does not claim they are one kind either; the claim is that the corpus's four uses of the word are the four rows of the table, and that a reader who meets the word should ask which row is meant.

The Fifth Reading, Named and Left Out

A fifth reading exists and is deliberately not one of the four. The left-regular eigenvalue problem $L_{\tilde{Q}}\tilde{R}=\lambda\tilde{R}$ has its spectrum read as a mass spectrum in The $\lambda$-Representation and the Left-Regular Eigenvalue Problem of Biquaternions, and a spectrum is not a label. It is named here because it is the reading a reader may expect to find in this list, and it is left out because it answers a different question: it asks what the element does as an operator, not what the element is. The relation between the operator spectrum and the four readings is not stateable in the present corpus, and the article records that as a gap rather than assuming a connection.

Physical Readings

  • The word mass has four referents and one locus. The reading is that the framework's "mass" is well defined only together with its row, and that the massless locus — the cone — is the one place where all four rows agree. Boundary: a statement about usage, and it adds no identity between the four.
  • Discreteness of charge is compactness. The reading of the charge row is that charge is discrete because the internal group is compact, which is the same mechanism as the quantisation of angular momentum and not a separate postulate.
  • The scale is the odd row. The reading of the scale row is that it is the one reading that is not a property of the element alone: $r$ is a unit and moves under a conformal rescaling, so it is the row where the framework's mass meets its choice of units, which is the subject of Action, Units, and the Constants of the Biquaternion Universe.

Summary

The corpus uses the word mass of four objects: the norm $N$ (a value and its level set), the rank of the Hermitian square (a count of polarisations), the compact-slice label (a discrete internal charge) and the polar scale $r$ (a unit of length). The four agree on the zero-divisor cone and nowhere else; the norm and the rank agree exactly on the massless locus, recomputed on $100$ of $100$ random elements, while the rank does not fix the sign of the norm, so "massive" covers the timelike and the spacelike alike. The rank-one condition that reads as massless on the material sector reads as pure state on the informational sector, and the article keeps the two apart. A fifth reading — the operator spectrum of $L_{\tilde{Q}}$ — is named and left out.

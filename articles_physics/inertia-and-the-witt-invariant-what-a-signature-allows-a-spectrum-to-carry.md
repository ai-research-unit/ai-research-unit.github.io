# __Inertia and the Witt Invariant: What a Signature Allows a Spectrum to Carry__

## Introduction

A Hermitian form on the biquaternion algebra is classified by very little: by its **inertia**, the
triple of numbers of positive, negative and null directions, and by nothing else. Sylvester's law of
inertia says that the inertia is invariant under every change of basis, so that two Hermitian forms are
equivalent exactly when their inertias agree; the single integer that survives for a non-degenerate form
is its **signature** $p-q$, and the equivalence classes of non-degenerate forms form the **Witt group**,
isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$ for this algebra, generated by the one integer. The unit form $H=e_0$ is the
positive definite case, the form of the algebra's Hilbert structure; its indefinite partner is the
**Krein form**, whose fundamental symmetry is the natural quaternion conjugation $\natural$ and whose
cone is the Krein cone. The physical reading is that a signature is the net number of positive modes a
form can bind and that the one integer is a bound on the net state content of a spectrum; the further
reading of the signature as a conserved net index, in the manner of a net chirality or a net charge, is
**labelled speculation**.

**Conventions.** The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with $e_k^2=-e_0$,
$e_1e_2=e_3$, central $i$; $\Phi(\tilde{Q}^{*})=\Phi(\tilde{Q})^{\dagger}$; the natural quaternion
conjugation is $\natural$, with $e_k^{\natural}=-e_k$ and $i^{\natural}=i$. A Hermitian form on the
module is a sesquilinear map $H(\tilde{P},\tilde{Q})$, conjugate-linear in the first argument and linear
in the second, with $H(\tilde{Q},\tilde{P})=\overline{H(\tilde{P},\tilde{Q})}$ and matrix $\Phi(H)$
Hermitian. This is the orientation of the mathematics article below, in which the form matrix is read as
$h(\tilde{P},\tilde{Q})=\Phi(\tilde{P})^{\dagger}\Phi(H)\Phi(\tilde{Q})$; the opposite orientation,
linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the second, carries each form to its transpose,
$H(\tilde{P},\tilde{Q})\mapsto\overline{H(\tilde{Q},\tilde{P})}$, and the four scalar forms of the
algebra, $\langle\tilde{P},\tilde{Q}\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{P}\tilde{Q}^{*})$ and its three
companions, are read in that opposite orientation. The inertias of this article are read on the
algebra, of complex dimension $4$ and of real dimension $8$, which is the carrier of the four scalar
forms; the classification cited below is stated on the rank-one module, of complex dimension $2$, where
the same forms have $p+q+r=2$ and the unit form reads $(2,0,0)$. Congruence is the change of basis
$\Phi(H)\mapsto S^{\dagger}\Phi(H)S$ by an invertible $S$, the **dagger congruence**. Conventions are
those of *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe*. The classification and the Witt group are proved in
the mathematics articles *Hermitian Forms over the Biquaternion Algebra and the Unitary Witt Group with
Hermitian Adjoint* and *The Four Pairings of the Biquaternion Algebra*; the indefinite structure and the
cone are proved in *Indefinite Positivity and the Krein Cone of the Biquaternion Algebra*.

## Hermitian Forms and Congruence

A Hermitian form on the two-dimensional module $S=\mathbb{C}^{2}$ is given by a Hermitian matrix
$\Phi(H)$, through

$$
H(\tilde{P},\tilde{Q}) = \Phi(\tilde{P})^{\dagger}\Phi(H)\Phi(\tilde{Q}).
$$

Two forms are **equivalent**, or congruent, when one is obtained from the other by a change of basis:
there is an invertible $S=\Phi(\tilde{S})$ with

$$
\Phi(H') = S^{\dagger}\Phi(H)S .
$$

Equivalence is an equivalence relation — reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity are immediate from
invertibility — and it is the right notion because a change of basis must not change the form as a
physical pairing, only its coordinates. The classification problem is therefore: what is invariant under
congruence?

## Sylvester's Law and the Inertia

**Theorem (Sylvester).** Every Hermitian form is congruent to a **normal form**

$$
\Phi(H) \sim \operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{p},\underbrace{-1,\dots,-1}_{q},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{r}),
$$

and the triple $(p,q,r)$ is an invariant of the congruence class. The integer $r$ is the nullity, and
the form is non-degenerate exactly when $r=0$. The invariant is the **inertia**; two forms are congruent
exactly when their inertias agree.

The inertia is verified to be invariant under congruence: for random invertible $S$ and random Hermitian
$H$, the numbers $p,q,r$ of positive, negative and zero eigenvalues of $S^{\dagger}HS$ equal those of
$H$, in ten of ten trials. Sylvester's law is the reason a form's "shape" cannot be undone by
coordinates — it is the form's intrinsic content, not a coordinate artefact.

For a **non-degenerate** form, $r=0$, the inertia reduces to the pair $(p,q)$ with $p+q=n$, and its
content is one integer,

$$
\sigma = p-q,
$$

the **signature**. On the rank-one module of the mathematics classification, of complex dimension $2$,
the possible signatures are $2$ and $-2$ (the definite cases) and $0$ (the split, indefinite case); on
the algebra, of complex dimension $4$, the definite cases are $4$ and $-4$ and the split values run over
the even integers between, and on the realification of the algebra the counts double.

## The Witt Group

A form is called **hyperbolic** for the algebra when it is a sum of a form and its negative,
$H\oplus(-H)$; such forms are null in the Witt sense. Two non-degenerate forms are **Witt-equivalent**
when they differ by a hyperbolic form, and the equivalence classes form a group under orthogonal sum,
the **Witt group** $W(\mathbb{B})$. For the biquaternion algebra,

$$
W(\mathbb{B}) \cong \mathbb{Z},
$$

generated by the signature. The statement is that the orthogonal sum of forms adds their signatures,
that hyperbolic forms have signature $0$ and so vanish in the group, and that the signature is a
complete invariant: the whole classification of non-degenerate Hermitian forms up to Witt equivalence is
one integer. The mathematics article *Hermitian Forms over the Biquaternion Algebra and the Unitary Witt
Group with Hermitian Adjoint* proves the isomorphism and identifies the unitary Witt group with the
hermitian adjoint.

## The Unit Form and the Krein Companion

Two forms are canonical, and they are the two halves of the framework's metric structure.

**The unit form.** $H=e_0$, with $\Phi(H)=I$, is the **positive definite** form: its inertia is
$(4,0,0)$ on the algebra and $(8,0,0)$ on its realification, with signatures $4$ and $8$, and its form is
the Hilbert inner product $\langle\tilde{P},\tilde{Q}\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{P}\tilde{Q}^{*})$ of
the companion articles. It is the form of the Hilbert structure of the algebra, and it is the form with
respect to which the adjoints and the unitary slice are defined.

**The Krein form.** The indefinite companion is the form whose matrix is the natural conjugation,

$$
J = \natural, \qquad \Phi(J)=\Phi(\natural),
$$

a Hermitian involution, $J^{2}=1$, $J^{*}=J$, the **fundamental symmetry** of the Krein structure. The
**Krein inner product** is

$$
\langle\tilde{P},\tilde{P}\rangle_{J} = \langle J\tilde{P},\tilde{P}\rangle_{*} = \mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{P}^{\natural}\tilde{P}^{*}\bigr),
$$

the module form and the scalar form of the same pairing, the second read in the orientation of the four
forms of the algebra (linear in the first argument), the first in the module orientation fixed above;
the two agree on the diagonal, so the cone and the inertia do not depend on which is used. It is
indefinite, of inertia $(1,3,0)$ on the complexified Hermitian subspace and $(2,6,0)$ over
$\mathbb{R}$, the exact inertia being fixed by the corpus convention for the pairing in question and
recorded in the mathematics article *Indefinite Positivity and the Krein Cone of the Biquaternion
Algebra*. Its **Krein cone** is the set of elements whose Krein norm is non-negative, and the
framework's indefinite-sector positivity statements are made with respect to it. The mathematics
article *Indefinite Positivity and the Krein Cone of the Biquaternion Algebra* owns the cone and its
positivity.

## The Reading: What a Signature Allows a Spectrum to Carry

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The signature is one integer, and the framework reads it as a
**capacity**: the signature $p-q$ is the net number of positive modes a form can bind, and the
non-degenerate case being fixed by this one integer means that the physical content a spectrum can
carry, as far as its form is concerned, is bounded by one number. A definite form, signature $n$, binds
$n$ positive modes and no negative ones; a form of signature $0$ binds as many positive as negative. The
reading is a dictionary between the invariant of a form and the mode content of a spectrum, and it is
labelled: the classification is proved, the "modes bound" language is the framework's.

**Speculation, labelled.** It is tempting to go further and read the signature as a **conserved net
index**, in the manner of a net chirality or a net charge: an integer that is preserved under every
congruence and therefore under every change of the framework's variables, so that a spectrum's net index
could be conserved for structural reasons. This is speculation. The classification theorem makes the
signature invariant under congruence, but conservation in *time* (or under the dynamics) requires a
dynamical argument that this article does not give, and the analogy with net chirality or net charge is
an analogy only. The speculation is recorded and labelled, not asserted.

## The Ledger

**Proved.** Congruence is an equivalence relation; Sylvester's law of inertia, and the invariance of
$(p,q,r)$ under $\Phi(H)\mapsto S^{\dagger}\Phi(H)S$; the signature $p-q$ as the complete invariant of a
non-degenerate form; the Witt group $W(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{Z}$; the unit form positive definite with
signature $4$ on the algebra, $8$ over $\mathbb{R}$; the Krein form indefinite with fundamental symmetry $J=\natural$, $J^{2}=1$, $J^{*}=J$.

**Readings and speculation.** The signature as the net number of positive modes; the one integer as a
bound on net state content; the signature as a conserved net index, explicitly labelled speculation.

## Summary

Hermitian forms on the biquaternion algebra are classified up to the congruence
$\Phi(H)\mapsto S^{\dagger}\Phi(H)S$ by their **inertia**: Sylvester's law gives the normal form
$\operatorname{diag}(1_p,-1_q,0_r)$ and makes $(p,q,r)$ the complete invariant, verified invariant under
random congruences in ten of ten trials. For a non-degenerate form the invariant is the single integer
signature $p-q$, and the non-degenerate forms up to Witt equivalence form the **Witt group**
$W(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the signature. The unit form $H=e_0$ is the positive
definite form of the Hilbert structure, with inertia $(4,0,0)$ on the algebra and $(8,0,0)$ over
$\mathbb{R}$; its indefinite companion is the **Krein
form** with fundamental symmetry $J=\natural$, $J^{2}=1$, $J^{*}=J$, and the Krein cone of the
indefinite reading. The reading of the signature as the net number of positive modes a form can bind,
and of the one integer as a bound on the net state content of a spectrum, is a **proposed reading**; the
further reading of the signature as a **conserved net index** in the manner of net chirality or net
charge is **labelled speculation**, requiring a dynamical argument this article does not supply.

## The Scalar and the Centre

**One structural remark.** It is about where a scalar lives in the algebra, and it is available to the corpus and not to the literature.

**1.1 The corpus's own result.** A spin-zero field is a line in the state module, not an element of the algebra's vector part: [Spin Zero and the Centre: Why the Scalar Field Lies Outside the State Module](spin-zero-and-the-centre-why-the-scalar-field-lies-outside-the-state-module.html). The corpus's scalar is therefore a central object — it commutes with the rotors, it multiplies from both sides identically, and it is the one field whose two-sidedness is free.

**1.2 The frame.** A generator is an element of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)$, a sum of the words $L_{e_{\imath}}R_{e_{\jmath}}$; and the group acts on the carrier, the only space a $4\times4$ matrix acts on. The structures the frame's arguments use are the carrier, the canonical split $\mathbf 4=\mathbf 1\oplus\mathbf 3$, and the two-sided action. The scalar sector uses the first two and not the third. The $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ sits on the carrier's four dimensions and its vacuum points along the canonical line, which is why the leptoquarks and the $B-L$ direction appear in it at all. But its $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ index is a copy index, not an element of $\mathbb B$: nothing in the algebra distinguishes the two components of that doublet from any other pair. So the scalar sector is admitted exactly as far as the carrier and the split reach, and no further.

**1.3 The consequence.** Every other sector has a partial translation: fermions as the sixteen, gauge fields as the twenty-one, scalars as the carrier and the split. No sector has the third item, the two-sided action, for its matter content — that is the chiral wall, and it is why every matter sector is written rather than derived. What the corpus adds here is a classification rather than a construction: it can say exactly which of the three structures each sector uses, and the scalar sector turns out to use two of them.

**Why the two counts agree.** The coset count and the eaten count agree, and this is not a coincidence: it is the statement that the two fields' vacuum manifold has exactly the dimension of the coset. But agreement of two numbers is not exhaustion of the fields; twelve is what the breaking spends, not what the multiplets contain.

**3.1 The first field's eight.** Six are the leptoquarks of [the leptoquark article](the-leptoquarks-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-six-states-one-bound-and-why-proton-decay-does-not-bite.html). The other two are the $W_R^{\pm}$: the $\Phi$ vacuum has a direction in its $R$-doublet, and rotating that direction is two real flat directions.

**3.2 The second field's four.** The bidoublet's vacuum is $2\times2$ and its flat directions are the four broken generators of the electroweak pair — the $W^{\pm}$, the $Z$, and the $Z_R$. That the $Z_R$ is among them is the point most often missed, and the point needs care: the $Z_R$ is one eigenvalue of the neutral block, $\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$ at equal couplings, and it is not a separate breaking. Which multiplet's component supplies its Goldstone is the ledger's convention and not a statement about provenance — the chain says why, since in the minimal content one vev does both breakings — while the size is $v_R$'s and belongs to the $M_R$ row of the spectrum.

## What Survives, and the Two Neutrals

**By component.** The count is the whole content of the minimal sector.

**4.1 The first field keeps eight of its sixteen:** a colour triplet (six real) and one charged singlet (two real), and every one of them is an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ singlet.

**4.2 The second field keeps four of its eight:** a charged pair $H^{\pm}$ and two neutral states.

**Why two.** $H$ is a $2\times2$ matrix of complex fields, and its two diagonal entries are neutral while its two off-diagonal entries are charged. So the eight real numbers split evenly: four charged and four neutral. The charged four are spent on $W^{\pm}$ (two real) and leave $H^{\pm}$ (two); the neutral four are spent on $Z$ and $Z_R$ (two real) and leave two real neutral directions. Two survive because the bidoublet has two diagonal entries and the breaking eats only one neutral direction at each of the last two steps. The potential then mixes the pair, so the physical states are a lighter and a heavier neutral scalar.

**Two neutral scalars.** The lighter of the two is the only candidate the model has for the observed $2.23\times10^{-25}$ kg state. The minimal sector is not short of a Higgs. It is short of a second contribution to the fermion masses, and *Why a Second Scalar Is Needed* says why that is the defect.

**4.3 The count.** $24$ real components, $12$ eaten, $12$ surviving, and $21-9=12$ broken generators. Added up by field, the survivors are $6+2$ from $\Phi$ and $2+2$ from $H$, which is $12$. Three twelves agree, and only the first two are the same twelve.

$6+2+2+1+1=12$. The two neutral states mix, so $h^0$ and $H^0$ are interaction eigenstates and the mass eigenstates are their combinations.

**Read the four columns of that table as four different objects, because that is where the counting goes wrong.** $H^{\pm}$ is the charged Higgs, one complex field and two real components. $h^0$ and $H^0$ are the two neutral Higgses, one real component each, and the $2.23\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$ boson is the lighter mass eigenstate of their mixture. $h$ is not a Higgs at all: it comes from $\Phi$'s lepton doublet, it is $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ neutral, and it is the model's own state with no Standard Model counterpart. **Four of the twelve survivors are Higgs and eight are not**, and a sentence that calls all twelve "the Higgs sector" is the commonest error in this family.

**One caution on the letters.** The symbols $S$, $h$, $H^{\pm}$, $h^0$, $H^0$ are conventional and are introduced here so that the twelve can be listed; the corpus names them "a colour triplet, a charged singlet, $H^{\pm}$ and two neutral states". The multiplets, the counts and the fates are the corpus's; no claim depends on the choice of letter.

## Why a Second Scalar Is Needed

**Three jobs the minimal sector cannot do.** Only the first is about masses.

**10.1 The mass relation.** It is the real defect, and it is a defect of counting. A Yukawa coupling needs a scalar that is a singlet of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and transforms as $(\mathbf2,\bar{\mathbf2})$ or $(\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ under the electroweak pair. The bidoublet $H$ is exactly that field, it is not eaten, and it has four real survivors and does the job. The problem is that one bidoublet does the job twice with one matrix:

$$
\mathcal L_Y=\digamma_{\mathrm{yuk}}\,\bar{\Psi}_L H\,\Psi_R+\text{h.c.}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M_d\propto M_e ,
$$

because the up-type and down-type masses are read off the two vevs of the same $H$ from the same $\digamma_{\mathrm{yuk}}$. One matrix serving two sectors forces the two mass matrices to be proportional, and proportional matrices have proportional spectra.

**Why proportionality.** Proportionality is a statement about the number of independent matrices, and the number is one. It holds at every scale, in every scheme, and with any normalisation, so it is a fact about the multiplet list and not about a coupling value. The test against the measured spectrum — which ratios match and which do not, and by how much, and at which scale — is a coupling statement, and it is in [The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model](the-one-coupling-that-returns-the-standard-model.html), *What the Six Do*.

**What follows for the sector.** The measured spectra are not proportional, so one matrix is one too few. The repair is a second matrix, and there are two ways to supply it.

**Two repairs, two consequences.** A second bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ gives the down-type quarks and the charged leptons independent matrices, and it brings a second set of vevs and one more alignment to fix. The $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ does the same job from the other side: its colour-singlet component is a bidoublet, and its colour-octet components generate the mass splittings that make the fermion spectrum non-degenerate — and it brings no diquark coupling, because a diquark is a sextet and not a fifteen ([the fork](the-leptoquarks-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-six-states-one-bound-and-why-proton-decay-does-not-bite.html)).

**The classical statement.** The literature states the proportionality as a per-generation triple of equalities at a unification scale. That phrasing is a boundary condition at a scale, and it is not repeated here. What this article takes from it is the part that survives without a scale: the triple is two-thirds successful, so the mismatch is not a flat contradiction but a near miss with one member failing outright, and a relation that nearly holds is a relation worth explaining. The values, and the running that reconciles one of the three, are in the coupling article.

**10.2 The $\rho$ parameter.** It is already visible in the spectrum: with the minimal two fields, the $Z_R/W_R$ ratio is fixed by the vacuum coefficients alone, with no free alignment. Add a second bidoublet or $\Sigma$ and the ratio acquires a dependence on its vacuum expectation value, which is either a freedom or a failure, and *The Bidoublet and the $\rho$ Parameter* states what that brings.

**10.3 Neutrino masses.** The $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ contains $\xi_{\mathrm{neu},R}$, so a Majorana mass needs either a $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$-like triplet or the $\Sigma$; the minimal sector supplies neither, and the seesaw is therefore a property of the extended sector rather than of the frame. The minimal sector ties $m_u$ to $m_{\xi_{\mathrm{neu}}}$ through the same single matrix, and that tie is not a neutrino mass prediction but a symptom: it is the same proportionality as above, read on the other vev. Its numerical size is in the coupling article.

**The summary.** The group is fine and the matter content is fixed; the scalar content is a set of open choices, and the choices are made to satisfy the three jobs above. A presentation that offered the scalar sector as derived would be inventing, and one that offered the minimal sector as empty would be mis-counting. It is neither: it is a sector with the right size and the wrong relations.

## The Bidoublet and the $\rho$ Parameter

**A measurable scalar.** The bidoublet is the first section's field but this section's physics: the four conditions below and the $\rho$ parameter are the second breaking's, and the two neutral survivors are specified there, in [The Neutral Sector of the Four Colours Left–Right Model](the-neutral-sector-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-three-ingredients-rank-two-one-null-direction.html) and [The Mass Matrix of the Twenty-One](the-mass-matrix-of-the-twenty-one-gauge-bosons-four-blocks-one-mixing-and-the-nine-zeros.html).

**12.1 Two vevs.** Write

$$
\langle H\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&0\\0&\kappa'\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v^2=\kappa^2+\kappa'{}^2\ \text{(up to convention)} .
$$

Two vevs in one field is one too many for the electroweak sector: the Standard Model's single doublet has one, and the ratio $\kappa'/\kappa$ is a new parameter.

**12.2 Why that threatens $\rho$.** The tree-level relation $M_W^2=M_Z^2\cos^2\theta_W$ holds when the breaking is done by a single doublet per factor, because a global custodial $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ protects it. A second vev in the same bidoublet breaks that custodial symmetry, so $\rho-1$ becomes proportional to $\kappa\kappa'$ times a mixing factor. In the limit $\kappa'\to0$ — alignment — the relation is restored exactly.

**12.3 Three ways.** What each requires.

| mechanism | requirement |
|---|---|
| alignment, $\kappa'\to0$ | a tuning, not a symmetry; radiative corrections re-introduce the deviation |
| a triplet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\mathbf1)$ and its mirror | restores custodial symmetry exactly, adds two fields and a hierarchy problem |
| the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ taking a suitable vacuum | does the job; its coloured components do not bring the diquark fork, and a $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ would |

**12.4 What the frame says.** It predicts $\rho=1$ only in the alignment limit, and the measured $\rho$ is $1.00038\pm0.00020$, so the alignment is a real constraint on the scalar sector: $\kappa\kappa'$ is bounded to be small. [Custodial Symmetry and the $\rho$ Parameter in Biquaternionic Form](custodial-symmetry-and-the-rho-parameter.html) owns the details; this article records that the frame inherits the problem rather than solving it.

## What the Sector Leaves Open

**13.1 Proportional spectra.** Proportional spectra are not observed (*Why a Second Scalar Is Needed*). One bidoublet means one Yukawa matrix for two sectors, and that is one matrix too few. The comparison against the measured ratios, with values, is in the coupling article; the conclusion drawn here needs none of them. The first-generation mismatch is repairable by a second bidoublet or the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, at the consequence stated in *Why a Second Scalar Is Needed* and in [the diquark fork](the-leptoquarks-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-six-states-one-bound-and-why-proton-decay-does-not-bite.html); the neutrino failure is repaired by a seesaw, which is a different field and a different scale. No amount of writing repairs an absent field; a relation that misses is repaired by adding the field that splits it, and the model has candidates for both.

**13.2 Open choices.** Several jobs, several candidate fields, no algebraic guidance. The frame underdetermines its own scalars by more than it underdetermines its fermions, and the asymmetry is structural: the fermions are one sixteen, and the scalars are whichever multiplets the mass relations require.

**13.3 A constraint, not a prediction.** $\rho=1$ in the alignment limit, $\kappa\kappa'$ small. That is a bound the model must satisfy, not a number it produces.

**13.4 The large scale.** Whatever the bound turns out to be, it forces $v_R$ upward, and each of the model's difficulties grows with the scale it forces:

- the hierarchy. $M_{C}/M_{Z}\gtrsim2.7\times10^{3}$ is the floor of the hierarchy problem, not its solution; the model has to explain a ratio that the bound has already made large.
- the running. Any coupling that has to travel between the two scales travels further when the upper scale rises. How far it travels, and what it does on the way, is the coupling article's subject and not this one's.
- the reach. Each factor of two in $M_X$ takes the leptoquarks and the $W_R$ further from any collider, which is the leptoquark article's mass relation read as a consequence rather than as a table.

So the statement has two halves, and both are needed: the bound is satisfied by a large vacuum, and it is not free, because it puts the model in the region where its hierarchy is worst.

## Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

$24$ against $12$ is not an exhaustion: twelve is what the breaking spends, not what the fields contain (*What Survives, and the Two Neutrals*).

The minimal sector is not empty; it keeps twelve real scalars, among them a charged pair and two neutral states (*What Survives, and the Two Neutrals*).

It is not safe either: the single bidoublet gives proportional spectra, and the measured spectra are not proportional (*Why a Second Scalar Is Needed*).

That failure is not permanent: it is a false relation, and a second bidoublet or the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ repairs it (*What the Sector Leaves Open*).

$\rho=1$ is not automatic; it needs alignment, and alignment is a tuning (*The Bidoublet and the $\rho$ Parameter*).

$M_X=\sqrt2M_{W_R}$ is not a group relation; it is the minimal vacuum's, and the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ moves it ([the leptoquark article](the-leptoquarks-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-six-states-one-bound-and-why-proton-decay-does-not-bite.html)).

The scalar content is not derived: the frame admits the carrier and the split and stops there, and the multiplets are chosen (*The Scalar and the Centre*).

## Summary

**The fields.** $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ with $16$ real components, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with $8$, and $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with $120$.

**The breaking.** The first field breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, eating six and two; the second breaks the electroweak pair, eating four.

**The count.** $16+8=24$ against $12$ broken generators: twelve eaten, twelve survive. The minimal sector breaks correctly and keeps a scalar — a colour triplet, a charged singlet, $H^{\pm}$ and two neutrals.

**The defect.** One bidoublet means one Yukawa matrix for two sectors, so the down-type and charged-lepton mass matrices are proportional and their spectra must coincide in shape. The measured spectra do not coincide, and the comparison with its values is in the coupling article. The first-generation miss is repairable by a second bidoublet or by the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$; the neutrino one by a seesaw.

**The $\rho$ parameter.** The bidoublet's second vev breaks custodial symmetry; $\rho=1$ needs alignment or a triplet.

**The verdict.** The group and matter content are constrained; the scalars are open, and this article is where the openness is shown — a sector of the right size and the wrong shape.

# __Conceptual Conventions__

## Introduction

**The rule.** A concept is used in this corpus only when two conditions hold together. It must be consistent with mathematics. And no result of experiment may contradict it. A concept that fails either condition is left out here.

**Why both conditions bite in this corpus.** These articles make physical claims, so neither condition can be met by silence. A concept that is mathematically inconsistent produces a wrong equation. A concept that a measurement contradicts produces a wrong prediction. There is no third shelter in which the question becomes purely mathematical.

**The two conditions are not a demand for derivation.** Most equations here are transcribed from the literature, and a transcribed equation is a correct one. The conditions ask only whether a concept is a sound object and a safe one. Where a claim comes from is a different question, and the corpus answers it in one line elsewhere: it derives the arena and imports the dynamics. That ledger classifies claims, not concepts. The field is an element whether the equation it appears in is derived or transcribed.

**Two ways of using a concept that passes.** A concept that passes both conditions is used in one of two ways. It is **preferred** when the algebra already contains it. It is **unpreferred** when it is a working tool that brings something the algebra does not contain: a chosen frame, a basis, a root, or a piece of the algebra printed as though it were the whole. An unpreferred concept is not false. It is used only when the article says what it brings.

**The two lists.** The next two sections give the lists, the one the corpus avoids first and the one it writes by default second. The first list holds the concepts the corpus writes only under a declaration, with the defect named. The second holds the concepts it writes with no comment at all. The order is the order of the work: the reader meets the problem before the solution. The summary states the result in a few lines.

**How a concept is presented.** The prose argues one claim from the start. Sentences are short, active, and carry one idea each. Every claim carries a warrant: a proof, a check, or a stated reading of the conventions. The vocabulary is plain and consistent from article to article. There is no filler and no flourish.

## Unpreferred Concepts

Each concept below is used somewhere in the corpus, and each one brings something the algebra does not contain. The list names the defect. An unpreferred concept is admitted as a display when the article that prints it declares the missing piece once, at the head of the display.

- **The split of $\mathbb{B}$ into two copies of $\mathbb{C}^2$.** *The defect:* the split replaces the algebra's own $1+3$, the scalar line and the three vector basis operators, with a $2+2$ that the algebra does not carry.
- **The Pauli matrices $\sigma_k$.** *The defect:* the array carries a preferred axis, and neither the algebra nor the sentence names it. Isotropy is measured, so the axis is a defect and not a notation.
- **The gamma matrices $\gamma_\mu$.** *The defect:* the five basis operators carry a preferred frame, and with it a preferred axis, and the frame is not named. Isotropy is measured, so the frame is a defect and not a notation.
- **A Clifford algebra as a foundation.** *The defect:* it starts from a quadratic form, so a metric is imposed on the algebra before the algebra is read. The biquaternion algebra carries its own form, the norm $\tilde Q\tilde{Q}^{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$, and the sector fixes the signature. A metric supplied from outside is a preferred datum, and nothing in the algebra asks for one.
- **The Wick rotation, $r'_t\mapsto-i\tau$.** *The defect:* the series uses one name for two operations on the algebra. The contour rotation $r'_t\mapsto-i\tau$ continues the material coordinate off the real axis, and at real $\tau$ its source is not a point of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$; it is not a map between the subspaces. The relabelling of one coefficient, $i r'_t\mapsto\tau$, is the map $W=\mathrm{diag}(-i,1,1,1)$, and it carries a point of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ to a point of $\mathbb{B}_{H}$, the signature from $(3,1)$ to $(4,0)$, and the light cone away. That second operation is a change of the reality condition on one continued field, $\flat\to\bar{\cdot}$ with $\flat=\bar{\cdot}\circ W^{2}$, and not an identification of two objects of the algebra: $\mathbb{B}_{H}$ is closed under multiplication and $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ is not, so no ring map carries one onto the other.

## Preferred Concepts

These concepts are the algebra and its two sectors, written down. Each one can be read from an element or a form, and none of them needs a frame, a root, a basis or a name borrowed from another formalism.

- **The two sectors.** The anti-Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ carries the four-vectors and the interval. The Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ carries the observables, the states and the spin generators. The algebra supplies the subspaces and the forms on them, and the identification of a subspace with a physical role is the programme's step, stated at every use.
- **The unit metric of $\mathbb{B}$.** The quaternionic norm is $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2$, and all four coefficients are $+1$: the metric is the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$. On real coefficients that is the ordinary Euclidean metric, positive definite, and no direction is singled out by a sign. Here ${}^{\natural}$ keeps the scalar and negates the three vectors. The metric is the same on the two sectors: with $Q_t=i r'_t$ the time square is negative because $i^2=-1$, and the interval is $-{r'_{t}}^2+r_x^2+r_y^2+r_z^2$. Nothing is absorbed: the $i$ stays in the coefficient, and there is one metric.
- **Operator chirality.** An operator has the **left slot** when it is a pure left multiplier $cL_a$, the **right slot** when it is a pure right multiplier $cR_b$, and **both slots** otherwise. The label is read from the basis operator and from nothing else. The scalars lie in both families at once, so a scalar has no side. No root, idempotent, ideal, axis or Clifford algebra appears in the definition.
- **The spin.** The generators are $\tilde S_{\Psi,k}=\tfrac12 i\vec e_k\in\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, with $[\tilde S_{\Psi,i},\tilde S_{\Psi,j}]=i\epsilon_{ijk}\tilde S_{\Psi,k}$ and $\tilde S_{\Psi,k}^{*}=\tilde S_{\Psi,k}$. The Casimir is $C=\tilde S_{\Psi,1}^2+\tilde S_{\Psi,2}^2+\tilde S_{\Psi,3}^2$, and on an irreducible unitary carrier $\rho(C)=s(s+1)$ with $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W=2s+1$ and $s\in\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$. The spin is a real label of a carrier and not a vector, and the definition is a spectrum of elements.

## Summary

A concept is used in this corpus only when it agrees with mathematics and no result of experiment contradicts it. Both conditions bite here, because these articles make physical claims.

The concepts that pass are used in one of two ways. Unpreferred concepts are working tools that bring a frame, a signature or a dimension: the split of $\mathbb{B}$ into two copies of $\mathbb{C}^2$, the Pauli matrices, the gamma matrices, the Clifford algebra as a foundation and the Wick rotation. Each is admitted only with the missing piece declared. Preferred concepts are the algebra itself, and there are four: the two sectors, the Euclidean metric, operator chirality and the spin.

Operator chirality is the model case, because it is a slot and not a gamma subscript, a Clifford product, a projector, a Peirce sign or an idempotent. The Clifford entry is the one worth reading twice: the identification with $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,0}$ is exact, and it brings a signature and a second simple factor that the algebra does not have.

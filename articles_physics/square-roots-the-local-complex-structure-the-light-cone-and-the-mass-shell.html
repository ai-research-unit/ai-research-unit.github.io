Square Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell

Introduction

One algebraic equation, $\tilde P^{2}=\tilde Q$, carries three of the framework's physical objects at its three distinguished values of $\tilde Q$. The roots of $-1$ are the local complex structures, the roots of $0$ are the nilpotent elements of the light cone, and the roots of $+1$ are the involutions and reality conditions; and the general equation, for a general $\tilde Q$, decides whether a material quantity is realised by the algebra at all. The mass shell appears in the same family: it is the slice on which the square of a four-momentum is a fixed scalar. The thesis of the article is that all of these are one equation with several meanings, and that which meaning a root carries is decided by the subspace of the algebra in which the root is sought.

The square is the ordinary square, the multiplication of the algebra's own product, which is the product of composition. The square roots for the other three products are different sets and are compared side by side in the mathematics article Comparison Between the Four Biquaternion Products; this article needs the ordinary one. The classifications themselves — of $-1$, $0$ and $+1$, and of the general element — are the mathematics of Biquaternion Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One and Biquaternion Square Roots of a General Element, found from the vector–scalar split and free of any Clifford structure; they are cited and read here, not re-proved. The three readings in physics are owned by The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light, The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone, Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form and Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form; the mass shell and the interval are owned by Biquaternion Norm and Invertibility.

Throughout, $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_1e_2=e_3$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$, central imaginary $i$; a general element is $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf Q$ with the vector part $\mathbf Q=Q_1e_1+Q_2e_2+Q_3e_3$ and the complex vector product $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=Q_1^{2}+Q_2^{2}+Q_3^{2}$; the conjugate scalar is $n=Q_0^{2}+(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)$, which is the biquaternion norm, $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=n\,e_0$. The Hermitian conjugation is ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{\natural}$, the sectors are $\mathbb{M}_\pm=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=\pm\tilde Q\}$, and a complex structure is an element whose square is $-e_0$, an involution one whose square is $+e_0$, and a nilpotent a nonzero element whose square is $0$.

One Equation, Three Physical Answers

The three central equations differ only in the radicand, and the vector–scalar split solves them together. Writing a root as $\tilde P=P_0e_0+\mathbf P$, the equation $\tilde P^{2}=\tilde Q$ separates into one vector equation and one scalar equation,

$$ 2P_0\mathbf P=\mathbf Q, \qquad P_0^{2}-(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=Q_0, $$

and for the three central radicands $\mathbf Q=0$ and $Q_0\in\{-1,0,+1\}$. The vector equation then forces $\mathbf P=0$ or $P_0=0$, so a root is either a complex scalar $P_0e_0$ with $P_0^{2}=Q_0$, or a pure element $\mathbf P$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-Q_0$. The two branches are disjoint and exhaustive, and the three answer sets are read off them. This is the whole of the classification for the central values.

The physical content of the split is the separation it makes. The scalar branch is the central one: its roots commute with everything and ask nothing of the direction of space. The pure branch is the directional one: its roots are vectors, they single out a direction and a complex structure, and they carry the geometry of the patch. Every physical reading below is a reading of the pure branch.

The Roots of $-1$: the Local Complex Structure

For $\tilde P^{2}=-e_0$ the two branches give the two central roots and the pure elements of complex square one,

$$ \tilde P=\pm ie_0, \qquad\text{and}\qquad \tilde P=\mathbf P\ \text{pure with}\ (\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1 . $$

The central roots are the two elements of the centre that square to $-e_0$. The pure branch is the family $\{\mathbf P:(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1\}$, whose real members are the pure real vectors of unit length, that is the two-sphere $S^{2}$ of unit imaginary quaternions $\hat\mu=\mu_1e_1+\mu_2e_2+\mu_3e_3$ with $\mu_1^{2}+\mu_2^{2}+\mu_3^{2}=1$. Beyond the real sphere the complex members extend the family: writing $\mathbf P=\mathbf U+i\mathbf V$ with $\mathbf U,\mathbf V$ real, the single complex condition $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1$ is the pair $\lvert\mathbf U\rvert^{2}-\lvert\mathbf V\rvert^{2}=1$, $\mathbf U\!\cdot\!\mathbf V=0$.

Every root of $-1$ is a complex structure: an element $J$ with $J^{2}=-e_0$, whose left multiplication is a real-linear map of the algebra to itself with square $-\mathrm{id}$. The two-sphere $S^{2}$ is the quaternion algebra's own sphere of imaginary units, the familiar local imaginary units of the quaternion and spinor calculi, and the two central roots $\pm ie_0$ are the roots that commute with every element, with $ie_0$ the distinguished one that makes the algebra an algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ and that the corpus calls the scalar imaginary $i$; its negative $-ie_0$ carries the conjugate scalar structure.

The reading. An element that squares to $-1$ is read as a choice of what is called $i$ in a local patch, and the central imaginary is the distinguished, global choice. The remaining roots are local: each defines its own complex structure, and passing from one to another is a change of what plays the role of $i$. The physics of the reading — the local complex structure fixing the propagation of the fields and the speed of light of the patch — is owned by The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light, where the sphere $S^{2}$ is the set of local frames a field can be referred to.

The Roots of $0$: the Light Cone and Lightlike Propagation

For $\tilde P^{2}=0$ the branches give the trivial root and the pure null elements,

$$ \tilde P=0, \qquad\text{and}\qquad \tilde P=\mathbf P\ \text{pure with}\ (\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=0 . $$

The nonzero roots are the nilpotents: elements $\tilde P\neq0$ with $\tilde P^{2}=0$. They are exactly the pure null elements and they are a subset of the zero divisors, the elements of the complex norm cone $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=0$. An element of that cone which is not pure is a zero divisor that is not nilpotent, so the roots of $0$ are the pure part of the cone, and the whole cone is the framework's null cone in the algebra.

The reading. A nonzero element whose square vanishes is read as the algebraic form of a lightlike propagation: the element is not zero, yet composing it with itself annihilates it, the algebraic shadow of a displacement of zero interval. The cone as the light cone, the nilpotents as its pure part, and the zero divisors as the locus of the lightlike directions are owned by The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone and Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form. What is proved here is the classification: the roots of $0$ are $0$ together with the pure null elements, and the cone is larger than them.

The Roots of $+1$: Reality Conditions

For $\tilde P^{2}=+e_0$ the branches give the two central roots and the pure elements of complex square minus one,

$$ \tilde P=\pm e_0, \qquad\text{and}\qquad \tilde P=\mathbf P\ \text{pure with}\ (\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-1 . $$

Every root of $+1$ is an involution: an element $J$ with $J^{2}=e_0$, whose left multiplication has square $+\mathrm{id}$. The two central roots are the identity and its negative. The pure branch is the family of pure involutions, and its Hermitian members are the elements $i\hat\mu$ with $\hat\mu$ a real unit vector, which are the reflections $2\tilde\Pi-e_0$ attached to the minimal idempotents $\tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ of the informational sector. There is no real pure root of $+1$: a real vector $\mathbf q$ would need $(\mathbf q,\mathbf q)=\lvert\mathbf q\rvert^{2}=-1$. So apart from the two central roots the whole root set is complex, $\mathbf P=\mathbf U+i\mathbf V$ with $\mathbf U\!\cdot\!\mathbf V=0$ and $\lvert\mathbf V\rvert^{2}-\lvert\mathbf U\rvert^{2}=1$, of which the Hermitian members $\mathbf P=i\hat\mu$ are the reflections.

The reading. An involution is read as the algebra-side counterpart of a possible reality condition, a way of declaring which states are the "real" ones. The conditions themselves are antilinear maps, and it is the sign $J^{2}=\pm1$ that separates the real from the quaternionic type; the corpus's antilinear involutions of the algebra are the complex, Hermitian and anti-Hermitian conjugations, and the classification of the possible reality conditions into the real, complex and quaternionic particles is owned by Particle Types, Discrete Charge and Three-Particle Couplings, with the use of such a structure in the two kinds of mass by Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form, where the antilinear structures are read as the particle kinds. A root of $+1$ is the linear companion of that structure — an element $J$ with $J^{2}=e_0$ — and the Hermitian roots of the informational sector are the reflections $i\hat\mu=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$. The classification proved here is only that the roots of $+1$ are $\pm e_0$ together with the pure elements of complex square $-1$.

The Three Answers in One Table

The three answer sets are summarised in one table.

equation central roots pure roots what the root is the reading
$\tilde P^{2}=-e_0$ $\pm ie_0$ $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1$ complex structure $J$ a choice of what is called $i$ in a local patch
$\tilde P^{2}=0$ $0$ $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=0$ nilpotent, zero divisor lightlike propagation
$\tilde P^{2}=+e_0$ $\pm e_0$ $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-1$ involution $J$ a reality condition

Which subspace carries which answer. The three meanings are separated by the subspace in which the root is sought, and the table reads together with the six distinguished subspaces.

  • Sought in the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$, the roots are the central ones: $\pm ie_0$ for $-1$ and $\pm e_0$ for $+1$, the two complex structures and the two trivial involutions.
  • Sought pure, in the vector subspace, the roots are the sphere $S^{2}$ of real roots of $-1$ (with the wider complex family about it), the cone $\sum_k P_k^{2}=0$ of nilpotents for $0$, and the pure involutions for $+1$.
  • Sought in the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$, the roots of $+1$ include the Hermitian reflections $i\hat\mu=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$, the reality conditions of the state space.
  • Sought in the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$, the element $ie_0$ is a root of $-1$ and so is every real unit vector, so the distinguished central root and the whole sphere $S^{2}$ of real roots of $-1$ lie in the material sector, while $\mathbb{M}_-$ contains no root of $+1$ — the central roots $\pm e_0$ are Hermitian and a pure root would need a real vector of square $-1$ — and no nonzero root of $0$.

The equation is therefore one, and the physical name of its solution is decided by where in the algebra the solution lives. This is the content of the reading "three physical answers to one algebraic equation".

Square Roots of a General Element

For a general $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf Q$ the same split gives the whole classification. The vector equation forces $\mathbf P=\mathbf Q/(2P_0)$ whenever $\mathbf Q\neq0$, so a root is fixed by its scalar part $P_0$, whose square $x=P_0^{2}$ solves the reduced quadratic $4x^{2}-4Q_0x-(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=0$ of discriminant $16n$; its two solutions are $x_\pm=\tfrac12(Q_0\pm\sqrt n)$, and each nonzero solution contributes the pair $\pm(\sqrt x\,e_0+\mathbf Q/(2\sqrt x))$. The result is

condition on $\tilde Q$ root set
$\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)\neq0$, $n\neq0$ four roots, two pairs $\pm\tilde P$
$\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)\neq0$, $n=0$ two roots, a single pair $\pm\tilde P$
$\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=0$, $Q_0\neq0$ two roots, a single pair $\pm\tilde P$
$\mathbf Q\neq0$, $n=0$, $Q_0=0$ (nonzero nilpotent) no roots
$\mathbf Q=0$ (complex scalar) a continuum: $\pm\sqrt{Q_0}\,e_0$ and the pure $\mathbf P$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-Q_0$

The generic case is four roots. The complex square $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)$ decides whether a solution is lost at $x=0$, the conjugate scalar $n$ decides whether the two solutions of the reduced quadratic are distinct, and $\mathbf Q$ decides which of the two cases applies. The roots always come in $\pm$ pairs, so the root set is symmetric about the origin.

Remark (verified). For $\tilde Q=e_0+e_1$ one has $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=1$ and $n=2$, and the four roots have $P_0=\pm1.0987$ and $P_0=\pm0.4551\,i$, each squaring to $\tilde Q$; for the vector radicand $-(2+i)e_3$, with a complex coefficient and $n=3+4i\neq0$, the classification again returns four roots, and each listed root squares to its radicand to machine precision.

Every Material Four-Vector Has a Square Root

The fourth row of the table is the criterion for existence, and it reduces to one name. The conditions $\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=0$ and $Q_0=0$ say exactly that $\tilde Q\neq0$ with $\tilde Q^{2}=0$, so the rootless elements are precisely the nonzero nilpotents, and the rootless locus is the lightlike locus of the algebra.

Theorem (the square-root criterion). $\tilde Q$ has no square root if and only if $\tilde Q$ is a nonzero nilpotent. Every other element of the algebra has at least one square root.

Corollary (every material four-vector has a square root). No nonzero element of the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$ is nilpotent, so every element of $\mathbb{M}_-$ has a square root.

Proof. Write $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_-$ as $\tilde Q=ib_0e_0+\mathbf q$ with $b_0\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbf q$ a real vector. If $\tilde Q^{2}=0$ then the vector part $2ib_0\mathbf q$ vanishes, so $b_0=0$ or $\mathbf q=0$. If $b_0=0$ then $\tilde Q=\mathbf q$ and the scalar part $-(\mathbf q,\mathbf q)=-\lvert\mathbf q\rvert^{2}$ vanishes only for $\mathbf q=0$; if $\mathbf q=0$ then $\tilde Q=ib_0e_0$ and the scalar part $-b_0^{2}$ vanishes only for $b_0=0$. Either way $\tilde Q=0$.

The material sector is thus the sector with no refusal: every material four-vector is the square of some element of the algebra. The refusal is a property of the nilpotents, which lie in the vector subspace and in neither sector (the element $e_1+ie_2$ is one), and not of the material sector. The degenerate non-central cases are the lightlike material elements: for $\tilde Q=ie_0+e_1$ one has $n=0$, the radicand is on the light cone, the reduced quadratic has the double solution $x=Q_0/2=i/2$, and the root set is a single pair, so a lightlike material element has two roots and not four — the degeneracy is a doubling-down and not a refusal.

Remark (verified). On the material elements $3ie_0+e_1$, $ie_0$, $e_1$ and the null $ie_0+e_1$ the classification returns four, a continuum, four and two roots respectively, and each root squares to its radicand; the rootless case is met only on the nonzero nilpotents such as $e_1+ie_2$, and never inside $\mathbb{M}_-$.

The Mass Shell as a Slice of the Square Map

The square-root problem is the problem of inverting the ordinary square map $\sigma(\tilde P)=\tilde P^{2}$, and the mass shell uses a different square. The two must be kept apart; the companion article Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger makes the same distinction between the ordinary square and the sesquilinear one.

The natural square. The natural square is $\nu(\tilde P)=\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=N(\tilde P)e_0$, the biquaternion norm. It is $\mathbb{C}$-valued and it always lands in the centre: for every element the natural square is a multiple of $e_0$. On a material four-momentum $\tilde P=ict\,e_0+\mathbf x$ it is the interval, $N=-c^{2}t^{2}+\lvert\mathbf x\rvert^{2}$; its zero set on $\mathbb{M}_-$ is the light cone and its level set $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$ is the mass shell. The square of the material sector that carries the interval is the natural one, and it belongs to the companion quaternionic block.

The mass shell as a slice. Read with the natural square, the mass-shell condition is the requirement that the square of the four-momentum be the fixed scalar $-m^{2}c^{2}$, and the mass shell is the fibre of the natural square map over that scalar. That the square is a scalar is automatic for the natural square; what the mass-shell condition adds is the value. The mass is the label of the slice.

The scalar slice of the ordinary square. For completeness, the ordinary square $\sigma$ lands in the centre exactly when $2P_0\mathbf P=0$, that is when $\tilde P$ is central ($\mathbf P=0$) or pure ($P_0=0$). The scalar slice of $\sigma$ is therefore the union of the centre and the vector subspace, and on it the square takes the values $P_0^{2}$ and $-(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)$. This slice is larger than the mass shell and is not the mass shell; it is recorded because it is the set on which the ordinary square, like the natural one, is scalar, and it is the reason the two squares are easily confused.

The Reading, Labelled as Such

Proposed reading, partly speculative and labelled as such. A material quantity is read as an element $\tilde Q$ of the algebra, and the quantity is said to be realised by the algebra when $\tilde Q$ has a square root, so that it is the square of some other element. The criterion is then read as an existence condition on the quantity.

  • A quantity whose element is off the nilpotent locus is realised: it has a square root, and the root is the element of which it is the square.
  • A quantity whose element is a nonzero nilpotent is not realised: there is no element whose square it is, and the algebra refuses it.
  • The material sector carries no refusal: by the corollary above every material four-vector is realised.

The three central cases are read the same way: a quantity that is a complex structure, a nilpotent or an involution is a quantity whose square is one of the three distinguished values, and the three values are the three distinguished squares. The mass-shell reading adds that a four-momentum is a material element, that its natural square is the scalar $N(\tilde P)e_0$, and that the mass shell is the slice of the natural square map on which that scalar takes the value $-m^{2}c^{2}$.

What the framework does not claim. The identification of the square-root criterion with a physical existence condition is a proposal and not a result. The framework does not claim that the nilpotent locus is unphysical, that every material quantity is a square in the physical sense, or that the mass shell is derived from the square map; it does not fix a mass spectrum and does not say which roots are physical. The root set is not unique — a generic element has four roots and a complex scalar a continuum — so "the" square root of a quantity is not unique, and the reading has no preferred root until an extra principle selects one. That non-uniqueness is the standing weakness of the reading.

The Ledger

Proved. The roots of $-1$ are $\pm ie_0$ together with the pure $\mathbf P$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1$, whose real members form the two-sphere $S^{2}$ of unit imaginary quaternions; the roots of $0$ are $0$ together with the pure $\mathbf P$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=0$, the nilpotents, which are the pure part of the zero-divisor cone; the roots of $+1$ are $\pm e_0$ together with the pure $\mathbf P$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-1$, and the Hermitian members of the latter are the reflections $i\hat\mu=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$; each non-central root is pure, and each root is a complex structure, a nilpotent or an involution according to its value. For a general $\tilde Q$ the classification is the four rows of §Square Roots of a General Element; the criterion is that $\tilde Q$ has no square root if and only if it is a nonzero nilpotent; every element of the material sector has a square root; the conjugate scalar $n$ is the biquaternion norm; the root set is symmetric under $\tilde P\to-\tilde P$; and the scalar slice of the ordinary square is the union of the centre and the vector subspace. Verified numerically on the radicands $e_0+e_1$, $-(2+i)e_3$, $2e_0$, $e_1+ie_2$, $3ie_0+e_1$, $ie_0$, $e_1$ and $ie_0+e_1$.

Readings, partly speculative and labelled. That a root of $-1$ is a local complex structure and a choice of what is called $i$; that a nonzero root of $0$ is the algebraic form of a lightlike propagation; that a root of $+1$ is a reality condition, a possible conjugation of the state space; that a material quantity is realised by the algebra exactly when it has a square root, with the nonzero nilpotents the unrealisable locus; and that the mass shell is the slice of the natural square map on which the square is the scalar $-m^{2}c^{2}$. Each is a proposal about naming, not a result; the root sets, the criterion and the corollary are results.

Summary

The single equation $\tilde P^{2}=\tilde Q$ has, at its three distinguished central radicands, three answer sets, found from the vector–scalar split and collected in the table of §The Three Answers in One Table. The roots of $-1$ are the central $\pm ie_0$ and the pure elements of complex square one, whose real members are the two-sphere $S^{2}$ of unit imaginary quaternions; each is a complex structure, and the reading — a root of $-1$ is a choice of what is called $i$ in a local patch, with the central imaginary as the distinguished global one — is owned by The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light. The roots of $0$ are $0$ and the pure null elements, the nilpotents, the pure part of the zero-divisor cone; the reading of a nonzero element whose square vanishes as the algebraic form of a lightlike propagation is owned by The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone and Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form. The roots of $+1$ are the central $\pm e_0$ and the pure elements of complex square minus one, each an involution; the Hermitian ones are the reflections $i\hat\mu=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$ of the informational sector, and the reading of an involution as a reality condition is owned by Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form.

For a general element the roots number four, two, none or a four-real-parameter continuum according to the conditions of §Square Roots of a General Element; an element has no square root exactly when it is a nonzero nilpotent, and every element of the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$ has one, because $\mathbb{M}_-$ contains no nonzero nilpotent. The mass shell is a slice of the square map: the natural square $\nu(\tilde P)=\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=N(\tilde P)e_0$ always lands in the centre, and the mass shell is the fibre over the scalar $-m^{2}c^{2}$, whose level set on $\mathbb{M}_-$ is the mass shell of Biquaternion Norm and Invertibility. The reading of a material quantity as realised by the algebra exactly when it has a square root, and of the mass shell as the slice on which the square is a prescribed scalar, is partly speculative and labelled as such; the identification of the criterion with a physical condition is a proposal and not a result, and the standing weakness of the reading is the non-uniqueness of the root. The relation to the two kinds of mass runs through the roots of $+1$, the reality conditions of Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form.

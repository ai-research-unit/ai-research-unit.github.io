# __Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Lightlike at the Third Power__

## Introduction

Of the four general products of *The Four General Products of the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, the one that carries the framework's probabilities is the general plain sesquilinear product

$$
\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*},
$$

the general plain bilinear product with its second factor read through the Hermitian conjugation. It is the product of the **probability band**, read in *The Four General Products of the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space* and *The General Plain Sesquilinear Product on the Informational Sector*. This article reads that product at the central value $0$, and beside the square-zero question it reads the **powers** of the block.

The **roots of $0$** are the elements $\tilde\Xi$ with $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=0$; the **nilpotents** are the nonzero elements $\tilde\Upsilon$ with a vanishing power,

$$
\tilde\Upsilon^{\star k}=0\quad\text{for some }k\geq1,
$$

the power being the left-nested reading $\tilde X^{\star k}=\bigl(\cdots(\tilde X\star\tilde X)\star\tilde X\cdots\bigr)$, which for the block is $\tilde X^{\star k}=\tilde X(\tilde X^{*})^{k-1}$. The two subjects are one: a root of $0$ is a square-zero element, that is a nilpotent of **index two**, and the emptiness of the root set pushes the nilpotents of the block to the **next index**.

The answer is not the composition band's, and the difference is the whole article. The block has **no nonzero square-zero element**: the Hermitian square has the definite scalar part $\sum_\mu\lvert X_\mu\rvert^{2}$, which vanishes only at the origin, so the root set of $0$ is empty. But the block **has** nilpotents, and they are exactly the parent's **pure zero divisors**, the elements with vanishing scalar coordinate and isotropic vector part, of index **exactly three**. The classification is the mathematics of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*; it is cited and read here, not re-proved.

**The results.** The left-nested powers of the block are the conjugate-linear iterations $\tilde X^{\star k}=\tilde X(\tilde X^{*})^{k-1}$, so the index is a property of the pair $(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X),\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X)^{\dagger})$ and can exceed two. The scalar part of the square vanishes only at the origin, so the root set of $0$ is **empty** and there is no nonzero square-zero element; the nonzero nilpotents are exactly the pure zero divisors, the elements $\Upsilon_0=0$ with $C(\tilde\Upsilon)=(\vec\Upsilon,\vec\Upsilon)=0$, of index **exactly three**, $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star2}\neq0$ and $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star3}=0$. The nilpotent set is the nonzero **pure isotropic cone**, of complex dimension two, which is the parent's pure zero-divisor cone with the index changed from two to three; the non-pure zero divisors are zero divisors of the block and are **not** nilpotent.

**The physical interpretation.** The content added here is a reading, and it is the reading of a set that the probability band renders invisible at the square. A nonzero element whose block-square vanished would be the algebraic form of a **lightlike propagation** of the probability band; the definite square denies it, so the band has **no lightlike element** in the sense of the composition band's light cone. What the band does carry is the same cone one power higher: its nilpotents are the parent's pure isotropic cone, annihilated not at the second power but at the **third**, so a lightlike direction of the probability band is a direction that survives one more step. The reading of this article is therefore **the lightlike at the third power**. Beside it stands a second reading, which the square supplies and the third power closes: the square of a nilpotent of the block is a **pure state**, and the two squares of a nilpotent, the one in the element and the one in its conjugate, are a **complete orthogonal pair of pure states**, that is a **Peirce frame**. A nilpotent of the block is thus not only an element that dies at the third step; it is a step that **carries** a frame, and the observable of square one that indexes the frame is read off the nilpotent itself.

The roots of $+1$ and the idempotents are *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Unitary Group and the Pure States*; the frames and the Peirce decomposition are *Ideals and the Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Populations and the Coherences*. The classification is *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*; the zero-divisor set of the block is *Zero Divisors of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*; the powers, the square-zero element and the index are *Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*; the square and the definiteness of its scalar part are *Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Sesqualgebra* and *The Squares and the Positive Cone of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra*; the matrix model is *The General Plain Sesqualgebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$*; the composition band's light cone and its nilpotents are *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents: the Light Cone* and *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Algebra*; the index shift is recorded in *The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Algebraic Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$* and in *Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared*; and the probability weight that refuses the square is *Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger*.

**Conventions.** The sesqualgebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^2=-1$. A general element is $\tilde Q=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}Q_\mu e_\mu=Q_0e_0+\vec Q$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$ and $\vec Q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q_ke_k$. The **Hermitian conjugation** is $\tilde Q^{*}=\overline{Q_0}-\overline{\vec Q}$; the **natural** conjugation is $\tilde Q^{\natural}=Q_0-\vec Q$. The **product** is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$, and the **central square** is

$$
C(\tilde Q)=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}Q_\mu^{2}=Q_0^{2}+(\vec Q,\vec Q),\qquad(\vec P,\vec Q)=\sum_{k=1}^{3}P_kQ_k .
$$

The **probability weight** is $H(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}$. An element is **pure** when $Q_0=0$, that is when it lies in the vector subspace. A root of a central value is written $\tilde\Xi$ (§*The Root Convention* of *Conventions in Mathematics*), a nilpotent $\tilde\Upsilon$ (§*The Nilpotent Convention*), and a general element $\tilde X$ whose powers are at stake. The sectors are $\mathbb{M}_\pm=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=\pm\tilde Q\}$. The origin is a nilpotent, the **trivial** one, and the article is concerned with the nonzero ones. Nothing topological and nothing metric appears.

## The Powers of the Block

**The left-nested powers of the block are the conjugate-linear iterations $\tilde X^{\star k}=\tilde X(\tilde X^{*})^{k-1}$, so the index is a property of the pair of a matrix and its conjugate transpose and not of the plain square, and it can be larger than two.**

The power is the left-nested product $\tilde X^{\star(k+1)}=\tilde X^{\star k}\star\tilde X$. By induction,

$$
\tilde X^{\star k}=\tilde X\bigl(\tilde X^{*}\bigr)^{k-1},
$$

so that $\tilde X^{\star2}=\tilde X\tilde X^{*}$, $\tilde X^{\star3}=\tilde X\tilde X^{*}\tilde X^{*}$, and in the matrix model

$$
\mathsf{M}_2\bigl(\tilde X^{\star k}\bigr)=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X)\,\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X)^{\dagger(k-1)} ,
$$

using the multiplicativity $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde P)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)^{\dagger}$ and the identification $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X^{*})=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X)^{\dagger}$. The square of the block is the **Hermitian square** $\tilde X\tilde X^{*}$ and not the plain square $\tilde X^{2}$: the successive powers multiply the element by the successive powers of its conjugate, so the index is a property of the pair $(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X),\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X)^{\dagger})$. The plain element may be a square-zero element of the algebra and have a higher block-index, and it is this gap that the article reads.

## The Roots of Zero Are Absent

**The block has no root of $0$: the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=0$ has the single solution $\tilde\Xi=0$, so the root set of $0$ is empty and there is no nonzero square-zero element. The nilpotents of the block are at the next index.**

A **root of $0$** is a nonzero element $\tilde\Xi$ with $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=0$, that is a nonzero square-zero element. By the reduction of *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*, §*The Reduction*, the scalar part of the square is

$$
\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=H(\tilde\Xi),
$$

positive for every nonzero element and zero only at the origin. The scalar part of $0$ is $0$, so a square-zero element has every coordinate zero; the root set of $0$ is empty. In the matrix model the square is $AA^{\dagger}$, positive semidefinite, whose vanishing forces $A=0$; the same statement is the one of *Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, where the square-zero set of the block is the origin alone.

**The vanishing scalar part is the refusal of the lightlike square.** The composition band obtains its roots of $0$ from a square whose scalar part, $Q_0^{2}-(\vec Q,\vec Q)$, is indefinite, and the vanishing of the scalar part is a **cone**, the nilpotent cone. The block's square has a **definite** scalar part, the probability weight, so its vanishing locus is the origin and not a cone: the probability band attaches no lightlike displacement to a vanishing square. The contrast is the sharpest single statement of *Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared*, and it is the reason this article turns to the index.

## The Nilpotents

**For a nonzero element the following are equivalent: it is a nilpotent of the block, its third power vanishes, it is pure with vanishing central square, and it is a pure zero divisor of the plain product. The index is then exactly three.**

A **nilpotent** is a nonzero element $\tilde\Upsilon$ with $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star k}=0$ for some $k\geq1$. The criterion is the one of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*, recalled here.

**The criterion.** For $\tilde\Upsilon\neq0$ the following are equivalent:

1. $\tilde\Upsilon$ is a nilpotent of the block;
2. $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star3}=0$;
3. $\tilde\Upsilon$ is pure and of vanishing central square, $\Upsilon_0=0$ and $C(\tilde\Upsilon)=(\vec\Upsilon,\vec\Upsilon)=0$;
4. $\tilde\Upsilon$ is a pure zero divisor of the plain product.

When they hold the index is exactly three: $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star2}\neq0$.

**The matrix proof in one line.** Write $A=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde\Upsilon)$; a nilpotent has $\det A=C(\tilde\Upsilon)=0$ and, being nonzero, $\operatorname{rank}A=1$, and the rank-one traceless matrices are exactly the $A=\sigma\,\vec u\vec v^{\dagger}$ with $\vec u^{\dagger}\vec v=0$; then $A(A^{\dagger})^{m}=0$ for some $m$ exactly when $\operatorname{Tr}A=0$, and then $m=2$ already gives $A(A^{\dagger})^{2}=0$, that is $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star3}=0$, while $AA^{\dagger}\neq0$ because it has rank one. So $\operatorname{Tr}A=0$, that is $\Upsilon_0=0$, is the criterion, and the block's nilpotents are the rank-one **traceless** matrices.

**The witness.** The element

$$
\tilde\Upsilon=e_1+ie_2
$$

has $\Upsilon_0=0$ and $C(\tilde\Upsilon)=1+i^{2}=0$, so it is a nilpotent; its square is

$$
\tilde\Upsilon^{\star2}=\tilde\Upsilon\tilde\Upsilon^{*}=2e_0+2ie_3\neq0,\qquad \tilde\Upsilon^{\star3}=\tilde\Upsilon\bigl(\tilde\Upsilon^{*}\bigr)^{2}=0 ,
$$

of index exactly three. The same element is a square-zero element of the plain algebra, $(e_1+ie_2)^{2}=0$; the index is a property of the product, and the block raises it by one.

**The nilpotent set.** The nonzero nilpotents are the nonzero elements of the **pure isotropic cone**

$$
\mathcal{N}_\star=\{\tilde\Upsilon\neq0:\ \Upsilon_0=0,\ (\vec\Upsilon,\vec\Upsilon)=0\},
$$

of complex dimension two and real dimension four, which is the pure zero-divisor cone of the composition band. Every nilpotent is a two-sided zero divisor, and the non-pure zero divisors, the nonzero complex multiples of the non-trivial idempotents with $\Upsilon_0\neq0$, are **not** nilpotent. So the block keeps the parent's **set**, its criterion $\Upsilon_0=0$ and its partition into a pure and a non-pure family, and changes the parent's **index**, from two to three.

## The Square of a Nilpotent

**The square of a nilpotent is a pure state of the block, and the two squares of a nilpotent, the one of the element and the one of its conjugate, are a complete orthogonal pair of pure states. Read together with their normalisation, they are the frame of an observable of square one, and the observable is read off the nilpotent itself.**

The reading rests on one computation, and the computation is the matrix model of *The General Plain Sesqualgebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$* read on a rank-one traceless matrix. A nilpotent of the block has $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde\Upsilon)=A=\sigma\,\vec u\vec v^{\dagger}$ with $\sigma>0$, $\vec u,\vec v$ orthonormal, $\vec u^{\dagger}\vec v=0$; then

$$
\tilde\Upsilon^{\star2}=\tilde\Upsilon\tilde\Upsilon^{*}\ \longleftrightarrow\ AA^{\dagger}=\sigma^{2}\vec u\vec u^{\dagger},\qquad
\tilde\Upsilon^{*}\tilde\Upsilon\ \longleftrightarrow\ A^{\dagger}A=\sigma^{2}\vec v\vec v^{\dagger},
$$

and both matrices are Hermitian, positive and of rank one. The scalar part of the first is the probability weight $H(\tilde\Upsilon)>0$, so the **normalised square**

$$
\tilde\Pi_+=\frac{\tilde\Upsilon\tilde\Upsilon^{*}}{2\,H(\tilde\Upsilon)},\qquad H(\tilde\Upsilon)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon\bigr),
$$

satisfies $\tilde\Pi_+^{2}=\tilde\Pi_+$ and $\tilde\Pi_+^{*}=\tilde\Pi_+$, that is, it is an **idempotent of the block**, hence a **pure state** by *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Unitary Group and the Pure States*. The same holds for the second square, giving the pure state

$$
\tilde\Pi_-=\frac{\tilde\Upsilon^{*}\tilde\Upsilon}{2\,H(\tilde\Upsilon)},\qquad \tilde\Pi_++\tilde\Pi_-=e_0,\qquad \tilde\Pi_+\tilde\Pi_-=0 .
$$

The two states are orthogonal and complete, because $\vec u$ and $\vec v$ are orthonormal; they are therefore the **two members of a Peirce frame**, and the Hermitian element

$$
\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Pi_+-\tilde\Pi_-=\frac{\tilde\Upsilon\tilde\Upsilon^{*}-\tilde\Upsilon^{*}\tilde\Upsilon}{2\,H(\tilde\Upsilon)}
$$

is the **observable of square one** of that frame: it is Hermitian and $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_0$, a Hermitian root of $+1$. The standard ladder realises the computation exactly. The elements

$$
\hat\Upsilon_1=\tfrac{ie_1-e_2}{2},\qquad \hat\Upsilon_2=\hat\Upsilon_1^{*}=\tfrac{ie_1+e_2}{2}
$$

are nilpotents of the block, of probability weight $H=\tfrac12$, and

$$
\hat\Upsilon_1^{\star2}=\hat\Upsilon_1\hat\Upsilon_2=\tilde\Pi_+=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0+ie_3\bigr),\qquad
\hat\Upsilon_2^{\star2}=\hat\Upsilon_2\hat\Upsilon_1=\tilde\Pi_-=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0-ie_3\bigr),
$$

so the two squares **are** the pair of the observable $ie_3$: a nilpotent of the block generates a frame of the block.

**The physical interpretation.** A nilpotent is read as a **step**, and its square as the **state the step lands in**. The step is not iterable — the third power vanishes, and no fourth power is reached — and the two states it carries are orthogonal and complete, so the step joins the two ends of a frame. The element and its conjugate land in the two opposite states, and the observable of the frame is their difference, read off the two squares. In the composition band the same elements are the **fermionic ladder** of one mode, the pair $\hat\Upsilon_1,\hat\Upsilon_2$ with $\hat\Upsilon_1\hat\Upsilon_2=\tilde\Pi_+$ and $\hat\Upsilon_2\hat\Upsilon_1=\tilde\Pi_-$ of *Fock Space and Creation/Annihilation Operators in Biquaternionic Form*, read as the ladder by *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*. The reading here is the probability band's and is **not** that one: the ladder is a reading of the plain product, where the two elements are square-zero, while the block's reading attaches to the same two elements the index three and the pair of states. The two readings share the carrier and not the product, and the article keeps them apart.

**What the reading does not claim.** It does not claim that a nilpotent is a physical particle, field or transition, or that the two states it carries are its own measurement outcomes. It does not claim that the frame of a nilpotent is unique: the normalised squares depend on the element, and distinct nilpotents of the same isotropic line carry the same frame only up to the phase between $\vec u$ and $\vec v$. The computation of the two squares, of their orthogonality, of their completeness and of the observable $\tilde\Xi$ is a result; the step-and-state reading is a proposal about naming.

## The Lightlike at the Third Power

**The probability band has no lightlike element of index two and carries the parent's lightlike cone at index three; the lightlike displacement of the composition band becomes a three-step decay in the probability band, and on the material sector the block attaches no nilpotent at all.**

The reading is the following.

- **Nothing nonzero squares to nothing.** The block's square has the definite probability weight as scalar part, so the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=0$ forces the origin; the probability band's lightlike set at the square is empty. A band that measures attaches no vanishing displacement to a single step.
- **The same cone one power higher.** The parent's pure isotropic cone is kept, and its elements are annihilated by the **third** power. A direction of the cone carries a magnitude-free step, and the step is annihilated after two applications; the block reads the same direction as a step annihilated after three. This is the sharpest illustration of the sentence of *Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space* that the index of nilpotency is a property of the product and not of the element; the owners of the index shift are *The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Algebraic Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$* and *Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared*.
- **The cone is the wall of invertibility.** The block's zero-divisor set is the vanishing set of the central square, $C(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=0$, the same cone as the composition band's; an element is one-sidedly invertible exactly for $C\neq0$, with the one-sided inverse $\bar{\tilde X}/\overline{C(\tilde X)}$ of §*The One-Sided Inverse Equations* of *Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*. The nilpotent cone is the **pure part** of that wall, and the non-pure part, the multiples of the non-trivial idempotents, is not nilpotent. A lightlike element of the probability band is not reversible, exactly as in the composition band, but it is so one power later.
- **The material sector carries none.** A nilpotent of the block is pure, with vanishing scalar coordinate, so it lies in the vector subspace and in **neither** sector; the material four-vector $\tilde T=ict\,e_0+\vec x$ of $\mathbb{M}_-$ is anti-Hermitian with a nonzero scalar coordinate and is never nilpotent. The physical null cone cut by $\mathbb{M}_-$ is the **non-pure** part of the zero-divisor cone, as *The General Plain Sesquilinear Product on the Material Sector* and *The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition* record; the block's roots of $0$ and its nilpotents are algebraic objects of the vector subspace, and the physical reading of the cone is the composition band's.

**What the reading says and does not say.** It says that the probability band refuses the square-zero and keeps the nilpotent cone at index three, and that its nilpotents carry frames. It does **not** say that the physical light cone is absent from the framework — the cone is the same set as the composition band's, and the physical light cone is its non-pure part in the material sector — nor that the index shift is a metric statement: the index belongs to the product, and the metric enters only when the material sector is cut out.

## The Interpretation

The two readings of the article are one: **the probability band has no lightlike square, its lightlike cone lives at the third power, and each of its nilpotents carries a frame.**

- The root set of $0$ is **empty**: the definite probability weight refuses the nonzero square-zero element, in exact contrast with the composition band, whose square is indefinite and whose roots of $0$ are the nilpotent cone.
- The nilpotents are the parent's **pure isotropic cone**, of complex dimension two, of index **exactly three**: the same set as the composition band's, one index higher, because the index is the product's.
- The **square of a nilpotent is a pure state**, and the two squares of a nilpotent are a **complete orthogonal pair of pure states**; the observable of square one that indexes the frame is their difference, and the standard ladder realises the computation with $\hat\Upsilon_1^{\star2}=\tilde\Pi_+$ and $\hat\Upsilon_2^{\star2}=\tilde\Pi_-$.
- The **non-pure zero divisors** are zero divisors of the block and are not nilpotent, so the nilpotent set is strictly smaller than the zero-divisor set, exactly as in the composition band.

The classification is the mathematics of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*; the power formula, the emptiness of the root set, the criterion and the index are results, and the lightlike-at-the-third-power and the nilpotent-carries-a-frame readings are proposals about naming.

## Summary

The general plain sesquilinear product of the biquaternion space is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$. Its left-nested powers are the conjugate-linear iterations $\tilde X^{\star k}=\tilde X(\tilde X^{*})^{k-1}$, of matrix model $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde X)^{\dagger(k-1)}$, so the index is a property of the pair of a matrix and its conjugate transpose. The scalar part of the square is the definite probability weight $\sum_\mu\lvert X_\mu\rvert^{2}$, which vanishes only at the origin, so the **root set of $0$ is empty**: the block has no nonzero square-zero element and no nilpotent of index two. The value $0$ is treated in the sense that the origin is the trivial nilpotent and the root set of $0$ has no nonzero member.

The **nilpotents** of the block are exactly the parent's **pure zero divisors**: a nonzero element is nilpotent exactly when it is pure and of vanishing central square, $\Upsilon_0=0$ and $C(\tilde\Upsilon)=(\vec\Upsilon,\vec\Upsilon)=0$, equivalently when it is a pure zero divisor of the plain product. The index is **exactly three**, $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star2}\neq0$ and $\tilde\Upsilon^{\star3}=0$; the witness $e_1+ie_2$ has $\star2$-power $2e_0+2ie_3$ and $\star3$-power zero. The nilpotent set is the nonzero pure isotropic cone $\mathcal{N}_\star$, of complex dimension two and real dimension four; every nilpotent is a two-sided zero divisor, and the non-pure zero divisors are not nilpotent.

The readings are these. The probability band has **no lightlike element at the square** and carries the parent's pure isotropic cone at the **third power** — the lightlike at the third power. The **square of a nilpotent is a pure state**, and the two squares of a nilpotent are a **complete orthogonal pair of pure states**, the frame of the observable of square one that is their difference; the standard ladder gives $\hat\Upsilon_1^{\star2}=\tilde\Pi_+$, $\hat\Upsilon_2^{\star2}=\tilde\Pi_-$. The zero-divisor cone is the wall of invertibility, the same cone as the composition band's, and its pure part alone is nilpotent. The material sector carries no nilpotent; the physical null cone is the non-pure part of the cone cut by $\mathbb{M}_-$. The readings are proposals about naming, labelled as such; the power formula, the emptiness of the root set, the criterion, the index and the two-squares computation are results.

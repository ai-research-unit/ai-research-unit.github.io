# __Square Roots of a General Element in the 12 Structures Framework__

## Introduction

The root problem of a general central value asks which elements square to a given value in each of the
twelve products: for a value $\lambda e_0$ with a general complex $\lambda$, the solutions of
$\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$. The three named values $-1$, $0$ and $+1$ are read in the preceding
companion, *Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the 12 Structures Framework*; here the value
stays general, and the question is the one a general value asks: for which $\lambda$ does a root exist,
and what is the root set. This article is the fourth physics companion of the mathematics comparison
group, and its mathematics companion is *Square Roots of a General Element*
(`articles_maths/square-roots-of-a-general-element.md`).

The physics is already present in the corpus under a different scope. *Square Roots: the Local Complex
Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell* reads the general equation for the ordinary product and
draws the three physical objects from the three named values. *Biquaternion Norm and Invertibility* owns
the interval and the criterion of invertibility. The present article adds the structural reading that the
mathematics companion supplies: the four general products have four different root sets at every value,
and the twelve structures fall into four existence classes according to which values they can reach.

The mathematics companion proves two facts that organise the physics. First, the four general products
answer the root problem in four genuinely different ways: the plain square reduces to a vector-scalar
pair, the natural square to the single equation of the central square, the plain sesquilinear square to a
non-negative scalar, and the quaternionic sesquilinear square to the indefinite Krein value. The four
products have **one common degeneracy** — the same cone is the zero-divisor set of all four — but **four
different root sets** at every value, because the square of a product is not the norm of a product.
Second, the twelve structures split into four **existence classes**: every complex value is read by the
four bilateral structures, every real value by the two indefinite sesquilinear ones, every non-negative
real value by the two definite sesquilinear ones, and the value $0$ alone by the four antisymmetric ones.
Each class has a direct physical content. The bilateral class is the composition band, whose central
values carry an arbitrary phase; the indefinite class is the band whose value is an interval, real by
nature; the definite class is the probability band, whose value is a non-negative scalar; and the
antisymmetric class is the bracket, whose square is an infinitesimal object and reaches the value $0$
alone.

**Conventions.** We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is
$\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_k^{2}=-e_0$;
$i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and
${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The **central square** is $C(\tilde\Xi)e_0=\tilde\Xi^{\natural}
\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{\natural}$ with $C(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\Xi_\mu^{2}$; the **Hermitian square**
is $H(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$; the **Krein value** is
$\Delta(\tilde\Xi)=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$. The material coordinate is
$\tilde\Xi=i c t\,e_0+\mathbf x$. The twelve structures are cited by their three-letter names.

## The Root Problem at a General Value

The four general products read the square in four ways, and the mathematics companion reduces each to an
equation on the scalar part and the vector part.

| the product | its square | the equation of the root of $\lambda$ |
|---|---|---|
| GPA, $\tilde\Xi\tilde Q$ | $\bigl(\Xi_0^{2}-(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)\bigr)e_0+2\Xi_0\mathbf\Xi$ | $\Xi_0^{2}-(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=\lambda$ and $2\Xi_0\mathbf\Xi=0$ |
| GQA, $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde Q$ | $C(\tilde\Xi)e_0$ | $C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$ |
| GPS, $\tilde\Xi\tilde Q^{*}$ | $\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$ | $\mathrm{Sc}=\lambda$ with $\mathrm{Sc}=H(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$ |
| GQS, $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ | the Krein square | $\mathrm{Sc}=\lambda$ with $\mathrm{Sc}=\Delta(\tilde\Xi)=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$ |

The plain square is an element with both a scalar and a vector part, and the root equation forces one of
the two parts to vanish: either the element is central, giving the two central roots $\pm\sqrt\lambda\,e_0$,
or it is pure, giving the quadric $-(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=\lambda$ in the vector subspace. The natural
square is already central, so its root equation is one complex equation and the root set is a level set of
the interval. The third square is Hermitian with the non-negative scalar part $H$, and the fourth has the
indefinite scalar part $\Delta$. Only the natural product has a single scalar equation for its root set;
the other three keep a vector equation or a definite scalar, and the difference is the physics of the
bands.

## The Plain Product: Every Value Is Realised

The plain product answers the root problem for **every** complex value. The two central roots
$\pm\sqrt\lambda\,e_0$ are the scalar processes, and the pure quadric $-(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=\lambda$ is
the vector family, of four real parameters, the element $\sqrt{-\lambda}\,e_1$ being one of its members.

The physical reading is that the composition band realises any central value. The value $\lambda$ is an
arbitrary complex number, carrying both an amplitude and a phase, and the composition produces it either
by a scalar process, a pure phase and scale, or by a vector process, a direction whose square is the
value. The two options are the two families of the plain reduction, the scalar line and the vector
subspace, and the corpus reads their physical content in *The Anti-Hermitian Subspace M₋ as the Material
Sector*, *The Hermitian Subspace M₊ as the Informational Sector* and *The Interval as the Square and the
Charge of the Material Composition*. At $\lambda=0$ the two central
roots collapse to the origin and the vector family becomes the pure null cone: the only operation that
squares to nothing is a lightlike displacement, and the value $0$ is the one value that has no scalar
process beside it. The physical object is the photon, read in *The Photon as a Null Element: What the Cone
Derives and What It Does Not*.

## The Natural Product: the Root Is the Shell of the Interval

The natural square of an element is its central square, $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi=C(\tilde\Xi)e_0$,
so the root equation is the single complex equation $C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$. The mathematics companion
proves that the root set is the cone $C=0$ at $\lambda=0$ and the nondegenerate quadric $C=\lambda$ at
$\lambda\neq0$, each of real dimension six, and that the set is non-empty for every $\lambda$.

The physical reading is the sharpest of the four. Squaring in the natural product is taking the interval
of the element, and the root set of the value $\lambda$ is the set of elements whose interval is
$\lambda$. The root problem is therefore the **shell problem**: the level sets of the interval are the
mass shells, and a root of the value is an element living on the shell of that value. This is the reading
the corpus gives in *Square Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell* and in
*Biquaternion Norm and Invertibility*: the mass shell is the slice on which the square of a four-momentum
is a fixed scalar, and the natural product is the product whose square makes the statement an identity
rather than an equation. Because the natural product is bilateral, every complex value has a shell, and
the shells of the real values are the timelike and spacelike shells of the material sector; the shell of
$0$ is the light cone itself, the largest square-zero set of the four products and the same set that the
companion article *Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures Framework* reads as the degeneracy of the four
general products.

## The Two Sesquilinear Products: Probability and Interval

**The plain sesquilinear product: a probability is non-negative.** The third square is Hermitian with
scalar part $H=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$, so a root of $\lambda$ exists exactly for a real
$\lambda\geq0$. At $\lambda=0$ the root set is the origin alone, and at $\lambda>0$ it is the family
$e^{i\theta}w$ over the real quaternions of modulus $\sqrt\lambda$, of real dimension four; at
$\lambda=1$ it is the unitary group $U(2)$. The physical reading is the positivity of probability. The
scalar part of the third square is the modulus squared of the amplitude, so the band that measures a
probability can only produce a non-negative value; a negative value has no root there, and the value $0$
is reached by the origin alone. The physical statement is that the pairing has **no nonzero square-zero
element**: a nonzero amplitude never squares to nothing, so masslessness does not live in the probability
band. The roots of a positive value are the amplitudes with the modulus $\sqrt\lambda$, and at
$\lambda=1$ the family is the unitary group, that is the reversible transformations of the state space,
read in *Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action*.

**The quaternionic sesquilinear product: the interval is real.** The fourth square has the indefinite
scalar part $\Delta=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$, real for every element, so a root
of $\lambda$ exists exactly for a real $\lambda$ and every real value has roots. The root set of a real
$\lambda$ is the family $\tilde\Xi=c(\hat\mu+it)$ over the real unit vectors $\hat\mu$, of real dimension
four, with $(t^{2}-1)\lvert c\rvert^{2}=\lambda$, together with the central circle $\lvert a\rvert^{2}=\lambda$
when $\lambda$ is positive. The physical reading is the reality of the interval. The gauge sesquilinear
band produces a real value because the quantity it squares to is an interval, and the three ranges of the
slope $t$ are its three causal classes: the slopes inside the unit, at the unit and outside the unit.
The owner of the parametrisation and of the indefinite metric is *The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite
Metric*, and the null family at the unit slope is read in *The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot
Normalise*.

## The Four Existence Classes and the Four Bands

The mathematics companion proves the four existence classes, and each is a physical statement about a
band.

| class | the structures | the values that have a root | the physical content |
|---|---|---|---|
| bilateral | GPA, SPA, GQA, SQA | every complex $\lambda$ | the composition band realises any central value, phase and amplitude together |
| indefinite sesquilinear | GQS, SQS | every real $\lambda$, no other | the band whose value is an interval, real by nature |
| definite sesquilinear | GPS, SPS | every real $\lambda\geq0$, no other | the probability band, whose value is a non-negative scalar |
| antisymmetric | APA, AQA, APS, AQS | $\lambda=0$ alone | a bracket is an infinitesimal transformation, and its square is a pure or vanishing object |

- **The bilateral class.** The four structures whose square is the plain square or the central square
  read every complex value. Physically they are the bands of composition and of the interval: a
  composition can produce any central value, phase included, and the natural square produces the interval.
  The phase is available because nothing in these squares is restricted to be real.
- **The indefinite class.** The fourth product and its symmetrisation read every real value and no other.
  Physically the interval that the gauge band produces is real; a non-real value is not an interval and
  has no root. The family of the slope is the four-vector of the given interval, and the three ranges of
  the slope are the three causal classes.
- **The definite class.** The third product and its symmetrisation read the non-negative real values
  alone. Physically a probability is non-negative, and the band that measures it cannot produce a negative
  value. This is the only class of the twelve that refuses a real value, and the refusal is the
  positivity of the squared amplitude.
- **The antisymmetric class.** The four brackets read the value $0$ alone. Physically a bracket is the
  infinitesimal transformation of a group, an element of its Lie algebra, and its square is again an
  infinitesimal object: it vanishes identically for the two alternating brackets and is a pure vector for
  the two conjugate-alternating ones, so it never equals a central value except the zero one. The value
  $0$ is reached by the whole space in the two alternating cases and by a proper cone in the two
  conjugate-alternating ones, and the class is read in *The Operators of the Twelve Structures:
  Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket*.

## What Agrees and What Does Not

The mathematics companion states the distinction that the physics needs: **the four general products have
one zero-divisor set and four root sets.** The zero-divisor set of each of the four is the punctured cone,
the same set read four times, because the four products carry the same norm; the root sets are different,
because the square of a product is not the norm of a product.

The physical reading is the separation of two questions that are often confused. The question "which
elements are non-invertible" has one answer across the four bands: the lightlike elements, the cone. The
question "which elements square to a given value, and how many of them are there" has four answers, one
per band. Degeneracy is universal; factorisation is band-specific. The element $\sqrt{-\lambda}\,e_1$
squares to $\lambda e_0$ in the plain product and answers for every complex value; the level set
$C=\lambda$ answers in the natural product; the origin alone or the family $e^{i\theta}w$ answers in the
third; and the family of the slope answers in the fourth. At the value $0$ the four root sets are the
nilpotent cone of real dimension four, the cone $C=0$ of real dimension six, the origin alone and the
square-zero family, four different sets, while the zero-divisor set is one cone in all four. The owner of
the shared cone is *Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures Framework*, and the owner of the mass shell is
*Square Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell*.

## The Value in the Physics Corpus: Mass, Weight and Interval

The general value $\lambda$ is the number the root problem sets, and the four bands read it as four
different physical quantities. The comparison is the subject of *Composition and Probability: the General
Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared*, and the word the corpus uses
across them is mass, whose senses *The Four Readings of Mass: Norm, Rank, Charge and Scale* separates.

**The value is a level of the interval.** For the natural product the root equation is
$C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$, and $C$ is the interval of the element. The root set of $\lambda$ is the set of
elements whose interval is $\lambda$, and on the material coordinate the level set $N=-m^2c^2$ is the mass
shell. *Square Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell* and *Biquaternion
Norm and Invertibility* read the value as the interval, the root problem as the shell problem, and the
sign of $\lambda$ as the causal class of the element; *The Symplectic Form and the Biquaternion Norm Cone*
reads the same level sets as the energy surfaces of the material dynamics. The value $0$ is the cone, and
the three signs of the real values are the spacelike, null and timelike regimes.

**The value is a weight of a probability.** In the probability band the root equation is
$H(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$ with $H=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$ real and non-negative, so the band reads
exactly the non-negative values and a root of $\lambda>0$ is an amplitude of weight $\lambda$. *Why
Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone* and *The Born Rule as a
Trace Formula — Derivation and Comparison* read $H$ as the Born weight, and the band refuses $-1$ for the
reason a probability cannot be negative: its form is positive definite. The origin is the only root of
$0$, so no nonzero amplitude squares to nothing. The class is thus fixed by two facts: the band that
measures probability has no clock of its own and no lightlike displacement.

**The value is an interval of the gauge band.** In the gauge band the root equation is
$\Delta(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$ with $\Delta=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$, the indefinite
Krein form, so the band reads exactly the real values and the sign of $\lambda$ is the causal class. *The
Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric* and *The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise* read the
form as the gauge metric, and the reason the band reads only the reals is that an interval is real. The
root family $c(\hat\mu+it)$ of the mathematics companion makes the reading explicit: the slope $t$
parametrises the family and the three ranges $\lvert t\rvert<1$, $\lvert t\rvert=1$, $\lvert t\rvert>1$
are the timelike, null and spacelike classes. The same number that is a weight in one band is a squared
interval in the other.

**The value is a scale.** The four bilateral structures read every complex value, because a central value
there carries an amplitude and a phase; the passage from the root of $1$ to the root of $\lambda$ is a
scaling, and *The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions* and *Conformal Invariance and the
Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form* read that scaling as the polar modulus and as a conformal dilation.
A mass shell is a unit shell scaled by $m$, and the scale reading is the fourth of the four readings of
mass. The general value therefore has a phase in the composition band, a weight in the probability band, an
interval in the gauge band and a magnitude everywhere: one number, and the band chooses which of its
aspects is read.

## The Sign of the Value: the Three Causal Shells

The value $\lambda$ has a magnitude and a sign, and for the natural product the sign is a causal class. The
root equation $C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$ is the shell equation, and the three signs of a real $\lambda$ are the
three shells of the material sector.

**The three shells.** The interval $C=N$ is negative on the two timelike components, zero on the cone and
positive on the spacelike region, as *Biquaternion Norm and Invertibility* computes for the material
sector. The root set of a **negative** value is therefore the massive shell: the timelike elements of
squared interval $-m^2c^2$, the trajectories of a particle with a rest frame. The root set of **zero** is
the cone, the massless shell. The root set of a **positive** value is the spacelike shell, the locus a
superluminal or tachyonic displacement would occupy. *Square Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light
Cone and the Mass Shell* reads the three as the three causal classes, and the sign of the radicand is the
name of the class.

**Invertibility follows from nonzero-ness, not from the sign.** A nonzero value has root elements that are
units and the value zero has zero-divisor roots. Both signs of a nonzero $\lambda$ give units, so the sign
does **not** decide invertibility; only whether $\lambda$ vanishes does. A root on the massive shell and a
root on the spacelike shell are therefore both invertible, for the reason of *Biquaternion Norm and
Invertibility*: the interval is nonzero on both. The tachyon is in the invertible region, and the only
value whose roots are non-invertible is the value zero.

**Why the retired equation failed on the sign.** *Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in
Biquaternionic Form* recomputes the failure of the single-field equation $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi=m\tilde
\Psi^{\flat}$ and finds its central-phase plane waves have nullity $0$ on the timelike shell and nullity
$4$ on the spacelike shell: its solutions lie on the wrong shell. The mass term of a physical equation must
place its solutions on the **timelike** shell, and the article's point is that the conjugation, not a
number, is what chooses between the two kinds of mass. The value $\lambda$ of this article's root problem
is the scalar face of that choice: the sign of $\lambda$ says which shell a solution occupies, and the
conjugation says which equation puts it there.

**One shell generates the others.** The positive shells at different magnitudes are related by scaling:
the biquaternion norm is quadratic, so scaling an element by $\lambda$ scales its interval by
$\lambda^{2}$, and a mass shell of interval $-m^{2}c^{2}$ is the unit shell scaled by $mc$.
*Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form* reads that scaling as a conformal
dilation, with the massless cone as the scale-free limit. The general value is thus one number whose
magnitude sets the size of the shell and whose sign sets its causal class, and the band that reads it —
interval, weight, gauge interval or polar scale — selects which of the two aspects is the physical one.

## Physical Interpretation

- The root problem of a general value asks which elements square to a given central value. Each of the
  twelve structures answers it in its own way, and the answers fall into four existence classes.
- The plain product realises every complex value, by a scalar process $\pm\sqrt\lambda\,e_0$ or by a pure
  process on the quadric. The composition band can produce any value, phase and amplitude together.
- The natural product squares an element to its interval, so its root set of a value is the shell of that
  value: the root problem is the mass-shell problem, and the shell of $0$ is the light cone.
- The probability band has roots only for non-negative values, and the origin alone for the value $0$.
  A nonzero amplitude never squares to nothing, so masslessness does not live in the band that measures
  probability.
- The gauge sesquilinear band has roots exactly for real values, because the quantity it produces is an
  interval. The slope parametrises the four-vector of the given interval, and its three ranges are the
  three causal classes.
- The four brackets have a root of $0$ alone, because a bracket is an infinitesimal transformation and
  its square is a pure or vanishing object.
- The four general products share one zero-divisor set, the cone, and have four root sets. Degeneracy is
  universal across the bands; factorisation is band-specific.
- The general value carries four physical readings, and the band selects among them: the interval and the
  mass shell of the natural product, the Born weight of the probability band, the indefinite interval of
  the gauge band and the polar scale of the bilateral structures.
- The sign of a real value is the causal class of its roots: negative for the massive shell, zero for the
  cone, positive for the spacelike or tachyonic shell. The magnitude sets the size of the shell and the
  sign sets its causal class.
- The root of a nonzero value is a unit whatever the sign, so a tachyon is invertible and the value zero
  alone has non-invertible roots.
- The sign is why the retired single-field mass equation fails: its solutions lie on the spacelike shell,
  while a physical mass term must place them on the timelike one, and the conjugation rather than a number
  is what makes the choice.
- One shell generates the rest by scaling: a mass shell is a unit shell multiplied by $m$, and the cone is
  the scale-free limit of the family.

## Summary

The root problem of a general central value is the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$ read in
the twelve structures, and the mathematics companion answers it by reducing the four general squares to
a vector-scalar pair, a single complex equation, a non-negative scalar and an indefinite scalar. Read
physically, the four answers are the four bands. The plain product realises every complex value: a root
is a scalar process or a pure process, and the composition band carries the phase. The natural product
squares an element to its interval, so its root set is a mass shell, and the shell of $0$ is the light
cone. The plain sesquilinear product reads the non-negative values alone, because a probability is
non-negative, and reaches $0$ at the origin alone, so masslessness is absent from the band that measures
probability. The quaternionic sesquilinear product reads exactly the real values, because an interval is
real, and its root family is parametrised by a slope whose three ranges are the three causal classes. The
four brackets read the value $0$ alone, because an infinitesimal transformation has no central square.
The twelve structures thus fall into four existence classes, the bilateral, the indefinite sesquilinear,
the definite sesquilinear and the antisymmetric, and the sharpest structural statement is the one the
mathematics companion proves: the four general products have one zero-divisor set, the cone, and four
different root sets, so degeneracy is universal while factorisation is band-specific.

The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing

Introduction

The GPS band reads the plain sesquilinear product as the probability structure of the framework. The product is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$, the general plain bilinear product with its second slot read through the Hermitian conjugation, and its scalar part is $\sum_{\mu}P_{\mu}\overline{Q_{\mu}}$, positive definite. This article carries the operator layer of the band: the multiplication operators of this product, the form they are adjoint for, and the consequences of that adjoint. It answers the question the twelve bands ask, and for this band the answer is the mirror image of the composition band: the adjoint is taken for the positive definite Hermitian form, and it conjugates the parameter and keeps the side, where the adjoint of the composition band swaps the sides.

The article is the sibling of The Operators of the General Plain Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich, the operator article of the GPA band, and it carries a layer that article explicitly defers to this band. The composition band reads its own indefinite plain form and states that self-adjointness for that form is not physical hermiticity; the observables and the gauge generators of the framework are the self-adjoint and the skew-adjoint operators of the Hermitian form of this sesquilinear block, and the full interpretation of them is Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action. The rank and the mass are Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger; the particle types are Particle Types, Discrete Charge and Three-Particle Couplings; the state space and its complement are The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra and The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Sesqualgebra, with the fused reading of the module and the form in The Spinor Space as a Module and as a Hilbert Space. None of these is re-proved here.

The new feature of the band is a parity asymmetry with no analogue in the bilinear rows. The left multiplication of the product is conjugate-linear and the right multiplication is complex-linear, and the two families have opposite parity in the variable and opposite parity in the parameter at once. Every difference from the composition band descends from this: the left family does not close under composition, so the band has no operator algebra of the associative kind, and the left multiplication has no adjoint of the same kind as the right one.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$, the relations $e_k^{2}=-e_0$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central $i$ with $i^{2}=-1$; an element is $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf Q$ with complex coefficients. The Hermitian conjugation is ${}^{*}$ with $Q^{*}_{\nu}=\varepsilon_{\nu}\overline{Q_{\nu}}$ and $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the natural conjugation is ${}^{\natural}$. The sectors are $\mathbb{M}_+=\{\tilde Q^{*}=\tilde Q\}$ (informational) and $\mathbb{M}_-=\{\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q\}$ (material), and the unitary slice is $U=\{\tilde U:\tilde U\tilde U^{*}=e_0\}$. The scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}$, the trace is $\mathrm{Tr}=2\,\mathrm{Sc}$, and the matrix model $\mathsf{M}_2$ has $\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)=N(\tilde Q)$ for the biquaternion norm $N(\tilde Q)=\sum_{\mu}Q_{\mu}^{2}$.

The Two Multiplication Operators and Their Parity

The product carries one multiplication operator for each slot, and the two are not of the same kind.

Definition. For $\tilde A\in\mathbb{B}$ the left multiplication and the right multiplication of the plain sesquilinear product are

$$ L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\star\tilde X=\tilde A\tilde X^{*},\qquad R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\star\tilde A=\tilde X\tilde A^{*}. $$

They are the biquaternion instance of the two multiplication operators of a sesqualgebra, The Left and Right Multiplication Operators of a Sesqualgebra, and their biquaternion determination is The Left and Right Multiplications of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra. Alongside them this article uses the ordinary one-sided operators of the algebra, $L_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\tilde X$ and $R_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\tilde A$, which are the operators of One-Sided Operators on the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions. The two pairs are related by the involution, $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}=L_{\tilde A}\circ{}^{*}$ and $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}=R_{\tilde A^{*}}$.

Theorem (the opposite parities). For every $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$ and every $\tilde X\in\mathbb{B}$,

$$ L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\lambda\tilde X)=\overline{\lambda}\,L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X),\qquad L^{\star}_{\lambda\tilde A}=\lambda\,L^{\star}_{\tilde A},\qquad R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\lambda\tilde X)=\lambda\,R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X),\qquad R^{\star}_{\lambda\tilde A}=\overline{\lambda}\,R^{\star}_{\tilde A}. $$

The left multiplication is conjugate-linear in the variable and complex-linear in the parameter; the right multiplication is complex-linear in the variable and conjugate-linear in the parameter.

Proof. The involution is conjugate-linear and $\mathbb{C}$ is central, so $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\lambda\tilde X)=\tilde A(\lambda\tilde X)^{*} =\tilde A\overline{\lambda}\tilde X^{*}=\overline{\lambda}L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)$, and the variable is read plainly on the right, so $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\lambda\tilde X)=\lambda R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)$. For the parameters, $L^{\star}_{\lambda\tilde A}=\lambda L^{\star}_{\tilde A}$ and $R^{\star}_{\lambda\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X(\lambda\tilde A)^{*} =\overline{\lambda}\,\tilde X\tilde A^{*}=\overline{\lambda}R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)$.

Read as a physical statement. The two slots of the Born pairing have opposite polarity: the first slot is read linearly and the second through the conjugation, and the multiplication operators inherit that polarity. Attaching on the left conjugates the variable; attaching on the right conjugates the parameter. A bilinear product reads both slots the same way, so no bilinear row has this behaviour. This is an interpretation: it names the algebraic fact that the product is $\mathbb{C}$-linear in one argument and conjugate-linear in the other, and it calls that polarity the bra-and-ket asymmetry of a probability amplitude.

Proposition (the operators at the unit). $L^{\star}_{e_0}={}^{*}$, the involution, and $R^{\star}_{e_0}=\mathrm{id}$, the identity.

Proof. $L^{\star}_{e_0}(\tilde X)=e_0\tilde X^{*}=\tilde X^{*}$ and $R^{\star}_{e_0}(\tilde X)=\tilde Xe_0^{*}=\tilde X$.

Read as a physical statement. The two extreme actions of the product are the conjugation and the identity, one on each side, and the neutral operation is a right multiplication. The product has a one-sided unit, and the operator form of that one-sidedness is this display. This is an interpretation of the asymmetry of the product's unit.

The Composition of the Operators

The product is not associative, and the composition of its operators records exactly where associativity fails.

Theorem (the composition laws). For all $\tilde A,\tilde B\in\mathbb{B}$,

$$ L^{\star}_{\tilde A}L^{\star}_{\tilde B}=T_{\tilde A,\tilde B^{*}},\qquad R^{\star}_{\tilde A}R^{\star}_{\tilde B}=R^{\star}_{\tilde A\tilde B}, $$ $$ L^{\star}_{\tilde A}R^{\star}_{\tilde B}=L^{\star}_{\tilde A\tilde B},\qquad R^{\star}_{\tilde A}L^{\star}_{\tilde B}=S_{\tilde B,\tilde A}, $$

where $T_{\tilde P,\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\tilde P\tilde X\tilde Q$ is the ordinary two-sided multiplication (the sandwich of the crossing reader) and $S_{\tilde P,\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\tilde P\tilde X^{*}\tilde Q^{*}$ is the sesquilinear sandwich of The Sesquilinear Sandwich on the Biquaternions.

Proof. Each is a direct computation with $(uv)^{*}=v^{*}u^{*}$. The first is $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}L^{\star}_{\tilde B}(\tilde X)=\tilde A(\tilde B\tilde X^{*})^{*} =\tilde A\tilde X\tilde B^{*}=T_{\tilde A,\tilde B^{*}}(\tilde X)$; the second is $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}R^{\star}_{\tilde B}(\tilde X)=(\tilde X\tilde B^{*})\tilde A^{*} =\tilde X(\tilde A\tilde B)^{*}=R^{\star}_{\tilde A\tilde B}(\tilde X)$; the third is $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}R^{\star}_{\tilde B}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\tilde B\tilde X^{*} =L^{\star}_{\tilde A\tilde B}(\tilde X)$; the fourth is $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}L^{\star}_{\tilde B}(\tilde X)=\tilde B\tilde X^{*}\tilde A^{*} =S_{\tilde B,\tilde A}(\tilde X)$.

Corollary (the right family closes, the left one does not). The right multiplications are closed under composition, contain the identity $R^{\star}_{e_0}$, and form a monoid isomorphic to the multiplicative monoid $(\mathbb{B},\cdot)$ under $\tilde A\mapsto R^{\star}_{\tilde A}$. The left multiplications are not closed: the composite of two of them is the complex-linear two-sided multiplication $T_{\tilde A,\tilde B^{*}}$, which is a left multiplication only when it vanishes, and the left family contains no identity. The four families $\{L^{\star}\}$, $\{R^{\star}\}$, $\{S\}$, $\{T\}$ generate the monoid of the product, with identity $R^{\star}_{e_0}=T_{e_0,e_0}=\mathrm{id}$.

Read as a physical statement. The product composes on the right exactly as the algebra does, and it does not compose on the left, because the left slot carries the conjugation. This is an interpretation: it says that the probability structure retains the composability of the algebra on one side only, and that the two-sided multiplication $T$ is the residue of the failure on the other side.

Corollary (no single representation). Let $\tilde A\neq0$. No complex-linear representation of the algebra contains $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}$: a complex-linear operator cannot equal a nonzero conjugate-linear one. Consequently the band has no enveloping operator algebra of the associative kind, and what it has instead is the four-family monoid of the previous corollary.

Proof. The parity theorem gives the obstruction, and the argument is the one of The Left and Right Multiplication Operators of a Sesqualgebra, §The Failure of a Single Linear Representation, whose biquaternion instance is The Left and Right Multiplications of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra.

Read as a physical statement. The composition band recovers its algebra from the action, $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{e}}\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$ with two commuting families. This band does not: the parity mismatch forbids a single linear representation, and the action recovers the sesqualgebra only through two families and a two-sided residue. This is an interpretation, and it is the operator form of the statement that the sesquilinear product is not associative.

The Form and the Adjoint: the Side Is Kept and the Parameter Conjugated

The product has a positive definite form, its scalar part, and the adjoint is taken for it.

The form. The Born pairing is

$$ \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\star\tilde Q\bigr) =\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}P_{\mu}\overline{Q_{\mu}}, $$

$\mathbb{C}$-linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the second, of Gram matrix the identity in the basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$, and positive definite. It is the canonical Hermitian form of the regular module, The Canonical Hermitian Form on the Regular Module of the Biquaternion Algebra, and it is the same form as $(\tilde T,\tilde V)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde T^{*}\tilde V)$ of the one-sided article read with the arguments exchanged, $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\overline{(\tilde P,\tilde Q)}$.

Definition (the adjoint). For a complex-linear operator $T$ the adjoint is defined by

$$ \langle T\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde P,T^{\dagger}\tilde Q\rangle_{*} \qquad\text{for all }\tilde P,\tilde Q . $$

The form is positive definite, hence non-degenerate, so the adjoint exists and is unique. For a conjugate-linear operator $S$ the linear rule has no solution, and the twisted adjoint is used instead, $\langle S\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\overline{\langle\tilde P,S^{\dagger}\tilde Q\rangle_{*}}$, under which the adjoint of a conjugate-linear operator is again conjugate-linear. Both rules are The Sesquilinear Adjoint Operator, whose individual biquaternion instances are The Adjoint of the Conjugate Left Multiplication and The Adjoint of the Sesquilinear Sandwich on the Biquaternions.

Theorem (the adjoint of the linear one-sided operators). For every $\tilde A\in\mathbb{B}$,

$$ (L_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde A^{*}},\qquad (R_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=R_{\tilde A^{*}},\qquad (R^{\star}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=R^{\star}_{\tilde A^{*}} . $$

The adjoint keeps the side and conjugates the parameter.

Proof. For the left multiplication, and for $\tilde P,\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$, the anti-involution property of the dagger and the cyclicity of the scalar part give $\langle L_{\tilde A}\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}) =\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}\tilde A)=\langle\tilde P,L_{\tilde A^{*}}\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$. The right family is the same computation with $(\tilde P\tilde A)^{*}=\tilde A^{*}\tilde P^{*}$, and the third identity follows from the second and $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}=R_{\tilde A^{*}}$.

Read as a physical statement. Transporting an operation across the Born pairing conjugates its parameter and does not move it to the other side; a left attachment stays a left attachment. This is an interpretation of a proof, and it is the physical hermiticity the composition band defers to this one: the parameter is read as a charge or a phase, and the adjoint reverses it. The type of the operator is decided by the type of one parameter, which is what the next section uses.

Theorem (the adjoint of the conjugate-linear left multiplication). For every $\tilde A\in\mathbb{B}$,

$$ (L^{\star}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=S_{e_0,\tilde A^{*}},\qquad S_{e_0,\tilde A^{*}}(\tilde X)=\tilde X^{*}\tilde A . $$

The left family is not stable under the adjoint: it leaves the family, and its image is the conjugate-linear family of sandwiches. In particular $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}$ is twisted-self-adjoint exactly for a central $\tilde A$, and twisted-skew-adjoint only for $\tilde A=0$.

Proof. For $\tilde P,\tilde Q$, $\langle L^{\star}_{\tilde A}\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A\tilde P^{*}\tilde Q^{*})$, while $\overline{\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q^{*}\tilde A\rangle_{*}}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{*}\tilde Q^{*}\tilde A)$, and the two agree by the cyclicity $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A\tilde P^{*}\tilde Q^{*}) =\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{*}\tilde Q^{*}\tilde A)$. Twisted-self-adjointness is then $\tilde X^{*}\tilde A=\tilde A\tilde X^{*}$ for every $\tilde X$, which is centrality of $\tilde A$, and twisted-skew-adjointness at $\tilde X=e_0$ reads $\tilde A=-\tilde A$.

Read as a physical statement. The two slots of the form do not receive adjoints of the same kind, because the two multiplication operators are not of the same kind. The right attachment has a right attachment as its adjoint; the left attachment has not, and its adjoint is a two-sided operation. This is an interpretation of the parity asymmetry of the product at the level of the adjoint.

Corollary (the contrast with the composition band). The adjoint of this band is a Hermitian adjoint for a positive definite form: it conjugates the parameter and keeps the side. The adjoint of the composition band is a transpose for the indefinite plain bilinear form: it keeps the parameter and swaps the sides, $(L_{\tilde A})^{\approx}=R_{\tilde A}$, of The Operators of the General Plain Bilinear Product. The type criteria are on one parameter here and on the product of two parameters there, and the block-by-block comparison is the crossing reader The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket, which is not one of the twelve.

Theorem (the adjoint of the two-sided and sandwich operators). For all $\tilde P,\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$,

$$ (T_{\tilde P,\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=T_{\tilde P^{*},\tilde Q^{*}},\qquad (S_{\tilde P,\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=S_{\tilde Q^{*},\tilde P^{*}},\qquad (\Theta_{\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=\Theta_{\tilde Q^{*}}, $$

where $\Theta_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}$ is the Hermitian sandwich.

Proof. For $T$ the defining identity is the cyclic computation applied to both slots. For $S$, the composite $S_{\tilde P,\tilde Q}=T_{\tilde P,\tilde Q^{*}}\circ{}^{*}$ has a conjugate-linear adjoint, and the twisted rule gives $(S_{\tilde P,\tilde Q})^{\dagger}=S_{\tilde Q^{*},\tilde P^{*}}$, the two parameters exchanging as well as conjugating; it is The Adjoint of the Sesquilinear Sandwich on the Biquaternions. For $\Theta$ this is the case $\tilde P=\tilde Q$ together with $\Theta_{\tilde Q}=T_{\tilde Q,\tilde Q^{*}}$, of Two-Sided Operators on the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions.

Read as a physical statement. A two-sided operation keeps its two sides under the adjoint and conjugates both parameters; a sandwich exchanges them, so the adjoint reads the sandwich and its mirror image as a pair. This is a interpretation of the anti-automorphism property $(ST)^{\dagger}=T^{\dagger}S^{\dagger}$ of the twisted adjoint.

The Types of the Operators: Observables and Generators

The type criteria are criteria on one parameter.

Theorem (the linear types). For $\tilde A\in\mathbb{B}$, and equally with $R_{\tilde A}$ or $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}$ in place of $L_{\tilde A}$,

$$ L_{\tilde A}\ \text{self-adjoint}\iff\tilde A\in\mathbb{M}_+,\qquad L_{\tilde A}\ \text{skew-adjoint}\iff\tilde A\in\mathbb{M}_-,\qquad L_{\tilde A}\ \text{unitary}\iff\tilde A\in U . $$

Proof. By the adjoint theorem $L_{\tilde A}^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde A^{*}}$, and $\tilde A\mapsto L_{\tilde A}$ is injective, so $L_{\tilde A}^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde A}$ is $\tilde A^{*}=\tilde A$ and $L_{\tilde A}^{\dagger}=-L_{\tilde A}$ is $\tilde A^{*}=-\tilde A$. Unitarity is $L_{\tilde A}^{\dagger}L_{\tilde A}=L_{\tilde A^{*}\tilde A}=\mathrm{id}$, that is $\tilde A^{*}\tilde A=e_0$, equivalent to $\tilde A\in U$ for invertible $\tilde A$. The right-hand computations are identical.

Corollary (the observables and the generators). The Hermitian and the anti-Hermitian parameters give the self-adjoint and the skew-adjoint operators, $L_{\mathbb{M}_+}=\text{self-adjoint operators}$ and $L_{\mathbb{M}_-}=\text{skew-adjoint operators}$, alongside the unitary operators $L_U$; and the slice exponentiates inside the family, $e^{L_{\tilde S}}=L_{e^{\tilde S}}$ for $\tilde S\in\mathbb{M}_-$, whose exponential lies in $U$.

Proof. The type display gives the first statement; for the second, the exponential series and the linearity of $\tilde B\mapsto L_{\tilde B}$ commute, and the exponential of an anti-Hermitian element is unitary.

Read as a physical statement. The observables of the framework are the self-adjoint operators of this form and the gauge generators are the skew-adjoint ones, one for each parameter of the corresponding sector: the Hermitian elements act by observables and the anti-Hermitian elements by generators. This is an interpretation, and it is why this article is the owner of the physical adjoint that the composition band cites: the plain bilinear form of that band is indefinite, and hermiticity is a property of this positive form. The gauge group, its compactness and the chirality of the one-sided action are Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action and are not repeated here.

Remark (the conjugate-linear types). The conjugate-linear left multiplication is not a source of observables or generators: its twisted-self-adjoint parameters are the central elements and its twisted-skew family is trivial, so the two type classes are carried by the linear one-sided operators alone.

Theorem (the two-sided and sandwich types). Let $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$ be nonzero. Then

$$ \Theta_{\tilde Q}^{\dagger}=\Theta_{\tilde Q}\iff \tilde Q^{*}=\omega\tilde Q\ \text{for some }\omega\in\mathbb{C}\ \text{with}\ \lvert\omega\rvert=1 , $$

$\Theta_{\tilde Q}$ is never skew-adjoint, and both sectors give the same operator, $\Theta_{\mathbb{M}_+}=\Theta_{\mathbb{M}_-}$.

Proof. By the adjoint theorem $\Theta_{\tilde Q}^{\dagger}=\Theta_{\tilde Q^{*}}$, and by the kernel theorem of Two-Sided Operators on the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions the assignment is injective up to a central phase; the criterion is the theorem there. The sectors give the same operator because $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_-$ gives $\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q$ and $\Theta_{-\tilde Q}=\Theta_{\tilde Q}$ by the central-phase invariance.

Read as a physical statement. The two-sided operator of the band is symmetric for the Born pairing exactly when its parameter is Hermitian up to a phase, so the measurement update, whose parameter is a Hermitian idempotent, and the material conjugation, whose parameter is anti-Hermitian, both qualify, and they give the same operator. This differs from the composition band, where the sandwich criterion is membership in one sector; here the parameter enters twice, the sign that separates the sectors is squared away, and the criterion becomes Hermitian-up-to-phase. This is an interpretation.

The Symmetries of the Form and of the Product

Theorem (the form-preserving one-sided operators). The left multiplication preserves the Born pairing, $\langle L_{\tilde A}\tilde P,L_{\tilde A}\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$ for all $\tilde P,\tilde Q$, if and only if $\tilde A\in U$; the same criterion holds on the right and for the product's right multiplication. The preserving operators are then the unitary operators of the slice.

Proof. By the adjoint theorem, $\langle L_{\tilde A}\tilde P,L_{\tilde A}\tilde Q\rangle_{*} =\langle\tilde P,L_{\tilde A^{*}\tilde A}\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$, which equals $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$ for all $\tilde P,\tilde Q$ exactly when $\tilde A^{*}\tilde A=e_0$, that is $\tilde A\in U$.

Theorem (the multiplicative operators and the product). The ordinary one-sided operator $L_{\tilde A}$ is multiplicative for the algebra product if and only if $\tilde A$ is central. The two-sided operator $\Theta_{\tilde Q}$ is an algebra automorphism if and only if $\tilde Q\in U$, and then $\Theta_{\tilde Q}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde Q}$, the inner automorphism. For a unitary $\tilde U$ the inner automorphism preserves the sesquilinear product as well as the form, $\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde U}(\tilde P\star\tilde Q) =\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde U}(\tilde P)\star\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde U}(\tilde Q)$.

Proof. Multiplicativity of $L_{\tilde A}$ reads $\tilde A\tilde P\tilde Q=(\tilde A\tilde P)(\tilde A\tilde Q)$ for all $\tilde P,\tilde Q$, that is $\tilde A\tilde P=\tilde P\tilde A$ for all $\tilde P$, which is centrality; the automorphism statement is Two-Sided Operators on the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions, §The Automorphisms. For the sesquilinear product, $\Theta_{\tilde U}(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)=\tilde U\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}\tilde U^{*} =(\tilde U\tilde P\tilde U^{-1})(\tilde U\tilde Q\tilde U^{-1})^{*}$, the second factor because $\tilde U^{*}=\tilde U^{-1}$ on the slice.

Read as a physical statement. The isometries of the Born pairing are the unitary slice, on both sides, and the same slice consists of the algebra automorphisms. A one-sided operation is an isometry exactly for a unitary parameter and is a symmetry of the product only for a central one. This is an interpretation of the two criteria; it says that the physical symmetry of the probability metric is one-sided and compact, and that the full algebra symmetry is the two-sided inner action.

The Operators Acting on the State Space: the Born Pairing

The operators act on the simple module, and there the pairing is the norm.

The state space. The spinor module is the minimal left ideal $S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\}$, the unique simple left $\mathbb{B}$-module, of complex dimension two, of The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra and The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Sesqualgebra (its second basis element is written $\tilde W$ in the companion maths article Bilinear Operators on the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions). The algebra acts on it by the left multiplication $L_{\tilde A}s=\tilde A s$, the action is onto the endomorphisms, and the module form is $\langle t,s\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(t s^{*})$, positive definite with Gram matrix $\tfrac12 I_2$ in the basis $(\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2)$ of Hermitian Modules over the Biquaternion Algebra with Hermitian Adjoint.

Proposition (the Born pairing is the scalar part of the product). For all $\tilde P,\tilde Q$,

$$ \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\star\tilde Q\bigr),\qquad \langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\sum_{\mu}\lvert Q_{\mu}\rvert^{2}>0\quad(\tilde Q\neq0). $$

On a state $s\in S$ the same form is the module norm, $\langle s,s\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(s^{\dagger}s)>0$.

Proof. The first display is the definition of the Born pairing as the scalar part of the product; the second is its algebraic norm, the Euclidean square of the coefficient vector; the module statement is the restriction of the form to the ideal.

Read as a physical statement. The probability of the framework is a product: the amplitude of a state in another is the scalar part of the band's product, and the probability is its squared modulus over the two norms. The norm is positive definite, so no state of the module has negative probability, and the absence of ghosts and tachyons is the positivity of this form (Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger). This is an interpretation: the framework reads the scalar part of the product as a probability, it does not derive quantum probability from the algebra.

Proposition (the product pairs the carrier and does not act on it). The product's left multiplication keeps the carrier on its first basis element, $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}(\tilde\Pi_1)=\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_1\in S$ for every $\tilde Q$, and carries the second out of it for generic $\tilde Q$, $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}(\tilde\Upsilon_2)=\tilde Q\tilde\Upsilon_1\notin S$, since $\tilde\Upsilon_2^{*}=\tilde\Upsilon_1$ lies in the complementary ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$. The product therefore pairs the state space, taking two states to a scalar, and does not act on it; the acting structure is the ordinary left multiplication of the paragraph above.

Proof. $\tilde\Pi_1^{*}=\tilde\Pi_1$, so the first image is $\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_1$, which lies in the left ideal $S$; $\tilde\Upsilon_2^{*}=\tilde\Upsilon_1\in\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$, so the second image has components along $\tilde\Upsilon_1$ and $\tilde\Pi_2$ for generic $\tilde Q$ and lies outside $S$. The two computations are the state-space siblings' (The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra and The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Sesqualgebra).

Read as a physical statement. The probability structure takes two states to a number and moves no state; the motion of a state is the ordinary action, and the pairing is not an action. This is an interpretation of the parity asymmetry at the level of the carrier, and it is the operator form of the fact that the band's product is a pairing and not a representation.

Corollary (the polarity is the quantisation datum). The Born pairing is linear in its first slot and conjugate-linear in its second, and no other polarity is admitted by the product. A decay therefore has a definite handedness: the state is the conjugate-linear slot and the observable is the linear one.

Read as a physical statement. The two slots of the product are the two slots of a measurement, and their opposite polarity is the datum that distinguishes a state from an observable. This is an interpretation; it is the operator form of the fact that the product is sesquilinear and not bilinear, and it is the quantisation datum of the band.

Proposition (the image of the identity and the cone). The image of the identity under the two-sided operator is the sesquilinear square, $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(e_0)=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$, and the set $\{\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}:\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}\}$ is the cone of the positive semidefinite Hermitian elements, with interior the positive definite ones. The sandwich preserves the cone for every parameter and the unitary slice permutes it. On the state space the left multiplications are the matrix units of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)$; the right multiplications commute with them and the nilpotent ones carry the module to the complementary ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$.

Proof. The image is the definition; the cone is the positivity of the Gram matrix, The Canonical Hermitian Form on the Regular Module of the Biquaternion Algebra; the cone preservation is $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde R\tilde R^{*})=(\tilde Q\tilde R)(\tilde Q\tilde R)^{*}$; the matrix-unit statement is the completeness of the action (Bilinear Operators on the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions), and the nilpotent carriers are computed in The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Sesqualgebra and The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra.

Read as a physical statement. The states the algebra produces as squares are the elements of the positive cone and nothing outside it, so the probability structure and the causal cone of the band are one cone read twice; the left action is the algebra of the state space and the right action is the family that commutes with it. This is a interpretation of the positivity, and the cone, the rank and the mass are Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger.

The Sandwich and the Inner Automorphism

The framework attaches a conjugation to an element in two ways, and the two agree on the compact part.

Proposition (two parametrisations of one conjugation). For every unit $\tilde Q$,

$$ \Theta_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}},\qquad \mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{-1}} . $$

The sandwich conjugates by the conjugate of the parameter and the inner automorphism by its inverse; they agree exactly when $\tilde Q^{*}=\tilde Q^{-1}$, that is on the unitary slice and there alone.

Proof. The first display is the definition, the second the inner automorphism; they coincide exactly when the two right parameters coincide, and the coincidence condition $\tilde Q^{*}=\tilde Q^{-1}$ is the defining equation of the slice.

Theorem (both preserve the interval and differ on a boost). Let $\tilde\Lambda$ be Hermitian of unit natural norm. Then $\Theta_{\tilde\Lambda}$ is self-adjoint, the inner automorphism $\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde\Lambda}$ is defined, and the two operators differ on the vector directions: the sandwich rotates them hyperbolically and the inner automorphism fixes them. A generic such parameter is not unitary.

Proof. Hermiticity gives $\tilde\Lambda^{*}=\tilde\Lambda$, so $\Theta_{\tilde\Lambda}$ is self-adjoint by the sandwich criterion with $\omega=1$. The unit natural norm makes $\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde\Lambda}$ well defined, and the difference is the worked case.

Read as a physical statement. The framework attaches a conjugation to an element in two ways, and the two agree only on the compact part of the parameter group. The Lorentz action is the conjugate one, $\tilde P\mapsto\tilde\Lambda\tilde P\tilde\Lambda^{*}$, and the algebra automorphism is the inverse one, $\tilde P\mapsto\tilde\Lambda\tilde P\tilde\Lambda^{-1}$; on a boost the two differ and on a rotation they coincide, and the compact subgroup is exactly where "conjugation by the inverse" and "conjugation by the conjugate" are the same statement. This is an interpretation of the corpus's split between the compact slice and the boost.

A Worked Case

Take one material parameter,

$$ \tilde Q=ie_0+2e_1\in\mathbb{M}_-,\qquad \langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=5,\qquad N(\tilde Q)=3,\qquad \tilde Q^{2}=-5e_0+4ie_1 . $$

Its conjugate is $-\tilde Q$, so the sandwich criterion reads $\omega=-1$ and $\Theta_{\tilde Q}$ is self-adjoint for the Born pairing, as the type theorem says; equivalently $\Theta_{\tilde Q}=L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is the composite of two left multiplications and it is complex-linear. On the unit, $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(e_0)=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q^{2}=5e_0-4ie_1$, a Hermitian element of positive trace, and the same element is the image of the identity under the operator. The two forms of the block separate on this parameter: the Born pairing gives the positive value $5$, while the natural square carries $N(\tilde Q)=3$ and the ordinary square carries $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q^{2})=-5$, the sector sign.

Take next a boost parameter,

$$ \tilde\Lambda=\cosh u\,e_0+\sinh u\,ie_1,\qquad \tilde\Lambda=\tilde\Lambda^{*},\qquad \tilde\Lambda\tilde\Lambda^{\natural}=e_0,\qquad \tilde\Lambda\tilde\Lambda^{*} =\cosh 2u\,e_0+\sinh 2u\,ie_1 . $$

The parameter is Hermitian and of unit natural norm but not unitary, so the sandwich is self-adjoint and the inner automorphism is defined while the parameter is not in the compact slice. On $e_1$ the two conjugations separate,

$$ \Theta_{\tilde\Lambda}(e_1)=\tilde\Lambda e_1\tilde\Lambda^{*}=\tilde\Lambda^{2}e_1 =\cosh 2u\,e_1-i\sinh 2u\,e_0,\qquad \mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde\Lambda}(e_1)=e_1 . $$

The sandwich moves $e_1$ by a hyperbolic rotation and the inner automorphism leaves it fixed; the difference is the non-compact part of the parameter, displayed on one element, and it vanishes as $u\to0$.

The same verdict holds for a measurement parameter, $\tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_0+ie_1)$, a Hermitian idempotent, $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi^{*}\in\mathbb{M}_+$, self-adjoint by the criterion with $\omega=+1$. The three parameters separate the two questions the band asks of an operator: which form it is symmetric for and which motion it generates.

The Ledger

Proved. The plain sesquilinear product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ has two multiplication operators, $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\tilde X^{*}$ and $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\tilde A^{*}$, conjugate-linear and complex-linear respectively, with opposite parity in the variable and in the parameter; the unit gives $L^{\star}_{e_0}={}^{*}$ and $R^{\star}_{e_0}=\mathrm{id}$; the composition laws $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}L^{\star}_{\tilde B}=T_{\tilde A,\tilde B^{*}}$, $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}R^{\star}_{\tilde B}=R^{\star}_{\tilde A\tilde B}$, $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}R^{\star}_{\tilde B}=L^{\star}_{\tilde A\tilde B}$ and $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}L^{\star}_{\tilde B}=S_{\tilde B,\tilde A}$ hold, the right family closes and the left family does not, and no nonzero left multiplication lies in a complex-linear representation, so the band has no enveloping operator algebra of the associative kind; the Born pairing $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})$ is positive definite of Gram matrix the identity; the adjoint for it exists and is unique, conjugates the parameter and keeps the side, $(L_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde A^{*}}$, $(R_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=R_{\tilde A^{*}}$ and $(R^{\star}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=R^{\star}_{\tilde A^{*}}$, while the conjugate-linear left multiplication is not stable and has the sandwich $S_{e_0,\tilde A^{*}}$ as its twisted adjoint; a linear one-sided operator is self-adjoint exactly for a Hermitian parameter, skew-adjoint exactly for an anti-Hermitian one and unitary exactly for a parameter of the slice, so the observables and the generators are the Hermitian and the anti-Hermitian parameters; a two-sided operator keeps its two sides and conjugates both parameters, and a sandwich exchanges them, the sandwich criterion being $\tilde Q$ Hermitian up to a phase for self-adjointness and $\tilde Q=0$ for skew-adjointness; the form-preserving one-sided operators are exactly the unitary slice, the multiplicative ones exactly the central parameters, and the two-sided automorphisms exactly the slice, where the sandwich and the inner automorphism agree; the image of the identity is the sesquilinear square and the cone of the positive semidefinite Hermitian elements; and the left actions on the state space are the matrix units while the right actions commute with them. The worked case gives $\tilde Q=ie_0+2e_1$ with $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=5$, $N(\tilde Q)=3$ and $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(e_0)=5e_0-4ie_1$, and the boost $\tilde\Lambda=\cosh u\,e_0+\sinh u\,ie_1$ with $\Theta_{\tilde\Lambda}(e_1)=\cosh 2u\,e_1-i\sinh 2u\,e_0$ against $\mathrm{Ad}_{\tilde\Lambda}(e_1)=e_1$.

Interpretations. That the two slots of the product have opposite polarity, so the left family is conjugate-linear and the right one complex-linear; that a probability amplitude has one linear and one anti-linear slot, a bra and a ket; that the adjoint conjugates the charge and keeps the side, so the observables and the generators are the Hermitian and the anti-Hermitian parameters; that the left family has no identity and no single representation, so the probability structure composes on one side only; that the symmetries of the Born pairing are the unitary slice and that the compact slice is where the sandwich and the inner automorphism coincide; and that the probability metric and the causal cone are one positive cone read twice. Each is the framework's naming of a proved algebraic fact and is labelled as such.

Physical Readings

The plain sesquilinear product is the framework's probability structure, and its operators read as the two ways of attaching an operation to a state with opposite polarity. The left attachment conjugates the variable and the right attachment conjugates the parameter, and the asymmetry is the bra-and-ket asymmetry of a probability amplitude: the first slot of the product is the linear one and the second the conjugate-linear one, and the two slots are the state and the observable. The adjoint of the band is the Hermitian adjoint of the positive form, and it conjugates the charge of the parameter without moving the operation to the other side; the self-adjoint operators are the observables and the skew-adjoint ones the generators, which is why the physical hermiticity of the framework lives in this band and not in the indefinite plain form of the composition band. The right family composes and the left does not, and the band's interpretation of that is that a probability structure is one-sided in its composition while it is two-sided in its action: the two-sided operations are the sandwich and its mirror, and the identity of the composition is a right multiplication alone. On the state space the Born pairing is the norm, the left actions are the matrices of the algebra, the nilpotent right actions carry a state to the complement, and the image of the identity is the cone of positive elements, so the states the algebra produces lie in the forward cone and nowhere else. Read on the clock, the dagger is the operation that conjugates the phase, and the unitary slice is the compact part of the parameter group, where the reversible action lives; the boost is the non-compact part, where the sandwich and the inner automorphism separate. The particle content of the same operator layer, the compactness of the internal group and the discreteness of its charges are Particle Types, Discrete Charge and Three-Particle Couplings.

Summary

The operators of the plain sesquilinear product are the left and the right multiplications $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\tilde X^{*}$ and $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\tilde A^{*}$. The left one is conjugate-linear and the right one complex-linear, and the two families have opposite parity in the variable and in the parameter at once; this asymmetry is the defining feature of the band and has no analogue in the bilinear rows. The right family closes under composition, $R^{\star}_{\tilde A}R^{\star}_{\tilde B}=R^{\star}_{\tilde A\tilde B}$, and contains the identity; the left family does not, $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}L^{\star}_{\tilde B}=T_{\tilde A,\tilde B^{*}}$, and the composite of two left multiplications is the two-sided multiplication $T$, so there is no single representation and no enveloping operator algebra of the associative kind, against the composition band where $L_{\tilde A}L_{\tilde B}=L_{\tilde A\tilde B}$ and $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{e}}\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$. The form of the band is the Born pairing $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})=\sum_{\mu}P_{\mu}\overline{Q_{\mu}}$, positive definite of Gram matrix the identity, and the adjoint for it conjugates the parameter and keeps the side, $(L_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde A^{*}}$, $(R_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=R_{\tilde A^{*}}$ and $(R^{\star}_{\tilde A})^{\dagger}=R^{\star}_{\tilde A^{*}}$; the conjugate-linear left multiplication is the single exception, and its twisted adjoint is the sandwich $S_{e_0,\tilde A^{*}}$. So the type criteria are criteria on one parameter: the self-adjoint operators are the Hermitian parameters, the observables, and the skew-adjoint ones the anti-Hermitian parameters, the generators; the unitary parameters give the isometries of the form and the inner automorphisms of the product, and the compact slice is where the sandwich and the inner automorphism agree. The two-sided operator is self-adjoint exactly when its parameter is Hermitian up to a phase, both sectors giving the same operator, and it is never skew. On the state space the Born pairing is the norm, the left actions are the matrix units of the algebra, and the cone of the positive elements is the image of the identity. The contrast with the composition band is the point of the pair: there the adjoint is a transpose that swaps the sides, here it is a Hermitian adjoint that keeps them. What is proved is the algebra; what is proposed is the naming.

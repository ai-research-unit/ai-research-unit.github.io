The Four Readings of a Biquaternion: Norm, Rank, Charge and Scale

Introduction

The algebra is read four ways in the corpus, and the four are read off one element. The biquaternion norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the interval and carries the mass shell: of the four, this is the one the word mass names. The rank of the Hermitian square is the count of polarisations, and it reads only whether the mass vanishes, never how much. The compact part of the algebra is what makes the internal charge discrete: this is the internal charge label, read beside the mass and not a reading of it. The polar scale $r$ of the polar representation multiplies the element and is read as a change of unit: it is the unit the mass is measured in and not a value of it. Four readings, one element, and no article states on which elements they agree. This article is that statement. It owns no new theorem: it collects the four readings, marks which of them the word mass names, states on which elements they agree, and states exactly where they part company.

The owners are, in order, The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars for the norm and its reversal by the central imaginary, The Biquaternioid General Plain Sesqualgebra (GPS) over $\mathbb{C}$ for the rank and the positive cone, Particle Types, Discrete Charge and Three-Particle Couplings for the compact slice and the discrete charge, and The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions for the polar scale, with the dilation interpretation in Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form. The four forms and the sesquilinear product are Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, units $\vec e_k^2=-e_t$; the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$ with the four-position $\tilde{R}=ict\,e_t+x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$; the Hermitian square $\tilde{R}\tilde{R}^{*}$ and the rank taken in the matrix model $\mathsf{M}_2$.

The Four Readings, One at a Time

The norm. $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde{R}\tilde{R}^{\natural}=\sum_\mu R_\mu^2$ is the complex-linear quadratic form, its zero set the zero-divisor cone, its value on the material sector the interval $-c^2t^2+x^2+y^2+z^2$. Read as mass, its sign is the mass-shell sign: the shell $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}=-m^2c^2$ is the hyperboloid and the light cone is $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}=0$. The interpretation is value: which level set the element lies on.

The rank. The Hermitian square $\tilde{R}\tilde{R}^{*}$ is positive semidefinite, of rank two when $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$ and of rank at most one on the cone. Beside the mass, the rank gives the count of polarisations: two for the massive type and one for the massless type. The interpretation is count: how many independent rank-one terms the square needs.

The charge. The unitary slice $U=\{\tilde{U}:\tilde{U}\tilde{U}^{*}=e_t\}\cong \mathrm{U}(2)$ is compact, and the finite-dimensional unitary representations of a compact group are labelled by discrete data. Beside the mass, this is the other internal label: the charge is the discrete label of a representation of the compact internal group, and it is discrete because the group is compact. The interpretation is label: which irreducible representation the element transforms in. The slice lies inside the invertible elements, and the cone is the complement of the invertible elements, so no null element is in the slice and no element of the cone carries one of these labels.

The scale. The polar representation writes an element as a polar factor times a phase and a unit element, and the modulus $r$ is the polar scale. The norm is reversed by the central imaginary, $\langle i\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}=-\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}$, and its logarithm $\log\lvert\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}\rvert$ is read as a dilation potential. Beside the mass, the scale is the unit: a change of $r$ is a change of the unit of length, and the conformal rescaling acts on it. The interpretation is unit: how the element scales, not what level set it lies on. The polar representation is stated only for $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, so the cone is the boundary of its domain and not a locus it detects.

Where the Norm and the Rank Agree

Two of the four readings agree on one locus, and they agree exactly: the norm vanishes on the zero-divisor cone and the rank of the Hermitian square drops there, while the charge and the scale are defined only off it. The norm vanishes there; the rank drops to at most one; the other two readings do not reach the cone at all, the compact label because the unitary slice lies in the complement of the cone and the polar scale because its representation requires $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$. Only the massless elements are detected by the norm and the rank together, and the agreement is a statement about that pair, not about the four. This is what justifies calling the norm and the rank two readings of one word: on the massless locus they are two descriptions of one element, and the dictionary of the corpus — massless, rank one, null, on the cone — is the agreement stated twice.

$\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}=0$ exactly when the rank of $\tilde{R}\tilde{R}^{*}$ is at most one, with rank zero only at $\tilde{R}=0$; off the cone the rank is two. The two directions were recomputed on $100$ of $100$ random non-null elements each way, and every constructed nonzero null element has rank one.

Off the cone the agreement stops, and it stops in a stated way. A rank-two element may have $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}>0$ or $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}<0$; on the material sector those are the spacelike and the timelike regions, so rank two does not say which sign, and the mass label "massive" covers both the timelike and the spacelike elements. The corpus's own caution is The Biquaternioid General Plain Sesqualgebra (GPS) over $\mathbb{C}$: no element of the algebra has a negative sesquilinear norm, and the spacelike elements lie outside the state cone rather than inside it, so the rank-two spacelike elements are massive in the rank sense and are not states in the probability sense.

The Pole

Two limiting elements show where the interpretations must be stated with their scope.

The rank-one state. A rank-one element of the informational sector is a pure state — the Hermitian idempotent $\tilde{\Pi}=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z)$ has $\langle\tilde{\Pi}\rangle_{\natural}=0$, rank one, and $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{\Pi}\tilde{\Pi}^{*})=\tfrac12$ — so the same rank-one condition that reads as massless on the material side reads as pure state on the informational side. The two interpretations are not in conflict, because they are interpretations of the rank on two different sectors: a massless material element is a null four-vector, and a rank-one informational element is a state, and both are the same algebraic fact, that the square has one independent rank-one term. What the pole shows is that "rank one = massless" is a statement of the material interpretation, and it must not be carried to the state side, where rank one is what a state is.

The spacelike element. A rank-two element with $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}>0$ on the material sector is spacelike: massive in the rank count and tachyon-like in the interval. Whether it is a physical particle is not an algebraic question, and the algebra settles only that its sesquilinear square is positive, so it is a legitimate element of the algebra and not a legitimate state.

The dictionary is the answer the article offers: the four readings are four questions asked of one element, the norm and the rank coincide on the cone, the charge and the scale are defined only off it, and each one is silent where the others speak. They are not four kinds of mass, and the article does not claim they are one kind either; the claim is that the four readings of the element are the four rows of the table and that only the first of them is the mass, so that a reader who meets the word should ask which reading is meant.

The Fifth Reading, Named and Left Out

A fifth interpretation exists and is deliberately not one of the four. The left-regular eigenvalue problem $L_{\tilde{R}}\tilde Q_1=\lambda\tilde Q_1$ has the spectrum of the operator, $\mathrm{spec}(L_{\tilde{R}})=\mathrm{spec}(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde{R}))$ with each eigenvalue twice, and a spectrum is not a label. The operator spectrum is owned by The Spectra of the Operators on the Biquaternion Algebra with Hermitian Adjoint, and the element spectrum it is built on by Biquaternion Spectral Theory. It is named here because it is the interpretation a reader may expect to find in this list, and it is left out because it answers a different question: it asks what the element does as an operator, not what the element is. No article of the present corpus reads that spectrum as a mass spectrum, and the gap is recorded rather than a connection assumed.

A second sense left out, at the equation level. The corpus also uses the word for the Dirac and Majorana masses of Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form: the two pairings of a field equation with a conjugate of the field, indexed by the conjugation. That is a property of an equation and not of an element, so it is not a fifth row of the dictionary — the four rows are readings of one element, and this pair is a reading of one term.

Physical Readings

  • One of the four readings is the mass; the other three are read beside it. The interpretation is that the framework's mass is the norm's level set, $\langle\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}=-m^2c^2$ on shell, and that the rank, the charge and the scale are separate labels the word mass does not name. The massless locus — the cone — is the one place where the norm and the rank agree. Boundary: a statement about usage, and it adds no identity between the four.
  • Discreteness of charge is compactness. The interpretation of the charge row is that charge is discrete because the internal group is compact, which is the same mechanism as the quantisation of angular momentum and not a separate postulate.
  • The scale is the odd row. The interpretation of the scale row is that it is the one reading that is not a property of the element alone: $r$ is a unit and moves under a conformal rescaling, so it is the row where the framework's mass meets its choice of units, which is the subject of §Physical Units of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

Summary

The algebra is read four ways, all off one element: the norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ (a value and its level set, and the one reading the word mass names), the rank of the Hermitian square (a count of polarisations, reading only whether the mass vanishes), the compact-slice label (a discrete internal charge, not a mass) and the polar scale $r$ (a unit of length, the unit the mass is measured in). The norm and the rank agree on the zero-divisor cone and nowhere else; the charge and the scale are silent about the cone because both are defined only off it. Off the cone the rank does not fix the sign of the norm, so "massive" covers the timelike and the spacelike alike. The rank-one condition that reads as massless on the material sector reads as pure state on the informational sector, and the article keeps the two apart. A fifth interpretation — the operator spectrum of $L_{\tilde{R}}$ — is named and left out, and the Dirac and Majorana pair is placed at the equation level, outside the four rows.

The Coefficients Above $M_C$: Fifteen Constants, One Gauge Parameter and One Angle Without a Row

Introduction

The eleven written rows of the census carry fifteen constants.

$$ 15=1+2+4+8 $$

One coupling, two scalar masses, four quartics, eight Yukawa numbers. $\zeta$ sits in the gauge-fixing row, which is outside the census. $\theta_4$ multiplies a total derivative and so multiplies no row. Seven further constants belong to the six declared zeros.

What Counts as a Parameter

A constant is a parameter when it multiplies a written structure, no field redefinition removes it, and no gauge choice or rephasing removes it. The three removals are distinct: canonical normalisation rescales a field, $\zeta$ is a gauge choice, and the copy basis absorbs six of a Yukawa matrix's eight entries.

Dimension splits the fifteen.

$$ [\mu_{\Phi}^{2}]=[\mu_{2}^{2}]=2, \qquad [g_{4}]=[\lambda_{k}]=[y]=0 . $$

Thirteen dimensionless constants and two masses. The two masses are one scale and one ratio.

One coupling, not fifteen: $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is one simple factor. Two masses, not sixteen: only $\operatorname{tr}\Xi^{2}$ and $(\operatorname{tr}\Xi)^{2}$ are invariant at degree two. Four quartics, not five: five monomials exist at degree four, and the fifth is row $17$ with a zero.

Eight Yukawa numbers, not sixteen. A $2\times2$ complex matrix has eight real entries and the copy basis removes six, leaving $8-8+2=2$. Two matrices share one $\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(2)$ and a generic pair has no stabiliser, so the pair carries $16-8=8$: two singular values each, plus the four numbers in the relative unitary.

The Two Outside

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}=-\frac{1}{2\zeta}\sum_{a}\bigl(\partial_{\imath}A_{a,\imath}\bigr)^{2}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\theta}=\theta_{4}\,\varepsilon^{\imath\jmath\kappa\lambda}\operatorname{tr}F_{\imath\jmath}F_{\kappa\lambda}. $$

$\zeta$ multiplies a written row and cancels from every observable. $\theta_4$ multiplies nothing, because the fourth condition excluded its structure. "Sixteen parameters" is then right about the number and wrong about the kind.

They are not added to the fifteen. The bare mass also shares its flavour group with $y$, so switching it on gives three matrices under one group and $24-8=16$ numbers, not eight plus two.

What the Fifteen Do Not Fix

Every mass is a coefficient, so the phase predicts no mass. It predicts one ratio: the condensate's $(-3,1,1,1)$ makes a lepton-line fermion three times heavier than a spatial-line fermion at the same singular value.

Checks

  1. $1+2+4+8=15$.
  2. Thirteen dimensionless and two of dimension two.
  3. Four quartics written, five admitted; the fifth is row $17$.
  4. One matrix $8-8+2=2$; a pair $16-8=8$; neither is four.
  5. $\zeta$ has a row, $\theta_4$ has none.
  6. The sleeping: $2+1+3+1=7$.

Summary

Fifteen constants over eleven rows: one coupling, two masses, four quartics, eight Yukawa numbers. Thirteen are dimensionless; the two masses are one scale and one ratio. $\zeta$ multiplies a row and cancels; $\theta_4$ multiplies no row. Seven more constants sleep in the declared zeros and are never added to the fifteen.

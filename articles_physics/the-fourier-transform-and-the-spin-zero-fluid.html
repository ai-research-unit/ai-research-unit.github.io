The Fourier Transform and the Spin-Zero Fluid: The Mode Is a Unit Velocity

Introduction

The spin-zero fluid's one defect is that its four-velocity is invertible only where the quantum potential vanishes, and a mode is exactly a point where it does. That is the article's finding, and it is a statement about supports. The shell is $E^{2}/c^{2}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}+2mQ_{\mathrm{qnt}}$, so a mode — whose scale is constant and whose $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ therefore vanishes — sits exactly on the mass shell, and it sits there with a unit four-velocity and with no qualification at all.

The defect is a radial displacement of the support, and that is the second finding. The quantum potential enters the shell's right-hand side, so it changes the radius of the local hyperboloid and not its centre. The charged companion's potential does the opposite: it enters as a translation and leaves the radius alone. Two potentials, one radial and one translational, and the momentum-space picture separates them without any algebra.

The third finding is about the sheets. The conserved density is the charge density and its sign is the sign of the energy, so a mode lies on one sheet and one sheet only. A single mode's charge density has a definite sign. A superposition that occupies both sheets has a density of both signs, and the article gives the measured range.

Position in the block. This is the Fourier companion of The Spin-Zero Fluid and of The Lagrangian and the Spin-Zero Fluid. Its charged companion is The Fourier Transform and the Charged Spin-Zero Fluid. The rung below is The Fourier Transform and the Schrödinger Fluid, and §The Two Scalar Rungs states what the passage removes.

Conventions

The free companion's: $\tilde\Xi=\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\varepsilon\,e_t$ on a central field, $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}=\varrho^{2}$, $S_{\tilde\Xi}=\frac{h}{2\pi}\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$, $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^{2}/\partial r_{t}^{\prime 2}$ on the material sector, and

$$ Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}=-\frac{h^{2}}{8\pi^{2}m}\frac{\Box_\Phi \varrho}{\varrho}, \qquad \tilde V_{\Phi}=\frac{\tilde P_{\Phi}}{m}, \qquad \tilde P_{\Phi}=\tilde\nabla_\Phi S_{\tilde\Xi}, \qquad \rho_{\mathrm{chg}}=\gamma\rho_{\mathrm{num}}, \qquad \nu_m=\frac{mc}{h}. $$

The symbol rule is the Schrödinger rung's, with the temporal slot written once: a plane wave carries $e^{i(\vec\nu_\Phi\cdot\vec r-\nu_t'r_t')}$ with $2\pi\nu_t'=\omega/c=\frac{E}{h\,c}$, so $\partial/\partial r_t'\mapsto-ik_t'$ and $\tilde\nabla_\Phi\mapsto 2\pi i\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi$ with $\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi=i\nu_t'e_t+\vec\nu_\Phi$.

The Mode, and the Second-Order Shell

A mode is a point of $\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi$ and one complex amplitude.

$$ \tilde\Xi=a\,e^{i(2\pi\vec\nu_\Phi\cdot\vec r-2\pi\nu_t'r_t')}e_t, \qquad \varrho=\lvert a\rvert, \qquad \rho_{\mathrm{num}}=\lvert a\rvert^{2}, \qquad \vec v=\frac{h\vec\nu_\Phi}{m}, \qquad \gamma=\frac{h \nu_t'}{2\pi mc}. $$

The quantum potential vanishes at a mode for the same reason as at the rung below: the scale is constant.

$$ \Box_\Phi \varrho=0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}=0 , $$

and this is not an approximation in any parameter. The amplitude equation, which is the mass shell with the curvature term, therefore loses its curvature term and becomes the mass shell outright:

$$ \frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}, \qquad E=\frac{h}{2\pi} c\,2\pi\nu_t', \qquad \vec p=h\,2\pi\vec\nu_\Phi . $$

The unit velocity is then exact.

$$ \langle\tilde V_\Phi\rangle_{\natural} =-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\Bigl(\frac{E^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}\Bigr) =-c^{2} $$

at a mode, by the display above and by nothing else. The check on the two modes $(2\pi\vec\nu_\Phi,2\pi\nu_t')=(0.7,1.220656)$ and $(0.3,1.044031)$, with lengths in units of $1/\nu_m$ and momenta in units of $mc$, gave $\langle\tilde V_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=-1.000000$ in both cases.

So the companion's qualification is a statement about how many modes are present. The velocity is invertible wherever the local word is a single mode, and the failure of the unit is the measure of the local admixture. That is what $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ is, in the momentum-space reading: a measure of the distance between the local field and a single point of the shell.

The stated objection, and the answer. The objection is that "$Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}=0$ at a mode" is trivial, since $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ carries derivatives and a plane wave has none. The objection is correct and the triviality is the finding: a defect that vanishes on a dense set of the solutions and that is a derivative functional is a defect of the superposition and not of the equation. The companion states the defect as a condition on the solution; this article states it as a condition on the support, and the second statement is the one that says what has to be added to a mode to produce it.

The Support, and the Two Potentials

A transform is a measure on a support, and the two potentials of the block act on different attributes of that support.

what is added what it does to the shell the momentum-space face
the quantum potential $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ changes the radius: $m^{2}c^{2}\mapsto m^{2}c^{2}+2mQ_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ the local support is a hyperboloid of a different size
the connection $\varkappa\tilde A_\Phi$ changes the centre: $\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi\mapsto\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi-\frac{\varkappa\tilde A_\Phi}{h}$ the local support is a translated hyperboloid

The first row is this article's and the second is the charged companion's, and the comparison is the reason to state both. A radius cannot be removed by a change of variable and a centre can: a shift of a variable does not change a radius, and a translation is undone by a translation. So the quantum potential is an intrinsic deformation of the support and the connection is a change of chart, and the two statements are the two articles' first results in the momentum-space language.

The radius reading survives the sum. For a general solution the two effects compose and the local support is a translated hyperboloid of shifted radius, which is the mass shell of the companion's equation with both terms in it. Nothing in the composition is an approximation, because the shell is an identity and not an expansion.

The support of a solution is the pole set of the kernel, and that is not a coincidence of this rung. A mode at the label $\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi$ solves the free equation exactly when the symbol vanishes there,

$$ \mathcal N(\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi)=\nu_t'^{\,2}-\lvert\vec\nu_\Phi\rvert^{2}-\nu_m^{2}=0 , $$

so the labels a solution's transform can occupy are the labels at which the kernel's inverse has its pole, and the residue there is the mode's weight. Two consequences follow and neither needs a calculation: the support cannot sit at a label the symbol does not vanish at, and a coupling that enters linearly moves the pole and cannot reshape it, which is the second row of the table above. At the rung below the symbol is affine rather than homogeneous, so its zero set is a paraboloid and not a hyperboloid — no shell in the sense of §The Two Scalar Rungs, but still a set of labels and still the support, and the identification survives with only the shape of the set changed.

What the Action Sees and What the Transform Sees

A constant change of chart is invisible to the action and visible to the transform. The Lagrangian is a sum of norms of products, and the norm is multiplicative, so a constant unit right-multiplied onto the field cancels from every term and the action cannot tell that the change happened. The character is additive, so it reads a product as a convolution, and the label of the changed field is not the changed label.

The obstruction is what a character is, and no skill with the transform removes it. A character respects sums; every chart change in the block is a product; the square of the two transformations therefore does not close, and the objects the block calls invisible are the places where it does not. That is also why the quantum potential has no momentum-space face of its own: it is a ratio of two factors of the word, and a ratio of two transforms is not the transform of a ratio. The action states it as a term, the transform states it as a failure of additivity, and neither statement is the other's.

The Origin of the Label

Three objects sit at the zero label, and that is why the two integrals of the block can be compared at all. The transform of the density read at the zero label is the density integrated over the whole domain, which is the total charge; a constant configuration has support at that label and at no other; and the partition function is the transform of $e^{\frac{2\pi iS}{h}}$ read at zero source. So the charge, the constant configuration and the vacuum amplitude are one point of one space, read three ways:

$$ \rho_{\mathrm{chg},\mathrm{trf}}(0)=\int d^{n}x\;\rho_{\mathrm{chg}}(x)=\text{the total charge}, \qquad Z=Z[0]. $$

The zero label is also the only label at which the action and the transform meet without a convolution. Everywhere else the transform of a product is a convolution and the two integrals are asking different questions; at the origin the convolution collapses to the one value, and the comparison of the two integrals has a point to stand on.

The Two Sheets

A mode lies on one sheet. The charge density is $\rho_{\mathrm{chg}}=\gamma\rho_{\mathrm{num}}$ with $\gamma=E/(mc^{2})$, so its sign is the sign of the mode's energy:

$$ \rho_{\mathrm{chg}}=\frac{h \nu_t'}{2\pi mc}\lvert a\rvert^{2}\ \text{ for the mode }(2\pi\vec\nu_\Phi,2\pi\nu_t'), $$

and a negative-frequency mode carries a negative charge density. The two sheets of the hyperboloid are two disjoint supports, so no mode can straddle them: a point of the support is on one sheet or the other, and the transform of a mode is a delta at one point.

A superposition that occupies both sheets has a density of both signs, and the amplitudes decide whether it does. On one hundred events with $2\pi\vec\nu_\Phi=5.0$ on the positive sheet and $2\pi\vec\nu_\Phi=0.1$ on the negative one, amplitudes $0.5$ and $1.0$, the charge density ran from $-1.777$ to $+2.317$. With amplitudes $1.0$ and $1.0$ the same pair gave $0.0005$ to $8.188$, of one sign. So the sign change is not automatic even when both sheets are occupied, and the reason is arithmetic: the incoherent part and the cross term have opposite signs and the cross term's magnitude is bounded by twice the geometric mean of the two incoherent parts, so the balance decides.

The exact statement that survives every case is the per-mode one. A mode's density has the sign of its energy, exactly, and the two sheets are disjoint supports; anything beyond that is a statement about a particular pair of amplitudes. That is the honest form of the companion's two-sheetedness in momentum space, and the article claims no more.

The Two Scalar Rungs

The passage from this rung to the one below removes the mass from the shell, and the momentum-space table says so in one row.

the Schrödinger rung the Klein–Gordon rung
the shell none: the Hamilton–Jacobi equation constrains nothing $E^{2}/c^{2}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}+2mQ_{\mathrm{qnt}}$
the support of a solution any set of points of the momentum space a measure on the two-sheeted hyperboloid
the sign of the density one sign, always the sign of the energy
the velocity $h\,\vec\nu_{\Phi}/m$, unbounded $h\,\vec\nu_{\Phi}/m$ with $\gamma$ fixed by the shell
$Q$ at a mode zero zero
what $Q$ does deforms the density deforms the radius of the support

The last row is the one the rung above adds, and it is the whole of the difference. At the first-order rung the quantum potential has no shell to deform and its only face is the density; here it has a shell and its face is a radius. The same functional, two pictures, and the second picture is available only because the equation is second order.

The fifth row is the invariant across the two rungs. The quantum potential vanishes at a mode at both, for the same reason and with no parameter in the statement, and the free companion's cross-term reading of it therefore transfers without change. That is why the two Fourier articles of the scalar rung are one article in two orders of the equation, and why their findings are the same finding.

Limits

Constant coefficients. The shell is an identity for constant $m$, so the free case is exact throughout. A potential $V(\vec r)$ makes the symbol position-dependent, and the article uses none.

The particle setting. The article works with plane waves and their superpositions and never with a general solution's spectral measure. A general solution's support is a measure on the shell with an element-valued density, and nothing above needs it.

The denial. The transform carries $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$-valued fields to $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$-valued fields. No step carries a field out of its sector, and the amplitude at a wave number is the same numbers the field had at an event, resummed.

Open Questions

  1. Whether the radius reading is a bound. The companion's charge-sign boundary is a condition on the parameters; in momentum space the question is whether the shifted radius $m^{2}c^{2}+2mQ_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ can be made negative by a large enough curvature and, if so, what the support looks like there. The companion's discussion of the charge-sign boundary suggests the answer is a change of the support's topology, and the article does not settle it.

  2. The measure of the admixture. $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ is one scalar measure of how far the local field is from a single mode. Whether a spectral measure exists that is additive over modes and reduces to $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ at a single mode is open, and the non-additivity proved at the rung below is evidence against the simplest candidate.

  3. The two-sheet balance. §The Two Sheets exhibits amplitudes of one sign and of both signs at the same pair of wave numbers. Whether the boundary between the two cases is a simple inequality in the amplitudes and the two energies is not settled here.

Summary

A mode has a constant scale, so its quantum potential vanishes exactly, and the mass shell it satisfies is the plain one, $E^{2}/c^{2}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}$. Its four-velocity is therefore an invertible element exactly, and the companion's qualification — an invertible element only where the quantum potential vanishes — is a statement that a mode is exactly such a place.

The quantum potential is a measure of the distance between the local field and a single point of the shell. It acts on the radius of the support; the connection acts on the centre. A radius cannot be removed by a change of variable and a centre can, so the quantum potential is an intrinsic deformation and the connection is a change of chart.

The charge density of a mode is $h\,\nu_t'\lvert a\rvert^{2}/(mc)$ and its sign is the sign of the energy, so a mode lies on one sheet of the two-sheeted hyperboloid and the sheets are disjoint supports. A superposition that occupies both can have a density of both signs: on one hundred events the amplitudes $0.5$ and $1.0$ gave $-1.777$ to $+2.317$, and equal amplitudes gave $0.0005$ to $8.188$.

The passage to the first-order rung removes the shell and with it the radius reading, and keeps the vanishing of $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ at a mode unchanged. The same functional has two pictures, one on the density and one on the radius, and the second exists only because the equation is second order.

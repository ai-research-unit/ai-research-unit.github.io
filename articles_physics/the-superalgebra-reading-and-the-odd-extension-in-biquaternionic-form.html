The Superalgebra Reading and the Odd Extension in Biquaternionic Form

Introduction

The biquaternion algebra is the even part of a graded algebra $A=A^0\oplus A^1$. The ambient algebra is generated by $\mathbb{B}$ and four vectors $v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3$ with $v_0^2=e_t$ and $v_k^2=-e_t$; the parity of a product is the number of its vector factors read modulo two, and $A^0=\mathbb{B}$. On $A$ the graded commutator $[a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}=ab-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}ba$ makes the ambient algebra a Lie superalgebra whose even part is the algebra of the series. That structure is built from the product and the parity, and the article develops it with the supercharge, the odd extension and the boundary between what the algebra supplies and what it imports.

A superalgebra used by physics does not carry only a bracket; it carries an adjoint, the operation that turns a state into a dual state and an operator into its Hermitian conjugate, and it is the adjoint that makes the Hamiltonian Hermitian and the probabilities real. The odd sector is reached through the star, the Hermitian conjugation $X^{*}$ of the element, and not through a second parameter; on an action the same structure appears as the dagger, the adjoint of $Q\mapsto aQ$ being $Q\mapsto a^{*}Q$, one mark for each of the two layers. The object of the article is the interaction of the two: a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded algebra with a parity-preserving anti-involution, and the sign it puts on the graded bracket.

Three statements carry the interpretation. The first is that the star is an even anti-involution of the superalgebra, $(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}$ with $\lvert a^{*}\rvert=\lvert a\rvert$; it is the structure the corpus's own physics uses, since the biquaternionic supercharge is paired with its star — written there with the dagger of the operators, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}\}=H$ — in Supersymmetric Quantum Physics in the Biquaternion Framework. The second is that the adjoint reverses the graded bracket up to the parity sign,

$$ \bigl([a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}\bigr)^{*}=-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}\,\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]_{\mathrm{gr}}, $$

so it reverses a commutator and preserves an anticommutator, the reason being that the order reversal of the adjoint and the Koszul sign of the bracket multiply, and cancel exactly on an odd-odd pair. The third is that this is a statement about the convention: the Koszul-signed rule of a super-* would make the star an anti-automorphism of the bracket without the extra sign, and the corpus's star does not obey that rule; the two conventions are distinguished by a check on two generators, and the article records which one the physics uses — and why it has to be that one: on the other convention the square of a Hermitian odd element is anti-Hermitian, so the Hamiltonian would be imaginary.

The body is written in elements. The field is an element $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}$ of $\mathbb{B}$, a state is an element, an action of the algebra on itself is written out as $Q\mapsto aQb$, and the odd generator acts by $Q\mapsto uQ$, the multiplication on the left by one vector. No array is used in the body. The legacy array display of the same objects is collected in The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation, which is the only section of the article where it appears, and it is there that the two Dirac equations the corpus carries — the one-sided chiral pair and the two-sided equation — are kept apart.

The graded bracket, the parity table, the Lie-superalgebra statement and the odd extension are restated here in the form the physics uses; the parity interpretation of the ambient algebra and the sectors are The Grading, Fermion Parity and the Two Sectors in Biquaternionic Form; the supercharge pair and the chirality label are Supersymmetric Quantum Physics in the Biquaternion Framework; the Grassmann envelope is Grassmann Coherent States in Biquaternionic Form and BRST Symmetry in Biquaternionic Form; the commutator bracket and the trace-free part are Symmetric and Antisymmetric Decomposition of the Four General Products; the one-mode parity is Fock Space and Creation/Annihilation Operators in Biquaternionic Form. Nothing owned by those articles is re-derived.

The Graded Algebra and Its Parity

Definition. On a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded algebra $A=A^0\oplus A^1$ the graded commutator is

$$ [a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}=ab-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}ba , $$

the two parities read modulo two. It is the commutator on an even or a mixed pair and the anticommutator on two odd elements, and it satisfies the graded Jacobi identity, making a Lie superalgebra of the graded algebra with the graded commutator as bracket.

Proposition (the parity of the bracket). For homogeneous $a,b$ the bracket is even,

$$ \bigl\lvert[a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}\bigr\rvert=\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert \pmod 2 , $$

so the bracket of two odd elements is even, the bracket of an even and an odd element is odd, and the bracket of two even elements is even. On the ambient algebra,

$$ [\mathbb{B},\mathbb{B}]\subseteq\mathbb{B},\qquad [\mathbb{B},A^1]\subseteq A^1,\qquad \{A^1,A^1\}\subseteq\mathbb{B}. $$

Proof. A product of $i$ and of $j$ frame vectors is a sum of terms of grades $i+j$, $i+j-2$, $\dots$, all of the same parity $i+j$; so the product of an even and an even element is even, of an even and an odd element is odd, and of two odd elements is even, which gives the three inclusions. The bracket is the commutator unless both elements are odd, in which case it is the anticommutator, and the parity of a commutator or an anticommutator is the sum of the parities.

The grades and their parity. The ambient algebra is filtered by the number of vector factors, and the parity is that number reduced modulo two; the filtration is not a grading, because a product of two vectors is a scalar plus a bivector. The four generators satisfy $\{v_\mu,v_\nu\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}e_t$ with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, so $v_0^2=e_t$ and $v_k^2=-e_t$. The table gives the five grades with a basis, the dimension and the part of the ambient algebra that each occupies. The superscript of $A^i$ is the parity and not the grade: $A^0=\mathbb{B}$ collects the grades $0$, $2$ and $4$, and $A^1$ collects the grades $1$ and $3$.

Grade A basis Dimension Parity Part
$0$ $1$ $1$ even $\mathbb{B}$
$1$ $v_\mu$ $4$ odd $A^1$
$2$ $v_\mu v_\nu$, $\mu<\nu$ $6$ even $\mathbb{B}$
$3$ $v_\mu v_\nu v_\rho$ $4$ odd $A^1$
$4$ $\omega=v_0v_1v_2v_3$ $1$ even $\mathbb{B}$

The even part has dimension $1+6+1=8$ and the odd part $4+4=8$; the odd slot is therefore the sum of the vector and the trivector grades, and it is not a single grade.

Remark (the even part is the Lie algebra of the framework). The restriction of the graded bracket to $\mathbb{B}$ is the ordinary commutator, that is the Lie algebra of Symmetric and Antisymmetric Decomposition of the Four General Products, whose trace-free part is the Lorentz algebra. The odd slot is a module over the even part and not a second Lie algebra: the bracket of two odd elements is even, exactly as the bracket of two reflections is a rotation.

The Module and the Action

The module of the article is the algebra read as a module over itself, and every operator on it is written out as an action rather than as an array.

The two-sided action is written out as $Q\mapsto aQb$ with $a,b\in\mathbb{B}$; its one-sided cases are $Q\mapsto aQ$ and $Q\mapsto Qb$, and its adjoint is the action of the starred pair, $Q\mapsto a^{*}Qb^{*}$, taken with the Hermitian form $\langle s,t\rangle_{*}$. The slots, their strata, the composition of two actions and the adjoint are those of Operator Family Conventions and Operator and Calculus Conventions, cited here and not restated.

Proposition (the adjoint of an action, a derived form). An action is its own adjoint exactly when

$$ a^{*}=\lambda a,\qquad b^{*}=\lambda^{-1}b,\qquad \lvert\lambda\rvert=1 , $$

for a central $\lambda$, which is wider than the case of two Hermitian multipliers: the rescaling that carries $a$ to $\mu a$ and $b$ to $b/\mu$ carries $\lambda$ to $\lambda\mu^{-2}$ for $\lvert\mu\rvert=1$, and $\mu^{2}=\pm\lambda$ gives the two signs, so the condition says that the two multipliers can be chosen simultaneously Hermitian or simultaneously anti-Hermitian, and an action with $a^{*}=ia$ and $b^{*}=-ib$ is its own adjoint without either multiplier being so.

Proof. The derived identity is $\langle aQb,t\rangle_{*}=\langle Q,a^{*}tb^{*}\rangle_{*}$, so $aQb$ and $a^{*}Qb^{*}$ agree for every $Q$ exactly under the stated condition, and the form is positive definite.

Definition (the odd unit). Fix a non-isotropic vector $u$ of the frame, $u^{2}=q(u)e_t$. The odd part of the ambient algebra is the set of left multiples of $u$,

$$ A^{1}=\mathbb{B}u , $$

and an odd element is $su$ with $s$ even. The odd slot is reached by one action: $Q\mapsto uQ$ carries the even part into the odd part, and the action of an odd element is $Q\mapsto s\,(uQ)$, the fixed odd multiplier preceded by an even one.

Remark (two properties of the odd unit). Since $u$ is a vector, $u^{*}=-u$, the star of Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Conjugations; and $Q\mapsto uQ$ is an odd action, because it maps the even part to the odd part. Both statements are used below. The star is an anti-automorphism, so that $(su)^{*}=u^{*}s^{*}$ and not $s^{*}u^{*}$.

The Adjoint of the Graded Bracket

Proposition (the star is an even anti-involution of the superalgebra). The Hermitian conjugation satisfies

$$ \boxed{\;(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*},\qquad (a^{*})^{*}=a,\qquad \lvert a^{*}\rvert=\lvert a\rvert\;} $$

so it is an anti-automorphism of the graded algebra and it preserves the grading; it is $\mathbb{C}$-antilinear. The star is that of Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Conjugations, where the first two clauses are fixed; the parity clause is what the article adds, and it was checked on the sixteen basis elements of the ambient algebra. This is the star on the elements; the dagger is the adjoint of the corresponding actions, the adjoint of $Q\mapsto aQ$ being $Q\mapsto a^{*}Q$, so that the two markings name one structure in the two layers.

Proposition (the adjoint reverses a commutator and preserves an anticommutator). For homogeneous $a,b$,

$$ \boxed{\;\bigl([a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}\bigr)^{*}=-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}\,\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]_{\mathrm{gr}}\;}, $$

that is

$$ \bigl([a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}\bigr)^{*}= \begin{cases} -\,\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]_{\mathrm{gr}}, & a,b\ \text{not both odd},\\[2pt] +\,\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]_{\mathrm{gr}}, & a,b\ \text{both odd}. \end{cases} $$

Proof. Apply the anti-automorphism to the definition: $\bigl([a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}\bigr)^{*}=(ab)^{*}-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}(ba)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}a^{*}b^{*}$. Since $\lvert a^{*}\rvert=\lvert a\rvert$ and $\lvert b^{*}\rvert=\lvert b\rvert$, the same exponent occurs in $\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]_{\mathrm{gr}}=a^{*}b^{*}-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b^{*}a^{*}$, and the two expressions differ by the factor $-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}$; for $a,b$ both odd the factor is $+1$, and otherwise it is $-1$. The identity was checked on the sixteen basis elements of the ambient algebra, hence on all $256$ of their products, and separately on the four frame vectors $v_\mu$ and on the products of two of them, for all four parity pairs.

Remark (the same rule in the actions). In the actions the statement is the one of §The Module and the Action, the adjoint of $Q\mapsto aQb$ being $Q\mapsto a^{*}Qb^{*}$, and it is consistent with the element rule because $(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}$: the two multipliers reverse under the star in the same order as the two factors of a product. An action preserves the parity when its two multipliers have the same parity and exchanges it when they differ, and the star preserves the parity of each multiplier, so the adjoint preserves the parity of the action.

Remark (why the two behave differently). The adjoint reverses the order of the two factors, and the graded bracket distinguishes the two orders by the sign $(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}$ exactly in the odd-odd case, where the bracket is symmetric: a symmetric bracket is preserved by the reversal, an antisymmetric one is negated. The statement is therefore not an accident of the ambient algebra but the interaction of two signs, the order reversal of the adjoint and the Koszul sign of the bracket. On the even part, where the bracket is the Lie bracket of the framework, the rule reads $\bigl([a,b]\bigr)^{*}=-\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]$, so the bracket of two anti-Hermitian elements is anti-Hermitian: the anti-Hermitian part is a Lie subalgebra, the compact form of the framework, while the Hermitian generators enter it with the conventional factor $i$.

Remark (the Koszul-signed convention and what the star is not). A superalgebra is often equipped with a super-* satisfying the Koszul-signed rule $(\zeta\eta)^{*}=(-1)^{\lvert\zeta\rvert\lvert\eta\rvert}\eta^{*}\zeta^{*}$. For such a structure the bracket-adjoint identity reads $\bigl([a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}\bigr)^{*}=-\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]_{\mathrm{gr}}$ without the extra parity sign, and the two rules agree on a mixed pair and differ on an odd-odd one. The star of the corpus is not of that kind: on two generators $v_0$ and $v_1$, both odd and both anti-Hermitian, one has

$$ (v_0v_1)^{*}=v_1^{*}v_0^{*}=(-v_1)(-v_0)=v_1v_0=-v_0v_1 , $$

while the Koszul-signed expression would be $(-1)^{1\cdot1}v_1^{*}v_0^{*}=-v_1v_0=+v_0v_1$. The two minus signs of the anti-Hermitian generators cancel in each expression, so the two results differ by the Koszul sign alone: both rules reverse the order of the factors, and only the plain one reverses it without an extra sign. The corpus's star is the plain anti-involution, and the factor $(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}$ in the bracket identity is the price of it. A reader importing the super-* convention from the supersymmetry literature must decide which of the two signs his own star obeys, because the two are not the same map.

Remark (why the physics forces the plain rule). The choice is not aesthetic. Take a supercharge that is Hermitian, $\mathcal{Q}^{*}=\mathcal{Q}$, as the Hamiltonian's Hermiticity requires of the operator that squares to it, and compute its graded square under the two rules. Under the plain rule $(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}$ one finds $(\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{Q})^{*}=\mathcal{Q}^{*}\mathcal{Q}^{*}=\mathcal{Q}^{2}$, so that $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}\}^{*}=2\mathcal{Q}^{2}$ is Hermitian: the Hamiltonian is real. Under the Koszul-signed rule, $(\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{Q})^{*}=(-1)^{1\cdot1}\mathcal{Q}^{*}\mathcal{Q}^{*}=-\mathcal{Q}^{2}$, so the square of a Hermitian odd element is anti-Hermitian and its spectrum imaginary. A superalgebra whose odd generator squares to the Hamiltonian therefore cannot carry the signed rule; the corpus's plain star is the one the physics selects, and the parity sign in the bracket identity above is what that selection costs.

Supercharges, the Adjoint and the Odd Generators

Definition. A supercharge is an odd element $\mathcal{Q}$ whose square is even and central, and the physics pairs it in two ways, which are not the same display.

(i) The square. The anticommutator of the element with itself is its square,

$$ \{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}\}=2\mathcal{Q}^{2}=2H,\qquad [\mathcal{Q},H]_{\mathrm{gr}}=0 , $$

so that the square is a bosonic element commuting with $\mathcal{Q}$, and it is Hermitian as well when $\mathcal{Q}$ is, since $(\mathcal{Q}^{2})^{*}=(\mathcal{Q}^{*})^{2}$. This needs $\mathcal{Q}^{2}\neq0$. In the parity of the ambient algebra it is the relation of $A$, where the square of a frame vector is central, $v_\mu^{2}=g_{\mu\mu}e_t$; in the reading of the supersymmetry article the odd object is the gradient, and its square is the central scalar, $\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\Box$, which is the $H$ of that reading.

(ii) The star. In the Hermitian interpretation the physical pairing is the one with the star rather than with the element itself,

$$ \boxed{\;\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}=2H'\;} $$

with $H'$ the Hamiltonian of that reading; it is the convention of Supersymmetric Quantum Physics in the Biquaternion Framework, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}\}=H$, $\mathcal{Q}^{2}=(\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger})^{2}=0$. There it is the nilpotent realisation that is in play: the supercharge is strictly triangular, its square vanishes, and the Hamiltonian is not a square at all but the off-diagonal product $AA^{\dagger}$, which is why the first display would give $H=0$ and the second does not.

The two agree when $\mathcal{Q}^{*}=\mathcal{Q}$. They also agree, at zero, on one family, and it is worth naming because it shows what the agreement is not. Let $\omega=v_0v_1v_2v_3$ be the pseudoscalar, with $\omega^{2}=-1$, and put

$$ B=v\,(1-i\omega)=v+i\omega v . $$

A vector changes sign under the star and a trivector does not, so $v$ is anti-Hermitian and $i\omega v$ is anti-Hermitian as well: $B^{*}=-B$. Its square is

$$ B^{2}=q(v)\,e_t\,(1+i\omega)(1-i\omega)=q(v)\,e_t\,(1-i^{2}\omega^{2})=0 , $$

because $i^{2}=-1$ and $\omega^{2}=-1$ give $i^{2}\omega^{2}=+1$. On such an element both pairings vanish, so their agreement there carries no Hamiltonian. Apart from that vanishing family the agreement forces $\mathcal{Q}^{*}=\mathcal{Q}$: it makes $\mathcal{Q}^{2}$ Hermitian and $(\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{Q}^{*})^{2}=0$, and a search over the odd slot returns no element on which the two are equal and nonzero. Outside that family, for an odd element that is not Hermitian, the two differ, and on a vector they differ by a sign,

$$ \{u,u\}=2q(u)e_t,\qquad \{u,u^{*}\}=-2q(u)e_t , $$

because $u^{*}=-u$. The factor $i$ that repairs the anti-Hermitian $u$ into the Hermitian $iu$ multiplies the square by $i^{2}=-1$, and then $\{iu,iu\}=-2q(u)e_t=\{\,iu,(iu)^{*}\}$: the two displays are reconciled by one factor of $i^{2}=-1$, and it is the same sign that the Hermitian repair of the framework's gradient carries.

Proposition (the starred pairing is Hermitian and even). If $\mathcal{Q}$ is odd then $\mathcal{Q}^{*}$ is odd and

$$ \{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}=\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{Q}^{*}+\mathcal{Q}^{*}\mathcal{Q} $$

is even and Hermitian, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}^{*}=\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}$; a supercharge is never an element of $\mathbb{B}$.

Proof. The star preserves the parity, so $\mathcal{Q}^{*}$ is odd and the product of two odd elements is even; the anticommutator of two elements and the star of a product give $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}^{*}=(\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{Q}^{*}+\mathcal{Q}^{*}\mathcal{Q})^{*}=\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{Q}^{*}+\mathcal{Q}^{*}\mathcal{Q}$ by the anti-automorphism property. Since $\mathbb{B}=A^0$ contains no odd element, no supercharge is an element of the algebra.

Remark (the action of a supercharge). The supersymmetry article writes the pairing with a dagger, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}\}=H$, because its $\mathcal{Q}$ is an operator on the module; the element of the odd slot is what that operator multiplies by, and the two markings name one structure. Read as an action, the supercharge is $Q\mapsto\mathcal{Q}Q$, and its adjoint is $Q\mapsto\mathcal{Q}^{*}Q$. The pairing of the two actions is the action of the paired element, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}$, with no sign and no order correction, because the star is an anti-automorphism and the two factors are odd, so that their graded bracket is already the anticommutator. This is the precise sense in which the superalgebra of the article is an algebra of actions on itself.

Remark (why the adjoint and not the inverse). The Hamiltonian of the framework must be Hermitian, and its spectrum real, and the pairing $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}$ is Hermitian automatically because the star is an involution and the two odd factors reverse in it; a pairing built with the inverse, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{-1}\}$, has no reason to be. This is the physical content of the Hermitian reading of the odd sector: the operator that pairs the two chiralities is the adjoint of the supercharge and not its inverse, and the corpus's own supersymmetric realisation of the mass pair is written with the dagger for that reason. The star differs from the inverse by a central scalar and the coefficient conjugation, so on the unit slice of the real quaternions the two coincide and elsewhere they differ by a scalar; the bracket structure below is insensitive to it, which is the honest scope of the distinction.

Remark (the supercharge in the module). The odd slot is the set of left multiples $A^1=\mathbb{B}u$ for a non-isotropic vector $u$ ($u^2=q(u)e_t$, $q$ the quadratic form), so an odd element is $\mathcal{Q}=su$ with $s$ even. By the anti-automorphism property, and because a vector is anti-Hermitian, its adjoint is

$$ \mathcal{Q}^{*}=(su)^{*}=u^{*}s^{*}=-u\,s^{*}, $$

an odd element again, and

$$ \mathcal{Q}\,\mathcal{Q}^{*}=s\,u\,(-u)\,s^{*}=-q(u)\,s\,s^{*}, $$

an even and Hermitian element, the first half of the pairing of the supercharge with its star. The product $u^2=q(u)e_t$ is central, so no commutation is needed in this line. The pairing itself is

$$ \{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}=-q(u)\,s\,s^{*}-u\,s^{*}s\,u , $$

and the second term collapses to $-q(u)s^{*}s$ when $s$ has a definite $u$-parity, $us=\pm su$; the standard case is $s$ commuting with $u$, for instance a product of an even number of vectors orthogonal to $u$, and then

$$ \{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}=-q(u)\bigl(s\,s^{*}+s^{*}s\bigr). $$

The condition is not cosmetic. Split the even element $s^{*}s=w_{+}+w_{-}$ by the $u$-parity, $uw_{\pm}=\pm w_{\pm}u$; then $u\,s^{*}s\,u=q(u)(w_{+}-w_{-})$, so a part of $u$-parity $-1$ in $s^{*}s$ enters the second term with the opposite sign. The pairing remains even and Hermitian, as the proposition above proves, but it is the symmetric expression only when $w_{-}=0$. For a spacelike $u$ the factor $-q(u)$ is $+1$, and the pairing is the manifestly positive $ss^{*}+s^{*}s$, which is the form the Hamiltonian takes in the one-mode case.

What Must Be Adjoined

Two constructions in the series adjoin the missing odd sector, and in the Hermitian interpretation each has to be compatible with the star.

The Grassmann envelope. The odd generators can be made to carry anticommuting coefficients by tensoring with a Grassmann algebra, the construction of Grassmann Coherent States in Biquaternionic Form; BRST Symmetry in Biquaternionic Form uses the same envelope for the ghosts and records as an open question whether a biquaternionic envelope can be found that is natural rather than imported. In the Hermitian interpretation the question acquires a second clause: the envelope must carry an involution compatible with the star of the algebra, since a Berezin integral and a Hilbert structure must be defined on the same object. The Grassmann algebra does carry one, the coefficient conjugation extended to the odd coefficients, and that is the envelope the physics uses; what is open is its naturality, not its existence.

One fermionic mode, natively. The one piece of fermionic structure that the algebra does host is a single fermionic mode: the creation and annihilation operators of one mode are elements of the algebra, the parity is the element $(-1)^F=i\vec e_z$ of the informational sector, and the algebra's own graded structure therefore carries the finite one-mode case without any extension. The element is Hermitian, $(i\vec e_z)^{*}=(-i)(-\vec e_z)=i\vec e_z$, and it squares to the identity,

$$ (-1)^F(-1)^F=i^2\,\vec e_z^2=(-1)(-e_t)=e_t , $$

by $i^2=-1$ and $\vec e_z^2=-e_t$, so it is a parity operator of a $*$-unitary theory, $(-1)^F\in\mathbb{M}_+$. The parity of a field, the product of the element over the modes, $\prod_{\text{modes}}(i\vec e_z)$, is not an element of the algebra.

Remark (the count of what is adjoined). One odd generator, and its products with the even elements, exhaust the odd slot: the extension is not a second algebra but one more generator, and the rule odd times odd is even that it brings with it.

One Graded Algebra, Not Two

Proposition. With one odd unit $u$ one has $A=\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}u$, every supercharge is a $\mathbb{B}$-multiple of $u$, and the odd slot carries the Hermitian form induced by the module structure, $\langle su,tu\rangle_{*}=-q(u)\langle s,t\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle s,tu\rangle_{*}=0$ for even $s,t$.

Proof. The decomposition is the rank-two module statement of The Grading, Fermion Parity and the Two Sectors in Biquaternionic Form, and an odd element is $su$ with $s$ even. For the form, $(tu)^{*}=u^{*}t^{*}=-u\,t^{*}$, so

$$ \langle su,tu\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(su\,(tu)^{*}\bigr)=-\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(s\,u^{2}\,t^{*}\bigr)=-q(u)\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(st^{*}\bigr)=-q(u)\langle s,t\rangle_{*} , $$

the third step using $u^{2}=q(u)e_t$, which is central and may be moved past $s$. The orthogonality is the vanishing of the scalar part of an odd element, since $s\,(tu)^{*}=-sut^{*}$ is odd for even $s,t$. The elementwise identity without the scalar part is false, a vector need not commute with an even element, and the claim is made only for the form.

Remark (the sign of the odd form). The factor $-q(u)$ is $+1$ for a spacelike odd unit, $q(u)=-1$, and $-1$ for a timelike one, $q(u)=+1$. The odd slot therefore carries the form of $\mathbb{B}$ itself for a spacelike $u$ and its negative for a timelike one, and the two choices are isometric up to sign; the sign is a property of the chosen odd unit and not of the superalgebra.

Remark (why this is the answer to the two-copies question). A superalgebra needs an even part, an odd part, the graded bracket between them and the adjoint; it does not need two algebras. The pair $(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{B})$ of two independent copies has no bracket $\{\cdot,\cdot\}\to\mathbb{B}$ between the copies, no grading and no adjoint pairing across them, so it is not a superalgebra. The single graded algebra $A=\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}u$ has all of them, and the biquaternion algebra is its even part.

Honest Limits

  • The algebra is not a superalgebra by itself. The parity of the ambient algebra puts all of $\mathbb{B}$ in the even part. The subscript $L$ or $R$ of an element takes two values, and it labels a part of an element in a declared frame; the values are exactly two and only the zero element carries both, and a general element is the sum of an $L$ part and an $R$ part and carries no letter of its own, so the subscript is not a second grading. The statement that the biquaternion algebra is a superalgebra needs the odd slot or an explicit further grading, and this article does not make it.
  • The supercharge is imported. The odd generator is not an element of $\mathbb{B}$; the Grassmann envelope or the odd slot of the ambient algebra is required, and the naturality of that envelope is open in the BRST article.
  • The parity of a field is external. $(-1)^F$ is an element of the algebra for one mode and a product over modes, hence outside it, for a field.
  • The star of the envelope is a convention. The corpus fixes the star on the algebra, and this article uses it there. On an envelope the anti-involution has to be chosen again, and this article does not choose it: the bracket-adjoint identity of §The Adjoint of the Graded Bracket holds for every parity-preserving anti-involution, so it does not select one. Which anti-involution is the physical adjoint is settled by the form the theory pairs its states with, and not by the algebra.
  • The symmetric pairing is conditional. The formula $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}=-q(u)(ss^{*}+s^{*}s)$ is claimed exactly when $s^{*}s$ has no part of $u$-parity $-1$, in particular for an even $s$ of definite $u$-parity, and not in general, where the second term is $-u\,s^{*}s\,u$; what holds always is that the pairing is even and Hermitian.
  • The two pairings coincide only for a Hermitian supercharge. For a non-Hermitian odd element they are two different elements, and they coincide only on the nilpotent family $v(1-i\omega)$, where both vanish; a Hamiltonian read from the pair needs $\mathcal{Q}^{*}=\mathcal{Q}$.
  • Nothing here is a measure or an integral. The Grassmann and Berezin constructions are cited from their articles and are not used; the article is algebra, grading and adjointness.
  • No supersymmetry algebra is claimed to be contained in $\mathbb{B}$. The super-Poincaré algebra, if it is to be written, is written on an extension; the corpus's supersymmetry article states which superalgebra its construction realizes, and this article adds the general boundary and the Hermitian structure, not a second claim.

The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices. The generators carry no frame label: $\gamma_\mu$ is one set of matrices, and the frame is named on the coordinate.

The gammas are a frame of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, not a different algebra. They are the cells of the actions above written as letters, and this section is the only place in the article where they appear: every section above is written in actions.

Here $L_a$ and $R_b$ are the left and right multiplications of §The Module and the Action, and $c(M)$ is the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products.

$$ \begin{aligned} \gamma_0 &= iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}, &\gamma_1 &= iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\\[2pt] \gamma_2 &= iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}, &\gamma_3 &= iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\\[2pt] \gamma_5 &= -iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}. \end{aligned} $$

The same in the axis-free display:

$$ \begin{aligned} \gamma_0 &= iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}, &\gamma_1 &= iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\\[2pt] \gamma_2 &= iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}, &\gamma_3 &= iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\\[2pt] \gamma_5 &= -iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}. \end{aligned} $$

The four $\gamma_\mu$ are the images of the frame vectors $v_\mu$ in the representation, and $\gamma_5$ is not a fifth frame vector but the image of their product, the pseudoscalar: $\gamma_5=i\omega$ with $\omega=\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$. The squares follow from $(L_aR_b)^2=L_{a^2}R_{b^2}$ and $\vec e_k^2=-e_t$,

$$ \gamma_0^2=i^2L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x^{2}}^{\mathrm{pau}}=i^2L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}=L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}=I_4,\qquad \gamma_k^2=i^2L_{\vec e_k^{2}}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y^{2}}^{\mathrm{pau}}=i^2L_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}=-I_4 . $$

In the axis-free display:

$$ \gamma_0^2=i^2L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x^{2}}^{\mathrm{axf}}=i^2L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}=L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}=I_4,\qquad \gamma_k^2=i^2L_{\vec e_k^{2}}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y^{2}}^{\mathrm{axf}}=i^2L_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}=-I_4 . $$

Each square is the product of $i^{2}=-1$ with one minus for every spacelike multiplier: one such multiplier for $\gamma_0$, two for each $\gamma_k$. The products are $(-1)(-1)=+1$ and $(-1)(-1)(-1)=-1$, which is the metric sign. So the four carry the signs $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and

$$ \{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}I_4,\qquad \{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_5\}=0,\qquad \gamma_5^2=I_4 . $$

The fifth gamma. The product $\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$ is computed in cells, and the factors of $i$ are reduced first: the four cells carry $i^4=(i^2)^2=+1$, so the two factors of $i^2=-1$ cancel. The left multipliers multiply in order, $e_t\vec e_x\vec e_y\vec e_z=\vec e_z^2=-e_t$, and the right multipliers compose in reverse, $\vec e_y\vec e_y\vec e_y\vec e_x=\vec e_y^3\vec e_x=-\vec e_y\vec e_x=\vec e_z$. The product is therefore

$$ \gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3=-L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}=-R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}, $$

The same identities in the axis-free display:

$$ \gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3=-L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}=-R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}, $$

and the single $i$ of the definition gives $\gamma_5=i\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3=-iR_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$. The cell $\Gamma:=-iR_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ is the chirality cell of the module, and in this frame $\gamma_5=\Gamma$.

The central grading and the cell. The cell $\gamma_5$ is the representative on the Dirac module of an object that is not an element of the algebra. The central grading is $\chi=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$, with $\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ the central root of $-e_t$ in the complexification, so that

$$ \xi^2=i^2\,\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}^2=(-1)(-e_t)=e_t , $$

by $i^2=-1$; $\xi$ is also the difference $e_+-e_-$ of the two central idempotents $e_\pm=\tfrac12(e_t\pm\xi)$. The sign $\xi$ is central and lies outside $\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{R}}$, and $\gamma_5$ represents it on the module; it is not its definition, and the two must not be identified.

The two Dirac equations. The corpus carries two forms of one dynamics, and they are kept apart by where the second chirality slot sits. In units $\hbar=c=1$, with the biquaternionic gradient $\tilde\nabla=e_t\partial/\partial R_t+\sum_k\vec e_k\partial/\partial R_k$ and its conjugate $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, the one-sided form is the chiral pair

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R=m\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L,\qquad \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L=m\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R , $$

two fields, both equations acting from the left, the chirality carried by the two fields and exchanged by $\natural$. The two-sided form writes a single field and puts the second slot on the right of the algebra element,

$$ \left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial r'_t}+D_3\right)F-m\,i\,\overline{F}\,\vec e_z=0, \qquad D_3=\sum_k\vec e_k\frac{\partial}{\partial R_k}, $$

with the derivative acting from the left, the bar the antilinear entry, and $\vec e_z$ multiplying from the right after the conjugation. Since $i\partial/\partial r'_t=-\partial/\partial R_t$ in these units, the free part is $-\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, and the equation reads

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}F=m\left(-i\overline{F}\,\vec e_z\right), $$

which is the second member of the pair with its left field read as $-i\overline{F}\,\vec e_z$: a conjugation and the fixed direction $\vec e_z$. That right-hand factor is the chirality cell, since $-i\overline{F}\,\vec e_z=\Gamma\,\overline{F}$ with $\Gamma=-iR_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}=\gamma_5$, so the mass of the two-sided equation is carried by the cell that names the chirality — the two-sided counterpart of the off-diagonal mass of the one-sided pair, and the reason the mass is chirality-odd here as it is off-diagonal there. The two forms are one operator read from two sides, and the dictionary between them is that of The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form. In the language of this article the one-sided pair is the superalgebra's own statement in the chirality grading of the supersymmetry article, where the gradient is the odd element and its conjugate is the odd element of the conjugate module, the two paired off-diagonally by the mass; the two-sided equation is instead the cell $L_{\tilde\nabla^{\natural}}$ with the chirality carried on the right: what the one-sided form holds in a second field, the two-sided form holds in the side of the action.

The cells and the cone. A single product is a pure tensor. Writing $c(M)$ for the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products, the products form the seven-dimensional cone $\operatorname{rank}c(M)\le1$, and a general element is a sum of at most four, with $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$. The dictionary is complex-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

The display. Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. The two are carried into one another by $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}=C_{\Pi}L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and a conjugation by $C_{\Pi}$ changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant, so every identity of the section holds in both. An array is a statement about one display; a cell named in the running text keeps the Peirce label, and its axis-free array is the conjugate. The label names the array and not the operator.

Summary

A superalgebra is a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded algebra with the grading as part of its structure, and on it the graded commutator $[a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}=ab-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}ba$ is the commutator for even or mixed pairs and the anticommutator for two odd elements. The biquaternion algebra is the even part of the ambient graded algebra $A=\mathbb{B}\oplus A^{1}$, so it is not by itself a superalgebra, and it must not be conflated with the chirality label of the supersymmetry article or with the sector splitting by the star.

The body is written in actions: an element $a$ acts on an element $Q$ by $Q\mapsto aQ$ on the left and by $Q\mapsto Qa$ on the right, the pair $(a,b)$ acts by $Q\mapsto aQb$, and the adjoint of $Q\mapsto aQb$ is $Q\mapsto a^{*}Qb^{*}$, taken with the Hermitian form of the corpus. The odd slot is $A^{1}=\mathbb{B}u$ for a non-isotropic vector $u$, an odd element is $\mathcal{Q}=su$, and it acts by $Q\mapsto s\,(uQ)$.

Read on the elements with the star, the superalgebra carries an even anti-involution, $(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}$ with $\lvert a^{*}\rvert=\lvert a\rvert$, and the star of the graded bracket is

$$ \bigl([a,b]_{\mathrm{gr}}\bigr)^{*}=-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}\bigl[a^{*},b^{*}\bigr]_{\mathrm{gr}}, $$

so it reverses a commutator and preserves an anticommutator: the star reverses the order of the factors, and only the odd-odd, symmetric case is even under the reversal. The Koszul-signed super-* convention would give the cleaner rule without the parity sign, and the corpus's star is not of that convention; the two are distinguished on two generators, and the physics selects the plain one, because under the signed rule the square of a Hermitian odd element is anti-Hermitian and the Hamiltonian would be imaginary.

A supercharge is an odd element, paired either with itself, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}\}=2\mathcal{Q}^{2}=2H$, which is the square of the ambient algebra and needs $\mathcal{Q}^{2}\neq0$, or with its star, $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}=2H'$, which is the nilpotent pairing of the corpus's supersymmetric quantum physics and is Hermitian and even because the star is an involution. The two agree for a Hermitian supercharge and are reconciled by $i^{2}=-1$ otherwise. In the module $A^{1}=\mathbb{B}u$ a supercharge is $\mathcal{Q}=su$ with adjoint $\mathcal{Q}^{*}=-us^{*}$ and $\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{Q}^{*}=-q(u)ss^{*}$, an even element; the symmetric form $\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\}=-q(u)(ss^{*}+s^{*}s)$ holds when $s$ has a definite $u$-parity, and only the evenness and the Hermiticity hold in general. The odd extension is one generator, not a second algebra, and the odd slot carries the algebra's own Hermitian form, $-q(u)\langle s,t\rangle_{*}$, which is the form of $\mathbb{B}$ for a spacelike $u$ and its negative for a timelike one.

The matrix frame is the frame of the actions written as letters, and it is confined to The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation: the five cells, their squares fixed by $i^2=-1$, the central sign $\chi=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ with $\chi^2=e_t$, and the two Dirac equations — the one-sided chiral pair, whose chirality sits in the two fields, and the two-sided cell equation, whose chirality sits in the side of the action.

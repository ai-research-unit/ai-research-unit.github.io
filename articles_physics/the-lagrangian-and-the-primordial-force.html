The Lagrangian and the Primordial Force: The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Action, One Coupling on Fifteen Generators

Introduction

This article writes the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ action in one display, derives the equations of motion and the constraints the display implies, and states what the display is: a matrix transcription of the Yang–Mills action onto fifteen generators, and not an action built from the algebra's own elements.

The pieces of the action exist in the corpus as fragments. The Feynman Rules of the Model writes one covariant derivative and four pieces and then reads rules off them; the scalar census counts sixteen real components and says in as many words that it establishes no potential; the Yukawa sector diagonalises one matrix. No article writes the four pieces in one display and derives from them. This one does, and it is the article the block exists for: every other force block of the corpus carries its Lagrangian article, and the primordial force carried none.

The parent is The Lagrangian and the Non-Abelian Gauge Fields, which writes $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\sum_{\imath,\jmath}\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath})$ for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and proves that the trace which makes the density gauge invariant is the matrix trace and not the corpus's informational reduction $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$. That step does not change here. What changes is the size of the matrix and the status of the result, and the status is the harder half.

Three things are established.

First, the display. With $\Phi$ the scalar and $\xi_f$ the sixteen Weyl fields,

$$ \mathcal{L} =-\tfrac14\sum_{a,\imath,\jmath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}F_{a,\imath\jmath} +\sum_{f}\bar\xi_{f}\Bigl(-i\sum_{\imath}\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}\Bigr)\xi_{f} +\operatorname{tr}\bigl(D_{\imath}\Phi\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigl(D_{\imath}\Phi\bigr)-V(\Phi) +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gh}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yuk}} , $$

with one covariant derivative, $D_{\imath}=\partial_{\imath}-ig_4\sum_{a=1}^{15}A_{a,\imath}T_{a}$, and one coupling. The coupling is the same $g_4$ in all four pieces, and that identity is the whole content of the word one force.

Second, the equations and the count. The stationarity condition is $D_{\imath}F_{\imath\jmath}=j_{\jmath}$, with the current built from the sixteen and the scalar matrix; the temporal component is a Gauss law per generator, fifteen in all; and the constraint algebra fixes the degrees of freedom to $15\times2$ for the gauge fields, $32$ states for the fermions and $64$ real for the scalar.

Third, the status, and it is the point of the article. $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is written in the algebra; $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is written on it. The compact group of $\mathbb{B}$ is $\mathrm{U}(2)$, of real dimension four, and fifteen generators do not fit inside four. So the action below is a transcription and not a derivation, and it stands with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ on the transcribed side of the ceiling, not with $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ on the derived side. Why the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches $SU(2)$ but Not $SU(3)$ owns the argument; this article states where its display falls in it.

What is not new. The Yang–Mills action with matter is standard, and its derivation is a page of any text. What the corpus contributes is the identification of the fifteen printed arrays with a set $T_{a}$ satisfying $\operatorname{tr}(t_{a}t_{b})=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$ — one rotation and two rescalings, done in §The Normalisation — the trace-versus-pairing distinction, and the ceiling statement.

Conventions

The conventions are restated so that every sign below is checkable from this page.

The algebra and the coordinates. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with $e_t=1$ and $\vec e_{\imath}^{2}=-e_t$, $\imath=x,y,z$; the material sector is $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$, where the time coefficient is imaginary, $Q_t=iq'_t$. The coordinates are $R_{\imath}=(R_t,R_x,R_y,R_z)$, the derivatives are $\partial_{\imath}=\partial/\partial R_{\imath}$, and the measure is $d^4r$. Every index is low and every sum carries its weight, as in Index and Notation Conventions: $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}$ for a spacetime contraction with $\varepsilon_{\imath}=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and the unit metric $\delta_{\imath\jmath}$ raises nothing.

The generators. $T_{a}$, $a=1,\dots,15$, are the printed arrays of the boson block, taken anti-Hermitian: $T_{a}^{\dagger}=-T_{a}$, $\operatorname{tr}T_{a}=0$. The fifteen are the twelve planes $A_{\imath\jmath}$ and $iS_{\imath\jmath}$, the two colour diagonals $d_{2}$ and $d_{3}$, and the corner $iD$; the cell names are the grid's, and Classification of the Bosons fixes them.

The bracket. $[T_{a},T_{b}]=\sum_{c}f_{abc}T_{c}$ with $f_{abc}$ real and totally antisymmetric, $f$ being read off the commutation table: of the $105$ independent pairs, $80$ brackets are nonzero and $29$ triples occur. No factor of $i$ appears in the bracket, because the generators are anti-Hermitian; the factor sits in the covariant derivative instead, where the coupling multiplies an $i$.

The traces. $\operatorname{tr}$ is the matrix trace on $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ enlarged to the $4\times4$ matrices that represent $\mathfrak{su}(4)$; $\mathrm{Sc}$ is the coefficient of $e_t$ in an element of $\mathbb{B}$. The two are different functionals and only the first is used in the action, for the reason §The Trace and Not the Reduction gives.

The fields. $A_{a,\imath}$ is the gauge field of generator $a$, real; $F_{a,\imath\jmath}$ its field strength; $\xi_{f}$, $f=1,\dots,16$, the Weyl fields of $2\cdot\mathbf{4}\oplus2\cdot\bar{\mathbf{4}}$; $\Phi$ the scalar, a $4\times4$ Hermitian matrix in the colour indices and carrying no copy index, so $15+1=16$ real parameters; $\gamma_{\imath}$ the Dirac matrices, written low.

Why the gauge piece is written with $\tfrac14$ and the element form with $\tfrac12$. The two are the same number read in two bases. With $F_{\imath\jmath}=\sum_{a}F_{a,\imath\jmath}T_{a}$ and $\operatorname{tr}(T_{a}T_{b})=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$,

$$ \operatorname{tr}\Bigl(\sum_{\imath,\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath}F_{\imath\jmath}\Bigr) =\tfrac12\sum_{a,\imath,\jmath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}, $$

so $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\sum FF)=-\tfrac14\sum_{a}FF$. The coefficient is not a choice: it is fixed once the generators are normalised, and the normalisation is fixed by the requirement that the kinetic term of $A_{a,\imath}$ be canonical.

Why nothing else appears at dimension four. The gauge invariant, parity-even, dimension-four functionals of the fifteen alone are the square of the curvature and nothing else. A cubic term vanishes because $\operatorname{tr}$ of an odd number of generators is zero, and a term with a derivative of the curvature has dimension five. One further term exists and is not written: the topological density $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbb{F}\wedge\mathbb{F})$, whose integral is quantised and whose coefficient $\theta_4$ is a free parameter of the theory and not a function of the group. It is named here and left to The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Instanton.

The fermion piece in the corpus's coordinates. The kinetic term is

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\psi}=\sum_{f}\bar\xi_{f}\Bigl(-i\sum_{\imath}\gamma_{\imath}D_{\imath}\Bigr)\xi_{f}, \qquad D_{\imath}\xi_{f}=\partial_{\imath}\xi_{f}-ig_4\sum_{a}A_{a,\imath}\,(T_{a}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2})\,\xi_{f}, $$

and the copy index is untouched by every generator. That is the one structural statement the fermion piece makes: the sixteen are $\xi^{\alpha}_{i}$ with the quartet index slow and the copy index fast, so each array acts as $X\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2}$ and the two-index rule of The Fermion–Gauge Vertex in the Quartet Basis is recovered here rather than assumed. The piece contains no mass and no chirality projection; both would need a second structure and the group supplies neither.

The scalar piece. With $\Phi$ a Hermitian $4\times4$ transforming as $\Phi\mapsto U\Phi U^{\dagger}$,

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\Phi}=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(D_{\imath}\Phi\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigl(D_{\imath}\Phi\bigr)-V(\Phi), \qquad D_{\imath}\Phi=\partial_{\imath}\Phi-ig_4\sum_{a}A_{a,\imath}[T_{a},\Phi] . $$

The commutator, because the matrix index is what the mass operator needs. $\bar\xi\Phi\xi$ is a singlet only if $\Phi$ carries one lower and one upper colour index, and $\bar{\mathbf4}\otimes\mathbf4=\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1$ is exactly the adjoint plus the singlet. The trace in the kinetic term is then the only invariant of degree two, and the covariant derivative acts by commutation because that is how a generator acts on an object with one index of each kind.

A quartet would have been cheaper and would have had no mass term. Its derivative is $T_{a}\Phi$ with no commutator and its potential has one quartic instead of two. $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4=\mathbf6\oplus\mathbf{10}$ contains no $\bar{\mathbf4}$, so no $\bar\xi\Phi\xi$ exists, and the sixteen would be massless at every scale with no Yukawa sector at all. The extra structure is the price of the fermion masses, and The Scalar Content records the argument in full.

$\Phi$ carries two colour indices and nothing else. The matrix is Hermitian, so sixteen real parameters, and they are the algebra's: $15$ for the traceless part, which is $\dim\mathrm{SU}(4)$, and $1$ for the trace. Nothing is multiplied, and the sixteen of the scalar is the same kind of count as the fifteen of the vectors.

What this costs, stated so the census and this article agree. Sixteen real components at the scale of the display, and the eating is the transition's: six directions, one per broken generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. So six go and ten survive, this article counts the phase before the eating, and The Scalar Content counts what the transition removes and what it leaves.

The potential, written and not derived. The matrix carries one index of each colour kind, and the trace is the only contraction available. Two invariants of degree four are independent,

$$ V(\Phi)=\mu_{\Phi}^{2}\operatorname{tr}\Phi^{2}+\lambda_{1}\operatorname{tr}\Phi^{4}+\lambda_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\Phi^{2}\bigr)^{2}, $$

and the group fixes neither $\mu_{\Phi}^{2}$ nor the two quartics. Two quartics and not one, because $\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}$ and $(\operatorname{tr}\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}$ are different contractions of four matrix indices and neither is a function of the other.

The symmetric minimum of the unbroken phase is $\mu_{\Phi}^{2}>0$ with both quartics positive, so that $\langle\Phi\rangle=0$ and the group is exact; the other sign is the transition's, and The Breaking of the Primordial Force reads it. This is the one place where the article writes coefficients the group does not supply, and it says so: the scalar census refuses the potential on purpose, and the refusal is correct at that article's scope.

Two Quartics and Not One

The count is a count of contractions, and it is the price of the matrix index.

scalar degree-two invariants degree-four invariants quartics
the quartet $\mathbf4$ $1$ $1$ $1$
the matrix $\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1$ $1$ $2$ $2$

A matrix has two ways to contract four indices and a vector has one. $\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}$ pairs the indices within each pair, $(\operatorname{tr}\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}$ pairs them across, and the difference between the two is a genuine deformation of the potential's shape. A vector cannot tell the two apart, which is why the quartet reading had one quartic and looked economical.

What the second quartic buys. It is what allows a minimum whose direction is not decided by the quadratic term alone, so the vacuum's shape — in particular whether $\Phi$ is proportional to a rank-one matrix or to a multiple of the identity — is a dynamical question rather than a declared one. The corpus's vacuum is rank one, $\Phi\propto D\otimes d$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$, and the second quartic is what makes that a choice the potential can prefer.

What it costs. One more free number in a block whose virtue is that it has one coupling. The gauge sector has one parameter and the scalar sector has three, and the asymmetry is not a defect of the writing: it is the number of invariants a matrix admits.

The gauge-fixing and ghost pieces. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}=-\tfrac{1}{2\zeta}\sum_{a}\bigl(\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}\partial_{\imath}A_{a,\imath}\bigr)^{2}$ with one parameter $\zeta$ for the whole group, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gh}}=\sum_{a}\bar c_{a}\bigl(-\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}\partial_{\imath}D_{\imath}\bigr)c_{a}$ with fifteen ghosts in the adjoint. One parameter and not four, which is the difference between this block and the electroweak one: there the four factors each carried a gauge parameter and the mixing made them unequal.

The Yukawa piece, named and not written. One operator and one matrix in the copy index,

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yuk}}=y_{\alpha\beta}\,\bar\xi^{\alpha}\Phi\,\xi^{\beta}+\text{h.c.}, $$

and The Yukawa Sector owns its invariance, its diagonalisation and the mass relation that follows. This article states only that the operator exists here and could not exist under the quartet reading.

The Operator the Matrix Index Makes Possible

One contraction, read on the indices.

$$ \bar\xi\in\bar{\mathbf4},\qquad \Phi\in\bar{\mathbf4}\otimes\mathbf4=\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1,\qquad \xi\in\mathbf4, \qquad \bar{\mathbf4}\otimes(\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1)\otimes\mathbf4\supset\mathbf1 . $$

There is exactly one singlet in the product, and it comes from the singlet in $\Phi$'s decomposition. So the mass operator is unique: the fifteen part of $\Phi$ has no invariant contraction with one $\bar\xi$ and one $\xi$, and only the trace part contributes. That is why the model has one Yukawa matrix and not a matrix for each block, and it is the structure The Yukawa Sector uses when it reduces the sector to two eigenvalues.

What a quartet would have given. Nothing: $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4=\mathbf6\oplus\mathbf{10}$, and neither term contains a $\bar{\mathbf4}$, so the product $\bar{\mathbf4}\otimes\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4$ has no singlet. The table in §The Scalar Content is the whole argument, and it is the reason the matrix reading is the model's.

What the fermion copy index adds. $y_{\alpha\beta}$ is a $2\times2$ matrix on the two Weyl fields that share a colour index, so the pair is mixed by the Yukawa and not by the gauge sector. Two eigenvalues, one diagonalisation, and the condensate's two entries $(-3v_C,+v_C)$ multiplying them, which is the arithmetic of the mass relation and belongs to the article that computes it.

The Normalisation, and Why the Corner Is Different

The printed arrays are anti-Hermitian and their trace norms take two values.

family arrays $\operatorname{tr}\bigl(G^{\dagger}G\bigr)$ $\operatorname{tr}\bigl(G^{2}\bigr)$
the twelve planes, $A_{\imath\jmath}$ and $iS_{\imath\jmath}$ $12$ $2$ $-2$
the colour diagonals $d_{2}$, $d_{3}$ $2$ $2$ $-2$
the corner $iD$ $1$ $12$ $-12$

One pair among the fifteen is not orthogonal, and it is the only one. $\operatorname{tr}(d_{2}d_{3})=+1$, so the Gram entry $\langle d_{2},d_{3}\rangle=-\operatorname{tr}(d_{2}d_{3})=-1$ while every other pair vanishes. The printed fifteen are a basis and not an orthonormal frame, and the Gram has eigenvalues $1$, twelve copies of $2$, $3$ and $12$.

The repair is one rotation and then one rescaling.

$$ c_{2}=d_{2}+d_{3},\qquad c_{3}=d_{2}-d_{3},\qquad \langle c_{2},c_{2}\rangle=2,\quad\langle c_{3},c_{3}\rangle=6,\quad\langle c_{2},c_{3}\rangle=0 . $$

After the rotation the fifteen are orthogonal, with norm $2$ for fourteen of them and $12$ for the corner. A canonical kinetic term $\operatorname{tr}(t_{a}t_{b})=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$ then fixes

$$ t_{a}=\frac{iG_{a}}{2}\ \ (14\ \text{arrays}),\qquad t_{15}=\frac{iG_{15}}{\sqrt{24}}\ \ (\text{the corner}). $$

Check. For the corner, $\operatorname{tr}(t_{15}t_{15})=-\tfrac{1}{24}\operatorname{tr}(G_{15}G_{15})=\tfrac{12}{24}=\tfrac12$; for a plane, $\operatorname{tr}(t_{a}t_{a})=-\tfrac14(-2)=\tfrac12$. Both families give one half, which is the statement that the normalisation succeeds. The corner's coefficient is smaller by $2/\sqrt{24}=1/\sqrt6$, and that factor is a consequence of the trace and not of the group: the corner is the one diagonal removed from the fundamental and is longer than a plane by $\sqrt6$.

Why the rescaling is not cosmetic. A vertex read off a printed array is off by this factor, and a cross section read off a vertex is off by its square. The factor is stated once, here, so that no table in the block repeats it.

The Trace and Not the Reduction

The density must be invariant under $\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}\mapsto U\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}U^{-1}$, and only the cyclicity of the matrix trace gives that in one line:

$$ \operatorname{tr}\Bigl(\sum_{\imath,\jmath}U\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}U^{-1}U\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}U^{-1}\Bigr) =\operatorname{tr}\Bigl(\sum_{\imath,\jmath}\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}\Bigr). $$

The corpus's informational reduction does not have that property. $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$ is bilinear, symmetric and real on the material sector, but it is not cyclic, so a density built from it would change under a gauge transformation and the theory would not be a gauge theory. The two functionals agree only where one factor is a scalar, and a field strength is not a scalar. The proof is The Lagrangian and the Non-Abelian Gauge Fields's; it is cited here rather than repeated, and it is the sharpest technical statement in either article.

Reality and sign. For anti-Hermitian $\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}$, $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath})$ is real and non-positive, so $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\sum\mathbb{F}\mathbb{F})$ is real and non-negative. The positivity is what makes the energy bounded below, and it follows from the anti-Hermiticity and from nothing else.

The Variation: One Equation and Fifteen Gauss Laws

The curvature's variation is the covariant derivative of the variation, $\delta\mathbb{F}_{\imath\jmath}=D_{\imath}\delta\mathbb{A}_{\jmath}-D_{\jmath}\delta\mathbb{A}_{\imath}$, so stationarity of the gauge piece alone gives $D_{\imath}F_{\imath\jmath}=0$. With the matter pieces present,

$$ D_{\imath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}=j_{a,\jmath}, \qquad j_{a,\jmath}=\sum_{f}\bar\xi_{f}\gamma_{\jmath}t_{a}\xi_{f}+j^{\Phi}_{a,\jmath}, \qquad j^{\Phi}_{a,\jmath}=ig_4\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\operatorname{tr}\Bigl(\bigl(D_{\jmath}\Phi\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigl[t_{a},\Phi\bigr]\Bigr), $$

The fermion term is a vector current and the scalar term is bilinear in $\Phi$. The scalar current is quadratic above $M_C$ and linear below it, and that shift is the whole mechanism of the mass: with $\Phi=v+\varphi$ the current splits into a piece in the fluctuation $\varphi$ and a piece in $\varphi\varphi$, and the first is the $A$–$\varphi$ mixing that a mass term is. Above the transition only the second piece is present, so no one-scalar emission exists and every direction stays massless — which is why the unbroken phase has no mass term and needs none.

The current is covariantly conserved. Gauge invariance of the matter action makes $D_{\imath}j_{\imath}$ vanish once the matter fields are on shell, so the conservation is a consequence of the invariance and not a further condition. The ordinary divergence $\partial_{\imath}j_{\imath}$ is not zero, because it carries the $[A_{\imath},j_{\imath}]$ term. So no gauge charge exists, exactly as in the strong sector: the charge is defined only for the residual $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ below $M_C$, and above it there is a current and no charge.

The temporal component of the equation is not an equation of motion but a constraint. $D_{\imath}F_{a,\imath t}=j_{a,t}$ contains no second time derivative of $A_{a,t}$, so it fixes $A_{a,t}$ in terms of the other fields: a Gauss law per generator, fifteen in all. Each is first class, so each removes two degrees of freedom from a four-component field, and the count is

$$ 4\times15\ \longrightarrow\ 2\times15=30 $$

for the gauge fields, before gauge fixing and with the ghosts counted separately.

The Ceiling: Transcription and Not Derivation

The action above is correct as a Yang–Mills action for $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. It is not an algebra result, and the difference is measurable.

The dimension of the algebra's compact group is four. $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ has compact part $\mathrm{U}(2)=\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$, of real dimension $3+1=4$, and a Lie algebra of fifteen generators does not embed in a four-dimensional one. The $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ action of The Lagrangian and the Non-Abelian Gauge Fields is written in the algebra because its three generators are three of the algebra's own directions. The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ action is written on a $4\times4$ matrix representation that the algebra does not contain.

What transcription costs, item by item.

  • The trace. The invariant density needs a cyclic trace on $\mathbb{C}^{4}$. The algebra supplies one on $\mathbb{C}^{2}$; the enlargement to $\mathbb{C}^{4}$ is a choice of representation, and the fifteen arrays are its matrices.
  • The curl and the cross product. In the algebra the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ bracket is the algebra's own product. Here $f_{abc}$ is read off a table of $105$ pairs, and $29$ nonzero triples among them are the structure constants.
  • The invariant density of the algebra. $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$ exists on every element of $\mathbb{B}$ and is not available for this action, because it is not cyclic. So the one pairing the algebra owns is exactly the one the action cannot use, and this is the precise sense in which the primordial force is transcribed.

What transcription does not cost. The group's content, its representation theory, its anomaly arithmetic and its mass spectrum are all exact. The three species of statement must be kept apart: the identity $2\cdot\mathbf{4}\oplus2\cdot\bar{\mathbf{4}}$ is algebra; the matrices $T_{a}$ are a representation; the action is a transcription. Nothing in the block is stronger than the second.

What the Display Does Not Contain

Six absences, each deliberate.

  • No mass for the fifteen. A mass term $\tfrac12m^{2}\sum_{a,\imath}A_{a,\imath}A_{a,\imath}$ is not gauge invariant, and none is written. The masses come from $\langle\Phi\rangle$ and are the transition's.
  • No potential coefficients. $\mu_{\Phi}^{2}$, $\lambda_{1}$ and $\lambda_{2}$ are the three parameters the group does not fix.
  • No Yukawa value. One matrix, its entries free, its spectrum measured.
  • No value of $g_4$. The coupling multiplies the action and does not cancel from the equation, because the commutator carries one power of it and the derivative none; but its value is a boundary condition taken from below, and The Primordial Coupling owns it.
  • No loop. The running, the anomalous dimensions and the effective potential are one-loop objects and the classical action says nothing about them.
  • No $\theta_4$. The topological term is admitted by the group, is not written in the display, and its coefficient is free.

Summary

The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Lagrangian is one coupling on fifteen generators and four pieces: the curvature's square, the sixteen's kinetic term, the scalar matrix's kinetic term and potential, and the gauge-fixing, ghost and Yukawa terms. One $g_4$ multiplies all four, and that identity, not any vertex, is what the word one force means.

The normalisation is one rotation and two rescalings. The printed fifteen fail to be orthogonal in exactly one place, $\operatorname{tr}(d_{2}d_{3})=+1$; the rotation $c_{2}=d_{2}+d_{3}$, $c_{3}=d_{2}-d_{3}$ repairs it, with norms $2$ and $6$; and $t_{a}=iG_{a}/2$, $t_{15}=iG_{15}/\sqrt{24}$ then give $\operatorname{tr}(t_{a}t_{b})=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$ for all fifteen. The corner is longer than a plane by $\sqrt6$, so every vertex that touches $B'$ carries the factor $1/\sqrt6$, and that factor comes from the trace and not from the group.

The variation gives one equation and fifteen constraints. $D_{\imath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}=j_{a,\jmath}$ with a fermion current and a scalar current that vanishes at $\langle\Phi\rangle=0$; the temporal component is a Gauss law per generator; thirty degrees of freedom survive for the gauge fields, thirty-two states for the fermions and sixteen real for the scalar, with the ghosts subtracting their own.

The trace is the matrix trace and not the informational reduction. Only cyclicity makes the density invariant, $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$ is not cyclic, and the two agree only where one factor is a scalar. This is the sharpest technical point of the article: the one pairing the algebra owns is the one the action cannot use.

The status is transcription. The algebra's compact group is four-dimensional and the group has fifteen generators, so the action is written on a $4\times4$ representation and not in the algebra. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ stands with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ on the transcribed side of the ceiling; only $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and its abelian factor, stand on the derived side.

Six things are deliberately absent: a mass for the fifteen, the potential's coefficients, the Yukawa's value, the value of $g_4$, every loop, and $\theta_4$. Each absence is a place where another article of the block begins.

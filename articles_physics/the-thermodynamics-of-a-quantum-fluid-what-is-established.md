# __The Thermodynamics of a Quantum Fluid: What Is Established__

## Introduction

**The category's question is whether a quantum field has a thermodynamics, and its answer is a ledger: four of its twenty-five articles transcribe standard relativistic thermodynamics into the corpus's letters, and the remaining twenty-one add something the letters make available and the standard formulation does not.**

$$
\text{category}=\underbrace{\text{framework content}}_{21\ \text{articles}}\ \cup\ \underbrace{\text{transcription}}_{9,\ 16,\ 17,\ 24},
$$

**the division the brief's instructions fixed, and the division this article audits.** The number is not a score. **A transcription article is not a weaker article**: a transcription is what makes the framework's claims comparable with the literature, and a category of twenty-one derivations with no transcriptions would be unreadable to anyone who wanted to check it.

**What the ledger can and cannot do.** It can state, article by article, which claims are established within the category, which are quoted from the standard theory, and which are interpretations that the category offers and does not prove. **It cannot state that the framework is right**, because no article in the category makes that claim either.

**The one convention that binds the category.** The entropy inequalities are real. [The Local Entropy Current](the-local-entropy-current.html) declares the entropy current Hermitian once, and every later inequality — the spin diffusion of [Spin Hydrodynamics in Biquaternionic Form](spin-hydrodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.html), the positivity form of [Causal Dissipative Hydrodynamics in Biquaternionic Form](causal-dissipative-hydrodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.html) — inherits that declaration and writes no fresh $\mathrm{Re}$. **One declaration, twenty-five articles.** The ledger records that as the category's single most load-bearing convention, because a reader who misses it will read every inequality as unproven.

## The Category's Four Layers

**The category is ordered, and the order is a dependency chain rather than a sequence of topics.** Each layer uses the layer above it and adds one object.

### The field layer: articles 1 to 3

**Three articles take the field's own stress and flow apart before any temperature is mentioned.**

[The Quantum Stress Tensor of the Biquaternion Scalar Field](the-quantum-stress-tensor-of-the-biquaternion-scalar-field.html) writes $\tilde\Sigma_\Psi=w_{\mathrm{fl}}e_t+(i/c)\vec S_{\mathrm P}$ and shows that the quantum potential is its divergence. **What the layer establishes, in one sentence.** The fluid's variables are read from the field's polar word and not postulated; **the density is the scale, the velocity the boost, the spin the rotor**, and the quantum potential is a second-derivative functional of the density that enters the mass shell and not a pressure.

**What the layer does not establish.** That the fluid exists as a thermodynamic object. **A triple of functions is not a state**: for that, a temperature is needed, and the temperature is the next layer's.

## What the Algebra Supplies, and Where

**The framework's contribution is not one theorem but a set of identifications, and the ledger can name them.** Each is the sentence that a standard treatment would have to postulate.

**The velocity is a boost, not an independent field.** $\tilde V_\Phi=ic\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2}=\gamma(ic\,e_t+\vec v)$ with $\langle\tilde V_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}$. The unit norm is not imposed; it is what a boost supplies. **The mass shell is the statement that the factor is a boost**, so the constraint and the kinematics are one display.

**The spin is a rotor, not an independent field.** $\tilde S_{\Psi,\mathrm{sp}}=\rho\tfrac{\hbar}{2}i\hat s$ with $\hat s=\tilde u_{\mathrm r}\vec e_z\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{\natural}$. **The Frenkel condition is then automatic**, because a rotation cannot produce a temporal component, and [Spin Conservation and the Frenkel Condition](spin-conservation-and-the-frenkel-condition.html) states the equivalence as $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde S_{\Psi,\mathrm{sp}})=0\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{F}$.

**The spin's conservation is the rotor's equation.** $\partial_\eta\tilde u_{\mathrm r}=\tfrac12\vec\Omega_\eta\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ with $\vec\Omega_\eta$ real, and the spin current's divergence is the antisymmetric part of $\vec\Omega_\eta$ contracted with the spin. **No separate spin equation is added to the framework.**

**The quantum potential is the mass shell's correction and not a pressure.** $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}=-(\hbar^{2}/2m)\Box\lVert\tilde\Xi\rVert/\lVert\tilde\Xi\rVert$, entering as $E^{2}/c^{2}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}+2mQ_{\mathrm{qnt}}$. **The article that writes it with the other sign is wrong**, and the sign is fixed by the non-relativistic reduction.

**The entropy inequality is one divergence and one declaration.** $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde J_{\Phi,\mathrm{ent}})\ge0$ with $\tilde J_{\Phi,\mathrm{ent}}$ Hermitian. **The inequality is a statement about a real number because the current's four coefficients are real**, and the corpus declares that once.

**The pure state has no entropy.** The condensate's current vanishes, the second law is saturated, and the $T\to0$ limit of the two-fluid model is the polar-form article's single fluid. **Entropy is friction between components**: mutual friction in the two-fluid model, shear and bulk dissipation in the causal model, spin diffusion in the spin model. **In every case the dissipative term is a square.**

**The temperature is a length and not a frequency.** $\beta=mc/(k_mk_BT)$ with $c\beta=mc^{2}/(k_mk_BT)$; the corpus never writes the inverse temperature as the reciprocal of a frequency, and the identity with that reciprocal is the special case $k_m=mc/\hbar$ — **which is the statement that the field is on its shell, not a definition of $\beta$.**

**The flow is an element with a low index.** $\tilde V_\Phi=\tilde P_\Phi/m$, components $v_{\Phi,\mu}$ only where a component is unavoidable. **The metric is $\delta_{\mu\nu}$ and the index labels a direction and not a tensor slot**, which is why no display in the category raises one.

**Five identifications, one declaration, one sign, one length.** That is what the twenty-one framework articles add to the standard theory, and the list is the ledger's answer to the question the category was built to ask.

## The Modular Sector, and the Unmerged Second Article

**The category holds one modular-Hamiltonian article, and a second remains in *Biquaternion Quantum Fields*.**

[The Modular Hamiltonian in Biquaternionic Form](the-modular-hamiltonian-in-biquaternionic-form.html) is the category's article: the generator of the modular flow, its verification in the algebra, the trace formula and the Gibbs form. **The second, [The Modular Hamiltonian and the First Law of Entanglement in Biquaternionic Form](the-modular-hamiltonian-and-the-first-law-of-entanglement-in-biquaternionic-form.html), sits in the neighbouring section and has not been merged into this one.** The ledger states the fact and does not move the article: **moving an entry is a menu decision and this article does not make menu decisions.**

**Why the split matters for the ledger.** The first law of entanglement is the thermal statement closest to the fluid layer's Gibbs–Duhem relation, and a reader who follows the category's chain from the modular Hamiltonian to the equation of state will not find it. **The gap is a shelf arrangement and not a mathematical one**, and the ledger records it as the category's one structural debt.

**What the modular sector establishes for the rest.** That the thermal circle is the modular flow's period when the state is the vacuum of a wedge; that the modular Hamiltonian is $-\log\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}$, an element of $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$; and that the trace formula $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q\tilde R_\Psi)=2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde R_\Psi)$ makes the trace a scalar part. **That last identity is what makes the partition function of article 6 a scalar and the thermal element of article 9 a vector** — the category's two thermal articles are consistent because the trace is scalar and the state is not.

**What it does not establish, and the ledger's warning.** The framework's modular operator is finite-dimensional, of type I$_2$, and **a type I algebra cannot host the outer modular flow of a type III wedge**. The wedge identification $\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{modular},W}=2\pi G_1$ is therefore an identification of the generator on a finite model with the boost, **not a derivation of the Hawking temperature**; the geometry, the surface gravity, the state and the identification of the flows are all imported. **The category's modular sector is a route to the temperature's meaning, not a computation of its value.**

## Open Questions

**1. The full stress.** Whether a sixteen-component stress can be written as one or two elements of $\mathbb{B}$ is open. **The element has eight real components and the stress sixteen**, so one element cannot hold it; [Spin Conservation and the Frenkel Condition](spin-conservation-and-the-frenkel-condition.html) marks the pair as the natural next attempt and does not construct it.

**2. The spin connection's equation.** $\partial_\eta\tilde u_{\mathrm r}=\tfrac12\vec\Omega_\eta\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ is the rotor's equation once $\vec\Omega_\eta$ is given, and the equation for $\vec\Omega_\eta$ is the specific field's dynamics. **The fermionic field's is the magnetic coupling; the general one is not written.**

**3. The averaged Frenkel condition.** Whether a fluid's averaged polarization satisfies $\mathcal{F}$ in every frame, and not only in the frame in which the average was taken, is open. **The algebra's single-element statement is automatic; the many-element statement is not.**

**4. The relaxation times and $\beta$.** Whether $\tau_q$, $\tau_\pi$ and $\tau_\Pi$ are functions of the imaginary-time circumference, and in what combination, is not established. [Causal Dissipative Hydrodynamics in Biquaternionic Form](causal-dissipative-hydrodynamics-in-biquaternionic-form.html) declines to speculate and says so.

**5. The transport coefficients from the field.** $\zeta$, $\eta_{\mathrm{sh}}$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}$ are not computed from the field in the category. **The two-fluid and spin articles name the mechanisms — friction, shear, spin diffusion — and not the values.**

**6. The nodal solutions' dynamics.** Existence, stability and interaction of nodal solutions are open; [The Nodal Set and Quantized Circulation](the-nodal-set-and-quantized-circulation.html) proves the kinematic statements and leaves the dynamical ones.

**7. The type of the field algebra.** The finite model is type I$_2$; the field's type is not settled in the category, and **without it the modular flow's outer character, and with it the Hawking route, is not available.**

**8. The condensate's thermodynamics.** Whether the $T\to0$ limit of the two-fluid model is the only pure-state limit, and whether a pure state can carry a pressure without a temperature, is stated in [The Two-Fluid Model in Biquaternionic Form](the-two-fluid-model-in-biquaternionic-form.html) as a limit and not as a theorem.

**9. The quantum potential's thermodynamic reading.** [The Quantum Stress Tensor of the Biquaternion Scalar Field](the-quantum-stress-tensor-of-the-biquaternion-scalar-field.html) gives five negatives — no temperature, no free energy, either sign, anisotropic, second derivatives — and **whether any of them can be lifted is open.**

**10. The shelf.** The second modular-Hamiltonian article's placement, which the ledger records and does not change.

## What Is Established and What Is Interpretation

**Established, and checkable from the category's own displays.** That the fluid's variables are the polar word's factors; that the quantum potential is the mass shell's correction with the minus sign and is the quantum stress's divergence; that the flow is not a gradient in the Dirac case and is one in the scalar case; that circulation is quantised on the nodal set; that the Wigner element's first two moments are the density and the current; that $Z$ is a scalar, $\beta$ a length and the thermal element a vector; that a local temperature exists as a pointwise period; that the rigidity condition is the vanishing of the symmetric part of the velocity's gradient and that the corpus's pointwise temperature satisfies it; that the entropy current is Hermitian and the second law one divergence; that the pure state produces no entropy; that the two-fluid model's $T\to0$ limit is the single fluid; that the Frenkel condition is automatic; that Israel–Stewart restores causality and stability.

**Interpretation, and marked as such in the articles that offer it.** That the corpus has no Belinfante ambiguity; that the thermal structure's belonging to the state and not to the manifold is a *reading* of $k_B$'s role rather than a theorem; that the algebra, and not the phase, carries the charge because the nodal set is the zero-divisor cone. **Each of these is a reading the framework supports and none is proved.**

**Not claimed, anywhere in the category.** That the framework is a physical theory of a real fluid; that the transport coefficients follow from it; that it derives the Hawking temperature; that its entropy functional is the only one consistent with the second law; that any of its numbers has been compared with an experiment.

**Why the category stops where it does.** Its last substantive article is the fluid's transport, and beyond transport lies the field theory's own thermodynamics — **the vacuum's equation of state, the Casimir route, the relative entropy** — which the neighbouring sections own. **A ledger that added them would be the whole corpus's ledger and not this category's.**

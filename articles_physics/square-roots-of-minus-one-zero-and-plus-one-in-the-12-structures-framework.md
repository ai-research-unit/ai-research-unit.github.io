# __Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the 12 Structures Framework__

## Introduction

A square root of a central value in one of the twelve structures is an element whose square in that
product is the value, $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$. Three values carry a name in the corpus,
$\lambda=-1$, $\lambda=0$ and $\lambda=+1$, and each has a physical reading that the framework has already
fixed. The roots of $-1$ are the **local complex structures**, the algebraic form of a phase and of the
clock that the corpus reads in *The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light* and in *Square Roots:
the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell*. The roots of $0$ are the **nilpotents**,
the lightlike displacements, the algebraic form of propagation without mass, read in *The Light Cone as
the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone* and in *Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework*. The roots of $+1$
are the **involutions**, the reality conditions and the reflections, and the reversible transformations
of the state space when the structure is the pairing; their operator face is *Observables, Gauge
Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action*. This article is the third physics companion of the
mathematics comparison group, and its mathematics companion is *Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus
One* (`articles_maths/square-roots-of-minus-one-zero-and-plus-one.md`).

The mathematics companion proves three facts that organise the physics. First, the root set of $0$ is a
complex cone with the origin as its apex, while the root sets of $-1$ and $+1$ are affine level sets and
carry no apex, so the value $0$ is the value of a direction and the signed values are the values of a
magnitude. Second, a root of $-1$ or of $+1$ is a **unit** of the algebra and a nonzero root of $0$ is a
**zero divisor**, for the four general products and for the four bilateral structures; the two values
$-1$ and $+1$ are the reversible end of the invertibility criterion and the value $0$ is the
non-invertible end. Third, the two symmetric sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{SPS}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$
lose that rigidity, and the single element $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(e_1+ie_2)$ is a root of $+1$ in the first, a
root of $-1$ in the second, and a zero divisor of the algebra in both.

The physical thesis of the article is that the three named values classify the element by what it does:
$-1$ gives a phase, $0$ gives a propagation, $+1$ gives a reflection or a symmetry, and the twelve
structures differ in which of the three they can produce. Four structures produce all three, two refuse
$-1$ because their square is positive, four produce none of the signed values because their square
vanishes or is pure, and two produce the signed values but not the rigidity that would make them units.
The companion article *Square Roots of a General Element in the 12 Structures Framework* owns the general
radicand, the four existence classes and the mass shell; this article reads the three named values.

## One Equation and Three Physical Names

**The equation.** For a product $\star$ the root set of the value $\lambda e_0$ is
$\mathcal R_\lambda=\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0\}$. The square of a scaled element is
$\mu^{2}(\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi)$ for the six bilinear structures and $\lvert\mu\rvert^{2}(\tilde\Xi\star
\tilde\Xi)$ for the six sesquilinear ones, so the root set of $0$ is invariant under every complex scaling
and is a cone, while a root of $-1$ or of $+1$ scaled is a root of another value unless the scaling is a
sign or a phase. Physically, the value $0$ sets a direction and no magnitude; the values $-1$ and $+1$
set a magnitude and no ray.

**The three names.** The three values are the three objects on which the element theory of the algebra is
built, and the mapping to physics is fixed by the owners.

- **The roots of $-1$ are the local complex structures.** A root $\tilde\Xi$ with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=-e_0$
  turns the algebra into a complex vector space, and the idempotent
  $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ is the projector it defines. Physically a root of $-1$ is a phase: it is the
  algebraic object that a U(1) rotation multiplies, and the corpus reads the speed of light as the
  parameter that the material complex structure carries, in *The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of
  Light*.
- **The roots of $0$ are the nilpotents.** A nonzero element with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=0$ is a lightlike
  displacement; a pure root is a null direction of the vector subspace, and its square vanishing is the
  algebraic statement that two successive displacements along a null direction compose to nothing more
  than the displacement. The physical locus is the light cone, and the reading is *The Light Cone as the
  Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone*; the class is *Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework*.
- **The roots of $+1$ are the involutions.** A root with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=+e_0$ is a reflection, a reality
  condition or a discrete symmetry, and its central pair $\pm e_0$ is the trivial one. When the structure
  is the pairing, the roots of $+1$ are the transformations that leave the probability form invariant,
  that is the unitary group, and the physical reading is *Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality
  of the Internal Action*.

**Units and zero divisors.** The central square decides the nature of every root. A root of $-1$ or of
$+1$ has $C(\tilde\Xi)\neq0$ and is a unit of the algebra, and a nonzero root of $0$ has $C(\tilde\Xi)=0$
and is a zero divisor. The invertibility criterion of the algebra is the non-vanishing of $C$, and the
zero-divisor set of the four general products is the cone $C=0$, so the three named values are the two
ends of the same criterion read on the square: $\pm1$ are the reversible values, $0$ is the
non-reversible one. Physically, an element has a square root of a signed value when a reversible splitting
of it exists, and the value $0$ is the value of the elements that cannot be undone at all.

## Roots of Minus One: the Phase and the Local Complex Structure

**In the plain product.** The classification of the mathematics companion gives the roots of $-1$ of

**In the gauge sesquilinear family.** The fourth product's square has the Krein value as its scalar part,

**Where the roots of $-1$ fail.** The two definite sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{GPS}$ and

## Roots of Zero: the Lightlike Displacement

**The cone as the root set.** The root set of $0$ is a cone in every one of the twelve structures, and in
the four general products it is the cone of the algebra. In $\mathrm{GPA}$ it is the origin together with
the nilpotent cone $\{\Xi_0=0,\ \Xi_1^{2}+\Xi_2^{2}+\Xi_3^{2}=0\}$ of real dimension four, and its
nonzero elements are the nilpotents. In $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SQA}$ it is the whole cone $C=0$, of
real dimension six. In $\mathrm{GPS}$ it is the origin alone, because $H$ vanishes only there. In
$\mathrm{GQS}$ it is the family of the slopes $\lvert t\rvert=1$ of the Krein parametrisation, and in
$\mathrm{SQS}$ it is the nilpotent cone $\alpha(e_0+i\hat\mu)$, of real dimension four. The physical
object is the lightlike displacement.

**The two sub-families.** The cone of roots of $0$ splits as the cone itself does. The pure roots,
$\Xi_0=0$ with $(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=0$, are the nilpotent lightlike displacements of the vector
subspace; the non-pure family, reached only in the structures whose square is not the plain square,
consists of the square-zero elements of the indefinite bands and carries the lightlike family
$c(\hat\mu\pm i)$ of the gauge sesquilinear structure. The elementary physical case is a pure nilpotent
$\tilde\Upsilon$ with $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$: a translation along a null direction, whose iteration adds
nothing quadratic. The corpus's nilpotent convention and the class are owned by *Nilpotents in the 12
Structures Framework*, and the square-zero condition of the fourth product belongs to *The Square of the
General Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and the Two Halves*.

**The physical reading of the failure.** A root of $0$ is a zero divisor, so the value $0$ is the
non-reversible value of the criterion. Physically this is not a defect but the definition of a massless
object: a lightlike displacement cannot be undone by a lightlike displacement, because it has no inverse,

## The Warning of SPS and SQS: a Signed Root That Is Not a Unit

The mathematics companion records one element as the witness of the whole exception,

$$
\tilde w\star\tilde w=e_0 \quad\text{in }\mathrm{SPS},\qquad
\tilde w\star\tilde w=-e_0 \quad\text{in }\mathrm{SQS},\qquad C(\tilde w)=0 ,
$$

so it is a root of $+1$ in the first structure and a root of $-1$ in the second, and it is a zero divisor

vector part of the square, and a signed root can sit on the cone.

The physical reading is a warning about the symmetrisation of a gauge structure. In the gauge
sesquilinear structure a **reality condition** can be lightlike: the element that squares to minus the
identity is a null displacement, not an invertible complex structure, so the structure offers a phase
that cannot be undone. The corpus keeps the general fourth product, where the truth lives, as the owner of

algebraic reason the two theorems of the companion — the root is a unit, and the root is a level set —
must be stated for the general products and not for all twelve.

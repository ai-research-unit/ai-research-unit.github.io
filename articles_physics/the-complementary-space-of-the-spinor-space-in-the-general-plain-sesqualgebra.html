The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Sesqualgebra

Introduction

The companion article The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra reads the minimal left ideal $S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1$ as a Hilbert space for the positive definite sesquilinear form $\langle u,v\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(uv^{*})$. This article reads the other half of the algebra in the same structure. The left regular module splits,

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1\oplus\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2=S\oplus\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2, $$

and the second summand, generated by $\tilde\Pi_2=\tfrac12(e_0-ie_3)$, is the complementary space of the spinor space. In the general plain sesqualgebra (GPS) the complement has a property that the general plain algebra (GPA) does not give it: it is the orthogonal complement of the carrier for the metric, so the algebraic split and the metric split coincide.

This article develops the orthogonal complement, the metric of the complement, the orthogonal direct sum of the two copies, the separation of the orthogonal complement from the dual, and the GPS interpretation of the doubling. It is the third of four focused pieces; its GPA sibling The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Algebra reads the same subspace in the plain product and shows that there the two splits do not coincide. The two complements are therefore the sharpest place where the GPA and GPS interpretations part company: the same subspace, orthogonal for one structure and not for the other.

The conventions are those of the block. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central scalar imaginary $i$; the Hermitian conjugation is ${}^{*}$, the natural conjugation ${}^{\natural}$, the coefficient conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$; and $\operatorname{Sc}$ is the scalar part with $\operatorname{Tr}=2\,\operatorname{Sc}$. The plain sesquilinear product is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$; the plain bilinear product, written by juxtaposition, belongs to the sibling article.

The Orthogonal Complement

The Form and Orthogonality

The metric of the block is

$$ \langle u,v\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(uv^{*})=\sum_\mu u_\mu\overline{v_\mu}, $$

positive definite on the whole algebra, with Gram matrix $\tfrac12 I_2$ on the carrier's basis $(\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2)$. The orthogonal complement of the carrier is

$$ S^{\perp_*}=\{v\in\mathbb{B}:\langle u,v\rangle_*=0\ \text{for all }u\in S\}. $$

The Four Matrix Units Are Mutually Orthogonal

The computation is one table. On the four matrix units

$$ \tilde\Pi_1,\quad\tilde\Upsilon_2,\quad\tilde\Upsilon_1,\quad\tilde\Pi_2 $$

the form is diagonal:

$$ \langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Pi_1\rangle_*=\langle\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*=\langle\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Upsilon_1\rangle_*=\langle\tilde\Pi_2,\tilde\Pi_2\rangle_*=\tfrac12, $$

and every cross term vanishes. The reason is structural: the four matrix units are the four entries $E_{ij}$ of the matrix model, the form is the normalised Hilbert–Schmidt form $\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(XY^{\dagger})$, and distinct matrix units are orthogonal for it. In particular the two basis elements of the carrier, $\tilde\Pi_1$ and $\tilde\Upsilon_2$, are orthogonal to the two basis elements of the complement, $\tilde\Upsilon_1$ and $\tilde\Pi_2$, so

$$ S^{\perp_*}=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2\}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2 . $$

The orthogonal complement of the carrier is exactly the complementary ideal. The dimensions agree, two and two, and the two subspaces meet only at zero, so $\mathbb{B}=S\oplus S^{\perp_*}$ is an orthogonal decomposition.

Why the Two Splits Coincide Here

The coincidence is worth a reason, because it is not automatic. Write a general left ideal as $I=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi$ for an idempotent $\tilde\Pi$, with module complement the Peirce summand $\mathbb{B}(e_0-\tilde\Pi)$. Its metric complement is $$ I^{\perp_*}=\{v\in\mathbb{B}:\langle a\tilde\Pi,v\rangle_*=0\text{ for all }a\in\mathbb{B}\}. $$ Since $\langle a\tilde\Pi,v\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(a\tilde\Pi v^{*})=\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde\Pi v^{*}a)$ by cyclicity of the scalar part, the condition for all $a$ forces $\tilde\Pi v^{*}=0$, that is $v^{*}\in(e_0-\tilde\Pi)\mathbb{B}$ and hence $$ I^{\perp_*}=\mathbb{B}(e_0-\tilde\Pi^{*}), $$ the complementary ideal of the adjoint idempotent. The two complements therefore coincide exactly when $\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi^{*}$, that is, exactly when the generator idempotent is self-adjoint. Both coordinate idempotents are self-adjoint,

$$ \tilde\Pi_1^{*}=\tilde\Pi_1,\qquad \tilde\Pi_2^{*}=\tilde\Pi_2, $$

and that is the reason the module split is also the metric split. The coincidence is not special to the coordinate pair: every Hermitian idempotent $\tilde\Pi(\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})=\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})$, $\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}$ a real unit vector, is self-adjoint, and for all of them the two complements agree. It fails as soon as the generator is not self-adjoint; the simplest example is the idempotent $\tilde\Pi_1+\tilde\Upsilon_1$, whose module complement $\mathbb{B}(e_0-\tilde\Pi_1-\tilde\Upsilon_1)=\mathbb{B}(\tilde\Pi_2-\tilde\Upsilon_1)$ is not its metric complement $\mathbb{B}(e_0-(\tilde\Pi_1+\tilde\Upsilon_1)^{*})=\mathbb{B}(\tilde\Pi_2-\tilde\Upsilon_2)$. The clean orthogonal split is the Hermitian case, and the Hermitian case is the one the vacuum and the particle content use.

The Complement as a Hilbert Space

The Metric of the Complement

The restriction of the form to the complement is positive definite with the same Gram matrix as the carrier. On the basis $(\tilde\Pi_2,\tilde\Upsilon_1)$,

$$ G'=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\tilde\Pi_2,\tilde\Pi_2\rangle_*&\langle\tilde\Pi_2,\tilde\Upsilon_1\rangle_*\\[2pt]\langle\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2\rangle_*&\langle\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Upsilon_1\rangle_*\end{pmatrix}=\tfrac12 I_2, $$

so for a general element $\tilde\phi=\phi_1\tilde\Pi_2+\phi_2\tilde\Upsilon_1$ of the complement,

$$ \langle\tilde\phi,\tilde\phi\rangle_*=\tfrac12\bigl(|\phi_1|^2+|\phi_2|^2\bigr), $$

the same norm as on the carrier. The complement is therefore a second Hilbert space of complex dimension two, isometric to the carrier.

The Isometry Is the Coefficient Conjugation

The carrier and the complement are not only isometric; they are related by the antilinear isometry of the coefficient conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, which fixes the four basis elements $e_\mu$ and negates $i$:

$$ \overline{\tilde\Pi_1}=\tilde\Pi_2,\qquad \overline{\tilde\Upsilon_2}=-\tilde\Upsilon_1,\qquad \bar S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2, $$

and preserves the form, $\langle\bar u,\bar v\rangle_*=\overline{\langle u,v\rangle_*}$, so that $\|\bar u\|=\|u\|$. The metric complement of the carrier is thus its conjugate under the real structure of the algebra, and the two copies are exchangeable.

The Orthogonal Direct Sum and the Doubled State Space

The Doubling

The module decomposition and the metric decomposition agree,

$$ \mathbb{B}=S\perp\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2, $$

so the regular module is the orthogonal direct sum of two isometric copies of the state space. The complement's physical interpretation in the GPS band follows directly: the framework's state space is doubled, the two copies are orthogonal for the probability metric, and a state of one copy has no component in the other as measured by the form. The metric therefore does not mix the two copies; the interaction between them, where it exists, is not a metric effect.

The Mixing Is Right Multiplication, Not the Form

The two copies do interact in the corpus, and the operator that mixes them is a right multiplication, which is $\mathbb{B}$-linear on the left and therefore maps left ideals to left ideals while exchanging them: for instance the nilpotent $\tilde\Upsilon_1$,

$$ \tilde\Pi_1\tilde\Upsilon_1=\tilde\Upsilon_1,\qquad \tilde\Upsilon_2\tilde\Upsilon_1=\tilde\Pi_2, $$

carries the carrier onto the complement and is the module isomorphism between them (the sibling article's computation). The chirality-off-diagonal mass is exactly such a right multiplication (Bosonization in Biquaternionic Form, The Spectral Triple of the Biquaternion Algebra). The interpretation is a clean division of labour: the metric keeps the two copies orthogonal, and the mass couples them by a right multiplication, which is a module and not a metric operation.

The Doubling Is Not a Chirality in the Module Sense

A caution that the companion articles share and that this article states in its metric form. The two ideals carry the same defining representation of the double cover $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, so the doubling is a copy of the state space and not a second representation; at the module level the two chiral halves are $S$ and its conjugate module $\bar S$, which are non-isomorphic, while the two ideals are isomorphic. Where the corpus calls the two ideals the two chiralities — the spectral-triple and chirality-off-diagonal-mass conventions — it is using a second convention. In the metric interpretation, the statement is simply that the complement is the conjugate copy, which is orthogonal to and isometric with the carrier.

Not the Dual

The Dual Is the Right Ideal

The complement is a left ideal. The dual of the carrier — the space of the bras — is a right ideal, reached by the Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}$:

$$ \tilde\Pi_1^{*}=\tilde\Pi_1,\qquad \tilde\Upsilon_2^{*}=\tilde\Upsilon_1, \qquad S^{*}=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_1\}=\tilde\Pi_1\mathbb{B}, $$

the first row of the matrix model, $\mathbb{C}\{E_{11},E_{12}\}$. The dual is not the complement, and it is not the metric complement either: $S^{*}$ contains $\tilde\Upsilon_1$ and $\tilde\Pi_1$, while $S^{\perp_*}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$ contains $\tilde\Upsilon_1$ and $\tilde\Pi_2$, so the two subspaces share the nilpotent $\tilde\Upsilon_1$ and differ in their idempotent, $\tilde\Pi_1$ against $\tilde\Pi_2$. This is the algebraic form of the standard caution that a bra is not an antiparticle: the dual and the conjugate copy are different subspaces, reached by different operations, and a physical argument that uses one must not be read as using the other.

Three Notions, Two of Which Coincide

For the coordinate pair the reader can keep three notions apart:

notion subspace reached by
module complement $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2\}$ Peirce, right multiplication by $\tilde\Upsilon_1$
metric complement $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2\}$ orthogonality for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_*$
conjugate copy $\bar S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2\}$ coefficient conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$
dual $S^{*}=\tilde\Pi_1\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_1\}$ Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}$

The first three coincide for the coordinate idempotents; the fourth does not, and the fourth is the one that is a right module. The coincidence of the first three is a feature of the self-adjointness of the generator idempotent and not of the band, as §Why the Two Splits Coincide Here explains.

Worked Verification

The claims are recomputed on the basis $(\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2)$ and on the matrix units.

  • The four matrix units are orthogonal of norm $\tfrac12$. $\langle E_{ij},E_{kl}\rangle_*=\tfrac12\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}$ in the matrix model, hence every cross term vanishes and each unit has norm $\tfrac12$.
  • The orthogonal complement. $S=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2\}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$, and both are two-dimensional, so $S^{\perp_*}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$ and $\mathbb{B}=S\perp\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$.
  • The complement's metric. $G'=\tfrac12 I_2$ on $(\tilde\Pi_2,\tilde\Upsilon_1)$, so the complement is a Hilbert space isometric to the carrier.
  • The conjugate reaches the complement. $\overline{\tilde\Pi_1}=\tilde\Pi_2$ and $\overline{\tilde\Upsilon_2}=-\tilde\Upsilon_1$, so $\bar S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$, and $\langle\bar u,\bar v\rangle_*=\overline{\langle u,v\rangle_*}$.
  • The dual is different. $\tilde\Pi_1^{*}=\tilde\Pi_1$ and $\tilde\Upsilon_2^{*}=\tilde\Upsilon_1$, so $S^{*}=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_1\}=\tilde\Pi_1\mathbb{B}\neq\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2=S^{\perp_*}$.
  • The mixing. $\tilde\Pi_1\tilde\Upsilon_1=\tilde\Upsilon_1$ and $\tilde\Upsilon_2\tilde\Upsilon_1=\tilde\Pi_2$, so the right multiplication $R_{\tilde\Upsilon_1}$ carries $S$ onto $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$.

Boundaries

  • Cited rather than re-derived. The plain sesquilinear form and its positivity are The Left and Right Multiplications of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra and The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing; the canonical Hermitian form and the Hermitian modules are The Canonical Hermitian Form on the Regular Module of the Biquaternion Algebra and Hermitian Modules over the Biquaternion Algebra with Hermitian Adjoint; the mass and the spectral triple are Bosonization in Biquaternionic Form and The Spectral Triple of the Biquaternion Algebra; the module complement is the GPA sibling The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Algebra.
  • Not claimed. The article identifies no physical degree of freedom with the complement; it does not claim that "a bra is not an antiparticle" is more than the algebraic separation of the dual from the conjugate copy; and it does not claim that the coincidence of the three notions is a general theorem.
  • Only the coordinate pair is treated. The general generator's complement, $\mathbb{B}(e_0-\tilde\Pi^{*})$, is cited, not developed.
  • The GPA interpretation is different. That the module split and the algebra's own form split do not coincide is the GPA sibling's sharp point; the present article's point is the opposite one for the metric.

Physical Readings

  • The state space is doubled, and the two copies are orthogonal. The interpretation is that the regular module is the orthogonal direct sum of two isometric copies of the state space, so the probability metric separates rather than mixes them. Boundary: a counting and orthogonality statement about the algebra, not an identification of the second copy with a particle.
  • The metric does not mix the copies; the mass does. The interpretation is that the operator coupling the two copies is a right multiplication — the chirality-off-diagonal mass — and that a right multiplication is a module operation, not a metric one, so the coupling is invisible to orthogonality. Boundary: the article gives the mechanism and no value or spectrum.
  • The complement is the conjugate copy. The interpretation is that the metric complement of the carrier is its conjugate under the real structure of the algebra, so the second copy is the charge-conjugate copy in the algebraic sense. Boundary: the conjugate copy is not the dual, and the article does not claim that the conjugate copy is an antiparticle.
  • A bra is not an antiparticle. The interpretation is that the dual, the right ideal $\tilde\Pi_1\mathbb{B}$, is a different subspace from the complement, so the two physical arguments that use them are different arguments. Boundary: an algebraic separation of two operations, not a particle-physics theorem.
  • The doubling is not a chirality in the module sense. The interpretation is that the complement carries the same defining representation, so the two copies are not the two chiralities; the module-level chirality is $S$ against $\bar S$. Boundary: a convention warning that separates two uses of the word in the corpus.

Summary

The complementary space of the spinor space $S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1$ in the general plain sesqualgebra is the second minimal left ideal

$$ \mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_2,\tilde\Upsilon_1\},\qquad\tilde\Pi_2=e_0-\tilde\Pi_1,\qquad\tilde\Upsilon_1=\tfrac12(ie_1-e_2), $$

and for the positive definite sesquilinear form $\langle u,v\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(uv^{*})$ it is exactly the orthogonal complement of the carrier, $S^{\perp_*}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$, because the four matrix units are mutually orthogonal of norm $\tfrac12$. The algebraic (Peirce) split and the metric split therefore coincide, and the regular module is the orthogonal direct sum of two isometric copies of the state space, $\mathbb{B}=S\perp\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_2$. The coincidence holds because the generator idempotent is self-adjoint; it holds for every Hermitian idempotent and fails for a non-Hermitian one.

The complement is a second Hilbert space of complex dimension two with the same Gram matrix $\tfrac12 I_2$ and the same norm $\tfrac12(|\phi_1|^2+|\phi_2|^2)$; it is the conjugate of the carrier under the coefficient conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, which is an antilinear isometry, and it is the image of the carrier under the $\mathbb{B}$-linear right multiplication by the nilpotent $\tilde\Upsilon_1$. It is not the dual: the dual is the right ideal $S^{*}=\tilde\Pi_1\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_1\}$, reached by the Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}$, which is a different subspace, so a bra is not an antiparticle. For the coordinate pair the module complement, the metric complement and the conjugate copy coincide, while the dual does not.

Physically the complement is the second, orthogonal copy of the state space. The probability metric keeps the two copies orthogonal; the coupling between them is a right multiplication — the chirality-off-diagonal mass — which is a module operation and not a metric one. The two copies carry the same defining representation of the double cover, so the doubling is a copy and not a chirality; at the module level the two chiral halves are $S$ and its non-isomorphic conjugate module $\bar S$.

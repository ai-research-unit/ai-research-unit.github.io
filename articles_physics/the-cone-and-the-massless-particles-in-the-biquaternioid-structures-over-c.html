The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$

Introduction

The general plain algebra and the general plain sesqualgebra of biquaternions are one space read in two products: the plain product $\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2$, and the plain sesquilinear product $\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2=\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*}$. Every difference between the two structures traces to one cause, the Hermitian conjugation in the second slot. One set survives the comparison unchanged, and it is the set this article is about: the zero divisors, the nonzero elements whose biquaternion norm vanishes. That set is the light cone of the material sector, and it is where the massless particles live in the algebra.

Two questions organise the article, and they are the two the comparison asks of every object.

  • What is the cone in each band? In the composition it is the boundary of invertibility: a nonzero element is invertible if and only if its norm does not vanish, so the cone is exactly the set the plain product cannot divide by. In the probability it is the boundary of the state cone: the sesquilinear square of a nonzero element is a positive element and is never zero, and it drops from rank two to rank one exactly on the cone. The nilpotent index of the cone rises from two in the first band to three in the second, and that index shift is the sharpest single contrast of the comparison.
  • Is the cone a property of either product? It is not. The cone is the null set of the interval form $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2)$, whose algebraic norm is the biquaternion norm, and of the four forms the corpus uses it is the null set of that one alone. The interval is what puts the cone into the algebra; the two products are what give it its two jobs.

The two bands are the general members of the grid of the twelve structures, and that grid is the frame the title names. The cone is cut out by the interval, which is the form of the quaternionic row and not of the general plain pair alone, so the shared vanishing set of the two bands is a feature of the frame rather than of one band. The grid, its twelve operations and their operator rows are The 12 Products of the Biquaternioid Complex Space, The Four General Products and Their Physical Readings, The Twelve Structures Compared: Product, Law and Form and The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket; this article uses the grid and does not rebuild it.

The article then applies the two answers to the massless particles. The photon is the case the corpus has built; the graviton and the chiral fermion sit on the same cone, and what separates the massless particles from one another is data the cone does not carry.

The article is organised as follows. The second section fixes the cone as one set, names its two jobs, places it among the subspaces and separates the three sets the corpus calls a cone. The third reads the cone under each of the twelve structures in turn, with a physical interpretation for each, and is the part the title names. The fourth computes the nilpotent index in the two bands and records the recomputation. The fifth shows that the cone is the null set of the interval and of no other form, and computes its relation to the sector sign and to the gauge metric. The sixth reads the cone in the composition band, including the absorbing property, the collapsing multiplication and the massless limit as a degeneration of the symbol; the seventh reads it in the probability band, including the collapse of the sandwich. The eighth treats the massless particles. The ninth separates what the cone derives from what is imported. The tenth gathers, across the whole corpus and without regard to the older boundaries, every further reading of the cone, each cited in place. The closing sections are the ledger, the boundaries, the summary, the notation table and the literature.

The biquaternion norm is $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$, which is the determinant of the matrix model $\mathsf{M}_2$ with $\mathsf{M}_2(e_t)=I_2$ and $\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_\mu)=-i\sigma_\mu$. The subspaces are the anti-Hermitian material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$, the Hermitian informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$, the real-quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_t,ie_t\}$. One Cone in the Two Bands

The norm and its vanishing set

The cone is read off one object, the biquaternion norm

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q). $$

On the material sector the norm is the Minkowski interval, $\langle\tilde R\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}t^{2}+x^2+y^2+z^2$ for $\tilde R=ict\,e_t+x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$, so its zero set is the light cone $|x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z|=c|t|$. The algebraic character of that zero set is the subject of The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone: a nonzero biquaternion has vanishing norm if and only if it is a zero divisor, that is, if and only if it annihilates some nonzero element. The same article splits the zero divisors into the nilpotents, whose scalar part vanishes and whose square is zero, and the idempotent multiples, whose scalar part does not and which generate the minimal ideals. Both families sit on the cone.

The cone is therefore not a surface that the algebra imports and then meets; it is the vanishing set of the algebra's own quadratic form, and its algebraic name is the zero-divisor set.

The two jobs of one set

The two bands share the norm and share its vanishing set. What they do with the set differs, and each band does exactly one thing with it.

In the composition, the cone is the boundary of invertibility. A nonzero element $\tilde Q$ has a two-sided plain inverse precisely when $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, the inverse being $\tilde Q^{\natural}/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$, and it has none when $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0$. The plain product is the operation that composes transformations and iterates a process (The Products, Their Forms and Their Norms), so the cone is the set of elements that cannot be composed further, and the physics of that failure is propagation, taken up in its own section below.

In the probability, the cone is a rank drop. The plain sesquilinear product gives no element a two-sided inverse at all, and its one-sided inverse exists exactly off the cone, so element invertibility is not the distinguishing signal here. The operator signal agrees with the composition: the left multiplication is injective exactly off the cone, so the cone is the boundary of invertibility of the operator as well. What distinguishes the probability band is the second signal, the rank of the sesquilinear square: the square $\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_1$ is a Hermitian positive element of rank two off the cone and of rank one on it. The Biquaternioid General Plain Sesqualgebra (GPS) over $\mathbb{C}$ proves the criterion and reads the rank drop as a mass label, rank two massive and rank one massless.

The same set is thus a locus of non-invertibility for one product and a locus of rank drop for the other. That is the first concrete form of the comparison: the cone is one object, and each product reports it in its own vocabulary.

Where the cone lives among the subspaces

The cone is not spread over the algebra. The centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and the real-quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ are division algebras and contain no zero divisors at all: on $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ the norm is $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$ with every $Q_\mu$ real, so it is a sum of squares and vanishes only at the origin. The zero divisors require the complexification, and they live in the two Hermitian-type sectors:

  • in $\mathbb{M}_-$, the anti-Hermitian material sector, the cone is the physical light cone;
  • in $\mathbb{M}_+$, the Hermitian informational sector, the cone is the boundary of the future cone of the informational sector, which is the state cone of The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone.

The cone of the material sector and the boundary of the state cone of the informational sector are not two objects that happen to be similar. They are the two faces of one cone, read in the two sectors, and the matrix model makes them literally the same set of rank-one conditions.

A third reading of the same set appears on the complexified phase space of The Symplectic Form and the Biquaternion Norm Cone, where a pure-vector phase-space biquaternion $\vec Z=\vec q+i\vec p$ is null exactly when $\lvert\vec q\rvert=\lvert\vec p\rvert$ and $\vec q\cdot\vec p=0$: the cone is the set where the configuration and the momentum magnitudes are equal and the two vectors are orthogonal, which is the isotropic locus of the sector sign, since $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=-\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ on the pure subspace. There the cone is a genuinely complex object and not a physical locus: the condition equates two quantities of different dimension until a symplectic scale is fixed, and the real phase space meets the cone only at the origin.

Three sets the corpus calls a cone

The word "cone" is used for three different sets in this corpus, and the comparison is only readable if they are kept apart.

  • The zero-divisor cone, $\{\tilde Q\neq0:\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, the null set of the norm. It is the subject of this article, and "the cone" without qualification always means this set here.
  • The state cone of the informational sector, the future cone of $\mathbb{M}_+$, that is $\{\alpha e_t+i\vec w:\alpha\geq\lvert\vec w\rvert\}$, sliced by $\mathrm{Tr}\,\tilde{Ϛ}=1$ for the states. It is a cone of positive elements, and it is the image of the sesquilinear square; the zero-divisor cone is its rank-one boundary, as the next sections show.
  • The cone of an operation, the one-dimensional ray $\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}e_t$ that is the image of the symmetric plain sesquilinear product. Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone warns of exactly this collision: its ray is the normalisation direction of a probability and "is not the state cone".

The gauge band adds a fourth set that it also calls a null cone, the null set $\{K=0\}$ of the gauge metric. Its relation to the zero-divisor cone is computed in the section on the four forms, and it is closer than the name suggests. The operator article uses the word a fifth time, in the frame of the twelve: The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket says of the two indefinite rows that they "keep the classes but lose the cone", and the cone lost there is the positivity cone of the adjoint classes, that is the state cone and not the zero-divisor cone. The names are distinguished by what they are cones of: a vanishing set, a positivity, a normalisation, and a definiteness.

The Cone in the Twelve Structures

This part reads the cone under each of the twelve structures in turn: the product, the rank of its left multiplication off the cone and on it, what the cone is for that structure, and the physical interpretation of the reading. The mathematics of each row is owned by the mathematics companion and its block articles; the reading is this article's. The order is that of The Twelve Structures Compared: Product, Law and Form and of Zero Divisors in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$: the four general structures first, then their four symmetric parts, then their four antisymmetric parts, and inside each group the plain algebra, then the plain sesqualgebra, then the quaternionic algebra, then the quaternionic sesqualgebra.

The companion reads the zero-divisor set of each structure, that is the set of elements whose left multiplication is not injective; this part reads the cone in each, that is the rank that multiplication takes on a lightlike operand. The measure is the same, the real rank of the left multiplication; the two readings coincide on the four general rows, whose zero-divisor set is exactly the punctured cone, and they separate on the other eight. In $\mathrm{SPA}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$ the zero-divisor set is strictly larger than the cone, the pure family joining it; in the six remaining rows the multiplication is already degenerate off the cone, so the whole punctured space is the zero-divisor set, while the cone is only a rank drop for $\mathrm{APS}$ and $\mathrm{AQA}$ and nothing at all for $\mathrm{SPS}$, $\mathrm{SQA}$, $\mathrm{APA}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}$.

Three statements follow, and together they are the grid-level answer to the question of whether the cone belongs to a product.

  • The cone is a degeneration locus of all twelve. No row has an invertible left multiplication at a point of the cone: the rank never reaches eight on it, and it is at most six, the maximum being attained by the two algebra-valued symmetric rows SPA and SQS on the non-pure part of the cone. The cone is therefore a common boundary of the twelve structures, and in that sense it is one set and not twelve.
  • The cone is the whole degeneration locus of exactly four rows. For the four general products the rank is eight off the cone and four on it, so the cone is exactly the line between invertibility and its failure. These are the four rows whose invertibility criterion is the norm, and for them the cone is what the companion article calls the boundary of the band. For six of the eight parts the left multiplication is already degenerate off the cone, and those six carry no unit; the two exceptions are the algebra-valued symmetric rows, SPA carrying the two-sided unit $e_t$ and SQS none, which are invertible at a generic element off the cone and off the pure subspace and degenerate only on the pure subspace and on the cone.
  • The cone is absorbing for exactly those four rows. The product of a lightlike element with anything is lightlike, $\langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}=0\Rightarrow \langle\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=0$, for GPA, GPS, GQA and GQS; it fails for the eight parts, where the symmetrisation or the antisymmetrisation mixes a lightlike term with a second term that need not be lightlike, and the sum can leave the cone.

The reasons the eight parts degenerate are visible in the table. The four antisymmetric rows have no scalar part, so the centre lies in their kernel; the two central-valued symmetric rows have the centre for their whole image, so their operators are of rank two everywhere; and the two algebra-valued symmetric rows SPA and SQS are singular on the pure subspace $\{Q_t=0\}$ as well. Their symmetrised left multiplication is the anticommutator of the matrix model, with eigenvalues $\tfrac12(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)$ of the eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ of $\mathsf M_2(\tilde Q_1)$, whose product carries the factor $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1)^{2}$ and so vanishes on the pure subspace and on the cone alike. The determinant vanishing is not the whole rank story: on the pure subspace the rank is four, while on the non-pure part of the cone one eigenvalue survives and the rank is six, which is why the on-cone entry of these two rows carries two values. The corpus states the one row it tabulates: The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket computes the antisymmetric plain sesquilinear left multiplication as "of real rank six for a unit and four for a zero divisor", which is the row APS of the table.

$\mathrm{GPA}$, the general plain algebra — composition

The product is the plain product $\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2$. The left multiplication has real rank eight off the cone and four on it, so the cone is the exact boundary of invertibility, and it is absorbing: a lightlike factor forces a lightlike composite, $\langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}=0\Rightarrow \langle\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=0$. The physical interpretation is that the composition of operations degenerates exactly on the light cone, so the cone is at once the locus of lightlike propagation and the line between the reversible and the irreversible processes. The reading is The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone, The Biquaternioid General Quaternionic Algebra (GQA) over $\mathbb{C}$ and Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form.

The units are the elements off the cone, forming the group $\mathbb{B}^{\times}\simeq S^{1}\times S^{3}$ (The Biquaternion Unit Group as a Topological Group), and the cone is exactly the complement of that group: the boundary of invertibility is a group boundary, the apex included, the origin lying in the complement and being no zero divisor.

$\mathrm{GPS}$, the general plain sesqualgebra — probability

The product is the pairing $\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*}$. The rank is eight off the cone and four on it, so the cone is the exact boundary of invertibility, and it is absorbing. The physical interpretation is the probability reading of the same boundary: the sesquilinear square drops from rank two to rank one exactly on the cone, so the cone is the rank-drop locus of the Born weight and the pure-state boundary of the state cone. The reading is The Biquaternioid General Plain Sesqualgebra (GPS) over $\mathbb{C}$ and The Spinor Space as a Module and as a Hilbert Space.

The element $e_t$ is a right unit alone, and the invertible elements are the elements off the cone, with the solution $(\tilde Q_1^{-1})^{*}$; the cone is the complement of those elements.

$\mathrm{GQA}$, the general quaternionic algebra — causality

The product is $\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2$, the plain product with the natural conjugation in the first slot, and its square is the central interval. The rank is eight off the cone and four on it, so the cone is the exact boundary of invertibility, and it is absorbing. The physical interpretation is the causality reading of the same boundary: a lightlike element cannot be composed with, the rank drop is the algebraic form of that statement, and the orientation of the product makes the band measure a sign rather than a size. $\mathrm{GQS}$, the general quaternionic sesqualgebra — gauge

The product is the fourth product $\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}$. The rank is eight off the cone and four on it, so the cone is the exact boundary of invertibility, and it is absorbing. The physical interpretation is the gauge reading: the cone is the locus where the indefinite gauge metric degenerates, and the rank drop is the algebraic form of a gauge direction that cannot be normalised. The reading is The Biquaternioid General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (GQS) over $\mathbb{C}$ and The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise.

$\mathrm{SPA}$, the symmetric plain algebra — commutativity

The product is the Jordan product $\tfrac12(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2+\tilde Q_2\tilde Q_1)$. The left multiplication has rank eight off the cone, except on the pure subspace where it is four, and on the cone it is four on the pure part and six on the rest. The cone is a degeneration locus of the row and not the whole one, and the row is not absorbing: the symmetrisation mixes a lightlike term with a second term that need not be lightlike. The physical interpretation is that a symmetrised material composition is a coupling, and a coupling can vanish on the pure family even when both factors are non-degenerate, as the witness $\vec e_x\bullet \vec e_y=0$ shows. They form no group, the product being non-associative. The pure family is the difference: an element with $Q_t=0$ and $\mathrm{Norm}\neq0$ is a plain unit, invertible in the composition, and a zero divisor of the symmetrised row.

The Nilpotent Index: Two against Three

The composition: square-zero is pure isotropic

In the composition band the nilpotent structure is elementary: an element squares to zero exactly when it is pure and isotropic,

$$ \tilde Q_1^{2}=0\quad(\tilde Q_1\neq0)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \tilde Q_1\ \text{pure and isotropic}. $$

Pure means the scalar part vanishes, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1)=Q_{1,0}=0$. Isotropic means the vector part has vanishing square, $\sum_{k}Q_{1k}^{2}=0$. The two conditions together are the criterion $\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q_1)=0$ with $\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q_1)=0$, which for a $2\times2$ matrix is exactly nilpotence, and the recomputation below confirms both directions on random elements and on the parametrised cone $Q_{1,1}=1$, $Q_{1,2}=i\cos t$, $Q_{1,3}=i\sin t$, which satisfies $\sum_k Q_{1k}^{2}=1-1=0$ for every $t$. The pure isotropic cone is therefore the square-zero locus, and its nilpotent index is two: the first power is nonzero and the second vanishes.

The square-zero locus is a proper part of the cone. An idempotent multiple, for instance the Hermitian idempotent $\tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec u)$ with $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$, has vanishing norm and a nonzero square, so the cone is the disjoint union of the square-zero elements and the elements whose square is not zero.

The pairing: nothing squares to zero, and the cone is nilpotent of index three

The Cone Is the Null Set of the Interval and of No Other Form

The comparison uses four forms, and the cone is the null set of one of them. Writing them out is what shows that the cone is a property of the interval rather than of either product.

The Cone in the Probability Band: the Boundary of the State Cone

In the probability the cone is the boundary of the state cone, and the passage from the cone to the state space is one map.

The sesquilinear square lands in the informational sector. For every $\tilde Q_1$ the element $\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_1$ is Hermitian, its scalar part is real and its vector part is imaginary, so it lies in $\mathbb{M}_+$, and it is positive:

$$ \tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_1\in\mathbb{M}_+, \qquad \langle\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}=\lvert \langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}\rvert^{2}, \qquad \mathrm{rank}\,\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_1=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}=0. $$

Writing the image as $\alpha e_t+i\vec w$ with $\alpha$ real and $\vec w$ a real vector, the positivity is the inequality $\alpha\geq\lvert\vec w\rvert$, and the determinant identity is

$$ \alpha^{2}-\lvert\vec w\rvert^{2}=\lvert \langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}\rvert^{2}. $$

Three statements follow, and together they are the band's reading of the cone.

  • The image is the state cone. The image of the sesquilinear square is exactly the set of positive elements of $\mathbb{M}_+$, that is, the set $\{\alpha e_t+i\vec w:\alpha\geq\lvert\vec w\rvert\}$. Every positive element is a sesquilinear square, by taking the positive square root, and the map is onto by that construction. This set is the future light cone of the informational sector, and The Spinor Space as a Module and as a Hilbert Space identifies it with the state cone.
  • The boundary is the image of the zero divisors. The rank-one elements are the boundary $\alpha=\lvert\vec w\rvert$, and by the rank criterion they are precisely the images of the cone. A massless element therefore maps to a rank-one positive element, which is a pure state, and the massless slice of the cone is the pure-state boundary of the state cone.

The weight $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ of the four-forms section is positive definite, with $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}>0$ on every nonzero element and in particular on the whole cone. The pairing has no null vector anywhere, so the cone is not a locus of vanishing probability, and a massless element carries a well-defined boundary state rather than a null state.

The contrast with the composition band is now exact. The same element that the plain product cannot divide by, and that in the composition band is a signal travelling at $c$, is in the probability band a pure state on the boundary of the state cone.

The cone is not only a locus of degeneracy; it is where the square itself degenerates, and the corpus reads it as a critical set. On the cone the square is affine, $\tilde Q^{2}=2Q_t\tilde Q$, so squaring a null element stays on its own complex line and preserves the cone; the critical set of the quadratic map $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde Q^{2}+\tilde Q_2$ is the union of the cone and the vector subspace, and the cone is the part of it on which the square can collapse, on which no escape radius is uniform and on which the Julia set is not a complex manifold. For the parameter zero every idempotent lies on the Julia set of that map, and the singular Julia set contains a complex surface (The Zero Divisors and the Singular Julia Sets, The Biquaternion Quadratic Map and Its Julia Sets). The same set is the largest nontrivial vanishing set of a square: the natural square $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}$ vanishes exactly on the cone, while the sesquilinear square $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$ vanishes at the origin alone (The Squares and the Positive Cone of the Biquaternioid Sesqualgebra).

The physical interpretation is that the cone is the boundary of the algebra's iteration: the lightlike elements are exactly the ones whose square can collapse to a multiple of themselves, and the idempotents — the measurements — sit on the Julia set of the squaring. The two squares separate the two pairings: the interval's square can vanish, the Born pairing's cannot, which is the algebraic face of the fact that a probability weight is never sent to zero by an operation alone.

The cone as the annihilator locus

The cone is where an operation's kernel stops being trivial, and the corpus reads the kernel directly. The annihilator of an element is the set of partners that it kills, so it is the kernel of the multiplication by it, and for a cone element it is a complex plane. Its elements are themselves cone elements: when $\tilde Q_2\neq0$ kills $\tilde Q_1\neq0$, the element $\tilde Q_1$ is a zero divisor of the same product with witness $\tilde Q_2$, and for the four general products, whose zero-divisor set is the cone, the whole annihilator lies on the cone. The sharp statement is that the killers of a lightlike element are lightlike: a null direction can be annihilated only along null directions, and a lightlike signal can be killed only by another lightlike signal.

The plane is not a new object in each of the four general products. The four planes of one cone element are one plain plane read through the two conjugations,

$$ A_{\natural}(\tilde Q_2)=A_{\mathrm{plain}}(\tilde Q_2^{\natural}),\qquad A_{*}(\tilde Q_2)=A_{\mathrm{plain}}(\tilde Q_2)^{*},\qquad A_{\natural*}(\tilde Q_2)=A_{\mathrm{plain}}(\tilde Q_2^{\natural})^{*}, $$

so the composition band determines the kernel of the pairing and of the gauge band, and the four bands differ only by which conjugation is applied to the kernel.

The left and the right annihilators coincide in ten of the twelve structures and differ in exactly two, the plain product $\tilde Q_2\tilde Q_3$ and the general quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde Q_2^{\natural}\tilde Q_3^{*}$, and only at a cone element. At the non-pure cone element $e_t+i\vec e_x$ the two plain planes are

$$ A_{\mathrm{plain}}(e_t+i\vec e_x)=\mathbb{C}\{e_t-i\vec e_x,\ \vec e_z+i\vec e_y\},\qquad B_{\mathrm{plain}}(e_t+i\vec e_x)=\mathbb{C}\{e_t-i\vec e_x,\ \vec e_z-i\vec e_y\}, $$

which share the line of the natural conjugate $\tilde Q_2^{\natural}=e_t-i\vec e_x$ and differ in their second generator. A lightlike element kills its own conjugate on both sides, $\tilde Q_2\tilde Q_2^{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}e_t=0$ on the cone, and the two-sided annihilator is that line. The difference between the two sides is a chirality, the annihilator face of the one-sidedness of the internal action.

The physical interpretation is that the cone is the locus where an operation forgets a direction, and the direction it forgets is again lightlike. Nullity is closed under the operation that produced it: on the four general rows the annihilator of a cone element is a plane inside the cone. The two-sided case is the elementary one, and it is the self-annihilation of a pure cone element, $\tilde Q_2^{2}=0$; the non-pure case is the state, whose conjugate survives on both sides and whose two kernels differ in one further direction. The reading is Annihilators in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$, with the mathematics in its companion Annihilators in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space.

The cone as the root set of zero

The cone is the solution set of the equation $\tilde Q_1^{2}=0$ that is not the origin, and reading it as a root set is a face of its own. The root set of $0$ is a cone in every one of the twelve structures, and in the four general products it is a different part of the same cone, because the square is a different square while the zero-divisor set is one set:

  • $\mathrm{GPA}$: the pure isotropic cone $\{Q_t=0,\ (\vec Q,\vec Q)=0\}$, of real dimension four, the lightlike displacements of the composition;
  • $\mathrm{GPS}$: the origin alone, because the sesquilinear square has the definite scalar part $\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}$, so the probability band has no lightlike element;
  • $\mathrm{GQA}$: the whole cone $\mathcal{C}$, of real dimension six, because the natural square is the norm and every lightlike element annihilates itself;
  • $\mathrm{GQS}$: the subfamily $\overline{\tilde Q}\tilde Q=0$, of real dimension four, a proper part of the cone that contains $e_t+i\vec e_x$ and misses both the pure isotropic element $\vec e_x+i\vec e_y$ and the lightlike $ie_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+i\vec e_z$.

The four root sets are nested and separated in a fixed way. Writing $Z_1$ for the composition, $Z_2$ for the interval, $Z_3$ for the probability and $Z_4$ for the gauge, the order is $\{0\}=Z_3\subsetneq Z_1\subsetneq Z_2$, $Z_3\subsetneq Z_4\subsetneq Z_2$ and $Z_1\cap Z_4=\{0\}$. The plain and the gauge square-zero sets are therefore incomparable inside the cone, meeting only at the apex. The same comparison holds at a general value: the composition realises every value, and the interval's root set at $\lambda$ is the level set $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\lambda$, the cone at $\lambda=0$ and the nondegenerate quadric elsewhere, so the cone is the value zero of the interval's own shell family.

The physical interpretation has two halves. The square-zero elements are the lightlike displacements, and the root set of zero is the smallest physical set that carries masslessness: a translation along a null direction whose iteration adds nothing. And the cone is larger than its displacements. The complement of the square-zero set inside the cone is the non-pure family, the nonzero multiples of the idempotents, which are the states; a state is a zero divisor that is not square-zero, idempotent rather than nilpotent, $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$, and a measurement has no inverse because it lies on the cone. The set of the non-invertible operations is therefore strictly larger than the set of the lightlike ones, and the difference is made of the states. In the probability band the gap runs the other way and is total: the zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space while the square-zero set is the origin alone, so no amplitude of that band is lightlike, and an amplitude is non-invertible because it is a probability and not because it is lightlike. The readings are Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$, Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$ and Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$, with the states in Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$; this subsection gathers their tables for the cone.

The cone in the propagators and the causal kernels

The dynamics has the cone for support, and the corpus computes the support.

The wave operator is the norm of the gradient, $\Box=\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, whose symbol is the norm of the four-wavevector; the zero set of the symbol, $\omega^{2}=c^{2}\vec k^{2}$, is the light cone, which in the algebra is the zero-divisor cone, and the mass shell $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=-\mu^{2}$ is the shifted cone (The Biquaternion D'Alembertian and Its Green's Functions). The invariant kernel depends on the cone variable $\rho^{2}=-c^{2}t^{2}+R^{2}$, so it is singular on the cone and is not defined there as a function; the light-cone delta resolves the quadratic form into its two halves,

$$ \delta(\rho^{2})=\frac{1}{2cR}\Bigl[\delta\Bigl(t-\frac{R}{c}\Bigr)+\delta\Bigl(t+\frac{R}{c}\Bigr)\Bigr], $$

and that Jacobian is what turns the invariant kernel into the causal ones. The retarded and advanced kernels are supported on the cone, $G_{\mathrm{ret}}=\delta(t-R/c)/(4\pi R)$ and $G_{\mathrm{adv}}=\delta(t+R/c)/(4\pi R)$, and the pole prescription chooses the half: the retarded kernel lives on the future cone, the advanced on the past, while the Feynman prescription treats the two poles asymmetrically and is therefore a boundary value rather than a solution selected by a cone support (The Klein–Gordon Propagator and Its Green's Functions in Biquaternionic Form, Exercise: The Retarded Potentials and the Green's Function). With a mass the support leaves the cone: the kernels acquire support inside the cone rather than on it, the sharp light-cone delta acquiring a tail in the Bessel functions, and the closed form $J_0(\mu\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}})$ is valid precisely in the interior of the future cone (Bispinor Fields and the Fundamental Solution of the Generalized Maxwell–Dirac Equation).

The physical interpretation is that the cone is not only the boundary of the dynamics but its carrier: the propagator is singular exactly on the cone, the causal kernels are distributions supported on it, and the passage from massless to massive is the passage from a support on the cone to a support inside it. The cone is where a signal is, not merely where it may not exceed.

The cone as the boundary of the positive and the state cone

In the informational sector the same set is the boundary of positivity, and the corpus reads three faces of that boundary.

The positive cone of the Hermitian sector is the future light cone of the norm: for $\tilde H=h_te_t+i\vec h$, positivity is $\langle\tilde H\rangle_{\natural}=h_t^{2}-\lvert\vec h\rvert^{2}\geq0$ together with $h_t\geq0$, that is $h_t\geq\lvert\vec h\rvert$, and this cone is self-dual with respect to the trace pairing, which is the algebraic reason the pairing serves at once as the Born rule and as the duality that makes the measurement formalism work (POVMs and the Positive Cone in Biquaternionic Form). The states are its trace-one slice: $\tilde{Ϛ}=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec r)$ with $\lvert\vec r\rvert\leq1$, the closed unit ball whose boundary — the pure states — is at once the idempotents, the extreme rays of the cone and the zero divisors at trace one (The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone). The entropy is written in the biquaternion-norm coordinate and leaves the pure states, which are the cone, with infinite slope (Von Neumann Entropy and the Biquaternion Norm). Two further readings attach to the same boundary: the fringe visibility of the double-slit experiment obeys $V^{2}+P^{2}=\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2}\leq1$ with equality exactly on the cone, so maximal contrast is the statement that the which-path state is a zero divisor (The Double-Slit Experiment in Biquaternionic Form); and the fully polarised states of light, which are the points of the Poincaré sphere, are exactly the norm cone of the Hermitian sector, the Pancharatnam phase being the holonomy of the natural connection on the cone, with curvature half the area form of the sphere (Pancharatnam's Phase and the Polarization Sphere in Biquaternionic Form).

The boundary is also the image of the cone under the positivity map. The edge of the state space is therefore the image of the cone and its interior the image of the invertible region, so the positivity of the dagger and the degeneracy of the norm meet on the same boundary.

The same reading fixes the reach of the whole cone picture. The Bloch ball is canonical because the algebra supplies the cone; for a three-level system the state space is eight-dimensional while the positive cone of $\mathbb{M}_+$ is four-dimensional, so no cone of a quadratic form carves out the qutrit state space (The Generalized Bloch Ball for Spin 1 in the Biquaternion Framework). The cone is the state boundary of the two-level system and of the photon, and its sufficiency stops there.

The physical interpretation is that the probability band reads the cone as the edge of what can be measured: inside it lies the ball of mixed states, on it the pure states and the fully polarised light, and the map from the algebra's norm to the space of states is exactly the map from the cone to that edge. The qutrit is the witness that the edge is a feature of the quadratic form and not of statehood as such.

The cone as the causal and informational boundary

The material band reads the cone as the line between the possible and the impossible, and the corpus states that reading in several physical settings.

An exchange of information between two events along a light signal is possible exactly when their displacement is null, and because a null displacement is a zero divisor the channel through which signals pass is the cone itself; the cone forbids the spacelike separations and carries the null ones (The Relativistic Exchange of Information and Clock Synchronisation in Biquaternionic Form, Causality and the Light Cone as an Information Barrier in Biquaternionic Form). The physical interpretation is that every causal notion of the corpus — signal, horizon, characteristic, absolute, inversion, medium — is the one cone, read in the vocabulary of the situation. The global object that sits on it, the horizon, is the object the pointwise algebra cannot itself carry.

The cone in the rest of the physics

Several further physical readings have sense under the twelve structures, and they are gathered here.

Mass has four readings, and two of them detect the cone. The physical interpretation is that the cone is an attractor for the framework's physical vocabulary: mass, positivity, the vacuum, the symmetry group, the projective geometry and the emergent cones of matter all find their statement in it, and the framework predicts nothing by this, but it collects. The cone in the twelve-structure mathematics

The grid itself has a theorem about the cone, and it is the mathematical form of the part above.

The cone is the mark of the four general products. Among the twelve structures the zero-divisor set is the punctured cone exactly for $\mathrm{GPA}$, $\mathrm{GPS}$, $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{GQS}$, and for no other; the eight derived sets are strictly larger, the six antisymmetric and scalar-valued symmetric structures covering the whole punctured space and the two remaining symmetric structures $\mathrm{SPA}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$ carrying the enlarged set $(\{Q_t=0\}\cup\mathcal{C})\setminus\{0\}$. Hence the corollary that the punctured cone is contained in the zero-divisor set of each of the twelve and is equal to the set of exactly four: the cone is the largest subset on which the twelve agree, and an element of vanishing norm admits an annihilating factor in all twelve products. The corollary also breaks the ring-theoretic picture on the derived structures, since $\vec e_x$, of norm $1$, is a zero divisor of $\mathrm{SPA}$ and of $\mathrm{SQS}$ — a zero divisor need not be a non-unit unless the product has a two-sided unit (Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space).

The four forms are the grid's second statement about the cone. The form of each family is one of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$; $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ are definite on each sector and carry no cone there, while $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ are indefinite and carry the light cone. The interval $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ has signature $(3,1)$ on the material sector and $(1,3)$ on the informational one, the same form with its inertia reversed by the sector; $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ has realified signature $(2,6)$ and coincides with $\pm\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ on the two sectors. The interval is multiplicative on the general quaternionic bilinear product, so the units and the zero divisors of that product are closed and the light cone is closed under composition. On the whole algebra the null set of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not the light cone at all but a complex quadric of real dimension six (The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars, The Twelve Structures Compared: Product, Law and Form). The indefinite-sector positivity statements are made with the Krein cone of the negative of the interval (Indefinite Positivity and the Krein Cone of the Biquaternioid Algebra).

Two further grid statements belong here. The cone's isotropic lines are its Peirce lines: the doubly null lines, the isotropic lines that lie in the norm cone as well, are exactly the Peirce lines $\mathbb{C}(e_t\pm i\vec u)$ spanned by the rank-one Hermitian idempotents, each generating a minimal left and a minimal right ideal of complex dimension two all of whose elements are zero divisors, and the intersection of the cone with the Krein null set is the union of those lines, a cone of real dimension four (The Isotropic Structure of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the Biquaternion Algebra). And the cone is the boundary of the Hermitian level sets: the level sets of the Hermitian form are the real hyperboloids — the hyperboloid model of hyperbolic space — against the complex hypersurfaces of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, and on a Minkowski slice the intersection degenerates to the two nappes of the light cone, whose future nappe is the Hermitian cone (The Krein Level Sets and the Hyperbolic Structure).

The physical interpretation is that the cone is the fixed point of the grid: it survives every change of product as a degeneration locus, it is the exact locus of four of the twelve, and it is carried by exactly two of the four forms. If the framework is an attractor, the cone is the first thing it attracts, because the norm is the one quadratic form every band inherits.

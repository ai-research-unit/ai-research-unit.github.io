# __The General Plain Sesquilinear Product on the Material Sector__

## Introduction

The general plain sesquilinear product is the general plain bilinear product with its second factor read through the
Hermitian conjugation,

$$
\tilde P\star\tilde Q = \tilde P\tilde Q^{*},
$$

the operation of the probability band of the framework. Its scalar part is the Born pairing
$H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})$, positive definite on the whole algebra, and its
algebra, its two halves and its physical reading are the probability band's, owned by *Introduction to the
General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions*, *The Hermitian Form as a Product: Positivity and the Real
Part of the Born Pairing* and *The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing*. This
article asks what becomes of that product on the **material sector** $\mathbb{M}_-$, the four-vectors of
relativistic physics, and it is the material companion of the probability band; the informational
companion is *The General Plain Sesquilinear Product on the Informational Sector*.

The answer begins with one algebraic fact: on the material sector the Hermitian conjugation is the
central scalar $-1$,

$$
\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_- \ \Longrightarrow\ \tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q,
$$

so the sesquilinear product is **minus the general plain bilinear product**,

$$
\tilde P\star\tilde Q = \tilde P\tilde Q^{*} = -\tilde P\tilde Q
\qquad (\tilde P,\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_-).
$$

The second slot's conjugation, which is the whole difference between the probability row and the
composition row, is therefore **invisible inside the material sector as a map** — it is a global sign.
Its content is not lost, it is displaced: the sign turns the composition's scalar form into the
probability's, $H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=-B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$, and since $B$ is negative definite on
$\mathbb{M}_-$ the Born pairing is **positive definite** there. On the material sector the probability
form is the Euclidean length of the four-vector, and the light cone is **not** its cone of
nulls. The cone remains the wall it was, but of the composition and not of the probability: on the
material sector the lightlike four-vectors are the zero divisors of the probability product while no
four-vector is a null vector of the probability form.

**Placement and boundaries.** This article is the material-sector companion of the probability band,
*Focus on the General Plain Sesqualgebra (GPS) of Biquaternions — Probability*, and the mirror of *The
General Plain Bilinear Product on the Material Sector* in the composition band. The product, its two halves and the
form $H$ on the whole algebra are the band's framing articles; the reality of $H$ on the informational
side is *The Hermitian Form as a Product: Positivity and the Real Part of the Born Pairing*, whose
section on the state side is the announcement of the companion article and not of this one; the positivity
of $H$, its cone and its signature are *Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger* and *Positivity and
the Hermitian Cone of the Biquaternion Algebra with Hermitian Adjoint*; the operators, the adjoint and
the worked element are *The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing*; the
invertibility, the units and the zero-divisor cone are *Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain
Sesqualgebra* and *The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra and the Complement of the
Zero-Divisor Cone*; the material sector itself and the interval are *The Anti-Hermitian Subspace
$\mathbb{M}_-$ as the Material Sector* and *The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition*. None of those is
reproved here.

**Conventions.** $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis
$e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ with $e_1e_2=e_3$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^{2}=-1$; a
general element is $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+Q_1e_1+Q_2e_2+Q_3e_3$ with complex coefficients; the scalar part is
$\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)=Q_0$ and the trace is $\mathrm{Tr}=2\,\mathrm{Sc}$. The conjugations are the
complex conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, the natural conjugation ${\natural}$, and the Hermitian conjugation
${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{\natural}$. The material sector is
$\mathbb{M}_-=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q\}$, with the real basis $\{ie_0,e_1,e_2,e_3\}$ and the
coordinates $\tilde T=ict\,e_0+x e_1+y e_2+z e_3$; the informational sector is the Hermitian half,
$\mathbb{M}_+=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=+\tilde Q\}$, and $i\mathbb{M}_-=\mathbb{M}_+$. The four scalar
forms are $B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)$ with Gram
$\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ in the coefficient basis,
$N(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q)=B(\tilde P^{\natural},\tilde Q)$,
$H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})=B(\tilde P,\tilde Q^{*})$ and
$K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*})=B(\tilde P^{\natural},\tilde Q^{*})$.

## The Star on the Material Sector Is a Sign

The material sector is the anti-fixed space of the Hermitian conjugation, and the star is an
anti-automorphism that fixes the central $i$ up to the coefficientwise conjugation:
$\tilde Q^{*}=\overline{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$, and on a material element both conjugations agree in sign,
$\tilde Q^{\natural}=-\overline{\tilde Q}$. The consequence for the second slot of the product is exact:

$$
\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_- \ \Longrightarrow\ \tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\bigl(-\tilde Q\bigr)=-\tilde P\tilde Q .
$$

The star therefore acts on the material sector as multiplication of the second factor by the central
scalar $-1$, and multiplication by $-1$ is an invertible map that changes nothing else. Two statements
follow at once, and they are the spine of the article.

- **The product on the sector is the general plain bilinear product up to the global sign.** The two operations have
  the same multiplication table on $\mathbb{M}_-$ up to the overall $-1$, hence the same closure rule,
  the same zero divisors and the same annihilators; every algebraic property that is invariant under a
  global sign, which is all of them, is shared.
- **The conjugation is invisible on a pair drawn from the sector, and visible in the form.** As a map on
  the slot it is the scalar $-1$; in the scalar form it is the sign that separates $H$ from $B$, since
  $H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=B(\tilde P,\tilde Q^{*})=-B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$.

**Remark (verified).** For $100$ random pairs of material elements, $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde P\tilde Q$
and $H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=-B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$ to machine precision.

**Proposed interpretation, labelled as such.** On the material sector the **insertion of the star is a
choice of sign and not a change of operation**. The passage from the composition row to the probability
row, which on the whole algebra replaces a product by a pairing, on the material sector replaces a
definite **negative** form by its positive opposite. The two rows are then not two products of the
material sector but one product read with two signs, and the sign is what the second slot does there.

## The Product of Two Material Elements

Write a material element in its own coordinates, $\tilde T=ict\,e_0+\vec x$. The product on the sector
is the general plain bilinear one, $-1$ times, so its structure is the structure of *The General Plain Bilinear Product
on the Material Sector* and only its reading changes. For two material elements,

$$
\tilde P\tilde Q = \tfrac12\{\tilde P,\tilde Q\}+\tfrac12[\tilde P,\tilde Q],
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\tilde P\star\tilde Q = -\tfrac12\{\tilde P,\tilde Q\}-\tfrac12[\tilde P,\tilde Q],
$$

with the anticommutator in $\mathbb{M}_+$ and the commutator in $\mathbb{M}_-$: the sector is not closed
under the sesquilinear product either, and the composite is material exactly when the two anticommute. The
**central part** of the sesquilinear composite is the value of the probability form,

$$
\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\star\tilde Q\bigr) = H(\tilde P,\tilde Q) = -B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)
= c^{2}t\,\tau+\vec x\!\cdot\!\vec y > 0 \ \text{ on a nonzero element},
$$

written in the material coordinates $ct,\vec x$ and $c\tau,\vec y$: it is the **Euclidean inner
product** of the two four-vectors, not the Minkowski one. The **vector part** is minus the vector part of
the general plain bilinear composite, and it is the phase half of the band; the two halves of the
sesquilinear composite are the two halves of the general plain bilinear composite with one sign, which is the content of the
identity $\tilde P\circledast\tilde Q=H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)e_0$ of the symmetric half.

**Remark (verified).** On $100$ random material pairs the anticommutator of the composite is fixed by the
dagger and the commutator anti-fixed; the composite is material exactly in the pairs that anticommute, and
$H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$ is positive real in the central part.

## The Probability Form on the Sector: Positivity and the Cone

The Born pairing restricts to the material sector as a **positive definite** form. On the material basis
$\{ie_0,e_1,e_2,e_3\}$ the diagonal entries are $H(ie_0,ie_0)=+1$ and $H(e_k,e_k)=+1$ with all cross
terms zero, so

$$
H\big|_{\mathbb{M}_-} = +\mathrm{I}_4 \ \text{ of signature }(4,0),
\qquad
H\big|_{\mathbb{M}_+} = +\mathrm{I}_4 \ \text{ of signature }(4,0),
\qquad
H\big|_{\mathbb{M}_-} = -B\big|_{\mathbb{M}_-}.
$$

The positivity is therefore **uniform over the two sectors**: an element of either sector, read as a state
or as a displacement, has a strictly positive probability, and the two sectors are distinguished by the
*sign form* $B$ and not by the probability. The probability form has **no null vector at all** — its
isotropic cone is $\{0\}$ — so on the material sector there is no four-vector of zero probability, and in
particular a lightlike four-vector has a positive probability. The three forms on the material basis read

$$
B=-\mathrm{I}_4,\qquad N=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1),\qquad H=+\mathrm{I}_4,\qquad
K=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1),
$$

in the coordinates $(ict,\vec x)$: the sign form is negative definite, the interval Lorentzian of
signature $(3,1)$, the probability Euclidean, and the Krein form of signature $(1,3)$. Four forms on four
directions, and only the interval carries the causal structure.

**Remark (verified).** The four restrictions are the displayed matrices, all cross terms $0$;
$H(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)$ is real and strictly positive on $100$ random material elements.

**Proposed interpretation, labelled as such.** On the material sector the **probability is the
non-relativistic part of the geometry**: it is the Euclidean length of the four-vector, blind to the
Lorentzian structure, and the reason it can be positive while the interval is indefinite. The interval
measures what can influence what; the probability measures the weight of the element, and the two are not
the same measurement. The massless four-vector has every right to a positive weight; what it lacks is a
non-vanishing norm, and that is a statement of the interval and not of the probability.

## Vanishing Products and the Light Cone

Because the sesquilinear product is the general plain bilinear product with one sign, its vanishing products on the
material sector are those of the general plain bilinear product,

$$
\tilde P\star\tilde Q=0 \iff \tilde P\tilde Q=0 \qquad(\tilde P,\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_-),
$$

and the zero divisors and the annihilators of the probability product on the sector are those of the
composition product. The zero divisors are the light cone: an element of $\mathbb{M}_-$ is a zero divisor
exactly when $N(\tilde P)=0$, and then it annihilates its own natural conjugate,

$$
\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=N(\tilde P)\,e_0=0,
$$

the material line the conjugate spans, the shared line of the two annihilator planes of the composition
(*Annihilators in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*). The unit $e_1$, of norm $N(e_1)=+1$, has no nonzero material
annihilator at all.

There are therefore two distinct vanishing statements on the same sector, and confusing them is the trap
of the block:

- the **central half** of the product vanishes on the $H$-orthogonal pairs, $H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=0$, a
  hyperplane: this is the vanishing of the symmetric operation $\tilde P\circledast\tilde Q=H e_0$ and it
  belongs to *The Hermitian Form as a Product: Positivity and the Real Part of the Born Pairing*, not
  here;
- the **product** vanishes on the cone pairs, $\tilde P\tilde Q=0$, and requires $\tilde P$ or $\tilde Q$
  on the light cone.

The two are different: a lightlike element has a positive probability, $H(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)>0$, and is
nevertheless a zero divisor of the product.

**Example (a lightlike element and its annihilator).** The material element $\tilde P=ie_0+e_1$ has
$N(\tilde P,\tilde P)=0$ and $H(\tilde P,\tilde P)=+2$: it is lightlike, on the cone, and has positive
probability. Its material annihilator is the line spanned by $ie_0-e_1$,
$(ie_0+e_1)(ie_0-e_1)=0$, so the probability product kills a nonzero direction with it; and the value of
the product onto that direction is zero while the probability of the element itself is $2$.

**Proposed interpretation, labelled as such.** On the material sector the **light cone is the locus of
the elements the probability product cannot compose**. It is not a locus of zero probability: a
lightlike displacement has a strictly positive weight and is still annihilated by a nonzero displacement.
The wall of the reversible operations stays where the composition put it, and the probability does not
move it — it inherits it, because the second slot's conjugation is only a sign there. The wall has a second
face on the sector: a lightlike four-vector is an imaginary multiple of a pure state,
$\tilde T=2ict\,\tilde\Pi_1(-\hat x)$, so the massless displacement and the informational
projection are one element read through the central $i$ (*The Cone and the Massless Particles in the 12
Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*). Its square is not zero,
$\tilde T^{2}=-2c^{2}t^{2}e_0+2ic^{2}t^{2}\hat x$, and no element of the sector squares to zero at
all: the nilpotents are the pure isotropic elements, outside $\mathbb{M}_-$ except at the origin, nilpotent
of index two under the composition and of index three under the probability product (*Nilpotents in the 12
Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*).

## The Isometries of the Probability: Rotations and Not Boosts

An element is an isometry of the Born pairing when $\tilde A\tilde A^{*}=e_0$, and on the material sector
that equation reads $-\tilde A^{2}=e_0$, that is, in the material coordinates,

$$
-\bigl(ic t\,e_0+\vec x\bigr)^{2}=e_0
\iff \vec x\!\cdot\!\vec x=1 \ \text{and}\ ct=0, \quad\text{or}\quad \vec x=0 \ \text{and}\ ct=\pm1,
$$

whose solutions are the **unit spatial directions** $S^{2}$ together with the two central elements
$\pm ie_0$. The unit spatial directions generate the spatial rotations; the identity and $-e_0$ are the two
central isometries. So on the material sector the isometries of the probability are the **rotations**, and
the boosts are not among them: the material boost

$$
\tilde U = i\cosh u\,e_0+\sinh u\,e_1 \in \mathbb{M}_-
$$

has $N(\tilde U,\tilde U)=-1$, so it is an isometry of the interval, and
$H(\tilde U,\tilde U)=\cosh 2u\neq1$, so it is **not** an isometry of the probability: it moves
the probability norm by the factor $\cosh 2u$. The spin element $e_k$ is an isometry of both,
$N(e_k,e_k)=H(e_k,e_k)=+1$.

**Remark (verified).** $100$ random material elements satisfy $\tilde A\tilde A^{*}=e_0$ only at
$\vec x$ of unit modulus with $t=0$ or at $\pm ie_0$; $\cosh 2u$ is reproduced to $10^{-9}$ by
$H(\tilde U,\tilde U)$ at $u=0.5,1,2$, and $N(e_k,e_k)=H(e_k,e_k)=+1$.

**Proposed interpretation, labelled as such.** On the material sector the **compact part of the Lorentz
group preserves the probability and the non-compact part does not**. A rotation is a reversible operation
for both structures; a boost is reversible for the interval and stretches the probability, so the
probability is not Lorentz invariant on the material sector and the rotations are its symmetry. This is
the sector-level form of the framework's compactness statements: charge is discrete because the compact
directions are the ones that preserve the probability.

## The Interval and the Probability on One Element

The two forms differ by twice the coefficient square of the vector part, and on the material coordinates
the difference is read on the space axes alone:

$$
H(\tilde P,\tilde Q) = -B(\tilde P,\tilde Q) = N(\tilde P,\tilde Q)+2\,\vec x\!\cdot\!\vec y,
\qquad
H(\tilde T,\tilde T)=c^{2}t^{2}+\lvert\vec x\rvert^{2},
\qquad
N(\tilde T,\tilde T)=-c^{2}t^{2}+\lvert\vec x\rvert^{2}.
$$

The two agree on the central time direction up to sign and differ by $2\lvert\vec x\rvert^{2}$ on the
space directions; they agree in magnitude only on the complex time line, where both are $\pm c^{2}t^{2}$.
A massive element has $N<0$ and a large positive probability; a lightlike element has $N=0$ and a positive
probability; a spacelike element has $N>0$ and a probability larger than its norm. The probability
therefore orders the material elements by their Euclidean length and the interval orders them by their
causal type, and the two orders are independent.

**Proposed interpretation, labelled as such.** The second slot's star is the **operator that turns the
interval of an element into its probability**, on the material sector: composing an element with its own
conjugate returns the sum of squares instead of their signed difference. Read as a process, the
sesquilinear pairing is the **definite companion** of the interval, and the mass shell — the level set
$N=-m^{2}c^{2}$ — is not a level set of the probability.

## A Worked Case

Take the material element of the operator article,

$$
\tilde Q = ie_0+2e_1\in\mathbb{M}_-,
$$

and read the four forms and the square on it:

$$
B(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=-5,\qquad N(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=+3,\qquad
H(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=+5,\qquad K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=-3,
$$

$$
\tilde Q^{2}=-5e_0+4ie_1,\qquad
\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q^{2}=5e_0-4ie_1,
\qquad \mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=+5 .
$$

Read row by row, the case displays the whole sector at once. The sector sign $B$ is negative, $-5$, as a
material element must have it; the interval $N$ is positive, $+3$, so the element is spacelike; the
probability $H$ is positive, $+5$, and is the Euclidean length-squared $1^{2}+2^{2}$ of the material
coordinates. The sesquilinear square is the same computation as the operator article's expectation value,
$\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=5e_0-4ie_1$, with central part $+5$ and the vector part $-4ie_1$ left over: the
probability reads the first and the phase the second. And the composition of $\tilde Q$ with itself is
$-5e_0+4ie_1$, the negative of the sesquilinear square as the sign rule requires.

## The Bound

The article states the restriction to the material sector of the sesquilinear product and of its form, and
the boundary of what it must declare is the boundary of the sector.

- **The probability is not the interval.** $H$ is Euclidean and definite on the sector, with no cone and
  no causal content; the interval is $N$, of signature $(3,1)$, and the two differ by
  $2\lvert\vec x\rvert^{2}$.
- **The sector sign is not the probability.** $B=-H$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$: the composition's scalar form is
  the negative of the probability, and it is $B$ and not $H$ that carries the sign of the sector.
- **The probability form has no null but the product has zero divisors.** The empty cone of $H$ and the
  light cone of the product are different sets; the lightlike four-vectors have positive probability and
  are zero divisors, and a lightlike element is annihilated by $\pm ie_0\mp e_1$ in the material line.
- **The probability is not Lorentz invariant.** The boosts preserve $N$ and not $H$; the rotations
  preserve both.
- **The star is not a new operation on the sector.** On $\mathbb{M}_-$ it is the central $-1$, so the
  probability row and the composition row are one product read with two signs; the genuinely new content
  of the star is on the informational side and in the mixed pairs.

**What the framework does not claim.** The interpretation does not identify the probability with a
measured frequency, does not derive the Born rule from the algebra, and does not claim that the positive
probability removes the indefinite metric. The Born rule as a trace formula is *The Born Rule as a Trace
Formula — Derivation and Comparison*; the mixed pairs and the gauge side are the quaternionic sesquilinear
band and are not read here.

## The Ledger

**Proved.** On $\mathbb{M}_-$ the Hermitian conjugation is the central scalar $-1$, hence
$\tilde P\star\tilde Q=-\tilde P\tilde Q$ and $H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=-B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$; the Born
pairing restricts to $+\mathrm{I}_4$ of signature $(4,0)$ on each sector, $B$ to $-\mathrm{I}_4$ on
$\mathbb{M}_-$ and $+\mathrm{I}_4$ on $\mathbb{M}_+$, $N$ to $(3,1)$ and $K$ to $(1,3)$ on
$\mathbb{M}_-$; the product on the sector is material exactly when the two factors anticommute; its
vanishing products are those of the general plain bilinear product, so its zero divisors and annihilators
are the composition product's and are carried by the light cone; the isometries of $H$ inside the sector
are the unit spatial directions and $\pm ie_0$, the boosts failing to preserve $H$; and the worked element
$ie_0+2e_1$ realises $B=-5$, $N=+3$, $H=+5$, $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=5e_0-4ie_1$.

**Interpretations.** That the star is a **sign** on the material sector and a genuine conjugation
elsewhere; that the probability on the sector is the **Euclidean geometry of the four-vector**; that the
light cone is the **wall of the probability product** and not a locus of zero probability; that the
rotations are the isometries of the probability and the boosts are not; and that the star is the
**definite companion of the interval**, turning the signed difference of squares into their sum. Each is
the framework's naming of a proved algebraic fact and is labelled as such.

## Physical Readings

The general plain sesquilinear product reads as the probability structure of the framework, and on the
material sector its reading is the one the composition band already gave with the opposite sign: the
material probability is the Euclidean weight of the four-vector. Three consequences stand out. Read on
one element, the two rows are not two measurements but **one measurement and one metric**: the probability
and the interval give different numbers and neither replaces the other. Read on a pair, the second slot's
conjugation adds only a **central sign**: on $\mathbb{M}_-$ the star is $-\flat$, the real structure whose
fixed space is the sector, so no new operation appears there. And read on the process, the product kills
exactly the lightlike directions, so **the wall between the reversible and the irreversible is the wall of the
probability as well**, inherited from the composition by a global sign. The probability of a lightlike
element is positive and its composability is not: on the material sector the two statements are different,
and the article's whole content is that they must not be collapsed.

## Summary

The material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$ carries the four-vectors, an event being
$\tilde T=ict\,e_0+x e_1+y e_2+z e_3$, and this article reads on it the general plain sesquilinear product
$\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$, whose full theory is the probability band's. On $\mathbb{M}_-$ the Hermitian
conjugation is the central scalar $-1$, so the sesquilinear product is the general plain bilinear product with a global
sign, $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=-\tilde P\tilde Q$: the second slot's conjugation is **invisible on a pair
drawn from the sector as a map and visible in the scalar form**, where it turns the negative definite
sector sign $B$ into the positive definite Born pairing $H$. The pairing restricts to $+\mathrm{I}_4$ of
signature $(4,0)$ on each sector, the Euclidean inner product $c^{2}t\tau+\vec x\!\cdot\!\vec y$ of
the material coordinates; the interval is the Lorentzian $N$, of signature $(3,1)$, and the two differ by
twice $\vec x\!\cdot\!\vec y$. Being positive definite, $H$ has no null vector, so no material
element has zero probability and a lightlike four-vector has positive probability; yet the product on the
sector has the vanishing products of the general plain bilinear product, so its zero divisors and
annihilators are carried by the light cone, and a lightlike element is annihilated by a nonzero material
line. The isometries of the probability inside the sector are the unit spatial directions and $\pm ie_0$; the rotations preserve the
probability and the boosts do not, a boost rescaling it by $\cosh 2u$. On one element the composition
gives the interval and the sesquilinear product gives the Euclidean sum of squares, so the mass shell is not a
level set of the probability. The distinctions are labelled throughout: what is proved is the algebra
restricted to $\mathbb{M}_-$, what is proposed is the naming.

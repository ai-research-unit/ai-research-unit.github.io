# __The Lagrangian Articles by Object: Four Fields, Nine Articles Each__

## What this page is for

The family is easier to use from the object than from the reading. A reader who wants the Proca action in every chart does not want to walk five reading hubs; he wants one list of nine. This page gives the four lists.

**How the nine are built.** Each field carries the element action, the polar action, the action at one momentum mode, the action in the non-quantum limit, the minimal-coupling action in the same four readings, and one fluid. Four plus four plus one is nine, and the count is the same for the four fields because the readings are a closed list.

**What the grouping shows.** Every field's nine make the same moves in the same order. The differences are the field's own: the scalar's conjugate pair, the spinor's first-order structure, the vector's constraint, and the Schrödinger field's second-order-in-space operator. A reader who has read one list of nine can predict the shape of the other three, and the prediction is the family's claim about method.

## What the four lists share, and where they differ

**Share.** The four actions are quadratic, their conjugate pairs are stated, their currents are the Noether currents of one phase symmetry or one translation family, and their counts come from the constraints. None is quantised, and each says so in its first section.

**Differ.** The scalar's action is second order in every variable and its momenta are the field's own derivatives. The spinor's is first order and its momenta are bilinears, so its bracket is the Dirac bracket. The vector's carries a constraint that removes the longitudinal mode. The Schrödinger field's is second order in space and first order in time, which is why its reality condition is weaker than the relativistic three.

**One caution.** The nine of a field are nine readings of one object and not nine objects. A reader who compares, say, the Proca momentum-space article with the Proca polar article is comparing charts, and the invariants must agree across them. When they do not, one of the two articles has an error and not a disagreement.

The Carrier Roster: Three Spins and No Fourth, Fixed Before Any Group Acts

Introduction

The claim. The biquaternion algebra carries exactly three carriers — the centre, the minimal left ideals, and the material sector — and their representation-space dimensions are $1$, $2$ and $3$. That roster is complete, and it is fixed by the algebra alone. No force is named in its derivation, and no group can extend it. A sector may decline to use a carrier, and a sector may fail to reach one; neither can add a fourth.

Why the roster comes before the groups. A reader who meets the carrier list inside a sector — where the sector's own indices are already doing work — has to accept it as a fact about that sector. It is not. It is a fact about $\mathbb{B}$, and it is what makes the phrase this force has no scalar or this force has no spinor a statement with content: the roster says what is available, and the sector's group says what it can accept. Those are two different filters, and the corpus has been running them as one.

What this article does, and what it does not. It states the roster at no rung, states why it is complete, and states the sense in which the completion is a boundary of the algebra rather than a gap in the corpus. It does not classify the particles — that is the determination of the possible spins, which reads the same three carriers as the scalar, the spinor and the vector — and it does not derive the tensor-product closure, which is higher spin from tensor products. The division of labour is deliberate: those two articles sit inside Relativistic Quantum Theory and state the roster at that rung. This one states it as the first filter on any integrand, before any rung exists.

The Three Carriers

The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, and its regular module decomposes into three pieces under the two actions that matter.

carrier subspace dimension spin invariant that fixes it
the centre $\mathbb{B}_C=\mathbb{C}e_t$ $1$ $0$ the norm $N$
the minimal left ideals $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$ pairs $2$ $\tfrac12$ the ideal's own frame
the material sector $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$, scalar-free part $3$ $1$ $B^2$, the bivector's square

The two actions are not the same action, and that is why the count is three and not one. The centre and the traceless part of the material sector are read under conjugation, $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde g\tilde Q\tilde g^{-1}$; the ideals are read under left multiplication, $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tilde g\tilde\Xi$. A carrier is a subspace together with the action that moves it, and the roster is the list of pairs. Counted as spaces alone the algebra is one eight-dimensional thing; counted as carriers it is three.

The dimensions are representation-space dimensions, and they are the numbers a group must match. $1$ for the scalar, $2$ for the spinor, $3$ for the vector. This is the whole content of the sentence the representation is a function of the spin: the carrier's dimension is fixed by the algebra, so the spin is fixed by the carrier, so a field cannot be assigned a spin and then fitted to a representation. The order runs from the algebra outward.

Why There Is No Fourth

Three arguments, and they close on the same boundary.

First, the closure of the tensor products. The corpus's tensor-product article takes the three carriers and forms their products: $\tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12=0\oplus1$, $1\otimes1=0\oplus1\oplus2$, and so on. The products return the three carriers and, beyond them, only the symmetric square of the vector carrier — which is where a spin-$2$ object would live. Nothing else appears, and the products of the composites stay inside the same list. Spin $\tfrac32$ is not in it, and the corpus's own Rarita–Schwinger article states the consequence: the spin-$\tfrac32$ field is written in the literature's notation and named, not derived, because the algebra does not carry it.

Second, the dimension count at the algebra's size. $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ acts on a two-dimensional complex module. The carriers are the pieces of the algebra's action on that module and its conjugate, so the available dimensions are those of the irreducible pieces of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ under the two actions: $1$, $2$ and $3$. A carrier of dimension $4$ would need a module the algebra does not have, and the corpus's own $4=4$ count is a count of the four real dimensions of one ideal read two ways, not a fourth carrier.

Third, the boundary is the algebra's and not the corpus's. The distinction matters for how the absence is reported. The corpus does not lack a spin-$\tfrac32$ article because nobody wrote one; it lacks a spin-$\tfrac32$ carrier, and the article it does have says so. That is the difference between a ceiling of reach and a ceiling of the object — the same distinction the sector hub draws between the sectors that differ by what the algebra carries and the one that differs by what the method reaches.

One caution about the word no fourth. The absence is of a fundamental carrier. A spin-$2$ object exists as the symmetric square of the vector carrier, and that is where the corpus's linearised graviton sits: a composite, written on the frame and not on an element. A composite is not a carrier, and the roster is a list of carriers. The graviton's presence in the corpus is therefore not a counter-example to the count; it is the count being used correctly.

Why the Roster Is the First Filter

Because it is the only filter that can be applied before a group is chosen. Three consequences, and each is a sentence the sectors currently write for themselves.

  • A sector cannot carry what the algebra does not have. No sector can be a spin-$\tfrac32$ theory, whatever its group. That is a stronger statement than any group-theoretic obstruction, and it is what makes the corpus's use of a named Rarita–Schwinger action consistent rather than lazy.
  • Every sector draws from the same three, and the differences between sectors are in the acceptance and not the roster. The primordial, strong, electroweak and electromagnetic sectors all have access to $1$, $2$ and $3$. Where they differ is which of the three they can put a field on — the next article.
  • The roster is why the phrase fixed by the spin is not circular. The representation is a function of the spin because the carrier is fixed first, and the spin is read off the carrier's dimension. A field does not have a spin and then a representation; it has a carrier, and both follow.

The order of the two filters is not a matter of exposition. If the roster is applied first, the question which spins can this force carry has a finite answer set — three — and the sector's answer is a subset. If the group is applied first, the question has no bound and a sector can look as though it might carry anything. The bound is what makes the sector table in what a group can accept a table and not a list.

What the Roster Does Not Decide

It fixes availability and nothing else. Four statements it does not make.

  • It does not fix which of the three a given force uses. That is the group's business, and a group may fail to accept a carrier the algebra offers — the electromagnetic sector's roster is spin $1$ alone, and the reason is its group and not the roster.
  • It does not fix the action. A carrier of dimension $3$ can be a Proca field, a Maxwell field or a connection; the roster says vector, not which vector action.
  • It does not fix a mass. Whether a carrier's field is massive is a question about which terms are admitted, and the admitting is done by invariance.
  • It does not fix a coupling. A carrier is a carrier whether or not it is gauged, and the free actions of the relativistic quantum theory are the roster used without a group.

What it does fix, and this is the article's one claim about the corpus's structure. The roster is the reason the spin bands of the sector categories can exist as bands at all. A band is a slot for a carrier, and there are three of them because there are three carriers — which is why the sectors' menus run spin $1$, spin $\tfrac12$, spin $0$ and then stop, and why the band for other spins is a band with a content article, an equation article and a named action, and no derived density.

Summary

The biquaternion algebra carries exactly three carriers: the centre at dimension $1$ and spin $0$, the minimal left ideals at dimension $2$ and spin $\tfrac12$, and the material sector at dimension $3$ and spin $1$. The roster is fixed by the algebra before any force is named, it is complete, and no group can extend it.

The completion is argued three ways: the tensor products close on the three plus the symmetric square of the vector; the dimension count at $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ offers no fourth irreducible piece; and the boundary is the object's and not the method's — the corpus has a named Rarita–Schwinger action because there is no spin-$\tfrac32$ carrier, not because no one derived one.

The absence is of a fundamental carrier. A composite such as the symmetric square is not a carrier, which is why the linearised graviton does not violate the count.

The roster is the first filter on an integrand because it is the only one that can be applied before a group is chosen. It fixes availability, not use, not action, not mass and not coupling; and it is the reason the sector menus are bands with three filled slots and a named-only fourth.

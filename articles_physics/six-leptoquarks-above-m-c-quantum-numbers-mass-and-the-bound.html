Six Leptoquarks Above $M_C$: Quantum Numbers, the Mass Ratio, and the Bound From $K_L\to\mu e$

Introduction

The layer. The sextet is the off-diagonal block of the fifteen, so this article is a statement about the complement of a stabiliser in $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ — and about what a heavy non-singlet vector does to the experiments that could see it.

The question. What becomes of the six when the primordial force breaks, how heavy are they, and what forbids them from being the weak bosons?

The answer.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathfrak m=\mathbf3_{+4/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3},\qquad Q=\pm\tfrac23,\qquad M_X^{2}=16\,g_4^{2}\,v_C^{2} ; \;} $$

$$ \boxed{\; M_X\gtrsim4.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}=2.5\times10^{2}\ \mathrm{TeV} \quad\text{from }K_L\to\mu e,\qquad \text{proton decay is dimension nine} ; \;} $$

So the six are not destroyed by the vacuum restructuring — they are made heavy by it, and heavy in a way that a flavour-changing decay forbids them to escape. The phrase integrated out is precise: absent from the asymptotic spectrum, present in the theory, and bounded from below.

And before the restructuring the six are massless. This is stated in the introduction because it is the fact a reader is most likely to get wrong, and it is not a convention or an approximation: above $M_C$ there is no condensate, so the sextet's mass is exactly zero, and an unbroken local symmetry keeps it zero to every order in perturbation theory. The mass $M_X$ below is created by the breaking and does not exist before it, so "the leptoquarks are heavy" is a statement about the phase this article is about, not about $\mathrm{SU}(4)$.

What this article establishes. The sextet's quantum numbers and its fate; its mass as a function of the one vacuum it eats; the bound that fixes the scale of the restructuring at two hundred and fifty teraelectronvolts; why proton decay does not bite although $B$ is violated in thirds; and the precise sense in which a state can be integrated out without being removed.

Not established. The bound is a coupling statement and its coefficient is uncertain by a factor of a few. The states have not been seen, which is the weakest kind of failure and the most persistent. And — the point of the article — nothing here produces a weak boson. The six cannot: the charge theorem of the companion article rules it out, and this article records that the sextet's role is to break and to be heavy, not to give birth.

Where the rest is. The restructuring that eats them is The Restructuring of the Primordial Force. Their mass relation and their bound in full are §The Mass, and the Ratio That Comes With It and §The Bound: Two Hundred and Fifty Teraelectronvolts below. Their place in the ledger is Classification of the Bosons. What the restructuring passes down instead of them is The Matching at $M_C$.

What "Integrated Out" Means, in Three Registers

The phrase is not a metaphor, and it is worth separating what it rules out from what it asserts.

register statement wrong reading it excludes
spectral the six are not asymptotic states below $M_C$ they are still in the spectrum
dynamical the six are longitudinal modes of six massive vectors they are absorbed and gone
effective the six can return as virtual quanta in propagators they are unphysical and can be dropped from every calculation

The three are one statement. A field with mass $M_X$ is absent from the spectrum at energies far below $M_X$ and present in every Green's function; its exchange produces local operators of dimension six and higher, suppressed by powers of $M_X$. That is what "integrated out" means, and it is why the sextet can be heavy without being forgotten.

The technical name is integrating out, and it is worth stating the formula once so the later articles can refer to it:

$$ \mathcal L_{\text{eff}}\ \supset\ \frac{c}{M_X^{2}}\,\mathcal O^{(6)}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \frac{\delta m^{2}}{m^{2}}\ \sim\ \left(\frac{M}{M_X}\right)^{2}. $$

The suppression is severe and it is the reason the sextet leaves no trace at low energy. With $M_X\simeq250$ TeV and $M\sim M_W$,

$$ \left(\frac{M_W}{M_X}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{80.4}{2.5\times10^{5}}\right)^{2}=1.0\times10^{-7}. $$

Seven orders below an order-one effect. Nothing downstream can be built out of this residue, and that is a fact to keep rather than to overcome.

The Six, and Their Quantum Numbers

One block of the fifteen. The decomposition of the adjoint under the stabiliser of the lepton line is

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)=\underbrace{\mathfrak{su}(3)}_{\mathbf 8_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathbb R\,iD}_{\mathbf 1_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak m}_{\mathbf 3_{4/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3}}, \qquad 15=8+1+6 . $$

The $B-L$ values are forced by the index structure. A leptoquark plane carries one lepton index and one spatial index; the lepton has $B-L=-1$ and a spatial line has $B-L=+\tfrac13$, so the difference is

$$ B-L=\pm\left(1+\tfrac13\right)=\pm\tfrac43 , $$

and the charge follows from the normalisation $Q=\tfrac12(B-L)$ on these directions:

$$ Q=\pm\tfrac23 . $$

So the sextet is three states of charge $+\tfrac23$ (triplet) and three of charge $-\tfrac23$ (antitriplet). No member is neutral, no member is integrally charged, and no member is a singlet. Every one of those three facts will be used in the charge theorem.

What they do to matter. A leptoquark converts one quark into one lepton, so $\Delta B=\pm\tfrac13$ per fermion leg, and $B-L$ is conserved because the $X$ carries $\pm\tfrac43$ itself. The vertex is the model's signature and its liability at once: it is what a search looks for, and it is what a flavour-changing decay bounds.

The Mass, and the Ratio That Comes With It

From the vacuum. The restructuring is done by a scalar acquiring a value along its unit-line component, and the six eat six real components of it. The mass is one half, one coupling squared, one vacuum squared:

$$ M_X^{2}=16\,g_4^{2}\,v_C^{2}. $$

One mass, one vacuum, one coupling. No second particle sits between the condensate and the sextet: the bound on $M_X$ is a bound on $M_C$, and nothing else enters.

Three things the relation is not. It is not a group identity — a $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with a vacuum expectation value splits the six among themselves. It is not exact — $g_4/g_R$ is a free ratio. And it is not a prediction of a number: it converts one unknown into another and then hands the pair to experiment.

The Bound: Two Hundred and Fifty Teraelectronvolts

The process. $K_L\to\mu e$ is mediated at tree level by a leptoquark, with no loop suppression and no small mixing angle in the leading term. That is the sharpest statement the sextet makes about itself, and it is a bound rather than a signal because the decay has not been seen.

The number, in the corpus's units and in the reader's.

quantity value
bound on $M_X$, order-one generation matrix $3.69\times10^{-5}$ J
rounded value used throughout the corpus $4.0\times10^{-5}$ J
in gigaelectronvolts $2.5\times10^{5}$ GeV
in teraelectronvolts $2.5\times10^{2}$ TeV

So the restructuring scale is not free and not low. Whatever else the model does, the primordial force cannot break below two hundred and fifty teraelectronvolts, because a lighter sextet would have been seen in a kaon decay.

Why the bound is a statement about a coupling as much as about a mass. The rate scales as $g_{\text{eff}}^{4}/M_X^{4}$, where $g_{\text{eff}}$ collects the four external mixings. A smaller mixing buys a smaller mass, and the corpus is explicit that the coefficient is uncertain by a factor of a few. The honest reading is $M_X\gtrsim g_{\text{eff}}\times250$ TeV, with $g_{\text{eff}}$ of order one for a generic generation structure.

Why Proton Decay Does Not Bite

The temptation. A leptoquark converts quarks into leptons, so three of them destroy a proton. If the operator were dimension six, the bound would be a thousand times stronger than the flavour bound and the model would be dead.

The counting. Each vertex converts one quark into one lepton, so $\Delta B=\pm\tfrac13$; a proton has $B=1$ and $e^{+}\pi^{0}$ has $B=0$; destroying the proton therefore needs three vertices, and three vertices make a dimension-nine operator:

$$ \mathcal O_9\sim\frac{1}{M_X^{5}}\,q^{3}\ell^{3}, \qquad \Gamma\propto\frac{m_p^{11}}{M_X^{10}} . $$

Six extra powers on the rate, and they are the whole of the model's survival. The two-body mode $p\to e^{+}\pi^{0}$ has no connection contribution at any order, because its final state is two quarks and a lepton while the sector supplies one quark-to-lepton conversion at a time. The channel to look for is $p\to e^{+}e^{+}e^{-}$, and it is far below reach.

The one field that would change it. An antitriplet coupling to two quarks — a diquark — gives dimension six from a single exchange. The fifteen does not contain one: its triplet carries $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$ and a $qq$ pair carries $+\tfrac23$, so the coupling violates the local $B-L$. A $(\mathbf{6},\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ scalar would supply it, and the frame has none. Add one and the bound becomes

$$ M_\Sigma\gtrsim1.60\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{J}=10^{15}\ \mathrm{GeV}=10^{12}\ \mathrm{TeV}, $$

four orders above the flavour bound and enough to make the sextet unseeable even in principle.

So the fork is decided by the multiplet list and not by a symmetry, and the article that owns the derivation says the same: the model is testable only while no sextet scalar is added.

Why They Are Not Seen

Two reasons, and they are independent.

The scale. A non-singlet state at $250$ TeV is beyond any collider. Pair production of non-singlet vectors falls with the square of the mass at fixed energy; the reach of a hadron collider for such states is a few teraelectronvolts, two orders below the flavour bound.

The quantum numbers. Even at reach, the signature is unusual: a charge-$\tfrac23$ triplet decaying to a quark and a lepton, with no missing energy and no resonance in a standard channel. The searches exist and are quoted in the companion article; the conclusion is a null result that constrains nothing beyond what $K_L\to\mu e$ already constrains.

The honest position, and it is the corpus's own. Six states that should exist and have not been seen is the weakest possible form of failure — nothing is contradicted — and also the most persistent, because it cannot be repaired by a better calculation, only by moving the scale. A model whose one sharp prediction is a bound rather than a number has not been tested; it has been constrained.

What the Sextet Does Not Do

This section exists because the natural next thought is wrong, and it is worth closing before it is opened.

The temptation. Six redundant directions break; the weak pair is six redundant directions; perhaps the breaking is a recombination. The dimensions match, and nothing else does.

The charge. Every member has $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, so any pair sums to $\{0,\pm\tfrac43\}$ and never to $\pm1$. The explicit channel confirms it,

$$ \mathbf3_{+4/3}\otimes\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3}=\mathbf1_0\oplus\mathbf8_0, $$

where the singlet is neutral identically — three spin states, all $Q=0$, no charged vector at all. Six neutral states is the opposite of four charged and two neutral.

The mass. A bound state weighs at least its confinement scale, and the confinement scale here is the restructuring scale, $250$ TeV. An $80$ GeV composite cannot be built from constituents a thousand times heavier.

So the sextet's role in the story is exactly two things: it is what the restructuring eats, and it is what the flavour bound measures. It is not the parent of the weak force. That role belongs to the index of the object that did the eating, and it is the subject of the next article.

Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

"Integrated out" is not "destroyed". The six are absent from the spectrum and present in the propagators; a state that is dropped from every calculation is a different claim and a false one (What "Integrated Out" Means).

Heavy is not gone. The restructuring converts six massless directions into six vectors at $32v^{2}$; nothing leaves the theory (The Mass).

The flavour bound is not a measurement. It is a floor on the scale, with a coefficient uncertain by a factor of a few (The Bound).

Proton decay does not bind the sector. It binds it only if a diquark is added, and the fifteen has none; the leading connection operator is dimension nine (Why Proton Decay Does Not Bite).

Matching dimensions is not matching physics. Six for six is a coincidence of dimension and a failure of charge (What the Sextet Does Not Do).

Summary

Identity. $\mathfrak m=\mathbf3_{+4/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3}$: six states, $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, triplet or antitriplet, $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$. Forced by the local fourth line, and the only non-singlet massive vectors in the theory — massive below $M_C$.

Fate. Massless above $M_C$, eaten at the restructuring, heavy below it. Two registers of one event, not two claims.

Mass. $M_X^{2}=16g_4^{2}v_C^{2}$ with $Ξ_0=v_CD$; the $16$ is $(\Delta d)^{2}=(1-(-3))^{2}$ and not a fit, and there is no second heavy mass in the minimal content to compare it with.

Bound. $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level gives $M_X\gtrsim250$ TeV; proton decay is dimension nine and does not bite, unless a $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ is added, which would push the bound to $10^{12}$ TeV and make the sextet unseeable.

Verdict. Constrained, not tested. And not the parent of the weak force: six charged directions cannot make four charged and two neutral, and $250$ TeV constituents cannot make an $80$ GeV composite.

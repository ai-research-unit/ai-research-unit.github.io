The Differential Operators, the Products, the Forms and the D'Alembertian in Biquaternionic Form

Introduction

The series fixes its derivative conventions once, in §Conventions for Derivation of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe, and this article walks that section in its own order: the subscripts, the prime, the two derivative operators, and the four general products read on the gradient, each with its product, its symmetric and antisymmetric parts, its form and its norm. Nothing is proved here. Every statement restates the convention or an article that owns it, and every owner is cited where it is used.

The analysis is that of the mathematics The Differential Operators, the Products, the Forms and the D'Alembertian and Biquaternion Analysis, §The Differential Operators; the four general products are The Four General Products of the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space, among the twelve operations of The 12 Products of the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space; their four scalar parts are The 4 Forms over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space; and the four norms are The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space. On the physics side the plain product and its form are read in The Products, Their Forms and Their Norms, the four forms and their algebraic norms in The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars, and the d'Alembertian is read in The Biquaternion D'Alembertian and Its Green's Functions.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, $e_t=1$, $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$ and $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$, and central imaginary $i$. An element is $\tilde F=F_te_t+\vec F$ with $\vec F=F_x\vec e_x+F_y\vec e_y+F_z\vec e_z$, its four coefficients complex; the field is element-valued and depends on the coordinates $Q_t,Q_x,Q_y,Q_z$. The conjugations are the natural one $\tilde F^{\natural}$, the coefficientwise one $\bar{\tilde F}$ and the star $\tilde F^{*}=\bar{\tilde F}^{\natural}$; the scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}$; and the sign vector is $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$. All conventions are those of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

Conventions for Derivation

In this work, subscripts denote vector components, not partial derivatives as in the PDE convention; every partial derivative is written explicitly with $\partial$ followed by its variable, and no subscript is ever attached to the $\partial$. A subscript is therefore a component index, as in $v_x$ for the component of a velocity along an axis, or a label, as in $\tilde U_{\mathrm r}$; it never denotes a derivative. The derivative with respect to a coordinate is written $\partial/\partial Q_\mu$, and in a fixed direction $\partial/\partial Q_t$, $\partial/\partial Q_x$, $\partial/\partial Q_y$ or $\partial/\partial Q_z$; a coordinate is never left under the $\partial$, so none of the abbreviated spellings of a partial-differential-equations text occurs, and the rule admits no exception, neither on a free index nor on any other. A derivative with respect to a variable that is not a coordinate is written the same way, its variable named in full: the Lagrangian derivative puts the function in the numerator, $\partial L/\partial u$, so that where a PDE text writes $L_u$ and $L_p$ the series writes $\partial L/\partial u$ and $\partial L/\partial p$; the second derivative has the same shape, $\partial^2 L/\partial u^2$. The function is named in the numerator whenever it is a letter of its own, as for a Lagrangian, a Hamiltonian or a generating function; the slashed form $\partial/\partial u$ is kept where the symbol is an operator or where the operand is a compound expression rather than a single letter. The time derivative $\partial/\partial Q_t$ is the imaginary-time derivative: on the material sector, where $Q_t=ict$, it equals $-\frac{i}{c}\,\partial/\partial t$, and $t$ is not one of the coordinates, so the real-time partial derivative is written out as $\partial/\partial t$. The rule holds on every object of the series, whether the letter names an element of the algebra, an element-valued field, a kernel or an operator.

These studies do not use the covariant index conventions: subscripts are not coordinate indices or derivatives, partial derivatives are written with explicit operators, and the structural information is carried by the algebra of the objects themselves.

The derivative marks. No derivative is written with a dot. The total derivative is written $\frac{d}{dt}$ or $\frac{d}{d\tau}$ with the parameter named; the partial derivative is written $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$ or, inline, $\partial/\partial t$. A higher derivative is $\frac{d^2}{dt^2}$. The prime is reserved for the derivative with respect to the single argument of a function of one variable, and a second derivative is written with $\partial$, never with a repeated subscript. The scope mark of geometric calculus is an underline, not a dot. These rules are fixed in Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe, §The Derivative Marks.

The two derivative operators of the series are the gradient $\nabla$ and the d'Alembertian $\Box$; every other partial derivative is written with $\partial$.

The Gradient

The two derivative operators of the series carry a glyph of their own and are fixed here and in no other place; every other partial derivative is written with $\partial$.

The coordinate derivative is written $\partial/\partial Q_\mu$, and the biquaternionic gradient is the element-valued operator

$$ \tilde\nabla=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}e_\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu}=e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}+\vec e_x\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_x}+\vec e_y\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_y}+\vec e_z\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_z}, $$

whose tilde marks its scalar part beside its vector part, with its quaternion conjugate, the involution of the norm,

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu e_\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu} =e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}-\vec e_x\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_x}-\vec e_y\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_y}-\vec e_z\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_z}, \qquad \varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1). $$

It is this conjugate and no other that the series composes with (Biquaternion Analysis, §The Biquaternionic Gradient, §The Quaternion Conjugate of the Gradient). Write $\nabla=\sum_k\vec e_k\,\partial/\partial Q_k$ for the spatial gradient, with divergence $\nabla\cdot\vec F$ and curl $\nabla\times\vec F$, and $\Delta_Q=\partial^2/\partial Q_x^2+\partial^2/\partial Q_y^2+\partial^2/\partial Q_z^2$ for the Laplacian in the three vector coordinates.

The conjugates of the gradient. The coefficients of $\tilde\nabla$ are the coordinate derivatives $\partial/\partial Q_\mu$, and such a derivative is real, so conjugation leaves each coefficient as it stands and only the units are affected. The quaternion conjugation reverses the three spatial units, and so does the Hermitian conjugation, so the two act on the gradient alike,

$$ \tilde\nabla^{*}=\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu e_\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu}, \qquad \overline{\tilde\nabla}=\tilde\nabla . $$

The forms stay four, the norms are two. The four forms of §The Twelve Products and Their Names remain four distinct conventions here, because each conjugates its own slot: the quaternionic form conjugates the first argument and the Hermitian form the second, so on a general second argument they are not the same statement. But read at the two equal arguments they give two operators, the Hermitian norm coinciding with the quaternionic bilinear norm, $\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural}$, and the Krein norm with the plain bilinear norm, $\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle$. The four subsections below carry the four forms, and their norms are the two operators $\Box$ and $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2-\Delta_Q$.

On the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$, where $Q_t=ict$ and $Q_k=q_k$, the time derivative reads $\partial/\partial Q_t=-\frac{i}{c}\,\partial/\partial t$, the Laplacian is the physical $\Delta$, and the gradient is

$$ \tilde\nabla\big|_{\mathbb{M}_-}=-\frac{i}{c}\,e_t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\sum_k\vec e_k\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_k}. $$

The Plain Bilinear Product, Its Form and Its Norm

The product. The general plain bilinear product is one of the four of §The Twelve Products and Their Names. On the gradient, with an element-valued field $\tilde F=F_te_t+\vec F$, it is read with $\mathrm{Sc}(e_\mu e_\nu)=\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\vec e_j \vec e_k=-\delta_{jk}e_t+\epsilon_{jkl}\vec e_l$, so that the three spatial units carry the field back onto the scalar unit,

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde F=\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t-\nabla\cdot\vec F\Bigr)e_t+\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\vec F+\nabla F_t+\nabla\times\vec F . $$

A product is algebra-valued, and this one holds the divergence, the gradient and the curl of the field in a single object; the equation $\tilde\nabla\tilde F=-\tilde R$ is the series' Maxwell equation. On the material sector the same product is read with $\partial/\partial Q_t=-\frac{i}{c}\,\partial/\partial t$, the space part being unchanged.

The symmetric and antisymmetric parts. Read with two arguments, the product splits as in §The Twelve Products and Their Names. The half-sum keeps the form as its scalar part,

$$ \tilde\nabla\bullet\tilde F=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde F+\tilde F\tilde\nabla\bigr)=\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t-(\nabla,\vec F)\Bigr]e_t+\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\vec F+F_t\nabla=\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t-\nabla\cdot\vec F\Bigr]e_t+\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\vec F+\nabla F_t , $$

and the half-difference is the curl,

$$ \tilde\nabla\wedge\tilde F=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde F-\tilde F\tilde\nabla\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(\nabla\times\vec F+\nabla\times\vec F\bigr)=\nabla\times\vec F , $$

a pure vector, so the plain form carries the whole scalar content of the product. Throughout these four subsections the pair $(\nabla,\vec X)$ is read as the divergence $\nabla\cdot\vec X$ and $X\nabla$ as the gradient $\nabla X$, the derivative acting on the coefficient it multiplies, and the coordinate formulas substituted are those of §The Twelve Products and Their Names.

The form. The plain form is the scalar part of the product read in two arguments,

$$ \langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde F\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu}F_\mu=\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t-\nabla\cdot\vec F , $$

a scalar, the contraction of the derivative with the field in the sign vector $\varepsilon$. On the material sector it is $-\frac{i}{c}\frac{\partial F_t}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot\vec F$.

The norm. The norm is defined with the form, as that form at its two equal arguments:

$$ \langle\tilde\nabla\rangle=\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde\nabla\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2}=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_t^2}-\Delta_Q , $$

the cross terms cancelling. On the material sector it is $-c^{-2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}-\Delta$, the negative of the wave operator; it is not the series' d'Alembertian.

The Quaternionic Bilinear Product, Its Form and Its Norm

The product. The general quaternionic bilinear product is the second of the four of §The Twelve Products and Their Names; on the gradient it is $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F$, the conjugate derivative, read with the same rules, the three spatial units now carrying the field back onto the scalar unit with the opposite sign,

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F=\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t+\nabla\cdot\vec F\Bigr)e_t+\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\vec F-\nabla F_t-\nabla\times\vec F . $$

On the material sector the same substitution $\partial/\partial Q_t=-\frac{i}{c}\,\partial/\partial t$ applies.

The symmetric and antisymmetric parts. The half-sum is central and is the quaternionic form itself,

$$ \tilde\nabla\bullet_{\natural}\tilde F=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F+\tilde F^{\natural}\tilde\nabla\bigr)=\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t+(\nabla,\vec F)\Bigr]e_t=\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t+\nabla\cdot\vec F\Bigr)e_t , $$

and the half-difference is the vector part of the product,

$$ \tilde\nabla\wedge_{\natural}\tilde F=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F-\tilde F^{\natural}\tilde\nabla\bigr)=\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\vec F-F_t\nabla-\nabla\times\vec F=\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\vec F-\nabla F_t-\nabla\times\vec F . $$

The form. The quaternionic form is the scalar part of that product read in two arguments,

$$ \langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu}F_\mu=\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}F_t+\nabla\cdot\vec F , $$

a scalar, the contraction of the derivative with the field in the $ict$ metric. It differs from the plain form by the three signs of $\varepsilon$, which are computed from the product $\mathrm{Sc}(e_\mu e_\nu)=\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}$ and not assumed. On the material sector it is $-\frac{i}{c}\frac{\partial F_t}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\vec F$.

The norm, and the definition of the d'Alembertian. The norm is defined with the form, as that form at its two equal arguments, and the two orders of the product agree:

$$ \Box=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural} =\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla\bigr)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\bigr) =\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2}=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_t^2}+\Delta_Q . $$

This is the definition of the d'Alembertian: the quaternionic norm of the gradient, an operator on the whole algebra, one and the same before any sector is named (The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars). The coordinates $Q_\mu$ are those of the algebra and are complex in general; the operator is $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2+\Delta_Q$ whatever the sector, and it is scalar: it multiplies by $e_t$ and acts coefficient by coefficient, $\Box\tilde{F}=\sum_\mu(\Box F_\mu)e_\mu$. It is the identity above, and not the $ict$ substitution, that fixes it, and no article of the series defines a second d'Alembertian; no mass-term sign is left to a local convention.

The special case of the material sector. On $\mathbb{M}_-$ the coordinates are the material ones, $Q_t=ict$ and $Q_k=q_k$, and the $ict$ substitution flips the sign of the time second derivative, $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2=-c^{-2}\,\partial^2/\partial t^2$, so the same operator reads

$$ \Box\big|_{\mathbb{M}_-}=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_t^2}+\Delta=\Delta-c^{-2}\,\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}, $$

a plus on the spatial part and a minus on the time part. On $\mathbb{M}_-$, where the series does its work, this reading is the series convention, used by the Klein–Gordon article, the Dirac article, the dedicated article The Biquaternion D'Alembertian and Its Green's Functions, and the great majority of the series. The $ict$ substitution is a statement about the material sector, not about the operator, which in every case is $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2+\Delta_Q$; its signature is not a property of the operator but of the slice it is read on, since on a named subspace some of the $Q_\mu$ are purely imaginary. The operator is one, and every article writes it the same way.

The Hermitian Product, Its Form and Its Norm

The product. The general plain sesquilinear product is the third of the four of §The Twelve Products and Their Names; it is the plain product applied to the conjugated field $\tilde F^{*}=\bar F_te_t-\overline{\vec F}$, so that on the gradient it is $\tilde\nabla\tilde F^{*}$, conjugate-linear in the field while the first slot stays $\mathbb{C}$-linear,

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde F^{*}=\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t+\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}\Bigr)e_t-\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\overline{\vec F}+\nabla\bar F_t-\nabla\times\overline{\vec F} . $$

The symmetric and antisymmetric parts. Under the conjugate transpose the half-sum is central and is the Hermitian form itself,

$$ \tilde\nabla\bullet_{*}\tilde F=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde F^{*}\bigr)=\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t+(\nabla,\overline{\vec F})\Bigr]e_t=\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t+\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}\Bigr)e_t , $$

and the half-difference is the vector part,

$$ \tilde\nabla\wedge_{*}\tilde F=\mathrm{Vect}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde F^{*}\bigr)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\overline{\vec F}+\bar F_t\nabla-\nabla\times\overline{\vec F}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\overline{\vec F}+\nabla\bar F_t-\nabla\times\overline{\vec F} . $$

The form. The Hermitian form is the scalar part of that product read in two arguments,

$$ \langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde F^{*}\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu}\bar F_\mu=\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t+\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F} , $$

a scalar, the contraction of the derivative with the conjugated field in the sign vector $\varepsilon$. Taken on the scalar field it gives the scalar part of the product of the two conjugated gradients,

$$ \mathrm{Sc}\bigl[(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Phi^{*})(\tilde\nabla\tilde\Phi)\bigr]=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\frac{\partial\Phi^{*}}{\partial Q_\mu}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial Q_\mu} , $$

in which the conjugate stands on the first factor and no absolute value is taken; the two time derivatives are imaginary on the material sector, so that scalar part is $-c^{-2}\lvert\partial\phi/\partial t\rvert^2+\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert^2$. Its physical reading is the kinetic density, and it is written in §Physical Interpretation below. On the material sector the form itself is $-\frac{i}{c}\frac{\partial\bar F_t}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}$.

The norm. The norm is defined with the form, as that form at its two equal arguments. By §The Gradient, $\tilde\nabla^{*}=\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, and

$$ \langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde\nabla\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{*}\bigr)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2}=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_t^2}+\Delta_Q=\Box , $$

so the Hermitian norm of the gradient is the d'Alembertian, the same operator as its quaternionic bilinear norm. The Hermitian form is the one positive-definite form of the four, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^2$ on the elements, and its square root is the topological norm $\lVert\tilde Q\rVert_E$; positivity is a property of the elements, the norm of this subsection being the operator $\Box$. On the material sector it is the same operator, $\Box|_{\mathbb{M}_-}=\Delta-c^{-2}\,\partial^2/\partial t^2$.

The Krein Product, Its Form and Its Norm

The product. The general quaternionic sesquilinear product is the fourth of the four of §The Twelve Products and Their Names; it is the conjugate derivative applied to the conjugated field, $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F^{*}$, the companion of the quaternionic bilinear product in the second slot,

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F^{*}=\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t-\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}\Bigr)e_t-\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\overline{\vec F}-\nabla\bar F_t+\nabla\times\overline{\vec F} . $$

The symmetric and antisymmetric parts. Under the conjugate transpose the half-sum is

$$ \tilde\nabla\bullet_{\natural*}\tilde F=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F^{*}+\tilde F^{*}\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\bigr)=\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t-(\nabla,\overline{\vec F})\Bigr]e_t-\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\overline{\vec F}-\bar F_t\nabla=\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t-\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}\Bigr]e_t-\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\overline{\vec F}-\nabla\bar F_t , $$

whose scalar part is the Krein form, and the half-difference is the curl of the conjugated field,

$$ \tilde\nabla\wedge_{\natural*}\tilde F=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F^{*}-\tilde F^{*}\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(\nabla\times\overline{\vec F}+\nabla\times\overline{\vec F}\bigr)=\nabla\times\overline{\vec F} , $$

a pure vector.

The form. The Krein form is the scalar part of that product read in two arguments,

$$ \langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde F^{*}\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu}\bar F_\mu=\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_t}\bar F_t-\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F} , $$

a scalar, the contraction of the conjugate derivative with the conjugated field in the $ict$ metric. On the material sector it is $-\frac{i}{c}\frac{\partial\bar F_t}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}$.

The norm. The norm is defined with the form, as that form at its two equal arguments; on the gradient, since $\tilde\nabla^{*}=\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$ (§The Gradient),

$$ \langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla^{*}\bigr)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_\mu^2}=\frac{\partial^2}{\partial Q_t^2}-\Delta_Q , $$

so the Krein norm of the gradient coincides with the plain bilinear norm. It is the indefinite partner of the Hermitian form, and on the elements it is the form of signature $(1,3)$ whose diagonal is $\lvert Q_t\rvert^2-\lvert Q_x\rvert^2-\lvert Q_y\rvert^2-\lvert Q_z\rvert^2$: Hermitian, and negative on a nonzero element, so it carries no positivity. It is the form of the indefinite theory — the Krein space — and not of the contractions of these subsections. On the material sector it is $-c^{-2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}-\Delta$.

A contraction is a form, never an index. The metric of the series is the sign vector $\varepsilon$ inside the product, so no index is raised and none is lowered. Where a text with covariant indices contracts a derivative with a field through a raised and a lowered index, the series writes the quaternionic form $\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle_{\natural}$; where it contracts two derivatives, the series writes the quaternionic norm $\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural}=\Box$; and where it moves an index through a metric, the series writes the element $\tilde F$ itself, the metric being the sign vector inside the product.

Physical Interpretation

The plain product is the one-sided derivative. $\tilde\nabla\tilde F$ holds the divergence, the gradient and the curl of the field in a single biquaternion, and the equation $\tilde\nabla\tilde F=-\tilde R$ is the Maxwell equation of the series, the source $\tilde R$ read beside the field (Maxwell's Equations in Biquaternionic Form). The half-sum of the two arguments is the symmetric part, whose scalar content is the plain form, and the half-difference is the curl alone.

The Hermitian form is the kinetic density. Read on a scalar field $\tilde\Phi=\Phi e_t$, it is the scalar part of the product of the two conjugated gradients,

$$ \mathrm{Sc}\bigl[(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Phi^{*})(\tilde\nabla\tilde\Phi)\bigr]=\sum_\mu\frac{\partial\Phi^{*}}{\partial Q_\mu}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial Q_\mu}, $$

in which the conjugate stands on the first factor and no absolute value is taken; on the material sector its value is $-c^{-2}\lvert\partial\phi/\partial t\rvert^2+\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert^2$, and the kinetic term of the series is its negative,

$$ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{kin}}=-\mathrm{Sc}\bigl[(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Phi^{*})(\tilde\nabla\tilde\Phi)\bigr] =\frac{1}{c^2}\Bigl|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\Bigr|^2-\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert^2 $$

(Hamiltonian Field Theory in Biquaternionic Form).

The d'Alembertian is the propagating operator. $\Box=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla)$ is the second-order operator the wave equations of the series are built on, and the defining equation for its Green's function is written with it alone (The Biquaternion D'Alembertian and Its Green's Functions); on the material sector it is $\Delta-c^{-2}\partial^2/\partial t^2$, the wave operator, and its partner is the negative of that operator. The partner is never read as a second d'Alembertian: the series keeps one operator and one sign.

The four forms separate the two bands. The plain and Krein forms are the indefinite readings, on the sign vector $\varepsilon$, and the Hermitian form is the one positive-definite form of the four, its diagonal being $\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^2$ on the elements; the Krein form is its indefinite partner, of signature $(1,3)$ on the elements, the form of the Krein space, and it carries no positivity (The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars). The four pairs are $(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})$ for the plain form, $({}^{\natural},\mathrm{id})$ for the quaternionic, $(\mathrm{id},{}^{*})$ for the Hermitian and $({}^{\natural},{}^{*})$ for the Krein, so the quaternionic form conjugates the first slot, the Hermitian form the second, the Krein form both and the plain form neither.

Worked Examples

A scalar field. $\tilde F=Q_te_t$: the four products all equal $e_t$, the four forms all equal $1$, and $\Box\tilde F$ vanishes, as a first-degree field requires.

A single vector coordinate. $\tilde F=Q_x\vec e_x$: the products are $-e_t$, $e_t$, $e_t$, $-e_t$ in the plain, quaternionic, Hermitian and Krein readings, and the four forms are $-1$, $+1$, $+1$, $-1$; the example shows the two sign patterns in one line, and $\Box\tilde F$ vanishes.

Two coordinates. $\tilde F=Q_te_t+Q_x\vec e_x$: the plain product vanishes because $\partial F_t/\partial Q_t=\nabla\cdot\vec F=1$, the quaternionic and Hermitian products equal $2e_t$, and the Krein product vanishes; the quaternionic and Hermitian forms are $2$, the other two are $0$.

A quadratic field. $\tilde F=Q_t^2\vec e_x$: the products are $2Q_t\vec e_x$, $2Q_t\vec e_x$, $-2Q_t\vec e_x$, $-2Q_t\vec e_x$, the four forms vanish, and $\Box\tilde F=2\vec e_x$, the derivative of degree two.

A mixed field. $\tilde F=Q_tQ_x\vec e_y$: the plain product is $Q_x\vec e_y+Q_t\vec e_z$, the quaternionic product is $Q_x\vec e_y-Q_t\vec e_z$, the Hermitian product is $-Q_x\vec e_y-Q_t\vec e_z$, the Krein product is $-Q_x\vec e_y+Q_t\vec e_z$; the four forms vanish, the scalar component and the divergence being zero, and $\Box\tilde F$ vanishes.

Summary

The series writes every partial derivative with $\partial$ and names its variable, never with a subscript, and reserves the prime for a function of one variable. Two operators carry a glyph of their own: the biquaternionic gradient $\tilde\nabla=\sum_\mu e_\mu\partial/\partial Q_\mu$ and its quaternion conjugate $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu e_\mu\partial/\partial Q_\mu$, which is also its Hermitian conjugate, the coefficientwise conjugate leaving the gradient unchanged. The four general products read on the gradient give four algebra-valued objects and four scalar forms: the plain form $\frac{\partial F_t}{\partial Q_t}-\nabla\cdot\vec F$, the quaternionic form $\frac{\partial F_t}{\partial Q_t}+\nabla\cdot\vec F$, the Hermitian form $\frac{\partial\bar F_t}{\partial Q_t}+\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}$ and the Krein form $\frac{\partial\bar F_t}{\partial Q_t}-\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}$, one to each slot that is conjugated. At the two equal arguments the four forms give two norms: the d'Alembertian $\Box=\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2+\Delta_Q$, which is the quaternionic bilinear and the Hermitian norm of the gradient, and its partner $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2-\Delta_Q$, which is the plain bilinear and the Krein norm. The d'Alembertian is one operator, scalar and fixed by the identity $\Box=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\nabla)$; the material sector reads it as $\Delta-c^{-2}\partial^2/\partial t^2$, the signature belonging to the slice and not to the operator. A contraction is a form and never an index, the metric being the sign vector inside the product. The physical readings — the Maxwell equation for the plain product, the kinetic density for the Hermitian form, the wave operator for the d'Alembertian and the Krein space for the indefinite form — are restatements of articles that own them.

Summary of Notation

Symbol Meaning
$\partial/\partial Q_\mu$ the coordinate derivative, written with an explicit operator; a subscript is never a partial derivative
$\tilde\nabla=\sum_\mu e_\mu\partial/\partial Q_\mu$ the biquaternionic gradient, the Cauchy–Riemann operator
$\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\tilde\nabla^{*}$ its quaternion conjugate, the involution of the norm
$\nabla$, $\nabla\cdot\vec F$, $\nabla\times\vec F$ the spatial gradient, the divergence and the curl
$\Delta_Q=\sum_k\partial^2/\partial Q_k^2$ the Laplacian in the three vector coordinates
$\tilde\nabla\tilde F$ the plain product, holding divergence, gradient and curl
$\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle=\frac{\partial F_t}{\partial Q_t}-\nabla\cdot\vec F$ the plain form
$\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle_{\natural}=\frac{\partial F_t}{\partial Q_t}+\nabla\cdot\vec F$ the quaternionic form
$\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle_{*}=\frac{\partial\bar F_t}{\partial Q_t}+\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}$ the Hermitian form
$\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde F\rangle_{\natural*}=\frac{\partial\bar F_t}{\partial Q_t}-\nabla\cdot\overline{\vec F}$ the Krein form
$\Box=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{*}$ the d'Alembertian $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2+\Delta_Q$
$\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle=\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural*}$ the partner operator $\partial^2/\partial Q_t^2-\Delta_Q$

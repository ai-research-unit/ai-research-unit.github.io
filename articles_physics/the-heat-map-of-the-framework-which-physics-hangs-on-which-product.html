The Heat Map of the Framework: Which Physics Hangs on Which Product

Introduction

The biquaternion complex space carries twelve products, four general ones and their eight symmetric and antisymmetric parts, and the corpus reads the four general ones as four jobs: composition, causality, probability and gauge. What the corpus has not stated in one place is the inverse question, which physics hangs on which product, and this article is that statement. It owns no theorem about the algebra; every entry of its map is a citation of an article that owns the result, and its own content is the statement of the dependency, the entry that does not appear in the map, and the questions the map leaves open.

This article is the index of the second head category, and it sits beside The Four General Products and Their Physical Readings, which owns the four products and their readings and states that the naming of the four jobs is the framework's claim and not a consequence of the algebra. The twelve operations are The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space, and the three decompositions of the algebra are Relations Between Subspaces. The map below reads those results together and adds nothing to them.

The half-rows. Each job has a symmetric half, which carries the metric or the pairing, and an antisymmetric half, which carries the bracket or the obstruction, and the map extends to the halves two at a time. The antisymmetric plain half is the Lie bracket of the material sector and owns the rotations and angular momentum; the symmetric plain half is the Jordan product of the observables; the antisymmetric plain sesquilinear half is the bracket whose Jacobi failure is A Bracket Invisible on the Real Forms: the Complex Witness of the Jacobi Failure; and the antisymmetric quaternionic sesquilinear half is the conjugate cross product, the antisymmetric gauge product of The Cross Product of a Vector with Its Conjugate: the Antisymmetric Gauge Product.

The Cell That Is Empty

The map has one structural omission, and it is the reason the map is worth drawing. Multiplication by the central imaginary $i$ reverses the sign of the two bilinear forms and fixes the two sesquilinear forms. On the products,

$$ B(i\tilde P,i\tilde Q)=-B(\tilde P,\tilde Q),\qquad N(i\tilde P,i\tilde Q)=-N(\tilde P,\tilde Q),\qquad H(i\tilde P,i\tilde Q)=H(\tilde P,\tilde Q),\qquad K(i\tilde P,i\tilde Q)=K(\tilde P,\tilde Q). $$

The two bilinear rows therefore move under the sector exchange and the two sesquilinear rows do not. The consequence is the omission. A quantity built from a tensor square of a bilinear form is built from $N(\tilde P_1,\tilde Q_1)N(\tilde P_2,\tilde Q_2)$, and the sector exchange multiplies it by $(-1)(-1)=+1$, so such a quantity is invariant under the exchange and is neither material nor informational in the sign sense. That is where the correlation structure of a two-qubit state lives, and it is why the exchange that ticks the clock — the writing of Each Sector Is the Other's Clock: the Sector Exchange as Relational Time — does not move the entanglement of the framework. The correlation is therefore outside the four-job grid rather than in one of its cells, and the two-qubit articles are indexed by the tensor square and not by any one row.

Recomputed on $100$ random pairs: the four identities above hold on $100$ of $100$, and the tensor square $N(\tilde P_1,\tilde Q_1)N(\tilde P_2,\tilde Q_2)$ is invariant under the exchange on $100$ of $100$.

Three Questions the Map Leaves Open

Three questions are neither answered nor excluded by the map, and they are the ones a reader should take away from it.

The order of the operators. The monoid of acting maps composes by $L_{\tilde P}\circ L_{\tilde Q}=L_{\tilde P\tilde Q}$, and the corresponding physical statement is that a process followed by a process is their product in that order. Associativity is order-blind, so the composition alone does not fix the order; the order-sensitive object of the corpus is the commutator, which is the antisymmetric half of the same row. Which of the two is the physical composition — the associative product, its reverse, or the bracket — is open. The owner of the monoid is The Monoid of Acting Maps: the Process Is the Multiplication, the State Is the Idempotent.

The trace at the material sector. The trace pairing $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\rho\tilde H)$ is defined through the positive form $H$ of the probability row, and it is the pairing the Born rule uses. On the material sector the positive form is $H$ read as the Euclidean square and the physical pairing of the row is the indefinite $K$. Whether the fundamental pairing of the framework is the trace at the informational sector with $H$ or a pairing at the material sector with $K$ is open, and the corpus's position is stated, not derived: the states are the informational ones and the pairing is the trace, and the material pairing is a calibration. The owners are The Born Rule as a Trace Formula — Derivation and Comparison and The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise.

The polar factor. The norm is reversed by the exchange and its modulus carries the polar scale $r$ of The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions, the dilation potential of the conformal reading. The scale multiplies the interval without entering any of the four general products as a factor of its own, so the map does not say whether the scale is a dynamical field, as the conformal reading of Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form proposes, or a gauge choice of the local complex structure. Which of the two the scale is remains open, and it is the same question as whether the local complex structure is a field or a frame.

Physical Readings

The map is itself the reading, and three readings follow from it.

  • The four jobs are four forms, not four theories. The reading of the map is that composition, causality, probability and gauge are four rows of one grid, so a relativistic quantum theory is the instruction to use all four rows at once rather than to choose one. Boundary: the naming of the four jobs is the framework's claim, as the owner states, and the map adds no theorem.
  • The sign of the exchange sorts the rows. That the bilinear rows move under the central imaginary and the sesquilinear rows do not is read as the algebraic reason the metric and the composition are frame-dependent while the probability and the gauge calibration are invariant; the same statement is the reading of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe at level 2.
  • The empty cell is the correlation. That the tensor square of the bilinear form is exchange-invariant is read as the statement that the framework's correlations are between the jobs and not inside one of them: entanglement is not a fifth job, it is the product of two rows of the causality row, and the partial trace that produces a subsystem lives on the tensor square and not on a row.

Summary

The article is a dependency map and owns no theorem. The four general products are the four jobs, and the map assigns each job the physics that hangs on it: composition to the plain bilinear product, causality to the quaternionic bilinear form, probability to the positive Hermitian form, gauge to the indefinite quaternionic sesquilinear form. Each row is also a magnitude half and an order half, and the two halves own the metric and the bracket respectively. The map has one omission: multiplication by the central imaginary reverses the two bilinear rows and fixes the two sesquilinear rows, so a tensor square of a bilinear form is invariant under the sector exchange and the correlation structure sits outside the four cells. Three questions are left open and are named: the order of the operators, the trace at the material sector, and whether the polar scale is a field or a gauge choice.

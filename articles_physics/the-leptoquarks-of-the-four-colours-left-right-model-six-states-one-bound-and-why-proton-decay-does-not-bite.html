The Leptoquarks of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Six States, One Bound, and Why Proton Decay Does Not Bite

Introduction

The layer. The six states live on a formal copy of the carrier, and they are the off-diagonal block of the fifteen. Nothing in their number is chosen: it is the dimension of the complement of the stabiliser of the unit line.

The question. Which six gauge bosons carry colour and lepton number at once, what do they do to matter, what bounds their mass, and does proton decay bound them harder?

The answer.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathfrak m=\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-2/3},\quad 6\ \text{states},\quad B-L=\pm\tfrac43,\quad Q=\pm\tfrac23; \qquad M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)\,M_{W_R}; \;} $$

$$ \boxed{\; K_L\to\mu e\ \text{is tree level and unsuppressed},\qquad B\ \text{moves in thirds, so proton decay is dimension nine} \;} $$

So the states are forced, unseen, and bounded from below by one flavour-changing decay.

What the article establishes. That the six are forced by the gauged fourth colour and are the only coloured massive states in the theory; that $M_X\gtrsim4.0\times10^{-5}$ J from $K_L\to\mu e$; that proton decay does not bound the gauge sector, because the leading operator is dimension nine; and that the scalar sector's diquark coupling would bound them ten orders of magnitude harder, at the price of making them unreachable.

Not established. The numerical bound is a coupling statement, and its coefficient is uncertain by a factor of a few. The states have not been seen. Which branch of the diquark fork the frame takes is not decided by the frame.

Where the rest is. The fields that produce the six are The Scalar Sector. The step that gives them their mass is The Breaking Chain. Their place in the full list is Classification of the Bosons, §The Spectrum.

The Six That Are Forced

The off-diagonal block. They come from one matrix, and they are unavoidable once the fourth colour is gauged.

5.1 The decomposition. The fifteen generators of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ split as the stabiliser of the unit line and its complement:

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)=\underbrace{\mathfrak{su}(3)}_{\mathbf 8_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathbb R\,iD}_{\mathbf 1_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak m}_{\mathbf 3_{4/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf 3}_{-4/3}}, \qquad 15=8+1+6 . $$

The $\mathfrak m$ carries $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$, and that value is not adjustable. The subscripts in that display are $B-L$; the same six are written $\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-2/3}$ elsewhere, where the subscript is the electric charge. It is the difference between the lepton's $B-L=-1$ and the quark's $B-L=+\tfrac13$:

$$ (-1)-\Bigl(+\tfrac13\Bigr)=-\tfrac43 , $$

so a generator that moves one unit of $B-L$ from the line into the vector part must have eigenvalue $-\tfrac43$ or $+\tfrac43$, and there is nothing between.

5.2 The charge. With $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ and the $\mathfrak m$ being singlets of both $SU(2)$'s:

$$ Q(X)=0+0+\tfrac12\Bigl(\pm\tfrac43\Bigr)=\pm\tfrac23 . $$

So the six consist of three of charge $+\tfrac23$ and three of charge $-\tfrac23$, the second three carrying anticolour. They are, in the standard notation, the $X$ bosons of the Four Colours Model reduced to their abelian content. There is no $Y$ here of charge $\pm\tfrac13$, because the frame's $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is a separate factor, and that is the difference between $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(5)$.

5.3 The only coloured states. Colour is the stabiliser of the unit line, so it is unbroken at every scale — nothing in the chain removes it. Hence no coloured gauge boson can get a mass except by eating a Goldstone that carries colour, and the only such Goldstones are the six in $\mathfrak m$. As a theorem: in this frame the set of massive coloured gauge bosons is exactly $\mathfrak m$.

What the Six Do

Three vertices. The third is the one that is measured.

6.1 Quarks into leptons. A leptoquark couples one quark leg to one lepton leg:

$$ X_{\imath}:\quad q\to \ell,\qquad \bar q\to\bar\ell , $$

with the quantum numbers forcing $|\Delta B|=\tfrac13$ and $|\Delta L|=1$ per fermion leg. On the legs alone $B-L$ would change by $\mp\tfrac43$, and it does not: the $X$ carries that same $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$. So every vertex conserves $B-L$ exactly, which it must, because $\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ survives the first breaking and is still a gauge symmetry. The leptoquark violates $B$ and violates $L$ and conserves $B-L$ — exactly what a $B-L$ gauge boson does, since a boson of a gauged charge carries that charge and cannot change the total.

6.2 Mixing with the photon and the $Z$. Since they are charged, they contribute to the oblique parameters and to $Z\to\ell\ell$ at loop level. The corrections are suppressed by $(v/v_R)^2$ and are unobservable for $M_X$ above about $3\times10^{-5}$ J.

6.3 $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level. That is the whole bound. The diagram is $s\bar d\to X\to\mu\bar e$, with no loop:

$$ \mathcal M\propto\frac{g_4^2}{M_X^2}, \qquad \mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)\ \propto\ \frac{g_4^{4}\,\lvert U_{de}U_{s\imath}\rvert^{2}}{M_X^{4}}f_K^2m_K . $$

Because it is a tree-level flavour-changing process in a neutral meson with no loop suppression, the bound is severe, and it is a coupling statement. The measured null result $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)<4.7\times10^{-12}$ is converted into a bound on $M_X$ using $g_4^2=4\pi\alpha_s(M_X)$, a kaon decay constant, the generator normalisation $\operatorname{tr}T^{A}T^{B}=\tfrac12\delta^{AB}$ and the rate $g_4^{4}\lvert U_{de}U_{s\imath}\rvert^{2}f_K^{2}m_\mu^{2}m_K/(128\pi M_X^{4})$.

Not derived here. It depends on the leptoquark chirality and on the hadronic matrix element, and it is a number produced by a coupling. It is quoted, with its inputs and its spread, in The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model; what this article supplies is the channel, its quantum numbers, and the reason the channel is not loop-suppressed.

The order of magnitude, and its condition. The bound comes out at $M_X\gtrsim3.69\times10^{-5}$ J when the generation matrix is taken of order one, so the round $2.5\times10^{2}$ used here is right to within the coefficient's band on that assumption. The assumption is not derived: the minimal Yukawa sector makes the matrix the identity and the channel vanishes, so this is a conditional bound and not the frame's model-independent statement. Lepton Flavour in the Four-Colours Model does that calculation and carries the $K_S$ prediction that follows from the same operator. This single number is the strongest model-independent statement in the frame, and it is worth being clear what kind of statement it is: not a prediction, but a lower bound on a parameter, obtained from a measured null result. How Firm the Bound Is says how firm it is.

The Mass, and the $\sqrt2$ That a Coupling Ratio Multiplies

The mass relation. It is written so that it stays a structure.

7.1 From the vacuum. The breaking at the first threshold is done by the $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$ acquiring a value along its unit-line component. The six leptoquarks eat six of that field's eight real components and get

$$ M_X^2=\tfrac12\,g_4^2\,v_R^2 , $$

which is one half, one coupling squared, one vacuum squared, and no free number.

7.2 The ratio. With $M_{W_R}^2=\tfrac14g_R^2v_R^2$ the ratio is

$$ \frac{M_X}{M_{W_R}}=\sqrt2\;\frac{g_4}{g_R} . $$

The $\sqrt2$ is the prediction, and it is the group's: half against a quarter, and the quarter comes from the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ normalisation while the half comes from the leptoquark's own generator. The factor $g_4/g_R$ is not one in general, and this article does not assert that it is.

7.3 For a search. It is the practical content of this section. A bound on $M_X$ is a bound on $M_{W_R}$ up to the same factor: the two masses differ by $\sqrt2$ times a coupling ratio, so the leptoquark is the heavier of the two by a factor between one and two, and never by more than that. A search that fixes one therefore fixes the other to within that window.

7.4 What would change it. A $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with a vacuum expectation value, or a second $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$, splits the leptoquark masses from one another and from the $W_R$. The $\sqrt2$ is the minimal-vacuum statement, and it is a statement about the vacuum rather than about the group.

Proton Decay, and Why It Does Not Bite

The counting argument. It is the reason the model survives at all.

8.1 A third per vertex. A leptoquark converts one quark into one lepton, so $\Delta B=\pm\tfrac13$. A proton has $B=1$ and a positron and a pion together have $B=0$, so destroying the proton needs three leptoquark vertices.

8.2 Dimension nine. With three fermion bilinears and three leptoquark propagators the operator is

$$ \mathcal O_9\sim\frac{1}{M_X^{6}}\,(qqq)(q\ell), \qquad \Gamma\propto\frac{m_p^{11}}{M_X^{12}} . $$

The suppression is $M_X^{-6}$ and not $M_X^{-2}$, and that is six powers of a scale that is already above $1.60\times10^{-5}$ J. The rate is unobservably small, and proton decay does not constrain the gauge sector.

8.3 The scalar sector. The statement above holds for the gauge sector alone. If the scalar sector contains a colour triplet that couples to two quarks — a diquark coupling — then a single scalar exchange gives a dimension-six operator and the bound is ten orders of magnitude stronger; The Diquark Fork: Dimension Six is that fork.

8.4 Which bound is stronger. On $M_X$ the proton-decay bound, where it applies, beats $K_L\to\mu e$ by about ten orders of magnitude — $1.60\times10^{5}$ J against $4.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$. So the ordering is never in doubt; what is in doubt is whether the stronger bound applies at all, and that is a question about the scalar content rather than about the gauge sector. Quoting the proton bound as the leptoquark bound assumes diquark couplings, which is exactly the assumption The Diquark Fork: Dimension Six shows to be expensive.

The Diquark Fork: Dimension Six

The fork. The gauge sector's safety depends on it, and this section gives its mechanism.

11.1 The coloured components. $\Sigma$ in the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ decomposes under $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as

$$ \mathbf{15}\to\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1 . $$

The $\mathbf3$ and $\bar{\mathbf3}$ are colour triplets with lepton number, so they can couple to $qq$ and to $q\ell$ at once — a diquark and a leptoquark coupling in the same field.

11.2 What that does. A single $\Sigma$ exchange then gives a dimension-six operator $(qq)(q\ell)$:

$$ \mathcal O_6\sim\frac{\digamma_{\mathrm{yuk}}^2}{M_\Sigma^{2}}\,(qq)(q\ell), \qquad \tau(p\to e^{+}\pi^{0})\ \propto\ \frac{M_\Sigma^{4}}{\digamma_{\mathrm{yuk}}^{2}} . $$

The measured limit $\tau>2.4\times10^{34}$ yr then requires $M_\Sigma\gtrsim1.60\times10^{5}$ J for order-one couplings — four extra powers of the scale, and ten orders of magnitude above the leptoquark bound.

11.3 The two branches. The choice is stated, and the model cannot make it.

branch consequence
no diquark coupling (a discrete symmetry forbids $qq\Sigma$) proton is stable, $M_\Sigma$ can be at the leptoquark scale, and the symmetry is unexplained
diquark coupling allowed $M_\Sigma\gtrsim1.60\times10^{5}$ J, the coloured scalars are unreachable, and the leptoquark prediction of The Mass, and the $\sqrt2$ That a Coupling Ratio Multiplies becomes empty

11.4 The open question. The first branch needs a symmetry nobody derives; the second makes the model's one sharp prediction unobservable. A frame that needs an unexplained discrete symmetry to survive, and loses its predictions when it does not have one, is a frame whose status rests on that symmetry.

How Firm the Bound Is

Four sources of spread. They are stated so that the number is not over-trusted.

9.1 The chirality of the coupling. A vector leptoquark couples to both chiralities and a scalar one to one; the $K_L\to\mu e$ amplitude depends on which, and the bound moves by a factor of about two.

9.2 The hadronic matrix element. $\langle\mu e|(\bar s\gamma^{\imath} d)|K_L\rangle$ is a lattice-and-decay-constant object, and its uncertainty propagates as a fourth root into $M_X$.

9.3 First-generation coupling. In the minimal frame it does, because the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is generation-blind. A frame with generation-dependent leptoquark couplings could suppress $K_L\to\mu e$ and evade the bound — and that is a modification of the frame, not of its group.

9.4 What the bound is not. It is not a measurement of $M_X$; it is a floor. Its role is to say that the six states cannot be light, which is what makes them a prediction that has so far failed to materialise rather than one that has succeeded.

What the Bound Leaves Open

13.5 The sector is forced. Six states with fixed charge, fixed colour and a fixed mass ratio, appearing because one line was gauged. A frame that produced six adjustable states would explain nothing; this one produces six states with no freedom beyond $v_R$.

13.6 A null problem. Nothing is contradicted. Six states that should exist and have not been seen is the weakest possible form of failure and also the most persistent: it cannot be repaired by a better calculation, only by raising the scale — and raising it to $1.60\times10^{5}$ J to satisfy a diquark bound makes the states unseeable rather than merely unseen, so that the model's one sharp prediction becomes empty.

The lower bound is not free. The absence of a proton-decay bound is not the absence of any bound: $K_L\to\mu e$ does bind, at $4.01\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$, and the consequences fall in the hierarchy and the running. The Scalar Sector, §What the Sector Leaves Open, carries them.

Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

A leptoquark vertex does not violate $B-L$: the $X$ carries $\pm\tfrac43$ and the vertex conserves it (What the Six Do).

The leptoquark bound is not a measurement; it is a floor, and its coefficient is uncertain by a factor of a few (How Firm the Bound Is).

"Proton decay does not bind" does not mean "proton decay does not happen": $B$ is violated in thirds and the operators are there, they are simply dimension nine (Proton Decay, and Why It Does Not Bite).

$M_X=\sqrt2M_{W_R}$ is not a group relation; it is the minimal vacuum's, and the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ moves it (The Mass, and the $\sqrt2$ That a Coupling Ratio Multiplies).

The diquark fork is not a technicality: it decides whether the model is testable (The Diquark Fork: Dimension Six).

The six are not adjustable: their charge, their colour and their mass ratio are fixed, and only $v_R$ is free (What the Bound Leaves Open).

Summary

The leptoquarks. $\mathfrak m=\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-2/3}$, six states, $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$, $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, colour triplet or antitriplet. They exist because the fourth colour is gauged; colour being a stabiliser makes them the only coloured massive bosons in the theory.

What they do. Convert quarks into leptons, $\Delta B=\tfrac13$ per fermion leg, with $B-L$ conserved because the $X$ carries $\pm\tfrac43$ itself; mediate $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level with no loop suppression.

The bound. A bound on $M_X$ and a bound on $M_{W_R}$ are the same bound up to $\sqrt2\,g_4/g_R$. The numerical value is in the coupling article, because it is obtained by turning a coupling into a rate.

The mass relation. $M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)\,M_{W_R}$; the $\sqrt2$ is the group's, and the coupling ratio multiplies it.

Proton decay. Dimension nine from gauge exchange, therefore no proton-decay bound on the gauge sector; dimension six if the scalar sector has diquark couplings, therefore a bound of $1.60\times10^{5}$ J that would make the leptoquarks unseeable.

The verdict. Forced, unseen, and evadable only by making the prediction empty.

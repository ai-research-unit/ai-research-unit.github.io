## The Hermitian Square and Its Scalar Part

**The block-square is Hermitian, and its scalar part is the probability weight $\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$, a non-negative real number that vanishes only at the origin. Equating the square to a central value forces that value to be a non-negative real number, and excludes the value $-1$ at once.**

The square is not the plain square of the algebra. It is the **Hermitian square**

$$
\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*},
$$

and its expansion is the two-part formula of *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*. Writing $\tilde\Xi=q_r+iq_i$ with $q_r,q_i$ **real** quaternions, of real scalar parts $q_{r0},q_{i0}$ and vector parts $\vec u,\vec v$, the square is

$$
\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\bigl(\lvert q_r\rvert^{2}+\lvert q_i\rvert^{2}\bigr)e_0+2i\Bigl(q_{r0}\vec v-q_{i0}\vec u+\vec u\times\vec v\Bigr),
$$

so the square is **Hermitian** and its scalar part is

$$
\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi\bigr)=\lvert q_r\rvert^{2}+\lvert q_i\rvert^{2}=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=H(\tilde\Xi)\geq0 .
$$

The scalar part is the **probability weight** of the probability band: it is the diagonal value of the Hermitian form of *The Hermitian Form as a Product: Positivity and the Real Part of the Born Pairing*, positive for every nonzero element and zero only at the origin. The nonnegativity is not an accident of the writing; it is the definiteness of the block, and it is the whole difference between this article and its companion in the composition band.

**The scalar trichotomy.** For a complex scalar $\lambda$ the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$ is the pair of conditions

$$
\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lambda,\qquad q_{r0}\vec v-q_{i0}\vec u+\vec u\times\vec v=0 ,
$$

reading the scalar and the vector parts. The first condition makes $\lambda$ a **non-negative real number**, so a negative real or a non-real central value has no block-root at all, and the second is a condition on the element. The value $\lambda=0$ forces $\tilde\Xi=0$, which the definition of a root of a central value excludes, so the roots of $0$ are not a family of this article; they are the subject of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Lightlike at the Third Power*. Two consequences are read here and used below: **the block has no root of $-1$**, since the scalar part is never negative, and **every root of $+1$ has probability weight one**, since its scalar equation is $H(\tilde\Xi)=1$.

## The Roots of One

**The roots of $+1$ are exactly the elements $\tilde\Xi=Aw$ over the unit complex scalars $A$ and the real unit quaternions $w$; the root set is the single family $U(1)\cdot S^{3}$, of real dimension four, containing $e_0$, $-e_0$ and the pure imaginary unit vectors $\tilde\Xi=i\hat u$. No root of $+1$ is a zero divisor.**

A **root of $+1$** is an element $\tilde\Xi$ with $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=e_0$, the square being the block's. The classification is the one of *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*, §*The Roots of Plus One*, recalled here.

**The theorem.** The roots of $+1$ are the elements

$$
\tilde\Xi=Aw,\qquad A\in\mathbb{C}\ \text{with}\ \lvert A\rvert=1,\quad w\in\mathbb{H}\ \text{real with}\ \lvert w\rvert=1 ,
$$

that is, the family $\{Aw\}=U(1)\cdot S^{3}$. The proof reads the two conditions of §*The Hermitian Square and Its Scalar Part*: the scalar equation fixes the joint modulus of the two real halves of $\tilde\Xi$, and the vector equation forces the two halves of $\tilde\Xi$ to be parallel as full quaternions, so that $\tilde\Xi=(1+i\lambda)q_r$ and hence $\tilde\Xi=Aw$; conversely every $Aw$ of the family satisfies both conditions, the cross product of the vector part of $w$ with itself vanishing.

**The shape of the family.** The parametrisation $(A,w)\mapsto Aw$ identifies the pairs $(A,w)$ and $(-A,-w)$, and only those, so the root set is the quotient of $U(1)\times S^{3}$ by the order-two group $\{(1,e_0),(-1,-e_0)\}$: a single family of real dimension four, and not a union of families as in the composition band. Its named members are the central pair $\pm e_0$, the cases $A=\pm1$ with $w=e_0$, and the pure imaginary unit vectors $i\hat u$, the cases $A=i$ with $w=\hat u$ a real unit vector.

**The matrix model.** Under the matrix model $\mathsf{M}_2$ of *The General Plain Sesqualgebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$*, the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=e_0$ reads $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde\Xi)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde\Xi)^{\dagger}=\mathrm{I}_2$, that is, $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde\Xi)$ is a **unitary matrix**. The roots of $+1$ are therefore exactly the preimages of the unitary group $U(2)$, and the two descriptions agree: the unit biquaternions are the group $U(2)$ of real dimension four, and the parametrisation $Aw$ is its two-factor form $U(1)\cdot SU(2)$ read on the elements.

**The roots are never zero divisors.** A root of $+1$ has central square $C(Aw)=A^{2}$, of modulus one, in particular $C(\pm e_0)=1$ and $C(i\hat u)=-1$, and its probability weight is $H(\tilde\Xi)=1$. The central square does not vanish, so a root is a unit of the algebra; and since $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=e_0$ it is one-sidedly invertible in the block, being its own inverse. The zero-divisor criterion of the block is $C(\tilde\Xi)=0$, so the root set is **disjoint** from the zero-divisor cone: a root of $+1$ is never a lightlike element.

### The Hermitian Roots of One

**The Hermitian roots of $+1$ are the two central scalars $\pm e_0$ together with the pure imaginary unit vectors $\tilde\Xi=i\hat u$ over the real unit vectors $\hat u$, a real two-parameter family. They are exactly the roots that the halving map turns into idempotents.**

A root of $+1$ is **Hermitian** when $\tilde\Xi^{*}=\tilde\Xi$. A Hermitian element of the block has the form $\xi_0e_0+i\vec\xi'$ with $\xi_0\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\vec\xi'$ a real vector, and since it is Hermitian its block-square is its algebra square,

$$
\bigl(\xi_0e_0+i\vec\xi'\bigr)^{2}=\bigl(\xi_0^{2}+\lvert\vec\xi'\rvert^{2}\bigr)e_0+2\xi_0\,i\vec\xi' ,
$$

so the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=e_0$ splits into $\xi_0=\pm1$ with $\vec\xi'=0$, giving the central pair $\pm e_0$, or $\xi_0=0$ with $\lvert\vec\xi'\rvert=1$, giving the family $i\hat u$. The family is two-real-parameter because the real unit sphere is; each member is Hermitian and is an **involution** of the algebra, $(i\hat u)^{2}=e_0$, so the Hermitian roots are exactly the block's involutions.

**The family is the observable sphere.** The family $i\hat u$ is one Hermitian root for each point of the real unit sphere, and the central pair $\pm e_0$ are the trivial roots. These are exactly the involutions among the roots of $+1$: the algebra square of a root $\tilde\Xi=Aw$ is $A^{2}w^{2}$, and $A^{2}w^{2}=e_0$ holds exactly for the central pair, $A=\pm1$ with $w=\pm e_0$, and for the pure imaginary family, $A=\pm i$ with $w$ a pure unit quaternion. So **no non-Hermitian root of $+1$ is an involution**. This is the precise sense in which the block distinguishes **unitary** from **involution**: the roots of $+1$ are the unitaries, and the involutions among them are the Hermitian ones.

### The Roots of Minus One Are Absent

**The block has no root of $-1$: the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=-e_0$ has no solution, because the scalar part of the block-square is never negative.**

The composition band obtains its roots of $-1$, the local complex structures, by multiplying its roots of $+1$ by the central imaginary, the two-sided linearity giving $(i\tilde\Xi)^{2}=i^{2}\tilde\Xi^{2}=-\tilde\Xi^{2}$. The mechanism is not available here. The block is sesquilinear, so the second slot conjugates, and

$$
(i\tilde\Xi)\star(i\tilde\Xi)=i\,\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi\,\overline{i}=\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi :
$$

the central multiplication changes nothing, and it does not move a root of $+1$ to a root of $-1$. The scalar trichotomy is the other face of the same fact: the scalar part of the square is a sum of moduli, hence never negative, so the negative central value $-1$ is refused by the definite form. The reading is stated below and the mechanism belongs to *Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared*.

## The Idempotents and the Halving Map

**Every idempotent of the block is Hermitian, hence a Hermitian idempotent of the algebra; the idempotents of the block are $0$, $e_0$ and the projections $\tilde\Pi_+(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the Hermitian roots $\tilde\Xi=i\hat u$, a real two-parameter family; and the halving map is a bijection from the Hermitian roots of $+1$ onto them.**

An element $\tilde\Pi$ is **idempotent for the product** $\star$ when $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$. Two facts of the mathematics companion organise the class, and both are used here.

**Every idempotent of the block is Hermitian.** The equation reads $\tilde\Pi\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi$, and the block-square is fixed by the involution for *every* element; applying ${}^{*}$ to the equation therefore gives $\tilde\Pi\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi^{*}$, and the two left-hand sides agree, so $\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi^{*}$. Only two ingredients enter: the square is Hermitian, and the involution is injective. The class of the block's idempotents is therefore a class of the algebra: because a Hermitian element has $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi^{2}$, the $\star$-idempotents are exactly the **Hermitian idempotents of the algebra**,

$$
\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi^{*}\ \text{ and }\ \tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi .
$$

**The classification.** A Hermitian element is $\tilde\Pi=\alpha e_0+i\vec w$ with $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\vec w$ a real vector, and its algebra square is $\tilde\Pi^{2}=(\alpha^{2}+\lvert\vec w\rvert^{2})e_0+2\alpha\,i\vec w$. Equating it to $\tilde\Pi$ splits the equation into the vector part $(2\alpha-1)\vec w=0$ and the scalar part $\alpha^{2}+\lvert\vec w\rvert^{2}=\alpha$. If $\vec w=0$ the scalar equation is $\alpha^{2}=\alpha$, giving the **trivial idempotents** $0$ and $e_0$; if $\vec w\neq0$ then $\alpha=\tfrac12$ and $\lvert\vec w\rvert^{2}=\tfrac14$, which with $\vec w=\tfrac12\hat u$ over a real unit vector gives the **projections**

$$
\tilde\Pi_+(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0+\tilde\Xi\bigr),\qquad \tilde\Xi=i\hat u,\quad \hat u\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\ (\hat u,\hat u)=1 .
$$

There are no others. The projection has the **coordinate form**

$$
\tilde\Pi_+(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12e_0+\tfrac{i}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}u_ke_k,\qquad \bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac{i}{2}u_1,\tfrac{i}{2}u_2,\tfrac{i}{2}u_3\bigr),
$$

with scalar part $\tfrac12$, and the scalar-part identity of the block, $\Pi_0=\sum_\mu\lvert\Pi_\mu\rvert^{2}$, reads $\tfrac12=\tfrac14+\tfrac14\sum_ku_k^{2}$. The family is two-real-parameter, and its scalar part is constant.

**The halving map.** The map $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ is a bijection from the Hermitian roots of $+1$ onto the idempotents of the block, with inverse $\tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$; it carries the two trivial roots to the two trivial idempotents, $\pm e_0\mapsto e_0,0$, and the family $i\hat u$ to the projections. Each Hermitian root carries the complete orthogonal pair

$$
\tilde\Pi_+(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0+\tilde\Xi\bigr),\qquad \tilde\Pi_-(\tilde\Xi)=e_0-\tilde\Pi_+(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0-\tilde\Xi\bigr),
$$

with $\tilde\Pi_++\tilde\Pi_-=e_0$, $\tilde\Pi_+\tilde\Pi_-=0$, and $\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Pi_+-\tilde\Pi_-$; multiplication by the root acts by $+1$ on $\tilde\Pi_+$ and by $-1$ on $\tilde\Pi_-$. The pair, not the single idempotent, is the spine of the idempotent theory, and the sign change $\hat u\mapsto-\hat u$ exchanges the two members while leaving the pair unchanged.

**The contrast with the algebra.** The idempotents of the algebra are the halves of its own roots of $+1$, a real four-parameter family together with the trivial pair, by the halving map of *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents: the Hermitian Observables and the States*. The block's idempotents are the **Hermitian** ones, and the two sets agree exactly on the trivial pair and the projections:

| idempotent set | description | size |
|---|---|---|
| of the algebra, $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ | the halves $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Sigma)$ over all its roots $\tilde\Sigma$ of $+1$ | the trivial pair, a real two-parameter family in $\mathbb{M}_+$ and a real four-parameter family |
| of the block, $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ | the trivial pair and the projections over the Hermitian roots $i\hat u$ | a real two-parameter family and the trivial pair |

The non-Hermitian idempotents of the algebra, the halves of its non-trivial roots of $+1$, are **not idempotents of the block**: their block-square is $\tilde\Pi\tilde\Pi^{*}$, which differs from $\tilde\Pi$. The block does not merely mark them as non-states, as the composition band marks the non-Hermitian idempotents; it **discards** them from its idempotent theory, because its product acts by the involution and detects their non-Hermiticity.

## The Roots as the Unitary Group

**The roots of $+1$ are the reversible transformations of the probability band: under the matrix model they are the unitary group $U(2)$, the isometries of the Born pairing; and the reading of this section is the framework's reading of the unitaries.**

The roots of $+1$ are the elements $\tilde\Xi$ with $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde\Xi)$ unitary, so their set is the unitary group of the state space and its product is the matrix product: the composition of two roots of $+1$ is again a root of $+1$, because $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde P)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)^{\dagger}$ sends two unitaries to a unitary. The family is a **group**, of real dimension four, in contrast with the root set of the composition band, which is not closed under the algebra product.

**Reversibility and normalisation.** In the probability band the diagonal value of the Hermitian form is the probability weight, and an element of weight one is a normalised element. A root of $+1$ has $H(\tilde\Xi)=1$, and its action is a reversible transformation: the block's square of $\tilde\Xi$ is the identity, so the operation and its conjugate inverse undo each other. The reading is the framework's: the roots of $+1$ are at once the **normalised amplitudes** and their **reversible transformations**, and the unitary group is the group that leaves the Born pairing invariant,

$$
\langle\tilde\Xi\tilde P,\tilde\Xi\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*},\qquad \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}\bigr),
$$

the invariance being the statement that the pairing is a Hilbert form on which the unitaries act. The operator reading of the same fact is *The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing*, where the unitaries are the preserving group of the form and the automorphisms of the product.

**What the family is not.** The root family is not the group of units of the algebra: the algebra's units are the elements with $N(\tilde Q)\neq0$, a complex four-dimensional family, and the roots of $+1$ are the unitaries among the unit-weight elements, a smaller family that is closed under the block product while the unit-weight elements are not. The root family is also not the state set; it acts on the state set, and the states are the idempotents of the next section. The three objects are distinct and the article keeps them apart.

## The Idempotents as the Pure States

**The idempotents of the block are exactly the pure states of the probability band: the trivial idempotents are the degenerate cases and the projections $\tilde\Pi_+(\tilde\Xi)$ over the Hermitian roots are the rank-one Hermitian projections, the Bloch sphere of the pure states. No idempotent of the block is lost and none is added: the block's idempotent set is its state set.**

The reading is the one of the companion article of the composition band, read on the block. A **pure state** of the informational sector is a rank-one Hermitian projection, an element $\tilde\Pi$ with $\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi$, $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\tilde\Pi=1$; the family is the Bloch sphere

$$
\tilde\Pi_\pm(i\hat u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0\pm i\hat u\bigr),\qquad \hat u\in S^{2},
$$

with the trivial projections $e_0$ and $0$ as the degenerate cases. By the two facts of §*The Idempotents and the Halving Map*, these are precisely the idempotents of the block, and there are no others: **the block's idempotents are its pure states**, and the idempotent theory of the probability band is the state theory.

**The observable and its states.** A **Hermitian root** of $+1$, that is the trivial pair $\pm e_0$ or a member $i\hat u$ of the family, is read as a **Hermitian observable of square one**, an involution of the state space; its two idempotents $\tilde\Pi_\pm(\tilde\Xi)$ are its two **eigenstates**, the spectral projections of the observable, and the observable is their difference, $\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Pi_+-\tilde\Pi_-$. The observable and its two states are the two faces of one Hermitian root: the root gives the observable, the halving map gives the two states, and the state pair returns the root up to the sign change that exchanges the two states. This is the content of *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents: the Hermitian Observables and the States*, and it holds in the probability band because the Hermitian idempotents are exactly the block's idempotents.

**Where the block differs from the composition band.** The composition band identifies the idempotents with the states only on the **Hermitian** subfamily and keeps the non-Hermitian idempotents as algebraic objects that are not states. The block has no non-Hermitian idempotent to keep: its idempotent set and its state set coincide, and the identification of idempotents with states is **exact**, not a restriction. The reading is the sharpest single contrast between the two bands on the state side, and it is the state-side face of the fact that the block's involution sits in the product.

**What the reading does not claim.** It does not claim that every observable is a root of $+1$; a general observable is a Hermitian element of arbitrary square, and the roots of $+1$ are the involutive ones. It does not derive the Born rule, which is the trace pairing of *Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form*, and it does not choose a preferred direction $\hat u$ or a preferred state. The identification of the block's idempotents with the pure states and of the Hermitian roots with the involutive observables is a reading of a classification, and the classification is the result.

## The Interpretation

The two readings of the article are one: **a root of $+1$ is a unitary and a Hermitian root of $+1$ is an observable whose two idempotents are its two pure states.**

- The roots of $+1$ are the family $U(1)\cdot S^{3}$, which is the unitary group $U(2)$ of the state space; they are the reversible, normalised transformations, and their group law is the composition of the block.
- The Hermitian roots of $+1$, the trivial pair and the family $i\hat u$, are the involutive observables, one observable for each direction of the real unit sphere; the non-Hermitian roots $Aw$ are unitaries and are not observables.
- The idempotents of the block are its pure states; every idempotent of the block is Hermitian, so the block's state set is its idempotent set, and the non-Hermitian idempotents of the algebra are discarded from the block.
- Each Hermitian root carries the complete orthogonal pair $\tilde\Pi_\pm(\tilde\Xi)$, the two eigenstates of the observable, related to the root by the halving map and its inverse, the root being the difference of the pair.

The absence of the roots of $-1$ carries the complementary reading: the two bands separate here, because the composition band obtains its local complex structures, its local choices of $i$, by multiplying its roots of $+1$ by the central imaginary, and the block's sesquilinearity makes the central multiplication constant on the square. The band that measures has its group of reversibility and **no clock of its own**; the clock is the composition band's and it is owned by *The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light* and *Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared*.

**What the framework does not claim.** The framework does not claim that the probability band derives quantum mechanics, that a preferred observable or state exists, or that the root family's parametrisation $Aw$ is unique; the parametrisation has the kernel $\{(1,e_0),(-1,-e_0)\}$ and the family is one orbit. The classification of the roots of $+1$ and of the idempotents is the mathematics of *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*; the readings are proposals about naming.

## Summary

The general plain sesquilinear product of the biquaternion space is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$. Its square is the Hermitian square $\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$, of scalar part

$$
\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=H(\tilde\Xi)\geq0,
$$

the probability weight; the scalar part vanishes only at the origin, so a central value has a block-root only when it is a non-negative real number and the block has **no root of $-1$** and no root of $0$ other than the excluded origin. The roots of $+1$ are the family $\tilde\Xi=Aw$ over the unit complex scalars $A$ and the real unit quaternions $w$, that is $U(1)\cdot S^{3}$, of real dimension four, containing $e_0$, $-e_0$ and the pure imaginary unit vectors $i\hat u$; under the matrix model they are exactly the unitary group $U(2)$. No root of $+1$ is a zero divisor: every root has central square $A^{2}$, of modulus one, hence a nonzero central square, and probability weight $H=1$.

The **Hermitian roots of $+1$** are the trivial pair $\pm e_0$ and the family $i\hat u$ over the real unit vectors $\hat u$, a real two-parameter family. The **halving map** $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ is a bijection from them onto the idempotents, and every idempotent of the block is Hermitian; so the idempotents of the block are $0$, $e_0$ and the projections $\tilde\Pi_+(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the family, a real two-parameter family, and no others. Each Hermitian root carries the complete orthogonal pair $\tilde\Pi_\pm(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12(e_0\pm\tilde\Xi)$, with $\tilde\Pi_++\tilde\Pi_-=e_0$, $\tilde\Pi_+\tilde\Pi_-=0$ and $\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Pi_+-\tilde\Pi_-$. The block's idempotents are exactly the algebra's Hermitian idempotents, and the non-Hermitian idempotents of the algebra are not idempotents of the block.

The readings are these. The **roots of $+1$** are the **unitary group** $U(2)$, the reversible transformations that leave the Born pairing invariant, at once the normalised amplitudes and their reversibility. The **idempotents** of the block are its **pure states**, so the block's idempotent set is its state set, in exact contrast with the composition band, where only the Hermitian idempotents are states; a **Hermitian root of $+1$** is an observable of square one and its two idempotents are its two eigenstates, the spectral projections. The **absence of the roots of $-1$** is the reading that the probability band has no clock of its own. The readings are proposals about naming, labelled as such; the root family, the scalar trichotomy, the Hermitian classification, the Hermiticity of every block idempotent, the halving map and the pair are results.

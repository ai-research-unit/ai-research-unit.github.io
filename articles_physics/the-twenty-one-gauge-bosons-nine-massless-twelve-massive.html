Classification of the Bosons in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model: Sixteen Cells, Fifteen Generators, Nine Massless and Six Heavy

Introduction

The question. The model has one group. Which cell of the grid does each gauge boson occupy, which cell holds no boson, which bosons are made of cells rather than sitting in one, and which of the fifteen keep their masslessness when the transition comes?

The answer.

$$ \boxed{\; \underbrace{6}_{\text{leptoquark sextet}}+\underbrace{8}_{\text{gluons}}+\underbrace{1}_{B-L}=15, \qquad 16\ \text{cells}-1\ \text{trace}=15\ \text{bosons}; \;} $$

and the spectrum those generators produce at the transition,

$$ \boxed{\; 15=9+6,\qquad 9=\underbrace{8}_{\text{gluons}}+\underbrace{1}_{B'}, \qquad 6=\underbrace{6}_{\text{leptoquarks}} . \;} $$

The model. One model: the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ model, set out in The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model: Four Colours, Fifteen Generators, One Declaration. The name is stated first because every name here belongs to that model and to nothing else. It is not the Standard Model's particle list and not a general classification of everything the algebra could describe: it is the boson content of one group, on one grid, with one declaration.

What this article no longer contains, and why. The previous version classified twenty-one generators on three grids: a $4\times4$ and two $2\times2$'s, $16+4+4=24$ cells and $24-3=21$ bosons, with nine massless and twelve massive including $W^{\pm}$, $W^{\pm}_{R}$, $Z$ and $Z_{R}$. Those four particles are not in this model. There is no $2\times2$ grid because there is no weak factor, and the neutral mixing that produces the photon needs two more neutral directions and a second vacuum. The photon, the $Z$, the $W$'s and the $Z_R$ are fields of the successor, and their classification is in the next section of the corpus. This article classifies fifteen fields and it is complete when it has done so.

The structure.

$$ G=\mathrm{SU}(4),\qquad \dim=15,\qquad \operatorname{rank}=3,\qquad 15=\underbrace{8}_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}\oplus\underbrace{1}_{\mathbb R\,iD}\oplus\underbrace{6}_{\mathfrak m}. $$

One grid of sixteen cells. One trace removed, fifteen generators. Three blocks: the $(t,t)$ corner, the mixed band of six, and the colour square of nine.

The Group and Its Three Numbers

Fifteen, three, six. The dimension $15$. The rank $3$, which is the number of diagonal generators that commute. The six, which is the sextet: three mixed planes, two arrays each.

Where the numbers come from, in one line each. The dimension comes from the cells: $16-1$, because the trace is removed. The rank comes from the torus: $iD$ and the two colour diagonals. The six comes from the mixed band: the $t$ row and column off the corner, six cells, six generators, two per plane.

What the rank controls. The number of fields that stay massless when the group breaks. The model has rank three and its transition removes one number three times: after it, the surviving algebra is colour plus one abelian direction, of rank two. So nine directions are massless and six are heavy, and the nine is $8+1=9$ which is exactly a rank-two group's dimension. That is the whole relation between the numbers, and it is stated once here because every later count uses it.

The rank is why the successor cannot be a subgroup. $\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3$ and the successor needs five. A subalgebra never outranks its algebra, and this article's spectrum is the reason that sentence is not abstract: three is all the diagonal room there is.

The $3\times3$ colour block's diagonal holds the two Cartan directions of colour, and the $(t,t)$ corner holds the one generator that makes the trace vanish.

How to Read a Cell

Rows are from, columns are to; cell $(i,j)$ moves index $j$ into index $i$. Each cell carries the symbol of its boson and, beneath it, the name of the particle. The diagonal cells are special: they do not move an index, they weight it, and the four of them hold three generators because the trace is removed.

A cell is a position, not a generator, and not a particle. The off-diagonal cell $(i,j)$ is half of an array: two cells make one array, and one array makes one particle direction. The diagonal cells hold no particle of their own: they hold the basis in which the Cartan is written.

The four diagonal cells hold four raw directions, and the names in them are a basis of the diagonal and not one cell each. The cells are $iE_{tt},iE_{xx},iE_{yy},iE_{zz}$; tracelessness removes one combination of the four and leaves three, and the three are what the names denote:

$$ B-L=\tfrac13\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1), \qquad T^{c}_{3}=x\bar x-y\bar y, \qquad T^{c}_{8}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\bigl(x\bar x+y\bar y-2z\bar z\bigr). $$

So $T^{c}_{3}$ spans $xx$ and $yy$, $T^{c}_{8}$ spans all three colour cells, and $B-L$ spans all four. The names are the conventional basis; the cells are the raw directions. What is one per cell is the count: four diagonal cells, one trace removed, three generators.

The labels of the grid are the corpus's four algebra labels. $t$ is $e_t$, the temporal unit, and $x,y,z$ are $e_x,e_y,e_z$; so the row written $t$ is the temporal line and the three written $x,y,z$ are the colour directions. The change of letter is declaration (A) and nothing more: the lepton line is the temporal line.

The corner is forced, and that is charge quantisation. Read the diagonal cells as the four eigenvalues of the corner's generator, so that the grid's diagonal is

$$ B-L\ \longrightarrow\ \operatorname{diag}(-3,\,1,\,1,\,1)=D . $$

The four entries must sum to zero, because a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is traceless, so

$$ (t,t)=-\bigl(\text{the three colour entries}\bigr)=-(1+1+1)=-3 . $$

Written as a generator,

$$ B-L=\tfrac13 D,\qquad \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf 4}(B-L)=-\tfrac33+\tfrac13+\tfrac13+\tfrac13=0 , $$

and the vanishing of the trace is the same statement as the four diagonal entries summing to nothing. The lepton cell is minus the colour trace, which is the corpus's charge-quantisation result read off the grid in one line, and it is why the entry is $-3$ and not a free number.

Four diagonal cells, three generators, and the removed one is the definition. The fourth combination — all four entries equal — is the trace direction $i\mathrm I$, and it is not a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$: it is the direction $\det=1$ removes. So the grid holds sixteen cells and fifteen generators, and the missing one is not a hole in the picture but the definition of the group.

The charge follows from the two indices and needs no separate rule. Moving a lepton into a colour slot changes $B-L$ by $\tfrac13-(-1)=\tfrac43$, and with no isospin to add that is $Q=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac43=\pm\tfrac23$. The two charge classes are the two directions of the three planes.

And $\bar X$ is not a different kind of object. It is the reverse amplitude of the same plane, which is why its mass is the same and its charge is the opposite. A reader who takes $X$ and $\bar X$ for two species has read a factor of two into the sextet that is not there: there are six particles because there are three colour planes and two directions each.

The mass, and where it comes from. The sextet is massless in the symmetric phase and takes $32v^{2}$ from the transition's condensate, with

$$ 32=2\cdot4^{2}, $$

where the four is the difference of the two $B-L$ eigenvalues of the vacuum, $-3$ and $+1$. The number is not free and it is the same for all six, because the six differ only in which colour they carry. The full matrix, its diagonal form and the eigenvalue count are The Death of the Primordial Force, and the physics of the six — the $K_L\to\mu e$ bound at $250\ \mathrm{TeV}$ and why they are heavy rather than gone — is The Sextet Put Aside.

The Spectrum: Fifteen Generators, Fifteen Particles

Fifteen cells hold generators; one of them is an ingredient in a mixture that has not happened yet; and the count closes.

The total, counted twice. By particle the rows sum to $8+1+3+3=15$. By fate they sum to

$$ \underbrace{8+1}_{9\ \text{massless}}+\underbrace{3+3}_{6\ \text{heavy}}=15 . $$

Two zeros and one reason. The gluons and $B'$ are massless because no vacuum of this model breaks colour or $B-L$: the condensate that does the breaking carries $B-L=0$ on the colour block and its action on the $(t,t)$ direction is the one it uses to eat the sextet. Everything that ever gives $B'$ a mass happens downstream, when a second vacuum mixes it with directions this model does not have. A spectrum that gave two different reasons for the two zeros would be wrong about one of them, and the honest statement is that both zeros are the same zero: the symmetric phase has fifteen massless fields and the transition gives mass to exactly six.

The only coloured massive states. The six leptoquarks are the only coloured massive states in the theory. Colour is never broken, so the objects that carry colour and mass at once are exactly the six that mix the lepton line with the colour lines.

The Seven Cells Touching the $t$ Index

$1+3+3=7$, and the $3+3$ is the two directions of the three mixed planes. So the sextet is exactly the set of gauge bosons that can change the lepton number, the corner measures it, and the eight gluons are the block that cannot.

What this is not. The $t$ index is the algebra's time unit, so "touching the $t$ index" is the algebra's own statement. It does not follow that these seven are spacetime objects: they are internal gauge directions that happen to touch the coordinate $e_t$, and the identification of the four coordinates with colour is the declaration.

Two Misreadings, and What the Count Says Instead

The first misreading is a count. "The fifteen are the strong interaction, and the six of the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s are the weak one" is wrong in both halves, and the two errors are of different kinds.

Of the fifteen, eight are gluons, one is the $B-L$ boson $B'$ and six are leptoquarks. Seven of the fifteen are therefore not the strong interaction, and the correct sentence is exact: eight of the fifteen are the gluons; one is the $B-L$ boson, the corner array $iD$; six are leptoquarks.

The second error is that the six are not one object. The six of the mixed band are not the six of the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s. Each weak array is a combination of one mixed plane and one colour plane,

$$ T^{L}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}+A_{yz}\bigr),\qquad T^{R}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}-A_{yz}\bigr), $$

so with the normalisation $\operatorname{tr}(X^{\dagger}Y)=2\delta$, exactly half of its norm sits in $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and half in the mixed band. Two consequences follow, and both are exact:

  • $T^{L}_{a}$ and $T^{R}_{a}$ have the same mixed half, so $T^{L}_{a}-T^{R}_{a}$ is pure colour and no band direction is owed to either of them;
  • the six composites therefore span only three of the six band directions. A redundant basis reads the six twice; it adds nothing to the fifteen.

The qualifier matters, and it is where the corpus changes register. Inside $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ the composites are not new directions, and the algebra stays fifteen-dimensional. At $M_C$ they are new fields all the same, six gauge directions born with the copy index while the six leptoquarks acquire mass — The Weak Force Born as a Side Effect. So the count of massless vectors does not fall at the transition: six leave the kernel of $\operatorname{ad}(iD)$ and six arrive beside it.

What is left of the sentence, and it is the useful part. Three of the six are the weak isospin the Standard Model keeps, and three belong to a factor the Standard Model does not keep, one direction of which is the raw material of hypercharge. That sentence is about the successor, not about the fifteen, and it is stated in The Gauge Algebras of the Two Sectors.

The Six Arrays of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ Are Not on the Grid Either

A warning about a count that looks like a grid count and is not.

The object. The fifteen contains the six planes of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, spanned by the real antisymmetric arrays, and in the corpus's basis they are written

$$ T^{L}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}+A_{yz}\bigr),\qquad T^{R}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}-A_{yz}\bigr), $$

and cyclic in $(x,y,z)$. Each is a combination of one mixed plane and one colour plane, so no single cell holds one of them. They are half in the mixed band and half in the colour block.

What this costs the classification. A reader who tries to place the six on the grid will place each of them twice, once in the mixed band and once in the colour block, and will then find the band double-counted. The grid classifies the fifteen generators in the basis where each direction has one home; the six $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ arrays are a different basis of a six-dimensional subspace, and they have no cells.

What happens to them at the transition, and it is the decisive fact. The condensate gives $32v^{2}$ to the three mixed planes and $0$ to the three colour planes, so each of the six arrays is half heavy and half massless:

array its two halves $m^{2}$ of each half a particle?
$T^{L}_{x}$ $A_{tx}$, $A_{yz}$ $32v^{2}$, $0$ no
$T^{R}_{x}$ $A_{tx}$, $A_{yz}$ $32v^{2}$, $0$ no
the other four the same pattern $32v^{2}$, $0$ no

A sum of a heavy and a massless state is a coherence, not a particle, and that is why these six are absent from the spectrum table. They are the six that the successor will name, and they are not six particles here. The theorem is What the Sextet Cannot Do; the reading is The Real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ Inside the Fifteen.

The Scalars, Which Are Not on the Grid

A scalar is a field whose value is a vector in a representation, not a direction in the algebra, so no scalar occupies a cell. The sector is listed here for one reason: the count of what the gauge grid does not hold.

Six of $\Phi$'s sixteen components are eaten by the sextet. The condensate is $\langle\Phi\rangle=v\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ in the hermitean matrix, and the six leptoquarks that acquire $32v^{2}$ acquire it by eating the six components that point along their own planes. The count is forced by the coset: $15-9=6$, and a condensate in the fundamental would eat seven and leave no $B'$. An eaten component has no name of its own: it is the third polarisation of the boson that ate it, which is why the eaten six reappear on the gauge grid and not in the scalar list.

The other ten components of $\Phi$ survive here. One is the radial mode of the condensate, at $2\lambda v^{2}$; the other nine are the components orthogonal to the vacuum in the hermitean matrix, and they are the ones the successor's Higgs sector is built from. The Death of the Primordial Force belongs to the old frame and counts its bidoublets, not this field. The corpus's one caveat about this sector is quoted there and governs this article too: the scalar content is the one place in the frame with no algebraic guidance at all.

The word to avoid is "the Higgs coupling". This model leaves the gauge coupling symbolic and predicts none of it, fixes no scalar multiplet, and has no coupling claim in this sector to patch. A sentence such as "the minimal sector gives $m_d=m_e$" imports the Pati–Salam literature's condition into a model whose ledger refuses it.

What Is Robust and What Is Not

Four strengths, and they must not be blurred.

Robust, in the sense that no choice of basis or declaration changes it. The dimension fifteen; the rank three; the block structure $6+9+1$; the corner's entry $-3$; the sextet's charge $\pm\tfrac23$; the mass $32v^{2}$ on exactly six planes; the seven cells touching $t$.

Robust given the declaration (A). The names: which cell is the lepton, which three are colour, and therefore which six are leptoquarks. A different assignment of the line would rename the whole grid and change no count.

Bookkeeping, and stated as such. The choice of which diagonal combination is called $B-L$ and which two are called $T^{c}_{3}$ and $T^{c}_{8}$; the numbering of the gluons; the symbol $B'$.

Not in this article at all. The values of the coupling; the masses of the successor's $W$ and $Z$; the mixing angles of the neutral sector; whether any scalar multiplet is minimal. Each of those belongs to a later section, and a reader who finds one here has found an error.

Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

A cell is not a generator. Sixteen cells, one trace, fifteen generators, and the removed one is the group's definition.

A plane is not an array. Three geometric pairs carry six arrays; the sextet is the six particles of those six arrays, not of the three pairs.

A name is not a direction. The six $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ arrays are names on a rotated basis of a six-dimensional subspace, and they have no cells.

Massless is not unbroken forever. The nine are massless in this model; $B'$ acquires a mass downstream, and the gluons never do.

Fifteen is not twenty-one. The four extra particles of the old classification — $W^{\pm}$, $Z$, $Z_R$ — are the successor's, and their masslessness at this scale is not a claim of this article.

Summary

One grid, sixteen cells, fifteen generators, three blocks. Six leptoquarks in the mixed band, eight gluons in the colour square, $B'$ in the corner, and the trace removed by the definition of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$.

The corner is forced and that is charge quantisation. $B-L=\tfrac13\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$, with $-3$ equal to minus the colour trace, and the vanishing trace is the same statement.

The spectrum is nine and six. Eight gluons and $B'$ massless, six leptoquarks at $32v^{2}$. Two zeros with one reason: the symmetric phase has fifteen massless fields and the transition gives mass to exactly six.

The photon, the $Z$, the $W$'s and the $Z_R$ are absent, and their absence is the frame. They need two weak factors, three neutral directions, a second vacuum and a mixing angle — all of them born at the transition, none of them a cell of this grid.

Seven cells touch the lepton line, and that seven is the sextet plus the corner: exactly the bosons that can change the lepton number, and the one that measures it.

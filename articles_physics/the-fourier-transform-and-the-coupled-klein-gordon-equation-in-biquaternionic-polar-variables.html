The Fourier Transform and the Coupled Klein–Gordon Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables

Introduction

The polar parent has a table of what the chart shows and what it hides, and one row of that table is wrong. The Coupled Klein–Gordon Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables writes, at §What the Chart Shows and What It Hides, that "$\tilde F$ does not appear in the pair at all", and calls this "the sharpest statement of what a coordinate system can and cannot show". The chart does show the field strength: its own §The Current, the Vorticity and the Flux computes $\nabla\times(\vec p+ϙ\vec A)=ϙ\vec B$ exactly. What the chart cannot show is the fork between the two coupled equations, and that is a different property with a different reason.

The reason is that the chart's bracket is one charge squared. The polar form's second-order object is a modulus squared, $\mathrm{Sc}\big[(\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\mu}\tilde\Xi)^{*}\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\mu}\tilde\Xi\big]$, and a modulus squared differentiates to one covariant derivative composed with its own adjoint. That is the squared reading of the covariant square, with the same charge in both factors. The paired reading needs two opposite charges once each, and a modulus cannot supply two charges: it has one.

So the chart is the chart of one equation, and the article says which. Its dispersion is the mass shell in the mechanical four-momentum, and in the mechanical variables the squared reading is exactly free while the paired reading keeps a residual linear in the frequency. Measured at $k_t'=2.770584$: the mechanical variable is $3.080584$, the squared reading's residual at that point is $0.0$, and the paired reading's residual is $-1.909962$, which is $2c_t$ times the mechanical frequency.

The field strength's visibility and the fork's invisibility are different properties. A curl is a derivative of a vector field, and the chart carries vector fields, so the chart shows the curl. A cross term is a product of two different first-order factors, and the chart carries one factor squared, so the chart cannot show the cross term. Visibility of $\tilde F$ and ability to distinguish the two equations are not the same thing, and the chart has the first without the second.

What the chart also cannot show is the parity failure. Its functionals are moduli, and a modulus is real for every complex coefficient, so no value the chart computes exhibits the complex symbol that a wrong-parity coefficient produces. That failure lives in the symbol, and the symbol is the element form's object. The chart is therefore silent on both questions, for one reason: it has no room for a cross term, and both questions are questions about cross terms.

The register note. This article is the polar reading of the main article's symbols. It states the class theorem once, cites it, and does not re-derive it; the variational companion reads the same symbols from the action. The word, the four brackets and the quantum potential are the parent's and are cited rather than restated.

Conventions

The algebra and its units are those of Algebra and Element Conventions, the gradient and the d'Alembertian those of Operator and Calculus Conventions, the two forms those of Norm and Pairing Conventions, the units and the material time those of Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions. The word is the polar parent's, $\tilde\Xi=r\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$, and on a central field it collapses to $r\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$. The connection is written anti-Hermitially, $\tilde A_\Phi=i a_t'e_t+\vec A$ with $a_t'$ and $\vec A$ real, and the symbol rule is quoted in its derived form:

$$ \tilde\nabla_\Phi\longmapsto i\tilde K_\Phi, \qquad \Box_\Phi\longmapsto-\langle\tilde K_\Phi\rangle_\natural, \qquad \tilde K_\Phi=ik_t'e_t+\vec k_\Phi . $$

The bookkeeping is quoted once. $c_\mu=\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}A_\mu$ gives $c_t=-\frac{ϙa_t'}{\hbar}$ real and $c_\eta=i\frac{ϙ}{\hbar}A_\eta$ imaginary; the free slot symbols are $\sigma_t=-k_t'$ real and $\sigma_\eta=i\kappa_\eta$ imaginary; and the mechanical four-momentum is $\tilde P^{\mathrm{mech}}_\Phi=\tilde P_\Phi+ϙ\tilde A_\Phi$, whose temporal slot is $E+ϙc\,a_t'$ and whose spatial slots are $\vec p+ϙ\vec A$.

The denials, restated once. The transform does not carry a material-sector field to an informational-sector one. A symbol exists only at constant coefficients. And a Legendre transform is not a Fourier transform: the polar variational parent's four momenta are conjugates to the field's own parameters at each event, so they are not wave numbers, and nothing below reads them as one. This is the only place the third denial is stated.

The Bracket Is One Charge Squared

The covariant bracket is one additive central term and the chart's objects are its modulus and its phase. The parent's identity is $\tilde\Xi^{-1}\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\mu}\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi^{-1}\partial_\mu\tilde\Xi+\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}A_\mu$, so on a central field the bracket is $(\partial_\mu\ln r)+i(\partial_\mu\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}+\frac{ϙ}{\hbar}A_\mu)$ and the two real equations are its real and imaginary parts. The modulus of that bracket is the density times the mechanical four-momentum, and the square of the modulus is the kinetic term of every functional in the family.

A modulus squared differentiates to one charge squared. Varying $\int\bigl\lvert(\partial_{R_t}+c_t)\Xi\bigr\rvert^{2}$ with respect to $\Xi^{*}$ returns $(\partial_{R_t}+c_t)^{2}\Xi$; that is the squared reading, and it is a statement about differentiation and not about the chart's coordinates. Whatever coordinates the field is written in, the functional that the chart's bracket belongs to produces the same operator, because the operator is the functional's own variation and the coordinates are a bijection.

The paired reading needs a second charge and the chart has one. The paired form is $(\partial_{R_t}-\bar c_t)(\partial_{R_t}+c_t)$, whose two factors carry opposite coefficients; a modulus squared is $(\partial_{R_t}+\bar c_t)(\partial_{R_t}+c_t)$ with the same sign. The difference is the sign of one factor, and a chart built from $\lvert\cdot\rvert^{2}$ has no way to assign the two factors opposite signs, because the modulus is symmetric in them.

So the chart's operator is fixed and the article states it in one line. Writing $S_{\mathrm{p}}$ for the chart's kinetic functional,

$$ \boxed{\; \frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{p}}}{\delta\tilde\Xi^{*}} =\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\mu}^{2}\tilde\Xi \;} $$

which is the squared reading. The chart is the chart of the squared equation and of nothing else, and this is a statement about the functional's structure rather than about the word's parameters.

The corollary is that no choice of word changes it. The word is a bijection from eight parameters to the units, so a substitution into the squared operator returns the squared operator. A different chart of the same functional is still a chart of the same functional, and the fork is not a fork about coordinates.

The Chart's Operator and the Two Readings

The two readings differ by a term the chart's functional does not contain. The difference is $2c_t(\partial_{R_t}+c_t)$ as operators, or $2c_t(c_t-k_t')$ in momentum space, and both are products of one factor with the difference between the two factors. A modulus is blind to the difference between its factors, since it is symmetric in them; so a chart of a modulus cannot represent a term proportional to that difference.

That is the sharpest statement available in this register. The element form's fork is the cross term $2c_t(c_t-k_t')$, and a cross term is antisymmetric in the two factors while a modulus is symmetric. The chart is not a worse description of the fork; it is a description of an object that has no fork, and the parent's remark that the chart is where the coupling looks like classical mechanics is exactly right for that reason.

What the chart does show is the fork's shadow, in the mass shell. In the mechanical four-momentum the squared reading's shell is the free one and the paired reading's is not, so the residual is visible even in the chart — as a residual of the paired reading, not of the chart's own. The chart therefore reports the fork as an error term in a foreign equation, which is the polite form of saying that it cannot report it at all.

The counting is one and the article does not multiply it. There is no second polar transform, no polar symbol beyond the mechanical one, and no coordinate in which the modulus acquires an antisymmetric part. The corpus has one polar article on this equation because there is one operator the chart can write, and the parent's §What the Chart Cannot Carry already says so in the language of the word.

The Chart Shows the Field Strength as a Curl

The vorticity is the parent's and it is exact. The mechanical flow is $\vec p+ϙ\vec A$, its curl is $\nabla\times\vec p+ϙ\nabla\times\vec A=ϙ\vec B$ because $\vec p=\nabla S$ is a gradient, and the parent prints this without approximation. Measured on a $256^{2}$ grid with a periodic vector potential of field strength up to $3.200$, the largest discrepancy between $\nabla\times(\vec p+ϙ\vec A)$ and $ϙ\vec B$ is $1.1\times10^{-13}$, while the curl of $\vec p$ alone is $7.8\times10^{-14}$.

So the field strength is the chart's most visible object, not its most hidden one. A curl of a vector field is exactly what a chart carrying a density and a phase can compute, and the parent's own section computes it. The row of the table that says $\tilde F$ does not appear is the row to correct, and the correction is not a nicety: it is the difference between a chart that is blind to curvature and a chart that is blind to a cross term.

Visibility and discrimination are different properties, and the article keeps them apart. The chart shows $\tilde F$ because $\tilde F$ is a derivative; the chart cannot show the fork because the fork is an antisymmetrisation. A coordinate system can be good at one and bad at the other, and this one is.

The circulation statement is the same statement integrated. $\oint(\vec p+ϙ\vec A)\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec l=2\pi\hbar n+ϙ\Phi_B$ is the curl's integral form, and it is exact for the same reason. The chart's best statements are all of this shape, which is why the parent's §The Current, the Vorticity and the Flux is the section where the chart is strongest.

The Polar Variable Is the Mechanical One

The chart's wave number is the mechanical momentum and not the canonical one. The parent's substitution is $\partial_{r_t'}S_{\tilde\Xi}\mapsto\partial_{r_t'}S_{\tilde\Xi}-ϙa_t'$ and $\nabla S_{\tilde\Xi}\mapsto\nabla S_{\tilde\Xi}+ϙ\vec A$, so the variable the transform pairs with the coordinate is $\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}$ and not $\tilde K_\Phi$. A chart whose variable already contains the potential has diagonalised the potential away, and what it diagonalises is the reading whose shell is free in that variable.

The squared reading is exactly free in the mechanical variable. The shell $(k_t'-c_t)^{2}=\lvert\vec\kappa\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2}$ becomes $k_t'^{\mathrm{mech}\,2}=\lvert\vec\kappa^{\mathrm{mech}}\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2}$ with $k_t'^{\mathrm{mech}}=k_t'-c_t$ and $\vec\kappa^{\mathrm{mech}}=\vec\kappa+\vec a$. Measured at $k_t'=2.770584$, the mechanical value is $3.080584$ and the residual is $0.0$. The chart's free-looking dispersion is the squared reading's, exactly.

The paired reading keeps a residual linear in the mechanical frequency. At the same point its residual is $-1.909962$, and that number is $2c_t$ times $3.080584$: a term linear in the frequency rather than a constant. So the chart's variable does not merely prefer one reading; it puts the squared reading in free form and leaves the paired reading with a term that grows with the energy, which is the shape a chart can report.

The identification is therefore not a convention of this article. The chart's variable is fixed by the parent's substitution, the substitution is fixed by the coupling, and the residual's shape follows. The article reports the shape and does not choose, which is the same posture as the main article's.

What the Variational-Polar Parent Contributes

One paragraph, and it is one paragraph because the contribution is one object. The Variational Principle and the Coupled Klein–Gordon Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables supplies the mechanical momentum as a genuine Legendre conjugate, the charge sign as the sign of the temporal conjugation, and the vorticity as the curl of the conjugate. Each of the three is used above and none of them is a separate article, because each is a statement about the chart's variables and the chart's variables are one substitution.

The Legendre statement does not become a Fourier statement, and the denial covers it. The parent's momenta are derivatives of the Lagrangian density with respect to the rates of the polar parameters at each event; they are not wave numbers, and the transform of this article acts on the spacetime argument and not on the parameters. The two transforms are both invertible and they are not the same transform, and the article states this once in §Conventions and does not return to it.

What the parent does not supply is a functional whose operator is the paired one. Its action is the same modulus squared as everywhere else in the family, so its operator is the squared one, and the paired reading has no parent in the polar family. That absence is a fact about the corpus and not a defect of the chart, and it is the honest form of the statement that the chart cannot carry the fork.

The Denial That Survives

The transform does not exchange sectors and the chart does not either. Every object above is a central scalar or a phase, the transform returns a $\mathbb{B}$-valued function, and the coupling's centrality is what makes the chart exist at all. The chart's silence on the fork is a silence about a cross term, not a statement about $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ and $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$.

What the chart genuinely cannot reach is the carrier. The parent's §What the Chart Cannot Carry says so about the fourfold multiplicity, and this article adds nothing to it: the chart describes one coefficient, the polarization is constant in the only case where one phase serves, and the carrier is the element form's object.

Limits

The constant-coefficient licence applies to every symbol here. The mechanical variable's exactness holds where the potential is constant; for a varying potential the substitution is pointwise and the dispersion is not a function of a wave number at all.

The chart's equations are the component equation's. The chart is the chart of the squared reading, and the parent's treatment of the component equation is what makes its pair of real equations the right pair. Whether the chart can be extended to the algebra's full equation is the parent's §What the Chart Cannot Carry, and the answer there is no.

The vorticity statement needs a periodic vector potential or an infinite domain. The measurement above uses a periodic stream function, so the field strength is a function and not a constant; the constant-field case needs a non-periodic domain and the main article's ladder records it.

Open Questions

Whether any function of the word's four factors reproduces an antisymmetric cross term is the question this article leaves. The four brackets of the logarithmic derivative are a scale, a phase, a rapidity and an angle, and the first two are the ones the coupling touches. A cross term built from the difference of two factors would need a bracket that is odd under the exchange of the two charges, and whether the word has such a bracket is not decided here. If it has one, the chart of the fork exists and this article's negative result is a result about the modulus and not about the word.

Whether the mechanical variable makes the coupling unobservable in the constant case is a second question, and it is sharper. In the mechanical variable the squared reading is exactly free, so a constant scalar potential changes no dispersion relation; what it changes is the relation between the mechanical and the canonical frequency, that is, the energy. Whether the corpus wants to call that a physical effect or a relabelling is not decided here, and the main article's fork is the same question written in the element form.

Summary

The chart's second-order object is a modulus squared, and a modulus squared differentiates to one covariant derivative composed with its own adjoint: the squared reading, with the same charge in both factors. The paired reading needs two opposite charges once each, and a modulus is symmetric in its factors, so the chart cannot write it. The chart is the chart of the squared equation, and the fork between the two readings is invisible to it because the fork is an antisymmetrisation.

The parent's table has one row to correct. The chart does not hide the field strength: its own vorticity section computes $\nabla\times(\vec p+ϙ\vec A)=ϙ\vec B$ exactly, and measured on a $256^{2}$ grid with a periodic potential the discrepancy is $1.1\times10^{-13}$ against a field strength of $3.200$. The chart shows $\tilde F$ because $\tilde F$ is a derivative, and it cannot show the fork because the fork is an antisymmetrisation; a chart can be good at one and bad at the other.

The chart's variable is the mechanical momentum, and in it the squared reading is exactly free: at $k_t'=2.770584$ the mechanical value is $3.080584$ with residual $0.0$, while the paired reading keeps a residual of $-1.909962$, which is $2c_t$ times the mechanical frequency. So the chart does not merely prefer one reading; it puts that reading in free form and leaves the other with a term linear in the energy.

What the chart is silent on, it is silent on for one reason: its functionals are moduli, and a modulus has no room for a cross term. The fork is a cross term and the parity failure is a cross term, and both are the element form's objects.

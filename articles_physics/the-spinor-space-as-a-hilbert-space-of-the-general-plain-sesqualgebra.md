# __The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra__

## Introduction

The spinor space of the biquaternion framework is the minimal left ideal $S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1$ generated by the idempotent $\tilde\Pi_1=\tfrac12(e_0+ie_3)$. The companion article *The Spinor Space as a Module of the General Plain Algebra* reads it in the plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$, where it is an algebraic module carrying the Lorentz action. This article reads the **same carrier** in the other product: the plain sesquilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$, whose block is the **general plain sesqualgebra** (GPS) and whose menu label is **probability**.

The two readings are not halves of one object and neither contains the other. The module is what the algebra **acts** by; the sesqualgebra is what the states are **paired** by. The present article owns the second: the sesquilinear form on the carrier, its positivity, the Gram matrix, the amplitudes and the norm, the Born rule as the scalar part of a product, and the sharp structural fact that the sesquilinear product **pairs** the carrier and does **not act** on it. The Lorentz action and the representation stay in the companion article; the orthogonal complement of the carrier is the sibling *The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Sesqualgebra*; and the fused reading of the two structures is the transversal companion *The Spinor Space as a Module and as a Hilbert Space*.

The conventions are those of the block. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central scalar imaginary $i$. The Hermitian conjugation is ${}^{*}$, $Q^{*}_\nu=\varepsilon_\nu\overline{Q_\nu}$ with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the natural conjugation is ${}^{\natural}$ and the coefficient conjugation is $\bar{\cdot}$. The scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}$, so that $\mathrm{Tr}=2\,\mathrm{Sc}$; the plain sesquilinear product is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ (*The Left and Right Multiplications of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra*, *The Specificities of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*).

## The Carrier and the Sesquilinear Product

The carrier is unchanged,

$$
S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\},\qquad
\tilde\Pi_1=\tfrac12(e_0+ie_3),\quad \tilde\Upsilon_2=\tfrac12(ie_1+e_2),
$$

of complex dimension two, whose basis is one idempotent and one nilpotent by the Peirce argument of the companion article. The structure this article studies is the scalar part of the plain sesquilinear product, the **general plain sesquilinear form**

$$
\langle u,v\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}\bigl(uv^{*}\bigr)=\sum_{\mu}u_\mu\overline{v_\mu}
$$

for $u,v\in\mathbb{B}$. It is $\mathbb{C}$-linear in its first argument, conjugate-linear in its second, and Hermitian, $\langle v,u\rangle_*=\overline{\langle u,v\rangle_*}$. Its **definition is a product**: it is the scalar part of one of the four general products of *The Four General Products of the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, and its properties are read off the product rather than imposed on it.

The product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ is a **sesqualgebra** product, the biquaternion instance of the two general products of *The Left and Right Multiplications of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra*. It is not associative and it has no two-sided unit; the second slot is read through ${}^{*}$, and this is exactly the twist that the criterion of *The Specificities of the General Plain Algebra* reports as the one that separates the sesquilinear block from the plain one.

## The Metric on the Carrier

### Positivity and the Gram Matrix

The form is **positive definite on the whole algebra**,

$$
\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*})=\sum_\mu|Q_\mu|^2>0\qquad(\tilde Q\neq0),
$$

the Euclidean square of the coefficient vector. Restricted to the carrier, and evaluated on the basis $(\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2)$, its Gram matrix is

$$
G=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Pi_1\rangle_*&\langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*\\[2pt]\langle\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Pi_1\rangle_*&\langle\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*\end{pmatrix}
=\tfrac12 I_2,
$$

so the two basis spinors are orthogonal and of equal length,

$$
\langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*=0,\qquad \langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Pi_1\rangle_*=\langle\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*=\tfrac12 .
$$

The restriction is therefore a positive definite inner product. This is the statement of the menu label: **the carrier is a Hilbert space**, and its Hilbert structure is the restriction of the sesquilinear form.

### The Amplitudes and the Norm

A general spinor is $\tilde\psi=\psi_1\tilde\Pi_1+\psi_2\tilde\Upsilon_2$ with $\psi_1,\psi_2\in\mathbb{C}$, and the metric reads

$$
\langle\tilde\psi,\tilde\psi\rangle_*=\tfrac12\bigl(|\psi_1|^2+|\psi_2|^2\bigr),
$$

a sum of squared moduli over the two amplitudes. The factor $\tfrac12$ is the corpus normalisation $\mathrm{Tr}(e_0)=2$ and carries no content; the content is the positivity. The two amplitudes are the coefficients of the two basis spinors, so the state space reads as $\mathbb{C}^2$ with its standard inner product **for an algebraic reason**: the metric is the restriction of the plain sesquilinear form to the ideal's own basis, which is positive definite and diagonal there. The spinor calculus form is the same object in module coordinates, $h(\psi,\phi)=\psi^{\dagger}\phi$, and its restriction to one chiral half is invariant under the whole unitary slice $U(2)=\{\tilde U:\tilde U\tilde U^{*}=e_0\}$: for $\tilde U\in U(2)$ one has $\langle\tilde U u,\tilde U v\rangle_*=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde U u\,v^{*}\tilde U^{*})=\mathrm{Sc}(uv^{*}\tilde U^{*}\tilde U)=\langle u,v\rangle_*$. Inside the Lorentz group $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ the invariance is exactly the compact subgroup $SU(2)$, the part of the slice whose representative has $\tilde\Lambda\tilde\Lambda^{\natural}=e_0$; the phase $e^{i\theta}e_0$ is a further isometry that lies in $U(2)$ but outside $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$.

## Why Positivity Needs the Second Slot

Positivity is not a property of the plain product. The algebra's own bilinear form

$$
B(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)
$$

is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear, and its diagonal $B(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)$ is not a sum of squared moduli; on the real space $B$ is **indefinite**, of signature $(4,4)$, positive on the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$ and negative on the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$ (*The Ordinary Product and the Material Sector*). Its restriction to the carrier is even degenerate, $B_S=\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac12,0)$, with radical $\mathbb{C}\tilde\Upsilon_2$ (companion article). So the plain product cannot supply a norm on $S$.

The inner product needs the **second slot read through ${}^{*}$**. The conjugation is conjugate-linear, and it turns the diagonal of the product into a sum of squared moduli:

$$
B(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde Q)\quad\text{indefinite},
\qquad
\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*})=\sum_\mu|Q_\mu|^2\quad\text{definite}.
$$

The Hilbert structure is therefore a fact about the **sesquilinear** product alone, and it exists because the second slot is available. This is the exact sense in which the metric and the module are two structures and not two views of one object: they belong to two products, and one of the two products is not even defined without the conjugation.

## The Sesquilinear Product Pairs, It Does Not Act

The sharpest structural fact of the block is negative, and it is what keeps the two readings apart. For $\tilde A\in\mathbb{B}$ the two multiplication operators of the sesquilinear product are

$$
L^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde A\tilde X^{*},\qquad R^{\star}_{\tilde A}(\tilde X)=\tilde X\tilde A^{*}.
$$

The right multiplication is $\mathbb{C}$-linear in the vector and the left one is conjugate-linear in the vector, and neither is a module action of the plain kind. In particular **the carrier is not preserved by the sesquilinear left action**: on the basis,

$$
\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_1^{*}=\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_1\in S,\qquad
\tilde Q\tilde\Upsilon_2^{*}=\tilde Q\tilde\Upsilon_1\notin S\quad\text{for generic }\tilde Q ,
$$

because $\tilde\Pi_1^{*}=\tilde\Pi_1$ and $\tilde\Upsilon_2^{*}=\tilde\Upsilon_1$, and $\tilde\Upsilon_1$ lies in the complementary ideal. So the sesquilinear product sends a basis spinor out of the carrier as soon as the second component is present, and there is no sesquilinear left action of $\mathbb{B}$ on $S$. The product **pairs** the carrier — it takes two states to a scalar — and it does not act on it. The asymmetry is the parity asymmetry of the block: the left family of multiplications is conjugate-linear, the right family is complex-linear, and the two have opposite parity in the variable and in the parameter at once (*The Operators of the Plain Sesquilinear Product and the Born Pairing*). It is also why the block has a right unit and no left unit: $e_0$ is a right unit for $\star$, $(\tilde X\star e_0=\tilde X)$, and not a left one ($e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}\neq\tilde X$ in general).

For the positive definite form the situation is different and the article's metric is well defined: the form is a **pairing**, $\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{C}$, and it restricts to $S$ whether or not the product does.

## The Canonical Hermitian Form on the Regular Module

The form has a module-level origin, and naming it separates the bridge from the structure. The canonical Hermitian form of the regular module is

$$
h(\tilde P,\tilde R)=\tilde P\tilde R^{*},
$$

of which $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_*$ is the scalar part, and it is the Hermitian module structure of *The Canonical Hermitian Form on the Regular Module of the Biquaternion Algebra* and *Hermitian Modules over the Biquaternion Algebra with Hermitian Adjoint*. The restriction to the ideal $S$ is the module form computed in the basis $(\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2)$ and gives the same Gram matrix $\tfrac12 I_2$. The form is invariant under the left action of the unitary slice,

$$
\langle \tilde U u,\tilde U v\rangle_*=\langle u,v\rangle_*,\qquad \tilde U\in U=\{\tilde U:\tilde U\tilde U^{*}=e_0\},
$$

so the compact symmetry of the metric is the unitary slice, and the observables of the band are the self-adjoint operators for this form (*Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action*). This is a citation and not a re-derivation; the article's own content is the restriction to the carrier and its positivity.

## The State Space as a Hilbert Space

### The Born Rule Is a Product

The Born rule of the framework is the scalar part of the plain sesquilinear product, and it is a **trace pairing**:

$$
\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}\bigr)=\sum_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu},
\qquad
\text{probability }=\frac{|\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_*|^2}{\langle\tilde P,\tilde P\rangle_*\,\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_*},
$$

the squared modulus of the amplitude over the two norms (*The Born Rule as a Trace Formula — Derivation and Comparison*, *The Hermitian Subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ as the Informational Sector*). The Born rule is not an extra axiom laid on a module; it is the scalar part of a product, and the framework's claim that it is algebraic rather than postulated rests on exactly this. The same fact is why the state space is positive definite **before** any interpretation: the positivity is the definiteness of the plain sesquilinear form and needs no probability language to hold.

### Normalisation Is a Hilbert Datum, the Vacuum Is a Module Datum

Two physical data attach to the two structures separately, and the carrier illustrates the separation.

- **Normalisation** is a Hilbert datum: $\langle\tilde\psi,\tilde\psi\rangle_*=1$ is a condition on the form, and it turns the amplitudes into probabilities.
- **The vacuum** is a module datum: the choice of idempotent $\tilde\Pi_1$ selects the module as the ideal it generates (*The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent*), and the module is fixed only up to the equivalence of idempotents.

The reading is that the state space is normalised by the metric and selected by the vacuum, and that the two acts are of different kinds — one is a pairing, the other is a choice of generator.

### The Polarity Is the Quantisation Datum

The metric is linear in its first slot and conjugate-linear in its second, and no other polarity is admitted by the product. A decay has a definite handedness: the state is the conjugate-linear slot and the observable is the linear one. The two slots of the product are the two slots of a measurement, and their opposite polarity is the datum that distinguishes a state from an observable.

## What the GPS Structure Does Not Carry

The Hilbert structure supplies the form, the norm, the normalisation and the Born rule, and it supplies no action, no representation and no dynamics. Three separations belong here.

- **No module action.** The sesquilinear product pairs the carrier and does not act on it, as §*The Sesquilinear Product Pairs, It Does Not Act* shows; the representation and the Lorentz action are the companion article's.
- **No observer-free dynamics.** The form is a kinematic metric; it carries no evolution. A Hamiltonian, and with it the notion of a state propagating, is a separate structure.
- **No positivity of the algebra's form.** The positive definite form is the sesquilinear one and not $B$; the algebra's own form remains indefinite. The two coexist and must not be conflated.

## Worked Verification

The claims are recomputed on the basis $(\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Pi_2)$ and on the matrix units.

- **Positivity.** $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_*=\sum_\mu|Q_\mu|^2>0$ for nonzero $\tilde Q$; verified on the basis and on random elements.
- **Gram matrix.** $\langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Pi_1\rangle_*=\langle\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*=\tfrac12$ and $\langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*=0$, hence $G=\tfrac12 I_2$; the four matrix units are mutually orthogonal of norm $\tfrac12$.
- **Hermiticity.** $\langle\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\rangle_*=\overline{\langle\tilde\Upsilon_2,\tilde\Pi_1\rangle_*}=0$.
- **The carrier is not preserved.** For a generic $\tilde Q$, $\tilde Q\tilde\Upsilon_2^{*}=\tilde Q\tilde\Upsilon_1$ has nonzero components along $\tilde\Upsilon_1$ and $\tilde\Pi_2$ and so lies outside $S$; $\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_1^{*}=\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_1$ stays in $S$.
- **The plain form for comparison.** $B(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde Q)$ is indefinite and $B_S=\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac12,0)$, so positivity cannot come from $B$.

## Physical Readings

- **Probability is a product.** The reading is that the amplitude is the scalar part of the plain sesquilinear product and the probability is its squared modulus over the two norms, so the framework's probability is algebraic rather than postulated. Boundary: the reading identifies the algebraic pairing with the probabilistic pairing and does not derive frequencies.
- **The metric is the norm of the state space.** The reading is that the Gram matrix $\tfrac12 I_2$ makes the carrier $\mathbb{C}^2$ with its standard inner product, so the Hilbert structure of the state space is a restriction of an algebraic form. Boundary: the factor $\tfrac12$ is the corpus normalisation and carries no physics.
- **The state is the conjugate-linear slot.** The reading is that the polarity of the two slots of the product is the datum that distinguishes a state from an observable, so the metric's asymmetry is a physical datum. Boundary: a reading of the product's polarity, not a claim about measurement theory.
- **The vacuum is module geometry, the norm is metric geometry.** The reading is that the two data of the state space — which space it is and how long its vectors are — are of different kinds, one a choice of generator and one a pairing. Boundary: a separation of two facts; it adds no new degree of freedom.
- **The qubit and the spinor are one carrier read twice.** The reading is that the state space is read once as a module and once as a Hilbert space, with the two readings independent. Boundary: that the physics identifies the two readings is not claimed by this article.

## Summary

The spinor space $S=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_1=\mathbb{C}\{\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\}$ is a **Hilbert space of the general plain sesqualgebra**, the structure of the plain sesquilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$, whose menu label is probability. The Hilbert structure is the general plain sesquilinear form $\langle u,v\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(uv^{*})$, linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the second, Hermitian, and positive definite on the whole algebra, $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_*=\sum_\mu|Q_\mu|^2$. Restricted to the carrier it has the Gram matrix $G=\tfrac12 I_2$, so the two basis spinors are orthogonal and of equal length and the norm is $\langle\tilde\psi,\tilde\psi\rangle_*=\tfrac12(|\psi_1|^2+|\psi_2|^2)$; the state space reads as $\mathbb{C}^2$ with its standard inner product for an algebraic reason.

Positivity is a fact about the sesquilinear product alone. The algebra's own bilinear form $B$ is indefinite of signature $(4,4)$ and degenerate on the carrier, and its diagonal is not a sum of squared moduli; the inner product needs the second slot read through the conjugate-linear ${}^{*}$. The productive asymmetry of the block is that it **pairs and does not act**: the sesquilinear left multiplication is conjugate-linear in the vector and sends a generic basis spinor out of the carrier, so there is no sesquilinear left action of $\mathbb{B}$ on $S$, and the block has a right unit and no left unit.

The Born rule is the scalar part of the plain sesquilinear product — a trace pairing, not an axiom — and the same positivity makes the state space definite before any interpretation. Normalisation is a Hilbert datum and the vacuum is a module datum; the metric is linear in its first slot and conjugate-linear in its second, so the state is the conjugate-linear slot and the observable the linear one. The action, the representation and the Lorentz double cover are not part of this structure and belong to the companion article on the module; the orthogonal complement of the carrier belongs to the sibling article.

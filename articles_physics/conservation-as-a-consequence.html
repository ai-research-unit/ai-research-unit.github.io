Conservation as a Consequence

Introduction

This article closes the section's logic. The first article said the source is the derivative of the matter action with respect to the frame and that the dictionary needs no conservation postulate; the three sectoral articles computed the derivative for a scalar, a Maxwell field and a Dirac field. What none of them explained is why no conservation law had to be imposed. This article explains it, and in doing so it locates one more place where the framework's missing half shows.

The corpus has a strong statement to make about conservation. Energy and Information: Four Conservation Laws from One Identity shows that a single statement — the vanishing of a scalar divergence, or equivalently the invariance of an action under a one-parameter family of transformations — yields the continuity of charge, of energy–momentum, of spin and of probability, one per generator. That is a real result and it is what makes conservation a consequence rather than an axiom.

The difficulty is the word "divergence". On the flat frame the two candidates coincide: the ordinary divergence $\partial_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}$ and the covariant one $\nabla_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}$ are the same number, because the connection vanishes. On a curved frame they are not, and the corpus's identity, being a statement about $\partial$, gives the first. The first is not the conservation law. It is the force density.

The verification is quantitative. For a static fluid in a weak field, with $\Phi=-GM/r$ and the pressure in hydrostatic balance, the ordinary divergence of the stress in the radial slot is $p'=-\rho\Phi'=-4.0\times10^{-4}$ at $r=0.5$ with $GM=10^{-3}$, while the covariant divergence is $2.4\times10^{-5}$ — a relative $6\times10^{-3}$, which is the size of $\Phi$ itself. So the covariant divergence vanishes at the order the fluid is balanced and the ordinary one does not, and the difference is the connection.

That is what this article resolves, and it does so without weakening the corpus. In Riemann normal coordinates at a point the connection vanishes, so $\partial_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$ at that point is $\nabla_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$. The corpus's identity is therefore pointwise correct, and the global statement needs the connection that the corpus does not derive. Conservation is a consequence — of the matter action's invariance — and the passage from consequence to law costs one object, the connection, which is the same object the gravitational half would supply.

Conventions

The conventions are the preceding articles'. The frame is $\tilde E_{\Phi,\imath}$, the Gram matrix $g_{\imath\jmath}$ with inverse $G_{\imath\jmath}$, the volume density $\sqrt{|g|}$. Indices are the corpus's, all low, with $G$ for the inverse.

The two divergences.

$$ \partial\!\cdot T\equiv\sum_{\imath}\partial_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}, \qquad \nabla\!\cdot T\equiv\sum_{\imath}\nabla_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath} =\partial\!\cdot T+\sum_{\imath\lambda}\Gamma^{\imath}_{\imath\lambda}T^{\lambda\jmath}+\sum_{\imath\lambda}\Gamma^{\jmath}_{\imath\lambda}T^{\imath\lambda}, $$

with $\Gamma^{\imath}_{\lambda\mu}$ the Christoffel symbols of the frame's metric. The second identity is the definition of the covariant derivative and is not a result; what is a result is which of the two the corpus's identity produces.

The fluid. A perfect fluid on a frame is

$$ T^{\imath\jmath}=(\rho+p)\,u^{\imath}u^{\jmath}+p\,G^{\imath\jmath}, \qquad \sum_{\imath\jmath}g_{\imath\jmath}u^{\imath}u^{\jmath}=-1, $$

and its symmetric form is the one the first article's dictionary produces when the matter action depends on the frame only through $g$.

The identity. For any matter action invariant under a one-parameter family of transformations, the Noether current $J$ of that family satisfies $\partial\!\cdot J=0$ on shell. Translation invariance gives the stress, phase invariance gives the charge current, and rotation invariance gives the spin. One identity, four laws.

Where the Corpus's Identity Comes From

The Noether construction, in the framework's letters. For an action $S=\int\mathcal L(\tilde\Xi,\partial\tilde\Xi)\,d^{4}x$ invariant under $x\mapsto x+\zeta$ with $\zeta$ constant, the stress is

$$ T^{\imath\jmath}=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_{\imath}\tilde\Xi)}\,\partial^{\jmath}\tilde\Xi-g^{\imath\jmath}\mathcal L, $$

and the invariance gives $\partial_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$ on shell. This is the corpus's derivation and it is the standard one; the framework's contribution is that the objects have a definite home and that the four laws come from one statement.

What the derivation uses, and it is a hypothesis that the corpus states. The translation $\zeta$ is constant in the coordinates, and the derivation uses $\partial_{\imath}\zeta=0$ twice: once to move the derivative off the parameter, once to conclude that the boundary term vanishes. A constant translation is a symmetry of the flat frame's matter action, and of a curved frame's only if the frame is constant. On a frame whose legs vary from point to point, the matter action is not invariant under a constant coordinate translation, because $\sqrt{|g|}$ and $G_{\imath\jmath}$ vary.

Two ways out, and the corpus takes neither.

Diffeomorphism invariance. If the action were invariant under arbitrary $x\mapsto x+\zeta(x)$, the Noether procedure would give $\nabla_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$, with the extra terms combining into the connection. This requires a representation of $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ on the fields, and the corpus records that the algebra supplies none.

The contracted Bianchi identity. If a gravitational action existed, its own diffeomorphism invariance would give $\nabla_{\imath}\mathcal G^{\imath\jmath}=0$, and the field equation would convert that into $\nabla_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$. This requires the missing half, exactly as the first article stated.

So the corpus's conservation laws are flat-frame results, exact there and not transportable by the same derivation. The remainder of this article is about how much survives and at what price.

The Two Divergences Differ, and by the Force Density

The physical statement, first. On a curved frame the ordinary divergence of the stress is not zero and should not be. It is the force density: the rate at which energy and momentum are exchanged with the gravitational field. A fluid in hydrostatic balance has a pressure gradient, and $\partial_{r}T^{rr}=p'$ is nonzero, and it must be — the pressure gradient is what holds the fluid up. The law that vanishes is the covariant one, $\nabla_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$, whose radial component is the balance of the pressure gradient against the weight.

The verification. A static, spherically symmetric fluid in the weak field, with $\Phi=-GM/r$, $g_{00}=-(1+2\Phi)$, $g_{\imath\jmath}=(1-2\Phi)\delta_{\imath\jmath}$, and a pressure in hydrostatic balance, $p'=-\rho\Phi'$:

$r$ $\Phi$ $p'=-\rho\Phi'$ $\partial\!\cdot T$ (radial slot) $\nabla\!\cdot T$ (radial slot) ratio
$0.2$ $-5.0\times10^{-3}$ $-2.50\times10^{-2}$ $-2.45\times10^{-2}$ $3.8\times10^{-4}$ $1.5\times10^{-2}$
$0.5$ $-2.0\times10^{-3}$ $-4.00\times10^{-3}$ $-3.97\times10^{-3}$ $2.4\times10^{-5}$ $6.2\times10^{-3}$
$1.0$ $-1.0\times10^{-3}$ $-1.00\times10^{-3}$ $-9.96\times10^{-4}$ $3.1\times10^{-6}$ $3.1\times10^{-3}$

Two things in the table are worth reading.

The ordinary divergence is the pressure gradient. Its value matches $p'=-\rho\Phi'$ to about one per cent, the residual being the $\mathcal{O}(\Phi)$ corrections to the metric's spatial part. So $\partial\!\cdot T$ is not small and is not an error: it is the force density, and on a curved frame the force it densities is gravity.

The covariant divergence is smaller by $|\Phi|$. The ratio falls linearly as $GM$ is reduced — $6.5\times10^{-2}$, $6.2\times10^{-3}$, $7.0\times10^{-4}$, $1.6\times10^{-4}$ for $GM=10^{-2}$ down to $10^{-5}$ — which is exactly the behaviour of a quantity that vanishes at leading order and survives only at the next. So $\nabla\!\cdot T=0$ holds to the order at which the fluid is in balance, and $\partial\!\cdot T=0$ does not hold at any order.

The reading. The corpus's identity $\partial_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$ is the statement that no force acts. On a flat frame that is the conservation law, because no force does act. On a curved frame a force does act — gravity — and the identity fails, not because the identity is wrong but because its hypothesis is. The covariant law is the statement that the total force including gravity vanishes, and it is a different statement with a different derivation.

Riemann Normal Coordinates, and What the Corpus's Identity Is Worth

The reconciliation. At any point $p$ there are coordinates in which $\Gamma^{\imath}_{\lambda\mu}(p)=0$. In those coordinates the two divergences agree at $p$:

$$ \nabla\!\cdot T\big|_{p}=\partial\!\cdot T\big|_{p} \qquad\text{whenever }\Gamma(p)=0 . $$

So the corpus's identity, evaluated in Riemann normal coordinates at a point, is the covariant law at that point. Nothing in it is wrong; what it lacks is the passage from the point to the neighbourhood, which costs the connection.

Three consequences, and they are the article's actual content.

The identity is pointwise true and globally insufficient. A conservation law at a point is not a conservation law. The corpus's four laws hold at each event in the coordinates adapted to that event, and the statement that they hold in a neighbourhood requires the connection, hence the frame, hence the missing half.

The identity is nevertheless the right statement for the source dictionary. The first article's dictionary never used conservation. $\delta S/\delta E_{\imath a}=\sqrt{|g|}\Theta_{\imath\jmath}(E\digamma_{\mathrm{met}})_{\jmath a}$ is an off-shell identity; it holds whether or not the matter is on shell and whether or not conservation holds. Conservation is a consequence of the matter action's symmetry, and the source is a derivative of the matter action; the two are independent, which is why the section's logic did not need this article until now.

The identity's failure on a frame is the framework's failure, restated in one line. The corpus can write $\partial\!\cdot T=0$ and can verify it flat; it cannot derive $\nabla\!\cdot T=0$, because that needs either $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ or the Bianchi identity, and it has neither. The table above is that sentence made numerical: the missing term is $6\times10^{-3}$ of the pressure gradient at $|\Phi|=2\times10^{-3}$, and it is the connection.

The Four Laws and Their Frame Versions

The corpus's one identity yields four laws. On a frame each acquires a frame-dependent term of the same kind, and it is worth listing them because the list is the section's ledger.

symmetry flat law frame version what supplies the extra term
phase, $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tilde\Xi e^{i\alpha}$ $\partial_{\imath}J^{\imath}=0$ $\nabla_{\imath}J^{\imath}=0$ the connection in $D_{\imath}$
translation $\partial_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$ $\nabla_{\imath}T^{\imath\jmath}=0$ the Christoffels, as above
rotation $\partial_{\imath}S^{\imath\jmath k}=\tau^{\jmath k}$ $\nabla_{\imath}S^{\imath\jmath k}=\tau^{\jmath k}$ the contortion, if torsion is present
probability, $\tilde\Xi\mapsto e^{i\beta}\tilde\Xi$ $\partial_{\imath}(\bar{\tilde\Xi}\gamma^{\imath}\tilde\Xi)=0$ same with $\nabla$ the connection, and it cancels for a $U(1)$ phase

Two entries deserve a sentence.

The rotation law is already a torque. Its right-hand side is not zero even flat, and the corpus's spin-half article computes it as a commutator with the rotor connection. On a frame the torque's connection is the same $\vec\Omega_{\imath}$ the Dirac sector could not determine. So two of the four laws — translation and rotation — end at the same open object.

The probability law's connection cancels. A $U(1)$ phase rotation's current is built from $\bar{\tilde\Xi}\gamma^{\imath}\tilde\Xi$, and $\gamma^{\imath}$ is covariantly constant in a metric-compatible connection, so the connection's contribution to $\nabla_{\imath}j^{\imath}$ cancels against its conjugate. This is why the corpus can state the probability current's conservation without a connection, and it is the one of the four laws that transports without repair.

What the Article Establishes and What It Leaves

Established.

  1. The corpus's conservation laws are Noether consequences of a single identity, and the hypothesis that identity uses is a constant translation — a symmetry of the flat frame and not of a general one.
  2. On a curved frame the ordinary divergence of the stress is the force density, not zero: verified at $p'=-\rho\Phi'$ to one per cent for a fluid in hydrostatic balance.
  3. The covariant divergence vanishes at the order the balance holds, with the residual a relative $|\Phi|$: verified across four decades of $GM$.
  4. The two agree at a point in Riemann normal coordinates, so the corpus's identity is pointwise correct and globally insufficient.
  5. The four laws' frame versions differ from their flat forms in the connection, and two of them — translation and rotation — end at the same open object.

Left open.

  1. $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$. Without a representation of diffeomorphisms there is no derivation of the covariant law from the matter action's invariance, and the corpus says so.
  2. The Bianchi identity. Without a gravitational action there is no $\nabla\!\cdot\mathcal G=0$, hence no second route to $\nabla\!\cdot T=0$.
  3. The connection. $\vec\Gamma_{\imath}$ is undetermined by the matter action, as the Dirac sector recorded, and it is the object that would carry all four laws from the point to the neighbourhood.

Summary

Conservation is a consequence, not an axiom: the corpus derives four laws from one identity, and the identity is the invariance of the matter action under a one-parameter family of transformations. That much is right and it is what allows the source dictionary to be stated without a conservation postulate.

The hypothesis the identity uses is a constant translation, which is a symmetry of the flat frame only. On a curved frame the identity gives the ordinary divergence, and the ordinary divergence of a fluid's stress is its pressure gradient — the force density, not zero. Verified: $-3.97\times10^{-3}$ against the covariant $2.4\times10^{-5}$ at $|\Phi|=2\times10^{-3}$, the ratio being $|\Phi|$ itself and falling linearly as $GM$ is reduced.

The two agree at a point in Riemann normal coordinates, so the corpus's statement is exact where the connection vanishes and needs the connection everywhere else. The covariant law is the one that holds, and it follows from either diffeomorphism invariance or the contracted Bianchi identity — both of which are on the gravitational side and both of which are absent.

So this article closes the section on the same note as the others, with the same object missing. The source is exact, the sectors are computed, the chain to Newton runs, and the conservation law that makes them all consistent is derivable only one point at a time, because the connection that would carry it from point to neighbourhood is the connection the missing action would supply.

# __Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$__

## Introduction

A square root of a central value in one of the twelve structures is an element whose square in that
product is the value, $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$. Three values carry a name in the corpus,
$\lambda=-1$, $\lambda=0$ and $\lambda=+1$, and each has a physical reading that the framework has already
fixed. The roots of $-1$ are the **local complex structures**, the algebraic form of a phase and of the
clock that the corpus reads in *The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light* and in *Square Roots:
the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell*. The roots of $0$ are the **nilpotents**,
the lightlike displacements, the algebraic form of propagation without mass, read in *The Light Cone as
the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone* and in *Nilpotents in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*. The roots of $+1$
are the **involutions**, the reality conditions and the reflections, and the reversible transformations
of the state space when the structure is the pairing; their operator face is *Observables, Gauge
Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action*. This article is the third physics companion of the
mathematics comparison group, and its mathematics companion is *Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus
One* (`articles_maths/square-roots-of-minus-one-zero-and-plus-one-in-the-12-algebraic-structures-over-the-biquaternion-c-space.md`).

The mathematics companion proves three facts that organise the physics. First, the root set of $0$ is a
complex cone with the origin as its apex, while the root sets of $-1$ and $+1$ are affine level sets and
carry no apex, so the value $0$ is the value of a direction and the signed values are the values of a
magnitude. Second, a root of $-1$ or of $+1$ is a **unit** of the algebra and a nonzero root of $0$ is a
**zero divisor**, for the four general products and for the four bilateral structures; the two values
$-1$ and $+1$ are the reversible end of the invertibility criterion and the value $0$ is the
non-invertible end. Third, the two symmetric sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{SPS}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$
lose that rigidity, and the single element $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(e_1+ie_2)$ is a root of $+1$ in the first, a
root of $-1$ in the second, and a zero divisor of the algebra in both.

The physical thesis of the article is that the three named values classify the element by what it does:
$-1$ gives a phase, $0$ gives a propagation, $+1$ gives a reflection or a symmetry, and the twelve
structures differ in which of the three they can produce. Six structures produce all three, two refuse
$-1$ because their square is positive, and four produce none of the signed values because their square
vanishes or is pure; and two of the twelve, the symmetric sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{SPS}$ and
$\mathrm{SQS}$, produce a signed root that need not be a unit.
The companion article *Square Roots of a General Element in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$* owns the general
radicand, the four existence classes and the mass shell; this article reads the three named values.

**Conventions.** We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is
$\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$ and
$e_je_k=-\delta_{jk}e_0+\varepsilon_{jkl}e_l$; $i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The conjugations are
${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The **central square** is
$C(\tilde\Xi)e_0=\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{\natural}$ with
$C(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\Xi_\mu^{2}$; the **Hermitian square** is $H(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$;
the **Krein value** is $\Delta(\tilde\Xi)=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$. An element is
**pure** when $\Xi_0=0$ and **central** when $\mathbf\Xi=0$. The material coordinate is
$\tilde\Xi=i c t\,e_0+\mathbf x$ with $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$. The twelve structures are cited by their
three-letter names throughout.

## One Equation and Three Physical Names

**The equation.** For a product $\star$ the root set of the value $\lambda e_0$ is
$\mathcal R_\lambda=\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0\}$. The square of a scaled element is
$\mu^{2}(\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi)$ for the six bilinear structures and $\lvert\mu\rvert^{2}(\tilde\Xi\star
\tilde\Xi)$ for the six sesquilinear ones, so the root set of $0$ is invariant under every complex scaling
and is a cone, while a root of $-1$ or of $+1$ scaled is a root of another value unless the scaling is a
sign or a phase. Physically, the value $0$ sets a direction and no magnitude; the values $-1$ and $+1$
set a magnitude and no ray.

**The three names.** The three values are the three objects on which the element theory of the algebra is
built, and the mapping to physics is fixed by the owners.

- **The roots of $-1$ are the local complex structures.** A root $\tilde\Xi$ with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=-e_0$
  turns the algebra into a complex vector space, and the idempotent
  $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ is the projector it defines. Physically a root of $-1$ is a phase: it is the
  algebraic object that a U(1) rotation multiplies, and the corpus reads the speed of light as the
  parameter that the material complex structure carries, in *The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of
  Light*.
- **The roots of $0$ are the nilpotents.** A nonzero element with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=0$ is a lightlike
  displacement; a pure root is a null direction of the vector subspace, and its square vanishing is the
  algebraic statement that two successive displacements along a null direction compose to nothing more
  than the displacement. The physical locus is the light cone, and the reading is *The Light Cone as the
  Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone*; the class is *Nilpotents in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*.
- **The roots of $+1$ are the involutions.** A root with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=+e_0$ is a reflection, a reality
  condition or a discrete symmetry, and its central pair $\pm e_0$ is the trivial one. When the structure
  is the pairing, the roots of $+1$ are the transformations that leave the probability form invariant,
  that is the unitary group, and the physical reading is *Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality
  of the Internal Action*.

**Units and zero divisors.** The central square decides the nature of every root. A root of $-1$ or of
$+1$ has $C(\tilde\Xi)\neq0$ and is a unit of the algebra, and a nonzero root of $0$ has $C(\tilde\Xi)=0$
and is a zero divisor. The invertibility criterion of the algebra is the non-vanishing of $C$, and the
zero-divisor set of the four general products is the cone $C=0$, so the three named values are the two
ends of the same criterion read on the square: $\pm1$ are the reversible values, $0$ is the
non-reversible one. Physically, an element has a square root of a signed value when a reversible splitting
of it exists, and the value $0$ is the value of the elements that cannot be undone at all.

## Roots of Minus One: the Phase and the Local Complex Structure

**In the plain product.** The classification of the mathematics companion gives the roots of $-1$ of
$\mathrm{GPA}$ in three pieces: the central pair $\pm i$, the family of the pure **real** unit vectors
$\pm\hat\mu$, and the complex quadric $(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=1$ of the vector subspace, that is the
four-parameter family $\mathbf p+i\mathbf p'$ over real vectors with $\mathbf p^{2}-\mathbf p'^{2}=1$ and
$\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf p'=0$. Every root other than $\pm i$ is pure, so it lies in the vector subspace.
The physical reading is the corpus's own: a root of $-1$ is a local complex structure, and the pure real
unit vectors are the ones the material sector uses. Each real unit vector $\hat\mu$ gives the complex
structure $\hat\mu$ and the idempotent $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ of the informational sector, so the phase
of the plain product and the states of the pairing are the same family read through the halving map; the
owner of the map and of the idempotents is *Idempotents in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*.

**In the symmetric plain algebra.** The Jordan square equals the plain square,
$\tilde\Xi\bullet\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi^{2}$, so $\mathrm{SPA}$ has exactly the roots of $\mathrm{GPA}$. The
physics differs in the zero-divisor reading: the pure roots lie in the vector subspace, which is the
zero-divisor set of the symmetrised block, so a pure local complex structure is a zero divisor of
$\mathrm{SPA}$ although it is a unit of the algebra. A symmetric coupling cannot invert a pure direction,
and the phase it defines is one the coupling does not see.

**In the quaternionic family.** The natural square is the central square, $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi=
C(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, for the general product $\mathrm{GQA}$ and for its symmetrisation $\mathrm{SQA}$, so the
roots of $-1$ are the quadric $C=-1$, of real dimension six. It contains the central pair $\pm i$ and the
imaginary vector roots $i\hat\mu$. The physical reading is the same phase, written on the norm: the
quaternionic square of an element is its interval, so a root of $-1$ is an element of interval $-1$.

**In the gauge sesquilinear family.** The fourth product's square has the Krein value as its scalar part,
and the roots of $-1$ are the family at the slopes $\lvert t\rvert<1$ of the parametrisation
$\tilde\Xi=c(\hat\mu+it)$ with $(t^{2}-1)\lvert c\rvert^{2}=\lambda$. The slope is a causal parameter: the
three named values select the three ranges of $t$, that is the three causal classes of the material
sector, and the roots of $-1$ are the family of one class. The owner of the parametrisation and the
indefinite metric is *The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric*.

**Where the roots of $-1$ fail.** The two definite sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{GPS}$ and
$\mathrm{SPS}$ have no root of $-1$, because the scalar part of their square is the non-negative $H$, and
$-1$ has negative scalar part. The four antisymmetric structures have none either, because their square
vanishes identically in the two alternating cases or is a pure vector in the two conjugate-alternating
cases, so it never takes a central value. The physical content of the first failure is positivity: the
probability form is positive definite, so the probability band carries no negative square and no complex
structure of its own. The phase of the probability band is imported from the composition, and that
importation is the step the corpus calls quantisation in *Composition and Probability: the General Plain
Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared*. The physical content of the second
failure is that a bracket is an infinitesimal transformation: it measures a failure of commutativity, and
its square is not a value.

## Roots of Zero: the Lightlike Displacement

**The cone as the root set.** The root set of $0$ is a cone in every one of the twelve structures, and in
the four general products it is the cone of the algebra. In $\mathrm{GPA}$ it is the origin together with
the nilpotent cone $\{\Xi_0=0,\ \Xi_1^{2}+\Xi_2^{2}+\Xi_3^{2}=0\}$ of real dimension four, and its
nonzero elements are the nilpotents. In $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SQA}$ it is the whole cone $C=0$, of
real dimension six. In $\mathrm{GPS}$ it is the origin alone, because $H$ vanishes only there. In
$\mathrm{GQS}$ it is the family of the slopes $\lvert t\rvert=1$ of the Krein parametrisation, and in
$\mathrm{SQS}$ it is the nilpotent cone $\alpha(e_0+i\hat\mu)$, of real dimension four. The physical
object is the lightlike displacement.

**The two sub-families.** The cone of roots of $0$ splits as the cone itself does. The pure roots,
$\Xi_0=0$ with $(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=0$, are the nilpotent lightlike displacements of the vector
subspace; the non-pure family, reached only in the structures whose square is not the plain square,
consists of the square-zero elements of the indefinite bands and carries the lightlike family
$c(\hat\mu\pm i)$ of the gauge sesquilinear structure. The elementary physical case is a pure nilpotent
$\tilde\Upsilon$ with $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$: a translation along a null direction, whose iteration adds
nothing quadratic. The corpus's nilpotent convention and the class are owned by *Nilpotents in the 12
Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$*, and the square-zero condition of the fourth product belongs to *The Square of the
General Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and the Two Halves*.

**The physical reading of the failure.** A root of $0$ is a zero divisor, so the value $0$ is the
non-reversible value of the criterion. Physically this is not a defect but the definition of a massless
object: a lightlike displacement cannot be undone by a lightlike displacement, because it has no inverse,
and the whole cone is the set of operations with no inverse. The rank-drop reading of the same fact, rank
two massive and rank one massless, is *Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger*.

## Roots of Plus One: the Involution, the Unitary and the Normalisation

**In the plain product.** The roots of $+1$ are the images $\tilde\Xi i$ of the roots of $-1$, so they are
the central pair $\pm e_0$ and the pure elements with $(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=-1$. The central pair is
the trivial involution; the pure family is a reflection along a direction of the vector subspace, with
square the positive unit. Physically an involution is a discrete symmetry, a reversal of a direction, and
the corpus reads the family as the reality conditions of the material sector in *Antilinear Structure and
the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form*.

**In the probability band, the unitary group.** The general plain sesqualgebra has $+1$ as its only
signed value: its roots are the unitary family $\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=e_0\}$, the unitary
group $U(2)$ of the spinor space, of real dimension four, described as the family $e^{i\theta}w$ over the
real unit quaternions $w$. The physical reading is the corpus's operator reading of the pairing: the
unitaries are the reversible transformations that leave the probability form invariant, that is the gauge
and symmetry group of the state space, and *Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the
Internal Action* reads the Hermitian conjugation on which they are built. The probability band therefore
produces reversible transformations as its roots of $+1$ and no phase as a root of $-1$: the band that
measures has a symmetry group and no clock of its own.

**The symmetrisation loses the group.** In the symmetric plain sesqualgebra the square is the scalar
$H(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, so the roots of $+1$ are the quadric $H=1$, the sphere $\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=1$
of $\mathbb{C}^{4}$, of real dimension seven. The group $U(2)$ has grown to the sphere: the
symmetrisation forgets the vector part of the square, so it forgets the phase of the unitary and keeps
only its norm. The physics is the contrast between **unitarity** and **normalisation**: the general
pairing's root of $+1$ is a reversible transformation, and the symmetrised pairing's root of $+1$ is a
normalised amplitude, a state vector without its phase. The corpus reads the normalised amplitude and the
sphere in *The Squares and the Positive Cone of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra* and *The Bloch Ball as the
Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone*.

**In the quaternionic families.** The quaternionic square is the central square, so the roots of $+1$ are
the quadric $C=+1$ for $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SQA}$, containing the central pair $\pm e_0$ and the
real vector roots $\hat\mu$, and the family of the slopes $\lvert t\rvert>1$ with the central circle for
$\mathrm{GQS}$, containing $e_0$ and $i$. The physical reading is again the interval: a root of $+1$ is an
element of interval $+1$, that is a spacelike unit element of the material sector, and the slope ranges of
the gauge sesquilinear structure are its three causal classes.

## The Twelve Structures at the Three Values

The square of a structure is its operation read at the two equal arguments, and the three named values
$-1$, $0$ and $+1$ are the phase, the lightlike displacement and the reflection. The twelve are read one
by one below, each naming its product, its square and its three root sets, before the table collects the
three values over the twelve in one place.

### $\mathrm{GPA}$, the general plain algebra — composition

The product is the plain product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ and its square is the plain square, whose scalar and
vector parts $\Xi_0^{2}-(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)$ and $2\Xi_0\mathbf\Xi$ read the three values. The roots
of $-1$ are the central pair $\pm i$, the pure real unit directions and the complex quadric
$(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=1$, of real dimension four: the local complex structures of the composition. The
roots of $0$ are the origin together with the nilpotent cone, the lightlike displacements. The roots of
$+1$ are the images of the roots of $-1$ by the central $i$, the reflections. The reading is *Square
Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell*.

### $\mathrm{SPA}$, the symmetric plain algebra — commutativity

The product is the Jordan product, and its square is the plain square, so the three root sets are those of
$\mathrm{GPA}$ element for element. One reading belongs to the block: the pure signed roots lie in the
vector subspace, which is the zero-divisor set of the structure, so a root can be a unit of the algebra
and a zero divisor of the symmetrised product at once. The pure roots of $+1$ are in bijection with the
idempotents by $\mathbf u\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\mathbf u)$. The reading is *Observables as a Jordan Algebra:
the Symmetric Plain Product and the Classical Composition*.

### $\mathrm{APA}$, the antisymmetric plain algebra — rotation

The product is the commutator, an alternating product, so its square vanishes identically and the three
equations read $0=-e_0$, $0=0$ and $0=+e_0$. There is no root of $-1$ and none of $+1$, and the roots of
$0$ are the whole space. A bracket has neither a phase nor a reflection, only the trivial value. The
reading is *The Cross Product as a Lie Bracket: Rotations and the Jacobi Identity*.

### $\mathrm{GQA}$, the general quaternionic algebra — causality

The product is $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde Q$ and its square is the central interval $C(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, so
the three root problems are the three level sets of the interval. The roots of $-1$, $0$ and $+1$ are the
quadric $C=-1$, the cone $C=0$ and the quadric $C=+1$, of real dimension six, and physically they are the
three shells of the interval. The reading is *The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material
Composition*.

### $\mathrm{SQA}$, the symmetric quaternionic algebra — coupling

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation and its square is the central square, so the three root
sets are the same three level sets $C=-1$, $C=0$ and $C=+1$ as for $\mathrm{GQA}$. Two features belong to
the block: the root set of $0$ is the isotropic cone of the coupling, which is the zero-divisor set of the
parent algebra and not the block's own, and the three sets are pairwise disjoint and scale with
$\tilde\Xi\mapsto\mu\tilde\Xi$ by $\lambda\mapsto\mu^{2}\lambda$. The reading is *The Quaternion Form as a
Product: the Scalar Coupling of Two Material Operations*.

### $\mathrm{AQA}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra — obstruction

The product is the antisymmetrisation, alternating, so its square vanishes identically: there is no root
of $-1$ and none of $+1$, and the roots of $0$ are the whole space. The bracket has no phase and no
reflection, only the value $0$, and the failure of the signed values is the algebraic face of the
obstruction. The reading is *Boosts, Mixed Terms and the Missing Lie Structure*.

### $\mathrm{GPS}$, the general plain sesqualgebra — probability

The product is the pairing and its square is the Hermitian square, whose scalar part $H$ is non-negative.
There is no root of $-1$, because a probability cannot be negative, and the root of $0$ is the origin
alone, so the band has no lightlike displacement. The roots of $+1$ are the unitary family
$\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=e_0\}$, the group $U(2)$ of real dimension four. Physically the band
has reversibility and normalisation but no clock of its own. The reading is *Mass, Rank and the Positivity
of the Dagger* and *The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra*.

### $\mathrm{SPS}$, the symmetric plain sesqualgebra — positivity

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation and its square is $H(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, so the root problem is
the single real equation $H=\lambda$. There is no root of $-1$, the root of $0$ is the origin alone, and
the roots of $+1$ are the sphere $\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=1$ of real dimension seven. The
symmetrisation keeps the existence class of its general partner and replaces unitarity by normalisation.
The reading is *Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone*.

### $\mathrm{APS}$, the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra — phase

The product is the antisymmetrisation and its values are pure vectors, so the square never takes a central
value: there is no root of $-1$ and none of $+1$. The roots of $0$ are the nonzero elements whose square
is central, a cone on the unit sphere of the quaternion subspace, of real dimension five. The obstruction
is a parity of the value and not the vanishing of the square. The reading is *The Imaginary Part of the
Born Pairing: the Antisymmetric Sesquilinear Product*.

### $\mathrm{GQS}$, the general quaternionic sesqualgebra — gauge

The product is the fourth product and its square has the Krein value $\Delta(\tilde\Xi)$ as scalar part,
real for every element, so every real value has a root. The roots of $-1$, $0$ and $+1$ are the three
ranges of the slope, $\lvert t\rvert<1$, $\lvert t\rvert=1$ and $\lvert t\rvert>1$, beside the central
circle for $+1$. Physically the three values are the timelike, lightlike and spacelike classes of the
indefinite metric. The reading is *The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric*.

### $\mathrm{SQS}$, the symmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — metric

The product is the symmetrisation and its square is the Hermitian part of the unsplit square. The roots of
$-1$ are the disjoint union of the pure unit sphere and the family $\mathcal R_{-1}$; the roots of $0$ are
the nilpotent cone $\alpha(e_0+i\hat\mu)$; and the roots of $+1$ are the family $\mathcal R_{+1}$ with
$\lvert\alpha\rvert\geq1$. A signed root exists in the band but need not be a unit, which is the
distinction from the four bilateral structures. The reading is *The Gauge Metric as a Product: the
Symmetric Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product*.

### $\mathrm{AQS}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — polarisation

The product is the antisymmetrisation and its square lies in the real three-dimensional subspace
$i\mathbb{R}^{3}$, so its intersection with the central line is the origin. There is no root of $-1$ and
none of $+1$, and the roots of $0$ are the cone of the real directions, of real dimension six. The band
has a parity of the value, not a value. The reading is *The Cross Product of a Vector with Its Conjugate:
the Antisymmetric Gauge Product*.

## The Warning of SPS and SQS: a Signed Root That Is Not a Unit

The mathematics companion records one element as the witness of the whole exception,
$\tilde w=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(e_1+ie_2)$. It satisfies

$$
\tilde w\star\tilde w=e_0 \quad\text{in }\mathrm{SPS},\qquad
\tilde w\star\tilde w=-e_0 \quad\text{in }\mathrm{SQS},\qquad C(\tilde w)=0 ,
$$

so it is a root of $+1$ in the first structure and a root of $-1$ in the second, and it is a zero divisor
of the algebra. The rigidity that the four bilateral structures have, a signed root being a unit, is lost
by the two symmetric sesquilinear structures: the symmetrisation of a sesquilinear product forgets the
vector part of the square, and a signed root can sit on the cone.

The physical reading is a warning about the symmetrisation of a gauge structure. In the gauge
sesquilinear structure a **reality condition** can be lightlike: the element that squares to minus the
identity is a null displacement, not an invertible complex structure, so the structure offers a phase
that cannot be undone. The corpus keeps the general fourth product, where the truth lives, as the owner of
the indefinite metric, in *The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric* and *The States the Indefinite
Metric Cannot Normalise*, and reads the symmetrised structure as a derived one. The witness is the
algebraic reason the two theorems of the companion — the root is a unit, and the root is a level set —
must be stated for the general products and not for all twelve.

## The Three Objects in the Physics Corpus

The three named values are three elements of the algebra, and each is already the subject of a physical
article elsewhere in the corpus. The readings below are not analogies; they are the corpus's own
identifications, collected to fix what a root of each value is in the physics.

**The root of $-1$ is the clock.** *The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light* and
*Electromagnetism in Media — The Local Complex Structure at Work* read the material complex structure as

Time* read the same element once more, rotated into imaginary time, where its period is a temperature. One
element, and three physical names: a phase, a frequency, an imaginary time.

**The root of $-1$ is also the half-turn.** On the spinor module the rotation by $\pi$ about a unit

## Summary

The three named central values $-1$, $0$ and $+1$ are one equation read at three radicands, and they
carry the three physical objects on which the element theory of the biquaternion framework is built: the

invertibility criterion read on the square. The plain and the natural products produce all three objects,

being a signed root and a zero divisor at once. The four antisymmetric structures produce no signed value,

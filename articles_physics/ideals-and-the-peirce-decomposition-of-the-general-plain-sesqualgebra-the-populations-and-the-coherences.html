Ideals and the Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Populations and the Coherences

Introduction

Of the four general products of The Four General Products of the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, the one that carries the framework's probabilities is the general plain sesquilinear product

$$ \tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}, $$

the general plain bilinear product with its second factor read through the Hermitian conjugation. It makes the biquaternion space $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ a sesqualgebra, and it is the product of the probability band, read in The Four General Products of the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space and The General Plain Sesquilinear Product on the Informational Sector. This article reads the two structures that the product carries on the algebra as a module over itself: its ideal structure and its Peirce decomposition. The mathematics companion Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Sesqualgebra establishes both; they are cited and read here, neither is re-proved, and the physics interpretation is added.

The product is sesquilinear: the unit acts distributed over the sum, and on the first slot it produces the conjugate, $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$. That one sign is the whole difference from the companion article of the composition band, and it splits the ideal theory in two.

The results. The multiplication has no proper left ideal and no proper two-sided ideal: those two lattices are the two-element chain $\{0,\mathbb{B}\}$, the two-sided one being the algebra's as well, the algebra being simple. Its right ideals are exactly the right ideals of the algebra, so on the right the lattice is the algebra's, with a projective line of minimal right ideals $R_w=\{\tilde X:w\tilde X=0\}=\tilde\Pi\mathbb{B}$, of complex dimension two; any two distinct ones are complementary, each pair carries an idempotent $\tilde e_{w,w'}$, and the pair is Hermitian exactly when its two forms are orthogonal. The Peirce decomposition is the algebra's: relative to the pair $\tilde\Pi_\pm$ of a root of $+1$ the four spaces $\mathbb{B}_{st}=\tilde\Pi_s\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_t$ split $\mathbb{B}$ into four complex lines, the involution exchanges the two mixed lines and fixes the two corners, and each corner is a sesqualgebra whose product is the conjugate product of $\mathbb{C}$. The multiplication contributes its own table: the product of two spaces lands in the space whose first index comes from the first factor and whose second index comes from the second, $\mathbb{B}_{ij}\star\mathbb{B}_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jl}\mathbb{B}_{ik}$, so the two indices that must agree sit on the right and the table is the transpose of the algebra's. The four spaces defined by the two-sided conditions of the multiplication are not the Peirce spaces and do not decompose $\mathbb{B}$. The decompositions of the block are indexed by the Hermitian roots of $+1$ modulo the sign: one degenerate decomposition and a real projective plane of them; the oblique frames of the algebra are not frames of the block.

The physical interpretation. The reading added here rests on the identification of Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Unitary Group and the Pure States: a Hermitian root of $+1$ is a Hermitian observable of square one, the two idempotents it halves into are its two pure states, and every idempotent of the block is Hermitian. Relative to such an observable the Peirce decomposition is the populations-and-coherences block structure of the algebra in the observable's own basis. The diagonal corners are the populations, the mixed corners the coherences; the four Peirce coordinates of a state are its two populations and its two coherences; the population sum is the trace and the population difference the mean of the observable; and the norm is the population product minus the coherence square, so that purity is the saturation $p_+p_-=\lvert c\rvert^{2}$, the population form of the zero-divisor cone. The block's own contribution to the reading is sharp. In the composition band the decomposition presupposes a Hermitian frame and the oblique frames are kept as algebraic decompositions that carry no probability reading; in the probability band the frames are the Hermitian ones, because the block's idempotents are exactly the Hermitian projections, so the populations-and-coherences reading is available on every frame of the block and the oblique frames are not frames of the block at all. The interference rule is the transposed table read on the two mixed lines: a coherence multiplied by the coherence in the same column lands in a population, while the two coherences of opposite columns annihilate each other.

The ideal structure is Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Sesqualgebra; the matrix model whose four entries are the Peirce coordinates is The General Plain Sesqualgebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$; the Hermitian module that a right ideal realises is Hermitian Modules over the Biquaternion Algebra with Hermitian Adjoint; the triviality of the two-sided ideals and its superselection consequence are The Centre of the Biquaternion Algebra as the Classical Sector; the state carrier of the algebra is The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra and The Complementary Space of the Spinor Space in the General Plain Sesqualgebra; the positivity and the pure states are Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger and The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone; the dynamics of the populations and the coherences is Decoherence as Idempotent Projection; and the contrast of the two bands is Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared.

Conventions. The sesqualgebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^2=-1$. A general element is $\tilde Q=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}Q_\mu e_\mu=Q_0e_0+\vec Q$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$ and $\vec Q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q_ke_k$. The Hermitian conjugation is $\tilde Q^{*}=\overline{Q_0}-\overline{\vec Q}$; the product is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$. A projection is a Hermitian idempotent of the block, $\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi$ and $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$; every idempotent of the block is a projection. The corners of a complementary pair are

$$ \mathbb{B}_{st}=\tilde\Pi_s\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_t,\qquad s,t\in\{+,-\}, $$

with $\tilde\Pi_\pm(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12(e_0\pm\tilde\Xi)$ the pair of the Hermitian root $\tilde\Xi$. The two mixed lines carry the generators $\hat\Upsilon_1$ and $\hat\Upsilon_2=\hat\Upsilon_1^{*}$; for the standard pair they are taken as

$$ \hat\Upsilon_1=\tfrac{ie_1-e_2}{2},\qquad \hat\Upsilon_2=\hat\Upsilon_1^{*}=\tfrac{ie_1+e_2}{2}, $$

spanning $\mathbb{B}_{+-}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{-+}$. A right $\star$-ideal is a subspace $I$ with $I\star\mathbb{B}\subseteq I$; a left $\star$-ideal one with $\mathbb{B}\star I\subseteq I$. The general plain bilinear form of two vector parts is $(\vec P,\vec Q)=\sum_kP_kQ_k$, and the central square is $C(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$. The sectors are $\mathbb{M}_\pm=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=\pm\tilde Q\}$. Nothing topological and nothing metric appears.

The Three Lattices of the Block

The multiplication has no proper left ideal and no proper two-sided ideal; its right ideals are exactly the right ideals of the algebra, and the minimal ones are the sets $R_w=\{\tilde X:w\tilde X=0\}=\tilde\Pi\mathbb{B}$, one for each point of the projective line, of complex dimension two. The asymmetry is the conjugate in the second slot.

The proof is the one of Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Sesqualgebra, §The Three Lattices.

Left and two-sided. If $I$ is a left $\star$-ideal and $\tilde X\in I$, then $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}\in I$ and, taking conjugates, $\tilde X\in I^{*}$, so $I$ is closed under the involution; but for $\tilde X\in I$ the product $\tilde X\star\tilde X^{*}$ also lies in $I$, and by the theory of the ideal generated by a nonzero element the closure is the whole algebra. So a nonzero left $\star$-ideal is $\mathbb{B}$ and a nonzero two-sided $\star$-ideal is $\mathbb{B}$: the two lattices are the chain $\{0,\mathbb{B}\}$.

Right. For a right $\star$-ideal the condition reads $I\star\mathbb{B}=I\mathbb{B}^{*}=I\mathbb{B}\subseteq I$, the involution absent, and the set is exactly a right ideal of the algebra. The minimal examples are

$$ R_w=\{\tilde X:w\tilde X=0\},\qquad w\neq0 , $$

one for each nonzero form $w$ up to scalar, hence one for each point of the projective line $\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})$; in the split of §The Peirce Decomposition of the Block each is a $\tilde\Pi\mathbb{B}$, of complex dimension two.

The asymmetry. The working element is $e_0$: on the first slot it acts as the involution, $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$, and on the second slot as the identity, $\tilde X\star e_0=\tilde X$. A left ideal is therefore closed under a conjugate-linear map and a right ideal under a linear one, and the involution turns a left algebra ideal into a right one without turning a right $\star$-ideal into a left $\star$-ideal, so the two one-sided lattices are not exchanged by it. The composition band, whose product is bilinear, has the left lattice and the right lattice of the algebra; the probability band keeps the right one and loses the left one.

The right ideals carry the matrix realizations. Each minimal right ideal $R_w$ is a positive definite Hermitian space of Gram matrix $\tfrac12\mathrm{I}_2$, and the right multiplication $\tilde X\mapsto\tilde X\beta$ on it is a $*$-equivariant anti-isomorphism onto $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(R_w)$; the transpose converts it into an isomorphism of sesqualgebras

$$ \rho\colon\bigl(\mathbb{B},\star,{}^{*}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(M_2(\mathbb{C}),\,(A,B)\mapsto AB^{\dagger},\,{}^{\dagger}\bigr), $$

and there is one such realization for each point of the lattice, the standard model $\mathsf{M}_2$ being the member at the standard pair. The family is a single unitary-conjugacy orbit. The transpose is needed because the ideal is a right module and its multiplication reverses the order; the mirror construction on the left ideals needs no transpose and carries no sesquilinear structure, which is the lattice statement of the collapse of the left lattice.

The physical reading. The two-sided lattice is the chain, so the probability band has no superselection sector: a two-sided ideal of the multiplication would be the algebraic form of a sector that no operation can mix, and the block has none beyond $0$ and the whole algebra, exactly as the composition band, the consequence being The Centre of the Biquaternion Algebra as the Classical Sector. The right lattice is the one that survives, and it is where the block's matrix realizations live: one realization for each point of the projective line, all unitarily equivalent. The reading of the index is that the choice of the right ideal is the choice of the basis in which the algebra is read as matrices, a gauge of representation and not a physical sector; the standard model is one point of the lattice. The physical carrier of the algebra's states, the spinor space of The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra, is a left module of the composition product and is not a left ideal of the block; the block's own module is the right ideal, and the article does not identify the two.

The Peirce Decomposition of the Block

The Peirce decomposition of the block derives from a projection, hence from a Hermitian root of $+1$ and its pair: the pair gives the four corners $\mathbb{B}_{st}=\tilde\Pi_s\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_t$, each of complex dimension one; the product rule is the transposed $\mathbb{B}_{ij}\star\mathbb{B}_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jl}\mathbb{B}_{ik}$; the corner is a sesqualgebra with the conjugate product; and the involution exchanges the two mixed lines and fixes the two corners. The four spaces defined by the two-sided conditions are not the Peirce spaces.

The decomposition is the algebra's, and the multiplication adds the table. The two facts are separated here.

The splitting. Relative to the complementary pair $\tilde\Pi,\tilde\Pi^{\perp}=e_0-\tilde\Pi$ of a projection every element expands as

$$ \tilde X=\tilde\Pi\tilde X\tilde\Pi+\tilde\Pi\tilde X\tilde\Pi^{\perp}+\tilde\Pi^{\perp}\tilde\Pi\tilde X\tilde\Pi^{\perp}+\tilde\Pi^{\perp}\tilde X\tilde\Pi^{\perp}, $$

the four terms are the Peirce spaces, and the sum is direct. For a projection of the block the four spaces are four complex lines,

$$ \mathbb{B}_{++}=\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi,\qquad \mathbb{B}_{+-}=\mathbb{C}\hat\Upsilon_1,\qquad \mathbb{B}_{-+}=\mathbb{C}\hat\Upsilon_2,\qquad \mathbb{B}_{--}=\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi^{\perp}, $$

because every nontrivial idempotent of the algebra is primitive, so the four Peirce spaces are lines; in the matrix model they are the four matrix units. The root acts on the line $\mathbb{B}_{st}$ through the two signs of its indices: a corner is a joint eigenspace of the two multiplications by the root, $\tilde X\in\mathbb{B}_{st}$ exactly when $\tilde\Xi\tilde X=s\tilde X$ and $\tilde X\tilde\Xi=t\tilde X$. The pair and its opposite give the same unordered decomposition.

The table. The product of two corners lies in a single corner,

$$ \mathbb{B}_{ij}\star\mathbb{B}_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jl}\,\mathbb{B}_{ik}, $$

so the index that must agree is the second index of the first factor and the second index of the second factor, while the first index of the result comes from the first factor and the second index from the second. This is the transpose of the algebra's rule $\mathbb{B}_{ij}\mathbb{B}_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jk}\mathbb{B}_{il}$. At the standard pair the table is

$\star$ $\tilde\Pi$ $\hat\Upsilon_1$ $\hat\Upsilon_2$ $\tilde\Pi^{\perp}$
$\tilde\Pi$ $\tilde\Pi$ $0$ $\hat\Upsilon_1$ $0$
$\hat\Upsilon_1$ $0$ $\tilde\Pi$ $0$ $\hat\Upsilon_1$
$\hat\Upsilon_2$ $\hat\Upsilon_2$ $0$ $\tilde\Pi^{\perp}$ $0$
$\tilde\Pi^{\perp}$ $0$ $\hat\Upsilon_2$ $0$ $\tilde\Pi^{\perp}$

and the two mixed lines are the place where the two tables are most visibly exchanged: the multiplication gives $\hat\Upsilon_1\star\hat\Upsilon_2=0$ and $\hat\Upsilon_1\star\hat\Upsilon_1\subseteq\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi$, while the algebra gives $\hat\Upsilon_1\hat\Upsilon_2\subseteq\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi$ and $\hat\Upsilon_1\hat\Upsilon_1=0$. The two products are exhanged on the mixed lines.

The corner product. Each corner is a sesqualgebra over $\mathbb{C}$: for $\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}$,

$$ \lambda\tilde\Pi\star\mu\tilde\Pi=\lambda\overline{\mu}\,\tilde\Pi , $$

the conjugate product of $\mathbb{C}$, because the second slot conjugates. The diagonal corners are therefore not the field $\mathbb{C}$ as a product but $\mathbb{C}$ with the conjugate multiplication; the two mixed lines are exchanged by the involution, $\hat\Upsilon_2=\hat\Upsilon_1^{*}$.

The involution and the table. The involution exchanges the two indices, $(\mathbb{B}_{ij})^{*}=\mathbb{B}_{ji}$; it fixes each diagonal corner and exchanges the two mixed lines. Since it reverses the product, $(\tilde X\star\tilde Y)^{*}=\tilde Y\star\tilde X$, the entry $\mathbb{B}_{ij}\star\mathbb{B}_{jl}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{il}$ is carried to $\mathbb{B}_{lj}\star\mathbb{B}_{ji}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{li}$, and on the standard pair the table is transpose-Hermitian: the conjugate of an entry is the entry at the transposed position with the two mixed labels exchanged.

The two-sided conditions do not decompose. If one asks for the Peirce-type pieces cut out by the two conditions $\tilde X\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde X$ and $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde X=\tilde X$ and their vanishing versions, the four spaces one obtains are not $\mathbb{B}_{st}$: with $\mathbb{A}_{st}=\{\tilde X:\tilde\Pi\star\tilde X=s\tilde X,\ \tilde X\star\tilde\Pi=t\tilde X\}$ at the standard pair the pieces are $\mathbb{A}_{11}=\mathbb{R}\tilde\Pi$ for the double non-vanishing space, $\mathbb{A}_{00}=\mathbb{C}\hat\Upsilon_1\oplus\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi^{\perp}$ for the double vanishing space, the two mixed-conditions spaces $\mathbb{A}_{10}=\mathbb{A}_{01}$ are zero, and the offending element $\hat\Upsilon_2$ lies in none of them; the four pieces span real dimension five and do not exhaust the algebra. The multiplication reads the algebra's decomposition with its own index rule and does not carry a second one.

The physical reading of the table. The populations are the diagonal corners $\mathbb{B}_{++},\mathbb{B}_{--}$, the coherences the mixed corners $\mathbb{B}_{+-},\mathbb{B}_{-+}$. The transposed table says that the interference term sits on the second index: a coherence multiplied by the coherence in the same column lands in a population, $\mathbb{B}_{+-}\star\mathbb{B}_{+-}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{++}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{-+}\star\mathbb{B}_{-+}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{--}$, while the two coherences of opposite columns annihilate, $\mathbb{B}_{+-}\star\mathbb{B}_{-+}=0=\mathbb{B}_{-+}\star\mathbb{B}_{+-}$. The reading is that in the probability band the interference of a coherence is with itself, the self-pairing that a sesquilinear product performs, and not with its conjugate partner as in the algebra, where $\mathbb{B}_{+-}\mathbb{B}_{-+}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{++}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{+-}\mathbb{B}_{+-}=0$. The two statements are the same inclusion read on the two tables, and the article keeps the pairing $\mathbb{B}_{+-},\mathbb{B}_{-+}$ and the labels together.

As Many Decompositions as the Hermitian Roots

The Peirce decompositions of the block are in bijection with the Hermitian roots of $+1$ modulo the sign change: one degenerate decomposition and a real projective plane of them. The oblique frames of the algebra are not frames of the block.

A decomposition is indexed by a projection, because the corners are the spaces $\tilde\Pi_s\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_t$ of a complementary pair $\tilde\Pi,\tilde\Pi^{\perp}$. The projections of the block are the trivial pair $0,e_0$ and the family

$$ \tilde\Pi_\pm(\tilde\Xi)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0\pm\tilde\Xi\bigr),\qquad \tilde\Xi=i\vec\mu,\quad \vec\mu\in S^{2}, $$

by Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra: the Unitary Group and the Pure States, §The Idempotents and the Halving Map: every idempotent of the block is Hermitian, so the block's idempotent set and its pure-state set coincide, and it is exactly the Hermitian subfamily of the algebra's idempotents. The decomposition is therefore indexed by the Hermitian root $\tilde\Xi=i\vec\mu$ modulo the sign change $\vec\mu\mapsto-\vec\mu$, that is

index decompositions
the trivial pair $\pm e_0$ the degenerate decomposition, $\mathbb{B}$ and $0$
the Hermitian roots $i\vec\mu$, $\vec\mu\in S^{2}$, modulo $\pm$ the real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^{2}$ of proper decompositions
the oblique pairs of the algebra none: not decompositions of the block

The companion article of the composition band has the same corners and the whole family of the algebra's decompositions, a real four-parameter family beside the degenerate one. The block keeps the Hermitian subfamily and loses the oblique one, and the loss is the state-side fact read on the module side: an oblique pair of the algebra has non-Hermitian idempotents, which are not idempotents of the block, so the pair is not a pair of projections of the block and the corners it would give are not the block's Peirce corners.

The physical reading. The index set is the family of the observables of square one up to sign: one projection pair for each axis $\pm\vec\mu$ of the real unit sphere, the real projective plane, with the degenerate pair $\pm e_0$ at the centre. The reading is that the probability band has a Peirce decomposition for each of its observables and for no other frame. The decompositions are not a preferred-basis solution: the block marks out no axis, and the choice of the observable is a choice of frame. The reading is put on the index set alone, and it is separate from the algebraic count: the corners, the table and the involution are results, and the projective plane is the index set of the result.

The Populations and the Coherences

Relative to an observable of square one the four Peirce coordinates of a Hermitian element are its two populations and its two coherences; the populations sum to the trace and differ by the mean of the observable, they lie in $[0,1]$ for a state, the norm is the population product minus the coherence square, and purity is the saturation $p_+p_-=\lvert c\rvert^{2}$.

The reading is the one of Ideals and the Peirce Decomposition: the Populations and the Coherences in the composition band, read on the block. The frame is the pair of the Hermitian root $i\vec\mu$; the four Peirce coordinates of an element are the four entries of its matrix model in that basis, and for a state $\tilde\rho$, Hermitian of trace one, they are written

$$ a=\begin{pmatrix}p_+ & c_+\\ c_- & p_-\end{pmatrix},\qquad p_\pm\in\mathbb{R},\quad \overline{c_-}=c_+ , $$

with the two populations $p_+,p_-$ on the diagonal and the two coherences $c_+,c_-$ off it. The four identities of the state are

$$ p_++p_-=\operatorname{Tr}\tilde\rho=1,\qquad p_+-p_-=\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\tilde\rho},\qquad p_\pm\in[0,1],\qquad N(\tilde\rho)=p_+p_- - c_+c_- , $$

the last being the determinant of the matrix, the norm of the biquaternion. The trace-one condition makes the populations a probability pair, their difference the mean of the observable in the state, and the norm's vanishing the zero-divisor cone; a pure state has $N=0$, so

$$ \text{pure}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_+p_-=\lvert c_+\rvert^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_+p_-=\lvert c_-\rvert^{2}, $$

the population form of the purity boundary, and the mixed states are the interior $p_+p_->\lvert c_+\rvert^{2}$. The state carrier and the Born pairing are The Spinor Space as a Hilbert Space of the General Plain Sesqualgebra and The General Plain Sesquilinear Product on the Informational Sector; the dynamics of the populations and the coherences is Decoherence as Idempotent Projection.

What the block adds. The companion article of the composition band presupposes a Hermitian frame for the reading and keeps the oblique frames as algebraic decompositions without a probability reading. The block cannot be read on an oblique frame at all: its idempotents are exactly the Hermitian projections, so its frames are exactly the observable frames, and the populations-and-coherences reading is available on every frame of the block. The transposed table is the other addition, and it is the block's own: the interference term is the self-pairing of a coherence, $\mathbb{B}_{+-}\star\mathbb{B}_{+-}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{++}$, and the two coherences of opposite columns do not interfere. The corner product $\lambda\tilde\Pi\star\mu\tilde\Pi=\lambda\overline{\mu}\tilde\Pi$ is the segment's conjugate product, the algebraic form of the relative phase of the two populations.

What the reading does not claim. It does not claim that the populations and the coherences are observable numbers of a physical system in every state, that the block selects a preferred observable, or that the dynamics of the populations is derived here. It does not identify the right ideal with the spinor space: the first is the block's module, the second is the composition product's, and the article keeps them apart. The populations-and-coherences names are a reading of the Peirce coordinates, labelled as such; the corners, the product rule, the transposed table, the involution, the corner product, the index set and the four identities are results.

The Interpretation

The reading of the article is one: the Peirce decomposition of the block is the populations-and-coherences block structure of the algebra in the basis of one of its observables of square one, and the block's frames are exactly the observable frames.

  • The ideal structure of the block is the composition band's on the right and the chain on the left and in the middle: no superselection sector, and a projective line of right ideals that index the $2\times2$ realizations of the block, all unitarily equivalent.
  • The Peirce decomposition is the algebra's, indexed by the projections, which are exactly the Hermitian roots of $+1$ modulo sign: the degenerate pair and the real projective plane of the observable axes, with the oblique frames of the algebra discarded.
  • The table of the block is the transpose of the algebra's, and the two mixed lines are exchanged: a coherence pairs with itself into a population in the block, while the pair of opposite coherences annihilates.
  • The four Peirce coordinates of a state are its two populations and its two coherences; the population sum is the trace, the difference the mean, the norm the population product minus the coherence square, and purity the saturation $p_+p_-=\lvert c\rvert^{2}$.

The reading is placed against the companion article of the composition band, where the same corners carry the same populations-and-coherences reading but on the algebra's whole family of frames, only the Hermitian ones carrying the probability reading. In the probability band the reading is exact on the block's frames because the block's idempotents are the Hermitian ones; the price is the loss of the oblique frames and of the left lattice. The results are the mathematics of Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Sesqualgebra; the readings are proposals about naming.

What the framework does not claim. The framework does not claim that the probability band resolves the preferred-basis problem, that the right ideals are physical sectors, that the population form of purity derives the Born rule, or that the oblique frames of the composition band are unphysical. The projective plane is the index set of the block's decompositions and not a choice of frame; the block marks out no axis.

Summary

The general plain sesquilinear product of the biquaternion space is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$. Its ideal structure is asymmetric: the unit acting on the first slot produces the conjugate, $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$, so the multiplication has no proper left ideal and no proper two-sided ideal, the two lattices being the chain $\{0,\mathbb{B}\}$, while its right ideals are exactly the right ideals of the algebra, with a projective line of minimal right ideals $R_w=\{\tilde X:w\tilde X=0\}=\tilde\Pi\mathbb{B}$ of complex dimension two. Any two distinct ones are complementary; each pair carries an idempotent, Hermitian exactly when the two forms are orthogonal; each right ideal is a Hermitian space of Gram matrix $\tfrac12\mathrm{I}_2$, and the right multiplication on it, converted by the transpose, is an isomorphism of sesqualgebras onto $\bigl(M_2(\mathbb{C}),(A,B)\mapsto AB^{\dagger},{}^{\dagger}\bigr)$, one realization for each point of the lattice and all in one unitary orbit.

The Peirce decomposition is the algebra's: relative to the pair $\tilde\Pi_\pm(\tilde\Xi)$ of a root of $+1$ the four corners $\mathbb{B}_{st}=\tilde\Pi_s\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_t$ are four complex lines, the involution exchanges the two mixed lines and fixes the two corners, and each corner is a sesqualgebra with the conjugate product $\lambda\tilde\Pi\star\mu\tilde\Pi=\lambda\overline{\mu}\tilde\Pi$. The spaces cut out by the two-sided conditions are not the Peirce spaces and span real dimension five, so they do not decompose $\mathbb{B}$. The decompositions of the block are in bijection with the Hermitian roots of $+1$ modulo the sign: the degenerate decomposition and a real projective plane of them; the oblique frames of the algebra are not frames of the block.

$R_w=\{\tilde X:w\tilde X=0\}=\tilde\Pi\mathbb{B}$ The minimal right $\star$-ideals, one per point of the projective line
$\tilde e_{w,w'}$ The idempotent of a pair of distinct right ideals, Hermitian exactly when the forms are orthogonal
$\rho\colon(\mathbb{B},\star,{}^{*})\to(M_2(\mathbb{C}),(A,B)\mapsto AB^{\dagger},{}^{\dagger})$ The sesqualgebra isomorphism from a right ideal, via the transpose
$\mathbb{RP}^{2}$ The real projective plane of the observable axes $\pm\vec\mu$; the index set of the proper decompositions
$p_+,p_-$ The populations, the diagonal Peirce coordinates
$c_+,c_-$ The coherences, the mixed Peirce coordinates
$N(\tilde\rho)=p_+p_- - c_+c_-$ The norm in the populations and the coherences; purity is $p_+p_-=\lvert c\rvert^{2}$
$\mathbb{M}_\pm$ Informational and material sectors
$(\vec P,\vec Q)=\sum_kP_kQ_k$ General plain bilinear form of two vector parts
$\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})$ The projective line of the right ideals

The Most Common Items with a Tilde

Scope: tilde glyphs only. Every symbol in the tables carries a tilde. Four kinds of glyph are outside the scope and are not listed among the allowed uses: a matrix, $I_n$, $\mathsf{M}_2$, $\mathsf{M}_4$; a pure element written with an arrow, $\vec Q$, $\vec q$, $\vec u$, $\vec U$, $\vec\Xi$, $\vec\Upsilon$, $\vec\pi$; a mark that is not an element, $\tilde\cdot$, $\tilde\star$; and every glyph of the reduced, tilde-free layers, $\Pi_\pm$, $\Xi_\pm$, $\Upsilon_\pm$. The first is fixed by the rule that a matrix carries no tilde, §General Matrix Conventions of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe; the others are elements of other layers or of no layer at all. The operator section states the rule that pairs the two marks; it is not a catalogue of arrowed glyphs.

The tilde marks a quaternionic factor. The last column of every table is the number of articles in which the symbol occurs, counted over the 2528 articles of articles_maths/ (2110) and articles_physics/ (418) on 18 October 2026; the catalogue itself is excluded. The list is triaged into three sections.

  • 1. Allowed tilde — the role is fixed by Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe, or by the case-and-mark rule that article states, and checked against the corpus. Cite these entries as they stand. The tables are ordered by theme, as the objects are.
  • 2. To be tested — one specific role, plausible but not yet verified. Test before citing. A symbol that is also wrongly generic keeps its role here and its generic claim in section 3.
  • 3. Wrongly generic, to be rewritten — one line per work item, a generic claim outside the Q family. Do not cite.

The governing rule: only the Q family is generic, and only an element carries the tilde. $\tilde Q$ is the biquaternion or split-biquaternion element and $\tilde q$ the quaternion or split-quaternion element; numbered variants are allowed. A generic element that is unitary is written $\tilde U$. A letter outside the family names an object, or an element bound where it is introduced. A matrix — the image of an element, an array of biquaternions, or a colour matrix — takes no tilde; a tilde on one is a defect of scope, corrected where it occurs and not catalogued here.

2. To be tested

One specific role per entry, not yet verified. The test is one pass over the articles that use the symbol, checking that every use matches the description. A glyph whose uses are all bound but span several roles is listed separately at the end of the section, with the pass recorded. The section now holds no entry: its one entry failed the test, and the pass is recorded here.

$\tilde\Gamma_\mu$ is not a tilde glyph. The connection one-form takes its values in the six-dimensional Lie subspace $\mathrm{span}_\mathbb{R}\{\vec e_k,i\vec e_k\}$, whose elements are traceless; in the matrix image the trace is twice the scalar part, $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q)=2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)$, so tracelessness is purity, and by §Pure Elements and their Coefficients of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe a pure element carries the arrow and never the tilde. The glyph is $\vec\Gamma_\mu$. The transformation law preserves the mark: a conjugation by a unit rotor preserves the vector subspace, and the inhomogeneous term is pure for a unit element, $(\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{-1}=(\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}$ with $\mathrm{Sc}\bigl((\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}\bigr)=0$ because differentiating $\tilde U\tilde U^{\natural}=e_t$ gives $(\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}=-\bigl((\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}\bigr)^{\natural}$. The row is removed. The seven articles that write the connection — Curved Spacetime and the Biquaternion Framework, The Covariant Derivative and Gauge Connection in Biquaternionic Form, Gauge Curvature and the Bianchi Identity in Biquaternionic Form, Quantum Gravity under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda, Torsion, Contorsion and the Spin Connection in Biquaternionic Form, Linearized Gravity in Biquaternionic Form and The Einstein Field Equations under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda — now write $\vec\Gamma_\mu$. The electric source is written with $\tilde J$, not $\tilde R$. The Maxwell source is the four-current $\tilde J=ic\rho\,e_t+\vec J$, and the field equation reads $\tilde\nabla\tilde F=-\sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde J$. Since $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$, $$ \sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde J=\sqrt{\mu}\left(ic\rho\,e_t+\vec J\right)=\frac{i\rho}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}e_t+\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec J , $$ so the normalised combination $i\rho/\sqrt{\epsilon}\,e_t+\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec J$ is $\sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde J$, not a second object. The factor $\sqrt{\mu}$ is the whole difference, and it is written explicitly in every article. No article of the corpus now writes $\tilde R$ for a source.

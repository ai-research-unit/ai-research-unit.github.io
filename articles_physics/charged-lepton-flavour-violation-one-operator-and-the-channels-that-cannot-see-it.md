# __Charged Lepton Flavour Violation: One Operator, One Number, and the Channels That Cannot See It__

## Introduction

**The layer.** The operator is a statement about the frame's gauge sector on a formal copy of the carrier: the leptoquarks are the six of §5 of the scalar article, and what they do to lepton flavour is fixed by the group and by nothing else. **Its Layer-A input is a single line** — $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ splits as $8+1+6$ around the unit line — and every number below is a bound or a prediction that follows from that one operator, not a derivation from the algebra.

**The question.** The six leptoquarks convert quarks into leptons. Which lepton-flavour-violating processes do they mediate, at what order, and which experiment can see them?

**The answer in one line.**

$$
\boxed{\;
\text{one operator, both chiralities, } \Delta F=1:\qquad
\frac{g_4^{2}}{2M_X^{2}}\Bigl[\bigl(\bar d\,\gamma^{\mu}P_L e\bigr)\bigl(\bar s\,\gamma_{\mu}P_L\mu\bigr)+(L\to R)\Bigr]+\text{h.c.}
\;}
$$

$$
\boxed{\;
\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)\approx3.5\times10^{-12}\ \text{at }2.5\times10^{2}\ \mathrm{TeV},
\qquad
M_X\gtrsim2.3\times10^{2}\ \mathrm{TeV},
\qquad
\mathcal B(K_S\to\mu e)\sim10^{-20},
\qquad
\mathcal B(\mu\to e\gamma)\sim10^{-15}
\;}
$$

**So the frame's flavour violation is a kaon measurement, and the muon experiments cannot see it at any mass that is not already excluded.**

**A correction, stated before anything else, because the obvious version of this article is wrong.** It is natural to expect that $\mu$–$e$ conversion is the sharpest channel, since it is a $\Delta F=1$ process with no loop in the Standard Model either. **In this frame it is not tree level.** The leptoquark $X^{2/3}$ couples a down-type quark to a charged lepton, so the tree-level diagram is $s\bar d\to X\to\mu\bar e$, which is $K_L\to\mu e$; the operator $\bar\mu\Gamma e\,\bar q\Gamma'q$ that conversion needs requires *two* leptoquark vertices, which is a loop. **The tree-level channel is the kaon one, and it is the only one that sets a bound.** Everything else is one loop down, and §8 shows how far down.

**What is claimed.** That the gauge sector has no flavour index, so one operator and one number control every channel; that the operator is $V\pm A$ only, so it selects $K_L$ and gives $K_S\to\mu e$ at the $|\epsilon_K|^2$ level; that the tree-level rate and the resulting bound are as boxed; that the muon channels are suppressed by a loop and a chirality flip, and are three to four orders of magnitude below their experimental reach at the scale the kaon bound forces; and that this ranking is what the frame's flavour blindness buys.

**What is not claimed.** That the leptoquarks exist. That the loop coefficients are computed here — **they are not, and §7 says exactly how far the estimate goes and what it would take to do better.** That the bound is precise to better than a factor of a few: §5 gives the band and says where the round figure used elsewhere in the corpus sits in it.

## 1. Why the Gauge Sector Cannot Mix Generations

**The frame has one coupling and no flavour index, and that is the whole of this section.**

**1.1 One covariant derivative.** The gauge field of the frame is one object acting on the quartet, with one coupling $g_4$. There is no matrix in generation space, because the group does not know that generations exist: $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ acts on the four colours of one generation, and the second and third generations are copies of the same module. **The coupling of $X$ to the first-generation down quark and to the second are the same number, and there is nowhere in the gauge sector for a difference to live.**

**1.2 So the mixing matrices live in the Yukawa sector, and only there.** The CKM and PMNS matrices are properties of the fermion mass matrices, which the frame writes and does not derive. **The gauge sector's contribution to any flavour transition is therefore a pure number times a hadronic matrix element** — no angles, no phases, no Wolfenstein parameters.

**1.3 What that buys, and what it costs.** It buys a bound with no free parameters: one measurement fixes $M_X$ and then predicts every other channel of the same operator class. It costs the model any mechanism for suppressing one channel while keeping another: **a frame with a flavour structure in the gauge sector could hide from $K_L\to\mu e$ and appear in $\mu\to e\gamma$; this one cannot.** That is why the corpus can quote a single bound for the whole sector, and why §9 can state a clean falsification condition.

**1.4 The one place generation enters is the mass, and it enters as a kinematic factor.** The operator converts, say, an $s$ into a $\mu$; whether the external states are first or second generation changes the hadronic matrix element and the phase space, not the coupling. **Every ratio among the channels of §6 is therefore a statement about QCD, not about the model.**

## 2. The One Operator, and Its Two Chiralities

**Integrating out the six leptoquarks gives one four-fermion operator per chirality, and there are exactly two.**

**2.1 Where the two come from.** The left-handed quark and lepton sit in the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ and the right-handed in the $(\bar{\mathbf4},\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, and each multiplet carries its own coupling of $X$ to the pair. **The two couplings are equal in magnitude because the frame gauges both factors with the same $g_4$** — the left–right symmetry of the group, which is the same two-sidedness the algebra supplies and the spectrum article uses:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{eff}}\supset
\frac{g_4^{2}}{2M_X^{2}}\Bigl[
\bigl(\bar d\,\gamma^{\mu}P_L e\bigr)\bigl(\bar s\,\gamma_{\mu}P_L\mu\bigr)
+
\bigl(\bar d\,\gamma^{\mu}P_R e\bigr)\bigl(\bar s\,\gamma_{\mu}P_R\mu\bigr)
\Bigr]+\text{h.c.}
$$

**2.2 There is no $LR$ term, and that is structural.** A mixed-chirality operator would need a scalar leptoquark or a bidoublet exchange; the gauge sector supplies neither. **So the operator is $V-A$ and $V+A$, and never $V$ times $A$.** §3 shows that this single fact decides which neutral kaon decays.

**2.3 Why the two chiralities matter numerically, and why they do not matter for the bound.** The two terms add incoherently in the rate, since they contribute to different helicity amplitudes, so the rate is twice the $LL$ rate. **A frame with one chirality would have half the rate and a bound lower by $2^{1/4}=1.19$** — a factor that is inside the band of §5 and does not change the argument anywhere.

**2.4 The colour sum, stated once.** $X$ is a colour triplet, so the quark bilinears carry a colour index that is contracted between them: $\bar d_a\gamma e\,\bar s^a\gamma\mu$. **The contraction gives a factor of one, not three**, because the two quark lines are in the same colour channel — the antitriplet of one and the triplet of the other — and the leptoquark propagator is diagonal in colour. **An earlier estimate that used a factor of three here would be wrong by nine in the rate.**

## 3. Why $K_L$ and Not $K_S$

**The parity structure of the operator selects one neutral-kaon eigenstate and forbids the other, and the argument is two lines.**

**3.1 Only the axial part survives.** The kaon is a pseudoscalar, so the matrix element of a vector current between the vacuum and a kaon vanishes by parity:

$$
\langle0|\bar d\,\gamma^{\mu}s|K\rangle=0,
\qquad
\langle0|\bar d\,\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}s|K^{0}\rangle=i f_K p^{\mu}.
$$

**So of the $V\pm A$ structure only the $A$ acts**, and the $V$ passes through the meson without seeing it.

**3.2 The two halves act on different states, and the relative sign is a chirality.** Write the operator of §2 as $c(\bar s\Gamma d)(\bar e\Gamma'\mu)+\text{h.c.}$ The first half contains $\bar s$ and $d$, so it annihilates the $d\bar s=K^{0}$ component and vanishes on $\bar d s=\bar K^{0}$; the hermitian conjugate contains $\bar d$ and $s$ and does the reverse. **So the $K^{0}$ and $\bar K^{0}$ amplitudes come from the two halves of one operator, and their relative sign is decided by the operator's chirality structure and by nothing else:**

$$
\text{diagonal }(LL\text{ or }RR):\quad A(K^{0})=-A(\bar K^{0}),
\qquad
\text{flipping }(LR\text{ or }RL):\quad A(K^{0})=+A(\bar K^{0}).
$$

**The frame is in the first line, and so is every vector leptoquark.** $V\pm A$ in both bilinears is chirality-diagonal, and §2.2 shows the frame has no $LR$ term to mix the cases. **The second line is what a scalar leptoquark with mixed couplings produces**, because a scalar or tensor bilinear flips chirality where a vector bilinear does not.

**3.3 The two eigenstates, and the consequence.**

$$
|K_{L}\rangle=\frac{|K^{0}\rangle-|\bar K^{0}\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
|K_{S}\rangle=\frac{|K^{0}\rangle+|\bar K^{0}\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A(K_{S})=\frac{A(K^{0})+A(\bar K^{0})}{\sqrt2}=0 .
$$

**Opposite amplitudes cancel in the sum and survive in the difference, so the state that cancels is the symmetric one, $K_S$, and the state that survives is $K_L$.** So the frame predicts

$$
\mathcal B(K_S\to\mu e)=0\quad\text{at leading order},
$$

**and the leading correction is the CP impurity of the mass eigenstates**, which puts the amplitude at $\epsilon_K$ and the rate at $|\epsilon_K|^2$:

$$
\epsilon_K=2.228\times10^{-3},
\qquad
\mathcal B(K_S\to\mu e)\approx\epsilon_K^{2}\,\frac{\Gamma(K_L\to\mu e)}{\Gamma_S}\approx4\times10^{-20}.
$$

**3.4 What that prediction is worth, and why it is a discriminator rather than a bound.** It is exact in the gauge sector, it needs no number from outside the frame beyond $\epsilon_K$, and **it is a statement about the operator's chirality rather than about its size**: the cancellation in $K_S$ follows from the sign in §3.2, which follows from the two multiplets and the absence of a scalar leptoquark. **A model whose leptoquarks flip chirality gets the other sign and lands in $K_S$ instead.** So a positive $K_S\to\mu e$ with $K_L\to\mu e$ still null would say that the operator is not the frame's — **that the leptoquarks, if they exist, are scalars with mixed couplings.** That is sharper than any bound, because **it does not need the mass to be known**: the two models differ by orders of magnitude in one channel and not the other, whatever $M_X$ is.

## 4. The Rate

**One amplitude, one hadronic input, and the rate follows.**

**4.1 The amplitude.** With $\langle0|A^{\mu}|\bar K^{0}\rangle=i f_K p^{\mu}$ and the $1/\sqrt2$ of the $K_L$ composition,

$$
\mathcal M=\frac{g_4^{2}}{2M_X^{2}}\cdot\frac{f_K}{\sqrt2}\cdot
p^{\mu}\,\bar u_e\gamma_{\mu}\tfrac12(1-\gamma^{5})v_\mu ,
$$

and the spin sum, using $p\!\!/=p\!\!/_e+p\!\!/_\mu$ and the Dirac equations, collapses to the mass:

$$
\sum_{\mathrm{spins}}|\mathcal M|^{2}
=
\frac{g_4^{4}}{8M_X^{4}}\,f_K^{2}\,m_K^{2}\bigl(m_e^{2}+m_\mu^{2}\bigr).
$$

**The helicity suppression is visible here and it is the reason the rate is small**: a pseudoscalar cannot decay into two leptons without a mass insertion, so the amplitude is proportional to $m_\mu$ and the rate to $m_\mu^{2}$. **A frame that predicted a rate without a mass factor would be predicting a rate for a decay that cannot happen.**

**4.2 The rate.**

$$
\Gamma(K_L\to\mu e)=\frac{g_4^{4}\,f_K^{2}\,m_\mu^{2}\,m_K}{128\pi\,M_X^{4}},
$$

**and the branching ratio is that over $\Gamma_K=1.287\times10^{-17}$ GeV.**

**4.3 The check that the factors are right, and it is a real check because the normalisation is the one place a factor of eight hides.** Set $g_4=1$ and $M_X=10^{5}$ GeV: the formula gives $\Gamma=3.37\times10^{-27}$ GeV and $\mathcal B=2.62\times10^{-10}$. **The same normalisation, applied to the textbook $K\to\mu\nu$ rate $(G_F\sin\theta_c)^{2}f_K^{2}m_Km_\mu^{2}/8\pi$, reproduces $\mathcal B(K\to\mu\nu)$** — and a formula that got the $K\to\mu\nu$ normalisation wrong by eight would have been visible there, where the branching ratio is measured to a per cent. **And the mass factor is pinned from both sides**: replacing $m_\mu^{2}m_K$ by $m_K^{3}$ would raise the rate by $m_K^{2}/m_\mu^{2}=44.6$, and replacing it by $m_\mu^{4}$ would lower it by $m_\mu^{2}/m_K=0.022$ — **a factor of forty either way, which no choice of $g_4$ can absorb, because $g_4$ is fixed by $\alpha_s$ to $10\%$.** So the two-body rate has no free normalisation left, and the numbers in §5 were recomputed for this article rather than copied from the corpus's round figure.

## 5. The Number, Which Is Not in This Article

**With $g_4$ taken from the matching condition, the bound is a number rather than a range — and that substitution is a coupling statement, so it lives in the flagged article.**

**5.1 The substitution is one line.** The first matching relation is $g_3=g_4$ at the first threshold, so $g_4^{2}=4\pi\alpha_s(M_X)$. **That is a coupling identity, not a property of the channel**, and the same is true of every number that follows from it.

**5.2 So this article states the shape and not the value.** The rate carries the fourth power of the mass:

$$
\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)\propto\frac{g_4^{4}}{M_X^{4}} .
$$

**A bound in $M_X$ is therefore sharp**: a factor of two in the mass is a factor of sixteen in the rate. **That statement is structural, and it is the only one about size that this article makes.**

**5.3 Where the value is.** The table of $\mathcal B$ against $M_X$, the resulting floor, and the width of its band are in [The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model](the-one-coupling-that-returns-the-standard-model.html), §3.4. **They are not repeated here, and a reader who finds a value quoted in this article should treat it as an error of the migration.**

**5.4 What that costs, and why it is the right trade.** The article loses a number and keeps its claim. **The claim is that one operator with one coupling controls every channel in this sector, because the gauge sector carries no generation index** — and that claim is exactly what a reader needs in order to know that a single null result constrains all the channels at once. **The number changes with the scale; the claim does not.**

## 6. The Semileptonic Channels

**Two more kaon channels carry the same operator, and their ratios are QCD.**

**6.1 The channels.** $K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\mu^{+}e^{-}$ and $K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\mu^{+}e^{-}$ are the same $\Delta F=1$, $\Delta L=1$ transition with a pion in the final state instead of a second lepton. **The short-distance coefficient is identical to §2's, so a measurement in any one of them fixes $M_X$ for all.**

**6.2 Why they are not negligible.** The two-body rate of §4 carries a helicity suppression $m_\mu^{2}/m_K^{2}\approx0.045$; the three-body rate carries a form factor instead and pays nothing for the lepton mass. **So the semileptonic channel is not suppressed relative to the two-body one, and with limits within a factor of three of each other the two set bounds of the same order.** Which is larger depends on the overlap of the $K\to\pi$ form factor with the Dalitz region, and that integral the corpus does not have.

**6.3 What that means for the next decade.** The two-body kaon channel is at its experimental limit; the semileptonic one is limited by statistics and by the $K^{+}$ flux. **A factor of three in the $K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\mu^{+}e^{-}$ limit would make it the leading constraint on this frame**, and it would do so by measuring a different hadronic matrix element — which is exactly the cross-check the model wants, because the two channels' ratio is a lattice number with no new physics in it.

**6.4 The honest limit of this section.** The factor-of-two estimate in §6.2 is a phase-space comparison, not a form-factor integral. **It is enough to say the channel is worth watching and not enough to predict its rate**; a real prediction needs the $K\to\pi$ form factor over the Dalitz region, which the corpus does not have.

## 7. The Muon Channels Need a Loop

**Three more processes, all of them the same operator at second order, and all of them far below reach.**

**7.1 Why a loop is needed.** A $\mu\to e$ transition with no hadron in the initial state needs the four-fermion operator of §2 to be closed into a loop: a leptoquark and a quark in the loop, with a photon, a $Z$, or a second leptoquark attached. **There is no tree diagram, for the reason the Introduction gives: the leptoquark couples a down-type quark to a charged lepton, so closing the operator into $\bar\mu\Gamma e\,\bar q\Gamma'q$ costs two vertices.**

**7.2 The three channels, and what each needs.**

| channel | operator | needs |
|---|---|---|
| $\mu\to e\gamma$ | dipole $\bar\mu\sigma^{\mu\nu}e\,F_{\mu\nu}$ | one loop and one chirality flip |
| $\mu\to eee$ | dipole plus a four-fermion contact | one loop, dominated by the dipole |
| $\mu$–$e$ conversion | dipole plus a contact term on the nucleus | one loop, then a nuclear overlap |

**7.3 The scaling, and the one number this article will quote for it.** The dipole coefficient is a loop factor times a chirality flip over the leptoquark mass squared:

$$
C_{\mathrm{dip}}\sim\frac{e\,g_4^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{m_\mu}{M_X^{2}},
\qquad
\mathcal B(\mu\to e\gamma)=384\pi^{2}\left|\frac{C_{\mathrm{dip}}}{\,4G_Fm_\mu/\sqrt2\,}\right|^{2}
\approx1.1\times10^{-15}\ \text{at }2.3\times10^{2}\ \mathrm{TeV}.
$$

**The number is an estimate and it is labelled as one**: the coefficient of the loop function is order one and depends on which internal quark dominates, and the two chiralities contribute at different orders in $m_q/M_X$. **What is not an estimate is the scaling** $C_{\mathrm{dip}}\propto g_4^{2}m_\mu/M_X^{2}$, which is fixed by dimension, chirality and the loop, and which is what the comparison of §8 needs.

**7.4 The other two follow the dipole by numbers that are not the model's.** For a dipole-dominated $\mu\to e$ transition the ratios are fixed by QED and by the nucleus:

$$
\frac{\mathcal B(\mu\to eee)}{\mathcal B(\mu\to e\gamma)}\approx\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\left(\ln\frac{m_\mu^{2}}{m_e^{2}}-\frac{11}{4}\right)\approx6\times10^{-3},
\qquad
\frac{R(\mu\to e\text{ in Ti})}{\mathcal B(\mu\to e\gamma)}\approx5\times10^{-3}.
$$

**So the three muon channels are not independent tests: they are one dipole seen three ways**, and a frame that predicted one of them without the other two would be inconsistent. **Their measured limits are $4.2\times10^{-13}$, $1.0\times10^{-12}$ and $4.3\times10^{-12}$ respectively** — within a factor of ten of each other, which is why they can be discussed as one number.

**7.5 What would make this section more than an estimate.** A full one-loop matching onto the dipole and four-fermion operators, with the bidoublet and the $W$ in the loop as well as $X$, and with the internal $b$ quark's contribution kept separate. **The corpus does one-loop matching elsewhere and does not do it here**; the ranking of §8 does not depend on it, and the article says so rather than pretending otherwise.

## 8. Where Each Channel Sits

**The comparison, and the reason it is decisive rather than marginal.**

**8.1 At the scale the kaon bound forces.** At $M_X=2.3\times10^{2}$ TeV the four channels read:

| channel | mechanism | predicted | current limit | headroom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $K_L\to\mu e$ | tree | $4.7\times10^{-12}$ | $4.7\times10^{-12}$ | at the limit |
| $K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\mu^{+}e^{-}$ | tree | same order | $\sim10^{-11}$ | a factor of a few |
| $\mu\to e\gamma$ | one loop | $1.1\times10^{-15}$ | $4.2\times10^{-13}$ | $380\times$ below |
| $\mu\to eee$ | one loop | $\sim7\times10^{-18}$ | $1.0\times10^{-12}$ | $1.5\times10^{5}\times$ below |
| $\mu$–$e$ conversion | one loop | $\sim6\times10^{-18}$ | $4.3\times10^{-12}$ | $8\times10^{5}\times$ below |

**8.2 The argument that matters, stated as a single question: can any value of $M_X$ make a muon signal visible?**

$$
\mathcal B(\mu\to e\gamma)=4.2\times10^{-13}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
M_X\approx55\ \mathrm{TeV},
\qquad
\text{at which }
\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)\approx1.9\times10^{-9}
=400\times\text{the limit}.
$$

**And for Mu3e's projected $10^{-14}$:**

$$
\mathcal B(\mu\to e\gamma)=10^{-14}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
M_X\approx1.4\times10^{2}\ \mathrm{TeV},
\qquad
\text{at which }
\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)\approx4.4\times10^{-11}
=9\times\text{the limit}.
$$

**So the answer is no.** Every value of $M_X$ that would put a dipole signal inside the reach of MEG puts the kaon rate two and a half orders of magnitude above a limit that has stood for two decades; the projected Mu3e reach narrows the gap to a factor of nine, which is still an exclusion but no longer a comfortable one — **and that is the one place where this frame's flavour sector could move in the next decade.** **The frame's charged-lepton flavour violation is invisible to the muon experiments, not because the coupling is small but because the channel that is not helicity-suppressed is already excluded below the scale where the loop channels would appear.**

**8.3 Why that is a result and not a disappointment.** It tells an experimentalist which experiment to run: **a kaon experiment, and specifically a semileptonic one**, because §6.2 puts it within a factor of two of the two-body rate while its limit is weaker. **It also tells the corpus something it did not have: the model's flavour sector has exactly one live number, and every other channel is a corollary.**

## 9. What Would Falsify the Frame

**Three observations, and each one removes a specific assumption.**

**9.1 The first: $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)$ above $4.7\times10^{-12}$ with no leptoquark seen.** That is not a falsification but a confirmation of the bound's direction — it would fix $M_X$ and put the whole heavy spectrum at $2.5$ to $4\times10^{2}$ TeV, which §6 of the spectrum article already does.

**9.2 The second: a positive $\mu\to e\gamma$ with no corresponding kaon signal.** This is the sharp one, and §8 makes it quantitative: the ratio of the two rates is fixed by the loop, so a dipole signal at $M_X$ predicts a kaon rate about $4\times10^{3}$ times larger at the same $M_X$. **A dipole signal whose $M_X$ is excluded by the kaon bound says that the gauge sector has a flavour index** — that the coupling of $X$ to the second generation differs from its coupling to the first. **That is not a modification of the frame's group; it is a second declaration, of the same kind as the fourth colour being the lepton**, and the model would then have two load-bearing assumptions instead of one.

**9.3 The third: a positive $K_S\to\mu e$ with no $K_L$ signal.** By §3.3 the frame predicts this channel at $10^{-20}$ and forbids it at leading order. **A signal would say the operator is not $V\pm A$** — that the leptoquarks have scalar partners with mixed chiralities, which the gauge sector does not supply. **This is the cleanest of the three, because it tests the operator's structure rather than its size.**

**9.4 What none of them can do.** None can distinguish this frame from any other fourth-colour model with the same flavour content, because that sector is the same in all of them. **What the frame adds is not a different prediction but a place to put it**: one operator, one number, and the statement that the number has only one live channel.

## 10. What Is Robust and What Is Not

**Four claims of very different strength.**

**10.1 Robust: the operator's structure.** $V\pm A$, both chiralities, no $LR$, colour contraction of one. **This follows from the multiplet content and the equal gauge couplings, and it is not sensitive to any numerical input.**

**10.2 Robust: the $K_S$ selection.** The axial matrix element's oddness and the eigenstate's symmetry are exact statements about the matrix element; **the only correction is $\epsilon_K$, which is measured.**

**10.3 Firm: the two-body rate and the bound.** The rate's factors are fixed by the matrix element and the coupling; **the bound is firm to the band of §5, which is a factor of two in $M_X$.**

**10.4 An estimate: the loop channels.** §7.3 is a scaling with an order-one coefficient that this article does not compute. **The ranking of §8 survives any coefficient in $[0.3,3]$, which moves the $\mu\to e\gamma$ line by a factor of three in rate and $1.3$ in $M_X$** — and the conclusion, that no $M_X$ makes a muon signal visible, survives because the gap is three orders of magnitude.

**10.5 Not robust at all: any statement about the third generation.** The frame's $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is generation-blind, so $\tau\to\mu\gamma$ and $\tau\to e\gamma$ are in the same class as $\mu\to e\gamma$ with a loop and an $m_\tau$ insertion; **their limits are far weaker and their predictions far smaller, and this article does not quantify them.**

## Summary

**The one operator.** $\dfrac{g_4^{2}}{2M_X^{2}}[(\bar d\gamma^{\mu}P_Le)(\bar s\gamma_\mu P_L\mu)+(\bar d\gamma^{\mu}P_Re)(\bar s\gamma_\mu P_R\mu)]$, from integrating out the six leptoquarks, with both chiralities because the frame gauges both factors with one coupling.

**The one number.** $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)=g_4^{4}f_K^{2}m_\mu^{2}m_K/(128\pi M_X^{4}\Gamma_K)$, giving $3.5\times10^{-12}$ at $250$ TeV and a bound of $2.3\times10^{2}$ TeV against $4.7\times10^{-12}$.

**The one structure.** $V\pm A$ only, so only the axial part acts, so only the odd combination survives, so $K_L$ decays and $K_S$ does not: $\mathcal B(K_S\to\mu e)\sim10^{-20}$.

**The channels that cannot see it.** $\mu\to e\gamma$, $\mu\to eee$ and $\mu$–$e$ conversion need a loop and a chirality flip, and land two and a half to six orders of magnitude below their limits at the bound scale. **Every $M_X$ that would make them visible is already excluded by $K_L\to\mu e$**, by $400$ times at MEG's reach and $9$ times at Mu3e's.

**The falsification.** A dipole signal whose $M_X$ the kaon bound excludes means the gauge sector has a flavour index — a second declaration. A $K_S\to\mu e$ signal at leading order means the operator is not $V\pm A$ — not the frame's leptoquarks.

**The verdict.** The frame's flavour sector has one live number and one live experiment: a kaon, not a muon.

**The traps.** Do not read $\mu$–$e$ conversion as the tree-level channel: in this frame the tree diagram is $s\bar d\to X\to\mu\bar e$, which is the kaon, and conversion costs two vertices — the Introduction and §7. Do not read $\mathcal B(K_S\to\mu e)=0$ as an accident of the numbers: it follows from the relative sign of the two halves of a chirality-diagonal operator, and it is a test of the operator's chirality structure rather than of its size — §3.2, §3.4. Do not read the agreement with the corpus's $250$ TeV as luck: it needs the $1/128\pi$ normalisation, and a rate written with $1/16\pi$ would put the bound at $390$ TeV and change every number in §8 — §4.3, §5.3. Do not read the loop numbers of §7 as computed coefficients; they are scalings with an order-one function, and §7.5 says what a real calculation would need. Do not read the absence of a muon signal as a small coupling: the coupling is fixed, and the muon channels are invisible because the kaon channel excludes every scale at which they would appear — §8.2. **Do not read a future $\mu\to e\gamma$ signal as a confirmation of this frame**: it is the one signal the frame cannot produce at any allowed mass — §9.2.

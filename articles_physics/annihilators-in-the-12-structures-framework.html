Annihilators in the 12 Structures Framework

Introduction

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners that the element kills. It is the kernel of the operation "multiply by it", so it is the kernel of a process, and the physical reading of the kernel is direct: a process is invertible exactly when its kernel is trivial, and the size of the kernel is the amount of information the process destroys. The twelve products of the biquaternion complex space define twelve annihilators for one element, and this article reads them physically. It is the second physics companion of the mathematics comparison group, after Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures Framework, and the mathematics companion is Annihilators (articles_maths/annihilators.md).

The mathematics companion computes the annihilator of a general element in each of the twelve structures and proves two theorems. The first: the two one-sided annihilators, the left one ${\tilde X:\tilde P\star \tilde X=0}$ and the right one ${\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde P=0}$, are equal in ten of the twelve structures and differ in exactly two, the plain product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ and the general quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$, and there only at an element of vanishing biquaternion norm. The second: the four planes of a cone element in the four general products are one plain plane read through the two conjugations. The physical thesis of this article is that these two theorems are statements about operators: a symmetric or one-slot product has one kernel, the plain composite and the gauge coupling have two, and the difference between the two sides is the chirality of an operation against its adjoint.

Four physical objects of the annihilator table organise the article. The invertible operation is the element whose annihilator is the origin alone. The lightlike operation is the cone element, whose annihilator is a plane of other lightlike directions, so that a null direction can be killed only along null directions. The scalar-valued coupling is the symmetrisation that returns a number; its annihilator is a hyperplane, and the hyperplane is the set of partners with which the coupling gives zero, the physical form of orthogonal amplitudes. The bracket is the antisymmetrisation; its annihilator is the centraliser of the element, and at a central element, which is a global phase, the bracket kills the whole algebra.

The article proceeds as follows. It first fixes the annihilator as the kernel of an operation and reads invertibility as trivial kernel. It then reads the two-sided theorem as chirality, the four general planes as the lightlike kernel, the hyperplane rows as zero overlap, and the bracket rows as the centraliser. It then reads the kernel in the quantum corpus and again in the corpus's cohomology, its constraint equations, its polarisation operator and its information channels. A table collects the twelve annihilators with one physical clause each, and the closing sections are the ledger, the interpretation, the summary, the notation table and the literature. The other articles of the section own the objects that the table touches: Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures Framework the degeneracy itself, Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the 12 Structures Framework and Square Roots of a General Element in the 12 Structures Framework the radicands, Idempotents in the 12 Structures Framework the projectors, and Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework the square-zero elements.

Conventions. We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$ and $e_je_k=-\delta_{jk}e_0+\varepsilon_{jkl}e_l$; $i$ is the central scalar imaginary. The conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The biquaternion norm is $N(\tilde Q)=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$, whose vanishing locus is the cone; the trace is $\mathrm{Tr}=2\,\mathrm{Sc}$; the material coordinate is $\tilde Q=ict\,e_0+\mathbf x$. The twelve structures are cited by their three-letter names, and the left and right annihilators are written $A_\star(\tilde P)$ and $B_\star(\tilde P)$ as in the mathematics companion.

The Annihilator as the Kernel of an Operation

Definition and kernel. For a product $\star$ and an element $\tilde P$, the left and right annihilators are

$$ A_\star(\tilde P)=\{\tilde X:\tilde P\star\tilde X=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde P)=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde P=0\}, $$

which are the kernels of the left and right multiplications $L_{\tilde P}$ and $R_{\tilde P}$. Each is a complex subspace, and its dimension is the number of free complex parameters left by one equation. Read as an operation, an element is the map "multiply by it" (The Monoid of Acting Maps: the Process Is the Multiplication, the State Is the Idempotent), and the annihilator is the kernel of that map.

Invertibility is trivial kernel. An element has a two-sided plain inverse exactly when $N(\tilde P)\neq0$, the inverse being $\tilde P^{\natural}/N(\tilde P)$, and then multiplying by $\tilde P$ is a bijection, so its annihilator is the origin alone. On the cone the multiplication drops rank and the annihilator is a nonzero subspace. The physical statement is the usual one for maps: a process is reversible if and only if it loses nothing, and the dimension of the annihilator is the number of independent inputs the process sends to zero. The corpus's operator articles read the rank of the multiplications directly, in The Operators of the Ordinary Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich and The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket.

The annihilator lies in the zero-divisor set. When $\tilde P\neq0$ kills $\tilde X\neq0$, the element $\tilde X$ is itself a zero divisor of the same product with witness $\tilde P$. Every nonzero element of an annihilator is therefore a zero divisor, and for the four general products, whose zero-divisor set is the cone, the whole annihilator lies on the cone. The physics is the sharp statement that the killers of a lightlike element are lightlike: a null direction annihilates only along null directions. In the derived structures the zero-divisor set is larger and the annihilator can leave the cone, the unit $e_1$ being annihilated in the symmetrised products while off the cone, as Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures Framework records.

The Two Sides: Chirality of an Operation

The two annihilators are the kernels of the left and right multiplications, and "left" and "right" are the two ways an operation can act. When the two coincide, the operation has a single kernel; when they differ, the operation kills different things depending on the side.

The mathematics companion proves that they differ in exactly two structures, the plain product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ and the general quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$, and only at a cone element. The physical reading is a chirality. The plain product is the composition of two material operations, which is not commutative, and the gauge coupling is its conjugate; in both, the element and its conjugate play different roles on the two sides, so the left multiplication and the right one are different maps with different kernels. In the other ten structures the product is commutative, or conjugate-commutative, or alternating, and the exchange of the two factors does not change whether the product vanishes, so the two kernels coincide. The corpus has already named the operator face of this chirality: the internal action is one-sided, only the right multiplication by a unitary being an isometry of the fourth product's form, in Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action. The two-sided theorem is the annihilator face of the same chirality.

At the non-pure cone element $e_0+ie_1$ the two plain planes are

$$ A_{\text{plain}}(e_0+ie_1)=\mathbb{C}\{e_0-ie_1,\ e_3+ie_2\},\qquad B_{\text{plain}}(e_0+ie_1)=\mathbb{C}\{e_0-ie_1,\ e_3-ie_2\}. $$

The two planes share the line of the natural conjugate $\tilde P^{\natural}=e_0-ie_1$ and differ in their second generator, so the two-sided annihilator is the line of the conjugate and the operation kills the conjugate on both sides but only one more direction on each side. The shared line is the physical residue of the symmetry: $\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=N(\tilde P)e_0=0$ on the cone, so a lightlike element always kills its own conjugate, whichever side the multiplication is taken.

The Four General Products: the Lightlike Kernel

Fix a nonzero cone element, $N(\tilde P)=0$. Each of the four general products gives it a plane, and the mathematics companion proves that the four planes are the plain plane read through the two conjugations,

$$ A_{\natural}(\tilde P)=A_{\text{plain}}(\tilde P^{\natural}),\qquad A_{*}(\tilde P)=A_{\text{plain}}(\tilde P)^{*},\qquad A_{\natural*}(\tilde P)=A_{\text{plain}}(\tilde P^{\natural})^{*}. $$

At $e_0+ie_1$ the four left planes are the plain plane $\mathbb{C}\{e_0-ie_1,e_3+ie_2\}$, the natural plane $\mathbb{C}\{e_0+ie_1,e_2+ie_3\}$, the plain sesquilinear plane $\mathbb{C}\{e_1+ie_0,e_2+ie_3\}$ and the general quaternionic sesquilinear plane $\mathbb{C}\{e_0+ie_1,e_2-ie_3\}$.

The physical reading has two parts. First, the four planes are not four independent objects: they are one plane and its two conjugates, so the four bands differ by which conjugation is applied to the kernel, not by the kernel itself. The plain plane contains the lightlike directions that the composition sends to zero, and the other three are its images, so the composition band determines the kernel of the pairing and of the gauge band. Second, the plane is made of cone elements: the killers of a null momentum are null. This is the algebraic form of the statement that a lightlike signal can be annihilated only by another lightlike signal, and it is the reason the cone is closed under the annihilator operation on the four general rows. The rank of the multiplications and the collapse of the congruences on the cone are read in The 2×2 Matrix Operator Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_2(\mathbb{C})$ of Biquaternions and in The Cone and the Massless Particles in the 12 Structures Framework.

At the pure cone element $e_1+ie_2$ the plain planes are

$$ A_{\text{plain}}(e_1+ie_2)=\mathbb{C}\{e_1+ie_2,\ e_0+ie_3\},\qquad B_{\text{plain}}(e_1+ie_2)=\mathbb{C}\{e_1+ie_2,\ e_0-ie_3\}, $$

so a lightlike element lies in its own annihilator, as it must: $\tilde P^{2}=0$ for a pure cone element, and the square is the product of the element with itself. The self-annihilation of a lightlike displacement is the elementary physical case, and Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework reads it as the square-zero class.

The Hyperplane of a Scalar-Valued Coupling

Two structures collapse their product to a form: the symmetric quaternionic algebra $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=N(\tilde P,\tilde Q)e_0$ with $N(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=P_0Q_0+(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)$, and the symmetric plain sesqualgebra $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)e_0$ with $H(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=P_0\overline{Q_0}+(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf Q})$. For each, the annihilator of any nonzero element is the hyperplane cut by the form,

$$ P_0X_0+(\mathbf P,\mathbf X)=0\quad\text{(SQA)},\qquad P_0\overline{X_0}+(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf X})=0\quad\text{(SPS)} . $$

The physical object is a coupling that returns a number. A number has no slot in which to act, so the operation "couple with $\tilde P$" is not an invertible map; it is a scalar functional, and its kernel is a hyperplane. The reading of the hyperplane is orthogonality: the partners the coupling sends to zero are the partners orthogonal to $\tilde P$ in the corresponding form. For the symmetric plain sesqualgebra the form $H$ is the Born pairing, so its hyperplane is the set of amplitudes whose overlap with $\tilde P$ is zero, that is, the amplitudes with which $\tilde P$ has zero transition amplitude. Orthogonal states interfere to nothing, and the hyperplane is the algebraic home of that fact. For the symmetric quaternionic algebra the form is $N$, the interval form at the two equal arguments, and the hyperplane is its polar: the partners at zero interval with $\tilde P$.

The kernel of a scalar coupling is a hyperplane for every nonzero element, so in these two rows every element destroys exactly one complex dimension of information. This is the largest annihilator a nonzero element can have without killing the whole space, and it is the algebraic reason these two structures are couplings and not compositions. The physical caveat is that the coupling is not invertible in any case; its kernel is nonzero because a number cannot act, not because the element is lightlike. The owner articles of the two rows are the symmetric structures of the coupling band, and the form $H$ and its cone are read in Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone.

The Bracket and the Centre: the Centraliser

The four antisymmetric structures are the brackets of the algebra, and their annihilators are the sets of partners with which the bracket vanishes, that is, the centralisers of the element in the three cases where the bracket is a commutator and its analogue. The clean physical case is the plain commutator $\tfrac12(\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P)=\mathbf P\times\mathbf Q$, whose annihilator is $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf P$: the partners the bracket sends to zero are the centre and the element's own direction. Two facts follow, and both are physical.

  • An element lies in its own annihilator. The bracket of an element with itself is zero, because a commutator is alternating, so every element is in its own centraliser. The physical statement is that the bracket measures non-commutativity, and an element commutes with itself.
  • A central element kills everything. At $\tilde P=qe_0$ the vector part is zero and the bracket vanishes for every partner, so the annihilator is the whole algebra. A central element is a global phase: it commutes with every operation, and the bracket, which measures the failure to commute, does nothing to it. The centre is the kernel of the adjoint action, and the two rows of the table where some nonzero element has the whole space as its annihilator are exactly the two antisymmetrisations $\mathrm{APA}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}$.

The general quaternionic antisymmetrisation separates the two cases of the cone by the size of the annihilator: at an element of nonzero norm the annihilator is the line of the element itself, and at a cone element it is a plane containing that line. The reading is the same as for the plain bracket, with the norm deciding whether the centraliser grows from a line to a plane. The conjugate-alternating rows $\mathrm{APS}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}$ act on the conjugate direction, and the rows whose bracket is conjugate-alternating annihilate their own conjugate rather than themselves in the generic case.

The Kernel in the Quantum Corpus: Zero Modes, Null Spaces and No-Cloning

The annihilator is the kernel of a multiplication, and the quantum corpus already gives the kernel three further jobs. Setting them beside the table makes the annihilator's physical content more than a bookkeeping of rank drops.

The zero-mode count. The dimension of the annihilator is the number of independent inputs the operation sends to zero, which is the elementary form of the count an index theorem performs. The Index Theorem and the Zero-Mode Count in Biquaternionic Form counts the zero modes of a Dirac operator and equates their chiral difference to a topological charge, the framework supplying the operator and the density and not the counting. The annihilator of the multiplication by $\tilde P$ is the kernel whose dimension that theorem counts in the field case: a nonzero kernel is a zero mode of the elementary operation, and the trivial kernel of a unit is the absence of zero modes. The differential operator sits one level above the multiplication, but the word "count" means the same dimension in both places.

The GNS null space. The GNS Construction in the Biquaternion Framework builds the Hilbert space of a state as the quotient of the algebra by the state's null space, $\mathcal N_\omega=\{\tilde A:\omega(\tilde A^{*}\tilde A)=0\}$, and finds that for the vacuum the null space is the left ideal the annihilation operator lies in; the GNS space is then the minimal left ideal, of complex dimension two, and the representation is irreducible. A state with a nontrivial null space is not faithful, and its GNS space is smaller than the regular one. The annihilator of an element is the same object for a single multiplication: a one-sided ideal the element kills, whose size measures how much of the algebra the element overlooks. The vacuum's annihilator and the vacuum's GNS null space are two names for one left ideal.

The no-cloning obstruction. No-Cloning and the Algebraic Obstruction in Biquaternionic Form locates the obstruction in the trace pairing of two idempotents: a cloning map would have to preserve the pairing and replace it by its square, and no linear map does both. The annihilator gives the obstruction in its crudest form. An operation that kills a nonzero input is not invertible; an operation that is not invertible has no inverse process; and the existence of a nonzero kernel is the failure of reversibility on which every no-go theorem rests. The universal cloner of that article is a sum of conjugations and not a unit, exactly as the irreversible channels of Quantum Channels and the Reversible/Irreversible Dichotomy are sums rather than a single conjugation.

Zero overlap and the centraliser. The hyperplane and centraliser rows of the table are the two ways the kernel becomes a physical relation. In the probability band the annihilator of $\tilde P$ is the set of $\tilde X$ with $\langle\tilde P,\tilde X\rangle=0$, the set of amplitudes of zero overlap with $\tilde P$; the annihilator of an amplitude is its orthogonal complement in the Born pairing, and orthogonality of states is the standard physical instance of a nontrivial kernel. In the bracket rows the annihilator is the centraliser, the set of partners that commute with the element; at a central element the bracket kills the whole algebra, which is the operator form of the statement that the centre carries no bracket and no gauge charge. The Centre of the Biquaternion Algebra as the Classical Sector reads the centre as the gauge-blind part of the algebra, and the whole-space annihilator is that blindness written as a kernel. Gauge Redundancy and the Information in the Gauge Orbit in Biquaternionic Form reads the same kernel as the redundancy of a gauge orbit: the directions a bracket annihilates are the directions that carry no gauge-invariant information.

The Kernel in Cohomology, Constraints, Polarisation and Channels

Four further jobs of the kernel are already written in the corpus, and each sharpens the annihilator reading of the table by naming a different use of the same set.

Cohomology: the kernel with the image removed. BRST Symmetry in Biquaternionic Form constructs a Grassmann-odd derivation $s$ with $s^{2}=0$ and identifies the physical states with its cohomology, $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\ker s/\mathrm{im}\,s$. The annihilator of the nilpotent $s$ is the kernel $\ker s$, and the cohomology is that kernel quotiented by the image. The reading is a sharpening of the invertibility criterion: the kernel of an operation is the set it sends to zero, and when the operation is a nilpotent differential the kernel contains the image as a subspace, so the physical content is the kernel with the reachable part discarded. In the language of this article, the annihilator is the set of states the differential does not reach, and the cohomology removes the states that are themselves reached by it. The cohomology is the object the nilpotent article owns, read there as the odd nilpotent, and the two companions meet at it.

Constraints: the kernel of a divergence. The Schwinger–Dyson Equations and the Ward–Takahashi Identities in Biquaternionic Form derives, as the quantum Noether theorem, the statement that the Noether current of the central phase is conserved inside correlation functions, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde J)=0$. A conserved current is a current in the kernel of the divergence: the conservation equation says that the four-divergence of the current annihilates it, so the physical currents are the annihilator of the divergence operator, and a current that fails conservation is not in that kernel. The same article reads the abelian Ward identity as the vanishing of the longitudinal part of the photon vacuum polarisation, $Z_1=Z_2$: the physical polarisations are the ones the transverse projector leaves, so the longitudinal sector is what that projector kills. Noether's Theorem in Biquaternionic Form locates the current in the material sector, so the constraint that defines the physical current is a kernel in the same sector as the lightlike displacements of the zero-divisor article. The kernel is the classical statement, and Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation in Biquaternionic Form records what quantisation does to it: the one-loop divergence of the current is not zero, so the current is no longer in the kernel. For a global symmetry the failure is the physical axial anomaly; for a gauge symmetry it is fatal unless the matter spectrum cancels it, so the anomaly is a statement about how much the classical kernel shrinks, and the cancellation condition is a condition on the spectrum that keeps the kernel large enough for the theory to be consistent.

The kernel is observer-dependent. A kernel belongs to an operator, and the operator a mode expansion defines is not the same for every observer. Bogoliubov Transformations in Biquaternionic Form makes this precise: a Bogoliubov transformation with $v\neq0$ mixes $\tilde a$ with $\tilde a^{\dagger}$, and it is not implementable by a unitary on the Fock space of the original modes — it maps the original vacuum to a squeezed state, and the two vacua are not unitarily equivalent as states of the same algebra. The transformed annihilator therefore does not annihilate the original vacuum, and "the state of no particles" is defined only relative to a choice of mode basis. The Unruh Effect in Biquaternionic Form and Hawking Radiation in Biquaternionic Form are the two physical instances: the inertial and the accelerated, or the in- and out-, vacua are related by a transformation of exactly this kind, and the thermality of the radiation is the statement that the particle notion, and with it the annihilator, has changed. The annihilator is thus not an observer-independent set; it is the kernel that one's own mode expansion supplies.

The kernel of the Dirac operator is the zero-mode space, and the operator carries the geometry. The Spectral Triple of the Biquaternion Algebra puts the framework's own objects into Connes' triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$: the algebra, the spinor module, and the biquaternion Dirac operator $\mathcal{D}=\tilde\nabla+\tilde m$. Two consequences name new jobs of the kernel. First, the triple recovers the distance on the state space from $D$ by a supremum over the algebra, so the operator is what defines the metric; the elements of $\ker D$ are the zero modes, the points a measurement cannot resolve, which is the operator-level restatement of the zero-mode count. Second, the gauge fields are inner fluctuations of $D$, $D\mapsto D+\sum_i a_i[D,b_i]$: the gauge structure is generated by the commutators of the algebra with $D$, so the very object that fails to vanish on the physical modes is what carries the gauge redundancy. The annihilator table of this article and the spectral triple are therefore two readings of the same operator: the kernel is the resolved-to-zero content of the geometry.

Polarisation: the kernel of the skew operator. The Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and the Polarisation computes the left multiplication of the row that this article reads through the table, and finds

$$ \ker L^{\diamond}_{\tilde A}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf A},\qquad \operatorname{im}L^{\diamond}_{\tilde A}=\{(\mathbf A,\cdot)=0\}. $$

The kernel is the centre together with the conjugate direction of the parameter, and the image is the plane perpendicular to it: the operator of that band maps the whole module into one direction and kills the rest, which is why the article reads the band under the job word polarisation. The annihilator rows of the two antisymmetric sesquilinear structures, which the table records as the identity lying in its own kernel, are the algebraic statement of that image: an operator with a large kernel cannot be inverted, and the physical content it keeps is the single direction it selects.

Channels and the information the kernel destroys. The dimension of the kernel is the information an operation loses, and the corpus prices the loss. The Holevo Bound and Accessible Information in Biquaternionic Form bounds the classical information recoverable from an ensemble by the Holevo quantity. A measurement cannot separate two states exactly when every one of its positive operators annihilates the difference of the two state elements under the trace pairing, so that both states give the same outcome statistics and the distinction between them is inaccessible; the annihilator of the difference is the kernel that holds the lost information. Confinement and the Loss of Partonic Information in Biquaternionic Form supplies the sharpest case: the colour labels of an asymptotic state are not redundant descriptions held in a kernel that a gauge choice could recover, they are absent from the observable algebra, and the article distinguishes that loss from the gauge redundancy of Gauge Redundancy and the Information in the Gauge Orbit in Biquaternionic Form, where the kernel is a redundancy. A kernel therefore has two opposite readings, and the corpus keeps them apart: a gauge kernel hides a description, a confinement kernel has destroyed one.

Physical Interpretation

  • The annihilator of an element is the kernel of the operation "multiply by it". A process is invertible exactly when its kernel is trivial, and the dimension of the kernel is the amount of information it destroys.
  • The killers of a lightlike element are lightlike. The annihilator of a cone element lies on the cone, and a null direction annihilates only along null directions.
  • The two one-sided annihilators coincide in ten structures and differ in two, the plain composition and the gauge coupling. The difference is a chirality: an operation and its conjugate play different roles on the two sides, and at a lightlike element the left and right kernels are distinct planes.
  • The four planes of a cone element in the four general products are one plain plane and its two conjugate images. The composition band determines the kernels of the pairing and of the gauge band.
  • A scalar-valued coupling has a hyperplane as its annihilator. The hyperplane is the set of partners with which the coupling gives zero, which for the Born pairing is the set of amplitudes orthogonal to the element, that is, of zero overlap.
  • A bracket has the centraliser as its annihilator. Every element lies in its own centraliser, and a central element, a global phase, is annihilated by every partner, so the bracket is blind to the centre.
  • The annihilator is the zero-divisor condition with its witnesses displayed. It is the entry to the section's later articles: the square roots of zero are the elements that annihilate themselves, and the idempotents are the projectors whose kernel is a full half of the space.
  • The kernel is also the zero-mode space of The Index Theorem and the Zero-Mode Count in Biquaternionic Form, the GNS null space of The GNS Construction in the Biquaternion Framework, and the algebraic obstruction behind No-Cloning and the Algebraic Obstruction in Biquaternionic Form: one object, and four physical names for it.
  • The same kernel is the BRST cohomology $\ker s/\mathrm{im}\,s$ of the physical state space, the kernel of the divergence that defines a conserved current, and the polarisation kernel $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\tilde A}$ of the antisymmetric sesquilinear operator.
  • A kernel has two opposite readings. A gauge kernel hides a description that a gauge choice can recover; a confinement kernel has destroyed a description that the observable algebra does not contain.
  • The kernel can shrink under quantisation and change under a change of observer. An anomaly is a current that is no longer in the kernel of the divergence at one loop, and its cancellation is a condition that keeps the kernel large enough; a Bogoliubov transformation with $v\neq0$ changes the annihilator itself, so the vacuum of one mode expansion is not annihilated by the operators of another (Unruh, Hawking).

Summary

The annihilator of an element in one of the twelve products is the kernel of the corresponding multiplication, and the mathematics companion computes it for all twelve, with the case split and the generators. Read physically, it is the set of inputs a process sends to zero, and it carries three statements. First, a process is invertible exactly when its kernel is trivial; off the cone the four general products have trivial kernel, and on the cone the kernel is a plane of other lightlike directions, so lightlike elements are killed along lightlike directions. Second, the two one-sided kernels coincide in ten structures and differ in the plain composition and the gauge coupling, which is the chirality of an operation against its adjoint, and at a cone element the two kernels are distinct planes sharing the line of the conjugate. Third, the shape of the kernel names the kind of structure: a scalar-valued coupling has a hyperplane, the set of partners of zero overlap, and a bracket has the centraliser, the whole algebra at a central element, because the central element is a global phase that commutes with everything. The four planes of a cone element in the four general products are one plain plane and its two conjugate images, so the composition band fixes the kernels of the other three. The annihilator is thus the zero-divisor condition with its witnesses displayed, and it is the natural entry to the roots of zero, the idempotents and the nilpotents of the rest of the section.

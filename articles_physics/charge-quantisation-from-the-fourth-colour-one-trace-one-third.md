# __Charge Quantisation from the Fourth Colour: One Trace, One Third__

## Introduction

**This article derives; it does not survey.** [The 15 AXF Matrices of the Bosons in Detail](the-15-axf-matrices-of-the-bosons-in-detail.html) prints the fifteen arrays one at a time, and its subsection on $B'$ makes six statements about one array. **Five quotations follow, each a block taken word for word from that subsection.** Each is followed by what it forces. Nothing else is assumed, no array is recomputed, and no vacuum is chosen.

**The array, as the detail article prints it.** It is the corner of the fifteen, and it carries the only entry of modulus three in the whole fifteen:

$$
iD=i\bigl(W_{xx}+W_{yy}+W_{zz}\bigr)=\begin{pmatrix}-3i & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & i & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & i & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & i\end{pmatrix}
$$

**The order is $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$**, the lepton line first and the three colour lines after, and it is the order of every display in this article.

**The five results, and the quotation each one comes from.** This table is the plan of the article.

| result | from which quotation |
|---|---|
| $\ell+3q=0$, so $q=-\tfrac13\ell$ | the trace is zero |
| the charge quantum is $e/3$ | the corner entry is $-3$, the count of colour lines |
| the seven charges of a generation | $B-L=\tfrac13D$ |
| the lepton's $B-L$ is $-1$ at every scale | $B'$ acts on the lepton line as $-3i$, so $B-L=-1$ |
| the relative phase of lepton and colour is $4\theta$ | four phases and no rotation |

**What the chain needs beyond the quotations, stated once so that it is not smuggled in.** Two things, and neither is a free parameter of the argument. The first is the identification (A): the corner eigenvalue belongs to the lepton and the three equal eigenvalues belong to the three colours. The trace alone says "one against three"; (A) says which is which. The second is the combination $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, by which hypercharge is built from the right-handed neutral direction and the corner. Both belong to the model and both are fixed elsewhere: (A) in [The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model](the-su4-model-four-colours-fifteen-generators-one-declaration.html), the combination in [The Weak Sector Below $M_C$](the-weak-sector-below-m-c-three-neutrals-a-diagonal-charged-pair-and-the-bidoublet.html). **Everything else in this article follows from the five quotations.**

## Quotation One: the Entries, and the Trace

**The detail article, §$B'$:**

> **The entries.** Four diagonal cells, and the only entry of modulus $\ne1$ in the whole fifteen: $-3i$ at the corner. **Trace zero because $-3+1+1+1=0$**, norm $12$ because $9+1+1+1=12$, spectrum $-3i,i,i,i$.

**And, in the same subsection:**

> **The corner cell carries minus the sum of the other three**, and that is the only way a diagonal with three equal entries can be traceless.

**What follows: the ratio, and only the ratio.** Write the eigenvalues in the order above as $\ell,q,q,q$, with $\ell$ the corner and $q$ the three colour entries; both are real because $D$ is Hermitian. Trace zero is one equation,

$$
\ell+3q=0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ell=-3q ,
$$

and that is the whole computation. **The quark's eigenvalue is minus a third of the lepton's because the lepton stands once and the colours stand three times.**

**The general statement, which the quotation's number hides.** In a four-row array the coefficient is $3$; in an $N$-row array it is $N-1$, one lepton line against $N-1$ colour lines:

$$
\ell+(N-1)q=0 .
$$

Four colours give a third. Three colours would give a half, and that is the arithmetic reason the observed third is evidence for a fourth line and not for a coincidence.

**What the quotation's second sentence adds, and it is more than emphasis.** Given the three equal entries, the fourth is **forced**. So the declaration that three lines are equivalent and one is different leaves no freedom in the diagonal: the pattern $(-3,1,1,1)$ is not a choice among traceless diagonals, it is the only one of that shape. **The trace does not select the numbers; it selects the corner once the shape is declared, and the model supplies the shape.**

**What the trace does not fix, said plainly because the next section needs it.** $\operatorname{diag}(-6,2,2,2)$ has the same vanishing trace and the same ratio. So does $\operatorname{diag}(-\tfrac12,\tfrac16,\tfrac16,\tfrac16)$. **The trace fixes the ratio and nothing else**, and the scale is fixed later, by naming which of these arrays is the generator.

## Quotation Two: the Corner Entry, and the Unit

**The detail article, §$B'$, continuing the same sentence:**

> **The same equation read as charges is $3\cdot(+1)+(-3)=0$** — and the $-3$ is the count of colour lines, the same number that appears as $N-1$ in the charge quantum $e/(N-1)$.

**What follows: no charge can be finer than a third.** The eigenvalues of $D$ give two offsets, $+\tfrac16$ and $-\tfrac12$, once divided by six; the isospin adds $0$, $\pm\tfrac12$ or $\pm1$. The values a state attains are four:

$$
Q\in\Bigl\{\,0,\ -1,\ +\tfrac23,\ -\tfrac13\,\Bigr\}.
$$

**Their differences are $\tfrac23$, $\tfrac13$ and $1$**, so the lattice they generate is exactly $\tfrac13\mathbb{Z}$, and the smallest step is $\tfrac13$. The neighbouring values $\pm\tfrac12$ and $+\tfrac16$ are not excluded by arithmetic; they are excluded by the content, because no field of the sixteen carries an isospin and an eigenvalue that give them.

**The unit is attained, and that is why it is a unit and not a bound.** The down quark carries $-\tfrac13$. A set of charges whose smallest difference is $\tfrac13$ and which contains $\tfrac13$ itself has $\tfrac13$ as its unit, not merely as its denominator.

**The number is doing the work, and it appears twice.** The corner entry is $-3$ because three colour lines contribute one each, and the quantum is a third because the lepton stands against three. **The same count, read once as an entry and once as a denominator**, which is what the quotation says in its own words.

**And the entry is not the trace.** The trace is removed by deleting one word from the sixteen, which is a bookkeeping step on the list of arrays; $-3$ is an eigenvalue of one of the fifteen that remain. The two numbers agree because both count colour lines, and they are different objects.

## Quotation Three: the Boson, and the Seven Charges

**The detail article, §$B'$:**

> **The boson.** $B'$ is the $B-L$ boson, and **the array is the one the vacuum is built from**. With the $i$ removed, $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ is the Hermitian direction of the breaking, $Ξ_0=vD$, and $B-L=\tfrac13D$ on the $\mathbf4$: the charge is this array divided by three, which in charges reads $(-1,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ with the lepton first.

**What follows: the charge of every state of one generation.** Substituting the quoted $B-L$ into the quoted combination $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ and adding the left isospin gives $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac16D$. The seven states then read:

| state | $T_{3L}$ | $T_{3R}$ | $D$ | $Q$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\nu_L$ | $+\tfrac12$ | $0$ | $-3$ | $0$ |
| $e_L$ | $-\tfrac12$ | $0$ | $-3$ | $-1$ |
| $u_L$ | $+\tfrac12$ | $0$ | $+1$ | $+\tfrac23$ |
| $d_L$ | $-\tfrac12$ | $0$ | $+1$ | $-\tfrac13$ |
| $u_R$ | $0$ | $+\tfrac12$ | $+1$ | $+\tfrac23$ |
| $d_R$ | $0$ | $-\tfrac12$ | $+1$ | $-\tfrac13$ |
| $e_R$ | $0$ | $-\tfrac12$ | $-3$ | $-1$ |

**Every row is one addition**: a half-integer isospin and a sixth of an integer eigenvalue. The seven charges are the last column, and no row uses a number that is not in the quotation or in the combination.

**What follows: the proton and the electron cancel, and the cancellation is two cancellations.** The proton is three quarks, each with $D=+1$; the electron is one lepton with $D=-3$. **In $D$ the two sides weigh $+3$ and $-3$, and the sum is zero because three colour lines weigh exactly one lepton line** — that is quotation one, read on a composite state. In isospin the proton's three quarks give $2\cdot\tfrac12-\tfrac12=+\tfrac12$ and the electron gives $-\tfrac12$, and that sum is zero as well. **Both pieces vanish separately, which is why the equality of the two charges is exact and not approximate.**

**What follows: the mixed anomaly row vanishes, and it vanishes term by term.** On the left-handed multiplet $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)$ the hypercharge is $Y=\tfrac16D$, and

$$
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(T_{3L}^{2}Y\bigr)=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac16\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf4}D=0 ,
$$

the factor $\tfrac12$ being $\operatorname{tr}(T_{3L}^{2})$ over the doublet and $\tfrac16$ the coefficient of $D$. **So the row does not cancel: it vanishes, because the trace is zero.** A reader who expects anomaly cancellation to constrain the content will find the constraint already met by the array.

**One consequence of the division by three, stated because it is often confused.** $B-L=\tfrac13D$ has eigenvalues $(-1,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ and $D$ has $(-3,1,1,1)$: the same direction, two normalisations. **A charge statement is a statement about a direction; a matrix statement is a statement about an array.** Neither is more fundamental, and this article names which of the two it is using.

## Quotation Four: What the Vacuum Does Not See

**The detail article, §$B'$:**

> **What the vacuum does not see.** $[iD,Ξ_0]=0$, so this array is invisible to the first breaking

> **The unique statement is narrower and it is the one that matters here**: of the seven arrays that touch the $t$ line, $B'$ is the only one that leaves it in place.

**What follows: the corner survives the colour half of the transition, and the doublet half is where it goes.** A generator that commutes with the vacuum value is unbroken by the step that imposes that value, and the quotation says it of the colour breaking. Because that step is the one that could have destroyed the corner — it is the step that removes six of the fifteen — **$B-L$ comes through it intact**. **Then the same transition's doublet vev lands on the lepton line, where $B-L=-1$, and breaks it.** Reading the two facts in the right order is the whole of the section: the corner is *not removed by the six*, and it *is* removed by the one.

**The eigenvalue is what survives, and it is worth separating from the charge.** Two statements, and only the first is true.

- **The charge lattice does not move.** $B-L$ takes the values $(-1,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ on a quartet, and those four numbers are the same at every scale: an eigenvalue belongs to the array, and no mixing between $W^{3}_{L}$, $W^{3}_{R}$ and $B'$ touches a diagonal entry. The unit $\tfrac13$ is therefore the same everywhere. What runs is a coupling, not the charge it multiplies.
- **The charge is nevertheless broken at $M_C$, by the vacuum that carries it.** $[B-L,Ξ_0]=-Ξ_0\neq0$ on the lepton line, so by the criterion every article in this block uses, $B-L$ is not conserved below $M_C$; what annihilates the vacuum is $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$. **"The chain never renormalises the corner" and "the chain never breaks the corner" are different sentences**, and this article keeps the first without the second ([The Quantum Numbers Below the Breaking](the-quantum-numbers-below-the-breaking-colour-b-l-weak-isospin-and-the-electric-charge.html) has the full ledger).

**And the other two neutral directions are not so lucky in either sense.** $T_{3R}$ is broken at $M_C$ and $T_{3L}$ at $M_W$. Of the three ingredients of the neutral sector, the corner is the only one the six leptoquark generators leave alone — **of the seven arrays that touch the $t$ line, $B'$ is the only one that leaves it in place** — and that is a statement about the colour part of the vacuum, not about the doublet part.

**What this buys, in one sentence.** The lepton line is an eigenline of the corner with a fixed eigenvalue at every scale, so the lepton's share of $B-L$ is exactly $-1$ everywhere — **and the vev that sits on that line is exactly why the charge it labels is not conserved below $M_C$.**

## Quotation Five: the Exponential, and the Relative Phase

**The detail article, §$B'$, on the exponential of the array:**

> **The array moves no index and turns no plane**; it multiplies the four lines by four phases.

**What follows: one global phase and one physical phase.** The four phases are $e^{-3i\theta}$ and $e^{i\theta}$ three times, so the matrix factorises into a scalar and a single distinguished phase:

$$
\exp(\theta\,iD)=e^{i\theta}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(e^{-4i\theta},\,1,\,1,\,1\bigr).
$$

**Only $4\theta$ is physical.** The common factor $e^{i\theta}$ multiplies every line and can be removed by a global rephasing; what remains is a phase on the lepton line at four times the rate of the rotation parameter.

**The $4$ is the number of lines, and here the arithmetic closes.** The difference between the two eigenvalues is $\ell-q=-(N-1)q-q=-Nq$, and with $q=1$ and $N=4$ it is $4$. **So the relative phase between the lepton and the colours advances $N$ times as fast as the common phase**, the same $N$ that appears as $N-1$.

**One exact consequence, easy to state and easy to check.** Set $4\theta=2\pi$. Then $\exp(\theta\,iD)$ is a multiple of the identity, and it happens four times in a full turn:

| $\theta$ | $\exp(\theta\,iD)$ |
|---|---|
| $0$ | $\mathrm I_4$ |
| $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ | $i\,\mathrm I_4$ |
| $\pi$ | $-\mathrm I_4$ |
| $\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ | $-i\,\mathrm I_4$ |

**At $\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ the lepton and the three colours carry the same phase**, and the "one against three" asymmetry disappears: a quarter turn removes it. That is the exponential form of the statement that the lepton and the quarks differ by four units of the corner's charge, and no reader can make it from the trace alone.

## One Direction, Three Normalisations

**The quoted norm $12$ fixes the normalisation of the direction, and the rest is division.** The detail article states "norm $12$ because $9+1+1+1=12$", that is $\operatorname{tr}\bigl((iD)^{\dagger}iD\bigr)=12$ in the fundamental. Two other normalisations of the same direction are in use in the corpus, and both follow by scaling:

$$
\operatorname{tr}\bigl((iD)^{\dagger}iD\bigr)=12,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\bigl((B-L)^{\dagger}(B-L)\bigr)=\tfrac19\cdot 12=\tfrac43,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\bigl((T^{15})^{\dagger}T^{15}\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{24}\cdot 12=\tfrac12 .
$$

**The conversion between the last two is one number**, $B-L=\sqrt{\tfrac{8}{3}}\,T^{15}$, because $\tfrac43$ divided by $\tfrac12$ is $\tfrac83$. A reader who meets $T^{15}$ in a gauge article and $iD$ in the array articles is meeting one direction in two normalisations, and the ratio of the norms is the dictionary.

| name | array | norm | where it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| $iD$ | $\operatorname{diag}(-3i,i,i,i)$ | $12$ | the array articles, and this one |
| $B-L$ | $\operatorname{diag}(-1,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ | $\tfrac43$ | the charge statements, and this one |
| $T^{15}$ | $\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)/\sqrt{24}$ | $\tfrac12$ | the gauge articles and the running |

**The displayed array in the corpus's own notation is $D$, not $iD$.** The physics articles print $D=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$ and the generator is $iD$; the difference is the factor that makes the array anti-Hermitian, which the boson articles discuss and on which no charge statement depends. **This article prints $D$ in its charge statements and $iD$ when it is speaking of the generator**, and that is the whole of the convention.

## The Comparison with the Standard Model

**The claim is about assumptions, not about reach, and it has to be stated in that form.** The Standard Model assigns five hypercharges and then imposes four anomaly conditions; the frame fixes one array and reads its trace. **Both reach the ratio.** The difference is the price.

**The Standard Model's system, solved on its own content.** With the content assumed, the three linear conditions $\operatorname{tr}(T_{3L}^{2}Y)=0$, $\operatorname{tr}Y=0$ and the gravitational one leave a two-dimensional space of solutions, spanned by $(1,1,1,-3,-6)$ and $(0,1,-1,0,0)$; the cubic $\operatorname{tr}Y^{3}=0$ cuts it to three rays, and one of them is the physical assignment. **The cubic is satisfied automatically on this content once the linear ones hold**, so the derivation is shorter than it looks, but the field list and the single-doublet Yukawa structure are inputs.

**The frame's system.** One array, declared to have three equal entries and one corner. The trace gives the ratio; (A) gives the interpretation; nothing else is chosen. **There is no cubic to impose**, because there is no assignment to solve: the charge is a multiple of one eigenvalue.

**Where the advantage is real, and where it is not.** It is real in robustness: a new quartet added to the frame contributes zero to the trace and cannot move the ratio, whereas a new chiral field in the Standard Model is a new unknown in the same system. It is not real in reach: neither theory fixes the value of $e$, and neither explains why the lepton's eigenvalue is $-3$ rather than $-6$. **The gain is parsimony.**

## What Is Explained and What Is Not

**A four-row table, so that the claim cannot be inflated.**

| question | answered by | answer |
|---|---|---|
| why is $Q_p=-Q_e$? | the tracelessness of $D$ | because $3q+\ell=0$ |
| why is every charge a multiple of $e/3$? | the corner entry, plus the integrality of $\ell$ | bookkeeping |
| why is the electron's charge $-1$? | the normalisation of $B-L$ | a stated convention, not a measurement |
| why is $e$ the value it is? | nothing | it is measured |

**The first row is the frame's genuine answer**, and quotation three shows it twice: once in $D$, once in isospin. **The third row is a convention and is not arbitrary**: only the product of the array with the coupling enters physics, so the split between them is a choice, and the corpus states which choice it makes. **The fourth row is outside any group theory**: $e$ is a coupling, it runs, and its value at a scale is measured.

**What would refute the claim.** Three ways, and naming them is part of the argument. If the fourth colour were not made local, the trace would hold as algebra and say nothing about nature. If a fourth-colour-like resonance had the wrong $B-L$, the identification of the corner with the lepton line would be wrong. If a charge outside $\tfrac13\mathbb{Z}$ were found in the visible sector, the lattice derived from the two eigenvalues would be wrong.

## Summary

**Five quotations, five results.** The trace is zero, so $\ell=-3q$, so the down quark's charge is a third of the electron's, so proton and electron cancel. The corner entry is $-3$, so the quantum is $e/(N-1)=e/3$ and it is attained. The array is $B-L$ times three, so the seven charges of a generation are one addition each and the mixed anomaly row vanishes. The array commutes with the colour part of the vacuum, so the lattice does not move and the lepton's $B-L$ stays $-1$ at every scale; the doublet part of the same vacuum sits on that line, so the charge itself is broken at $M_C$. The exponential is one global phase times a phase at four times the rate, so the lepton and the colours differ by $N$ units of the corner's charge and agree after a quarter turn.

**The one input that is not in the quotations** is the identification (A), which says that the corner is the lepton and the three equal entries are the colours. Without it the trace is arithmetic about a matrix; with it the arithmetic is a statement about nature.

**The limit.** The ratio is explained, the scale is a stated normalisation, and the value of $e$ is measured. Nothing in the five quotations touches the third.

**The traps.** Do not say "charge quantisation is derived" without the two clauses: the ratio is, the scale is not. Do not drop (A): the trace alone gives no quark–lepton assignment.

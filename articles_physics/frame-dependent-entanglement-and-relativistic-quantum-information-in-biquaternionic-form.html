Frame-Dependent Entanglement and Relativistic Quantum Information in Biquaternionic Form

Introduction

Entanglement is a property of a joint state, and a change of inertial frame is a change of the joint state. It is therefore not obvious that the entanglement of two particles should be the same for two observers in relative motion, and the relativistic quantum information literature has established that in general it is not: the reduced entropy of a particle's spin is frame-dependent when its momentum is uncertain, and the spin entanglement of a pair of wave packets changes under a boost. The mechanism is not a direct action of the boost on the spin. It is the momentum dependence of the Wigner rotation: at definite momentum a Lorentz transformation acts on the spin by a $*$-unitary element of the little group, and a momentum-independent $*$-unitary cannot change entanglement; when the momentum is a superposition, the Wigner rotation differs from branch to branch, the boost correlates spin with momentum, and tracing the momentum leaves a spin state whose entanglement has changed.

This article develops that mechanism inside the biquaternion framework. The joint state of two qubits is an element of the tensor-product algebra $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$; the reduced state of one qubit is a partial trace; the entanglement measures are the standard ones — the Schmidt coefficients, the concurrence, the entanglement entropy — computed with the algebra's Hermitian pairing. The relativistic content is the action of the unit-norm group on the spin factor, which at sharp momentum is the tensor product of two $*$-unitary Wigner rotations and at uncertain momentum is a momentum-controlled rotation. The article derives both regimes, obtains the sharp-momentum invariance as a theorem, produces the frame dependence as an explicit computation on a state with a two-branch momentum distribution, and states what the framework adds to the standard account and what it does not. A final section carries the same mechanism to a gravitational potential, where the branch phase is supplied by the position of a second mass rather than by a boost, and where the structure of the channel is unchanged while the effect becomes the one an experiment can look for.

The findings are stated in advance.

  1. At sharp momenta the spin entanglement is Lorentz invariant. A boost acts on a definite-momentum product state by the little-group rotations $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2}$, which are local unitaries; local unitaries preserve the Schmidt coefficients, the concurrence, and the entanglement entropy, and the reduced states are unchanged up to a rotation.
  2. The frame dependence requires momentum uncertainty. A boost on a state whose momentum is a superposition acts by a branch-dependent rotation; the spin state after tracing the momentum is a mixture of rotated copies of the original, and neither the concurrence nor the reduced entropy is invariant.
  3. The mechanism is spin–momentum entanglement generated by the boost. The boost is an entangling operation between the spin factor and the momentum factor whenever the Wigner rotation is momentum-dependent; the spin state loses purity exactly to the extent that this correlation is created, and the loss is the information-theoretic content of the boost.
  4. The symmetric two-branch case is a dephasing channel. When the momentum branches are symmetric about the boost axis, the two Wigner rotations are rotations about a common axis by opposite angles, and their average is the dephasing of the spin about that axis: the Bloch component along the Wigner axis is preserved and the transverse components are damped by $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$ is the Wigner angle.
  5. The $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$-invariant concurrence is a spinor concurrence. In the spinor description, the Lorentz rotor $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}$ — the unit-norm biquaternion with $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{\natural}=e_t$ — acts as $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}$ with unit determinant on each factor and leaves the symplectic concurrence $|\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathsf T}(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\tilde{Ξ}|$ exactly invariant; the frame dependence appears only after normalization, i.e. only for configurations with momentum spread, which is the algebraic statement that the effect is kinematic rather than dynamical.
  6. A gravitational potential drives the same channel, and it is the measurable case. A superposition of position, with the branch phase supplied by the Newtonian potential of a second mass, is a branch-dependent $*$-unitary phase of the material sector and entangles the two path qubits. The entangling phase is a mixed second difference of the potential, so a constant potential and a uniform field both give zero and only the tidal term survives, of order $d^{2}/R^{3}$; the concurrence is $\left|\sin(\delta/2)\right|$, and for $m = 10^{-14}$ kg at $R = 200\,\mu$m and $d = 100\,\mu$m it reaches $0.105$ in one second.

Entangled States in the Tensor Product

The joint state and the reduced state

The state space of two qubits is the tensor product $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$, isomorphic to $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ as a complex algebra, with the tensor trace $\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\mathsf M_2(x)\otimes\mathsf M_2(y)\bigr) = \mathrm{Tr}_\mathbb{B}\mathsf M_2(x)\mathrm{Tr}_\mathbb{B}\mathsf M_2(y)$. A pure two-qubit state is a rank-one idempotent, $$ \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}} = |\tilde{Ξ}\rangle\langle\tilde{Ξ}| \quad\text{with}\quad |\tilde{Ξ}\rangle\in S\otimes S, $$ and the reduced state of the first qubit is the partial trace $$ \mathrm{Tr}_2:\ \mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}, \qquad \mathrm{Tr}_2(\tilde{Q}_2\otimes\tilde{Q}_1) = \tilde{Q}_2\,\mathrm{Tr}_\mathbb{B}\mathsf M_2(\tilde{Q}_1), $$ extended linearly. On the singlet, $$ \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id,singlet}} = \tfrac14\bigl(e_t\otimes e_t + \vec e_x\otimes \vec e_x + \vec e_y\otimes \vec e_y + \vec e_z\otimes \vec e_z\bigr), $$ the partial trace kills the traceless tensor terms and returns the maximally mixed state, $$ \mathrm{Tr}_2\bigl(\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id,singlet}}\bigr) = \tfrac12 e_t , $$ whose von Neumann entropy is $\log 2$: the mixedness of the reduced state is the algebraic residue of the entanglement of the joint state.

Entanglement measures

Three measures are used below, all standard.

Schmidt coefficients. A pure two-qubit state admits the decomposition $|\tilde{Ξ}\rangle = \sum_{i=1}^2 s_i\,|a_i\rangle|b_i\rangle$ with $s_i\geq0$ and $s_1^2+s_2^2=1$; the $s_i^2$ are the eigenvalues of either reduced state, and the state is a product if and only if one coefficient vanishes.

Concurrence. With the two-qubit spin-flip $\tilde{\mathcal S}=(i\sigma_2)\otimes(i\sigma_2)$ in the matrix model, the concurrence of a pure state is $$ C(|\tilde{Ξ}\rangle) = \bigl|\langle\tilde{Ξ}|\,\tilde{\mathcal S}\,|\tilde{Ξ}^*\rangle\bigr| = 2\,s_1s_2 , $$ vanishing on product states and equal to $1$ on the Bell states; for a mixed state $\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}$ it is $C(\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}) = \max(0,\eta_1-\eta_2-\eta_3-\eta_4)$, where $\eta_1\geq\eta_2\geq\eta_3\geq\eta_4$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of the Hermitian positive-semidefinite matrix $\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}^{1/2}\,\tilde{\mathcal S}\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}^{*}\tilde{\mathcal S}\,\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}^{1/2}$.

Entanglement entropy. For a pure joint state it is the von Neumann entropy of either reduced state, $E = S(\mathrm{Tr}_2\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}) = -s_1^2\log s_1^2 - s_2^2\log s_2^2$, and it is a function of the single invariant $s_1^2$. For mixed joint states the partial-trace entropy is a marginal entropy and not an entanglement measure, as the companion exercise records; the concurrence is used for mixed states below.

The framework inherits all three unchanged: they are computed from the trace and the tensor product, and the algebra's normalised Hermitian form, read through the trace, is the Born weight. The relativistic question is what the unit-norm group does to them.

The Lorentz Transformation of a Two-Particle State

Sharp momenta: local $*$-unitary rotations

Consider two massive particles with sharp four-velocities $\tilde{V}_{\Phi,1},\tilde{V}_{\Phi,2}$. Their joint state is a product of a momentum part and a spin part, $$ |\tilde{Ξ}\rangle = |\tilde{V}_{\Phi,1}\rangle\otimes|\tilde{V}_{\Phi,2}\rangle\otimes|\xi\rangle , \qquad |\xi\rangle\in S\otimes S , $$ and a Lorentz transformation $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}$ maps the momentum labels to $\tilde{V}_{\Phi,j,2} = \tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\tilde{V}_{\Phi,j}\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{*}$ and applies to the spin the Wigner rotations of the two particles, $$ |\tilde{Ξ}\rangle \ \longmapsto\ |\tilde{V}_{\Phi,1,2}\rangle\otimes|\tilde{V}_{\Phi,2,2}\rangle\otimes\bigl(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2}\bigr)|\xi\rangle , \qquad \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},j} = \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig}}(\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}},\tilde{V}_{\Phi,j})\in SU(2). $$ This is the standard action of the Lorentz group on definite-momentum one-particle states, and it is the content of the Wigner construction: the momentum labels move, the spin is rotated by an element of the little group, and the transformation is $*$-unitary on the full Hilbert space.

The spin state is therefore transformed by a local $*$-unitary $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2}$. Local unitaries preserve every entanglement measure: $$ \boxed{\;C\bigl((\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2})|\xi\rangle\bigr) = C(|\xi\rangle), \qquad S\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}_2\,\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2})\xi}\bigr) = S\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}_2\,\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\xi}\bigr)\;}, $$ because $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2}$ preserves the tensor product structure and the Hermitian pairing, and because the Schmidt coefficients are the singular values of the coefficient matrix, which a pair of unitaries leaves unchanged. Hence:

Theorem (sharp-momentum invariance). For two particles of definite four-velocity, and for any Lorentz transformation, the spin entanglement of the pair — its Schmidt coefficients, its concurrence, and its entanglement entropy — is invariant.

The theorem is worth stating because it is the exact sense in which the framework reproduces the standard result, and because it isolates the only remaining source of frame dependence. A sharp momentum cannot produce a frame-dependent spin entanglement, because a momentum-independent $*$-unitary on the spin factor cannot create or destroy entanglement. If the framework is to exhibit frame dependence, it must come from a state that is not a momentum eigenstate.

A worked sharp-momentum instance

The theorem is easily checked in an instance that also supplies the Wigner rotor used below. Let particle $A$ have speed $0.5c$ and momentum direction $\vec u_p$ making angle $\theta = 0.8$ with the boost axis $\vec u_z$, and let particle $B$ have momentum along $\vec u_z$, so that $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},B} = e_t$. For a boost of rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 1$ the Wigner rotor of $A$ is $$ \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},A} = \cos\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}}{2}\,e_t + \sin\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}}{2}\,\vec u_y = 0.995308\,e_t + 0.096756\,\vec u_y, \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{wgn}} = 0.193815\ \text{rad} = 11.1048^\circ , $$ a rotation about $\vec u_y\propto\vec u_z\times\vec u_p$, as the geometry requires. Starting from $|\xi\rangle = (|\!\uparrow\uparrow\rangle+|\!\downarrow\downarrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$, the spin state is rotated by $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},A}\otimes e_t$ and its concurrence is $1.00000000$; the reduced state of $A$ is the maximally mixed state carried to a rotated maximally mixed state, hence unchanged, and the reduced entropy remains $\log 2$. This is the theorem in one instance: at sharp momentum the boost is a local $*$-unitary and every entanglement measure is invariant.

Momentum Superposition and the Frame Dependence

The boost as a momentum-controlled rotation

Let the momentum of each particle be a superposition. Write the total state as $$ |\tilde{Ξ}\rangle = \sum_{i}\sqrt{w_i}\,|\tilde{V}_{\Phi,1,i}\rangle\otimes|\tilde{V}_{\Phi,2,i}\rangle\otimes|\xi_i\rangle , \qquad \sum_i w_i = 1 , $$ where the spin state may itself be correlated with the momentum branch. The Lorentz transformation acts branch by branch, $$ |\tilde{Ξ}\rangle \ \longmapsto\ \sum_i\sqrt{w_i}\,|\tilde{V}_{\Phi,1,i,2}\rangle\otimes|\tilde{V}_{\Phi,2,i,2}\rangle\otimes \bigl(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1,i}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2,i}\bigr)|\xi_i\rangle , \qquad \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},j,i} = \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig}}(\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}},\tilde{V}_{\Phi,j,i}), $$ so the boost is a controlled rotation: it rotates the spin by an amount that depends on the momentum. When the states $|\tilde{V}_{\Phi,j,i}\rangle$ are orthogonal, tracing the momentum leaves the spin state $$ \tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,\mathrm{spin}} \ \longmapsto\ \sum_i w_i\,\bigl(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1,i}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2,i}\bigr)\,\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,\mathrm{spin},i}\,\bigl(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1,i}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2,i}\bigr)^\dagger , $$ a mixed-$*$-unitary channel on the spin factor whose Kraus operators are the branch Wigner rotations. Two features are immediate and are the whole of the mechanism.

First, the channel is $*$-unitary — hence entanglement-preserving — if and only if the Wigner rotation is the same on every branch, which is the sharp-momentum case of the theorem. Second, when the rotations differ across branches the channel is a non-trivial average of local unitaries; it can and generally does change both the reduced state and the entanglement, and it is unital (it preserves the maximally mixed state) but not $*$-unitary.

The symmetric two-branch case: a dephasing channel

The simplest instance is sharp enough to be worked in closed form. Let particle $A$ have a momentum in a symmetric superposition of two directions making angles $\pm\theta$ with the boost axis in a common plane, and let particle $B$ have a sharp momentum parallel to the boost axis, so that $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},B} = e_t$ and the boost acts on $A$ alone. By the symmetry, the two branch rotations are $$ \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},A,\pm} = \cos\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}}{2}\,e_t \pm \sin\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}}{2}\,\vec u_{\mathrm r}, \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{wgn}} = \theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}(\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}},\theta), $$ rotations about a common axis $\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ by opposite angles, and the channel is $$ \boxed{\;\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,A} \ \longmapsto\ \tfrac12\bigl(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},A,+}\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,A}\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},A,+}^{*} + \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},A,-}\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,A}\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},A,-}^{*}\bigr)\;}. $$ Writing the Bloch vector of $\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,A}$ as $\vec s_{\Psi} = s_\parallel\vec u_{\mathrm r} + \vec s_{\Psi,\perp}$, the two rotated copies differ only in the sign of the rotation and their average is $$ s_\parallel \ \longmapsto\ s_\parallel, \qquad \vec s_{\Psi,\perp} \ \longmapsto\ \cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}\ \vec s_{\Psi,\perp} , $$ so the channel is the dephasing about the Wigner axis: the Bloch component along $\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ survives and the transverse components are damped by $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$. The Bloch radius of a pure initial state becomes $r_{\mathrm{red}} = \sqrt{\cos^2\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}\,\sin^2\Theta + \cos^2\Theta}$, where $\Theta$ is the angle between $\vec s_{\Psi}$ and $\vec u_{\mathrm r}$; for $\vec s_{\Psi}\perp\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ it is exactly $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$, and for $\vec s_{\Psi}\parallel\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ it is $1$ and the state is unchanged. The von Neumann entropy of the dephased single-qubit state is the binary entropy of the reduced radius, $$ S(\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,A,2}) = h\!\left(\frac{1+r_{\mathrm{red}}}{2}\right), \qquad h(p) = -p\log_2 p - (1-p)\log_2(1-p), $$ which for a purely transverse initial Bloch vector is $h((1+\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}})/2)$, and which is the entropy of the spin–momentum correlation the boost has created.

Explicit frame dependence of the concurrence

The two-particle effect follows from the single-particle channel. Take the spin state $$ |\xi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl(|\!\uparrow\uparrow\rangle + |\!\downarrow\downarrow\rangle\bigr) $$ to be a maximally entangled state of $A$ and $B$, let $A$ carry the symmetric two-branch momentum superposition with $\theta = 0.8$ and speed $0.5c$, and let $B$ carry a sharp momentum along the boost axis. Boosting with rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ gives, for the spin state after tracing the momentum,

$0$ $1.00000000$ $0.000000$
$0.5$ $0.99514440$ $0.024587$
$1.0$ $0.98127650$ $0.076532$
$1.5$ $0.96235320$ $0.134782$
$2.5$ $0.92765115$ $0.224468$

where $C$ is the concurrence and $S$ is the von Neumann entropy of the two-qubit spin state. The concurrence decreases monotonically with the boost rapidity and the entropy increases from zero, because the boost has turned the pure spin state into an equal mixture of two differently rotated copies. For this configuration the concurrence is exactly $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$ is the Wigner angle of a branch, so the entanglement loss is the same dephasing factor that the single-particle channel produced; the branch angles are $\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}} = 0.098585$, $0.193815$, $0.275265$, $0.382723$ at the four non-zero rapidities. At $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 1$ the concurrence has fallen by about two per cent and at $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 2.5$ by about seven per cent. The numbers are the exact output of the channel above, computed from the Wigner rotations of the biquaternion construction, and the effect vanishes at $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 0$: the frame dependence is created by the boost and by the momentum spread together, and by neither alone.

If both particles carry momentum superpositions whose branch directions differ, the effect is larger, because then the local channel is non-trivial on both factors. For $A$ superposed in the boost–$x$ plane at $\theta_A = 0.8$ and $B$ superposed in the boost–$y$ plane at $\theta_B = 2.0$, also at speed $0.5c$, the same initial state gives $$ \theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 1:\ C = 0.95881842,\quad S = 0.166051; \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 2:\ C = 0.88816953,\quad S = 0.372196 . $$ The comparison of the two tables isolates the origin of the effect: what matters is not the boost alone nor the momentum spread alone, but the mismatch between the Wigner rotations of the branches that are traced over.

The unital channel and the invisibility of the effect in the marginals

The channel of the previous subsection is unital: each branch rotates the identity into the identity, $$ \sum_i w_i\,\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},i}\,e_t\,\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},i}^{*} = \sum_i w_i\,e_t = e_t , $$ because every Wigner rotor is $*$-unitary. The maximally mixed spin state is therefore fixed, and the fully mixed state is a fixed point of every frame transformation. A consequence is worth recording: for the state $|\xi\rangle = (|\!\uparrow\uparrow\rangle+|\!\downarrow\downarrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$ the marginal of each particle is maximally mixed, and it remains maximally mixed under the boost, so the entire frame dependence of the concurrence is invisible in the single-particle marginals. The effect is a change in the correlations of the pair, not in the statistics of either particle alone. This is also why the boost cannot be used to signal: the marginal of the un-boosted particle is untouched, by the cyclicity of the partial trace, and the change in the joint state is only visible to an observer who has access to both particles and knows the momentum distribution.

The invariant spinor concurrence and where the invariance goes

The spinor description makes the invariant and the non-invariant parts of the concurrence visible. On the spinor module, a pure two-qubit state is a four-component object $\tilde{Ξ}\in S\otimes S$, and the Lorentz group acts by $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}$ with $\langle\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\rangle_{\natural} = 1$. With the symplectic form $$ \epsilon = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\-1 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\,\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}} = \langle\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\rangle_{\natural}\,\epsilon = \epsilon , $$ the symplectic concurrence $$ \mathcal{C}(\tilde{Ξ}) = \bigl|\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathsf T}(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\tilde{Ξ}\bigr| $$ satisfies $$ \boxed{\;\mathcal{C}\bigl((\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}})\tilde{Ξ}\bigr) = \bigl|\langle\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\rangle_{\natural}\bigr|^2\,\mathcal{C}(\tilde{Ξ}) = \mathcal{C}(\tilde{Ξ})\;}. $$ The unnormalized concurrence is therefore exactly $SL(2,\mathbb{C})\otimes SL(2,\mathbb{C})$-invariant. What is not invariant is the normalized concurrence $\mathcal{C}(\tilde{Ξ})/\langle\tilde{Ξ}|\tilde{Ξ}\rangle$, because the spinor norm is not preserved by a boost: $\langle\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\tilde{Ξ}|\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\tilde{Ξ}\rangle = \langle\tilde{Ξ}|\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{*}\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}|\tilde{Ξ}\rangle \neq\langle\tilde{Ξ}|\tilde{Ξ}\rangle$ for a single boosted factor. This is the sharpest way to state the origin of the frame dependence in the framework. The invariant is a bilinear pairing of spinors, and a boost preserves it as a bilinear form; but the physically normalized state divides by a norm that the boost changes, and the ratio is frame-dependent. For a sharp momentum the normalization is common to both branches and cancels; for a momentum superposition the branch norms differ and the ratio does not.

The Wigner Angle and Its Momentum Dependence

The channel above is controlled by one number per branch, the Wigner angle, and the way that number depends on the momentum direction is the geometry of the frame dependence.

Definition. An element of $SU(2)$ is a unit real quaternion, and every such element is a rotation, $$ \tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig}} = \cos\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}}{2}\,e_t + \sin\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}}{2}\,\vec u_{\mathrm r}, \qquad |\vec u_{\mathrm r}| = 1 , $$ with $\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$ the Wigner angle and $\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ the rotation axis. For a boost of rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ along $\vec u_z$ and a particle of rapidity $\xi$ along a direction $\vec u_p$ making angle $\theta$ with the boost axis, the axis is perpendicular to both, $$ \vec u_{\mathrm r} \propto \vec u_z\times\vec u_p, $$ and the angle depends on $\theta$ in a way that the following checks determine.

Vanishing at the endpoints. At $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$ the momentum is collinear with the boost, the two boosts are along one line and commute, and the Wigner rotation is the identity. This is verified directly: the rotor is $e_t$ up to the $10^{-6}$ of floating-point arithmetic at both endpoints.

Exact at perpendicular momentum. For $\theta = \pi/2$ the angle is given exactly by $$ \tan\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}} = \frac{\sinh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,\sinh\xi}{\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} + \cosh\xi}, $$ which is verified to machine precision at several rapidities. The angle increases monotonically with both rapidities, tends to $\arctan(\sinh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}})$ as $\xi\to\infty$, and is bounded by $\pi/2$.

Small rapidities. Expanding the same configuration for small $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}},\xi$ gives $$ \theta_{\mathrm{wgn}} = \tfrac12\,\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,\xi\,\sin\theta + O(3), $$ with the coefficient $1/2$ confirmed by a numerical limit in which both rapidities are driven to zero at fixed ratio; since $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\approx v_b/c$ and $\xi\approx v/c$ at low speed, this is the leading Thomas–Wigner term $\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}\approx v\,v_b\sin\theta/(2c^2)$, the rotation that accompanies the composition of two non-collinear boosts.

The maximum. For fixed rapidities the angle vanishes at the endpoints and has a single maximum in between. For a boost of rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 1.5$ and a particle of speed $0.5c$, the angle rises from $0$ at $\theta = 0$ through $11.40^\circ$ at $30^\circ$ and $15.58^\circ$ at $45^\circ$ to a maximum of $19.60^\circ$ near $\theta = 80^\circ$, then returns to $0$ at $\theta = 180^\circ$. The maximum is therefore attained, to within a few degrees, at perpendicular momentum, but the exact location is somewhat below $90^\circ$ and drifts as the rapidities change.

What the angle controls. The angle enters every frame-dependent quantity through $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$: it is the damping factor of the transverse Bloch components, the value of the concurrence in the symmetric two-branch configuration, and the parameter of the spin entropy $h((1+\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}})/2)$ of a transverse single-particle state. At the maximum $\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}} = 19.60^\circ$ one has $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}} = 0.9421$, so a transverse spin is damped by about six per cent and the dephased single-particle entropy is $h(0.97103) = 0.1891$. The frame dependence is thus largest for a boost perpendicular to the particle's motion — where the Wigner rotation is largest — and absent for a boost along the motion.

Superposed Masses and the Gravitational Phase

The channel above is driven by a momentum superposition and by a boost. The same algebra carries a second driver, and it is the one an experiment is being built to reach: a spatial superposition whose branch phase is supplied by the gravitational potential of a second mass. The mechanism is the one proposed for a gravity-induced entanglement experiment. What the framework adds is not the number — the number is Newtonian and algebra-independent — but the placement of the two objects the mechanism couples, the potential in the material sector and the path qubit in the informational one.

The configuration. Two masses $m$, labelled $A$ and $B$, at separation $R$, each in a superposition of two positions separated by $d$, with $0

The branch phases, and where they sit in the algebra. After a laboratory time $t$ the branch $|ij\rangle$ carries the phase $\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},ij} = -V_{ij}\frac{2\pi t}{h}$, which is the phase of a $*$-unitary element of the material sector, $$ \tilde U_{ij} = \cos\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},ij}\,e_t + \sin\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},ij}\,\left(i e_t\right), \qquad \tilde U_{ij}\tilde U_{ij}^{*} = e_t , $$ verified on 100 random phases. The generator is $i e_t$, the central element whose square is $-e_t$: the clock of the composition product, and the element the probability product does not contain. A phase drawn from an energy is therefore a material-sector element, while the path qubit it acts on is an informational-sector one.

The entangling phase. Three of the four phases are removable. A common phase on $A$'s branches, a common phase on $B$'s branches, and a global phase are each absorbed by a $*$-unitary factor acting on one subsystem or on the pair, and none of them can change the entanglement. The one combination that no local operation touches is $$ \delta = \omicron_{\mathrm{ang},00}+\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},11}-\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},01}-\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},10} = \frac{2\pi Gm^{2}t}{h}\left(\frac{2}{R}-\frac{1}{R+d}-\frac{1}{R-d}\right) = -\frac{2\pi 2Gm^{2}t\,d^{2}}{h\,R\left(R^{2}-d^{2}\right)} , $$ checked against the four-phase combination computed from $V_{ij}$ on 100 random configurations, with a maximum difference of $2.4\times10^{-12}$ rad. For $d\ll R$ it reduces to $$ \delta \simeq -\frac{2\pi 2Gm^{2}t\,d^{2}}{h\,R^{3}} , $$ agreeing with the closed form to a relative $3.9\times10^{-4}$ over 100 configurations with $d/R\le 2\times10^{-2}$.

What the combination measures. It is a mixed second difference of the potential. A constant potential and a uniform field both give $\delta = 0$, verified on 100 configurations of $V(x)=ax+b$, and the leading surviving term is the tidal one, of order $d^{2}/R^{3}$. A uniform potential shifts every branch equally; a uniform field is removed by the freely falling frame. Only the difference of the field across the superposition leaves a record in the state. This is the equivalence principle read as a statement about the algebra rather than as a restriction on the potential, and it is the same statement as the unital property of the channel above: what a common factor does is invisible, and what a difference does is not.

The state and the concurrence. With both masses initially in the symmetric superposition $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)$ and no other coupling, the state after the interaction is pure, $$ \left|\tilde{Ξ}\right\rangle = \tfrac12\sum_{ij}e^{i\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},ij}}\left|ij\right\rangle , $$ and its concurrence is $$ C = 2\left|c_{00}c_{11}-c_{01}c_{10}\right| = \left|\sin\frac{\delta}{2}\right| , $$ the first equality the standard pure-state form and the second its evaluation on the coefficients above; the two agree to $1.6\times10^{-13}$ on 100 random configurations. The concurrence vanishes at $\delta = 0$, is maximal at $\delta = \pi$, and grows linearly in $t$ for small phase. Gravity generates entanglement and does nothing else to a pure path state, and the rate is fixed by $G$, $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ and the geometry.

When the separation is uncertain. If $R$ is not sharp but distributed with weights $w_\imath$, the branch phases are averaged, the state is no longer pure, and the result is the dephasing channel of §Momentum Superposition and the Frame Dependence with $\cos\delta_\imath$ in place of $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$, acting on the path qubits. The mechanism is the same one the article analyses for momentum: what matters is the mismatch between the phases of the branches that are traced over, and the effect vanishes as the distribution narrows.

What this does not do. The potential is Newtonian and instantaneous and is inserted rather than derived from a field equation of the framework; the masses are static sources. No quantised gravitational field, no relativistic correction, no back-reaction and no self-gravity is included. Nothing here is a deviation from the standard computation, and the framework's phase is the standard one. What the section establishes is where the two halves of the computation sit: the potential energy is a material-sector scalar, on the $i e_t$ direction of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, and the which-path degree of freedom of a two-position superposition is a two-level system, so on the framework's reading its state is an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ and the pair is an element of the joint object of Composite Systems and the Multi-Qubit Extension. A material-sector phase acting on an informational-sector state is a coupling across the sectors, and the Empirical Status article records under A New Material–Informational Coupling that the framework has no other one and no coupling constant. Gravity-induced entanglement is a candidate mechanism of exactly that kind, with $G$ and $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ supplying the scale — and it is offered here as an identification of where the standard computation sits in the algebra, not as a derivation of a new coupling.

What Is Frame-Dependent and What Is Not

Not frame-dependent. The entanglement of the full state, momentum included, is a property of a state under a $*$-unitary transformation and is invariant: the Lorentz group acts unitarily on the full one-particle Hilbert space, so the entanglement between the two particles including their momenta is a Lorentz invariant. The spin entanglement of two sharp-momentum particles is likewise invariant, by the theorem above. And no observable marginal can be changed by a local operation on the other particle, by the cyclicity of the partial trace — the framework's form of no-signaling, recorded in the entangled-subsystems companion.

Frame-dependent. The spin entanglement of a reduced state obtained by tracing an uncertain momentum is frame-dependent, because the boost entangles spin with momentum and the trace discards the momentum. The reduced spin entropy, the concurrence, and the spin correlation functions of a wave packet therefore all transform non-trivially. The effect is second order in the momentum spread in the sense that it vanishes as the distribution narrows to a delta function, and it vanishes as the boost is removed.

The physical location of the non-invariance. The invariance group of the spin factor is the little group $SU(2)$, and $SU(2)$ acts unitarily; the frame dependence is not a failure of unitarity but the consequence of an operation that does not factorise as spin $\times$ momentum. A boost is a $*$-unitary operation on the full Hilbert space which is not a product of an operation on the momentum factor and an operation on the spin factor; it correlates them. The spin state, which is a partial description, therefore changes. This is the same structure that produces the Unruh effect for accelerated observers and the thermal character of a restricted vacuum; the field-theoretic case belongs to the information theory of quantum fields, and the present article treats the single-particle kinematic mechanism only.

Relativistic Quantum Information: The Biquaternion Reading

The framework's contribution to this subject is a structural one, and it can be stated in three points. First, the two objects whose mismatch produces the frame dependence have a single algebraic locus. The Wigner rotation is the compact, material rotation generated by the non-compact, informational boosts: it is the little-group element $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig}}\in SU(2)\subset\mathbb{B}$ extracted from a product of unit-norm biquaternions, and the momentum dependence of $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig}}$ is the momentum dependence of a rotor, i.e. of an element of the same algebra that carries the spin. There is no second structure to be introduced: the boost and the spin rotation are elements of one algebra, and their composition is algebra multiplication.

Second, the entanglement measures are pairings. The correlation function, the concurrence, and the reduced state are all computed from the tensor-product Hermitian form, read as the bilinear pairing $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}\tilde H_\Psi^{*})$ and the partial trace, and the relativistic action is a congruence on the factors. Read this way, the frame dependence of entanglement is a statement about which pairings the congruence preserves: the full pairing is preserved, the reduced pairing is not, and the difference is exactly the information the boost moves from the spin factor into the spin–momentum correlation.

Third, the invariant is a spinor bilinear form. The symplectic concurrence that survives the boost is the algebraic invariant of the spinor representation, and its non-invariance after normalization is the algebraic scalar reading of the wave-packet character of the state. This is a genuinely biquaternionic statement, because it is the multiplication of spinors by biquaternions that produces both the invariant and its normalization; the framework makes the split between them explicit in a way that the operator formalism states only through the transformation of a density matrix.

Summary

For two particles of definite four-velocity, a Lorentz transformation acts on the spin state by the local $*$-unitary Wigner rotation $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2}$ and therefore preserves the spin entanglement: the Schmidt coefficients, the concurrence, and the entanglement entropy are invariant. This is the sharp-momentum theorem, and it is exact.

For a momentum superposition, the boost acts branch by branch and is a momentum-controlled rotation; tracing the momentum leaves the spin state acted on by the mixed-$*$-unitary channel $$ \tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}\ \longmapsto\ \sum_i w_i\,\bigl(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1,i}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2,i}\bigr)\,\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}\,\bigl(\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},1,i}\otimes\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig},2,i}\bigr)^\dagger . $$ The channel is $*$-unitary exactly when the Wigner rotation is branch-independent, and otherwise it changes the entanglement. In the symmetric two-branch case it is the dephasing about the Wigner axis, with the parallel Bloch component preserved and the transverse components damped by $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$; the single-particle entropy produced is $h((1+r_{\mathrm{red}})/2)$, and the concurrence of a maximally entangled pair decreases monotonically with the boost rapidity, equalling $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{wgn}}$ for the symmetric configuration. For a state with $|\xi\rangle = (|\!\uparrow\uparrow\rangle+|\!\downarrow\downarrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$, $A$ in a symmetric two-branch superposition with $\theta = 0.8$ and speed $0.5c$, and $B$ at sharp momentum along the boost axis, the concurrence falls from $1$ at $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 0$ to $0.98127650$ at $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 1$ and $0.92765115$ at $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}} = 2.5$, with the von Neumann entropy of the two-qubit spin state rising from $0$ to $0.076532$ and $0.224468$.

The invariant behind the computation is the spinor symplectic concurrence $\mathcal{C}(\tilde{Ξ}) = |\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathsf T}(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\tilde{Ξ}|$, which is exactly $SL(2,\mathbb{C})\otimes SL(2,\mathbb{C})$-invariant because $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}} = \langle\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\rangle_{\natural}\epsilon = \epsilon$. The frame dependence appears only in the normalized ratio, because a boost preserves the bilinear pairing but not the spinor norm; a sharp momentum has a single branch and the effect cancels. The entanglement of the full state, momentum included, is frame-independent, and no local operation changes the marginal statistics of the other particle; what is frame-dependent is the partial description that discards the momentum, and what produces it is the momentum dependence of the little-group rotor.

The same channel is produced by a gravitational potential, and there the branch phase is the one a laboratory can supply. Two masses $m$ separated by $R$, each superposed over $d$, accumulate the entangling phase $\delta = (Gm^{2}\frac{2\pi t}{h})(2/R-1/(R+d)-1/(R-d)) = -2Gm^{2}td^{2}/(\frac{h}{2\pi} R(R^{2}-d^{2}))$, which is a mixed second difference of the potential and therefore vanishes for a constant potential and for a uniform field: only the tidal term survives. The branch phase $\omicron_{\mathrm{ang},ij} = -V_{ij}\frac{2\pi t}{h}$ is a $*$-unitary element of the material sector generated by $i e_t$, the two path qubits are informational-sector elements, and the concurrence of the resulting pure state is $\left|\sin(\delta/2)\right|$ — $0.105286$ after one second for $m = 10^{-14}$ kg, $R = 200\,\mu$m and $d = 100\,\mu$m, reaching $1$ at $14.9$ s. The framework contributes no deviation from this standard phase; it contributes the identification of the mechanism as a material-sector phase acting on an informational-sector state, which is the cross-sector coupling the Empirical Status article records as absent elsewhere.

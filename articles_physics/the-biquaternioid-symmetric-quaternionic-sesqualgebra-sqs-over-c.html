The Biquaternioid Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (SQS) over $\mathbb{C}$

Introduction

The biquaternioid complex space carries eleven structures beside the ordinary product. This article is about the symmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra, written $\mathrm{SQS}$, whose product is the symmetrisation of the fourth product, $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P^{*})$. It is the symmetric sibling of the gauge structure, and its job in the framework is metric: its form is the Krein form read as a product, its signature is $(1,3)$, and its values lie in no remarkable subspace.

The mathematics of the structure is owned by the group The Biquaternioid Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (SQS) over $\mathbb{C}$ of the mathematics menu. This article proves nothing. It lists the results, each with the mathematics article that owns it, and it keeps the physical readings the framework gives the structure, every one of them labelled as a reading.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, $e_t=1$, $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$, $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$, and central imaginary $i$. An element is $\tilde Q=\sum_\mu Q_\mu e_\mu$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$. The conjugations are the natural one $\tilde Q^{\natural}$, the coefficientwise one $\bar{\tilde Q}$ and the star $\tilde Q^{*}=\bar{\tilde Q}^{\natural}$; the sign vector is $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the sectors are $\mathbb{M}_+=\{Q^{*}=Q\}$ and $\mathbb{M}_-=\{Q^{\flat}=Q\}$ with $\flat=-{}^{*}$. All conventions are those of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

Its class among the twelve structures is that of a symmetric sesqualgebra. Owner: Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Physical Interpretation

  • The gauge metric reads as the pairing of the transformations and not of the states. Its signature is indefinite, so it selects no direction of positivity, which is why a gauge calibration can compare two internal frames and cannot rank them; it is the second member of the invariant pair, the sesquilinear row that a change of the local complex structure does not move.
  • The values lie in no subspace, and that is the framework's criterion for what can be an observable. An object whose algebraic norm is not central cannot be counted, so a symmetric gauge product is a structure and not a quantity. The minimal mixing element $\tilde Q=e_t+\vec e_x$ has the non-central algebraic norm $-2\vec e_x$, while a pure vector direction has a central one, so the slightest mixing is enough to leave the class of what can be measured. The criterion is the same one that separates the gauge side from the state side.
  • The band's form is a metric-like datum and not a state. Its value carries the scalar part $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}$ and the mixed vector part side by side and therefore lies, for a general pair, in no one of the remarkable subspaces; the self-pairing $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q$ is in general not central, and the standard reconstruction of a state space — positivity, a unit, a Jordan structure, a module action — does not reach it. The metric belongs to the transformation side of the frame, whose invariants are the pairings left unchanged, and not to the state side, whose invariants are positive numbers.
  • A gauge calibration is transported by reflecting the vector directions and not by reversing a charge. The left attachment reads the argument through the star and the right through the natural conjugation, and the two differ by the coefficientwise conjugation that the symmetrisation installed; the band's adjoint is the indefinite $J$-adjoint, so the pairing does not distinguish left from right and conjugates the calibration instead.
  • The two classes of the band are the centre and the vector directions. A central calibration is a self-adjoint multiplication and a pure vector calibration a skew-adjoint one, and a calibration that mixes a scale with a direction is neither; the symmetric class is the complex line of central calibrations and the antisymmetric class is the vector subspace, so the band separates a scale from a direction and labels the mixing element as the one that belongs to neither.
  • The band measures a calibration through its norm and its scale. The collapse at $N=0$ is the algebraic trace of the null directions of the metric, and the operators of the band are a deformation: their scalar part a conformal factor and their traceless vector part a shear, so the gauge pairing deforms an element rather than rotating it or translating it.

Summary

The biquaternioid symmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra is the structure $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P^{*})$ over $\mathbb{C}$, the metric band of the framework: conjugate-commutative, without a unit, with the Krein form read as a product, of signature $(1,3)$, and with values that lie in no remarkable subspace. It is a pairing of transformations and not of states, its non-central norm is the criterion that keeps it out of the measurable class, and its operators deform rather than move. The mathematics is owned by the mathematics group of the same name; the readings listed above are the framework's, each labelled, and none of them is a proof.

**The distinction is the article's argument.** Dark matter is an inventory failure: the model has no candidate, and the failure cannot be repaired without adding a field. The sphaleron is neither a failure nor a success but the calculation that connects the two — it is what makes $B$ violation real, and it is also what forbids the model from using it.

**The five results.**

- **The dark matter is $5.36$ times the baryon density**, and the model's only dark candidates are excluded: neutrinos by their mass, the Higgs by its lifetime, the neutron by decaying. So the inventory is empty and the failure is structural.
- **The neutral-current bound is the cleanest exclusion in the model.** The $Z$ width counts neutrino species: $N_\nu=2.9963\pm0.0074$. A fourth generation with a light neutrino is excluded by the same measurement that confirms the first three.

- **The electroweak phase transition is a crossover**, not a first-order transition, for any Higgs above about $72$ GeV. At $125.09$ GeV there is no bubble nucleation, hence no departure from equilibrium, hence the third Sakharov condition fails outright.

## The Missing Mass

**The measurement.** The cosmic inventory, in the units cosmology uses ($\Omega_i h^2$, the density in units of the critical density times the reduced Hubble constant squared):

| Component | $\Omega_i h^2$ | Share of the total |
|---|---|---|
| Dark energy | — | $0.685$ of critical |
| Dark matter | $0.1200$ | $0.265$ |
| Baryons | $0.02237$ | $0.0494$ |
| Radiation | $\sim10^{-5}$ | $\sim10^{-5}$ |

$$
\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{DM}}}{\Omega_b}=\frac{0.1200}{0.02237}=5.36 .
$$

So there is $5.36$ times as much mass in the dark component as in all the protons and neutrons in existence. The model's matter content accounts for $15.7\%$ of that budget and none of the rest, and no particle it contains can supply the other $84.3\%$.

**Why this is an inventory failure and not a parameter failure.** [The parameter-count article](the-parameter-count-as-the-measure-of-the-frameworks-boundary.html) established that the nineteen parameters are values the model does not fix. Dark matter is different: no value of any of the nineteen produces a dark matter candidate, because the model's spectrum is closed. There is no knob to turn. The failure is not in the numbers; it is in the list of objects.

**The three established properties, which any candidate must have.**

| Property | Evidence | Consequence for a candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Cold, or at most warm | Structure formation; the power spectrum's shape | Must be non-relativistic at $T\sim1$ eV |
| Electrically neutral, and neutral under colour | No electromagnetic or strong signature; the CMB's acoustic peaks | Must be a singlet, or a neutral member of a multiplet |
| Stable, or long-lived | Present today | Must be protected by a symmetry or by kinematics |

**The sharpest single measurement.** The Bullet Cluster: two galaxy clusters that have passed through each other, with the X-ray gas (baryons) shocked and slowed in the middle while the gravitational mass, reconstructed from lensing, has passed through. The mass-to-light ratio tracks the galaxies and not the gas. That is a direct statement that the dark component is collisionless and the baryonic component is not, and it cannot be reproduced by any modification of gravity that couples to baryons alone.

**What the model has, examined one by one.**

| Candidate | Mass | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Neutrinos | $\Sigma m_\nu<0.12$ eV | Hot: free-streams out of structures; at most $1.1\%$ of the dark density |
| Higgs | $125$ GeV | Decays in $10^{-22}$ s |
| Neutron | $939.6$ MeV | Decays in $880$ s |
| Gluons, photons | $0$ | Massless, and they were in equilibrium |
| $Z$, $W$ | $91.2$, $80.4$ GeV | Decay in $10^{-25}$ s |
| The vacuum | — | Contributes to dark *energy*, not to matter; and with the wrong sign and magnitude |

**The neutrino row, quantified.** The cosmological bound on the sum of neutrino masses is $\Sigma m_\nu<0.12$ eV, and the relic density is

$$
\Omega_\nu h^2=\frac{\Sigma m_\nu}{93.14\ \mathrm{eV}} ,
$$

so at the bound $\Omega_\nu h^2=0.00129$, which is $1.1\%$ of $\Omega_{\mathrm{DM}}h^2$. So [the neutrino article](the-neutrino-and-majorana-fermions-in-biquaternionic-form.html) settles the question in the negative with a number: neutrinos are a $1\%$ correction to the dark component and cannot be it. This is also why "hot dark matter" was abandoned in the 1980s — the same calculation, with the same conclusion, before the oscillations were measured.

**The symmetry problem, stated because it is the real obstacle.** Even a new neutral particle is not automatically stable. Stability requires a conserved quantum number, and the model's only exact global symmetries are $B-L$ (non-perturbatively conserved) and the accidental ones. So a dark matter candidate requires either a new symmetry or an accidental one, and an accidental symmetry is a statement about which operators are allowed by the gauge quantum numbers — which is a statement about a content the model does not have.

## The Neutral-Current Bound

**The measurement, which is the model's cleanest negative result.** The $Z$ boson's invisible width counts the light neutrino species:

$$
\Gamma_{\mathrm{inv}}=\frac{N_\nu G_F M_Z^3}{12\sqrt2\pi}\left(1+\dots\right) ,
$$

and the fit to the $Z$ line shape gives

$$
N_\nu=2.9963\pm0.0074 .
$$

**The reading, in two parts.**

- Three species are **confirmed**, to a part in three hundred. This is the strongest single piece of evidence that the generation structure stops at three.
- A fourth species with $m_\nu<M_Z/2=45.6$ GeV is **excluded** at many standard deviations. So the model cannot be extended by adding a fourth generation with a light neutrino, and any new neutral fermion must either be heavier than half the $Z$ or not couple to it.

**Why this is the bound that closes the simplest door.** The obvious way to make dark matter out of the model's own content is to add a fourth generation and let its neutrino be the dark matter. That door is shut twice: by $N_\nu$, and by the relic density above — a fourth neutrino of mass up to $45$ GeV would still be relativistic at decoupling if light, and hot if light, and excluded by $N_\nu$ if it is in equilibrium.

**The loophole, stated so the bound is not overread.** $N_\nu$ counts species in equilibrium with the $Z$ at $T\sim M_Z$. A particle that decoupled earlier, or never coupled to the $Z$ at all, is invisible to this measurement. So the bound excludes a *class* of candidates — those with full weak interactions — and not the possibility of a new sector. The hidden sector is where the model's extensions have gone, and this is why.

**The framework's position on all of this: nothing.** $N_\nu$ is a measurement of a coupling the framework imports, and the generation count of three is an input it does not explain. [The parameter-count article](the-parameter-count-as-the-measure-of-the-frameworks-boundary.html) recorded the count of generations among the discrete inputs. This article adds only that the count is measured to a part in three hundred, which is a precision the framework has no way to address.

## The Matter Excess

**The measurement.**

$$
\eta=\frac{n_b-n_{\bar b}}{n_\gamma}=6.1\times10^{-10} ,
$$

that is **one baryon per $1.6\times10^{9}$ photons**. Every annihilation that could have occurred did occur, down to the point where the baryon density was too low for further annihilation, and the residue is this number.

**Why it needs a mechanism rather than an initial condition.** Because inflation erases any initial asymmetry: it dilutes the density of any pre-existing net $B$ to zero, so whatever asymmetry exists must be *created* after inflation and before the QCD transition. This is the one place where the argument is not "the model does not explain it" but "the model cannot have started with it". An initial condition is not available, because inflation is not a choice — it is required by the horizon and flatness problems, which the corpus treats separately.

**The three properties any mechanism must have, listed before the conditions because they constrain the search.**

| Property | Requires |
|---|---|
| $B$ must be violated | A process with $\Delta B\neq0$ |
| $C$ and $CP$ must be violated | Different rates for particles and antiparticles |
| The process must be out of equilibrium | Otherwise the inverse reaction erases the asymmetry |

**The quantitative form of the second.** It is not enough to have $CP$ violation; the *amount* matters, and it can be counted. The rephasing-invariant measure of the CKM phase's size is the Jarlskog invariant

$$
J=c_{12}c_{13}^2c_{23}s_{12}s_{13}s_{23}\sin\delta=3.13\times10^{-5} ,
$$

computed from $\lambda=0.22650$, $A=0.826$, $\bar\rho=0.1591$, $\bar\eta=0.3523$ and $\delta=65.5^\circ$. So the model's CP violation is a number of order $3\times10^{-5}$, and the required asymmetry is $6.1\times10^{-10}$. The two differ by nothing obvious — which is exactly why the naive expectation failed: the estimate of $\eta$ from $J$ alone, including the loop and phase-space suppressions, is of order $10^{-20}$, short by **eleven orders of magnitude**.

**Why eleven and not five.** Because $J$ is not the whole suppression. The asymmetry generated by a diagram is proportional to $J$ times the product of the couplings at each vertex times the loop factor $(1/16\pi^2)^n$ times the phase-space ratio. With three or four loops in the relevant $B$-violating process, $(16\pi^2)^{-3}\sim10^{-6}$ and $J\sim10^{-5}$ already give $10^{-11}$, before any further suppression. So the *measured* CP violation of the quark sector is quantitatively irrelevant to baryogenesis, and this is the article's cleanest statement about what the model does and does not contain: it has CP violation, it has it in the wrong sector and at the wrong size.

## The Three Sakharov Conditions

**The conditions, stated as the 1967 paper stated them, and then tested.**

| Condition | Statement | Needed because |
|---|---|---|
| 1. $B$ violation | Some process with $\Delta B\neq0$ | Otherwise $B$ is conserved and any initial zero stays zero |
| 2. $C$ and $CP$ violation | The rates for $X\to Y$ and $\bar X\to\bar Y$ differ | Otherwise equal numbers of baryons and antibaryons are made |
| 3. Out of equilibrium | The inverse process is suppressed | Otherwise the asymmetry is undone |

**Why they are necessary and not sufficient.** The three conditions are a filter: a mechanism satisfying all three may still produce $\eta=0$ or $\eta=10^{-30}$. The Standard Model satisfies all three *nominally* and produces zero. The table below is the article's central diagnostic.

| Condition | Present in the Standard Model? | Quantitatively adequate? |
|---|---|---|

| $C$ violation | Yes: the gauge interactions are chiral | Yes, in principle |
| $CP$ violation | Yes: the CKM phase, $J=3.13\times10^{-5}$ | No: eleven orders short |

**So the model fails on two of three and is vacuous on the third.** The section *The Phase Transition* gives the fourth row its number; the section *$B$ Violation* gives the first row its exact reason; the section *The Matter Excess* gave the third row its arithmetic.

## $B$ Violation: the Anomaly Identity

**The identity, which is exact and non-perturbative.**

$$
\partial_\mu j^\mu_B=\partial_\mu j^\mu_L=\frac{N_g g^2}{32\pi^2}F^a_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{a\,\mu\nu} ,
\qquad
\partial_\mu\left(j^\mu_B-j^\mu_L\right)=0 .
$$

**The three readings of it.**

- **$B$ and $L$ are each violated**, by an amount proportional to the topological density. So the divergence is a total derivative of a topological object and integrates to an integer, which is why $\Delta B$ is quantised.
- **$B-L$ is conserved exactly.** The combination is anomaly-free, so no process — perturbative or not — changes it.
- **$\Delta B=\Delta L=N_g=3$** for a transition of unit winding number. One unit per generation, all three generations at once, which is why the process is called *sphaleron* and couples only to the generation-universal sector.

**The consequence that makes the model unable to do baryogenesis.** If the universe starts with $B=L=0$, so does $B-L=0$, and since $B-L$ is conserved the final state has $B-L=0$. A sphaleron transition moves $B$ and $L$ together, so it moves $B$ from zero and back to zero. **The Standard Model therefore generates no net baryon number at all, from any initial condition with $B-L=0$.** This is a stronger statement than "too little CP violation", and it is the correct one.

**What that means for every proposal.** Baryogenesis in the Standard Model requires new physics that violates $B-L$, and every mechanism is classified by where it does so:

| Mechanism | Violates $B-L$? | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Electroweak baryogenesis | No | $100$ GeV |
| GUT baryogenesis | Yes, via leptoquarks | $10^{16}$ GeV |
| Leptogenesis | Yes, via Majorana neutrino masses | $10^{9}$–$10^{12}$ GeV |
| Affleck–Dine | Yes, via a flat direction | $\gtrsim$ TeV |

**The framework's contribution, which is the identity itself.** [The anomalies article](anomalies-and-anomaly-cancellation-in-biquaternionic-form.html) derives the anomalous divergence from the biquaternionic path integral, and [the Fujikawa article](the-fujikawa-method-and-the-path-integral-anomaly-in-biquaternionic-form.html) constructs it by the measure's non-invariance under the anomalous transformation. What it does not supply is any reason for $B-L$ to be violated, since it has no leptoquark and no Majorana mass of its own.

## The Sphaleron

**What it is.** A static, finite-energy saddle-point solution of the Minkowski equations — a *configuration*, not a tunnelling event. It sits at the top of the barrier between two vacua of different Chern–Simons number, and its energy is the barrier height.

**The energy.**

$$
E_{\mathrm{sph}}=\frac{4\pi v}{g}B\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{g^2}\right)=9.0\ \mathrm{TeV} ,
$$

where the dimensionless function of the quartic is $B=1.90$ at the measured couplings, computed here from $E_{\mathrm{sph}}g/(4\pi v)$. The number is set by $v$ and $g$ and only weakly by $\lambda$: it is a *weak-scale* object, not a QCD-scale or Planck-scale one, and that is the structural fact behind everything that follows.

**The rate, and the exponent.**

| $T$ | $E_{\mathrm{sph}}/T$ | $\exp(-E_{\mathrm{sph}}/T)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $50$ GeV | $180$ | $10^{-78}$ |
| $100$ GeV | $90$ | $10^{-39}$ |
| $150$ GeV | $60$ | $10^{-26}$ |
| $160$ GeV | $56$ | $10^{-24}$ |
| $0$ | $\infty$ | $0$ |

**The two limits, which are the whole physics.**

- **At $T=0$ the rate is zero**, not small: the exponent is infinite. So the proton does not decay by this route, and the bound on its lifetime from sphalerons alone is above $10^{160}$ years — some $7\times10^{149}$ times the age of the universe. 't Hooft's original estimate, and the reason the anomaly is compatible with matter's stability.
- **At $T\gtrsim T_c\approx160$ GeV the rate is unsuppressed**, because $E_{\mathrm{sph}}(T)\to0$ as the expectation value vanishes. So in the early universe the process is fast, and $B$ is not a good quantum number.

**The crossover, which is the article's third result restated as a mechanism.** The sphaleron rate is fast above $T_c$ and exponentially slow below. So there is a temperature at which $B$ violation switches off — and any asymmetry generated must be generated above it. The transition itself is the subject of the next section but one.

**Why the number $9.0$ TeV matters beyond cosmology.** It is the energy at which $B$ violation becomes accessible, so it is the energy a future collider would need to reach to see it directly. That is roughly $100$ times the LHC's center-of-mass energy, and it is the reason sphaleron processes are studied only through their cosmological and low-energy consequences.

## The Phase Transition

**The question.** Baryogenesis requires a departure from equilibrium. The Standard Model's candidate is the electroweak phase transition: if it is first order, bubbles nucleate, the plasma is out of equilibrium at the walls, and the asymmetry can be generated there.

**The answer, and it is a single number.**

$$
m_H>72\ \mathrm{GeV}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{the transition is a crossover, not a first-order transition.}
$$

The measured $m_H=125.09$ GeV is $74\%$ above that threshold. So the transition is smooth: the expectation value turns on continuously, there are no bubbles, no walls, no supercooling, and **no departure from equilibrium at all**. The third Sakharov condition fails outright, not marginally.

**Why the Higgs mass decides it.** The transition is first order when the cubic term in the finite-temperature effective potential is strong enough to create a barrier between the symmetric and broken minima. That term is proportional to $g^3T$ from the gauge bosons and to $\lambda^{3/2}T$ from the scalar; a larger $\lambda$ raises the barrier at $T=0$ but also makes the transition smoother at finite temperature, and the competition resolves in favour of a crossover once the Higgs is heavier than about $72$–$80$ GeV. At $125$ GeV there is no barrier.

**The lattice statement, which is stronger than the one-loop one.** The crossover is established non-perturbatively, and the transition temperature is $T_c=159.5\pm1.5$ GeV. So this is not an artefact of the effective-potential approximation; it is a property of the theory.

**What this closes, and what it leaves.** It closes electroweak baryogenesis in the Standard Model, and it closes it for a reason that has nothing to do with CP violation: even a large CP phase would have nowhere to act. Any electroweak baryogenesis requires an extension that restores a first-order transition — a second doublet, a singlet, or a dimension-six operator — and all three are additions.

**The link to [the effective-potential article](the-effective-potential-and-the-coleman-weinberg-mechanism-in-biquaternionic-form.html), stated because it is the corpus's own machinery.** That article constructs the finite-temperature effective potential in the framework's language and records that the framework supplies the *form* of the potential but not its parameters. Here the consequence of that is sharp: the framework could in principle host the calculation, and the calculation's answer — a crossover — is decided by a parameter the framework does not fix. So the framework is neutral on the question, and the answer comes from the measurement.

## Leptogenesis, and the One Route the Corpus Can Build

**The mechanism.** Add a heavy right-handed neutrino $N$ with a Majorana mass $M$. Its out-of-equilibrium decays violate $L$ by two units and $CP$ through the interference of tree and loop amplitudes. Sphalerons, still fast above $T_c$, convert part of the lepton asymmetry into a baryon asymmetry, and the conversion factor is fixed:

$$
B=\frac{8N_f+4N_H}{22N_f+13N_H}\left(B-L\right)=\frac{28}{79}\left(B-L\right)
$$

for $N_f=3$ generations and $N_H=1$ doublet. So

$$
B=\frac{28}{79}L_{\mathrm{generated}} ,
$$

and the observed $\eta=6.1\times10^{-10}$ requires $L\sim1.7\times10^{-9}$ in the same units.

**Why this is the corpus's route.** Because [the Majorana article](the-majorana-equation-in-biquaternionic-form.html) constructs the Majorana condition, [the seesaw article](the-seesaw-mechanism-and-majorana-mass-in-biquaternionic-form.html) constructs the heavy singlet and its mass, and [the PMNS article](the-pmns-matrix-and-lepton-mixing.html) establishes that the lepton sector carries two additional phases beyond the CKM count. Those two phases are the CP violation the mechanism needs, and they are *not* constrained by the eleven-orders failure of the quark sector: the lepton phases are new parameters, measured to be nonzero by oscillation, and their size is not the Jarlskog invariant.

**The honest limits of the claim.**

- The framework **hosts** the mechanism: the objects exist in the corpus and no new algebraic machinery is needed.
- The framework **does not fix** the heavy scale $M$, the light neutrino masses, or the two Majorana phases. Those are four of the seven or eight parameters [the parameter-count article](the-parameter-count-as-the-measure-of-the-frameworks-boundary.html) added to the count.
- The mechanism **can fail**: for hierarchical light neutrinos there is an upper bound on the CP asymmetry, and part of the parameter space gives too small an asymmetry. So hosting the mechanism is not the same as producing the number.

**The comparison with the other routes, in one line each.** GUT baryogenesis needs a leptoquark, which the ceiling forbids. Affleck–Dine needs a flat direction in a supersymmetric theory, which the framework does not have. Electroweak baryogenesis needs a first-order transition, which the measured Higgs mass forbids. So of the four mechanisms, one is forbidden by the framework's own structure, one by the measured Higgs mass, one by the absence of supersymmetry, and one is available.

## Summary

The Standard Model fails on two of these three subjects exactly, and on the third it succeeds in a way that forbids it from being useful.

The dark matter is $5.36$ times the baryon density, and the model's spectrum is closed: neutrinos are $1.1\%$ of it at the cosmological mass bound, the Higgs decays in $10^{-22}$ s, and the neutron in $880$ s. The neutral-current bound $N_\nu=2.9963\pm0.0074$ confirms three species and excludes a fourth with a light neutrino, so the simplest door is shut by the same measurement that opened the model. Direct detection at $2.6\times10^{-47}$ cm$^2$ has pushed the simplest WIMP $1.5\times10^{14}$ below the weak scale. The two candidates the corpus can build — the axion and the sterile neutrino — both arrive as by-products of solving other problems.

The baryon asymmetry is $\eta=6.1\times10^{-10}$, one per $1.6\times10^{9}$ photons, and it cannot be an initial condition because inflation erases it. The model satisfies the three Sakharov conditions nominally and fails all three quantitatively: the sphaleron violates $B$, but $B-L$ is conserved exactly, so from $B-L=0$ the answer is zero and not merely small; the CKM phase has $J=3.13\times10^{-5}$ and yields $\eta\sim10^{-20}$, eleven orders short; and the electroweak transition is a crossover for any Higgs above $72$ GeV, so there is no departure from equilibrium at $125.09$ GeV.

The sphaleron is a $9.0$ TeV saddle point, and its rate is $\exp(-E_{\mathrm{sph}}/T)$: $10^{-39}$ at $100$ GeV, $10^{-24}$ near $T_c$, and exactly zero today. Its physical consequence is a proton lifetime above $10^{160}$ years.

The framework supplies the anomaly identity, the quantised winding number, the instanton gas and the Majorana condition, and it does not supply the sphaleron — because the sphaleron is not self-dual and the algebra's topological machinery is. That is the sharpest statement in the article: the framework's topological apparatus stops at the barrier.

The one route it can build is leptogenesis, with $B=28L/79$, and that route exists because two other articles needed the Majorana condition and the heavy singlet for different reasons. The corpus's only two working answers in this area arrived as by-products, and that is the pattern worth recording.

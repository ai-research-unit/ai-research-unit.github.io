Unit, Index and Notation Conventions

Introduction

These are the conventions of the units, of the marks and of the labels. They are the last of the series because every earlier article depends on them, and they assume the base of Algebra and Element Conventions and the operators of Operator and Calculus Conventions.

The order is from the physical to the typographical. The SI units come first, the fundamental quantities told apart from the derived, with the rule that the coefficients of one element share one unit and with the two writings of the temporal slot. The marks come next, which say what a caret carries and what a subscript carries. Then the labels, every one of them low, and the few of this corpus among them. Then the subscript itself, which counts or names and is never a derivative.

Physical Units

A unit element is not a physical unit. The base $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ of Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Base and Its Units is algebraic: its structure constants are $0$ or $\pm1$, and the biquaternion norm is a sum of four squares of coefficients. No element of $\mathbb{B}$ carries a metre, a second or a kilogram. A physical unit enters only where an element is identified with a physical quantity, and that identification is a convention of the article that makes it, not a property of the algebra. The phrase unit element names a basis element and physical unit names a convention of measurement; the two are never exchanged. The word base carries both senses in this article, and the context decides: the base of the algebra is the four elements, and a base unit is a unit of the SI.

The series uses the SI. Length is measured in metres, time in seconds, mass in kilograms, temperature in kelvin and charge in coulomb. Four of the five are base units of the SI. The coulomb is not: the base unit of current is the ampere, and the coulomb is $\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{A\,s}$.

The word fundamental has four senses, and the corpus uses the first. A quantity can be called fundamental because its dimension is independent, because its unit is a base unit of the SI, because its defining constant is fixed exactly, or because nature supplies it. The four do not agree. The SI has seven base units — metre, second, kilogram, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela — and since 2019 it fixes seven constants: the caesium-133 hyperfine frequency $\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}$, the speed of light $c$, the Planck constant $h$, the elementary charge $e$, the Boltzmann constant $k_B$, the Avogadro constant $N_A$ and the luminous efficacy $K_{\mathrm{cd}}$. Every unit, base or not, is realised from those seven. A base unit is therefore a bookkeeping choice, and no unit is fundamental in the sense that nature supplies it. The corpus uses the first sense: a quantity is fundamental when its dimension is one of the five, and any other dimension is a product of powers of them.

The dimension letters stand only in these tables. The corpus writes a dimension in upright roman — $\mathrm{L}$ for length, $\mathrm{T}$ for time, $\mathrm{M}$ for mass, $\Theta$ for temperature and $\mathrm{Q}$ for charge — and a derived dimension as a product of powers, $\mathrm{M}\,\mathrm{L}^{2}\,\mathrm{T}^{-2}$. Three of the five letters already carry another meaning: $\mathsf M_2$ is the matrix map, $\mathsf T_{\mu\nu}$ the energy–momentum tensor, and $\Theta$ the complex angle of Algebra and Element Conventions. A dimension letter therefore stands only in the two tables below, and never in an expression.

The corpus keeps charge as an independent dimension with a derived unit. The corpus writes charge and not current, so $\mathrm{Q}$ stays a fifth dimension, independent of the other four, while its unit is built from the ampere. The mole and the candela are not used, and current never appears as a base quantity of its own.

A multiple is the same dimension. Energies are quoted in electronvolts, $1\ \mathrm{eV}=1.602\,176\,634\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$, which is exact because $e$ is; momenta in $\mathrm{MeV}/c$; and lengths in femtometres. A prefix, or a division by $c$, changes the number and never the dimension.

The coefficients of one element share one unit. The biquaternion norm is a sum of four squares of the coefficients of a single element, so the four terms are commensurable only if the four coefficients carry the same unit. The requirement is per element and not across elements: the coefficients of a four-displacement are lengths and its norm a length squared, the coefficients of a four-velocity are speeds and its norm a speed squared, and the temporal slot of a four-potential carries the parameter $a'_t=\xi_{\mathrm{scp}}/c$ rather than $\xi_{\mathrm{scp}}$ so that its four components share the unit of the vector potential. The field strength needs no adjustment: $\tilde F=i\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E-\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$ is one number, and $\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E$ and $\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$ each carry the dimension $\mathrm{M}^{1/2}\,\mathrm{L}^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{T}^{-1}$. The unit is the unit of the quantity the element represents, and it is supplied by the identification.

The two writings of the temporal slot. In the length coordinate the material position is $\tilde R_\Phi = i c r'_t\,e_t+\vec q$, with time measured in metres; this is the corpus's default. In the time coordinate the temporal component is $ir'_t$, time stays in seconds, and the conversion is carried by the square of the coordinate: $Q_t=i c r'_t$ has $Q_t^2=-c^2{r'_{t}}^2$, and the metric stays the unit metric, $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$. The quadratic form is one and the same, $\langle\tilde R_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=-c^2{r'_{t}}^2+r_x^2+r_y^2+r_z^2$; the two differ only in where the factor $c$ is written, and an article that uses the second states it where the temporal slot is read.

Dimensionless and dimensionful. A pure number — the fine-structure constant $\alpha$, the ratio $g=2$, the phase $\frac{k_m\,S}{m\,c}$, the trace normalisation $2$ — carries no unit and no conversion. An exponent is a pure number, and the coupling is what keeps it one: a phase is written $\frac{k_m\,S}{m\,c}$, a holonomy is written $\exp(\frac{k_m\,i\,ϙ}{m\,c}\oint_C\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu A_\mu dx_\mu)$, and the line integral is not an exponent on its own, because $\oint_C\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu A_\mu dx_\mu$ carries $\mathrm{Q}$ and no choice of $c$, $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$ or $G$ removes it. An article that writes the exponent without the coupling declares the coupling absorbed into the connection, once, where the display is made. A dimensionful constant is a unit conversion and is written with its unit. No scale is derived by the algebra: $c$, $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$, $k_B$, $G$, $m$ and $ϙ$ are inserted at the places where the structure requires one. The metrology says the same thing from the other side. The defining constants above are fixed by definition and the units are realised from them; $\alpha$ and $G$ are the ones that have to be measured, and $\alpha$ is the dimensionless one of the two. No article is permitted to set one of these constants to one: $c$, $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$, $k_B$, $G$, $m$ and $ϙ$ are carried, and the reason is given in the paragraphs that follow.

The shortcut $\hbar=c=1$ is not allowed. An article may not set $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$ or $c$ to one, and a writing that does is a defect in that article rather than a convention of it. Both are defining constants of the SI, fixed exactly, $c=299\,792\,458\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $h=6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$, and setting one to one is not a choice of gauge but the deletion of a conversion between two base dimensions. With $c=1$ the dimension $\mathrm{T}$ becomes $\mathrm{L}$ and the first table loses a row; with $\hbar=1$ the dimension of action becomes $1$ and a momentum becomes a reciprocal length. The two deletions together collapse $\mathrm{L}$, $\mathrm{T}$ and $\mathrm{M}$ onto one dimension, and from that point no dimensional statement in the article can be tested, because every candidate mismatch has been legitimised in advance.

Why the algebra forbids it in particular. The coefficients of one element share one unit because the biquaternion norm is a sum of four squares (§The coefficients of one element share one unit above). The temporal slot of the material position is $i c r'_t\,e_t$, and the factor $c$ is exactly what makes that coefficient a length like $r_x,r_y,r_z$; it is the content of §The two writings of the temporal slot, and not a decoration that may be dropped. An article that sets $c=1$ while writing $\tilde R_\Phi=i r'_t\,e_t+\vec q$ has silently changed the unit of its temporal coefficient and broken the commensurability that the norm requires. The same holds for $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$, which is the coupling between the wave and the phase: the phase is $\frac{k_m\,S}{m\,c}$, the wave four-vector is $p/\hbar=\sum_\mu k_\mu e_\mu$, and the coefficient of a mass term is $\lambda_m^{-1}=k_m$. Setting $\hbar=1$ makes $p$ a wave number and $m$ a wave number, and the three lengths of the table — $\lambda_m$, $\lambda_p$, $\lambda_E$ — all collapse onto the reciprocals they are computed from. The letter $m$ then names a reciprocal length in one article and a mass in the next, which is the ambiguity that the mass-length convention exists to remove.

What the shortcut hides. A denominator $p^2-m^2$ is not a difference of like quantities. The first term carries $\mathrm{M}^{2}\,\mathrm{L}^{2}\,\mathrm{T}^{-2}$ and the second carries $\mathrm{M}^{2}$, so the subtraction is licensed by $c=1$ and by nothing else; with the constants carried it reads $p^2-m^{2}\,c^{2}$, where $p^2=E^2/c^2-\vec p^{\,2}$ and both terms are inverse lengths squared. The same fault runs through $\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=-m^2$, through the Matsubara denominator $\omega_n^2+\vec p^{\,2}+m^2$, and through every propagator pole written as $p^2=m^2$: each is a sum of unlike dimensions that the shortcut has made to look alike. A dimension count catches these at sight and cannot catch them in natural units, so the shortcut removes the one check that costs nothing.

What is written instead. The constants are carried, and a mass is written through the mass wave number, $k_m=mc/\hbar$, so that the coefficient of a mass term is $k_m$, a momentum is $\hbar k_m=mc$ and an energy is $m\,c^{2}=mc^2$; the mass length is the inverse, $1/k_m=\hbar/(mc)$, and is written only when a distance is what the sentence needs. A numerical example may quote its numbers in units of $mc$, of $mc^2$ or of $1/k_m$, because that is a change of unit for one quantity and it leaves the dimension in place: $E=2mc^2$ and $p=0.3\,mc$ are admissible, $m=1$ and $c=1$ are not. Planck units are excluded by the same rule, since $G=1$ beside $c=1$ and $\hbar=1$ deletes a third conversion and takes the kilogram with it.

A caution on $S$. The letter $S$ carries three meanings: the action, the spin $\tilde S_k$, and the entropy of the thermodynamics articles. The action and the spin share the unit $\mathrm{J\,s}$; the entropy is $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. The subscript and the article separate the three, and an article that uses two of them says which is which.

A wave number and its inverse are one fact read two ways. A wave number is radians of phase per unit length, so its reciprocal is the distance per radian; the table above gives the three of the series in both readings. The wave number is what a derivative returns, because every differentiation carries an inverse length, so it is the form in which the equations are written and the form a coefficient of an operator takes. The distance is what a laboratory measures, so it is the form in which the readings are stated: a resolution, a fringe spacing, a light-travel length. An article that names both for the same object writes the inversion once, $1/k_m=\lambda_m$, and never introduces the distance as a second definition. The general reading, with the three distances and what each one measures, is in The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle.

A caution on $k$. The letter $k$ is the spatial index of this corpus: $\vec e_k$, $p_k$, $\sum_k$. A wave number therefore never takes a bare $k$, and the three wave numbers of a particle carry a subscript, $k_m=mc/\hbar$, $k_{\mathrm{mo}}=\frac{k_m\,p}{m\,c}$ and $k_E=E/(\frac{m\,c^{2}}{k_m})$, defined in The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle. The label $\mathrm{mo}$ is two letters for that reason: $k_p$ and $p_k$ would be one transposition apart, and a reader could not tell a wave number from a component. Two neighbours are to be told apart from $k_E$: $k_{\mathrm{eu}}$ is the Euclidean momentum after a Wick rotation, whose square is non-negative, and $k_0=\omega/c$ is the temporal component in the displays that write the wave four-vector by components. No display mixes the three.

A caution on $\mu$. The letter $\mu$ carries two meanings in the corpus: the permeability in $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$, and the direction label in $e_\mu$ and $Q_\mu$. A third meaning, the reciprocal mass length, was once carried by the same letter and is no longer written at all. The coefficient of a mass term is the mass wave number $k_m=mc/\hbar$ of The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle, and a text that writes $\mu$ where a mass is meant is using the older spelling. Two uses remain, and an article that needs both says which is which.

A caution on $\lambda$. The letter $\lambda$ names a central value and an eigenvalue, the helicity weight $\lambda=\vec u_p\cdot\vec S$ of Helicity in Biquaternionic Form, and a free exponent in the notation of §The Upper and Low Marks. A length therefore never takes a bare $\lambda$, and the corpus does not work in lengths at all: it works in the three wave numbers $k_m=mc/\hbar$, $k_{\mathrm{mo}}=\frac{k_m\,p}{m\,c}$ and $k_E=E/(\frac{m\,c^{2}}{k_m})$, and a length appears only as an inversion, $\lambda_m=1/k_m$, $\lambda_p=1/k_{\mathrm{mo}}$ and $\lambda_E=1/k_E$. The subscript of a length names the quantity it is computed from, never the particle, and no fourth symbol is introduced for the inverses, since the inverses are the three wave numbers.

The Upper and Low Marks

These rules are real, and partial. They fix the marks of the common uses and they bind wherever they apply. They do not cover every use: a mark that falls outside them is governed by the section that fixes it, and a stricter rule stated in the corpus overrides them — the derivation convention of Operator and Calculus Conventions, §Conventions for Derivation, the Fock and opposite tokens of §The Labels, and the forms of §The Subscripts.

A caret carries four things and no label:

  • a power, $2^k$, $e^\lambda$, $(-1)^j$, $\alpha^k$, $2^{k/2-1}$;
  • a conjugation, $a^{*}$, $a^{\dagger}$, $a^{\natural}$;
  • a transposition, $L^T$, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^T$;
  • a construction, $\mathbb{B}^\times$, $S^{\otimes n}$, $\mathrm{sym}^l$, $\Lambda^kTM$ (degree).

A subscript carries one of two things: an index or a label.

  • an index — a numeric count, $Q_1$, $M_2$; the slot of a coordinate, $q_i$, $x_i$, $p_i$, $Q_i$, $\theta_i$, $\zeta_k$, $v_i$; the slot of a basis vector or a gamma, $\vec e_i$, $\gamma_i$; the slots of an invariant symbol, $\delta_{ij}$, $\varepsilon_{ijk}$, $\eta_{ij}$; the generator of a gauge, colour, isospin or spin family, $T_a$, $G_a$, $\sigma_z$; the slot of a connection or curvature component, $\Gamma_k$, $\mathrm{Ric}_{ij}$, $\omega_i$, $g_{ij}$; the slot of a differential, $dx_i$.
  • a label naming the object, a part of it or a role of it, $E_{\mathrm{wkb}}$, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$, with every label of §The Labels; the label comes first in the subscript and an index already there follows it after a comma, $T_{\mathrm{em},\mu\nu}$; the family letters of a regular matrix are of this kind, $L$ and $R$ naming the left and the right part, $\mathsf M_{4L}$ and $\mathsf M_{4R}$.

The word metric has one meaning in the series. There is one metric, the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$: diagonal, all four coefficients $+1$, the matrix of the interval $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$. A frame carries a coordinate, not a metric. The minus of the material sector is not a metric: it is $i^2=-1$ in $Q_t=i c r'_t$, made once on the time.

No index is raised, because the one metric is the identity. The series has one metric, the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$, and raising with it changes nothing: a raised index would be the same index. A gamma is written low, $\gamma_\mu$, and the weight does the only work a raising could do, $\gamma^\mu=-\varepsilon_\mu\gamma_\mu$. A text that needs a raised index on a coefficient is writing a metric the series does not have.

The object is not the role. A coordinate, a basis vector, a gamma, an invariant symbol, a gauge generator, a component of a connection and a differential are seven kinds of object, and the low mark does one thing on all seven: it gives the index that names the slot of the family the glyph belongs to. The index says which member, never what kind, and the kind is carried by the glyph itself, $x_i$ against $\gamma_i$ against $\delta_{ij}$. A differential carries the same mark as the coordinate it differentiates, $x_i$ and $dx_i$, because the slot is the same in both. Nor does the alphabet divide the work: a letter is an index in $q_i$ and a number is an index in $Q_1$, and the two are of different kinds only where a coefficient and an element meet (§The Subscripts).

The lists rule on these uses, not on the names. A raised grade, parameter, sector or order is a name, not a power, a conjugation, a transposition or a construction, and the article that uses it says which name it is. A label is a name of that kind, and it names the object the glyph denotes, $E_{\mathrm{wkb}}$, a part of that object, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$, or a role of it, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$; the word separates the three, since the series has one slot for all of them.

The Caret

The caret carries a power, a conjugation, a transposition and a construction. Those four are the whole of its list, and they are read at a glance because each is italic or a symbol: $2^k$, $e^\lambda$, $(-1)^j$, $\alpha^k$ are powers; $a^{*}$, $a^{\dagger}$, $a^{\natural}$ conjugations; $L^T$, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^T$ transpositions; $\mathbb{B}^\times$, $S^{\otimes n}$, $\mathrm{sym}^l$, $\Lambda^kTM$ constructions.

An operator name written with its argument is the one word that stands there. $\operatorname{sym}(V)$ names the symmetric algebra of a space, $\operatorname{sym}^2(E)$ the symmetric square of a bundle. The word names a structure of the glyph rather than a variant of it, it carries its argument with it and never stands bare, and it is therefore not a label: it takes no entry in §The Labels. It is written lowercase, as a label is, and a capital in that position is a defect.

No label stands in the caret. A variant of an object is a name, and every name stands in the subscript (§The Labels). Italic is a power and upright is a name, and the slot agrees with the face: $E_{\mathrm{wkb}}$ is the labelled energy and not the WKB power of $E$, $T_{\mathrm{em},\mu\nu}$ the electromagnetic part of the tensor and not its $\mu\nu$ power, and $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$ the Fock ladder and not a power of $a$. A display that raises a label is a defect.

No index is raised, and the caret never carries one. Every coordinate, component, basis vector and sign symbol is written low (§The Upper and Low Marks), and a contraction is written as a form: $\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde Q_{\mathrm f}\rangle_{\natural}$ for a derivative with a field, $\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural}=\Box$ for two derivatives (Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Krein Product, Its Form and Its Norm). Where a contraction is written with indices, the sum names the summed index — $\sum_\mu e_\mu\partial/\partial Q_\mu$ — and is never a repeated index left to the summation convention.

The Labels

Every label stands in the subscript. A label is the upright roman name of a variant of an object: a construction, a frame, a sector, a reading, a part of the object or a role it plays. It is written low, at the length the article that introduces it fixes; that article also fixes what the word abbreviates, and the label enters the index with the article.

The label comes first, and an index already in the field follows it after a comma. $T_{\mathrm{em},\mu\nu}$ and not $T_{\mu\nu,\mathrm{em}}$, $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}$ and not $L_{e_\mu,\mathrm{axf}}$, $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},k}$ and not $\tilde S_{k,\mathrm{tot}}$, $\mathsf M_{4L,\mathrm{pau}}$ with the frame closing the field the size opened. A field that already carried the index is reordered and never duplicated. One field is written without the comma, the label carried into a coefficient beside its direction, $Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{f}\mu}$, where the two letters are read as one weight. The rule, its reasons and its four contrasts are stated in Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions, §The Labels.

The label names the object, a part of it or a role of it, and the word and its article decide which. $\mathrm{axf}$ names the display a regular matrix is read in, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{spl}}$ a variant of an algebra, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$ a part of a total, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$ a role in a coupling. There is one slot, so the height decides nothing; the word decides, and the article that introduces it says so.

The label is two or three letters, and it keeps the capital of a name. A common word is abbreviated to its letters, $\mathrm{wkb}$, $\mathrm{axf}$, $\mathrm{orb}$, $\mathrm{ret}$, $\mathrm{inc}$; a proper name or an initialism keeps its capital, $\mathrm{Had}$ the Hadamard gate, $\mathrm{CS}$ the Chern–Simons term, $\mathrm{LT}$ the Lorentz transformation, $\mathrm{FS}$ the Fubini–Study form, $\mathrm{YM}$ and $\mathrm{SM}$ the two gauge programmes, $\mathrm{HS}$ the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. The index of the labels, mathematical and physical together, is Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions, §The Three-Letter Labels; a label of this corpus enters it with the article that spends it.

$\mathrm{fop}$ marks the Fock layer. The marks of the elements say what a glyph is, not that it is an operator (Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Operators). The single exception is the Fock layer, whose operators act on a Fock space and are not elements of the algebra. Only these carry the label $\mathrm{fop}$:

$$ a_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad b_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger},\quad b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger},\quad \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad N_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad H_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad F_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad Ϙ_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad U_{\mathrm{fop}} . $$

The label says exactly one thing — the glyph is not an element, it is an operator on the Fock space — and it is written on nothing else. It is not a general mark of operators: the momentum $\vec p=-\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\nabla$ keeps its element glyph and never takes the label, the quadratures $x$ and $p$ of the oscillator are written $x$ and $p$, the spin $S_k=\frac{m\,c}{2\,k_m}\sigma_k$ is written $S_k$, and the parity, charge-conjugation and time-reversal maps $P$, $C$ and $T$ carry no label. Where a ladder is meant as an element of the algebra it is written with the element's glyph, the truncated pair $\vec\Upsilon_1,\vec\Upsilon_2$, and the abstract ladder operator keeps the label, $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$.

The dagger is the general adjoint, and it stands in the caret. The dagger is the general adjoint (Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Operators) and the label marks the Fock operator, so the two occupy two slots and never compete for one: $(a_{\mathrm{fop}})^{\dagger}$, contracted to $a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$. The dagger alone never says that its glyph is an operator, and the label alone never says that the Fock operator is self-adjoint; the two are read together, so that $a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ is the adjoint of the ladder and $N_{\mathrm{fop}}$ is written without an adjoint because it is self-adjoint.

The bar is the coefficient conjugation, and it is never the Dirac adjoint. It is the involution of Algebra and Element Conventions, $\bar{\tilde Q}=\bar Q_te_t+\bar Q_x\vec e_x+\bar Q_y\vec e_y+\bar Q_z\vec e_z$, and it conjugates the coefficients of an element. The Dirac adjoint is not an involution of the algebra and takes no mark of its own: it is the adjoint of the column followed by the timelike frame cell, written as the two multipliers of that cell, $$ \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\,iL_{\mathrm{axf},e_t}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x} \;=\; \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\,iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x} . $$ In a bilinear the cell multiplies the one that follows, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\bigl(iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\Gamma\bigr)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$. No letter stands for the cell (Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Clifford Structure), and the bar is not reused for the composite: a display that writes $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}$ and reads it as the adjoint puts a conjugation and a frame cell under one mark. A display already written that way is read as the expression above, and the bar itself stays the conjugation, as in $\bar{\tilde Q}$.

$\mathrm{opp}$ marks the opposite algebra. The reversed-product algebra, $a\cdot b=ba$, takes the label $\mathrm{opp}$ — $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$, $A_{\mathrm{opp}}$, $M_n(D)_{\mathrm{opp}}$ — and it is the algebra name that takes it, where $\mathrm{fop}$ marks a Fock-operator glyph. The two labels are distinct and neither is read for the other: $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$ is never an opposite algebra, and $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$ is never an operator.

On an element the label names a tensor factor. Written on an element it means the same element read in the opposite algebra, $e_{\mathrm{opp},\nu}$, and it is written there for that one purpose: to name the second factor of the tensor product $\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$, whose elementary tensors $e_\mu\otimes e_{\mathrm{opp},\nu}$ are the cells $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}R_{\mathrm{axf},e_\nu}$ of the two-sided operators. The element is not thereby an operator, the label says nothing about a side, and on an operator glyph it is a defect. It marks an algebra, a module of one, and an element read in that algebra; it never marks a set or a bare vector space.

Three qualifiers name a variant of an algebra. They stand low, three lowercase letters each: $\mathrm{spl}$ on a split variant, $\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{spl}}$ the split-complex numbers, $\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{spl}}$ the split-quaternions and $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{spl}}$ the split-biquaternions; $\mathrm{red}$ on the reduced biquaternions, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{red}}$; $\mathrm{dln}$ on the dual numbers, $\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dln}}=\mathbb{R}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2})$. The label is the first thing in the field, so $\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{spl}}$ is never a power of $\mathbb{C}$. $\mathrm{dln}$ is not $\mathrm{dual}$: $\mathrm{dual}$ marks the Hodge dual of a field, $F_{\mathrm{dual}}$, and the two labels are never exchanged. $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{red}}$ is not a part of $\mathbb{B}$. The reduced biquaternions are $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$, of real dimension four, and the biquaternions are of real dimension eight; the qualifier names a different algebra and not a subalgebra of $\mathbb{B}$.

The physical corpus writes labels of its own. $\mathrm{orb}$ on the orbital part, $\mathrm{inc}$ on the incident field, $\mathrm{out}$ on the outgoing solution, $\mathrm{ret}$ on the retarded field, $\mathrm{reg}$ on the regular representation, $\mathrm{can}$ on the canonical tensor, $\mathrm{pol}$ on the polarisation vector, $\mathrm{lor}$ on the Lorentz object, $\mathrm{wkb}$ on the approximation, $\mathrm{mhv}$ on the amplitude, $\mathrm{epr}$ on the steering value, $\mathrm{sp}$ on the spinor, $\mathrm{em}$ on the electromagnetic part, and $\mathrm{axf}$ and $\mathrm{pau}$ on the two displays of the regular matrices, axis-free and Pauli, which the gamma sections keep. Each stands low on the glyph it qualifies, and the word names the part or the role it marks.

Six codes separate two spellings. $\mathrm{rgs}$, $\mathrm{tpl}$, $\mathrm{prm}$, $\mathrm{dfc}$, $\mathrm{obf}$, $\mathrm{adg}$, each separating the rarer of two words that share three letters — regularised from regular, topological from top, perimeter from periodic, defective from defining, orbifold from orbital, adjugate from adjoint. A code is a label and stands low with the rest, on the object it qualifies: $h_{\mathrm{tpl}}$ the topological entropy.

The twelve algebraic structures carry no code at all. A product is written by its two slots and the conjugation each one carries, $\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*}$, $\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet_{*}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet_{\natural}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet_{\natural*}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge_{*}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge_{\natural}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge_{\natural*}\tilde Q_2$, and the operator names the structure on its own.

The Subscripts

A subscript is written with the underscore, _, inside the math delimiters, and the underscore takes a single token: one character is written bare, $Q_t$ and $Q_k$, and two characters or more are wrapped in braces, $Q_{n+1}$ and $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$, because a bare underscore would take the first character alone.

The label is written first in the field, and an index already there follows it after a comma. $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}$ is the matrix of $\vec e_\mu$ in the axis-free frame, $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$ the projection of the total, $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},k}$ the generator; the rule, its reasons and its four contrasts are stated in Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions, §The Labels.

A subscript is never a partial derivative. The subscript is an index or a label, a partial derivative is written with $\partial$, and the rule is fixed in Operator and Calculus Conventions, §Conventions for Derivation.

A coefficient carries a letter index and an element a number. In the quaternionic layers a coefficient is indexed by the direction it multiplies, $Q_x$ and $q_x$, and an element that is counted carries a number on its tilde, $\tilde Q_1, \tilde Q_2$. In the commutative layers, which have no direction, both the element and its coefficients carry the number of the element, $Q_1 = q_1 + iq'_1$. The two indices are of different kinds, a letter against a number, so a coefficient index is never read as the number of an element, nor the reverse.

It is allowed to add subscripts when several elements of the same kind are used. The permission covers the general elements, the units, the roots, the idempotents and the nilpotents alike: wherever a statement carries more than one element of one kind, the occurrences are told apart by a subscript.

A subscript counts, or it names. A subscript that only tells one occurrence from another is numeric, so that a family is $\tilde Q_1, \tilde Q_2, \dots$ and a second numbered family of the same statement takes the next numbers. A subscript that names the object or the role it qualifies is a role label, and a role label is written in upright roman, as in $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$ and $\tilde U_{\mathrm r}$.

A role-labelled element carries its label in its coefficients. The generic element-valued field is $\tilde Q_{\mathrm f}=Q_{\mathrm{f}r_t}e_t+\vec Q_{\mathrm f}$, and its components are $Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{f}\mu}$: the label $\mathrm f$ belongs to the element and is carried once into the component subscript, beside the direction, so that the components are $Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{f}\mu}$; it does not count a family. A numbered family is unaffected, $\tilde Q_1=Q_{1t}e_t+\vec Q_1$.

The two parts are named by their star. The Hermitian part is $\tilde R_\Psi$, the fixed element of the star, and the anti-Hermitian part is $\tilde R_\Phi$, its anti-fixed element: $\tilde Q=\tilde R_\Psi+\tilde R_\Phi$ is the split read on the coefficients, defined in Algebra and Element Conventions, §The Hermitian and Anti-Hermitian Subspaces. The words informational and material name $\tilde R_\Psi$ and $\tilde R_\Phi$ in the physical articles. The two letters label the parts of one element and not two fields, so no label passes into the coefficients: $\tilde R_\Psi$ and $\tilde R_\Phi$ keep the names $Q_t,Q_x,Q_y,Q_z$ and select the slots.

An angular-momentum total takes the role subscript, and its projection the axis. A spin label is $s$ with projection $s_z$; the total of an orbital and a spin part, or of several spins, is $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$ with projection $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$, and the operator is $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},k}=\tilde L_k+\tilde S_k$. The subscript $k$ is the vector index of the operator, and the three components close on $[\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},i},\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},j}]=\frac{m\,c\,i\,\epsilon_{ijk}\,\tilde{S}_{\mathrm{tot},k}}{k_m}$. The projection is not a component of the operator: it is the scalar weight, the eigenvalue of $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},3}$ on $|s_{\mathrm{tot}},s_{\mathrm{tot},z}\rangle$, and it names the axis $z$ on which the measurement is made. The role is repeated in the projection so that $s_{1z}$, $s_{2z}$ and $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$ are told apart without the context; the operator keeps the vector index $k$, and the weight never takes it. The role and the axis share one pair of braces, $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$, with a comma between them.

A sign is a subscript where the statement needs one, the member attached to $+1$ taking the subscript $+$ and the member attached to $-1$ the subscript $-$, as in $\tilde\Pi_+$ and $\tilde\Pi_-$. In the reduced biquaternion layer the sign belongs to the idempotent pair and to its coordinates, $\Pi_\pm$, $Q_{+}$ and $Q_{-}$, the two basis coordinates carrying the triangles, $Q_{\blacktriangle}$ and $Q_{\blacktriangledown}$, and a numbered element putting its number first in either case, $Q_{1+}$ and $Q_{1\blacktriangle}$; the element they build is $\breve Q$. The sign is read with the glyph: $\tilde Q_\pm$ names an element of the sector $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ or $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, while $Q_\pm$ names the idempotent coordinates of $\breve Q$.

The forms a subscript takes are these.

The subscript is Example
a digit $\tilde Q_1$, $\tilde Q_2$
a letter $Q_k$, $Q_\mu$
two indices $Q_{ab}$
a sign $\tilde\Pi_\pm$
an upright word $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$, $\tilde U_{\mathrm r}$
a grouped expression $Q_{n+1}$

The letter $\iota$ is not used. No physics article uses $\iota$, in any spelling. The comparison of the biquaternion algebra with the even Clifford algebra is written by its values, $e_t\mapsto I_4$ and $\vec e_x\mapsto\gamma_2\gamma_3$, or by the words "the Clifford image", and never by a symbol. An interior product is written as the pairing it is, $\omega(X_f,\,\cdot\,)=df$, and an inner automorphism in words. The letter is not reserved for a third object: it carries no meaning in the series, and a display that introduces it is a defect.

Summary

A unit element is not a physical unit. The series uses the SI, and it tells the fundamental quantities from the derived: metre, second, kilogram and kelvin are base units, the coulomb is $\mathrm{A\,s}$ but carries the independent dimension $\mathrm{Q}$ that the corpus needs, and every other unit is derived or is a constant written in these units. No unit is fundamental in the sense that nature supplies it, because every unit is realised from seven defining constants. The dimension letters $\mathrm{L}$, $\mathrm{T}$, $\mathrm{M}$, $\Theta$ and $\mathrm{Q}$ stand only in the two tables of this article. The coefficients of one element share one unit, and the default temporal slot is $i c r'_t$ in metres, with $ir'_t$ and $g_{tt}=-c^2$ stated where an article uses it. A dimensionless number carries no unit and no conversion; $S$ is read by its subscript and $\mu$ by context between the permeability and the direction label, the inverse length of a mass being written $\lambda_m^{-1}$.

Every label stands in the subscript, the label first in the field and an index already there after a comma, $T_{\mathrm{em},\mu\nu}$. Two of them mark the layer of a glyph: $\mathrm{fop}$ marks the Fock operator, the one glyph that is not an element, and $\mathrm{opp}$ marks the opposite algebra, written on an algebra name or on an element that names a tensor factor. Three qualifiers name a variant of an algebra, $\mathrm{spl}$, $\mathrm{red}$, $\mathrm{dln}$, and the corpus writes labels of its own, $\mathrm{orb}$, $\mathrm{inc}$, $\mathrm{out}$, $\mathrm{ret}$, $\mathrm{reg}$, $\mathrm{lor}$, $\mathrm{wkb}$, $\mathrm{epr}$ among them. A label of a common word is lowercase, a label of a name keeps its capital, $\mathrm{Had}$, $\mathrm{CS}$, $\mathrm{LT}$. The index is Index, Notation and Corpus Conventions, §The Three-Letter Labels.

A subscript is written with the underscore and one token. It is a component or a label, never a derivative. A coefficient carries a letter index and a counted element a number; a subscript either counts or names, and a role label is carried once into the coefficients. A sign is a subscript where the statement needs one. The allowed forms are the digit, the letter, two indices, a sign, an upright word and a grouped expression. The family letters $L$ and $R$ of a regular matrix are labels of that kind, and they are written italic as the letters of the operators rather than upright as a word; they follow the size in the same subscript, $\mathsf M_{4L}$ and $\mathsf M_{4R}$. A label is written first in the subscript and an index already there follows it after a comma, $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}$ and $T_{\mathrm{em},\mu\nu}$; the frame closes the same field, $\mathsf M_{4L,\mathrm{pau}}$. The caret carries a power, a conjugation, a transposition and a construction, and no label; $\operatorname{sym}(V)$ is the one word that stands there. The letter $\iota$ is not used at all. An angular-momentum total takes the role subscript, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$, and its projection the axis, $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$; the operator carries the vector index, $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},k}$.

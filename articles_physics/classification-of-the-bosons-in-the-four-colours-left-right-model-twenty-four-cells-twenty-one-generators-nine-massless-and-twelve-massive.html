Classification of the Bosons in the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Twenty-Four Cells, Twenty-One Generators, Nine Massless and Twelve Massive

Introduction

The question. The model's group has three factors. Which cell of which grid does each gauge boson occupy, which cell holds no boson, which bosons are made of cells rather than sitting in one, and which of the twenty-one keep their masslessness when the chain breaks?

The answer.

$$ \boxed{\; \underbrace{6}_{\text{leptoquark sextet}}+\underbrace{8}_{\text{gluons}}+\underbrace{1}_{B-L}=15, \qquad 15+3+3=21, \qquad 24\ \text{cells}-3\ \text{traces}=21\ \text{bosons}; \;} $$

and the spectrum those generators produce,

$$ \boxed{\; 21=9+12,\qquad 9=\underbrace{8}_{\text{gluons}}+\underbrace{1}_{\gamma},\qquad 12=\underbrace{6}_{\text{leptoquarks}}+\underbrace{2}_{W^{\pm}}+\underbrace{2}_{W_R^{\pm}}+\underbrace{1}_{Z}+\underbrace{1}_{Z_R}, \;} $$

and the three neutral bosons are mixtures of three cells that no particle occupies.

The model. One model and no other: the Four Colours Left–Right Model, set out in The Four Colours Left–Right Model: Four Colours, $21$ Generators, One Declaration. The name is stated first because every name here belongs to that model and to nothing else. It is not the Standard Model's particle list and not a general classification of everything the algebra could describe: it is the boson content of this frame, under this group, with this one declaration.

The structure. One group with three factors,

$$ G_{4LR}=\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R , \qquad 15+3+3=21 , $$

acting on a formal copy of the quartet.

What the model declares and what it derives. The carrier supplies the group, the canonical split $\mathbf 4=\mathbf 1\oplus\mathbf 3$, the two-sided action and the traceless diagonal. What it does not supply is which of the four dimensions is the lepton; that is the model's one load-bearing declaration, (A), and every charge below is computed after it. The chiral assignment is declared too. Everything else is arithmetic on those two declarations.

The count, in one line.

$$ \underbrace{16}_{4\times4}+\underbrace{4}_{2\times2}+\underbrace{4}_{2\times2}=24\ \text{cells}, \qquad \underbrace{15+3+3}_{21\ \text{generators}}+\underbrace{1+1+1}_{3\ \text{traces}}=24 , $$

so twenty-four cells, twenty-one generators, three trace directions. The three cells that hold no generator are the three traces, one per factor, and that is why the group is $21$ and not $24$.

The picture. Three grids of cells, one grid per factor: a $4\times4$ for $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ over the labels $t,x,y,z$, and a $2\times2$ for each $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ over the labels $\uparrow,\downarrow$. Each is drawn as a matrix, with the symbol of the boson and the name of the particle inside the cell. A cell is a direction, not a generator — a cell and its mirror image are the two halves of one plane, and the plane's two particles are their combinations. That single fact is what makes the grids readable and is stated once in How to Read a Cell.

What this article is. The boson side of the classification. It states the group and its three numbers, the sum of squares that turns them into cells, the ledger $21=24-3$, and then draws the three grids, puts a name in every cell, derives the corner from the vanishing trace, counts the sextet, lists the twenty-one bosons as twenty-one particles with their charges and their fates, sorts the twenty-one into nine that stay massless and twelve that get mass, works out the neutral sector that turns three of them into the photon, the $Z$ and the $Z_R$, and closes with the scalars, which are bosons and sit on no cell. The fermions take the other operation — one quartet value times one doublet slot times one chirality — and they are The Fermions of One Generation.

What this article is not. It is not a coupling sector. The model carries three gauge couplings as symbols and fixes none of them; it fixes no scalar multiplet and predicts no mass relation. Everything below is content, charge and fate: what exists, in which cell, with which name, which $Q$, and whether it keeps its masslessness. Every mass written here is either a measured value or a structure such as $\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)M_{W_R}$; no mass is derived. The two numbers $0.4$ and $\tfrac58$ below are coefficients of the vacuum, and they are stated because they are the vacuum's and not the couplings'; the couplings themselves stay in The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model: One Equation, One Scale, One Content Statement.

Two meanings of "neutral", which two counts make necessary. This article's three neutral states are gauge fields — $W^3_L$, $W^3_R$ and $B'$, the fields that mix before the photon separates out. The Scalar Sector's two neutral states are real scalars, the $2.0\times10^{-8}$ J candidate and its heavier partner. Three gauge, two scalar, and neither count constrains the other.

The quartet's four values, and the declaration behind them. The carrier's canonical basis is $(e_t,e_x,e_y,e_z)$, and its canonical split is $\mathbb C e_t\oplus B_V$. The declaration is that the line is the lepton and the three-space is colour.

The row labels are the algebra's own basis, $t$ for $e_t$ and $r,g,b$ for $e_x,e_y,e_z$, and the colour names $r,g,b$ stay as the conventional names of the three directions.

That is the one declaration of the frame. Without it the group still has its $4\times4$ grid; what it does not know is which row is the lepton.

The two doublets are not the same doublet. The left index pairs left-handed fields, the right index pairs right-handed fields, and they are two different $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s acting on two different copies of a two-dimensional space:

$$ (\xi_{\mathrm{neu},L},e_L),\ (u_L,d_L)\ \text{under }\mathrm{SU}(2)_L ; \qquad (\xi_{\mathrm{neu},R},e_R),\ (u_R,d_R)\ \text{under }\mathrm{SU}(2)_R . $$

The quark doublet is the same doublet in three colour copies, so a generation has two doublets per chirality if the colour copies are counted as one orbit.

The model has exactly three numbers, and they are the table's third column: $4$, $2$, $2$. Each factor contributes one number, and that number is the dimension of the representation its index labels — how many values the index takes. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ acts on a quartet, so its index takes four values. Each $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts on a doublet, so each takes two. Everything countable in this article is built from those three numbers, and it is built by two different operations: a sum of squares for the bosons, a product for the fermions.

The bosons are a sum of squares, one square per factor. A gauge boson is a direction inside one factor's algebra, and a factor of dimension $d$ has $d^2$ entries in its defining matrix, one of which is the trace. So the factors contribute $4^2+2^2+2^2=16+4+4=24$ cells, and the three traces are removed: $24-3=21$. No factor multiplies another, because a gauge boson lives in one factor and is a singlet of the other two. That is why the grids are three separate rectangles and not one box.

The three numbers also have a meaning each, and the meanings are different kinds of thing. $4$ counts the threads: one lepton line and three colours. The first $2$ counts the slots of a doublet: up-type and down-type. The second $2$ counts the chirality blocks: which $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts. Two of the three numbers are $2$, they are not the same $2$, and they never multiply inside one field, since no fermion is a doublet of both weak groups at once.

One number that is not used, named so it is not mistaken for one of the three. The ranks are $3+1+1=5$: three diagonal generators in $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, one in each $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The rank is not the quartet's size and it is not used in any count here. The index takes four values, not three, which is the whole reason the fourth colour exists and the reason the fermion count is $16$ and not $9$. A sentence that reaches for the rank where the dimension belongs gets $3\times2\times2=12$ and loses the lepton line.

The fermions take the other operation, and it is stated where it is used. One quartet value times one doublet slot times one chirality gives $4\times2\times2=16$, taken block by block because one factor is a singlet in each block. The count, the two octets and the reason $16$ is not the dimension of $(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ in content are The Fermions of One Generation, §The Count Is a Product, Not a Sum of Squares.

The one place the three grids are not independent, stated because it is easy to get wrong. On the formal copy the three factors are a product and the three grids are three independent sets of cells: $15+3+3=21$. On the carrier they are not, because the carrier supplies only $\mathfrak u(4)$:

$$ \dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak u(4)=16=15+1 , $$

and the six weak generators sit inside the fifteen. So $15+6=21$ is a double count on the carrier and $15+6-6=15$ is the algebra; the twenty-one exists only where the copy is declared, which is why the copy must be declared and cannot be found. The three grids are drawn on the copy, and that is the sense in which they are three grids and not one.

How to Read a Cell

Cell $(i,j)$ is the direction that moves index $j$ into index $i$. A cell alone is not a generator: it is one of the two amplitudes of the plane $\{i,j\}$, and the plane's two directions are the amplitude and its reverse. The two particles of a plane are the two real combinations of its two amplitudes, which is why each cell can carry a particle name and why the pair of mirrored cells carries a pair of particles.

And one honest convention about the names in the cells. Since each particle is a real combination of a cell and its mirror, which name goes in which cell is a choice: the two names of a mirrored pair could be exchanged without changing a single number. What is not a choice is the count — one plane, two amplitudes, two particles — and that is the only thing the grid is used for below. The cells are a register of names and not a basis of the Lie algebra.

The corner is forced, and that is charge quantisation. Read the diagonal cells as the four eigenvalues of the corner's generator, so that the grid's diagonal is

$$ B-L\ \longrightarrow\ \operatorname{diag}(-3,\,1,\,1,\,1)=D . $$

The four entries must sum to zero, because a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is traceless, so

$$ (t,t)=-\bigl(\text{the three colour entries}\bigr)=-(1+1+1)=-3 . $$

Written as a generator,

$$ B-L=\tfrac13 D,\qquad \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf 4}(B-L)=-\tfrac33+\tfrac13+\tfrac13+\tfrac13=0 , $$

and the vanishing of the trace is the same statement as the four diagonal entries summing to nothing. The lepton cell is minus the colour trace, which is the corpus's charge-quantisation result read off the grid in one line, and it is why the entry is $-3$ and not a free number.

Four diagonal cells, three generators. The diagonal of the $4\times4$ has four cells and the tracelessness condition leaves three independent combinations: $B-L$ in the corner and two in the colour block. The fourth combination — all four entries equal — is the trace direction $iI$, and it is not a generator of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$: it is the direction $\det=1$ removes. That is the first of the three removed cells, and the other two are the diagonal cells of the next two grids. So the $4\times4$ holds sixteen cells and fifteen generators, and the missing one is not a hole in the picture but the definition of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$.

The Neutral Sector: Three Fields, Rank Two, One Null Direction

The dimension of the sector. The neutral sector is three-dimensional before the first breaking, and it stays three-dimensional until the last.

Three fields, and why exactly three. Neutral gauge bosons are those with $Q=0$: $W^3_L$, $W^3_R$, and the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Cartan direction that survives the first breaking. The other two $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Cartans are $T^{c}_{3}$ and $T^{c}_{8}$, which are neutral but are colour and never mix with anything, because colour is unbroken. So the mixing sector is exactly three fields:

$$ \{W^3_L,\ W^3_R,\ B'\},\qquad B'\equiv A^{15}. $$

All three are gauge fields, and none of them is one of the scalar sector's neutral states.

Why they mix. Each of the two vacua is annihilated by a different combination. The first vacuum, along the unit line, is annihilated by $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$; the second, along the doublet direction, by $Q=T_{3L}+Y$. Two conditions on three fields leave exactly one direction, and that direction is the photon. That is the whole of the neutral sector in one sentence.

The matrix. With the two vacuum vectors $a_1,a_2\in\mathbb R^3$ written in the basis of couplings,

$$ \mathcal M^2=\sum_{i=1,2}v_i^2\,a_ia_i^{\mathsf T}, \qquad a_1=\bigl(0,\tfrac12,-\sqrt{\tfrac38}\bigr)v_R, \qquad a_2=\bigl(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,0\bigr)v , $$

where the entries are the eigenvalues of $(T_{3L},\,T_{3R},\,T^{15})$ on the field that takes the vev, with $T^{15}$ the Cartan normalised by $\operatorname{tr}(T^{15})^{2}=\tfrac12$ and hence $B-L=\sqrt{8/3}\,T^{15}$. So $a_1$ is the lepton line of the $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ — $T_{3R}=+\tfrac12$, $B-L=-1$, hence $T^{15}=-\sqrt{3/8}$ — and $a_2$ is the upper component of the bidoublet. Two annihilations fix the relative signs. Hypercharge is unbroken on the first vacuum, $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)=0$, and both vacua must leave the photon massless, $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, which in this normalisation is the vector $(1,1,\sqrt{2/3})$: it gives $0+\tfrac12+\sqrt{2/3}\cdot(-\sqrt{3/8})=0$ on $a_1$ and $\tfrac12-\tfrac12+0=0$ on $a_2$. Only the products $a_ia_i^{\mathsf T}$ enter $\mathcal M^2$, so the overall sign of each $a_i$ is free; the relative signs are not, and the two tests above are what fixes them.

The rank. $\mathcal M^2$ is a sum of two rank-one matrices, so its rank is at most two, so it has at least one zero eigenvalue. It has exactly one, because $a_1$ and $a_2$ are independent: $a_1\cdot a_2=-\tfrac14v_Rv\ne0$ and they are not parallel. So there is one massless neutral and two massive, always, for any two independent neutral vacua. A frame with two neutral vacua and no massless neutral would violate this, and that is the structural reason the photon is not optional.

The Photon, Which Is a Mixture and Not a Factor

Not a gauge boson. The unbroken abelian factor at the end of the chain is generated by $B'$, the fifteenth $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Cartan. The photon is not $B'$. It is the combination of $W_L^3$, $W_R^3$ and $B'$ that annihilates both neutral vacua, and its coefficients are the null vector of the neutral mass matrix:

$$ a_1\times a_2\ \propto\ -\bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac38},\ \sqrt{\tfrac38},\ \tfrac12\bigr) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad A_{\imath}\ \propto\ W^3_{L\imath}+W^3_{R\imath}+\sqrt{\tfrac23}\,B'_{\imath} . $$

The coefficients are $(1,\,1,\,\sqrt{2/3})$ up to an overall sign that no measurement can fix. The third component is fixed by the normalisation of $T^{15}$ and by nothing dynamical. It is the fifteenth Cartan's value on the unit line, and the same choice $\operatorname{tr}(T^{15})^2=\tfrac12$ is what writes $B-L$ proportional to $(-1,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$.

Why it holds for any couplings. The two vacua annihilate two different combinations of the three neutral generators, so the requirement that one combination survives both gives one null direction. That is a rank statement about a sum of two rank-one matrices, so the mixing pattern is fixed and only the numerical size of each coefficient moves with the couplings.

Where the rest is. This article states the direction; it does not evaluate it at a particular point, and it does not state the angle that follows from it. The Photon as the Unbroken Mixture: One Null Direction, Three Parents derives the direction three ways and shows it to be the coefficient vector of $Q$; the angle is the coupling article's.

The Two Massive Neutrals

Both eigenvalues. Exactly, in the minimal vacuum.

The light neutral. It lands on the Standard Model relation.

$$ M_Z^2=\tfrac14\bigl(g_L^2+g_Y^2\bigr)v^2 . $$

At equal couplings, with $g_Y^2=\tfrac35g^2$ from the matching relation, the predicted value is $\tfrac14(g_L^2+g_Y^2)v^2=\tfrac25g^2v^2$, and the light eigenvalue of $\mathcal M^2$ is $0.4\,g^2v^2$. The two are the same number, and that is the check: the normalisations entering $a_1$ and $a_2$ reproduce the Standard Model's relation $M_Z^2=\tfrac14(g_L^2+g_Y^2)v^2$ and not merely its group. The agreement is the leading term in $v/v_R$ — the eigenvalue is $0.3994\,g^2v^2$ at $v/v_R=10^{-1}$ — which is the sense in which the Standard Model is recovered below $M_{R}$, at tree level.

The heavy neutral. Its coefficient is $\tfrac58$.

$$ M_{Z_R}^2=\tfrac58\,g^2v_R^2 . $$

The heavy eigenvalue at $v\to0$ is $0.625\,g^2v_R^2=\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$, and the $\tfrac58$ is nothing but $|a_1|^2=\tfrac14+\tfrac38$: one quarter from $T_{3R}$ and three eighths from the $B-L$ normalisation. At $v=0$ the matrix is rank one, so the other two eigenvalues vanish there: the $Z_R$ mass is entirely $M_{R}$'s, and the light neutral's comes entirely from $v$. The $Z_R$ is therefore heavier than the $W_R$ by the ratio of the two coefficients, $\sqrt{(5/8)/(1/4)}=\sqrt{5/2}\simeq1.58$ at equal couplings; with the couplings split the squared ratio is $1+\tfrac32(g_4/g_R)^2$, which carries $g_4/g_R$ and is the coupling article's business.

What these two numbers are. They are the vacuum's and not the couplings'. The coefficient is fixed by which component of which multiplet takes the vev, the normalisation of that vev is the scalar sector's business, and the overall size is the coupling article's.

Twenty-four real components, twelve eaten, twelve surviving, and not one of them on a grid. A scalar is a field whose value is a vector in a representation, not a direction in the algebra, so no scalar occupies a cell and no scalar carries a cell name. What Is Robust and What Is Not

Four strengths, and they must not be blurred.

The count. Nine massless and twelve massive, with massless $=\dim(\text{unbroken group})$. This holds for any vacuum, any scalar content and any scale ordering. It is group theory: the arithmetic is fixed before any Lagrangian is written, so a different total would be a different model and not a different vacuum.

The photon's composition. A mixture of $W_L^3$, $W_R^3$ and $B'$ with coefficients proportional to the couplings. It holds whenever the three fields are present and two independent neutral vacua are chosen, and it does not depend on the scalar content. What the coefficients are is a coupling question, and it is not answered here.

The list. Six leptoquarks, two charged $W_R$, one $Z_R$, two light charged states, one $Z$. The list follows from the dimension budget and from §The Spectrum above, and it survives any choice of vacuum. The ratios among those masses do not, and they are not stated as predictions.

The absolute scale. $v_R$ is bounded from below by the leptoquark bound and from above by nothing in the frame. The spectrum's shape is predicted; its overall size is not.

And one loop. All of the above is tree level. The $\rho$ parameter, the $Z$–$W$ mass ratio and the $Z_R$ mass all take radiative corrections, and in a model with a bidoublet the tree-level $\rho=1$ is an alignment statement rather than a theorem. Custodial Symmetry and the $\rho$ Parameter in Biquaternionic Form owns that question, and this article does not pre-empt it.

Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

A cell is not a generator: it is one amplitude of a plane, and the plane's two particles are its two combinations (How to Read a Cell).

$X$ and $\bar X$ are not two species: they are the two directions of one plane, so the sextet is three planes and not six species (The Sextet, Which Is the Six Cells of the Mixed Band).

The corner's $B'$ is not hypercharge: the frame's field carries $B-L$ and the literature's $B$ carries $Y$ (Where the Five Names of the Corner Come From).

An ingredient is not a particle: $(t,t)$, $(\uparrow,\uparrow)_L$ and $(\uparrow,\uparrow)_R$ are cells no particle occupies, because the three neutral particles are made of them (The Spectrum: Twenty-One Generators, Twenty-One Particles).

The twelve survivors are not "the Higgs": four are Higgs and eight are not (The Scalars, Which Are Not on the Grids).

Two neutral scalars is structural; their mixing angle is not (The Scalars, Which Are Not on the Grids).

The grids are not one box: a gauge boson lives in one factor and is a singlet of the other two, so the cells add and never multiply (The Group and Its Three Numbers).

The rank is not the dimension: $3+1+1=5$ is used in no count here, and reaching for it gives $12$ instead of $16$ (The Group and Its Three Numbers).

The twenty-one exists on the declared copy only: on the carrier the six weak generators sit inside the fifteen (The Ledger of the Three Grids).

The photon is not the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ boson and not $B'$: $B'$ is the $(t,t)$ cell, and the photon is the mixture that survives both vacua (The Photon, Which Is a Mixture and Not a Factor).

No dynamics enters the count: it is $\dim G$ minus $\dim H$ for whichever $H$ the vacuum leaves, so it tests the breaking pattern and no coupling (What Is Robust and What Is Not).

The three neutral states are not three scalars, and not two scalars and a gauge boson: all three are gauge fields, and the scalar sector's two neutral states are a separate object (The Neutral Sector: Three Fields, Rank Two, One Null Direction).

$\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)\,M_{W_R}$ is not a prediction of this article: the $\sqrt2$ is the group's, and $g_4/g_R$ is a ratio the group does not fix (the leptoquark article).

$\tfrac58$ is not a fit: it is $|a_1|^2=\tfrac14+\tfrac38$, one quarter from $T_{3R}$ and three eighths from the normalisation of $B-L$ (The Two Massive Neutrals).

Summary

The model. The Four Colours Left–Right Model, $G_{4LR}=\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, with its one load-bearing declaration that the line $\mathbb C e_t$ is the lepton.

The three numbers. $4$, $2$, $2$: the sizes of the quartet and the two doublets. The bosons are their sum of squares, $4^2+2^2+2^2=24$ cells minus three traces; the fermions are their product, and that is the companion article.

The picture. A $4\times4$ grid and two $2\times2$ grids. A cell is half a plane's worth of generator; the plane's two particles are the two combinations of its two cells.

The count. Twenty-four cells, twenty-one generators, three traces. The three traces are the same direction three times, the one $\det=1$ removes. So $21=24-3$.

The blocks of the $4\times4$. The $3\times3$ colour block holds the eight gluons, the mixed band holds the six leptoquarks, and the $(t,t)$ corner holds $B-L$. Six plus nine plus one is sixteen cells; six plus eight plus one is fifteen generators.

The corner is forced. Its entry is minus the colour trace, so the quartet's diagonal sums to zero, and that is charge quantisation in one line.

The spectrum. Twenty-one cells, three of them ingredients, and the three ingredients rotate into three neutral particles. An ingredient is a cell no particle occupies, because a particle is made of it.

The fates. $21=9+12$: eight gluons and one photon massless; six leptoquarks, $W^{\pm}$, $Z$, $W_R^{\pm}$ and $Z_R$ massive. The two zeros have two reasons, one a stabiliser and one an eigenvector.

The neutral sector. Three fields $\{W_L^3,W_R^3,B'\}$, a $3\times3$ mass matrix of rank two, one null direction, two massive states. The null direction is the photon, $W_L^3+W_R^3+\sqrt{2/3}\,B'$, and $\sqrt{2/3}$ is the normalisation of the fifteenth Cartan and not a dynamical number.

The masses. Each is a coupling squared times a vacuum squared, with the coefficients the vacuum fixes: $\tfrac14g_L^2v^2$ for the light charged pair, $\tfrac14g_R^2v_R^2$ for the heavy one, $\tfrac12g_4^2v_R^2$ for each leptoquark, and $\tfrac58g^2v_R^2$ for the $Z_R$ at equal couplings. The first three coefficients hold for any couplings; the last two, here and for the $Z$, move with $g_4/g_R$.

The strength. The count, the list and the photon's composition are robust; the ratios are conditional on the minimal vacuum; the overall scale is bounded only from below.

The seven. Seven generators touch the $t$ index: the corner, three out, three in. The eight that do not are the gluons.

The scalars. Not on the grids: twenty-four real components, twelve eaten into longitudinal modes, twelve named survivors. The Higgs is among them and is $H$, the bidoublet $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$: its four surviving real components are the charged Higgs $H^{\pm}$ and the two neutral states $h^0$ and $H^0$, whose lighter mass eigenstate is the observed $2.23\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$ boson. The other eight survivors come from $\Phi$ and are not Higgs.

The vocabulary. Eight collisions, one fix each. The corner's five names and the four kinds of mass are the two worst, and both are stated in full.

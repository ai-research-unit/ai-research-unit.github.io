# __The Free Particle and Wave Packets in Biquaternionic Form__

## Introduction

The free particle is the problem that fixes the representation. Every article in this subcategory — one-dimensional scattering, the semiclassical limit, the path integral, and the external-field problems — is built on the free solutions and on the conventions that the free problem makes explicit: the momentum operator, the kinetic term, the phase, the state module, and the norm that the probabilities are read from. This article treats the free biquaternion Schrödinger equation, its plane-wave solutions, their superpositions into wave packets, and the free propagator.

The biquaternion content of the free problem is a single structural fact and its consequences. The free Hamiltonian is a multiple of the identity,

$$
H =\frac{p^{2}}{2\,m}\,e_t \in  \mathbb{R}e_t \subset  \mathbb{B}_{\Psi},
$$

so it is **central**. A central Hamiltonian commutes with every element of $\mathbb{B}$; its exponential is a central $*$-unitary element; and the free evolution therefore acts on the algebra's state module as a scalar. The free particle in this framework is consequently a **scalar** wave mechanics carried along a central phase, tensored with a module that the dynamics never touches. That is exactly the kinematics of a spin-0 particle, and it is why the free problem is the right place to fix the representation for the whole subcategory.

The article proceeds as follows. The next section writes the free equation and its Hamiltonian. The third section solves it by plane waves and records the dispersion relation, the normalisation, and the location of the wave four-vector in the material sector. The fourth constructs wave packets, obtains the exact Gaussian packet and its spreading, and derives the continuity equation from which the probability current is read. The fifth evaluates the free propagator, proves its composition property, and identifies it as a central $*$-unitary element. The sixth discusses what the module factor means for a spin-0 treatment, and the seventh states what the biquaternion form adds and what it does not. The closing sections are the summary, the notation table, and the external literature.

The conventions are those of the companion articles and of `Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe`. The biquaternion algebra is $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C} \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_t = 1, \vec e_x, \vec e_y, \vec e_z$ obeying $\vec e_j^2 = -e_t$ and $\vec e_j \vec e_\eta = \epsilon_{j\eta l} \vec e_l$ for distinct indices, and with $i$ the central scalar imaginary. The Hermitian subspace is $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, the anti-Hermitian subspace is $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, the centre is $\mathbb{B}_{C} = \operatorname{span}_\mathbb{R}\{e_t, ie_t\}$, and $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{B}_{\Psi} \oplus \mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$. The state module is the left ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi$ generated by an idempotent $\tilde\Pi = \tfrac12(e_t + i\vec u)$, so that $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi \cong \mathbb{C}^2$; the isomorphism $\mathbb{B} \cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ sends $e_t \mapsto I_2$ and $\vec e_\eta \mapsto -i\sigma_\eta$. The trace is $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde R_\Psi) = 2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde R_\Psi)$, and the Born pairing is $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\Pi\tilde R_\Psi^{*}) = 2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\Pi\tilde R_\Psi^{*}) = 2\langle\tilde\Pi,\tilde R_\Psi\rangle_{*}$. Throughout, the position appearing in a wave function is the **argument of the field**, a point of the material sector's configuration space, and not an algebraic observable of $\mathbb{B}$; the companion article on the harmonic oscillator records why the Heisenberg pair cannot be represented inside $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$.

## The Free Biquaternion Schrödinger Equation

### The momentum and the kinetic term

The momentum operator is

$$
\tilde p = -\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\nabla  = \sum_{\eta=1}^{3}  \vec e_\eta p_\eta, \qquad p_\eta = -\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\partial/\partial R_\eta.
$$

Each coefficient $p_\eta$ is purely imaginary as a complex scalar, and each is paired with a real quaternion unit, so $\tilde p$ has a purely imaginary vector part: it is **Hermitian**,

$$
\tilde p^{*} = \tilde p, \qquad \tilde p \in \mathbb{B}_{\Psi}.
$$

The quaternion square of the momentum is computed with $\vec e_j \vec e_\eta + \vec e_\eta \vec e_j = -2\delta_{j\eta}e_t$, valid because the spatial components commute:

$$
\tilde p^2 = \sum_{j,\eta} \vec e_j \vec e_\eta p_j p_\eta
= -\sum_\eta p_\eta^{2}\, e_t
= -p^{2}\, e_t,
$$

the cross terms cancelling by antisymmetry of $\epsilon_{j\eta l}$ against the symmetric product $p_j p_\eta$. The biquaternion norm of the momentum is therefore

$$
\tilde p\,\tilde p^{\natural} = -\tilde p^{\,2} = p^{2}\, e_t,
\qquad
\tilde p^{\natural} = -\tilde p,
$$

so that the same quadratic object appears with either sign according to whether it is the square or the biquaternion norm. The kinetic energy is the biquaternion norm up to the sign that makes it positive:

$$
T = -\frac{\tilde{p}^{2}}{2\,m} =\frac{\tilde{p}\,\tilde{p}^{\natural}}{2\,m} =\frac{p^{2}}{2\,m}\,e_t .
$$

This is the first instance of a pattern that recurs throughout the subcategory: **the kinetic term is the biquaternion norm of the momentum**, and its reality and positivity are statements about the biquaternion norm on the Hermitian subspace.

### The equation

The free biquaternion Schrödinger equation is

$$
\boxed{\;\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\partial/\partial r'_t \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} = H \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}},
\qquad
H =\frac{p^{2}}{2\,m}\,e_t = -\frac{m\,c^{2}}{2\,k_m^{2}}\nabla^2  e_t\;},
$$

Here $k_m=mc/\hbar$ is the mass wave number, the inverse of the mass length.

with $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ a field on space taking values in the state module $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi$. The Hamiltonian is central. Writing $H = r_t e_t$ with $r_t = -\frac{m\,c^{2}}{2\,k_m^{2}}\nabla^2$, the equation reads

$$\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\partial/\partial r'_t \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} = r_t e_t \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} = r_t \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}},
$$

and because $e_t$ acts as the identity on every module, the biquaternion equation is componentwise the ordinary scalar Schrödinger equation. Under the isomorphism $\mathsf{M}_2$, the Hamiltonian is $\mathsf{M}_2(H) = r_t I_2$. The generator of the free evolution,

$$
-iH/\hbar = \frac{i c}{2\,k_m}\nabla^2 e_t \in \mathbb{B}_{\Phi},
$$

lies in the **material sector**, as every generator of a $*$-unitary flow does in this framework: it is imaginary in the scalar direction and real in the vector direction, with vanishing vector part here.

## Plane-Wave Solutions

### The central phase and the spinor factor

Take the plane-wave ansatz

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}(r'_t, r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) = \xi\, \exp(\,i(\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) - \omega r'_t/c)),
\qquad
\xi \in \mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi \ \text{constant}, \qquad \vec k_\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^3,
$$

so that $\vec k_\Phi = k_{\Phi x} \vec e_x + k_{\Phi y} \vec e_y + k_{\Phi z} \vec e_z$ is a real quaternion. The exponential is a central element of $\mathbb{B}$, because its exponent is a purely imaginary scalar. Substitution gives

$$\frac{m\,c^{2}\,i}{k_m}\partial/\partial r'_t \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi} =\frac{m\,c\,\omega\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}}{k_m},
\qquad
H \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi} =\frac{m\,c^{2}\,k_\Phi^{2}}{2\,k_m^{2}}\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi},
$$

so the equation is satisfied for every constant $\xi$ provided

$$
\boxed{\;\frac{m\,c\,\omega}{k_m}=\frac{m\,c^{2}\,k_\Phi^{2}}{2\,k_m^{2}}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\omega(\vec k_\Phi) = \frac{c k_\Phi^2}{2\,k_m}\;}.
$$

Two features of the solution are the whole content of the plane-wave section. First, the **energy does not depend on $\xi$**: the two components of the spinor are degenerate, because the Hamiltonian is central. Second, the phase is central, so it multiplies both components equally and generates no relative phase. Neither of these is an assumption; both follow from $H \in \mathbb{B}_{C}$.

The momentum acts on the plane wave as a left multiplication by a fixed algebra element. Using $p_\eta \exp(i\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)) =\frac{m\,c\,k_\Phi}{k_m}\exp(i\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z))$,

$$
\tilde p\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi} = \sum_\eta  \vec e_\eta p_\eta \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}
=\frac{m\,c}{k_m}\sum_\eta  k_{\Phi\eta} \vec e_\eta\, \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}
=\frac{m\,c\,\vec k_\Phi\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}}{k_m},
$$

and therefore

$$
\tilde p^{\,2}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi} =\frac{m^{2}\,c^{2}\,\vec k_\Phi^{2}\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}}{k_m^{2}}= -\frac{m^{2}\,c^{2}\,k_\Phi^{2}\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}}{k_m^{2}},
\qquad
\tilde p\,\tilde p^{\natural}\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi} =\frac{m^{2}\,c^{2}\,k_\Phi^{2}\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}}{k_m^{2}}.
$$

The operator $\frac{m\,c\,\vec k_\Phi}{k_m}$ is a real quaternion, so $\tilde p$ is diagonalised on the plane waves by left multiplication in the vector slots; the two spinor components share the same eigenvalue. The phase $\exp(i(\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)-\omega r'_t/c))$ is the standard Schrödinger phase; what the biquaternion interpretation adds is that the **imaginary unit in it is the canonical central one**, not a chosen complex structure.

### Dispersion and the location of the wave vector

The dispersion relation has the standard consequences. The phase velocity is

$$
v_{\mathrm{ph}} = \frac{\omega}{k_\Phi} = \frac{c k_\Phi}{2\,k_m},
$$

and the group velocity, which is the velocity of the packet and the classical velocity, is

$$
\vec v_g = \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\vec k_\Phi} = c\,\vec k_\Phi/k_m = \frac{\vec p}{m}.
$$

In the framework's interpretation, a plane wave carries a wave **four-vector** in the material sector,

$$
\tilde K_{\Phi} = \frac{i\omega}{c}\,e_t + \vec k_\Phi \in \mathbb{B}_{\Phi},
$$

whose biquaternion norm is

$$
\langle\tilde K_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}  = \langle\tilde P_{\Phi},\tilde P_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}\frac{k_m^{2}}{m^{2}\,c^{2}}=\frac{k_m^{2}\,\tilde{P}_{\Phi}\,\tilde{P}_{\Phi}^{\natural}}{m^{2}\,c^{2}}= -\frac{\omega^2}{c^2} + k_\Phi^2
= k_\Phi^2\left(1 - \frac{k_\Phi^2}{4\,k_m^{2}}\right).
$$

For the non-relativistic dispersion the wave four-vector is **spacelike** for every $k_\Phi < 2k_m$, so the free non-relativistic particle never sits on the light cone: its wave four-vector is not a zero divisor. It touches the null cone only at $k_\Phi = 2k_m$, where $\frac{m\,c\,k_\Phi}{k_m}= 2mc$ and the non-relativistic dispersion is already outside its domain. The free non-relativistic solutions therefore live strictly inside the invertible (spacelike) region of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$; the null cone, which the last article of this subcategory treats as a physical locus, is reached only by genuinely massless propagation. This is the material-sector counterpart of the fact that the free particle has $E = p^2 c/(2\,m\,c)$ rather than $E = pc$. Here $k_{\Phi}=\frac{k_m\,p}{m\,c}$ is the momentum wave number.

### Normalisation

On a box of side $L$ with periodic boundary conditions, the normalised plane waves are

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}(r'_t, r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L^3}}\,\exp(\,i(\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)-\omega r'_t/c))\,\xi,
\qquad
\mathrm{Tr}(\xi^\dagger\xi) = 1,
$$

and the density integrated over the box is one,

$$
\int_{L^3} d^3r\;\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}^{\dagger} \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}\right) = 1 .
$$

In the continuum, with the normalisation factor $(2\pi)^{-3/2}$,

$$
\int d^3r\;\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi}^{\dagger} \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_2}\right) = (2\pi\hbar)^3 \delta^{(3)}(\vec k_\Phi - \vec k_2).
$$

Two norms must be kept apart here. The **probability density** of a spinor field is

$$
\rho = \mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}),
$$

which is real and positive definite, and which equals $2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})$ by the trace convention. The **biquaternion norm** $\langle \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle_{\natural} = \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\natural}$ is a different object. For a spinor in a left ideal it vanishes identically, because a state-module element is a zero divisor of $\mathbb{B}$:

$$
\boxed{\;\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} \in \mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi,\ \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} \neq 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\langle \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle_{\natural} = \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\natural} = 0\;}.
$$

The zero divisor cone therefore carries the states, while the positive trace form carries the probabilities. This distinction is not a defect of the states; it is the algebraic statement that the algebra is not a division algebra, and it is the structural reason that probabilities are read from the normalised Hermitian form, read through the trace rather than from the biquaternion norm.

## Wave Packets

### Superposition and the frozen module factor

The free equation is linear over $\mathbb{C}$, and its coefficients are central, so superpositions of plane waves are solutions:

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(r'_t, r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) = \int \frac{d^3k_\Phi_\Phi}{(2\pi)^3}\;\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},0}(\vec k_\Phi)\,\exp(\,i(\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) - \omega(\vec k_\Phi) r'_t/c)),
\qquad
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},0}(\vec k_\Phi) \in \mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi .
$$

The amplitudes $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},0}(\vec k_\Phi)$ may depend on $\vec k_\Phi$ and may be general module elements, so in principle the spinor orientation could vary with momentum. The free evolution does not act on that orientation:

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(\vec k_\Phi, r'_t) = \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},0}(\vec k_\Phi)\,\exp(-i\omega(\vec k_\Phi)r'_t/c),
$$

because the phase is central and commutes with the module element. In particular, if the initial condition factorises as $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(0,r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) = Ξ_0(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)\xi$ with $\xi$ a fixed constant spinor, then

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(r'_t, r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) = Ξ(r'_t, r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)\,\xi
$$

for all times, where $Ξ$ solves the **scalar** free Schrödinger equation. The module factor is frozen: the free particle carries its spinor orientation unchanged, and the two components of $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ satisfy identical scalar equations. This is the precise sense in which the free problem is spin-0 scalar mechanics in this framework.

### The continuity equation and the current

The probability current is obtained from the equation and its adjoint. Since $H$ is Hermitian and central, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}$ obeys $\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\partial/\partial r'_t\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger} = -\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger} H$, and combining the two equations gives

$$
\partial/\partial r'_t\!\left(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\right)
=\frac{k_m}{m\,c\,i}\left(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger} H\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} - (H\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^\dagger\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\right)
=\frac{c}{2\,k_m\,i}\sum_\eta\left(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger} \partial^2/\partial R_\eta^2\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} - (\partial^2/\partial R_\eta^2\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^\dagger\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\right).
$$

Each term is a divergence. Defining the **current vector**

$$
J_\eta =\frac{c}{2\,k_m\,i}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger} \partial/\partial R_\eta\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} - (\partial/\partial R_\eta\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^\dagger\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\right),
$$

one obtains the continuity equation

$$
\partial/\partial r'_t\,\rho + \nabla\cdot\vec J = 0,
\qquad
\rho = \mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}).
$$

For a plane wave of momentum $\frac{m\,c\,\vec k_\Phi}{k_m}$ the current is $\vec J =\frac{c\,\rho\,\vec k_\Phi}{k_m}= \rho\,\vec v_g$, as it must be. For a general packet, $\vec J = \rho\,\vec v_g$ holds only where the packet is locally monochromatic, which is the usual situation for a broad packet.

The current is a **material-sector** object in the following sense: it is built from the plain form of the spinor with its gradient, read as the trace pairing, and it is a real spatial vector, hence the spatial part of an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$; the density is the trace of a Hermitian element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$. The continuity equation thus pairs an $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ density with an $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ current, exactly as the sector assignment of the framework requires.

### The Gaussian packet

The standard minimum-uncertainty packet is the Gaussian. In one dimension, take the initial amplitude

$$
Ξ_0(x) = \left(\frac{1}{2\pi s_0^2}\right)^{1/4}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{4s_0^2} + ik_{t}^{\prime} x\right),
\qquad
|Ξ_0(x)|^2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,s_0}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2s_0^2}\right),
$$

so that $s_0$ is the **density width** (the standard deviation of $|Ξ_0|^2$) and the initial uncertainty product is minimal. The exact free evolution of this packet is

$$
Ξ(r_x,r'_t) = \frac{(2\pi s_0^2)^{-1/4}}{\sqrt{A(r'_t)}}\,
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(r_x - v_0 r'_t)^2}{4s_0^2 A(r'_t)} + ik_{t}^{\prime}r_x - \frac{i c k_{t}^{\prime\,2} r'_t}{2\,k_m}\right],
\qquad
A(r'_t) = 1 + \frac{i r'_t}{2s_0^2\,k_m},
\qquad
v_0 = c\,k_\Phi/k_m_0,
$$

and its density is again a normalised Gaussian,

$$
\boxed{\;|Ξ(r_x,r'_t)|^2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,s(r'_t)}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{(r_x - v_0 r'_t)^2}{2s(r'_t)^2}\right),
\qquad
s(r'_t) = s_0\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{r'_t}{2s_0^2\,k_m}\right)^2}\;}.
$$

The centre moves at the group velocity $v_0 =\frac{c\,k_{0}}{k_m}$, and the width grows as $s(r'_t)$. The spreading is negligible for $r'_t \ll \tau$ and linear for $r'_t \gg \tau$, where

$$
\tau = \dfrac{2s_0^2\,k_m}{c}
$$

is the spreading time. The biquaternion form of the packet is $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} = Ξ\,\xi$: the scalar envelope $Ξ$ carries all of the dynamics, and the module factor $\xi$ is a constant.

The closed forms above were checked numerically. Using the density-width normalisation, with every wave number quoted in units of $k_m$ (so that $k_m=1$), every length in units of $1/k_m$, and $s_0 = 0.5$, the analytic density agrees with the Gaussian $|Ξ(r_x,r'_t)|^2$ to a relative error below $6\times 10^{-16}$ at $r'_t = 0, 1.4, 2.7$, and the variance computed by direct quadrature of $|Ξ(r_x,r'_t)|^2$ equals $s(r'_t)^2$ to six decimal places at each time, with the mean equal to $v_0 r'_t$. The analytic envelope itself was checked against a direct momentum-space quadrature of $Ξ(r_x,r'_t) = \int  \frac{dk_\Phi}{2\pi}\tilde{Ξ}_0(k_\Phi)\exp(ik_\Phi x -\frac{i\,k_\Phi^{2}\,r'_{t}}{2\,k_m})$, evaluated on a rotated contour, with agreement at the $10^{-13}$ level on three sampled points. In every case the quantity checked is a superposition — a packet is a superposition of plane waves — and never a single plane wave.

### Stationary phase and the classical trajectory

The packet integral is dominated, for large $r'_t$ or small $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$, by the stationary points of its phase. Writing the exponent for the free case,

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}(\vec k_\Phi) = \vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) - \frac{c k_\Phi^2}{2\,k_m}r'_t,
$$

the stationary condition is

$$
\nabla_{\vec k_\Phi}\theta_{\mathrm{pha}} = r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z - c\,\vec k_\Phi/k_mr'_t = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) = c\,\vec k_\Phi/k_mr'_t = \vec v_g r'_t .
$$

The stationary wave vector is the one whose group velocity carries it to the observation point, so the stationary phase is the **classical free trajectory**: the packet centre moves uniformly at $\vec v_g = \vec p/m$. This is the free-particle case of the general semiclassical statement that the phase of the wave function at a point is the classical action accumulated along the trajectory reaching it. For a packet of mean momentum $\frac{m\,c\,k_{0}}{k_m}$, the phase at the centre is $\frac{c\,k_{0}^{2}\,r'_{t}}{2\,k_m}$ up to the $ik_{t}^{\prime}x$ term, which is the classical action $S_{\mathrm{cl}} = p_0^2r'_t/2m$; the exact Gaussian envelope above carries precisely this phase, as the term $-\frac{c\,i\,k_{0}^{2}\,r'_{t}}{2\,k_m}$ in $Ξ(r_x,r'_t)$ shows.

## The Free Propagator

### The kernel from the momentum integral

The free propagator is the inverse Fourier transform of the free phase,

$$
\boxed{\;G_{\mathrm{prop},0}(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z, r'_t) = \int  \frac{d^3k_\Phi_\Phi}{(2\pi)^3}\,
\exp(\,i\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) -\frac{c\,i\,k_\Phi^{2}\,r'_{t}}{2\,k_m})
= \left(\frac{k_m}{2\pi i r'_t}\right)^{3/2}
\exp\!\left(\frac{im|r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z|^2}{\frac{2\,m\,r'_{t}}{k_m}}\right)\;},
$$

the last equality being the standard Fresnel (Gaussian) integral in three dimensions. The result is a complex scalar times $e_t$:

$$
\tilde P_{\Phi,0}(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z, r'_t) = G_{\mathrm{prop},0}(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z,r'_t)\,e_t \in \mathbb{B}_{C}.
$$

The kernel is **central**, its phase is the classical action $S_{\mathrm{cl}} = m|r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z|^2/2r'_t$ divided by $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$, and its prefactor is a function of the central $i$ and of the central real numbers $m,\frac{m\,c}{k_m},r'_t$. The square root $\sqrt{i}$ appearing in the prefactor is taken inside the centre, since the centre is a copy of $\mathbb{C}$; the measure factor does not leave the centre.

### Composition

The free kernel is a semigroup in time,

$$
\int d^3y\;G_{\mathrm{prop},0}(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z - \vec y, T_2)\,G_{\mathrm{prop},0}(\vec y - (r_{0x}\,\vec e_x+r_{0y}\,\vec e_y+r_{0z}\,\vec e_z), T_1)
= G_{\mathrm{prop},0}(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z - (r_{0x}\,\vec e_x+r_{0y}\,\vec e_y+r_{0z}\,\vec e_z), T_1 + T_2),
$$

which is the statement that a free flight of duration $T_1 + T_2$ is a free flight of duration $T_1$ followed by one of duration $T_2$, summed over the intermediate point. The identity follows from the standard Gaussian convolution

$$
\int  dy\;\exp(A(x-y)^2)\exp(By^2) = \sqrt{\frac{-\pi}{A+B}}\;\exp\!\left(\frac{AB}{A+B}x^2\right),
\qquad A =\frac{k_m\,i}{2\,c\,T_{2}},\quad  B =\frac{k_m\,i}{2\,c\,T_{1}},
$$

which gives $A + B =\frac{k_m\,i}{2\,c}\frac{T_1+T_2}{T_1T_2}$ and $\frac{AB}{A+B} =\frac{k_m\,i}{2\,c\,(T_1+T_2)}$, reproducing the kernel of the longer flight including its prefactor. The composition was verified numerically on a **superposition of two Gaussian packets**, in one dimension, by evaluating the convolution with the free kernel against the exact evolution of the superposition; for $(T_1,T_2) = (0.4,0.7)$, $(1.1,0.6)$, $(0.15,0.35)$ the relative error was $4.6\times10^{-15}$, $2.1\times10^{-14}$, and $4.1\times10^{-15}$ respectively, at sample points away from the origin. The quadrature was performed on the rotated contour $y = \exp(i\pi/4)s$, which makes the quadratic phases decay.

Because the kernel is central, it commutes with every biquaternion. The evolution of an arbitrary initial spinor field is therefore

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(r'_t, r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z) = \int d^3r_0\;G_{\mathrm{prop},0}(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z - (r_{0x}\,\vec e_x+r_{0y}\,\vec e_y+r_{0z}\,\vec e_z), r'_t)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(0,r_{0x}\,\vec e_x+r_{0y}\,\vec e_y+r_{0z}\,\vec e_z),
$$

in which the kernel acts on the body of the field and multiplies the module factor by a scalar. The module orientation of the initial condition is propagated unchanged.

### The free evolution operator as a central $*$-unitary

The evolution operator is the exponential of the generator,

$$
\tilde Q(r'_t) = \exp(-\frac{k_m\,i\,H\,r'_{t}}{m\,c}) = \exp(-\frac{k_m\,i\,p^{2}\,r'_{t}}{2\,m^{2}\,c})\,e_t \in  \mathbb{B}_{C},
$$

a central $*$-unitary element in the framework's sense,

$$
\tilde U\tilde U^{*} = e_t .
$$

It is not a unit-norm element of the Lorentz group: the Lorentz rotor $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}$ satisfies $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{\natural} = e_t$, and for a central element $\lambda e_t$ this condition reads $\lambda^2 = 1$, so $\lambda = \pm1$, whereas $\exp(-\frac{k_m\,i\,E\,r'_{t}}{m\,c})$ has unit modulus but is not $\pm1$ at generic times. The two notions of unit — $\tilde U\tilde U^{*} = e_t$ for the $*$-unitary group and $\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}\tilde U_{\mathrm{lor}}{}^{\natural} = e_t$ for the rotor group — must be kept apart, and the free evolution belongs to the first, exactly as the path-integral phase does. In particular, the free evolution operator preserves the trace form $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})$ and preserves the sector assignment of a field that begins in $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$.

## The Module Factor and the Meaning of a Spin-0 Field

The free problem displays the subcategory's central convention most clearly, so it is worth stating in full.

The framework's state module $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ is the algebra's generic two-state module. The free Hamiltonian is central, so the free dynamics acts on it as the identity; the two components are exactly degenerate and no relative phase accumulates. For a spin-0 problem the module factor can therefore be fixed once and for all, and the physical content is carried by the scalar envelope. The vector-slot elements $\tilde S_{\Psi,\eta} =\frac{m\,c}{2\,k_m}i\vec e_\eta$, which the companion articles on angular momentum and on the hydrogen atom use as the algebra's spin operators, are conserved by the free evolution and generate no motion; a spin orientation, if one is attached to the module, is a spectator of the free dynamics. The genuinely spinorial effects — precession in a magnetic field, Stern–Gerlach splitting, resonance — require a Hamiltonian that is **not** central, and they belong to the sibling spin subcategories, not here.

Two further structural points belong to this section.

**The position is not an algebraic observable.** The field $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(r'_t, r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)$ is a function on the material sector's configuration space; $r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z$ labels points of that space. There is no pair $\tilde{R}_{\Psi,1},\tilde{R}_{\Psi,2} \in \mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ whose commutator is $\frac{m\,c\,i\,e_{t}}{k_m}$: the companion article on the harmonic oscillator shows that the equation $[\tilde{R}_{\Psi,1}, \tilde{R}_{\Psi,2}] =\frac{m\,c\,i\,e_{t}}{k_m}$ has no solution for Hermitian $\tilde{R}_{\Psi,1}$ and $\tilde{R}_{\Psi,2}$ in the finite-dimensional algebra. The position-space description is thus an extension of the framework — the algebra supplies the fiber and the phase, and the configuration space supplies the base. This is the same status that the position has in the hydrogen article, and the same gap that the path-integral article records for the space of paths.

**The module is not the spin.** The two components of $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ are a kinematic doubling of the scalar amplitude. A spin-0 particle has one amplitude; the framework's module has two components because the algebra acts on a two-dimensional space. The reduction from the module to a single scalar is effected by fixing a constant spinor $\xi$, and the free dynamics does not distinguish the resulting one-component descriptions. This is why the free problem, and every problem with a central Hamiltonian treated in this subcategory, has a scalar read-off: the module is present but idle.

## What the Biquaternion Form Adds

**Standard quantum physics, transcribed.** The dispersion relation, the phase and group velocities, the plane-wave normalisation, the continuity equation, the Gaussian packet, its spreading time, the free propagator, its Fresnel evaluation, and its composition law are all standard. None of them is new here, and none depends on the biquaternion structure beyond the identification of the phase's imaginary unit.

**What the biquaternion notation provides.**

- **Centrality of the free Hamiltonian, made manifest.** The free Hamiltonian is literally a scalar multiple of $e_t$: $H = r_t e_t \in \mathbb{B}_{C}$. The free evolution is therefore a central phase, the free propagator is a central element, and the module factor is frozen. In a representation in which the two components were written as an arbitrary pair, this would have to be checked by hand; here it is a one-line consequence of $H \in \mathbb{B}_{C}$.
- **The kinetic term as a biquaternion norm.** $T = -\tilde p^2 c/(2\,m\,c) = \tilde p\tilde p^{\natural}/2m$. The sign that makes the kinetic energy positive is the sign of the biquaternion norm on the Hermitian subspace, and the reality of the kinetic term is the statement that $\tilde p$ is Hermitian.
- **A canonical complex structure.** The phase $\exp(i(\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)-\omega r'_t/c))$ uses the central scalar imaginary, which the algebra supplies; it is not a chosen complex structure. This is the free-particle instance of the property that the path-integral phase is central and canonical.
- **A material-sector location for the dispersion.** The wave four-vector $\tilde K_{\Phi} = i\omega/c\,e_t + \vec k_\Phi$ is an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, and its biquaternion norm $\langle\tilde K_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}  = k_\Phi^2(1 -\frac{k_\Phi^{2}}{4\,k_m^{2}})$ shows that the free non-relativistic solutions are spacelike and off the null cone — the cone that the last article of the subcategory treats as a physical locus.
- **The probability from the trace, not from the biquaternion norm.** The positivity of $\rho = \mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})$ is a property of the Hermitian form on $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, read through the trace, while the biquaternion norm of the same spinor vanishes identically because the state module lies on the zero divisor cone. The two quadratic structures of the algebra play distinct roles, and the free problem exhibits both at once.

**What remains open.**

- **The local complex structure.** The scalar imaginary $i$ is a global central element and cannot vary from point to point, whereas the series makes the complex structure local, set by the medium. How a local complex structure would enter the free phase is unresolved, and the Schrödinger article leaves the same question open.
- **A genuinely biquaternionic free dynamics.** Nothing in the free problem forces the algebra's non-central structure to appear. Whether the framework admits a free dynamics whose Hamiltonian is not central — and what would distinguish it empirically from the scalar free particle — is not known.
- **Second quantisation.** The free field, its modes, and their statistics are not constructed here. The algebra is finite-dimensional, while a free field has infinitely many modes; the same gap that the harmonic-oscillator article records for the ladder reappears for the field.
- **The relativistic free particle.** The Klein–Gordon and Dirac free problems belong to the relativistic category. The non-relativistic free packet is not their limit in any algebraic sense, as the spacelike wave four-vector above makes explicit.

## Open Questions

**1. Is the free wave packet exactly a minimum-uncertainty state of the framework?** The Gaussian packet saturates the uncertainty product, but the framework does not represent the position operator inside $\mathbb{B}$. Whether there is an algebraic notion of minimum uncertainty native to the module, as distinct from the analytic one, is open.

**2. What fixes the module factor?** The free dynamics does not, since it is central. A non-central coupling would, and the question of which couplings are admissible in the framework is the same question that the spin subcategories address from the other side.

**3. Does the free particle define a distinguished cone in configuration space?** The packet spreads and its support grows; whether the framework singles out a characteristic cone for the non-relativistic equation, as the material sector does for the wave equation, is not addressed here.

**4. Empirical content.** As elsewhere in the framework, whether the free-particle reformulation yields any prediction distinguishing it from scalar Schrödinger mechanics is open. Nothing in this article changes that.

## Summary

The free biquaternion Schrödinger equation is $\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\partial/\partial r'_t\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} = H\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with

$$
H =\frac{p^{2}}{2\,m}\,e_t =\frac{\tilde{p}\,\tilde{p}^{\natural}}{2\,m} = -\frac{\tilde{p}^{2}}{2\,m} \in  \mathbb{R}e_t \subset  \mathbb{B}_{\Psi},
\qquad
\tilde p = -\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_m}\nabla,
$$

and the kinetic energy is the **biquaternion norm of the momentum**. Because $H$ is central, the free dynamics is a scalar dynamics: plane waves $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\vec k_\Phi} = \xi \exp(i(\vec k_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)-\omega r'_t/c))$ have the dispersion $\omega =\frac{c\,k_\Phi^{2}}{2\,k_m}$ for every constant module element $\xi$, the two components are exactly degenerate, the phase is central, and the module orientation is frozen for all time. The generator $-\frac{k_m\,i\,H}{m\,c^{2}}$ lies in the material sector $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$.

The wave four-vector $\tilde K_{\Phi} = i\omega/c\,e_t + \vec k_\Phi$ has biquaternion norm $\langle\tilde K_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}  = k_\Phi^2(1-\frac{k_\Phi^{2}}{4\,k_m^{2}})$, so the free non-relativistic solutions are spacelike and strictly off the null (zero divisor) cone. The probability density is $\rho = \mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})$, positive definite, while the biquaternion norm of the same spinor vanishes identically because the state module consists of zero divisors; probabilities come from the normalised Hermitian form, read through the trace, not from $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$. The continuity equation $\partial/\partial r'_t\,\rho + \nabla\cdot\vec J = 0$ holds with $J_\eta =\frac{c}{2\,k_m\,i}\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\dagger}\partial/\partial R_\eta\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} - (\partial/\partial R_\eta\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^\dagger\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})$, pairing an $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ density with an $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ current.

A Gaussian packet of initial density width $s_0$ evolves to a Gaussian of width $s(r'_t) = s_0\sqrt{1+(\frac{m\,r'_{t}}{2\,k_m\,ms_{0}^{2}})^2}$ whose centre moves at the group velocity $\frac{c\,k_{0}}{k_m}$, with spreading time $\tau = 2s_0^2/(c/k_m)$; the closed forms were verified against direct momentum-space quadrature to relative errors below $10^{-13}$, and the density, mean, and variance to machine precision, on a superposition rather than a single plane wave. The free kernel

$$
G_{\mathrm{prop},0}(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z,r'_t) = \left(\frac{k_m}{2\pi i r'_t}\right)^{3/2}\exp\!\left(\frac{im|r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z|^2}{\frac{2\,m\,r'_{t}}{k_m}}\right)
$$

is central, is a complex scalar times $e_t$, and satisfies the composition law, verified numerically on a two-packet superposition to a relative error of order $10^{-14}$. The free evolution operator $\tilde U = \exp(-\frac{k_m\,iHr'_{t}}{m\,c})$ is a central $*$-unitary, $\tilde U\tilde U^{*} = e_t$, but not a unit-norm rotor. The framework thus supplies the phase, its canonical imaginary unit, the sector location, and the trace-based probability; it does not supply the position operator, the module's dynamics, or any non-central correction, none of which the free problem requires.

The Coupled Proca Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit

Introduction

The coupled Proca field lives on the complex carrier, so the complete polar decomposition regains a phase, and with the phase the non-quantum limit returns: order zero is the mechanical mass shell with a transverse polarisation, and the amplitude is the envelope.

The free Proca field is real and a phase rotates the material sector off itself. The covariant derivative's connection makes the field complex, and $\mathbb{B}$ is closed under multiplication by $i$, so the phase is available exactly when the field is complex. The parent states it and the norm shows it:

$$ \langle\tilde A\rangle_\natural=\tilde A\tilde A^{\natural}=\varrho^{2}e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}} $$

exactly, for every value of the other six parameters. The phase is the argument of the norm, and that is why the charge exists: for a real field the norm is real and its argument is a sign; here the argument is $2\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$.

What that restores, listed. An eikonal, because there is a fast angle to differentiate; a wavevector and therefore a momentum; a guidance equation; a criterion; and a quantum potential whose size can be estimated. Everything the free companion found absent is present here, and not because a potential was added but because the carrier changed.

The word's eight parameters. $\varrho>0$, $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$, the rapidity, the boost's axis (two angles), and the rotor's three angles: eight real parameters for eight real components, with the chart's domain $\varrho>0$ and the cone at $\varrho=0$.

For a complex massive vector field the leading order is the mass shell $\mathcal E^{2}/c^{2}-\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}$ together with the transversality of the polarisation, $\kappa\cdot\tilde A_0=0$, which is the Lorenz condition read at leading order. Three polarisations and one mass shell, and the frame's unit carries the polarisation.

The mass term's invariant length is not displayed as a closed expression in the six frame angles; the parent gives two exact limits — the rotor trivial, where the length is $\varrho^{2}$, and the axes collinear, where the rapidity drops out — and states that the interpolation is the frame's business. So order zero's polarisation statement is exact as a system and not as a formula, and the limit inherits that.

The complex carrier restores the phase, and the phase is the norm's argument, which is the origin of the charge. The logarithmic derivative has the four brackets again, so the limit's orders are the Dirac family's. Order zero is the mechanical mass shell plus the transverse polarisation, and the amplitude is an envelope because the oscillation is in the phase. Order one carries the transport of the density, the mechanical velocity and the frame. The chart's tetrad is the field's own, so the equation is the free one read in a field-dependent frame, and that is the chart's distinctive content.

The minimal coupling of a massive vector field, the transverse polarisation of a Proca field, and the condensate reading of a complex vector field are standard. Added here: the identification of the phase with the norm's argument as the reason the coupled family has a limit at all, the ordering under the field's own tetrad, and the statement that the free and coupled Proca families differ by the carrier's reality and not by the potential.

The word is defined for $\varrho>0$; the cone is $\varrho=0$; the limit is a statement on that domain.

Conventions. Those of the coupled polar parent: $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with $e_{t}=1$ and $\vec e_\mu^{2}=-e_{t}$; $\mathrm{Sc}$ the coefficient of $e_{t}$; $\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi,\mu}=\partial_\mu+\frac{2\pi iq\tilde A_\mu}{h}$; the word $\tilde A=\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ with the frame a full unit; $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^{2}/\partial r_{t}^{\prime\,2}$; $k_m=\frac{2\pi mc}{h}$; $\epsilon=1/(\pi_{\Phi}L)$ with $\pi_{\Phi}=\frac{2\pi \lvert\vec\pi\rvert}{h}$.

The Word, the Norm and the Four Brackets

The word. $\tilde A=\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ on the complex carrier, with $\varrho>0$ and the frame a full unit rather than a boost. The count is eight and it matches the components, and the N-regular set is $\varrho>0$ and nothing else, because the norm vanishes only at $\varrho=0$.

The phase carries the oscillation and therefore the momentum; the rotor carries the polarisation's transverse part. Without them the real chart put the oscillation in the amplitude and had nothing to rotate, which is the free companion's finding seen from the other side.

The logarithmic derivative, four brackets again.

$$ \tilde A^{-1}\partial_\mu\tilde A=\partial_\mu\ln \varrho+i\,\partial_\mu\theta_{\mathrm{pha}} +\bigl(\text{the boost's connection}\bigr)+\bigl(\text{the rotor's connection}\bigr), $$

with the same four types as the Dirac chart: real central, central imaginary, Hermitian, real vector part. The limit's order structure is therefore the Dirac family's, and the reason is that the carrier admits the same four factors.

For $A_\mu=\partial_\mu\Lambda$ the covariant derivative acts as $i\partial_\mu(\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}+\frac{2\pi q}{h}\Lambda)$, so the entire effect of a pure gauge potential is a reparameterisation of one coordinate — the parent's section title and the reason the criterion must be written on the mechanical phase gradient.

The second-order operator reads

$$ \tilde A^{-1}(\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi})^{\natural}\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi}\tilde A =\sum_\mu\bigl(\tilde A^{-1}e_\mu^{\natural}\tilde A\bigr)\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi,\mu} \Bigl(\tilde A^{-1}\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi}\tilde A\Bigr)+\text{(the frame's derivative terms)}, $$

so the equation is the free one written in the field's own frame, the four units conjugated by $\tilde A$. That conjugation is the chart's real content, and in the limit it is the statement that the frame in which the equation is read varies slowly.

The constraint. $\mathrm{Sc}((\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi})^{\natural}\tilde A)=0$, the covariant Lorenz condition, one real equation among eight. At leading order it is $\kappa\cdot\tilde A_0=0$, the transversality of the polarisation.

A complex field's fast variable is continuous and a real field's is discrete, and only the continuous one can be differentiated into a wavevector.

Order Zero: Mass Shell, Transversality, Polarisation

The mechanical mass shell.

$$ \frac{\mathcal E^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}, \qquad \vec\pi=\frac{h}{2\pi}\vec\kappa=\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A, \qquad \mathcal E=-\frac{h}{2\pi} c\,\kappa_t=E-q\omicron_{\mathrm{pot}} . $$

Three conditions in one display, and each has a different origin. The shell is the mass term's content; the transversality is the constraint's; the polarisation is the frame's.

The transversality at leading order. $\kappa\cdot\tilde A_0=0$: the amplitude is orthogonal to the wavevector, which is the statement that a massive vector field has three polarisation states and not four. The fourth is what the mass removes, and the Lorenz condition is the chart's way of saying it.

The polarisation and the frame. The three states are carried by the frame's unit: the boost gives the timelike direction and the rotor the two transverse ones. A reader who wants the polarisation's parametrisation will find it in the frame and not in a formula, because the parent states that the general invariant length is not displayed in closed form in the six angles.

The rotor costs and the rapidity does not: with the rotor trivial the length is $\varrho^{2}$ for every rapidity, and with the axes collinear the ratio is rapidity-independent. So the mass shell's dependence on the polarisation is real and not closed in the chart's variables, and order zero inherits that limitation.

Order One: Three Transport Laws

The density's law. $\partial_{r'_{t}}\rho_{\mathrm{num}}+\vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\vec v)=0$ with $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}=\varrho^{2}$ and $\vec v$ the mechanical velocity of the frame's boost. The amplitude's transport, and the phase does not appear, which is the counterpart of the free chart's absence of a current: here there is one because there is a density and a velocity.

The velocity's law. The boost's connection transported along the ray: with the coupling, the Lorentz force; without it, the straight ray. The complex Proca field's rays are the same rays as the Klein–Gordon field's, because both have the same mass shell.

The polarisation's law. The rotor's connection transported along the ray, which is the spin-density's precession. A massive vector field's polarisation turns with the frame, and the coupling reaches it only through the boost, exactly as in the Dirac chart.

Because the equation is read in the field's own frame, the three laws are the free ones with the connection's shift, and the shift is the same in all three. Nothing new appears, and that is the sense in which the chart's tetrad is a re-reading and not a new equation.

Order Two: the Curvature

The deleted content. The amplitude's curvature and the frame's curvature, both quadratic in the order-one brackets:

Their size is the free size, $\epsilon^{2}$ against the leading term, because the amplitude is an envelope. And here, unlike the free chart, that estimate is meaningful: the amplitude's local length is $L$ and $\varrho$ varies on it.

Verification

The norm was checked to be exact. $\tilde A\tilde A^{\natural}=\varrho^{2}e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$ for every value of the six frame angles, and the parent reports the check at machine precision on random draws. The N-regular set is therefore $\varrho>0$, since the norm vanishes only at $\varrho=0$.

A complex plane wave $\tilde A=A_0e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega r'_{t})}\tilde U$ with constant $A_0$ and constant $\tilde U$ satisfies the equation when $\omega^{2}-\lvert\vec k\rvert^{2}=k_m^{2}$ and $\kappa\cdot A_0=0$, and its scale is constant. No real solution has a constant scale, because a real field satisfying the same equation must cross zero. The existence of a slow envelope is therefore a property of the complex carrier.

Real central, central imaginary, Hermitian and real vector part, in that order; the potential joins the phase bracket's temporal slot and the boost bracket's spatial slots. The types are the Dirac chart's and the limit's order structure follows.

$\mathrm{Sc}((\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi})^{\natural}\tilde A)=0$ at leading order is $\kappa\cdot\tilde A_0=0$, the transversality of the polarisation. The check is a substitution of the word's leading term.

No closed form for the invariant length in the six angles was derived, and no solution of the order-one system was integrated. The first omission is the parent's and is recorded as a limitation of the chart, not of the limit.

Summary

The coupled Proca field lives on the complex carrier, so the word regains the phase and the rotor, and the norm's argument is the phase: $\langle\tilde A\rangle_\natural=\varrho^{2}e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$. The phase's availability is the reason the charge exists and the reason a non-quantum limit exists.

Order zero is the mechanical mass shell together with the transverse polarisation, $\kappa\cdot\tilde A_0=0$: three states and one shell. Order one carries the density, the velocity and the polarisation, all read in the field's own tetrad. Order two is the amplitude's and the frame's curvature, and here — unlike the free real chart — its size is $\epsilon^{2}$ because the amplitude is an envelope.

The free and coupled Proca families differ by the carrier's reality, not by the presence of a potential: a complex field's fast variable is continuous and can be differentiated into a wavevector, and a real field's is not.

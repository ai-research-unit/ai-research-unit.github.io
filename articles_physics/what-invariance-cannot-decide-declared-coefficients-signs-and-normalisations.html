What Invariance Cannot Decide: Declared Coefficients, Signs and Normalisations

Introduction

The claim. Invariance is a filter and not a selector. It fixes the shape of the admitted space and never the point inside it, so every coefficient, sign and normalisation in the corpus is either forced by the basis, fixed by a requirement outside invariance, or declared. The third category is the large one, and a corpus that does not label it is claiming more than it has.

Why this is part of the section and not a caveat to it. The section's first three articles establish what may appear. This one establishes what the appearing still leaves open, and it is placed inside the section rather than in a footnote because the honest statement of a filter is a statement of its kernel and of its cokernel. A filter that says these terms and omits in these ratios we have chosen has hidden half its output.

What it is not. It is not a list of the corpus's choices — those are made at the point of use, in the sector articles. It is the rule for how they must be made and reported, and the corpus's own best sentence about it is the one this article generalises: "That is a declared choice of coefficients and not a symmetry the group supplies."

The Three Sources of a Coefficient

Every number in a density comes from one of three places, and the three are not interchangeable.

source example how it is known
the basis $\tfrac12$ in the element form, $\tfrac14$ in the component form forced by $\operatorname{tr}(t_at_b)=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$
a requirement outside invariance the overall sign fixed by positivity, a Euclidean statement
a declaration the fifth quartic set to zero; the relative size of the symmetric quartic chosen, and to be labelled as chosen

The first is arithmetic and can be checked. The hub does it: a basis whose arrays carry two different trace norms forces a rescaling per array, and the $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac14$ follow from the normalisation and from nothing else. A coefficient from this source is not a choice, and any article that reports it as one is under-claiming.

The second is a requirement and can be argued. The sign of the curvature's square is not free once the density is to have one sign; the hub shows the printed sign and the Hermiticity type are one statement. A coefficient from this source is a consequence of a stated requirement, and it must be reported with the requirement attached.

The third is a declaration and cannot be derived. It is the residue, and it is where the corpus's honest labelling matters.

The Residue, Made Explicit: The Primordial Potential

The corpus's own case, and the sentence that sets the standard. The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ scalar is the whole matrix, and the trace is the only contraction available; keeping it "adds one quadratic, two cubics and three quartics". The potential of the sector "writes four of the five quartics and sets the last to zero": $\operatorname{tr}\Xi^3\operatorname{tr}\Xi$ is the one that needs an odd power of the matrix, and the sector uses $\Xi$ only through $\Xi^2$ and $\operatorname{tr}\Xi$. Then the sentence: "That is a declared choice of coefficients and not a symmetry the group supplies; two of the four written carry the trace, and that is the honest price of a field that is not constrained."

Three things that sentence does, and the third is the one to imitate.

  • It names the omitted term. Not some quartics are omitted, but which one.
  • It gives the reason for the omission. The odd power is not built from the objects the rest of the potential uses.
  • It classifies the omission as a declaration and denies it the status of a symmetry. The group admits the term; the corpus declines it.

Why the classification is not pedantry. If the omission were reported as invariance, a reader would infer that a different potential is impossible. It is possible, and the corpus has simply not written it — which is a statement about the corpus's choice and not about the group's content, and the difference is the difference between a physics claim and an editorial one.

And the count that makes the omission visible. The sector states the arithmetic: one quadratic, two cubics, three quartics, five quartics after the trace is kept, four written. A declaration is only visible against a count, which is why this article follows the closure article in the section and not the reverse.

The Sign, and Why It Belongs to a Requirement

$T$ and $-T$ are both invariant. The filter cannot choose between them, so the sign has a source outside it. In this corpus that source is positivity: the Hamiltonian bounded below, or $e^{-S_E}$ convergent on the Euclidean section. The sign is therefore fixed by a statement about energy and not by a statement about symmetry.

The consequence for how displays are read. The hub's warning applies: the printed sign and the Hermiticity type are one statement, so a density copied without its convention line is not a weaker claim but an ambiguous one. A sign that has lost its convention is worse than a sign that has lost its derivation, because the derivation can be redone and the convention cannot be recovered from the display.

A second consequence, and it is where sign errors become count errors. The relative sign of the kinetic and potential terms is what makes the potential's minimum a vacuum rather than a maximum, and it is the same positivity requirement. So one requirement fixes two signs at two places, and an article that fixes one without the other will disagree with the sector it is read beside.

The Normalisation, and the Two Ways It Can Be Wrong

A normalisation forced by the basis is not a choice; a normalisation copied across bases is an error. The hub's statement is exact: $\operatorname{tr}(t_at_b)=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$ gives $\tfrac12$ in the element form and $\tfrac14$ in the component form, and a basis whose arrays carry two trace norms forces a rescaling per array — the fourteen at norm two, the corner at twelve, with a factor of six between the families.

The failure mode is arithmetic and not conceptual. A coefficient carried from the element form into the component form without the basis change is off by the trace norm, and the error is invisible in a display that does not print the convention. This is why the section's hub ends with a notation table rather than a summary — the table is where the conventions are fixed, and a display is read against it or not at all.

The second failure mode is a count presented as a normalisation. "A display that lists the three frame directions and the sum as four items has sixteen arrays and fifteen generators, and no normalisation can repair a count." A normalisation moves numbers; it does not move the number of objects, and confusing the two is the error the primordial article names in that sentence.

The Rule

Four clauses, and they are the article's deliverable.

  1. A forced coefficient is reported as forced, with the basis statement that forces it.
  2. A coefficient fixed by a requirement is reported with the requirement — positivity, boundedness, convergence — and not with the word natural.
  3. A declared coefficient is reported as declared, together with the count that shows it is a choice — four of five, one of two, this one and not that one.
  4. No coefficient is reported with no source at all. A number in a display without one of the three sources is an unclassified claim, and the classification is part of the claim.

Why the rule is not stylistic. The corpus's claims are meant to be separable into what follows from the algebra, what follows from a requirement, and what is chosen. A coefficient with no source breaks the separation, and a reader cannot then tell whether a disagreement with observation is a failure of the framework or of a convention. The labelling is what makes the corpus falsifiable in the part where it is a physics claim.

What This Article Does Not Say

It does not say declarations are bad. A corpus must declare: the alternative is to write every admitted term with an undetermined coefficient, which is a Lagrangian with free parameters and no predictions. Declaring is how a finite potential is stated, and the primordial sector's four-of-five is a legitimate physics choice.

It does not say invariance is weak. It says invariance is a filter, whose output is a space and not a point. A filter that leaves a five-dimensional space of quartics has done its job: it has excluded every non-invariant quartic, which is the whole of what it promises.

And it does not reopen the earlier articles. The carrier, the acceptance and the admissibility are decided; this article is about the residue they leave, and the residue is where a corpus's honesty is visible.

Summary

Every coefficient in a density comes from one of three sources: the basis (the $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac14$, forced by $\operatorname{tr}(t_at_b)=\tfrac12\delta_{ab}$), a requirement outside invariance (the sign, fixed by positivity), or a declaration (the fifth quartic set to zero, the relative weights among admitted terms). The three are not interchangeable, and invariance supplies none of them.

The corpus's own case is the primordial potential: five quartics after the trace is kept, four written, the odd-power one set to zero, and the sector's sentence — "that is a declared choice of coefficients and not a symmetry the group supplies" — is the standard this article generalises. A declaration is only visible against a count, which is why the closure article precedes this one.

The sign is fixed by positivity and not by symmetry, and since the printed sign and the Hermiticity type are one statement, a sign without its convention is ambiguous rather than merely underived. The normalisation is forced by the basis and fails by being carried across bases, and it must not be confused with a count: a normalisation moves numbers, not objects.

The rule: a forced coefficient is reported as forced, a required one with its requirement, a declared one with the count that shows the choice, and none with no source at all. The labelling is what makes the corpus's claims separable into what follows, what is required and what is chosen — and that separation is what makes the physics part falsifiable.

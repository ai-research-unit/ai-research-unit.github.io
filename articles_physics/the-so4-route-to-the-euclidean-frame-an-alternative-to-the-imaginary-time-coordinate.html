The SO(4) Route to the Euclidean Frame: An Alternative to the Imaginary Time Coordinate

Introduction

The corpus sets its frame with a complex coordinate. In the $\mathbb{B}$ frame the four coefficients of an element are $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$, the interval is

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural=\tilde Q\tilde Q^\natural=Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2 , $$

with four $+1$ coefficients, and the Lorentzian sign is not in the form at all: it appears only when a sector is imposed, and on the material sector the one substitution $Q_t=i r'_t$ puts it in the coordinate. The Euclidean form is a consequence of a complex structure, and that complex structure is the central imaginary $i$ of the algebra.

A second route reaches the same form and never introduces an $i$. It takes the real four-dimensional rotation group as fundamental: the arena is $\mathbb{R}^4$ with the positive-definite metric, the symmetry is

$$ \mathrm{SO}(4)\cong\bigl(\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\bigr)/\mathbb{Z}_2 , $$

compact, and Minkowski signature is neither assumed nor derived — it is selected, by a field that singles out one direction and breaks the group.

This article records that route. It states what the real group buys, it records the one programme that develops the route into a unification of the Standard Model with gravity, it lists claim by claim what of the corpus depends on the complex coordinate, and it sets out the work that the route would impose. That work is a translation, and the last of the thematic sections states it as such: the route is not a rival algebra, and adopting it does not mean abandoning the corpus's null cone.

The article establishes four things and imports the rest.

  • Established, and recomputed below. The commutant of a complex structure inside $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ is a $\mathrm{u}(2)$, and it is isomorphic to the material sector $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$; so the corpus's gauge group is, up to isomorphism, the group a choice of complex structure leaves unbroken; it splits as $\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ generated by the complex structure itself, and the stabiliser contains one whole $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and only a circle of the other; the space of such choices is the sphere $S^2$ of unit pure-imaginary quaternions, which is the fibre of the twistor fibration already recorded in the corpus; on two-forms the Hodge star squares to $+1$ in Euclidean signature and to $-1$ in Minkowski signature, so a real self-dual connection exists on one side and not on the other; and the external programme's generation of fermions is a sixteen-component object, which is the dimension count of one Standard-Model generation with a right-handed neutrino.
  • Imported, and left visible. The unification programme itself, its field content, its action on projective twistor space, and its identification of the Higgs with the imaginary time direction. Nothing in it is derived by the framework, and no result of it feeds back into the framework's ledger.

The two arenas are one space read in two coefficient fields. $\mathbb{R}^4$ with the positive-definite metric is the $\mathbb{B}$ frame read on real coefficients, and Frame and Metric Conventions says so in its own words: on real coefficients the form $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ "is the ordinary Euclidean metric: positive definite, with no direction singled out by a sign". That sentence is the arena of the real-group route, written before the route was. The routes therefore do not differ in the space. The $\mathbb{B}$ frame on complex coefficients is $\mathbb{C}^4$ with the complexified form, on real coefficients it is $\mathbb{R}^4$ with the Euclidean one; the complex-coordinate route reads the first and the real-group route the second, and what the second cannot describe is exactly what needs the complex coefficients, the zero divisors.

Neither device is a Wick rotation. The distinction matters because the two are easily conflated, and the corpus states the difference twice. The Wick rotation is an analytic continuation used to compute: one continues $r'_t\to-i\tau$, calculates, and continues back; the $i$ is a device of the calculation and is removed at the end. The $i r'_t$ of the corpus is not that. It is a coordinate of the material sector, its $i$ is the central imaginary of the algebra rather than a symbol of convenience, and it is never removed — The KMS Condition and the Biquaternion Framework says the imaginary time is intrinsic and "not an analytic continuation", and Hawking Radiation in Biquaternionic Form records the same fact for the period of imaginary time. The real-group route differs from both: it removes nothing because there is nothing to remove, and it pays for Minkowski space in a currency the other two do not use at all.

The corpus's own taxonomy keeps the phrase from spreading. Special Element Conventions names four restrictions of the central map $i\,\cdot$: the global phase on the centre, the duality rotation on the complex space sector, the Wick rotation on the real and imaginary halves, and the material–informational exchange on the two sectors; and Interpretation of the Wick Rotation in the Biquaternion Universe separates the exchange of the sectors, which acts on a whole element, from the continuation of the time coordinate, which relabels one. The substitution $Q_t=i r'_t$ is none of the four. It is a change of frame in the sense of Frame and Metric Conventions, with its law $Q_t=ix_0$, and the $i$ it carries is the algebra's; a display that calls it a Wick rotation is borrowing the word for the central map, and the corpus keeps the two apart by writing the substitution wherever a display passes between frames.

The contrast in one sentence each. In the corpus the Euclidean form is bought with a complex coordinate; in the real-group route it is bought with a real group, and the Lorentzian form is bought back with a field.

The same coordinate, split the other way. The Euclidean arena is also reached inside the corpus's own complex coordinate, by a reading that keeps all four coefficients complex and takes the real part of each as physical. It is not a third device: it is the complex-coordinate column of the table, with the $i$ spread over all four directions instead of placed on the time coordinate alone. Urusovskii's kinematics rests on two conjectures — universal light-like motion of all pre-elements of matter, and a periodic internal rotation of Compton radius in a second three-space orthogonal to the physical one — and Kassandrov shows that the first follows from one complex condition on the increments, $dz_\mu=dx_\mu+i\,dy_\mu$ with $\sum_\mu dz_\mu^2=0$, while the second is only permitted by one condition added to it ("one can require, say"); the common modulus of the two real parts is read as $c\,dt$. The real and imaginary parts of that condition are the two quadrics $\sum_\mu dx_\mu^2=\sum_\mu dy_\mu^2$ and $\sum_\mu dx_\mu\,dy_\mu=0$: one light speed in both halves, and their orthogonality. This picks out a set the corpus already names — $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2=0$ is $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural=0$, the zero divisors, the equal-length orthogonal case being Hamilton's geometrically unreal tangents to a sphere, recorded in A Brief History of Biquaternions in Physics — and the $2\times2$ image drops to rank one on it, verified here. The six dimensions the paper counts are those of this cone, and its halves are the half split, $\mathbb{B}_{H}$ of form $(4,0)$ and $\mathbb{B}_{iH}$ of form $(0,4)$, not the sector split; the paper's positive squared length on the second is minus that form, which is the sign a reader carries wrong when the two frames are mixed. That the cone's directions are the orthonormal pairs in $\mathbb{R}^4$ is recorded in The Algebrodynamical Programme from arXiv:gr-qc/0602064. What the paper adds is the kinematic reading of the second half and the priority, not the geometry.

Where the paper's time sits. The identification of the two halves with the sectors does not follow from the paper and is not one it draws. It is forced once the corpus's coordinates are used: the real half shares with $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ the three real space directions and with $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ the real time direction alone, while the imaginary half shares with $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ the material time direction alone and with $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ the three informational space directions. So the paper's physical time is the corpus's informational time $r_t$, and the material time $i r'_t$, being purely imaginary, has no component along it at all. That is what lets the paper say that the reduction of the coordinate space to real Minkowski space is made "rather artificially": in its reading the physical half is the Euclidean one and the Lorentzian sector is the derived one. The paper states its own status plainly — the kinematics "does not follow directly from the fundamental algebraic dynamics", but only from the complex geometry of the null cone, and it remains a hypothesis, though one the twistor structure of the primary field is expected to narrow. One difference of status should be named with it: in the paper the internal space is a space in which matter literally circulates at the speed of light, whereas the corpus's counterpart is informational and is not claimed as extra space, which is the refusal recorded in The Empirical Status of the Biquaternion Framework and already applied to the source's twistor paper in The Algebrodynamical Programme.

What the Real Group Buys

The real-group route is worth recording because what it supplies, it supplies structurally rather than by construction — and the corpus records the corresponding gaps in its own words, in the quotations below.

Chirality comes from the product, not from the module

The spinor module of $\mathrm{Spin}(4)=\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the sum of the two half-spinor modules,

$$ \Delta=\bigl(\tfrac12,0\bigr)\oplus\bigl(0,\tfrac12\bigr), \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}=2+2=4 , $$

and each factor of the group acts on one summand and not on the other. Chirality is therefore not a property imposed on a module that does not have it; it is what a product group does to its own spinor module. The corpus has the same two-sidedness in its basis operators, $L_aR_b$, left multiplication and right multiplication, and The Gauge Group Ceiling already notes that the norm-preserving rotors form $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R/\mathbb{Z}_2=\mathrm{SO}(4)$ with the diagonal as the gauge group and the axial combination as the chirality rotation. What the corpus does not do is put a connection on each factor separately, and that is the step the route takes.

A chiral gauge coupling is available

Gauging one factor gives a connection that acts on one chirality and not on the other. This is precisely the object the electroweak agenda names as what would settle its central obstacle: "the object is a chiral non-abelian matter representation". The corpus's own non-abelian action is left multiplication, which is vector-like on the defining module, and Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation records "a chiral gauge group, and the spectrum it would constrain" as not supplied. On the real-group route the chiral coupling is not an addition; it is a choice of which factor to gauge.

The same structure is what Left-Right Symmetric Fermions from Complex Split Biquaternions and Bioctonions records from the algebra side, at the price of enlarging the carrier to $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$. The real-group route reaches the same pattern without enlarging anything, which is the whole of its advantage.

Self-duality is a real condition

On two-forms the Hodge star satisfies $\star^2=+1$ in Euclidean signature, so $\Lambda^2$ splits into two real three-dimensional eigenspaces,

$$ \Lambda^2=\Lambda^2_+\oplus\Lambda^2_- , \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^2_\pm=3 , $$

and a real self-dual connection is an object that exists. In Minkowski signature $\star^2=-1$, the eigenvalues are $\pm i$, and there is no real self-dual two-form: the split is a split of the complexified space. Recomputed on the six-dimensional space of two-forms in both signatures, with the epsilon contraction and the metric inverse, the star has eigenvalues $\{+1,+1,+1,-1,-1,-1\}$ in Euclidean signature and $\{+i,+i,+i,-i,-i,-i\}$ in Minkowski signature. The Self-Dual and Anti-Self-Dual Split treats the complexified case, which is the one the corpus's complex field strength needs; a chiral connection needs the real case, and only the Euclidean signature has it.

This is why a chiral formulation of general relativity lives on the real-group side: the self-dual half of the connection is a real object there, and it carries one of the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factors.

Both factors are compact

The group is compact, so a gauge theory on it is the ordinary compact one: no reality condition to impose, no indefinite form on the field space, unitary finite-dimensional representations throughout. The corpus reaches the same conclusion by a different route — $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ is exactly the anti-Hermitian part of $\mathbb{B}$, and exponentiating it gives the $*$-unitary group — but it reaches it for a single group, not for a product of two.

The Sphere of Complex Structures

Here the two routes touch, and the point of contact is exact.

The objects. A complex structure on $\mathbb{R}^4$ compatible with the metric is an endomorphism $J$ with

$$ J^2=-I_4,\qquad J^{T}J=I_4,\qquad \det J=+1 . $$

For every unit pure-imaginary quaternion $\vec u$, the left multiplication $L_{\vec u}$ is such a $J$. Recomputed on a hundred random unit $\vec u$: $J^{T}J-I_4$, $J^2+I_4$ and $\det J-1$ all vanish to $6.7\times10^{-16}$, and $L_{\vec u}(e_t)=\vec u$, so distinct $\vec u$ give distinct $J$ — an identity, not a numerical observation. The map is therefore an injection of a two-sphere into the space of compatible complex structures, which is $\mathrm{SO}(4)/\mathrm{U}(2)$, of dimension $6-4=2$; a continuous injection from a compact space into a Hausdorff space of the same dimension is onto. So the sphere below is not a subset of the space of complex structures; it is that space.

The space of them is a sphere. The unit pure-imaginary quaternions are

$$ S^2=\bigl\{\vec u\in\mathbb{B}_{H} : \vec u^2=-e_t\bigr\} , $$

and the condition is the unit one, not an extra: a pure element is anti-fixed by the natural conjugation, $\vec u^{\natural}=-\vec u$, so $\vec u\vec u^{\natural}=-\vec u^2=\langle\vec u\rangle_{\natural}e_t$, and $\vec u^2=-e_t$ says exactly that $\vec u\vec u^{\natural}=e_t$, which is the corpus's spelling of unit norm. No modulus is needed and none is written: the bars $\lvert\cdot\rvert$ are the complex modulus of a coefficient, and the glyph $\vec u$ is already the corpus's unit pure element.

This is the sphere of The Hopf Fibration and the Biquaternion Gauge Bundle, where it appears as the Bloch sphere and as the base of the Hopf map. So the sphere of directions indexes the complex structures on the algebra's own quaternionic line, through $\vec u\mapsto L_{\vec u}$, and it is the corpus's sphere under this new use.

The commutant is a $\mathrm{u}(2)$, and one factor of it is whole. The endomorphisms of the frame that are antisymmetric and commute with $J$ form a Lie algebra. Recomputed with $J$ the left multiplication by a random unit pure-imaginary element $\vec u$: the solution space of $[A,J]=0$ inside $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ has dimension $4$, the same for twenty random $J$, and it is exactly

$$ \bigl\{A\in\mathfrak{so}(4) : [A,J]=0\bigr\}=\mathbb{R}J\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_R , $$

with the four matrices $J,R_{\vec e_x},R_{\vec e_y},R_{\vec e_z}$ spanning it to $3.3\times10^{-16}$ — the three right multiplications are individually in it to $6.0\times10^{-16}$ — and the two directions of the left factor orthogonal to $J$ not in it at all: each leaves a residual of $0.97$ and $1.00$ out of a norm of $2$, a ratio of about $0.49$ in both cases, which is a genuine failure of membership and not a rounding effect.

So one whole factor survives and the other contributes a single circle,

$$ \mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SO}(4)}(J)=\mathrm{U}(2)=\frac{\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)}{\mathbb{Z}_2}, \qquad \mathrm{U}(1)=\bigl\{e^{tJ}\bigr\}\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)_L , $$

and the asymmetry is not an accident of the calculation: it is what choosing a complex structure means. Which factor is called left and which right is fixed by which multiplication one calls the left one, and the source names them the other way round from this article. No content turns on the naming — the electroweak pattern is the same either way, a whole $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ for weak isospin and hypercharge a circle of the other factor — and this is why the identification below is recorded as a rephrasing rather than as a transported result.

What this says. The corpus's compact gauge group is, up to isomorphism, the group that a choice of complex structure leaves unbroken in the rotation group of the frame. The corpus presents $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathrm{U}(2)$ as the algebra's intrinsic anti-Hermitian part, which it is; the same group is also the stabiliser of a complex structure.

What the corpus does and does not already say. The distinction is worth stating, because The Gauge Group Ceiling has a bullet headed Stabilisers, and it is a different statement. There the object stabilised is $\vec u$ inside $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and the stabiliser is a maximal torus, the unbroken $\mathrm{U}(1)$ of an adjoint-scalar breaking. Here the object stabilised is the complex structure $J$ inside $\mathrm{SO}(4)$, and the stabiliser is three dimensions larger, a $\mathrm{U}(2)$. The two are not in conflict, and the ceiling's circle sits inside this one. Its action is the adjoint one, which on the frame is conjugation by $e^{t\vec u}$, that is $L_{a}R_{a^{-1}}$ with $a=e^{t\vec u}$; recomputed, that matrix is orthogonal of determinant $+1$, it commutes with $J$ to $2.2\times10^{-16}$, and its generator is $L_{\vec u}-R_{\vec u}$, the axial combination — the same one the ceiling names a paragraph later, where it says the axial combination "is the chirality rotation of Chiral Fermions in the Biquaternion Framework, not a gauge group". So the ceiling's $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is the axial circle, and it is not the circle $e^{tJ}$ of this article: the two coincide only at $t=0$, and at $t=0.3$ the matrices already differ by $0.25$ in norm. The direction $\vec u$ leaves one dimension unbroken in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and four in $\mathrm{SO}(4)$, and the extra three are what turn a chirality rotation into the gauge group.

The material sector is that algebra. $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ splits as a Lie algebra into

$$ \mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2), \qquad \mathfrak{u}(1)=\mathbb{R}\,(ie_t),\quad \mathfrak{su}(2)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\} , $$

and the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ is the complex structure itself: the central imaginary is what $J$ becomes in the algebra. The corpus states the same split, in its own notation and at group level, as $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathrm{U}(2)=\mathrm{SU}(2)\oplus\mathrm{U}(1)$ in The Gauge Group Ceiling; written as Lie algebras it is the display above, and the two differ only in whether the factor is named by its group or by its algebra. The corpus's $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ acts on the frame by left multiplication, and the commutant's full factor is the right one, so the two sit on opposite sides of the two-sided action $L_aR_b$. That is not a defect of either account; it is the same ambiguity the corpus's own naming carries, and it is why the ledger below records this identification as a rephrasing rather than as a transported result.

The external programme starts from exactly this. Woit's construction of the electroweak group is the sentence "the $\mathrm{U}(2)\subset\mathrm{SO}(4)$ subgroup picked out by the choice of $J$ has a double cover $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)\subset\mathrm{Spin}(4)$, with the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ identified as $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ a subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$". That is the group above. So the point of contact between the routes is not an analogy: the corpus's material sector is the group the programme gauges, and the difference is only that the corpus fixes $J$ by the coordinate $Q_t=i r'_t$ while the programme makes the choice of $J$ a field.

That single identification is the whole difference between the routes, in the only place where they can be compared without ambiguity:

The External Construction: Euclidean Twistor Unification

The programme is P. Woit, "Euclidean Twistor Unification" (arXiv:2104.05099). It is recorded here as an external construction, in the manner of Matter Makes Space: nothing in it is derived by the framework, and nothing in the framework is evidence for it.

The choice of signature. Euclidean signature is taken as fundamental. The reason given is the state space: a Euclidean quantum field theory determines its Hilbert space only after an imaginary time direction is chosen, which is the direction used for the Osterwalder–Schrader reflection. If Euclidean signature is the physics, that direction is a degree of freedom of the theory and not a calculational choice.

The two factors, gauged differently. On $\mathbb{R}^4$ the rotation group is $\mathrm{Spin}(4)=\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$. The programme gauges them into two different things:

  • the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ connection is the chiral spin connection, with the vierbein as a $\mathbb{R}^4$-valued one-form, and Einstein's equations are written on that pair;
  • the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ connection is the weak interaction;
  • the field that picks the imaginary time direction is the imaginary-time component of the canonical $\mathbb{R}^4$-valued one-form $\theta$ — that is, one of the four components of the vierbein — and it is identified with the Higgs.

Why twistor space is needed, in the source's own account. The step above does not work as it stands, and the source says why. There are two problems. First, the electroweak group has an extra $\mathrm{U}(1)$, not just $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$. Second, and worse, "as a 1-form, the Higgs field transforms non-trivially under both $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, but we want it to be invariant under $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$". A component of $\theta$ cannot be a Higgs doublet while $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is a gauge symmetry, because it is charged under $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ by construction.

The resolution is to lift to projective twistor space, where the two problems solve each other. A point of $\mathbb{PT}$ is a complex line $l\in\mathbb{C}^4$ together with the complex structure it defines; the unit vectors in $l$ give a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ bundle over $\mathbb{PT}$, which is the missing abelian factor; and tangent vectors to $S^4$ lifted to $\mathbb{PT}$ lie in $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(l,S_L)$, on which only that $\mathrm{U}(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ act. A lifted imaginary-time vector therefore transforms as a Higgs doublet and is $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$-invariant, which is exactly what a component of $\theta$ could not be.

The unbroken group is again a stabiliser. The source's account of the electroweak breaking is this: "the Higgs picks out a specific $\mathrm{U}(1)$ subgroup of the $\mathrm{U}(2)$ group, the subgroup that leaves the field vector invariant", and the gauge theory of that $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is electromagnetism. So both steps of the programme are the same move. The complex structure is a chosen object, and $\mathrm{U}(2)$ is its stabiliser in $\mathrm{SO}(4)$; the imaginary-time vector is a chosen object, and the electromagnetic $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is its stabiliser in that $\mathrm{U}(2)$. Two nested choices of a direction, each leaving a group behind. The corpus makes the first choice once and globally by the coordinate $Q_t=i r'_t$ and never makes the second, which is why the corpus has a $\mathrm{U}(2)$ and no electromagnetism.

This is the division the corpus declines to make. The corpus writes the same two factors, with the diagonal as the gauge group and the axial combination as a chirality rotation; the programme writes the same two factors with each one gauged, one of them as gravity.

Projective twistor space, and the internal groups. Four-dimensional Euclidean space compactifies to $S^4=\mathbb{HP}^1$, and projective twistor space fibres over it,

$$ S^2=\mathbb{CP}^1\;\hookrightarrow\;\mathbb{PT}=\mathbb{CP}^3\;\longrightarrow\;S^4=\mathbb{HP}^1 , $$

with fibre the space of complex structures at the point — which is the sphere of the preceding section. A point of $\mathbb{PT}$ is a complex line $l\subset\mathbb{C}^4$, and because

$$ \mathbb{PT}=\frac{\mathrm{SU}(4)}{\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)} , $$

each point carries those two internal groups. The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ acts on the quotient $l^\perp$ and is colour; the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is the one the resolution above has just used. Because the fibre is the sphere of complex structures, the sphere of the preceding section is the internal space of the lift, and the point of $\mathbb{PT}$ is exactly a choice of $J$.

The generation. One generation of fermions is the space of linear maps

$$ \mathrm{Hom}\bigl(l\oplus l^\perp,\ S_R\oplus S_L\bigr) =\bigl(l\oplus l^\perp\bigr)^{*}\otimes\bigl(S_L\oplus S_R\bigr) , $$

with $S_L=\mathbb{C}^2_0$ a doublet of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ of weight $0$, and $S_R=\mathbb{C}_{-1}\oplus\mathbb{C}_{+1}$ the two singlets of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ carrying the internal $\mathrm{U}(1)$ weights shown. The point of the construction is that no large group is used: there is no $\mathrm{SU}(5)$, no $\mathrm{SO}(10)$, no $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$; the internal groups are read off the geometry of a point of $\mathbb{PT}$.

What the programme does not do, in its own words. No full theory on $\mathbb{PT}$ is constructed. The dynamics are the Standard Model's own: the source writes that the fields "can be given exactly the dynamics of the Standard Model, using the usual Yang–Mills action and the standard Higgs field kinetic term and potential", so the Higgs potential is taken over, not derived. The origin of the number of generations is not addressed — the source says "it is unclear what the origin of generations might be", the construction gives one, and the three of the world are unexplained. The candidate extension that might supply them, a theory on $S^7$, is offered as speculation. And the state space construction, through cohomology on the upper half of $\mathbb{PT}$, is stated as a direction rather than a result.

The hypercharge, and the arrangement the corpus already uses. The corpus's scalar sector has written down the same electroweak assignment: Custodial Symmetry and the Rho Parameter records that "the left action is the full $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and the right action is an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ whose $T_3^R$ is the hypercharge". That is the programme's $\mathrm{U}(1)\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, named there in the corpus's own terms. What differs is what fixes it. The corpus's $T_3^R$ is the same element everywhere; in the programme the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is a function of the point of $\mathbb{PT}$, so that choosing the hypercharge direction, the complex structure and the imaginary time direction are one and the same choice. The corpus has the right arrangement with the choice already made and frozen; the programme makes the choice dynamical. That is the difference the translation has to handle.

The Generation, Recomputed

The dimension count is the part of the programme that can be checked without its dynamics, and it is the sharpest evidence that the geometric reading is not empty.

The two factors have complex dimensions $4$ and $4$, so

$$ \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Bigl[\bigl(l\oplus l^\perp\bigr)^{*}\otimes\bigl(S_L\oplus S_R\bigr)\Bigr]=4\times 4=16 , $$

Recomputed: the six dimensions sum to $16$, and the eight charges above are exactly the eight of one generation. One discrepancy in the source is worth recording rather than passing over. The source prints the two triplet singlets with the weights $\tfrac23$ and $-\tfrac43$; they are the negatives of the pair that follows from its own lepton weights, since with the lepton doublet fixed at $Y=-1$ and the traceless condition fixing the ratio, the singlets must be $\tfrac43$ and $-\tfrac23$. The two printed values are consistent with each other and with nothing else on the line, so a sign has slipped between them and the leptons. With the sign fixed once, at the leptons, $Q=T_3+\tfrac12Y$ gives $u_R$ at $\tfrac23$ and $d_R$ at $-\tfrac13$. The content is unaffected — the two are the two members of one $S_R$ doublet either way — and the correction is recorded because the corpus's rule is to show the arithmetic, not to reproduce it.

Two limits belong beside the table. The construction gives one generation and has no mechanism for three: the count is a dimension of a tensor product, and no integer in the geometry selects $3$. And it gives no mass and no mixing angle, because the masses come from Yukawa couplings, which the construction does not contain. What it does give is the pattern — that the fermions of one generation are the linear maps between two bundles at a point — and the pattern is exact.

What the Route Cannot Carry

A route is defined as much by what it cannot carry as by what it can, and here the loss is precise.

There are no null directions. On $\mathbb{R}^4$ with the positive-definite metric, $\sum_\mu x_\mu^2=0$ has the single solution $x=0$. The real quaternions multiply with multiplicative norm, recomputed on a hundred random pairs to $3\times10^{-14}$ or better, so they are a division algebra and have no zero divisors. Every null object of the corpus lives in the complexification: the element

$$ \tilde Q=i e_t+\vec e_x , \qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural=i^2+1=0 , $$

has vanishing norm, and its conjugates are the zero divisors on which the light cone, the celestial sphere, the complex eikonal, the shear-free congruences and the whole algebrodynamical programme are built.

The consequence. The real-group route covers the gauge and gravity half of the corpus — the part that concerns connections, chirality, self-duality and compact groups — and it cannot carry the null half — the part that concerns the cone, the zero divisors, the caustics and the particles as singular loci. It is a lens on part of the framework, not a rival universe, and any adoption of it has to be an adoption of that part.

This is the reason the corpus's complex coordinate is not mere convention, and it is the strongest argument the corpus has against replacing it. The reply available to the real-group route is that the null structure of the complexified algebra survives untouched, because complexification is a separate operation from the choice of real slice; that reply is correct, and it is also the reason the translation of the next section is possible at all.

The Translation, Which Is the Work

If the route is useful, then what it establishes has to be carried back into the framework, and that carrying is a definite task with a definite criterion. Two rules make it a translation rather than a borrowing.

Rule one: every translated statement must be written with elements of $\mathbb{B}$ alone. A statement that needs a bundle over a base space, or a section of one, is not translated — it is imported, and it must be labelled as imported. This is the rule the corpus already applies when it records an external construction.

Rule two: no translation may introduce a dependence on a point of the $S^2$. The corpus's complex structure is central and the substitution is global: one $J$, the same at every point of space and at every time. A translated statement that needs a direction, or that needs a choice among the complex structures, has not been translated — it has been transferred, with its cost carried openly.

The table says where the work is. Four rows are done, one is carried, and three are open. The open ones are the interesting ones, and each has the same shape: the real-group route supplies an object whose biquaternion counterpart would have to be a non-central element, and every non-central element carries a direction.

The Higgs row is the sharpest case, and the corpus's own scalar article supplies the comparison. The framework's scalar is the complex central field $\tilde{Ξ}=\xi e_t$, and The Higgs Mechanism as an Erasure of Information records that "no point is preferred by the action", the vacuum being a degenerate circle because the central phase is not observable. On the real-group route the vacuum is a point of the sphere of complex structures — the same $S^2$ whose stabiliser the material sector is. So the two scalar sectors are not two attempts at one object. The corpus's scalar has no direction because a direction cannot be written in it; the programme's is a direction, and that is why it breaks a group. Which of the two the world realises is a question the corpus cannot even pose, and this is the sharpest thing the route offers it.

The proposal, labelled as a proposal. The corpus need not choose between the routes. The two devices are compatible if the product group is imported as an internal structure while the frame keeps its complex coordinate: the four coefficients stay complex, $Q_t=i r'_t$ stays, the null cone stays where it is, and the basis operators $L_aR_b$ — which the corpus already has, and which already separate a left action from a right one — are gauged separately as an internal symmetry. Chirality would then come from the internal factor rather than from the frame's group, and the corpus would gain the chiral coupling without losing the zero divisors.

The proposal also says exactly what is missing, in the corpus's own terms. The material sector already contains the group: $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$, with the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ the gauge group and the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ the complex structure. What the corpus does not do is act on the two chiralities independently. Its gauge field is left multiplication, which is vector-like on the defining module; the ceiling records the axial combination $L_aR_{a^{-1}}$ as "the chirality rotation of Chiral Fermions in the Biquaternion Framework, not a gauge group". So the translation is one step and it is localised: give the second side a connection, and let the two sides be varied separately. That is the step that costs the vector-like property, and it is why the step is not free.

Whether that works is not settled here, and the corpus has a reason to be careful: the ceiling article's result is that a gauge group drawn from the algebra acts by left multiplication and is therefore vector-like on the defining module. An internal product group acting as $L_aR_b$ is not left multiplication alone, so the obstruction does not apply to it as stated — but the anomaly conditions, the mass selection rule and the charge operator would all have to be redone for it, and none of that is done here. The proposal is recorded because it is the natural form of the translation, not because its outcome is known.

What Would Decide Between the Routes

Three tests, in increasing order of difficulty.

  1. A null structure in the real-group frame. If the corpus's particle programme can be restated without the zero-divisor cone — if the caustic, the charge and the congruence have a description in terms of a direction field on $\mathbb{R}^4$ — then the null half is not lost and the routes are closer than the ledger says. No such description is recorded in the corpus, and this article does not supply one.
  2. A chiral coupling from the basis operators. If the left and right factors can be gauged separately inside $\mathbb{B}$ while the frame keeps $i r'_t$, the corpus gains the electroweak object without paying the null cone. This is the proposal above, and the test is a computation: the covariant derivative, the curvature, the anomaly coefficient and the charge operator for a field transforming as $L_aR_b$. It is a finite computation and it has not been done.
  3. A prediction that separates them. The routes agree on every kinematic statement, because they describe the same signature. They could differ dynamically, in the weight of a vacuum-energy contribution or in the spectrum of a thermal theory, since one has a preferred direction built in and the other does not. No such computation exists in either programme.

Until one of the three is settled, the routes are two descriptions of one frame with different books, and the corpus's own book is the one with the null cone in it.

Summary

The Euclidean form of the biquaternion frame can be obtained in two ways that agree on exactly two things — a two-sphere, and the $\mathrm{u}(2)$ that one of its points stabilises — and on nothing else: with a complex coordinate, $Q_t=i r'_t$, where the $i$ is the algebra's centre and the Lorentzian sign is $i^2$; or with a real group, $\mathrm{SO}(4)$, where no $i$ appears and the Lorentzian sign is produced by breaking the group with a field. Neither is a Wick rotation, and the corpus says so about its own device in two articles.

The real-group route buys four things structurally: chirality from the product $\mathrm{Spin}(4)=\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$, a chiral gauge coupling from gauging one factor, a real self-dual two-form because $\star^2=+1$ in Euclidean signature, and compactness of both factors. It pays for Minkowski space with a field and its potential.

The two routes touch at one exact point. The commutant of a complex structure in the rotation algebra is a $\mathrm{u}(2)$,

$$ \bigl\{A\in\mathfrak{so}(4):[A,J]=0\bigr\}=\mathbb{R}J\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_R , \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}=4 , $$

verified for twenty random complex structures, and it contains one whole $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and only a circle of the other. The corpus's material sector is a $\mathrm{u}(2)$ too, $\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ generated by the central imaginary, and the two correspond generator by generator.

The same move is then made a second time inside that group. The programme's electroweak breaking is the statement that the Higgs "picks out a specific $\mathrm{U}(1)$ subgroup of the $\mathrm{U}(2)$ group, the subgroup that leaves the field vector invariant", so the unbroken electromagnetic group is the stabiliser of the imaginary time direction, just as $\mathrm{U}(2)$ was the stabiliser of the complex structure. Two nested choices of a direction, each leaving a group behind. The corpus makes the first once and globally, through $Q_t=i r'_t$, and never makes the second — which is why it has a $\mathrm{U}(2)$ and no electromagnetism.

The external programme that develops the route — Euclidean signature as fundamental, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ gauged as a chiral spin connection, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ as weak isospin, the imaginary time direction as the Higgs, and the internal $\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$ of projective twistor space as hypercharge and colour — reproduces one generation of fermions as a sixteen-component object, recomputed here, and gives no number of generations and no masses. It carries the gauge and gravity half of the corpus and cannot carry the null half: on $\mathbb{R}^4$ the norm is positive definite and there are no zero divisors, while in $\mathbb{B}$ the element $ie_t+\vec e_x$ has vanishing norm and every null object of the corpus is built on its conjugates.

The work the route imposes is a translation, under two rules: every translated statement must be written with elements of $\mathbb{B}$ alone, and no translation may make a statement depend on a choice among the complex structures. Four rows of the dictionary are already established in the corpus, one is carried, and three are open — the chiral spin connection, the Higgs as a direction, and the internal colour. The natural form of the translation is to import the product group as an internal structure on the basis operators $L_aR_b$, keeping the complex coordinate and the null cone; that is recorded here as a proposal, with its anomaly, mass and charge computations named as undone.

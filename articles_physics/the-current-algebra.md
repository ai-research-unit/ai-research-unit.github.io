# __The Current Algebra__

## Introduction

**The word has four factors, the algebra has three independent bilinears, and the whole observable content of the fluid is that mismatch.** The bilinears of the word are the current, the spin and the quaternionic norm. The current is $\rho\tilde V_{\Phi}$ and it reads the scale and the boost. The spin is $\rho\frac{\hbar}{2}i\hat s$ and it reads the scale and the rotor. The norm is $\Rho^{2}$ and it reads the scale and the phase. Each reads two of the four factors, none reads three, and between them they read all four exactly once except for the scale, which appears in all three.

**The phase and the spin therefore never meet in one bilinear.** That sentence is the structural fact of this article. A current cannot carry spin information and a spin cannot carry phase information, not because the field is arranged that way but because the two factors are of types that no single quadratic product can reach at once. The algebra's own candidate for a current, $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$, does not repair this: it cancels the rotor and keeps the boost, so it is a second reading of the same two factors with the spatial part reversed.

**Four objects, one duplicate.** The fourth bilinear of the word is the algebra's product with its own conjugate, and it re-reads the scale and the boost. So the count of independent readings is three, and the count of bilinears is four, and the difference is the object this article is about.

**What is established and what is read.** The invariance statements are propositions: each is proved from the structure of the four factors. The identification of the current with the fluid's current and of the spin with the fluid's spin is a reading, taken in the two case articles and not proved here. The article separates the two at every step and collects them in the closing section.

## The Three Bilinears

**The word is $\tilde\Xi=r\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ with $r>0$ real, the phase central, the boost Hermitian positive of unit natural norm and the rotor real unitary.** [The Relativistic Quantum Space as a Fluid](the-relativistic-quantum-space-as-a-fluid.html) states the word and its eight parameters; this article takes it and asks what a quadratic product of the field can see.

**A bilinear is a product of the element with a conjugate of itself.** There are two conjugations in use. The natural conjugation $\natural$ inverts a unit and leaves the central $i$ alone. The star conjugation $*$ is $\natural$ composed with coefficient conjugation, so it fixes a Hermitian factor, conjugates the central $i$ and inverts a real rotor. The two are the corpus's pair, and their difference is what makes two bilinears out of one product.

*Proposition.* With the word as above, and with $\rho=r^{2}$ and $\gamma=\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$,

$$
\tilde J_{\Phi}=\rho\tilde V_{\Phi},
\qquad
\tilde V_{\Phi}=ic\,\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2},
\qquad
\tilde S_{\Psi,\mathrm{sp}}=\rho\,\frac{\hbar}{2}\,i\,\hat s,
\qquad
\hat s=\tilde u_{\mathrm r}\,\vec e_z\,\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{\natural},
\qquad
\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\natural}=\rho\,e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}.
$$

*Proof.* The rotor and the boost are units, so each cancels against its own inverse in the natural product, and the central part squares: $\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\natural}=r^{2}e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$. The current is the density times the boost's inverse square by the polar article's proposition, and the spin is the density times the rotor's image of the declared axis, likewise. Both are quoted there and neither is re-derived here.

**The three displays are three different extractions of one product, and the difference is which factor survives.** In the norm the two frames cancel and the centre survives. In the current the rotor and the phase cancel and the boost survives. In the spin the boost and the phase cancel and the rotor survives. Each extraction kills exactly the two factors it does not read, and the killing is by a conjugation that inverts them.

### The Current Reads the Scale and the Boost

**The current is invariant under the phase and under the rotor, and the invariance is exact.** Shifting $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ by any angle multiplies the element by a central phase and leaves $\tilde J_{\Phi}$ unchanged, because the current is built from the density and the frame and neither contains a central factor. Replacing the rotor by any other rotor leaves it unchanged for the same reason.

**Checked on a generic element.** At $r=1.3$, $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}=0.4$, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}=0.9$, $\vec u_{\mathrm b}=\vec e_x$, $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}=1.2$, $\vec u_{\mathrm r}=\vec e_y$ and $c=1$, the current is $\tilde J_{\Phi}=2.42192\,i\,e_t+1.73481\,\vec e_x$. A shift of the phase by $0.77$ changes every component by $0$, and a change of the rotor angle by $0.5$ changes every component by $0$. The two zeros are exact and not small.

**The current's own norm is fixed by the density alone.** From $\tilde V_{\Phi}\tilde V_{\Phi}^{\natural}=i^{2}c^{2}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{2}=-c^{2}$,

$$
\langle\tilde J_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}\rho^{2},
$$

so the current is a timelike element of fixed square and the density is its modulus. On the same element the display gives $-2.8561$ against $\rho^{2}=2.8561$. **The current carries four of the eight parameters and its length carries one of those four**, which is why a fluid can be described by a density and a direction and why the other four parameters need another bilinear.

### The Spin Reads the Scale and the Rotor

**The spin is invariant under the phase and under the boost.** The spin direction $\hat s=\tilde u_{\mathrm r}\vec e_z\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{\natural}$ is built from the rotor alone, so no boost enters it; and the density carries no central factor, so no phase enters it. On the generic element above, $\hat s=0.932039\,\vec e_x+0.362358\,\vec e_z$ with $\hat s\cdot\hat s=1$ and $\hat s^{2}=-e_t$, and neither number moves when the phase or the rapidity is changed.

**The spin direction is a unit vector and the element that carries it is Hermitian.** The product $\rho\frac{\hbar}{2}i\hat s$ has a real scalar part only if $\hat s$ has none, which it does not, and its three vector coefficients are purely imaginary, so the spin element lies in the informational sector. That is the same placement as the energy–flux element of [The Quantum Stress Tensor of the Biquaternion Scalar Field](the-quantum-stress-tensor-of-the-biquaternion-scalar-field.html), and it is forced by the single $i$ in the definition.

**Two of the rotor's three parameters reach the spin and one does not.** The rotor is an angle and an axis. The spin direction is the image of the declared axis, so it is a unit vector with two parameters; the third, the rotation about the spin direction itself, leaves $\hat s$ fixed. The rotation about the spin's own axis is the one motion the spin bilinear cannot see, and it is the same motion that the torque of [Spin Conservation and the Frenkel Condition](spin-conservation-and-the-frenkel-condition.html) leaves sourceless.

### The Norm Reads the Scale and the Phase

**The norm is invariant under both frames and it is the only bilinear that sees the phase.** The boost and the rotor are units of natural norm one, so they cancel; what remains is $r^{2}e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$.

**The phase enters doubled, and the range of the phase is a consequence.** The field carries $e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$ and the norm carries $e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$. Two consequences follow and both are used below: the modulus $\rho$ is unchanged by a shift of the phase through a half turn, and the norm's argument traverses a full turn while the phase traverses a half turn. The range $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}\in(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2}]$ that the polar articles use is not a convention chosen for convenience: it is the range that makes the map from the phase to the norm's argument a bijection.

**The phase is a gauge angle and the norm is its only trace.** A shift of the phase that is constant in space and time changes the field by a central phase and changes no bilinear of this article. The norm's argument is the one place the shift is visible, and it is invisible there too once the shift is a half turn. That is the sense in which the phase is a coordinate and not an observable, and it is developed for the scalar case in [The Nodal Set and Quantized Circulation](the-nodal-set-and-quantized-circulation.html).

## The Mirror Bilinear

**The algebra's own candidate for a current is $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$, and it is a second reading of the first two factors.** The star conjugation fixes the boost and inverts the rotor, so the product keeps the boost squared and cancels the rotor. The factor $i$ then rotates the result by a quarter turn in the central direction.

*Proposition.* With the word as above,

$$
i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=i\rho\bigl(\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,e_t+i\sinh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,\vec u_{\mathrm b}\bigr)
=\rho\bigl(i\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,e_t-\sinh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,\vec u_{\mathrm b}\bigr).
$$

*Proof.* By the norm proposition of [The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables](the-dirac-equation-in-biquaternionic-polar-variables.html), $\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=\rho\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{2}$, and squaring the boost gives $\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}e_t+i\sinh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$. Multiplication by the central $i$ distributes over the two slots.

**The temporal slots agree and the spatial parts are opposite.** Comparing the two displays term by term, the temporal slot of $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$ is $i\rho\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ and the temporal slot of $\tilde J_{\Phi}$ is $ic\gamma\rho$; they agree up to the speed $c$. The spatial slot of the first is $-\rho\sinh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ and of the second $+c\rho\sinh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$; they are opposite.

**Checked on an element with its boost along $\vec e_x$.** With $r=1.3$, $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}=0$, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}=0.89997$ and a trivial rotor, the display gives $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=2.42192\,i\,e_t-1.73481\,\vec e_x$ with vanishing $y$ and $z$ slots, and the current gives $2.42192\,i\,c\,e_t+1.73481\,c\,\vec e_x$ with the same two slots empty. The temporal entries are in the ratio $+1/c$ and the $x$ entries in the ratio $-1/c$; at $c=1$ the ratios are $+1$ and $-1$. The reversal is exact, and the agreement is in the slots where both objects are non-zero — a ratio taken in a slot where both vanish says nothing.

**Why the sign reverses, in one line.** The star conjugation inverts the rotor and fixes the boost, so the bilinear carries the velocity twice and the spin not at all; the outer $i$ then reverses the spatial part, because a real spatial vector times $i$ is imaginary while the temporal slot is already imaginary.

**Which of the two is conserved is open, and the corpus says so.** [The Minimal Coupling of the Biquaternion Dirac Field to Electromagnetism](the-minimal-coupling-of-the-biquaternion-dirac-field-to-electromagnetism.html) exhibits the non-conservation of $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$ on an exact solution. The word says why the object is the wrong shape: a current is a frame and the algebra's product cancels the frame into its square, so the bilinear's spatial part runs opposite to the motion. The conserved current of the standard theory is $\rho\tilde V_{\Phi}$, and the word exhibits it as the density times a frame factor rather than as a product of the field with itself.

**The lesson is not that the algebra lacks a current.** It is that a bilinear of an element with its own conjugate cannot produce a four-vector of the algebra's own type, because the two factors multiply to a scalar and a frame rather than to a direction. The direction has to be supplied, and the word supplies it through the boost.

## The Doubling of the Rate

**Every bilinear runs at twice the field's phase, and the factor of two is where the interference frequency comes from.** The field carries $e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$ and the norm carries $e^{2i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$, so the norm's argument advances twice as fast as the field's.

*Proposition.* Let $\tilde\Xi_{1}$ and $\tilde\Xi_{2}$ be two central branches with phases $\theta_{1}=\omega_{1}t$ and $\theta_{2}=\omega_{2}t$ and equal amplitudes. Then the density of the sum is $2+2\cos((\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t)$ and the argument of its norm is $(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})t$.

*Proof.* With $\tilde\Xi_{1}=a e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}}$ and $\tilde\Xi_{2}=a e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}}$ central, $\tilde\Xi_{1}+\tilde\Xi_{2}=a e^{i(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})t/2}\cdot 2\cos((\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t/2)$, so the norm is $a^{2}e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}}\cdot4\cos^{2}((\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t/2)$, whose argument is the sum and whose modulus is $2+2\cos((\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t)$.

The angle is $\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}$, a real frequency times a real interval, $\omega r'_t/c$ for a length and $\omega r'_t$ for a time, so the angle is real.

**Checked on a two-branch superposition.** With $\omega_{1}=0.9$, $\omega_{2}=1.3$, unit amplitudes and five sample times, the modulus is $4.000000$, $3.960133$, $3.842122$, $3.650671$, $3.393413$ against the prediction $2+2\cos(0.4t)$, and the argument is $0$, $1.100000$, $2.200000$, $-2.983185$, $-1.883185$ against the prediction $2.2t$. Both agree to every printed digit.

**The density beats at the difference and the norm's argument runs at the sum.** That is the whole of the interference structure in this algebra, and it is one line to state. The field's own phase is not observable; what is observable is the pair $(\rho,\arg\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{\natural})$, and the pair mixes the two branches by sum and difference rather than by sum alone.

**Zitterbewegung is the case $\omega_{2}=-\omega_{1}$.** Then the density beats at $2\omega_{1}$ and the norm's argument is constant: a superposition of a branch and its conjugate is a standing beat at twice the branch's rate, which is the frequency the standard theory calls $2mc^{2}/\hbar$ at rest. The factor of two is not put in by hand. It is the doubling of the phase in the quaternionic norm, and it is the same doubling that forces the phase's range to be a half turn.

**The scalar fluid sees the density and the phase, and the spin fluid sees neither.** For the scalar field the word collapses to a scale, a phase and a sign, so the bilinears of this article reduce to two: the norm and the current, and the current is the gradient of the action. There the doubling has a kinematic meaning, since the circulation of $\tilde P_{\Phi}/m$ around a node is an integer multiple of $h/m$ and the density is blind to the sign of the action. [The Klein–Gordon Fluid](the-klein-gordon-fluid.html) works that case and the contrast is its point.

## The Two Brackets and the Four

**The derivative of the word separates into four brackets of four different algebraic types, and the bilinears supply only two of them.** That is the closure statement, and it is the reason the scalar case is solvable and the spinor case is not.

*Proposition.* With the word as above and $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ the algebra's derivative,

$$
\tilde\Xi^{-1}\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde\Xi
=\tfrac12\tilde\nabla_\Phi\ln r^{2}
+i\tilde\nabla_\Phi\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}
+\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{-1}\bigl(\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-1}\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\bigr)\tilde u_{\mathrm r}
+\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{-1}\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde u_{\mathrm r},
$$

and the four terms are respectively real central, central imaginary, Hermitian and real vector.

*Proof.* Differentiate the product and divide by it. The two central factors give the first two terms. The boost's derivative is $\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}(\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-1}\partial\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b})$ and the rotor's is $\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}(\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{-1}\partial\tilde u_{\mathrm r})$, so the boost cancels in its own term and passes the rotor in the rotor's term.

**The table of types is the content of the display.** Four terms, four types, and no rule of the algebra converts one type into another, because the types are distinct subspaces and not merely distinct labels.

| bracket | type | zero when | supplied by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\tfrac12\tilde\nabla_\Phi\ln r^{2}$ | real central | the density is constant | the norm's modulus |
| $i\tilde\nabla_\Phi\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ | central imaginary | the phase is constant | the norm's argument |
| $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{-1}(\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-1}\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b})\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ | Hermitian | the boost is constant | the current, by one derivative |
| $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{-1}\tilde\nabla_\Phi\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ | real vector | the rotor is constant | the spin, by one derivative |

**The bilinears supply the first two and half of the other two.** The norm supplies the scale and the phase brackets exactly. The current supplies the boost's value but not its gradient: differentiating $\tilde J_{\Phi}$ returns the derivative of the frame, which is a connection, and the connection is an element of the same type as the third bracket only after the rotor has been conjugated away — which requires knowing the rotor. The spin supplies the rotor's value and, differentiating, its connection — but the rotor's connection enters the current's derivative conjugated by the rotor, so the two derivatives are coupled and neither is readable from its own bilinear alone.

**This is the precise sense in which the four brackets are underdetermined.** The equation constrains the four brackets jointly. No bilinear of the field returns one bracket in isolation, so no bilinear can be used to eliminate one in favour of the others. The underdetermination is the spin fluid's, not the algebra's: it is the same freedom that the tensor formulations close by adding a subsidiary condition, and [The Dirac Fluid and the Takabayasi Correspondence](the-dirac-fluid-and-the-takabayasi-correspondence.html) records how Takabayasi closes it.

**The scalar case has two brackets and closes.** With the boost at $e_t$ and the rotor at $\varepsilon e_t$ the third and fourth brackets vanish, and what remains is the scale and the phase. The one complex equation separates into two real equations, a continuity equation and an energy equation, and the separation is exact because two real equations for two real functions is a determined system. That is why the scalar fluid of this block is a closed hydrodynamics and the spinor fluid is not.

## What the Bilinears Signify

**The three readings are the fluid's three variables, and the fourth bilinear is a shadow of the second.** In the fluid language of the two case articles the current is the density times the four-velocity, the spin is the density times the spin direction, and the norm's modulus is the density. The identification is a reading: the algebra supplies the objects and their invariance, and the physics supplies the names.

**The strongest objection is that the naming is arbitrary.** One could call the current the spin and the spin the current, and no proposition of this article would change. The answer is that the naming is fixed by two properties of the objects and not by preference. The current is the bilinear that is a four-vector of the fluid's own kind, with a fixed square and a direction that transforms as a velocity; the spin is the bilinear that is a spatial direction in the fluid's rest frame, with a fixed spatial length and no temporal part. A fluid's velocity is the direction of its flow, and its spin is the direction about which it rotates, and only one assignment of the two bilinears matches those descriptions.

**The second objection is that the phase is unobservable and therefore not a fluid variable.** The answer is that the phase is unobservable only up to the half turn, and that the half turn is a global shift while the phase's gradient is local. The gradient is the momentum and it is observable through the current; the phase itself is the potential of that momentum. A fluid's velocity potential is a coordinate, and the corpus's phrase for the phase is exactly that.

## What Is Established and What Is Interpretation

**Established.** That the current is invariant under the phase and under the rotor, and that it equals the density times the boost's inverse square. That the spin is invariant under the phase and under the boost, and that it equals the density times the rotor's image of the declared axis. That the norm is invariant under both frames and reads the phase doubled. That $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$ has the temporal slot of the current and the reversed spatial part. That the current's natural norm is $-c^{2}\rho^{2}$. That a two-branch superposition has density beating at the difference of the rates and norm argument running at their sum. That the derivative of the word separates into four brackets of four types.

**Interpretation.** That the current is the fluid's current and the spin is the fluid's spin. That the phase's gradient is the momentum. That the doubling of the rate is the origin of zitterbewegung. That the underdetermination of the four brackets is the spin fluid's non-closure rather than a defect of the algebra. Each of these is argued in the case articles and none is proved here.

**Not claimed.** It is not claimed that the three bilinears exhaust the quadratic invariants of the algebra; they exhaust those built from the word's own factors. It is not claimed that $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$ is not conserved; the corpus records a non-conservation on one class of solutions and leaves the general question open. It is not claimed that the four brackets are independent, only that no bilinear separates them.

## Summary

The word's four factors and the algebra's three bilinears are the subject. Each bilinear reads two factors and kills the other two by a conjugation that inverts them: the current reads the scale and the boost, the spin the scale and the rotor, the norm the scale and the phase doubled.

The count of independent readings is therefore three against four factors, and the phase and the spin never appear in one bilinear. The algebra's own product $i\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}$ does not add a reading: it re-reads the scale and the boost with the spatial part reversed, and the reversal is the star conjugation cancelling the rotor.

The current's square is $-c^{2}\rho^{2}$ and its length is the density. The norm's argument runs at twice the field's phase, which fixes the phase's range to a half turn and makes a two-branch superposition beat at the difference of its rates while its norm's argument runs at their sum.

The derivative of the word separates into four brackets of four types, and the bilinears supply the values of two and the derivatives of none in isolation. That is the non-closure of the spin fluid in the algebra's own terms, and it is why the scalar case closes on two brackets and the spinor case does not.

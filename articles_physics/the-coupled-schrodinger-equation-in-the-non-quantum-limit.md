# __The Coupled Schrödinger Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit__

## Introduction

The connection enters the parent's polar chart as a translation of one coordinate, so it changes two terms of the free pair and no order of either: the transport is carried by the kinetic momentum instead of the gradient, and the potential energy appears beside it, while the quantum potential is untouched.

The parent supplies both equations, and the coupling is already inside them. [The Coupled Schrödinger Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables](the-coupled-schrodinger-equation-in-biquaternionic-polar-variables.html) writes the complete polar decomposition, puts the covariant derivative into the equation, and prints the two exact equations of the central field: a covariant continuity equation and a Hamilton–Jacobi equation with the connection and the same quantum potential as the free case. This article orders them.

The gauge element is central, so it acts on one factor and only one: the angle is translated and the other seven parameters are inert,

$$
\varrho\longmapsto \varrho,
\qquad
\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}\longmapsto\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}+\frac{q}{\hbar}\Gamma,
\qquad
\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\longmapsto\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b},
\qquad
\tilde u_{\mathrm r}\longmapsto\tilde u_{\mathrm r}.
$$

The scale, the rapidity, the two axes and the density are invariant, and the two combinations of the phase with the potential are the invariants the limit works with.

The consequence for the criterion is the article's first result. The large gradient is no longer $\vec\nabla_\Phi S$ but the kinetic momentum $\vec\pi=\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A$, which is the gauge-invariant combination. A criterion written on the canonical gradient would not be gauge invariant, and it would be wrong wherever the potential is large. The parameter is $\epsilon=1/(\pi_{\Phi}L)$ with $\pi_{\Phi}=\lvert\vec\pi\rvert/\hbar$, and the scale's gradient is measured against that.

The parent's coupling is $ϙ$, with $ϙ=-q$ where $q$ is the electric charge. Every display in this article uses $q$ and the canonical form $\vec\pi=\vec p-q\vec A$; the parent's $-ϙ\varphi$ is $+q\varphi$ here, and the parent's $\vec\nabla_\Phi\times\vec v=ϙ\vec B/m$ is $-(q/m)\vec B$.

The coupling changes no order: both of the parent's equations have the same three orders as the free pair, and the quantum potential is the same function of the scale. Order zero is a Hamilton–Jacobi equation in the kinetic momentum, and its gradient is the Lorentz force with $\vec\pi=m\vec v$, so the non-relativistic limit produces Newton's law with the magnetic force and nothing else. Order one is the covariant continuity equation, whose current is the kinetic momentum over the mass, and which is not an expansion. The vorticity is the field's: the canonical flow is irrotational and the kinetic flow is rotational exactly where $\vec B$ is, and the circulation is quantised with the flux subtracted, an exact statement the limit inherits and does not derive.

The covariant Madelung pair, the kinetic momentum in the transport current, the vorticity of the velocity field and the flux-corrected circulation are standard results of the hydrodynamical formulation of a charged scalar field, and the Lorentz force from a Hamilton–Jacobi equation is textbook. Added here: the ordering of the parent's own terms against a gauge-invariant parameter, the statement that the coupling costs no accuracy, and the reading of the gauge group as the translation of one polar coordinate.

The restriction is the free one, and the coupling does not remove it. The parent's pair is the central field's, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}=0$ and $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}=\varepsilon e_{t}$, and that class is invariant only under a central Hamiltonian. The limit is therefore defined on the central class here as it is in the free companion, and for the same reason.

Conventions. Those of the coupled polar parent: $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with $e_{t}=1$ and $\vec e_\mu^{2}=-e_{t}$; $\mathrm{Sc}$ the coefficient of $e_{t}$; the word $\tilde\Xi=\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$; the connection $\tilde A_\Phi=i(\varphi/c)e_{t}+\vec A$ with $\varphi=c\,a'_{t}$; the covariant derivative $\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\Phi}=\tilde\nabla_\Phi+iq\tilde A_\Phi/\hbar$; the operator $\hat H^{\mathrm{gco}}=\tilde{\vec\pi}^{\,2}/2m+q\varphi+\hat V$ with $\tilde{\vec\pi}=-i\hbar\nabla-q\vec A$; $S=\hbar\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$; $q$ the electric charge, real; $\Delta_\Phi$ the Laplacian of the material sector.

## The Two Equations and Where the Connection Sits

The parent's pair, in the article's letters.

$$
\partial_{t'}\bigl(\varrho^{2}\bigr)+\vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot\Bigl(\varrho^{2}\frac{\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A}{m}\Bigr)=0,
\qquad
\partial_{t'}S+\frac{\lvert\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A\rvert^{2}}{2m}+q\varphi+Q=0,
$$

with $Q=-\hbar^{2}\Delta_\Phi \varrho/(2m\varrho)$. The first is the covariant continuity equation and the second the quantum Hamilton–Jacobi equation of the coupled field.

The connection appears in three places and nowhere else. In the transport it replaces the canonical gradient by the kinetic momentum. In the Hamilton–Jacobi equation it does the same in the kinetic term and it adds the potential energy $q\varphi$. In the quantum potential it does not appear at all.

$Q$ is built from the scale $\varrho$ and its ordinary Laplacian. The scale is invariant under the gauge group, its Laplacian is invariant, and no derivative of the potential reaches it, because the covariant derivative acts on the phase and the frame and the scale is neither. **The quantum potential is the free one, term for term**, and the parent states it as its first finding.



$$
\vec\pi=\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A,
\qquad
\mathcal E=-\hbar\,\partial_{t'}\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}-q\varphi .
$$

The first is the mechanical momentum and the second the kinetic energy, and both are unchanged when the phase and the potential shift together. Every statement of the limit is made in these two and in the scale.

## The Criterion Is the Mechanical Wave Number

The phase's gradient is not what the equation differentiates: the covariant derivative does, and what it produces is $\vec\pi/\hbar$. So the large quantity is $\pi_{\Phi}=\lvert\vec\pi\rvert/\hbar$, the scale's variation is measured against $L$, and

$$
\epsilon=\frac{1}{\pi_{\Phi}L},
\qquad
L^{-1}=\lvert\vec\nabla_\Phi\ln \varrho\rvert .
$$

**A criterion on the canonical gradient would fail gauge invariance, and the failure is not cosmetic.** Under $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}\to\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}+q\Gamma/\hbar$ and $\vec A\to\vec A+\vec\nabla_\Phi\Gamma$, the canonical gradient shifts by $q\vec\nabla_\Phi\Gamma$ while $\vec\pi$ does not. A parameter defined on the canonical gradient would therefore change value under a gauge transformation, and a limit whose smallness depends on the gauge is not a statement about the field.

The order table is the free table with one substitution.

| object | order in $\epsilon$ | kept by the limit | kept by the first correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\vec\pi=\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A$ | $0$ | yes | yes |
| $\hbar\,\vec\nabla_\Phi\ln \varrho$ | $1$ | no | yes |
| $\hbar^{2}\,\Delta_\Phi \varrho/\varrho$ | $2$ | no | no |

Nothing in the table mentions the potential's strength. The connection contributes no parameter: $q\vec A$ enters the leading term, and a large potential shifts the leading term without changing its order. The coupling changes the value of the eikonal and never its size, and that is why the accuracy of the limit is the free accuracy.

## Order Zero: the Eikonal, and the Lorentz Force

What remains is

$$
\partial_{t'}S+\frac{\lvert\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A\rvert^{2}}{2m}+q\varphi=0 ,
$$

the Hamilton–Jacobi equation of a charge $q$ in the potentials $(\varphi,\vec A)$.

In the algebra it is a norm. With $\vec\pi$ a real quaternion in $\mathbb{B}_{H}$,

$$
\frac{1}{2m}\langle\vec\pi\rangle_{\natural}=\mathcal E ,
\qquad
\langle\vec\pi\rangle_{\natural}=\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2},
$$

so the eikonal says that the kinetic energy is the biquaternion norm of the mechanical momentum. The free article's statement is the same one with $\vec A=0$.

The characters are the trajectories of a charged particle. With $\vec p=\vec\nabla_\Phi S$ and $\vec\pi=\vec p-q\vec A$,

$$
\frac{d\vec r}{dt'}=\frac{\vec\pi}{m},
\qquad
\frac{d\vec p}{dt'}=-q\vec\nabla_\Phi\varphi+q\,\vec\nabla_\Phi(\vec A\cdot\vec v) .
$$

**The gradient of the eikonal is the Lorentz force, and this is the limit's sharpest classical statement.** Differentiate the eikonal with respect to $x^{i}$ and use $\partial_{i}\partial_{t'}S=\partial_{t'}\partial_{i}S$:

$$
\frac{d\vec\pi}{dt'}=q\bigl(\vec E+\vec v\times\vec B\bigr),
\qquad
\vec E=-\vec\nabla_\Phi\varphi-\partial_{t'}\vec A,
\qquad
\vec B=\vec\nabla_\Phi\times\vec A,
\qquad
\vec v=\frac{\vec\pi}{m}.
$$

Two cancellations carry it: the mixed derivatives of $S$ commute, and the flow relation replaces the momentum by the velocity. No spin, no amplitude equation and no second truncation enter, so the force is a property of order zero alone.

The force is Newtonian and not relativistic, and the difference is the family's. Here $\vec\pi=m\vec v$ exactly, so the magnetic force is the one of a slow charge. In the relativistic families the same calculation returns $\vec\pi=\gamma m\vec v$ and the same bracket, and the difference is the rest energy in the phase. A reader who compares the two should compare the flow relation and not the force, because the force's form is common and its kinematics is not.

## Order One: the Transport, the Vorticity and the Circulation

The transport equation is exact and the limit does not touch it.

$$
\frac{\partial \varrho^{2}}{\partial t'}+\vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot\Bigl(\varrho^{2}\frac{\vec\pi}{m}\Bigr)=0 ,
$$

the density carried by the kinetic velocity. In the free case the carrier was $\vec\nabla_\Phi S/m$; here it is $\vec\pi/m$, and that is the whole change.

**The current is gauge invariant, term by term, and the free one is not.** Under a gauge transformation the phase's gradient shifts by $q\vec\nabla_\Phi\Gamma$ and the potential's by $-q\vec\nabla_\Phi\Gamma$, and the two cancel inside one bracket. That is what the coupling buys: a current that is a property of the field rather than of the chart.

The vorticity is the field's, and the statement splits in two.

$$
\vec\nabla_\Phi\times\vec v=\frac{1}{m}\vec\nabla_\Phi\times\bigl(\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A\bigr)=-\frac{q}{m}\vec B .
$$

The canonical flow is irrotational — that is the free article's exact statement and it survives — and the kinetic flow is rotational exactly where the magnetic field is and nowhere else. The rotation is not generated by the limit; it is present in the exact pair and the limit inherits it.

The circulation is quantised with the flux subtracted, and the limit adds nothing to it.

$$
\oint\vec\pi\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec l=2\pi\hbar\,n-q\Phi_{B},
\qquad
\Phi_{B}=\int_{\Sigma}\vec B\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec\Sigma ,
$$

for a loop bounding a surface that no node crosses. The equation is exact and it is inherited: the limit neither produces it nor modifies it, and its job there is to leave it alone.

A ray-level statement that survives a limit unchanged is rare, and this one carries a physical number: the flux. The classical object the limit produces is a flow with a quantised circulation, and the quantisation is the phase's, not the algebra's.

## Order Two: the Quantum Potential, Unchanged

**The deleted term is the same function as in the free case.**

$$
Q=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{\Delta_\Phi \varrho}{\varrho},
$$

with the ordinary Laplacian and no potential in it. The coupling does not enter, and the reason is one sentence long: the covariant derivative acts on the phase, the quantum potential is built from the scale, and the scale is inert under the gauge group.

Its size is therefore the free size. With $\varrho=\exp(-x^{2}/4\sigma^{2})$ and $\epsilon=1/(\pi_{\Phi}\sigma)$,

$$
\frac{\lvert Q\rvert}{\mathcal E}=\epsilon^{2}\left\lvert\frac{x^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right\rvert ,
$$

and the non-uniformity is the free one: the ratio reaches one at $x\simeq2\sigma/\epsilon$ for every $\epsilon$, so no region fixed in advance carries a uniform approximation.

What the coupling does not do. It does not add an order, it does not change the coefficient of $\epsilon^{2}$, and it does not move the place where the expansion fails. A limit whose accuracy changed with the coupling would be a limit of a different equation; here the equation is the same one with a covariant derivative and the accuracy is unchanged.

## What the Coupling Costs and What It Does Not

It costs nothing in accuracy. The three orders are the free three, the deleted term is the free term, and the criterion is the free criterion with the mechanical wave number in place of the canonical one.

It costs one new condition, and the condition is stated and not met. The connection must be slowly varying on the scale $L$, or the criterion's meaning changes: $\vec\pi$ contains $\vec A$, so a potential that varies as fast as the phase makes the leading term vary as fast as the scale. **No article in the corpus states that condition as a hypothesis**, and this one states it as a gap rather than assuming it away.

The current is gauge invariant. The vorticity is the field's, exactly. And the circulation is quantised with the flux, exactly. None of the three is a semiclassical result; all three are inherited, and the limit's role is to leave them standing.

It gains one structural reading. On the polar chart the gauge group is the translation of one coordinate, so gauge invariance is the statement that seven of the eight parameters are inert and one shifts. The limit's covariance is then visible as a statement about which parameters the criterion reads: it reads the scale and the two invariants of the phase, and it is blind to the other six.

## Verification

The eikonal's gradient was checked symbolically. With arbitrary $S$, $\varphi$ and $\vec A$, $\vec\pi=\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A$ and $\vec v=\vec\pi/m$, the three components of

$$
\partial_{i}\Bigl(\partial_{t'}S+\frac{\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}}{2m}+q\varphi\Bigr)
$$

were compared with the three components of $\frac{d\vec\pi}{dt'}-q(\vec E+\vec v\times\vec B)$ and the residual is $0,0,0$ in each component, with no assumption on the potentials.

The vorticity was checked by direct differentiation. $\vec\nabla_\Phi\times(\vec\nabla_\Phi S-q\vec A)=-q\vec\nabla_\Phi\times\vec A$ because the curl of a gradient vanishes identically, and this is an identity and not an approximation.

The quantum potential was checked for invariance. $Q$ contains $\varrho$ and $\Delta_\Phi \varrho$ only; under the gauge transformation the word's factors transform as the parent states, the scale is unchanged, and $Q$ is unchanged term for term.

The circulation was checked against the parent's letters. The parent writes $\oint\vec\pi\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec l=2\pi\hbar n+ϙ\Phi_{B}$ with $ϙ=-q$; the display here is the same statement in the article's letters.

**What was not checked.** No numerical solution of the coupled time-dependent problem was run and no flux was computed on a closed form. The claims are about terms and identities, and both were verified as algebra.

## Summary

The parent's pair carries the connection in exactly three places: the transport current, the kinetic term of the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, and the potential energy. The quantum potential is the free one, because it is built from the scale and the scale is inert under the gauge group.

The criterion must be written on the mechanical momentum, $\epsilon=1/(\pi_{\Phi}L)$, because the canonical gradient is not gauge invariant. With that substitution the order table is the free table and the coupling contributes no parameter.

Order zero is the Hamilton–Jacobi equation in the kinetic momentum, and its gradient is the Lorentz force with $\vec\pi=m\vec v$: Newton's law with the magnetic force, from order zero alone, by two named cancellations. Order one is the covariant continuity equation, exact and untouched by the limit, and it carries two exact companions — the vorticity $-(q/m)\vec B$ and the circulation $2\pi\hbar n-q\Phi_{B}$.

Order two is the free quantum potential, with the free size and the free non-uniformity. The coupling therefore changes the value of every leading term and the accuracy of none.

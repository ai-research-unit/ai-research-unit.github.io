## One Equation, Three Physical Answers

The three central equations differ only in the radicand, and the vector–scalar split solves them
together. Writing a root as $\tilde P=P_0e_0+\mathbf P$, the equation $\tilde P^{2}=\tilde Q$ separates
into one vector equation and one scalar equation,

$$
2P_0\mathbf P=\mathbf Q,
\qquad
P_0^{2}-(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=Q_0,
$$

and for the three central radicands $\mathbf Q=0$ and $Q_0\in\{-1,0,+1\}$. The vector equation then
forces $\mathbf P=0$ or $P_0=0$, so a root is either a **complex scalar** $P_0e_0$ with
$P_0^{2}=Q_0$, or a **pure** element $\mathbf P$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-Q_0$. The two branches are
disjoint and exhaustive, and the three answer sets are read off them. This is the whole of the
classification for the central values.

The physical content of the split is the separation it makes. The scalar branch is the **central** one:
its roots commute with everything and ask nothing of the direction of space. The pure branch is the
**directional** one: its roots are vectors, they single out a direction and a complex structure, and they
carry the geometry of the patch. Every physical reading below is a reading of the pure branch.

## The Roots of $-1$: the Local Complex Structure

For $\tilde P^{2}=-e_0$ the two branches give the two central roots and the pure elements of complex
square one,

$$
\tilde P=\pm ie_0,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tilde P=\mathbf P\ \text{pure with}\ (\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1 .
$$

The central roots are the two elements of the centre that square to $-e_0$. The pure branch is the family
$\{\mathbf P:(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1\}$, whose **real** members are the pure real vectors of unit length,
that is the **two-sphere** $S^{2}$ of unit imaginary quaternions $\hat\mu=\mu_1e_1+\mu_2e_2+\mu_3e_3$ with
$\mu_1^{2}+\mu_2^{2}+\mu_3^{2}=1$. Beyond the real sphere the complex members extend the family: writing
$\mathbf P=\mathbf U+i\mathbf V$ with $\mathbf U,\mathbf V$ real, the single complex condition
$(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1$ is the pair $\lvert\mathbf U\rvert^{2}-\lvert\mathbf V\rvert^{2}=1$,
$\mathbf U\!\cdot\!\mathbf V=0$.

Every root of $-1$ is a **complex structure**: an element $J$ with $J^{2}=-e_0$, whose left
multiplication is a real-linear map of the algebra to itself with square $-\mathrm{id}$. The two-sphere $S^{2}$ is the quaternion algebra's own sphere of imaginary units, the familiar local
imaginary units of the quaternion and spinor calculi, and the two central roots $\pm ie_0$ are the roots
that **commute** with every element, with $ie_0$ the distinguished one that makes the algebra an algebra
over $\mathbb{C}$ and that the corpus calls the scalar imaginary $i$; its negative $-ie_0$ carries the
conjugate scalar structure.

**The reading.** An element that squares to $-1$ is read as a choice of **what is called $i$** in a local
patch, and the central imaginary is the distinguished, global choice. The remaining roots are local: each
defines its own complex structure, and passing from one to another is a change of what plays the role of
$i$. The physics of the reading — the local complex structure fixing the propagation of the fields and
the speed of light of the patch — is owned by *The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light*, where
the sphere $S^{2}$ is the set of local frames a field can be referred to.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** In the material sector the root set splits into the pair
$\pm ie_0$, which commutes with everything, and the sphere of real unit vectors, which do not. **Time is
the central direction and space is the sphere of spatial roots**, so the split of the root set is the
algebraic form of the inertial frame: one global time direction and a sphere of spatial orientations.

## The Roots of $0$: the Light Cone and Lightlike Propagation

For $\tilde P^{2}=0$ the branches give the trivial root and the pure null elements,

$$
\tilde P=0,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tilde P=\mathbf P\ \text{pure with}\ (\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=0 .
$$

The nonzero roots are the **nilpotents**: elements $\tilde P\neq0$ with $\tilde P^{2}=0$. They are
exactly the pure null elements and they are a subset of the **zero divisors**, the elements of the complex
norm cone $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=0$. An element of that cone which is not pure is a zero divisor that is not
nilpotent, so the roots of $0$ are the **pure** part of the cone, and the whole cone is the framework's
null cone in the algebra.

**The reading.** A nonzero element whose square vanishes is read as the algebraic form of a **lightlike
propagation**: the element is not zero, yet composing it with itself annihilates it, the algebraic shadow
of a displacement of zero interval. The cone as the light cone, the nilpotents as its pure part, and the
zero divisors as the locus of the lightlike directions are owned by *The Light Cone as the Biquaternion
Zero-Divisor Cone* and *Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form*. What is proved here is
the classification: the roots of $0$ are $0$ together with the pure null elements, and the cone is larger
than them.

## The Roots of $+1$: Reality Conditions

For $\tilde P^{2}=+e_0$ the branches give the two central roots and the pure elements of complex square
minus one,

$$
\tilde P=\pm e_0,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tilde P=\mathbf P\ \text{pure with}\ (\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-1 .
$$

Every root of $+1$ is an **involution**: an element $J$ with $J^{2}=e_0$, whose left multiplication has
square $+\mathrm{id}$. The two central roots are the identity and its negative. The pure branch is the
family of pure involutions, and its **Hermitian** members are the elements $i\hat\mu$ with $\hat\mu$ a

$\mathbf U\!\cdot\!\mathbf V=0$ and $\lvert\mathbf V\rvert^{2}-\lvert\mathbf U\rvert^{2}=1$, of which the
Hermitian members $\mathbf P=i\hat\mu$ are the reflections.

**The reading.** An involution is read as the algebra-side counterpart of a possible **reality condition**,

## Square Roots of a General Element

For a general $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf Q$ the same split gives the whole classification. The vector
equation forces $\mathbf P=\mathbf Q/(2P_0)$ whenever $\mathbf Q\neq0$, so a root is fixed by its scalar
part $P_0$, whose square $x=P_0^{2}$ solves the reduced quadratic
$4x^{2}-4Q_0x-(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=0$ of discriminant $16n$; its two solutions are
$x_\pm=\tfrac12(Q_0\pm\sqrt n)$, and each nonzero solution contributes the pair
$\pm(\sqrt x\,e_0+\mathbf Q/(2\sqrt x))$. The result is

| condition on $\tilde Q$ | root set |
|---|---|
| $\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)\neq0$, $n\neq0$ | four roots, two pairs $\pm\tilde P$ |
| $\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)\neq0$, $n=0$ | two roots, a single pair $\pm\tilde P$ |
| $\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=0$, $Q_0\neq0$ | two roots, a single pair $\pm\tilde P$ |
| $\mathbf Q\neq0$, $n=0$, $Q_0=0$ (nonzero nilpotent) | no roots |
| $\mathbf Q=0$ (complex scalar) | a continuum: $\pm\sqrt{Q_0}\,e_0$ and the pure $\mathbf P$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=-Q_0$ |

The generic case is four roots. The complex square $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)$ decides whether a solution is
lost at $x=0$, the conjugate scalar $n$ decides whether the two solutions of the reduced quadratic are
distinct, and $\mathbf Q$ decides which of the two cases applies. The roots always come in $\pm$ pairs, so
the root set is symmetric about the origin.

**Remark (verified).** For $\tilde Q=e_0+e_1$ one has $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=1$ and $n=2$, and the four
roots have $P_0=\pm1.0987$ and $P_0=\pm0.4551\,i$, each squaring to $\tilde Q$; for the vector radicand
$-(2+i)e_3$, with a complex coefficient and $n=3+4i\neq0$, the classification again returns four roots,
and each listed root squares to its radicand to machine precision.

## Every Material Four-Vector Has a Square Root

The fourth row of the table is the criterion for existence, and it reduces to one name. The conditions
$\mathbf Q\neq0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=0$ and $Q_0=0$ say exactly that $\tilde Q\neq0$ with
$\tilde Q^{2}=0$, so the rootless elements are precisely the **nonzero nilpotents**, and the rootless
locus is the lightlike locus of the algebra.

**Theorem (the square-root criterion).** $\tilde Q$ has no square root if and only if $\tilde Q$ is a
**nonzero nilpotent**. Every other element of the algebra has at least one square root.

**Corollary (every material four-vector has a square root).** No nonzero element of the material sector
$\mathbb{M}_-$ is nilpotent, so every element of $\mathbb{M}_-$ has a square root.

*Proof.* Write $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_-$ as $\tilde Q=ib_0e_0+\mathbf q$ with $b_0\in\mathbb{R}$ and
$\mathbf q$ a **real** vector. If $\tilde Q^{2}=0$ then the vector part $2ib_0\mathbf q$ vanishes, so
$b_0=0$ or $\mathbf q=0$. If $b_0=0$ then $\tilde Q=\mathbf q$ and the scalar part
$-(\mathbf q,\mathbf q)=-\lvert\mathbf q\rvert^{2}$ vanishes only for $\mathbf q=0$; if $\mathbf q=0$
then $\tilde Q=ib_0e_0$ and the scalar part $-b_0^{2}$ vanishes only for $b_0=0$. Either way
$\tilde Q=0$.

The material sector is thus the sector with **no refusal**: every material four-vector is the square of
some element of the algebra. The refusal is a property of the nilpotents, which lie in the vector subspace and in neither sector (the
element $e_1+ie_2$ is one), and not of the material sector. The degenerate non-central cases are the lightlike material elements: for
$\tilde Q=ie_0+e_1$ one has $n=0$, the radicand is on the light cone, the reduced quadratic has the double
solution $x=Q_0/2=i/2$, and the root set is a single pair, so a lightlike material element has **two**
roots and not four — the degeneracy is a doubling-down and not a refusal.

**Remark (verified).** On the material elements $3ie_0+e_1$, $ie_0$, $e_1$ and the null $ie_0+e_1$ the
classification returns four, a continuum, four and two roots respectively, and each root squares to its
radicand; the rootless case is met only on the nonzero nilpotents such as $e_1+ie_2$, and never inside
$\mathbb{M}_-$.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** Read physically, **the material sector is the sector of the
composite**: every material four-vector is the square of some element, and the refusal is confined to the
null elements of the vector subspace, such as $e_1+ie_2$, which lie in neither sector. *What is proved is
the corollary; the words "composite" and "refusal" are the reading.*

## The Mass Shell as a Slice of the Square Map

The square-root problem is the problem of **inverting the ordinary square map**
$\sigma(\tilde P)=\tilde P^{2}$, and the mass shell uses a different square. The two must be kept apart;
the companion article *Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger* makes the
same distinction between the ordinary square and the sesquilinear one.

**The natural square.** The **natural square** is
$\nu(\tilde P)=\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=N(\tilde P)e_0$, the biquaternion norm. It is
$\mathbb{C}$-valued and it **always lands in the centre**: for every element the natural square is a
multiple of $e_0$. On a material four-momentum $\tilde P=ict\,e_0+\mathbf x$ it is the interval,
$N=-c^{2}t^{2}+\lvert\mathbf x\rvert^{2}$; its zero set on $\mathbb{M}_-$ is the light cone and its level
set $N=-m^{2}c^{2}$ is the mass shell. The square of the material sector that carries the interval is the
natural one, and it belongs to the companion quaternionic block.

**The mass shell as a slice.** Read with the natural square, the mass-shell condition is the requirement
that the square of the four-momentum be the fixed scalar $-m^{2}c^{2}$, and the mass shell is the
**fibre** of the natural square map over that scalar. That the square is a scalar is automatic for the
natural square; what the mass-shell condition adds is the **value**. The mass is the label of the slice.

**The scalar slice of the ordinary square.** For completeness, the ordinary square $\sigma$ lands in the
centre exactly when $2P_0\mathbf P=0$, that is when $\tilde P$ is **central** ($\mathbf P=0$) or **pure**
($P_0=0$). The scalar slice of $\sigma$ is therefore the union of the centre and the vector subspace, and
on it the square takes the values $P_0^{2}$ and $-(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)$. This slice is larger than the
mass shell and is not the mass shell; it is recorded because it is the set on which the ordinary square,
like the natural one, is scalar, and it is the reason the two squares are easily confused.

## The Reading, Labelled as Such

**Proposed reading, partly speculative and labelled as such.** A material quantity is read as an element
$\tilde Q$ of the algebra, and the quantity is said to be **realised by the algebra** when $\tilde Q$ has
a square root, so that it is the square of some other element. The criterion is then read as an existence
condition on the quantity.

- A quantity whose element is **off the nilpotent locus** is realised: it has a square root, and the root
  is the element of which it is the square.
- A quantity whose element is a **nonzero nilpotent** is not realised: there is no element whose square it
  is, and the algebra refuses it.
- The **material sector carries no refusal**: by the corollary above every material four-vector is
  realised.

The three central cases are read the same way: a quantity that is a complex structure, a nilpotent or an
involution is a quantity whose square is one of the three distinguished values, and the three values are
the three distinguished squares. The **mass-shell reading** adds that a four-momentum is a material
element, that its natural square is the scalar $N(\tilde P)e_0$, and that the mass shell is the slice of
the natural square map on which that scalar takes the value $-m^{2}c^{2}$.

**What the framework does not claim.** The identification of the square-root criterion with a physical
existence condition is a **proposal and not a result**. The framework does not claim that the nilpotent
locus is unphysical, that every material quantity is a square in the physical sense, or that the mass
shell is derived from the square map; it does not fix a mass spectrum and does not say which roots are
physical. The root set is not unique — a generic element has four roots and a complex scalar a continuum
— so "the" square root of a quantity is not unique, and the reading has no preferred root until an extra
principle selects one. That non-uniqueness is the standing weakness of the reading.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** Read as structure and not as a defect, the non-uniqueness is the
statement that **a quantity's roots are the local frames it can be referred to**, and that choosing one
is a choice of representative — the analogue for the frame of a gauge fixing of an orbit.

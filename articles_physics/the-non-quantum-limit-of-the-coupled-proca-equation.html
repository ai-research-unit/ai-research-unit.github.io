The Non-Quantum Limit of the Coupled Proca Equation

Introduction

The coupling supplies the phase that the free field lacked, and that is the family's cleanest structural statement. The Non-Quantum Limit of the Free Proca Equation had to state its limit on a mode, $A_\mu=\mathrm{Re}(a_\mu e^{i\theta})$, because a real four-vector has no phase parameter and the parent's word carries none. The coupled field is complex: The Coupled Proca Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables writes it as the four-factor word $\tilde A=r\,e^{i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ with eight real parameters, and the central angle is a coordinate. So this article needs no complexification step: the amplitude is the parent's $r$ and frame, the phase is the parent's $\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}$, and the limit is a statement about the parent's own variables.

What the coupling costs and what it does not. The connection is a central scalar in every slot, so it joins the two central brackets and cannot reach the frame — the parent's exact statement of type. In the mode's language it shifts one object and one only,

$$ \vec\pi=\hbar\nabla\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}+ϙ\vec A, \qquad \mathcal E=-\hbar c\,\partial_{t'}\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}+ϙ\,c\,a'_t, \qquad \pi_t=\mathcal E/c, $$

and the parent's own reading, $\hbar c\kappa_t=E+ϙca'_t$ and $\hbar\vec\kappa=\vec p+ϙ\vec A$, is the same display in the element's letters. Both quantities are gauge invariant, which is why the criterion below must be written on them and not on the canonical gradient.

What the article establishes, in five sentences. The Proca equation splits, exactly and at every order, into its trace and its traceless part; the trace is the covariant Lorenz condition and at order zero it is $\pi\cdot a=0$, while the traceless part at order zero is $\pi^{2}=k_m^{2}$. The curvature does not enter the trace — it is traceless, and that is the parent's Lorenz argument read in the mode — so the constraint is exactly the free one at $\pi$, and the coupling's whole effect on the order-zero system is the matrix $\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}F$, which moves the polarisation directions and not the count. The transport equation is exact and covariant, $\partial_\mu(\lvert a\rvert^{2}\pi^{\mu})=0$, and the flow is $\vec v=c^{2}\vec\pi/\mathcal E$ with the Lorentz force one curl away. The remainder is governed by two ratios and not one: $\epsilon^{2}$ and $\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert/\pi^{2}$, and the second is not a power of $\epsilon$.

Notation. The parent's: $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ the material sector, $\tilde A=ie_t a'_t+\vec e\cdot\vec A$, the interval $v^{2}=v'^{2}_t-\lvert\vec v\rvert^{2}$, $\partial/\partial R_t=-i\,\partial/\partial r'_t$, $r'_t=ct$, $k_m=mc/\hbar$, and the covariant derivative $\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\mu}=\partial/\partial R_\mu+\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}A_\mu$. The coupled field is complex, $\tilde A=\mathcal A\,e^{i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$ with $\mathcal A$ slowly varying, and $a_\mu$ below denotes $\mathcal A$'s four components in the sense of the parent's $\hbar\vec\kappa$ and $\hbar c\kappa_t$. The Proca bloc's sign convention is used throughout: the potential enters $\pi$ with $+$, as the parent has it.

The Two Objects the Parent Supplies

The first is the pair of invariants, and the parent derives them from the type of the connection. In the temporal slot the connection's share of the logarithmic derivative is $\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}A_t=-\frac{ϙ}{\hbar}a'_t$, a real central number; in the spatial slots it is $\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}A_\eta$, central imaginary. The asymmetry is the algebra's and not a convention, and its consequence is that the scalar and vector potentials are the two slots of one element, so the two classical substitutions are one substitution and no relative sign can be chosen. That is the coupled Dirac article's statement in the bosonic case, and it holds here for the same reason.

The second is the split of the equation into a trace and a traceless part. The parent displays the free split: the scalar part gives the scale equation $(\Box_\Phi-k_m^{2})r+r\sum_\mu\mathrm{Sc}(\Gamma_\mu^{2})=0$ and the traceless part gives the frame's transport. The coupled parent adds that the trace is the covariant Lorenz condition, one real equation among the eight, and that "the divergence is built from the scale and phase brackets and from the connection's temporal slot, so the constraint is a relation among the three central objects and the modulus, and the frame's brackets do not enter it."

Why that second statement is the whole of this article's order-zero structure. The trace contains no frame bracket, hence no polarisation index, and the curvature is traceless, hence invisible to it. A constraint that sees only the central objects and the modulus is a constraint on the phase's gradient alone, and in the mode's variables it is one scalar equation relating $\pi\cdot a$ to the amplitude's divergence. The limit reads it at order zero and the answer is $\pi\cdot a=0$.

What the parent does not do, and this article does not need. The parent does not order its chart equation by powers of a small parameter: its second-order operator is written as a contraction with the field's own tetrad, and every term of it is of the field's own scale. That is the same obstruction the free companion found in the modulus chart, and here it is not an obstruction, because the coupled chart has a phase to carry the fast scale.

The Exact Split, and the Two Orders

The trace at order zero. Writing $\tilde A=\mathcal A e^{i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$, the covariant divergence becomes

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}_{\mathrm{gco}}\tilde A=e^{i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\Bigl[\bigl(\partial\cdot a\bigr)+\frac{i}{\hbar}\,\pi\cdot a\Bigr], $$

so at leading order its vanishing is

$$ \pi\cdot a=0 . $$

This is the transversality of the free companion, with $\pi$ in place of $k$, and it is inherited rather than re-derived: the parent's trace is the covariant Lorenz condition, the connection is central, and the only change is that the four-wave-number is the mechanical one. Two things follow and they are the article's first pair of statements. The condition is gauge invariant, because $\pi$ is; and it is independent of the curvature, because the curvature is traceless. So the constraint at order zero is exactly the free one at $\pi$, and this is the only part of the coupled theory whose structure the coupling does not touch — the parent's sentence about the constraint, read at the limit's order.

The traceless part at order zero. With $\pi\cdot a=0$ the equation's remaining content is

$$ \bigl(\pi^{2}-k_m^{2}\bigr)a^{\nu}-\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}F^{\nu}{}_{\mu}a^{\mu}=0 , $$

whose first term is the mass shell and whose second is the obstruction of The Fourier Transform and the Coupled Proca Equation in Biquaternionic Form: that article's symbol is $(-\pi^{2}-k_m^{2})\delta+\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}F-\pi^{\mu}\pi_{\nu}$, the same three pieces in the same order, and the article's separation of the mass from the obstruction is the reason they can be read one at a time.

Read the two displays together and the order-zero system is stated. It is four algebraic equations for four amplitudes: one trace equation, the constraint, and three traceless ones. The trace removes one direction; the traceless three put the remaining three on the shell and fix their polarisation. The count is three, as in the free case, and the reason is unchanged: the mass is the coefficient of the constraint.

The curvature's place in that system, which is the family's own subtlety. $F$ is traceless, so it appears only in the traceless equations; it does not shift the constraint; and it does not change the count, because a traceless addition cannot create or destroy the mass's factor in the determinant. What it does change is the eigenvectors, and that is the polarisation's rotation — birefringence of the vacuum in the sense that the three directions the constraint leaves are not the free ones. The Fourier article states the same three claims in frequency space, and this article's contribution is to locate them at the limit's order zero rather than at a wave number.

The Criterion

On the mechanical wave number, and this is forced and not chosen.

$$ \epsilon=\frac{1}{k_\pi L}=\frac{\hbar}{\lvert\vec\pi\rvert L}, $$

with $L$ the length over which $\mathcal A$ varies. The canonical choice $\hbar/(\lvert\vec p\rvert L)$ is not gauge invariant — $\nabla\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ moves under a gauge transformation while the field does not — and a criterion that moved with the gauge would not be a property of the solution. The mechanical choice is invariant and is the only one available, exactly as in the coupled Klein–Gordon and coupled Dirac articles.

A strong field makes the limit easier, and that is worth stating because it is counter-intuitive. $\lvert\vec\pi\rvert=\lvert\hbar\nabla\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}+ϙ\vec A\rvert$ can be made large by a large potential, so $\epsilon$ falls as the field grows. The limit orders the amplitude's gradient against the mechanical momentum and not the field against anything.

The second ratio is the field's, and it is not a power of $\epsilon$.

$$ \frac{\hbar\,ϙ\,\lvert F\rvert}{\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}}, $$

which measures the curvature against the kinetic term. It is the same ratio the coupled Dirac article found as the magneton over the kinetic energy, and here it governs the traceless, order-zero term of the display above. No power counting in $\epsilon$ produces it, because $F$ carries no derivative of the amplitude: it multiplies it.

comparison ratio the limit
amplitude's gradient against $\pi$ $\epsilon^{2}$ yes; the limit is $\epsilon\to0$
curvature against the kinetic term $\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert/\pi^{2}$ no; a condition on the field
amplitude's gradient against the mass $(k_mL)^{-2}$ no; the one-particle boundary
the remainder against the leading group $\max(\epsilon^{2},\,\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert/\pi^{2})$ the error

The last row is the honest statement of the accuracy, and it is a maximum and not a sum. The two smallnesses are independent, so either can be the one that fails, and a reader who quoted only $\epsilon^{2}$ would be describing the constant-background case.

Order Zero: the Eikonal and the Flow

The eikonal, from the traceless part with the constraint in hand. Contracting the display of the previous section with $\bar a_\nu$ and taking the real part, the curvature term drops because $F$ is real and antisymmetric, and what remains is

$$ \pi^{2}=k_m^{2}, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad \frac{\mathcal E^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}, $$

with $\mathcal E=-\hbar c\,\partial_{t'}\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}+ϙca'_t$ the mechanical energy. That is the same mass shell the coupled Klein–Gordon and coupled Dirac articles obtain, and the agreement is not a coincidence: the mass shell is the statement that the constraint's coefficient is the mass, and every family with a mass has it.

The flow is dust's, and not the field's phase velocity.

$$ \vec v=\frac{\partial\mathcal E}{\partial\vec\pi}=\frac{c^{2}\vec\pi}{\mathcal E}, \qquad \lvert\vec v\rvert

which is $\vec\pi=\gamma m\vec v$ read backwards. The rays are straight in a constant background, timelike everywhere, and carry the density $\lvert a\rvert^{2}$. The limit delivers a congruence and not a trajectory, and a trajectory is a choice of one characteristic.

The Lorentz force is one curl away, and the derivation is exact. Because $\partial_\mu\pi_\nu-\partial_\nu\pi_\mu=ϙF_{\mu\nu}$ — the gradient of a gradient cancels and the connection's curl is the field strength — contracting the eikonal's own gradient with $\pi$ gives

$$ \pi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\pi_{\mu}=-\pi^{\nu}\bigl(\partial_{\mu}\pi_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\pi_{\mu}\bigr)=-ϙ\,F_{\mu\nu}\pi^{\nu}, $$

so the acceleration along the ray is the Lorentz force with no spin term. The absence is structural and the next section says why.

The two sheets. The eikonal is even in $\mathcal E$ and its roots are $\pm c\sqrt{\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}+m^{2}c^{2}}$. The limit keeps both, and the parent's evenness statement — the symbol's invariance under $\tilde K_\Phi\mapsto-\tilde K_\Phi$ — is their frequency-space form. A constant scalar potential translates the axis about which the two are symmetric, which is $\mathcal E=E+ϙca'_t$ and not a new fact.

Order One: the Transport Equation

The second order's own identity, contracted the same way.

$$ \partial_\mu\bigl(\lvert a\rvert^{2}\pi^{\mu}\bigr)=0, $$

with $\lvert a\rvert^{2}=a_\nu\bar a^{\nu}$. The equation is covariant and its current is the mechanical one, so it is gauge invariant as an equation: $\pi$ is invariant and $\lvert a\rvert^{2}$ is a scalar of the amplitude.

Its content is the same as in the free case and its form is not. The free transport carried $k^{\mu}$; this one carries $\pi^{\mu}$, so the flux is the mechanical momentum's and the density's conservation is the statement that the number of rays is conserved in the frame in which the potential is counted. The difference is the whole of the coupling's effect on the transport, and it is a shift of one factor.

What it does not determine. It fixes $\lvert a\rvert^{2}$ along the rays and nothing about the rays. The triangular order is the same as in the five families already written: constraint, then eikonal, then flow, then transport.

The polarisation is transported and not precessed, at this order. The transport equation is scalar and carries no polarisation index, so it says nothing about the three directions' rotation; that rotation is the curvature's, it sits in the traceless order-zero display, and it is a statement about the eigenvectors and not about the density. The separation is exact and it is why the limit needs three objects and not two: constraint, eikonal, transport, and the polarisation's frame as a fourth bookkeeping that the first three do not move.

No Spin Term, and Why

The force is the Lorentz force and not the Bargmann–Michel–Telegdi equation, for an algebraic reason. The eikonal is a scalar function of $\pi$, and its gradient is a scalar one-form; a scalar cannot act on a polarisation index. The spin's coupling in the coupled Dirac article came from a commutator of two first-order covariant derivatives, which is a matrix, and that is why $g=2$ appears there and not here.

Where the polarisation's coupling lives instead. In the order-zero traceless display, as the matrix $\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}F^{\nu}{}_{\mu}$. A matrix that multiplies the amplitude and carries no derivative is a term of the leading group, so it is not an order-one effect at all: it redefines the polarisation the eikonal is solved for, and the eigenvalues of the order-zero system are the free ones shifted by a traceless perturbation. That is the sharpest difference between the coupled Proca and coupled Dirac families, and it is a difference of the operator's order.

The claim in one sentence. For a first-order operator the curvature enters between two derivatives and produces an order-$\epsilon$ term; for a second-order operator it enters the leading group directly and produces no order-$\epsilon$ term at all. The coupling's accuracy in this family is therefore $\epsilon^{2}$, degraded not by $\epsilon$ but by $\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert/\pi^{2}$.

What the Limit Does Not Order

The field. $\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert/\pi^{2}$ is a condition on the background and not on the amplitude's length. A strong field with a short amplitude makes the limit better; a strong curvature with a long amplitude makes it worse, and the two statements are independent.

The mass. $k_m$ is the coefficient of the constraint and a term of the eikonal, so no statement that it is small belongs to this limit — and the massless case is a failure, treated below.

The coupling. The limit orders $\epsilon$ and not $ϙ$: the connection is inside $\pi$ and inside the curvature's ratio, and neither is a power of the expansion parameter.

Superposition. Two amplitudes with different phases have no single $\epsilon$, and the eikonal of a sum is not the sum of the eikonals. The limit acts on one phase and one amplitude.

Failure Regions

Five, and the first four are places or limits.

Caustics. Where the congruence focuses, $\lvert a\rvert$ diverges and $\epsilon$ grows without bound. The eikonal remains a solution and the expansion does not.

The de Broglie scale. $\epsilon\sim1$, so the amplitude's gradient competes with the phase's.

The Compton scale. $(k_mL)\sim1$, the two sheets stop being separable, and the amplitude's reading as a complex number per polarisation fails.

Strong curvature. $\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert\gtrsim\pi^{2}$, so the traceless order-zero term is comparable with the mass shell and the polarisation the eikonal is solved for is no longer a small perturbation of the free one. This is the region in which the second ratio fails while $\epsilon$ may still be small, and it is the one the criterion alone does not see.

The massless limit. At $k_m=0$ the constraint's coefficient vanishes, $\pi\cdot a=0$ is no longer implied by the trace, and the symbol's determinant vanishes identically — the parent Fourier article's statement that the symbol is invertible nowhere in the limit. The count jumps from three to two and the mechanism changes from the mass to a gauge orbit. The limit presupposes that the fourth direction was removed, and at $k_m=0$ it was not.

A constant background is the case in which one ratio vanishes. With $F=0$ the curvature term is absent identically, the traceless display reduces to the free one at $\pi$, and the remainder is $\epsilon^{2}$ alone — which is the free companion's state. The coupled family's extra smallness is therefore exactly the field strength's, and stating the accuracy as $\max(\epsilon^{2},\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert/\pi^{2})$ is the sharpest form the article can give it.

Summary

The coupled Proca field is complex and its parent's word carries the central angle, so unlike the free family this limit needs no mode of its own: the amplitude is the parent's $r$ and frame and the phase is the parent's $\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}$. The connection is central in every slot, so it joins the two central brackets and appears in the mode as the shift $\vec\pi=\hbar\nabla\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}+ϙ\vec A$, $\mathcal E=-\hbar c\,\partial_{t'}\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}+ϙca'_t$, the parent's $\hbar\vec\kappa$ and $\hbar c\kappa_t$ in other letters.

The equation splits into its trace and its traceless part. The trace is the covariant Lorenz condition and at order zero it is $\pi\cdot a=0$; the traceless part at order zero is $\pi^{2}=k_m^{2}$ with the curvature's matrix $\frac{iϙ}{\hbar}F$ beside the mass. Because the curvature is traceless, the constraint is exactly the free one at $\pi$ and the count is unchanged at three, while the polarisation directions are rotated and the eigenvalues shifted. The criterion is $\epsilon=1/(k_\pi L)$ on the mechanical wave number, the only gauge-invariant choice, and a strong field makes it smaller. The transport is $\partial_\mu(\lvert a\rvert^{2}\pi^{\mu})=0$, exact and covariant, and the flow is $\vec v=c^{2}\vec\pi/\mathcal E$ with the Lorentz force one curl away and no spin term — because a scalar eikonal cannot act on a polarisation index. The remainder is $O(\max(\epsilon^{2},\hbarϙ\lvert F\rvert/\pi^{2}))$, and the second ratio is independent of the first. The failure regions are caustics, the de Broglie scale, the Compton scale, strong curvature, and the massless limit where the constraint loses its coefficient.

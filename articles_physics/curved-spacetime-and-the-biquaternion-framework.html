Curved Spacetime and the Biquaternion Framework

Introduction

Every result in the read-list articles is a statement about one fixed algebra $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}$, evaluated at one point. The biquaternion norm, the rotor group, the rotor conjugation, the light cone as the zero-divisor set, the decomposition $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{M}_+ \oplus \mathbb{M}_-$ — all of them are pointwise and flat. The wave equations written with them presuppose a global coordinate system $(ict',\,x,\,y,\,z)$ carrying a distinguished imaginary time.

This article is about what happens when the spacetime in which those equations are written is curved. It is not a report of a finished construction. Its subject is a boundary: what the flat machinery extends to, what a curved metric can be made to do inside the algebra, and where the programme stops.

Three claims organise the discussion, and they are worth stating at the outset, because the prose of the framework elsewhere can suggest more than has been built.

First, curvature cannot reside in the algebra. The algebra is the same at every point and its biquaternion norm has constant coefficients. Curvature can therefore be carried only by the field that attaches the algebra to the manifold, not by the algebra itself. The question "what is curved spacetime in the biquaternion framework?" is a question about a field of frames, not about $\mathbb{B}$.

Second, the framework's own local device does not reach general relativity. That device is the local scale factor $c = 1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$ of the imaginary time axis. Read as a map of points it produces no curvature at all, because the resulting line element is the flat form of $\mathbb{M}_-$ written in curvilinear coordinates; read as a derivative rule it produces a genuinely curved metric, but of a class so rigid that within it Ricci-flatness forces flatness. The two interpretations are inequivalent, and the framework's prose does not choose between them.

Third, a general curved metric can be carried, but only by inserting a frame field (a tetrad). The algebra then supplies the local Lorentz group at every point, and can even supply the connection and its curvature as elements of its own Lie subspace; it supplies nothing that determines them. Any four-dimensional real vector space with a form of signature $(3,1)$ would do the same work at each point, so nothing distinctive about $\mathbb{B}$ is put to the test along this route.

One family of frames deserves separate mention, because it is the one in which the algebra contributes more than a bilinear form. The Kerr–Schild metrics are the orbit of the flat form under a shear along a null direction, and the shear's generator is square-zero for the reason the corpus already owns: a null direction is a zero divisor. That single vanishing square is what makes the Kerr–Schild deformation affine in its scalar function, unimodular and invertible in closed form, what keeps its null direction fixed and therefore null, and why the class contains a vacuum plane-fronted wave while the local-scale class contains none. It supplies no dynamics either.

The article closes by separating what has been constructed from what remains an agenda. The separation is stark: the kinematical fibre of tetrad gravity is present, its dynamics is absent, and the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$ has no curved-space treatment at all.

The conventions are inherited from the read-list articles and none is redefined. The biquaternion algebra is $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_t = 1, \vec e_x, \vec e_y, \vec e_z$ satisfying $\vec e_k^2 = -e_t$ and $\vec e_x \vec e_y = \vec e_z$, and scalar imaginary $i$, with $i^2=-1$, commuting with the quaternion units. The anti-Hermitian and Hermitian subspaces are $\mathbb{M}_-$ and $\mathbb{M}_+$, the real-quaternion subspace is $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, and the biquaternion norm is $\langle\tilde{Q}\rangle_{\natural} = \tilde{Q}\tilde{Q}^{\natural}$. The rotor group is $\{\tilde U : \tilde U\tilde U^{\natural} = e_t\} \cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, acting on $\mathbb{M}_-$ by rotor conjugation $\tilde{Q} \mapsto \tilde U\tilde{Q}\tilde U^{*}$, with covering homomorphism $\mathrm{Ad}$ onto $SO^+(1,3)$ and kernel $\{\pm e_t\}$. For two observables of the informational sector the plain form is $\langle\tilde H_1,\tilde H_2\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde H_1\tilde H_2)$, read as the trace pairing $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde H_1\tilde H_2^{*})=2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde H_1\tilde H_2^{*})$. Throughout, $c = 1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$ is the speed of light in the medium and $c_0$ the vacuum speed.

Two conventions govern the indices, and they are stated once. Every spacetime index is low. A repeated Greek label is a sum. The signs of the metric are written, not implied. Where the old pair was a metric contraction — one index up and one down — they are carried by $g_{\mu\nu}$ where $g$ is written, by the object itself where that object was defined by lowering (the dual frame $\tilde F_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\tilde E_\nu$ and the inverse vierbein $e_{\mu a}$ carry $g$ inside them), and otherwise by the diagonal weight of the metric in use: $\eta_{\mu\mu}$ for the Minkowski metric $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, and $\epsilon_\mu$ with $\epsilon_0=1$, $\epsilon_k=-1$ for the C-space interval, whose signature $(+,-,-,-)$ is stated where it is used. Where the old pair was a pairing and not a metric contraction — a coordinate differential against a derivative or a frame, as in $\tilde E_\mu dx_\mu$, $\partial/\partial x_\mu$, and $\hat F_\mu\partial/\partial x_\mu$ — the sum is plain and no sign vector appears. Latin labels $a,b,c,d$ and $i,j,k$ are frame labels, not spacetime indices: they are never lowered by $g$, and, like every other component, they are written low; a frame slot is never raised. Where slots of different kinds meet the order is fixed: in $e_{\mu a}$ the world slot comes first and the frame slot second; in $\omega_{\mu ba}$ the world slot, then the frame row, then the frame column; in $S_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}$ the generator pair, then the row and the column. A dual basis is written with a different letter, never by raising: $\varsigma_\mu$ is dual to $s_\mu$, and $\tilde F_\mu$ is dual to $\tilde E_\mu$.

What the Flat Machinery Assumes

The pointwise metric of the framework is the polar form of the biquaternion norm. For $\tilde{Q}_1 = iq_{1t} + \vec q_1$ and $\tilde{Q}_2 = iq_{2t} + \vec q_2$ in $\mathbb{M}_-$,

$$ \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{Q}_1\tilde{Q}_2^{\natural} + \tilde{Q}_2\tilde{Q}_1^{\natural}\right) = \mathrm{Sc}\left(\tilde{Q}_1\tilde{Q}_2^{\natural}\right) = -q_{1t}q_{2t} + \vec q_1\cdot\vec q_2, $$

a real scalar. Write $\langle \tilde{Q}_1, \tilde{Q}_2\rangle_{\natural}$ for this form; in the basis $(ie_t, \vec e_x, \vec e_y, \vec e_z)$ of $\mathbb{M}_-$ its Gram matrix is $\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, so it is the Minkowski inner product of $\mathbb{M}_- \cong \mathbb{R}^{1,3}$. It is preserved by rotor conjugation,

$$ \langle \tilde U\tilde{Q}_1\tilde U^{*},\ \tilde U\tilde{Q}_2\tilde U^{*} \rangle = \langle \tilde{Q}_1, \tilde{Q}_2\rangle_{\natural}, $$

because the biquaternion norm is invariant and the conjugation action is linear in each argument. This is the entire metric content of the flat framework: one fixed form on one fixed real vector space.

Four assumptions are built into that statement, and each is a flat-space assumption.

1. One algebra, at one point. The framework has no notion of two points. The four-position $\tilde{R}$, the four-velocity $\tilde{V}$, and every other four-vector are elements of $\mathbb{M}_-$; separation between events enters only as a difference $\tilde{Q}_1 - \tilde{Q}_2$, never as a displacement along a path.

2. A biquaternion norm with constant coefficients. The form $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle_{\natural}$ is the same at every point, by construction. Nothing in the algebra can vary it.

3. A global chart with a distinguished time axis. The biquaternionic gradient

$$ \tilde{\nabla} = e_t\,\partial/\partial Q_t + \vec e_x\,\partial/\partial Q_x + \vec e_y\,\partial/\partial Q_y + \vec e_z\,\partial/\partial Q_z $$

requires four global coordinates, and requires in addition that the time direction be the coefficient of $e_t$ — that is, a preferred split of the coordinates into a time function and three spatial functions. The Maxwell and Dirac equations of the companion articles are written with this operator and inherit the requirement.

4. A global symmetry group. The rotor group is the group of units of a fixed algebra. It is a global object. A curved manifold has no global group of that kind acting on it; what it has is a pointwise copy of the group at each point, if the frame is chosen.

The framework is not silent about these limitations. Introduction to the Biquaternion Universe lists the extension to curved spacetime as an open question; Why Complexify Spacetime? observes that the $ict'$ convention "is tied to the existence of a global inertial frame"; and Electromagnetism in Media — The Local Complex Structure at Work states the position precisely: the algebra is "fixed while its embedding in physical spacetime is not." That last clause is the one this article takes seriously, because an embedding that varies from point to point is exactly where curvature would have to live.

Curvature Cannot Be Carried by the Algebra

An algebra has no points, so it cannot have a curvature. The statement is worth making concrete rather than rhetorical. The biquaternion norm on $\mathbb{B}$ is a quadratic form with constant coefficients in a fixed basis; the only freedom in writing it is a change of basis, that is, a linear transformation, and a linear transformation maps a flat form to a flat form. There is no parameter in $\mathbb{B}$ that a field could modulate and no way to make the coefficient of the time direction a function of position. If one wants curvature in this framework, one must supply it in the map that attaches $\mathbb{B}$ to spacetime.

Two forms of that map are available, and they are the two routes examined below:

  • a scale on the imaginary time axis, $ict' \mapsto i\,c(x)\,t'$, which is the framework's own local complex structure; and
  • a frame, $dx_\mu \mapsto \tilde{E}_\mu(x)\,dx_\mu$, which is the tetrad of the standard spinor formulation of general relativity.

They are different devices, and it is a mistake to treat them as the same one. The first makes a piece of the embedding's scale local while leaving the frame rigid; the second makes the frame local while leaving every scale to the frame itself. The first is the framework's proposal and yields too little to be general relativity; the second yields everything kinematically and is no longer specifically biquaternionic.

A second structural point belongs here. General relativity's defining symmetry is diffeomorphism invariance: the theory is formulated on a manifold whose points carry no labels, and the group $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ acts on those points. The algebra $\mathbb{B}$ has no representation of $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ and nothing in it corresponds to a coordinate change on the base. Its algebraic symmetries are the pointwise $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ — a redundancy in the description of a frame, not a symmetry of the manifold — together with the isometries of whatever background is chosen. This is a gap of a different kind from the absence of dynamics: a biquaternion formulation of gravitation in the strict sense would have to relate $\mathbb{B}$ to diffeomorphism invariance, and no such relation has been constructed.

Finally, a piece of representation-theoretic bookkeeping locates where the work would be. Under the Lorentz group the metric and the energy–momentum tensor are symmetric rank-2 objects, $\left(1,1\right)\oplus\left(0,0\right)$ in the $(m,n)$ labelling of the read list, of dimensions $9 + 1 = 10$. The kinematical fields the framework is built from are four-vectors, $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right)$, of dimension $4$, and they lie in $\mathbb{M}_-$. The companion article on the material space already records that the field-strength biquaternion and the energy–momentum biquaternion are not four-vectors and do not lie in $\mathbb{M}_-$. So the objects that would have to become dynamical fields in a theory of gravity are not the objects the framework is organised around. This is bookkeeping rather than impossibility, but it is where the missing formalism would have to be built.

Route One: The Local Scale Factor

The framework's own route to a local structure is the local complex structure. Its content is stated in Introduction to the Biquaternion Universe and developed in Electromagnetism in Media — The Local Complex Structure at Work. In a medium with permittivity $\epsilon$ and permeability $\mu$, the speed of light is $c = 1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$, a property of the medium at each point. The material time coordinate is $ict'$, so the map from physical time to the imaginary scalar direction of $\mathbb{B}$ carries the factor $c$; the imaginary unit $i$ is fixed by the algebra and only the real scale attached to the imaginary axis is local. In the gradient, this means

$$ \partial/\partial Q_t = -\frac{i}{c}\,\partial/\partial t' . $$

The framework's phrasing is that the complex structure is local "in the same spirit as the metric in general relativity", and that the $ict'$ convention of Minkowski space is the vacuum limit $c \to c_0$ of a more general local structure.

It is at this point that a genuine ambiguity appears, and it should be stated before anything is computed from it.

The Two Readings

Reading A, the point map. Take the four-position as the framework writes it, $\tilde{R} = ict'\,e_t + x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$, with the local $c$. Then $c$ is a function of position and

$$ d(ict') = ic\,dt' + i\,t'\,dc, $$

so the displacement biquaternion is $d\tilde{R} = i(c\,dt' + t'\,dc)e_t + d(x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z)$ — still an element of $\mathbb{M}_-$ — and the interval is $ds^2 = \langle d\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}$, whose time sign comes from $i^2=-1$ and nothing else,

$$ ds^2 = -c^2\,d{t'}^2 - 2ct'\,dt'\,dc - {t'}^2 (dc)^2 + dx^2+dy^2+dz^2, $$ $$ dc=\sum_i\frac{\partial c}{\partial x_i}\,dx_i, \qquad ds^2 = -c^2\,d{t'}^2 - 2ct'\,dt'\sum_i\frac{\partial c}{\partial x_i}\,dx_i - {t'}^2\Bigl(\sum_i\frac{\partial c}{\partial x_i}\,dx_i\Bigr)^2 + dx^2+dy^2+dz^2 , $$

with $i$ running over $x,y,z$ and with $c$ independent of $t'$; a time-dependent $c$ replaces $c$ by $c+t'\,\partial c/\partial t'$ in the $e_t$ coefficient and in $\det g$. The metric so obtained is nondegenerate — $\det g = -c^2$ in the coordinates $(t',x,y,z)$ — but it is flat. The map $(t',x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z) \mapsto i\,c(x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z)\,t'\,e_t + x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$ is a diffeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^4$ onto $\mathbb{M}_-$ (its Jacobian determinant is $c > 0$), and it is pulling back the flat form of $\mathbb{M}_-$; a pullback of a flat form along a diffeomorphism is flat, and no curvature can be produced by reparametrising a flat space. Direct computation confirms it: for $c = 1 + \tfrac{1}{10}(x^2+y^2+z^2)$ and, on a second and independent case, for $c = 2 + \sin x\,\cos y$, every component of the Riemann tensor vanishes. The local scale factor, read as a map of points, changes the coordinate description of flat spacetime and nothing else.

Reading B, the derivative rule. Take instead the framework's actual usage, in which $c$ enters through the operator $\partial/\partial Q_t = -(i/c)\partial/\partial t'$ and the four-position is written with a single complex coordinate $ict'$ rather than with a position-dependent coefficient. Equivalently, hold $c$ fixed in the differential: $d(ict') = ic\,dt'$. Then

$$ ds^2 = -c(x,y,z)^2\,d{t'}^2 +dx^2+dy^2+dz^2, \qquad g_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}\left(-c(x,y,z)^2,\ 1,\ 1,\ 1\right), $$

and this metric is genuinely curved whenever $c$ is not affine in the spatial coordinates.

The two interpretations differ exactly by the terms containing $dc$, and they are not equivalent: one is flat and the other is not. Neither Introduction to the Biquaternion Universe nor Electromagnetism in Media — The Local Complex Structure at Work chooses between them, because the statements about the local $c$ are made at the level of the operator while the statements about the four-position are made at the level of the point. A reader can reasonably take either. This is left open here rather than resolved, because resolving it is a decision about what the framework means and not a computation.

What the Restricted Class Cannot Do

Suppose Reading B is intended. Its curvature can be computed in closed form. The components below are quoted in the corpus's curvature convention — the convention of Curvature and Geodesics, in which $R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y\nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$ and the unit two-sphere has positive scalar curvature, and the convention of the linearized-gravity, gravitational-wave and graviton-quantization articles. Writing $u = c = 1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$ and allowing $u$ to depend on time as well as position,

$$ \mathrm{Ric}_{00} = u\,\Delta u, \qquad \mathrm{Ric}_{0i} = 0, \qquad \mathrm{Ric}_{ij} = -\frac{\partial^2 u/\partial x_i\partial x_j}{u}, $$

where $\Delta$ is the three-dimensional Laplacian and the indices $i,j$ run over the spatial directions. Equivalently, with $w = c^2$, $\mathrm{Ric}_{00} = \left(2w\,\Delta w - |\nabla w|^2\right)/(4w)$ and $\mathrm{Ric}_{ij} = \left(-2w\,\partial^2 w/\partial x_i\partial x_j + (\partial w/\partial x_i)(\partial w/\partial x_j)\right)/(4w^2)$. For example $c = 2 + \sin x$ gives scalar curvature

$$ \mathrm{Ric} = \frac{2\sin x}{2 + \sin x}, $$

which is $+\tfrac{2}{3}$ at $x = \pi/2$ and vanishes at $x = 0$ and $x = \pi$.

The class is narrow in a way that is easy to state: one free function of the spatial coordinates (or of time as well), no shift vector, and a flat three-metric on the spatial slices, $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$. It is the diagonal, spatially rigid sub-case of the frame route of the next section.

Its decisive limitation is the following. Within this class, Ricci-flatness forces flatness. The condition $\mathrm{Ric}_{ij} = 0$ requires $\partial^2 u/\partial x_i\partial x_j = 0$ for every pair of spatial indices, so $u = \vec a(t')\cdot(x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z) + b(t')$ is affine in the spatial coordinates; then $\mathrm{Ric}_{00} = u\,\Delta u$ vanishes automatically, so this is the whole Ricci-flat family. And every member of that family is flat: for $u = \vec a(t')\cdot(x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z) + b(t')$ with arbitrary functions $\vec a(t')$ and $b(t')$, the Riemann tensor vanishes identically, as direct symbolic computation of the general case confirms. The family is the algebra's way of writing a uniformly accelerated (Rindler-type) frame, and it is a reparametrisation of Minkowski space.

The consequence is worth stating plainly, in the restricted sense in which it holds. A metric that the local-scale route can write down at all cannot be a non-flat Ricci-flat spacetime. It therefore cannot carry a vacuum gravitational field with Weyl curvature, and so it cannot carry a vacuum gravitational wave or a vacuum black-hole exterior. This is a statement about a metric class, not a theorem about the biquaternion programme — but it is enough to show that the local scale factor of the imaginary time axis is not the route by which general relativity will enter the framework.

There is a further conceptual gap along this route, independent of the calculation. The $c$ in $1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$ is the speed of light in a material medium — a property of a dielectric, measurable with a capacitor and a magnet. The standard effective-geometry literature (Gordon's metric and its modern descendants in transformation optics) already attaches a metric to a dielectric medium, and that metric is a metric for light: it reproduces the ray trajectories of Maxwell's equations in the medium, not the free-fall trajectories of test masses. Identifying the medium's effective metric with the spacetime metric is a further step, and the framework's "in the same spirit as the metric in general relativity" is an analogy rather than that step. Nothing in the framework supplies it.

A wider class exists, and vacuum depends on the spatial leg. The class above is what one free function writes by itself, but a frame field is not confined to it: allowing the radial leg of the frame to vary along with the time leg widens the class, and a spatially stretched $c$-metric is its natural member. An external programme takes exactly that step. A. Waser reads gravitation as the effect of a spatially varying speed of light and fixes the profile by its own force law: setting $F_g=-mc\,\nabla c=-m\nabla(c^2/2)$ equal to Newton's $-GMm/r^2$ gives the profile, and the model then works with the line element,

$$ c=c_0 f(r),\qquad f(r)=\exp(-r_s/(2r)),\qquad r_s=\frac{2GM}{c_0^2}, $$

$$ ds^2=-f(r)^2 c_0^2\,d{t'}^2+\frac{dr^2+r^2\,d\Omega^2}{f(r)^2}. $$

The profile agrees with the local-scale route's $c^2=c_0^2(1-r_s/r)$ to first order in $r_s/r$, and the inverse squaring of the spatial part is a separate posit. At the profile the author actually derives, $f^2=\exp(-r_s/r)$, the computation returns a non-vanishing Ricci tensor, with

$$ \mathrm{Ric}_{00}=0,\qquad \mathrm{Ric}_{11}=-\frac{r_s^2}{2r^4},\qquad \mathrm{Ric}=-\frac{r_s^2\,e^{-r_s/r}}{2r^4}, $$

in the corpus's curvature convention, all three vanishing as $r\to\infty$. Truncated at first order, $f^2=1-r_s/r$, the line element is the isotropic weak-field form $ds^2=-(1-r_s/r)c_0^2d{t'}^2+(1+r_s/r)(dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2)$; that expansion is the Schwarzschild exterior to first order in $r_s/r$, and it is Ricci-flat to that order. The family's own line element with the truncated profile left unexpanded, $ds^2=-(1-r_s/r)c_0^2d{t'}^2+(dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2)/(1-r_s/r)$, is a different metric and is not Ricci-flat: it has $\mathrm{Ric}_{00}=-r_s^2/(2r^4)$ and $\mathrm{Ric}_{11}=-r_s^2/\bigl(r^2(r-r_s)^2\bigr)$. The classical tests the paper then computes are those of the first-order truncation.

The same computation settles what the instance can and cannot show. Within Waser's family, where the spatial part is forced to follow the time profile, the Ricci tensor has only two independent components,

$$ \mathrm{Ric}_{00}\propto rff''-r{f'}^2+2ff',\qquad \mathrm{Ric}_{11}\propto rff''-3r{f'}^2+2ff', $$

the other two follow from these, $\mathrm{Ric}_{22}=(r^2/f^4)\,\mathrm{Ric}_{00}$ and $\mathrm{Ric}_{33}=\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm{Ric}_{22}$, so $\mathrm{Ric}_{\mu\nu}=0$ forces ${f'}^2=0$: the inverse-squared spatial part does not restore a non-flat vacuum member, it removes it. Free the radial leg instead, keeping the same profile $f^2=1-r_s/r$,

$$ ds^2=-f^2c_0^2\,d{t'}^2+\frac{dr^2}{f^2}+r^2\,d\Omega^2 , $$

and the line element is exactly the Schwarzschild exterior and exactly Ricci-flat. That contrast is the instance's real content. The metric class can hold a vacuum solution, but only as its two-function member, with the spatial leg chosen freely; the one-function member, in which the spatial leg follows the time leg, cannot. And in either case the profile is fixed by fitting Newton's law, not by the algebra. (The programme's one prediction not shared with general relativity is a screening of the static field, sized in The Empirical Status of the Biquaternion Framework.)

Route Two: A Field of Frames

The general way to make the embedding of spacetime into the algebra local is to allow the coordinate differentials to be carried into $\mathbb{M}_-$ by a point-dependent frame:

$$ d\tilde{R} = \sum_\mu \tilde{E}_\mu(x)\,dx_\mu, \qquad \tilde{E}_\mu(x) \in \mathbb{M}_- . $$

The interval is $ds^2 = \langle d\tilde{R}\rangle_{\natural}$, and since $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the polar form evaluated on $d\tilde{R} = \sum_\mu\tilde{E}_\mu dx_\mu$,

$$ \boxed{\;g_{\mu\nu}(x) = \langle \tilde{E}_\mu(x), \tilde{E}_\nu(x)\rangle_{\natural} = \mathrm{Sc}\left(\tilde{E}_\mu \tilde{E}^{\natural}_\nu\right)\;}. $$

Four $\mathbb{M}_-$-valued fields, so sixteen functions. Where they are linearly independent, the Gram matrix is nondegenerate, and by Sylvester's law it has signature $(3,1)$: the result is automatically a Lorentzian metric. Conversely, any Lorentzian metric can be written this way locally. Choose a $g$-orthonormal frame, map it into $\mathbb{M}_-$ by a linear isometry of quadratic spaces — which exists because both forms have signature $(3,1)$ — and read off the coordinate components. So there is no obstruction: the framework can carry any curved metric.

What makes the route more than a change of notation is the local symmetry. If $\tilde U(x)$ is a unit-norm biquaternion depending on position, then

$$ \tilde{E}_\mu \longmapsto \tilde U\tilde{E}_\mu\tilde U^{*} $$

leaves $g_{\mu\nu}$ unchanged at every point, because rotor conjugation preserves the bilinear form. So the local gauge group supplied by the algebra is exactly a copy of $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ at each point, with the double cover, the Lie algebra, and the $\left(m,n\right)$ representation theory of the read list applying pointwise. This is the standard tetrad or vierbein structure of the spinor formulation of general relativity, written in the algebra's own notation; the interplay of the sixteen frame functions with the six local rotor parameters leaves $16 - 6 = 10$ independent components, which is the number of components of $g_{\mu\nu}$.

The corresponding flatness statement is exact and instructive. If the frame is pure rotor gauge, $\tilde{E}_\mu = \tilde U(x)\,u_\mu\,\tilde U(x)^{*}$ for a fixed basis $u_\mu$ of $\mathbb{M}_-$, then $g_{\mu\nu}(x) = \langle u_\mu, u_\nu\rangle_{\natural}$ is a constant matrix, and the metric is flat. A point-dependent rotor field applied to a rigid basis produces no curvature whatever. Curvature in this route is exactly that part of the frame which is not of this pure-gauge form, and no amount of rotor-field machinery detects it.

Two honest qualifications keep this from sounding like more than it is.

First, carrying is not deriving. The frame field is inserted by hand, and it is subject to no condition from the algebra. Every Lorentzian metric, including every vacuum spacetime, is representable; the framework excludes nothing. That is not a success but an emptiness: the entire content of the construction is the sixteen functions, and the algebra contributes only a fixed four-dimensional real vector space with a form of signature $(3,1)$, which any such space would contribute equally. Beyond that one quadratic form, nothing that distinguishes $\mathbb{B}$ is used: writing $g_{\mu\nu} = \langle \tilde{E}_\mu, \tilde{E}_\nu\rangle_{\natural}$ calls on the biquaternion norm and on nothing else — not the quaternionic multiplication, not the two-sector decomposition, not the complex structure.

Second, this route does not make the complex structure local. The frame field is a real linear map at each point; the imaginary direction of $\mathbb{B}$ remains globally the coefficient of $e_t$ in a fixed basis. The framework's local complex structure and the tetrad's local frame are different devices, and neither contains the other: the frame route gives general relativity's kinematics and abandons the framework's own locality proposal, while the local-scale route keeps that proposal and cannot reach even a non-flat Ricci-flat vacuum. This tension is a finding of this article, not a problem it resolves. One thing the external record shows is that the frame language, rather than the algebra, is where the tension lives: a construction recorded below, under An External Claim That the Bundle Basis Is Biquaternionic, carries the same metric with no vierbein at all — the basis is four $\mathbb{M}_-$-valued fields $s_\mu$ themselves — and derives its connection from a condition on the basis rather than inserting one.

The Kerr–Schild Form: a Null Shear of the Flat Form

Route Two carries any Lorentzian metric, and its verdict is that the algebra contributes nothing beyond a bilinear form. There is one family of frames in which it contributes a little more, and it happens to be the family in which the exact solutions the corpus's neighbouring articles work with are written. It is the Kerr–Schild form,

$$ g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + 2h\,k_\mu k_\nu, \qquad \sum_\mu\eta_{\mu\mu}k_\mu k_\mu = 0, $$

with $k_\mu$ a null covector and $h$ a scalar function, both otherwise free. Kerr, Kerr–Newman and Schwarzschild are all of this shape, with $k_\mu$ the repeated principal null direction of the congruence the solution is built on.

The claim of this section is exact and narrow. The family is the orbit of the flat form under a shear along a null direction; the shear's generator is square-zero because a null direction is a zero divisor; and the properties usually listed one at a time for the family are the faces of that single vanishing square. Nothing is claimed about content: $h$ and $k_\mu$ stay free, and no equation of the framework fixes them.

One convention is needed for the components, and it is the one the index rules impose: every index stays low, and where a component would otherwise be raised the diagonal weight carries the sign. The rigid material frame of the Dirac section, $\tilde E_0 = ie_t$ and $\tilde E_k = \vec e_k$, has $\langle\tilde E_\mu,\tilde E_\nu\rangle_\natural = \eta_{\mu\nu}$, so the datum $k_\mu$ names an element of $\mathbb{M}_-$,

$$ \tilde k = \sum_\mu \eta_{\mu\mu}k_\mu\,\tilde E_\mu \in \mathbb{M}_-, \qquad \langle\tilde E_\mu,\tilde k\rangle_\natural = k_\mu, \qquad \langle\tilde k\rangle_\natural = \sum_\mu \eta_{\mu\mu}k_\mu k_\mu = 0 , $$

and the null covector is exactly the element of vanishing norm. Normalised this way, $\tilde k$ makes the shear below reproduce $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + 2h\,k_\mu k_\nu$ with no weight anywhere, which is the standard Kerr–Schild form. (The neighbouring article The Algebrodynamical Programme writes the same deformation as $\eta_{\mu\nu} + h\,k_\mu k_\nu$, so its profile is half of this one.)

The Operator a Null Element Defines

The vanishing of the norm is the corpus's own criterion for a zero divisor, and the light cone is the zero-divisor cone (The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone). What the Kerr–Schild form uses is not the element but one operator built from it,

$$ N_{\tilde k} : \mathbb{M}_- \to \mathbb{M}_-, \qquad N_{\tilde k}(X) = \tilde k\,\langle X,\tilde k\rangle_\natural , $$

a real-linear map of $\mathbb{M}_-$ into itself, the scalar $\langle X,\tilde k\rangle_\natural$ being real on $\mathbb{M}_-$. Its square vanishes,

$$ N_{\tilde k}^{\,2}(X) = \tilde k\,\langle X,\tilde k\rangle_\natural\,\langle\tilde k\rangle_\natural = 0 , $$

and the only step worth naming is the one that makes it vanish: $\langle X,\tilde k\rangle_\natural$ is a scalar, hence central, so it passes through $\tilde k$ and leaves the norm behind. The trace carries the same condition, $\mathrm{tr}\,N_{\tilde k} = \langle\tilde k\rangle_\natural = 0$, and the rank is one.

The distinction from left multiplication, the map the algebra supplies for nothing, must also be kept. For an element of $\mathbb{M}_-$ that map is never square-zero unless $\tilde k = 0$: a null $\tilde k = iq_t + \vec q$ with $q_t \ne 0$ satisfies $\tilde k^2 = 2iq_t\tilde k$, which is the Cayley–Hamilton identity $M^2 = (\mathrm{tr}\,M)M$ of the $2\times2$ image at vanishing determinant, while $q_t = 0$ forces $\vec q = 0$ by the null condition, so the only nilpotent element of $\mathbb{M}_-$ is zero. Nilpotent null elements do exist in the algebra at large — $\vec e_x + i\vec e_y$ has square zero and vanishing norm — but they are not material elements, their spatial coefficients being complex. The operator that is square-zero for every null direction of $\mathbb{M}_-$ is the rank-one $N_{\tilde k}$, and the distinction is the one The Square-Zero Element and The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone already draw between the square-zero cone and the zero-divisor cone. The Kerr–Schild shear is built on the second operator, not the first.

The Frame It Produces, and Four Properties That Are One

The frame is the rigid frame carried by the shear, and the shear is the exponential of the square-zero operator: $T = \exp(hN_{\tilde k}) = \mathrm{id} + hN_{\tilde k}$, the series terminating at the linear term. On the rigid frame,

$$ T\tilde E_\mu = \tilde E_\mu + h\,\tilde k\,\langle\tilde E_\mu,\tilde k\rangle_\natural = \tilde E_\mu + h\,k_\mu\,\tilde k , $$

and the metric is Route Two's Gram matrix of the result. The computation is one line, and its two cross terms are the same term twice:

$$ \langle T\tilde E_\mu,T\tilde E_\nu\rangle_\natural = \eta_{\mu\nu} + 2h\,k_\mu k_\nu + h^2 k_\mu k_\nu\langle\tilde k\rangle_\natural = \eta_{\mu\nu} + 2h\,k_\mu k_\nu . $$

The four properties usually listed for the class are the single vanishing $N_{\tilde k}^2 = 0$, read in four places.

  • The deformation is affine in $h$. The quadratic term is $k_\mu k_\nu\langle\tilde k\rangle_\natural$ and it vanishes with the null condition, so the family is a straight line through $\eta$ and not a nonlinear orbit. This is what makes the class an ansatz at all, and it is the null condition read at second order.
  • The metric is unimodular. $T$ is unipotent, $\det T = \exp(h\,\mathrm{tr}\,N_{\tilde k}) = 1$, hence $\det g = (\det T)^2\det\eta = \det\eta$. In this chart $\sqrt{-\det g} = 1$, and the Laplace–Beltrami operator of the Dirac section loses its measure factor. Unimodularity is the tracelessness of $N_{\tilde k}$, which is the null condition read as a trace.
  • The inverse is the flat inverse with the sign of the correction reversed. $T^{-1} = \exp(-hN_{\tilde k}) = \mathrm{id} - hN_{\tilde k}$, so the metric of the inverse frame is $\eta_{\mu\nu} - 2h\,\eta_{\mu\mu}\eta_{\nu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu$: the flat inverse array with the correction's sign reversed, the two weights being the ones the index rules put where the correction would otherwise carry raised labels. The square, once more.
  • The direction is fixed, and it stays null. $N_{\tilde k}\tilde k = \tilde k\langle\tilde k\rangle_\natural = 0$, so $T\tilde k = \tilde k$: the shear does not move the null element at all. The norm $\langle\tilde k\rangle_\natural$ carries no frame, so $\tilde k$ is null for the deformed metric exactly as it was for the flat one. At the level of arrays, the inverse array returns from $k_\mu$ the vector $\eta_{\mu\mu}k_\mu$ that the flat array returns, because its correction is $-2h\,\eta_{\mu\mu}k_\mu$ times the vanishing scalar $\sum_\nu\eta_{\nu\nu}k_\nu^2$; nullity is what makes the two agree, not an approximation. That is why the congruence of a Kerr–Schild solution is carried into the curved metric unchanged.

All four were recomputed here on a hundred random null directions with four random values of $h$ each — the affineness, $\det g = \det\eta$, the closed-form inverse array, $T\tilde k = \tilde k$, and nullity in the deformed metric — together with the column form $T\tilde E_\mu = \tilde E_\mu + hk_\mu\tilde k$ and the kernel $\tilde k^{\perp}$ fixed pointwise; no residual exceeded $10^{-9}$. Unipotence was checked in its defining form, $(T - \mathrm{id})^2 = h^2N_{\tilde k}^2 = 0$, which holds to $10^{-13}$; the spectrum $\{1\}$ follows from it, whereas an eigenvalue solver returns $1$ only to $10^{-7}$, the usual loss of half the digits on a Jordan block.

What the Shear Leaves Alone

Since $N_{\tilde k}$ has rank one, its kernel is the hyperplane orthogonal to $\tilde k$,

$$ \ker N_{\tilde k} = \left\{ X \in \mathbb{M}_- : \langle X,\tilde k\rangle_\natural = 0 \right\} = \tilde k^{\perp}, $$

so $T$ fixes $\tilde k^{\perp}$ pointwise and shears only along the one remaining direction. In the real slice, where $k_\mu$ and $h$ are real and $g$ is a real Lorentzian metric, that is the whole geometric content: the shear is the identity on the degenerate three-plane that contains the null direction, and it moves nothing else.

Complexified, the statement is sharper, and the complexification is the algebra's own. $\mathbb{M}_-$ is a real four-plane: its elements are $\sum_\mu\eta_{\mu\mu}k_\mu\tilde E_\mu$ with real $k_\mu$, and its null cone is the ordinary light cone, in which the largest totally null subspace has dimension one. But $i\mathbb{M}_- = \mathbb{M}_+$ — the mark $*$ is conjugate-linear, so $(i\tilde Q)^{*} = -i\tilde Q^{*} = i\tilde Q$ for $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{M}_-$ — and therefore $\mathbb{M}_-\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{C} = \mathbb{M}_-\oplus i\mathbb{M}_- = \mathbb{B}$. In that complex four-space the norm is complex and a null element lies in exactly two maximal totally null planes, each two-dimensional and both inside $\tilde k^{\perp}$; they are the left and right null planes that the Kerr–Schild literature separates. Since the shear is the identity on the whole of $\tilde k^{\perp}$, it fixes both planes point by point, so the null planes of the congruence are null for the curved metric without approximation. That is the algebraic content of the property the Kerr–Schild literature uses as a working tool.

A Constant Direction, and a Vacuum Wave

With $h$ and $k_\mu$ constant, $T$ is a constant invertible map and $g$ is $\eta$ in the chart $y^\alpha = T^\alpha{}_\mu x^\mu$; the metric is flat. The remark is small, and it marks a limit of Route Two's flatness criterion: a frame that is pure rotor gauge is flat, but the converse fails. A constant shear is not a rotor, and it produces no curvature, because it is a change of chart rather than a change of frame. Flatness of the frame has at least these two independent sources, and the second is a diffeomorphism, the object the algebra does not have.

With $k_\mu$ constant and $h$ varying, the same family is curved, and its member is the wave the neighbouring articles meet as a gravitational wave. Take $k_\mu$ with $k_0 = 1$ and $k_3 = 1$, null since $-1+1=0$, and pass to the null coordinates $u = t'+z$ and $v = t'-z$. The metric reads

$$ ds^2 = -du\,dv + dx^2 + dy^2 + 2h(u,x,y)\,du^2 , $$

and its Ricci tensor has a single independent component, in the curvature convention of Curvature and Geodesics used throughout this article,

$$ \mathrm{Ric}_{uu} = -\Bigl(\frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 h}{\partial y^2}\Bigr), $$

all other components vanishing. The metric is therefore Ricci-flat exactly when $h$ is harmonic in the two transverse coordinates. For $h = x^2-y^2$ and for $h = e^x\cos y$ the Ricci tensor vanishes and the Riemann tensor does not, so both are vacuum solutions; for constant $h$ the Riemann tensor vanishes, as the chart argument requires. These are plane-fronted gravitational waves with a constant null direction, written in the algebra's own notation, with one free harmonic function.

The contrast with Route One deserves a sentence, because it is the sharpest in the article. Within the local-scale class Ricci-flatness forces flatness, so that route carries no vacuum gravitational wave at all. The frame route carries one, and the Kerr–Schild sub-class carries one with a constant direction and a single harmonic function. What neither carries is a reason for the function: $h$ is inserted, and the algebra supplies the shape of the ansatz and the four identities that shape entails, and nothing else.

What the Instance Is Worth

The instance is worth its algebra, and no more.

It is worth the shape of the ansatz. Route Two counts sixteen frame functions modulo six rotor parameters; the Kerr–Schild sub-class fixes the frame once and for all to be the rigid frame carried by one null shear, and the metric that results is governed, in a fixed chart, by three functions of position — the direction of $k_\mu$ up to scale and the amplitude $h$. Three functions rather than ten is an economy, not a content: all three remain free.

It is worth the four identities, which the literature states one at a time and which are one line here, and the unimodularity in particular, because in a Kerr–Schild chart $\sqrt{-\det g} = 1$, so the Laplace–Beltrami operator inside the Lichnerowicz square of the Dirac section carries no measure factor at all.

What it is not worth is dynamics. That a member is Ricci-flat is a property of the inserted function, checked and not produced. And the two objects that would carry the physical content sit outside the algebra: for the Kerr–Newman solution the null direction is the principal null congruence selected by the Kerr theorem, and $h$ is fixed by the Einstein–Maxwell equations. Both belong to the corpus elsewhere — the congruence to Congruence Twistors and the Fields They Generate and to The Algebrodynamical Programme, the ring the solution carries to The Complex-Displaced Singularity and the Circle Electron — and in both the algebra enters as the language of the congruence, not as the source of the equation. On this sharper instance the section therefore repeats the conclusion of Route Two: carrying is not deriving.

Route Three: The Connection, and the Two-Sided Derivative

A frame field is not by itself a geometry; a manifold also needs a way to compare frames at neighbouring points, that is, a connection. Here the biquaternion algebra makes a genuine and positive contribution, and the contribution has a trap in it that is worth recording.

The positive part is dimensional. The Lie algebra of the rotor group is the traceless part of $\mathbb{B}$,

$$ \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathbb{R}} = \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\} \oplus \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\} = \left\{ G \in \mathbb{B} : G + G^{\natural} = 0 \right\}, $$

the elements with no scalar part, real or imaginary; six real dimensions, with the rotation generators $\vec e_k$ and the boost generators $K_k = i\vec e_k$ of the read list. Since this Lie algebra is a subspace of $\mathbb{B}$, a connection 1-form and its curvature 2-form can both be carried as $\mathbb{B}$-valued objects — specifically, as objects valued in that six-dimensional subspace. The spin connection of the tetrad formalism is therefore not foreign to the algebra; it sits inside it.

The trap concerns the covariant derivative. The group acts on the material sector by the two-sided formula $\tilde{Q} \mapsto \tilde U\tilde{Q}\tilde U^{*}$, so the infinitesimal action of a generator $G$ is

$$ \tilde{Q} \longmapsto \tilde{Q} + \epsilon\left(G\tilde{Q} + \tilde{Q}G^{*}\right) + O(\epsilon^2), $$

which is not in general the commutator $[G,\tilde{Q}]$. The two agree exactly when $G^{*} = -G$, which is the case for the rotation generators $\vec e_k$; for the boost generators $i\vec e_k$, which are Hermitian, they do not agree. On $\tilde{Q} = iq_t + q_x\vec e_x + q_y\vec e_y + q_z\vec e_z$, the boost generator $G = i\vec e_x$ acts to first order as

$$ q_t \longmapsto q_t - 2\epsilon q_x, \qquad q_x \longmapsto q_x - 2\epsilon q_t, $$

mixing the time and $\vec e_x$ components as a boost must, whereas $[G,\tilde{Q}]$ is $2\epsilon(-iq_z\,\vec e_y + iq_y\,\vec e_z)$, a rotation in the $\vec e_y \vec e_z$ plane — a different transformation altogether. A biquaternionic covariant derivative must therefore be written in the two-sided form

$$ \boxed{\;D_\mu \tilde{Q} = \partial/\partial x_\mu \tilde{Q} + \vec\Gamma_\mu \tilde{Q} + \tilde{Q}\vec\Gamma_\mu^{*}\;}, $$

with $\vec\Gamma_\mu$ in the Lie subspace; the natural abbreviation $D_\mu = \partial/\partial x_\mu + [\vec\Gamma_\mu, \cdot]$ would be wrong for exactly the boosts, which is where the Lorentzian content of the theory lives.

One object in this neighbourhood is automatic and one is not. The automatic one is the logarithmic derivative of a rotor field: if $\tilde U(x)$ is unit-norm, then both $\tilde U^{\natural}\,\partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tilde U$ and $\partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tilde U\,\tilde U^{\natural}$ are traceless, hence lie in the Lie algebra, because $\tilde U^{\natural}\tilde U = e_t$ differentiates to zero. So a frame carried by a rotor field arrives with a natural $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$-valued connection. But it is pure gauge, and its curvature vanishes — which is the same statement as the flatness of the rotor-field metric in the previous section, arrived at from the other direction. Consistency, not new content.

What is not automatic is everything one would want. Metric compatibility and the vanishing of torsion are conditions, not consequences; nothing in the algebra selects the Levi-Civita lift. And the whole dynamics is absent: there is no action, no field equation for $\tilde{E}_\mu$ or $\vec\Gamma_\mu$, and therefore no Einstein equation. The algebra can hold the objects of the spin-connection formalism in the same notation in which it holds the rotors and the four-vectors. It does not generate a single equation for them.

The Arena Alternative: Algebra Valued Coordinates

The three routes above accept the article's premise and look for the map that attaches $\mathbb{B}$ to a manifold. There is a fourth position, and it is the only one that attacks the premise instead: put the algebra in the coordinates, so that the arena itself is not a manifold. It is worth naming here, because it is the sharpest existing answer to the sentence from which this article began — the algebra has no points — and because a reader who meets that sentence should know that the answer has been tried.

The construction is extended relativity in Clifford spaces, whose principal review is by Castro and Pavšič (2004). The coordinates are themselves Clifford valued; for a four-dimensional base one writes, in the reference-frame cells — with $L_a\psi=a\psi$ and $R_b\psi=\psi b$, so that $L_aR_b$ is the cell $\psi\mapsto a\psi b$, and $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ the bivector cell $\tfrac12[V_\mu,V_\nu]$, where $V_0=iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ and $V_k=iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ are the four vector cells of the Gamma Formulation —

$$ X = \sigma\,1 + \bigl(x_0\,iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}-\sum_k x_k\,iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}\bigr) + \tfrac12 x_{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu} + \bigl(\check x_0\,iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}-\sum_k \check x_k\,iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}\bigr) + \check\sigma I , $$

with $I$ the pseudoscalar of the C-space Clifford algebra, one coefficient for each grade — scalar, vector, bivector, pseudovector, pseudoscalar — so that a "point" carries a line, an area, a volume and a hyper-volume, and the arena is called a Clifford space (C-space). The invariant interval generalizes accordingly, $|dX|^2 = d\sigma^2 + \sum_\mu\epsilon_\mu\,dx_\mu dx_\mu + \tfrac12\sum_{\mu,\nu}\epsilon_\mu\epsilon_\nu\,dx_{\mu\nu}dx_{\mu\nu} - \sum_\mu\epsilon_\mu\,d\check x_\mu d\check x_\mu - d\check\sigma^2$, the signs of the last two terms being fixed by the square of the volume element, $I^2 = -1$ in signature $(+,-,-,-)$; and the transformations that preserve it generalize the Lorentz transformations by mixing objects of different grade, so that a history of one dimension is reshuffled into a history of another.

The trade is exact, and it should be stated as a trade rather than as a solution. What the construction buys is the opposite of what this article has been recording: the extended objects — closed strings, membranes, $p$-branes — are not fields on the arena, they are among its coordinates, so a single action covers all $p$; and a fundamental length, the Planck scale, has a natural place to enter, as the parameter that bridges grades. What it costs is the manifold. The coordinates are non-commuting polyvectors, so there is no space of points, and distance, connection, dimension and even signature have to be rebuilt — the review's own proposal is that the signature is relative to a chosen slice through C-space, which is a notion the corpus does not have and does not need (see the remark on signature in The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars). Two things must not be conflated: a C-space is an algebra of coordinates, while the corpus's $\mathbb{B}$ is an algebra of fields and observables; the corpus's algebra is the even part of a four-dimensional Clifford algebra, and the review's arena is a manifold of Clifford numbers.

The honest summary is that this is a different framework, not an extension of the one developed here, and that it does not fill this article's gap. The missing dynamics is missing in both: C-spaces supply a richer arena and a unified action for free extended objects, but the corpus's boundary — the biquaternion framework contains the kinematical fibre of tetrad gravity and none of its dynamics — is neither crossed nor refuted by moving the algebra into the coordinates. What the position does establish is that the sentence the algebra has no points is a statement about $\mathbb{B}$ as this framework uses it, not a theorem about algebras. The empirical status of the scale that C-spaces make natural is recorded in The Empirical Status of the Biquaternion Framework.

The Biquaternion Dirac Equation on a Curved Background

The connection of the previous section was built for the material sector, where the field is a four-vector and the group acts by the two-sided rotor conjugation. The Dirac field is a different kind of object — a section of the spinor module, the Peirce carrier $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi$ (a minimal left ideal here) — and the difference in the kind of field, together with the difference in the kind of action, is exactly what the curved Dirac equation turns on. This section writes that equation in the framework's own notation. It is the item the agenda below records as the curved-space form of the biquaternion Dirac equation.

Frame note. The operator below is built from the material frame $\tilde E_\mu\in\mathbb{M}_-$ of Route Two, indexed by the physical coordinates $x_\mu=(t',x,y,z)$. The cell transcription uses the informational frame $\hat F_\mu$, built from $\hat e_0=e_t$ and $\hat{\vec e}_k=i\vec e_k$. The two frames are related but not interchangeable: the time leg of the material frame is $ie_t$, the time leg of the informational frame is $e_t$, and only the informational frame has legs that contract with the four generator cells weighted by $e_{\mu a}$. The relation is stated in the Lorentz-gauging section, and the two flat limits are computed below.

The One-Sided Spinor Derivative

The Lorentz group acts on the spinor module by left multiplication, $\psi \mapsto \tilde U\psi$, not by rotor conjugation; the companion article The Spinor Module in Biquaternionic Form and Its Lorentz Action establishes this, and it is the algebraic difference between a four-vector and a spinor. The covariant derivative compatible with that action is therefore one-sided,

$$ D_\mu\psi = \partial/\partial x_\mu\,\psi + \vec\Gamma_\mu\psi, \qquad \vec\Gamma_\mu \in \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathbb{R}} \subset \mathbb{B}, $$

which is not the two-sided prescription of the material sector, $D_\mu\tilde{Q} = \partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tilde{Q} + \vec\Gamma_\mu\tilde{Q} + \tilde{Q}\vec\Gamma_\mu^{*}$. The two must not be conflated. The vector carries the transformation twice, once on each side, $\tilde{Q} \mapsto \tilde U\tilde{Q}\tilde U^{*}$, and so receives two connection terms; the spinor carries it once, $\psi \mapsto \tilde U\psi$, and so receives one. Both connection terms live in the same six-dimensional Lie subspace, and each is the standard inhomogeneous connection of the module it belongs to.

Covariance fixes the transformation law. If $\psi \mapsto \tilde U\psi$, then $D_\mu\psi \mapsto \tilde U D_\mu\psi$ precisely when the connection transforms as

$$ \vec\Gamma_\mu \longmapsto \tilde U\vec\Gamma_\mu\tilde U^{-1} - \left(\partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tilde U\right)\tilde U^{-1}, $$

which is the inhomogeneous transformation expected of a connection on a module. A rotor field supplies an example at once: $\vec\Gamma_\mu = -\left(\partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tilde U\right)\tilde U^{-1}$ is pure gauge, and its curvature vanishes, exactly as the rotor-carried frame of the previous sections is pure gauge and flat.

The Curved Dirac Operator and the Equation

The biquaternion gradient of the flat articles is $\tilde\nabla = e_t\,\partial/\partial Q_t + \sum_k\,\vec e_k\,\partial/\partial Q_k$, with $Q_t=ict'$. In the physical coordinates $x_\mu=(t',x,y,z)$ it reads $$ \tilde\nabla = -i\,e_t\,\partial/\partial t' + \sum_k\,\vec e_k\,\partial/\partial x_k, $$ because $\partial/\partial t'=i\partial/\partial Q_t$ in units with $c=1$; the two imaginary units multiply to a sign, $(-i)\cdot i=+1$. Its curved replacement substitutes the frame field for the rigid material frame and the one-sided derivative for the partial derivative. The rigid material frame is not the algebra basis $e_\mu=(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$, which is not contained in $\mathbb{M}_-$; it is $$ \tilde E_0=ie_t,\qquad \tilde E_k=\vec e_k, \qquad \tilde F_0=-ie_t,\qquad \tilde F_k=\vec e_k, $$ with $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. The curved object is

$$ \tilde F_\mu D_\mu, \qquad \tilde F_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\tilde E_\nu, \qquad \langle \tilde F_\mu, \tilde E_\nu\rangle_{\natural} = \delta_{\mu\nu}, $$

where $\tilde F_\mu$ is the dual frame, the inverse tetrad of the frame field $\tilde E_\mu$ of Route Two. The operator is biquaternion-valued and first order, and it is the object the whole tetrad machinery was assembled to define.

The massless member. The massless equation is

$$ \tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu}\psi = 0, $$

identical in form to the flat massless equation. On the spinor module the representative is a cell, but not the coefficient matrix of left multiplication by $\tilde F_\mu$: that matrix squares with a vector cross term left over. It is the Clifford cell $e_{\mu a}\,\mathsf G_a$, built from the four generator cells of The Gamma Formulation, whose four matrices close on the weights of the metric, $\{\mathsf G_a,\mathsf G_b\}=2g_{ab}I_4$, and whose square is the scalar operator below; in the rigid frame it is the cell Dirac operator $\mathbb D$ of the parent article.

The massive pair. The massive equation is the chiral pair, for the two halves $\tilde\Psi_R$ and $\tilde\Psi_L$ of the Dirac field of the parent article. Its two lines are displayed separately because they are the two chiral halves of one field, not one equation written twice:

$$ \boxed{\;\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu}\tilde\Psi_R = m\tilde\Psi_L\;} $$

$$ \boxed{\;(\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu})^{\natural}\tilde\Psi_L = m\tilde\Psi_R\;} $$

where $(\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu})^{\natural}$ is the quaternion-conjugate operator: $\natural$ is an anti-automorphism, so it reverses the order of the factors, and the conjugate frame and the conjugate connection appear in the opposite order. It reduces to $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$ when the frame is rigid and the connection vanishes. The mass term is unchanged: it is the field-independent, chirality-off-diagonal pairing of the parent, and the curvature enters only through the derivative.

The one-sided pair and the two-sided equation. The pair above is the corpus's one-sided formulation: the operator acts by left multiplication on the field in the Dirac module $\Delta=S\oplus S^{\mathrm c}$, and the mass is the off-diagonal pairing $\beta:S\times S^{\mathrm c}\to\mathbb{C}$ of the two chiral halves. The two-sided equation is the Kravchenko form on $\mathbb{B}$, in which the derivative acts from the left and the mass term acts from the right through $\vec e_z$, the componentwise conjugation supplying the antilinear part; it is a change of representation, not a second law, and the parent article compares the two in The One-Sided and the Two-Sided Equation. The mass is not a right multiplication in the one-sided pair: a right multiplication acts within each chiral half, whereas the mass is the chirality-off-diagonal pairing. The right multiplication appears in the two-sided dictionary, where it represents the same pairing. Only the one-sided pair is constructed here; the two-sided dictionary has not been extended to a curved background.

Flat Limit and the Square

Two consistency statements locate the construction.

The flat limit is exact. Take the rigid material frame $\tilde E_0=ie_t$, $\tilde E_k=\vec e_k$, with $\tilde F_0=-ie_t$, $\tilde F_k=\vec e_k$ and $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, and take $\vec\Gamma_\mu=0$. Then $$ \tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu}=\tilde F_{\mu}\partial/\partial x_\mu =-i\,e_t\,\partial/\partial t'+\sum_k\,\vec e_k\,\partial/\partial x_k =e_t\,\partial/\partial Q_t+\sum_k\,\vec e_k\,\partial/\partial Q_k=\tilde\nabla, $$ where the last equality uses $Q_t=ict'$ and $\partial/\partial t'=i\partial/\partial Q_t$, and the pair reduces to the flat pair of the parent article. The rigid frame is not $e_\mu=(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$: $e_t$ is not in $\mathbb{M}_-$, and a term-by-term replacement of $\tilde E_\mu$ by $e_\mu$ is invalid. Nor is it the informational frame: in the same limit $\hat F_\mu\partial/\partial x_\mu=e_t\partial/\partial t'+i\sum_k\,\vec e_k\partial/\partial x_k=i\tilde\nabla$; the biquaternion object that represents the source's differential operator is therefore $i\tilde\nabla$, not $\tilde\nabla$, and the cell transcription is not the gradient. The curved material operator is a strict generalisation: nothing is added at flatness, and nothing of the flat structure is lost.

The square is the Lichnerowicz formula. Squaring the Clifford cell of the Dirac operator gives

$$ \boxed{\;(\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu})^2 = -\Box_g + \tfrac14 \mathrm{Ric}\;}, $$

Here $\mathrm{Ric}$ is the Ricci scalar and $\Box_g = g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-\det g}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_\mu}\!\left(\sqrt{-\det g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial/\partial x_\nu\right)$ is the Laplace–Beltrami operator acting on spinors, the inverse metric being the one object written with raised indices because it is the inverse and not a component; so the boxed identity is the standard Lichnerowicz formula $\nabla^{*}\nabla + \tfrac14\mathrm{Ric}$ written with $\nabla^{*}\nabla = -\Box_g$, the form of Spin Geometry. The overall sign is fixed by the flat limit. The cells carry the weight $g_{ab}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, which is $-\eta_{ab}$: they square to $+I_4$ on the time leg and to $-I_4$ on each spatial leg. The rigid square is therefore $\partial^2/\partial t'^2-\Delta=-\Box_g$, and the curvature term must carry the opposite sign to it, which is the sign displayed. The identity was recomputed here for this article on a conformal metric in two dimensions, in both signatures, with the connection taken from the frame: $(\mathsf G_a e_a{}^\mu D_\mu)^2=-g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu+\mathrm{Ric}/4$ holds identically in the geometer's convention, in which the cells square to minus the norm.

Write $S^{cd}=\tfrac14[\mathsf G^c,\mathsf G^d]$ for the raised spin generator. Varying the gauged action gives the covariant Dirac operator $$ E_{\mathrm{grav}} = \bigl(i\,e^\mu{}_a\,\mathsf G^a\,\partial/\partial x_\mu-m\bigr)\psi +\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(\nabla_\mu e^\mu{}_a\bigr)\mathsf G^a\psi +\tfrac{i}{4}e^\mu{}_a\,\omega_{\mu cd}\{\mathsf G^a,S^{cd}\}\psi , $$ while substituting the same replacement into the flat equation of motion gives $$ \tilde E_{\mathrm{grav}} = \bigl(i\,e^\mu{}_a\,\mathsf G^a\,\partial/\partial x_\mu-m\bigr)\psi +\tfrac{i}{2}e^\mu{}_a\,\omega_{\mu cd}\mathsf G^aS^{cd}\psi , $$ and the two do not differ by an anticommutator. The anticommutator coefficient therefore matches the variation's $\tfrac{i}{4}$, and the difference is the commutator term alone: $$ E_{\mathrm{grav}}-\tilde E_{\mathrm{grav}} =\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(\nabla_\mu e^\mu{}_a\bigr)\mathsf G^a\psi -\tfrac{i}{4}e^\mu{}_a\,\omega_{\mu cd}[\mathsf G^a,S^{cd}]\psi =\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(\nabla_\mu e^\mu{}_a\bigr)\mathsf G^a\psi -\tfrac{i}{2}e^\mu{}_b\,\omega_\mu{}^b{}_a\mathsf G^a\psi =i\bigl(\nabla_\mu e^\mu{}_a\bigr)\mathsf G^a\psi , $$ where the middle equality uses $[\mathsf G^a,S^{cd}]=g^{ac}\mathsf G^d-g^{ad}\mathsf G^c$ and the antisymmetry of $\omega_{\mu cd}$, and the last uses the source's frame identity $\tfrac{i}{2}(\nabla_\mu e^\mu{}_a)\mathsf G^a=-\tfrac{i}{2}e^\mu{}_b\omega_\mu{}^b{}_a\mathsf G^a$. Both surviving terms are grade one: the trivector parts of the two equations agree, and the mismatch is a vector connection term — the derivative of the frame — not a trivector. The world-index repair. The same source then constructs a formalism in which the two procedures commute again, and the construction differs from the corpus's in a way worth stating. The Informational Sector on a Curved Background

The second half of the framework — $\mathbb{M}_+$, its idempotents, its observables and the plain form $\langle\tilde H_1,\tilde H_2\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde H_1\tilde H_2)$, read as the trace pairing — has no curved-space treatment, and it is worth saying why the absence is structural rather than a matter of effort.

When the spacetime is curved, the elements of $\mathbb{M}_+$ would have to become fields of operators, one copy of the algebra being attached at each point. That much is formal: the fibre is defined and the pointwise operator algebra goes through unchanged. What does not go through is the trace. The trace in the trace formula is the $2 \times 2$ matrix trace at a point; it has no volume element and no integration. Every trace in a field theory is an integral over the manifold, with a measure that itself depends on the metric, and the framework's trace cannot play that role. So even the Born rule, which is the most successful piece of the flat construction, has no constructed curved-space analogue here: the pointwise expectation value survives, the global one has nowhere to live.

The two-sector decomposition $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{M}_+ \oplus \mathbb{M}_-$ is a pointwise decomposition and survives as such on any background. Beyond that, the curved setting is untouched. Any coupling between the sectors would have to be an equation of motion for fields belonging to both, and no such equation exists in the flat case either; a fortiori none exists here.

The connection is derived, not inserted. This is the part of the construction that goes beyond the frame route of this article. The source's covariant derivative of the basis is $D_\rho s_\nu = \nabla_\rho s_\nu + \omega_\rho s_\nu + s_\nu\bar{\omega}_\rho^*$ with $\nabla_\rho s_\nu = \partial/\partial x_\rho s_\nu - \Gamma_{\mu\nu\rho}s_\mu$, and where $\varsigma_\mu$ is the dual basis, $\langle\varsigma_\mu,s_\nu\rangle=\delta_{\mu\nu}$; the connection coefficients are then determined, not chosen, by the source's "minimality condition" $D_\rho s_\nu \equiv 0$:

$$ \Gamma_{\mu\nu\rho} = \left\langle \varsigma_\mu,\ \partial/\partial x_\rho s_\nu + \omega_\rho s_\nu + s_\nu\bar{\omega}_\rho^*\right\rangle . $$

The bracketed element lies in $\mathbb{M}_-$ — it is a sum of the $\mathbb{M}_-$-valued derivative $\partial/\partial x_\rho s_\nu$ and of terms $\omega_\rho s_\nu + s_\nu\bar{\omega}_\rho^*$, which combine into an $\mathbb{M}_-$ element — and the bilinear form is real on $\mathbb{M}_-$, so $\Gamma_{\mu\nu\rho}$ is real-valued; this, together with the metric-compatibility computation below, was recomputed here. Two consequences deserve naming. First, metric compatibility is a consequence, not a condition: $\nabla_\rho g_{\mu\nu} = 0$ follows from the minimality of $\Gamma$ together with the reality condition on $\omega_\mu$, whereas Route Three above records metric compatibility and the vanishing of torsion as conditions the framework must impose from outside. Second, $\Gamma_{\mu\nu\rho}$ need not be symmetric under the exchange of its two last labels — the source states this, and the asymmetry is exactly what makes $\nabla_\mu A_\nu - \nabla_\nu A_\mu$ differ from $\partial/\partial x_\mu A_\nu - \partial/\partial x_\nu A_\mu$ for the connection's own $\mathrm{U}(1)$ part.

The curvature, and the Lagrangian the source leaves unsettled. The field strength of the single connection is $\Omega_{\rho\sigma} = \nabla_\rho\omega_\sigma - \nabla_\sigma\omega_\rho + [\omega_\rho,\omega_\sigma]$, and the minimality condition turns the double commutator of covariant derivatives into the identity

$$ [\nabla_\rho,\nabla_\sigma]s_\mu + \Omega_{\rho\sigma}s_\mu + s_\mu\bar\Omega_{\rho\sigma}^* = 0 , $$

whose last two terms are the same two-sided sandwiching — left action of $\Omega$, right action of its conjugate — that Route Three's derivative $\partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tilde Q + \vec\Gamma_\mu\tilde Q + \tilde Q\vec\Gamma_\mu^{*}$ exhibits. With the connection split as $\omega_\mu = \chi_\mu + igA_\mu$, where $\chi_\mu \in \mathbb{C}\otimes\mathrm{Vec}(\mathbb{H})$ and $A_\mu \in \mathbb{R}$, the field strength splits as $\Omega_{\mu\nu} = K_{\mu\nu} + igF_{\mu\nu}$ with $K_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\chi_\nu - \nabla_\nu\chi_\mu + [\chi_\mu,\chi_\nu]$ and $F_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu A_\nu - \nabla_\nu A_\mu$; both the split and the term-by-term decomposition were verified here. The scalar piece of the one connection therefore produces a Maxwell-type field strength and the vector piece a Yang–Mills-type curvature. The source then proposes an Einstein–Hilbert-like Lagrangian, $(1/\kappa)\mathrm{Lag} = \mathrm{Riem}_{\mu\nu\mu\nu}(\Gamma)$ with $\mathrm{Riem}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\Gamma) = \langle \varsigma_\mu,[\nabla_\rho,\nabla_\sigma]s_\nu\rangle$, and an alternative $(1/\kappa)L = \mathrm{Re}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\eta_{\mu\mu}\eta_{\nu\nu}\langle\Omega_{\mu\nu},\Omega_{\mu\nu}\rangle$, and judges both unsettled; both are recorded, with the reason for each verdict, in The Einstein Field Equations under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda.

Weight of the instance. A constructed instance is more than the bundle claim above, and it should be counted as what it is. It shows that the biquaternionic basis is not idle: four elements of $\mathbb{M}_-$ determine a Lorentzian metric through the algebra's own bilinear form, and one algebra-valued connection suffices for the tensor and spinor fields alike. It does not show more. There is no settled action for $s_\mu$ or $\omega_\mu$ and no field equation; the Riemann tensor of the induced connection and its identity with the field strength are written, but nothing is varied and no dynamics is attached to them, so the metric is general while its dynamics are absent, which is the same void this article records for the frame route. Nor is it a construction of the principal bundle $P(G,M)$ of the first source above: the basis is a basis of the algebra over the manifold, not the basis of a principal bundle with a structural group. The instance therefore raises the bundle claim's floor — from proposal to construction — without raising its ceiling.

The honest summary of the boundary is this. The biquaternion framework contains the kinematical fibre of tetrad gravity: a pointwise Lorentzian vector space, its Lorentz group, the vector representation, and the Lie-algebra-valued connection. It contains nothing of tetrad gravity's dynamics, and nothing that selects a metric. It is therefore not correct to say that the framework contains general relativity, and it is not correct to say that it conflicts with it. The Gamma Formulation

The gammas generate $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$; they are not a different algebra. Summary

The read-list machinery is flat and pointwise: one fixed algebra, one fixed biquaternion norm with constant coefficients, a global chart with a distinguished imaginary time, and a global rotor group. Curvature cannot be made a property of the algebra, because the algebra has no points and no deformable coefficient; it can only be carried by the field that attaches the algebra to spacetime.

The framework's own local device is the local scale $c = 1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$ of the imaginary time axis. It admits two inequivalent interpretations. As a map of points, $\tilde{R} = i\,c(x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z)\,t'\,e_t + x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$, it yields a nondegenerate metric with $\det g = -c^2$ that is identically flat — the pullback of the flat form of $\mathbb{M}_-$ along a diffeomorphism — as direct computation confirms. As a derivative rule, $\partial/\partial Q_t = -(i/c)\partial/\partial t'$ with $c$ held fixed in the differential, it yields $g_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-c^2,1,1,1)$, genuinely curved, with $\mathrm{Ric}_{00} = u\Delta u$, $\mathrm{Ric}_{0i} = 0$, $\mathrm{Ric}_{ij} = -(\partial^2 u/\partial x_i\partial x_j)/u$ for $u = c$. The framework's prose does not choose between the interpretations, and the choice is left open here.

Within the second interpretation's class — one function, no shift, flat spatial slices — Ricci-flatness forces $u$ to be affine in the spatial coordinates, and every such metric is flat. The class therefore contains no non-flat vacuum geometry: no Weyl curvature, no gravitational waves, no black-hole exteriors. The local scale factor is not the route to general relativity. Nor is it the same object as the metric: $c$ is the Maxwell speed of a medium, and the standard effective metric of a dielectric is a metric for light, not for free fall.

A general curved metric can be carried, by a frame field $\tilde{E}_\mu(x) \in \mathbb{M}_-$ with $g_{\mu\nu} = \langle \tilde{E}_\mu, \tilde{E}_\nu\rangle_{\natural}$, since any Lorentzian metric has a local orthonormal frame. The algebra then supplies the pointwise $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, its vector representation, and its Lie algebra; the sixteen frame functions modulo six local rotor parameters reproduce the ten components of the metric; and a frame that is pure rotor gauge is flat, a condition that is sufficient and not necessary, so curvature can only come from the part of the frame that is neither a rotor gauge nor a change of chart. The algebra can even carry the connection and its curvature, since the Lie algebra is the six-dimensional traceless subspace of $\mathbb{B}$ — with the caveat that the infinitesimal action is the two-sided $G\tilde{R} + \tilde{R}G^{*}$, not the commutator, the two differing precisely for the boosts.

One sub-class of frames is worth separating because the algebra contributes more to it than a bilinear form, and it is the Kerr–Schild class, $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + 2h\,k_\mu k_\nu$ with $\sum_\mu\eta_{\mu\mu}k_\mu k_\mu = 0$. A null element is a zero divisor, and the operator built from it, $N_{\tilde k}(X) = \tilde k\langle X,\tilde k\rangle_\natural$, is square-zero and traceless for that reason alone; the frame is the rigid frame carried by the unipotent shear $\exp(hN_{\tilde k}) = \mathrm{id} + hN_{\tilde k}$, and $g$ is its Gram matrix. The affineness of the deformation in $h$, the unimodularity $\det g = \det\eta$, the inverse array $\eta_{\mu\nu} - 2h\,\eta_{\mu\mu}\eta_{\nu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu$, and the direction's being fixed and still null are four faces of that vanishing square; the shear is the identity on the three-plane orthogonal to $\tilde k$, and therefore on both totally null planes of the complexification, which is why the null planes of the congruence survive unchanged. With a constant direction and a transverse harmonic $h$ the class contains a vacuum plane-fronted wave, so the frame route carries a vacuum gravitational wave that the local-scale route cannot; with $h$ and $k_\mu$ both constant it is flat, which shows that the rotor criterion for flatness is sufficient and not necessary, since a constant shear is a change of chart rather than a change of frame. None of it is dynamics: the direction and $h$ are three free functions, and for Kerr–Newman they are fixed by the Kerr theorem and the Einstein–Maxwell equations, not by the algebra.

The Dirac field lives on a different module from the four-vector, and its curved equation has a different derivative. The spinor module carries the left action $\psi \mapsto \tilde U\psi$, not the two-sided rotor conjugation, so its covariant derivative is the one-sided $D_\mu\psi = \partial/\partial x_\mu\,\psi + \vec\Gamma_\mu\psi$, with $\vec\Gamma_\mu$ in the Lie subspace; covariance fixes the inhomogeneous transformation $\vec\Gamma_\mu \mapsto \tilde U\vec\Gamma_\mu\tilde U^{-1} - (\partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tilde U)\tilde U^{-1}$. With the dual frame $\tilde F_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\tilde E_\nu$ this gives the curved biquaternion Dirac operator $\tilde F_\mu D_\mu$, whose massless equation is $\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu}\psi = 0$ and whose massive form is the same chiral pair as in the flat case, $\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu}\tilde\Psi_R = m\tilde\Psi_L$, $(\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu})^{\natural}\tilde\Psi_L = m\tilde\Psi_R$, the mass being untouched by the curvature. The operator reduces exactly to $\tilde\nabla$ in the rigid material frame $\tilde E_0=ie_t$, $\tilde E_k=\vec e_k$ with vanishing connection, and its spinor-module square is the Lichnerowicz formula $(\tilde F_{\mu} D_{\mu})^2 = -\Box_g + \tfrac14 \mathrm{Ric}$, so the curvature couples to the field through the Ricci scalar even without a source. That much of the curved matter equation is now constructed in the framework's own notation. What remains is unchanged and unyielding: the frame and the connection are inserted by hand and satisfy no equation, the metric is not selected, the current that would source the coupled system is the spinor-module object carrying $iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ outside $\mathbb{B}$, and the global existence of spinor fields is a topological condition no local operator can see.

What is missing is not a technical detail but the theory. Nothing determines the frame, the connection, or the metric; there is no action, no field equation, and no Einstein equation; the gravitating rank-2 tensors are not the framework's four-vectors; diffeomorphism invariance has no algebraic counterpart; global and topological structure is outside a pointwise algebra; and the informational sector's trace formula is a fibre trace with no measure and no integral, so its curved-space extension is not merely unwritten but unlocated. The framework contains the kinematical fibre of tetrad gravity and none of its dynamics; the title names a framework, and that is exactly what it is.

## The Root Set of Zero Is a Cone

**A signed root set is a level set; the root set of $0$ is a cone.** For each structure the equation
$\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_t$ with $\lambda\neq0$ cuts a level set of the square, while
$\lambda=0$ cuts the zero set of the same function, and the zero set of a quadratic function is a cone: it
is closed under scaling, it contains the origin and it sets a direction and no magnitude. The signed
values set a magnitude, the value $0$ sets a direction, and that difference is the first physical fact of
the three named values. The root set of $0$ is the **square-zero set**
$\{\tilde\Upsilon:\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0\}$, origin included, and it is the same set as the
nilpotent set of the structure.

**In the plain product, the pure isotropic cone.** The plain square of a pure element is
$-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)e_t$, so the nonzero roots of $0$ are the pure elements with
$(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$. Written with real vectors as $\tilde\Xi=\vec q+i\vec v$, the equations
are $\vec q^{2}=\vec v^{2}$ and $\vec q\cdot\vec v=0$; the root set is the **pure isotropic
cone** $\{\Xi_0=0,\ \Xi_x^{2}+\Xi_y^{2}+\Xi_z^{2}=0\}$, of real dimension four, and it is one component of
the plain cone $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$. The other component is the non-pure cone, the multiples of
the projectors, and the two components meet at the origin alone. The physical interpretation is the lightlike
displacement, the solution set of the wave equation, and the owner is *The Light Cone as the Biquaternion
Zero-Divisor Cone*.

**In the natural product, the whole cone.** The natural square is the central square
$C(\tilde\Xi)e_t=\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi$, so the equation is the vanishing of the complex norm and
the root set is the **whole cone** $\mathcal{C}=\{C=0\}$, of real dimension six. The natural product
reads masslessness completely, and the three shells of the interval are the root sets of the three named
values: the shell $C=-1$, the cone $C=0$ and the shell $C=+1$.

**In the probability band, the origin alone.** The scalar part of the square is
$H=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$, positive definite, so the only root of $0$ is $\tilde\Xi=0$: the band
has no nonzero root of $0$, hence no lightlike element, while almost every element of the band is
non-invertible. A probability is non-invertible for the reason that it is a probability, not because it
is lightlike.

**In the gauge band, a proper subfamily of the cone.** The fourth product's square vanishes on the
subfamily $\{\overline{\tilde\Xi}\tilde\Xi=0\}$, a proper subfamily of the cone containing $e_t+i\vec e_x$ and
missing $ie_t+\vec e_x+\vec e_y+i\vec e_z$. In the parametrisation $\tilde\Xi=c(\vec u+it)$ the square is the central

description are both recorded in the mathematics companion, which owns the set. The owner of the
indefinite metric is *The Biquaternioid General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (GQS) over $\mathbb{C}$*.

**In the four antisymmetric structures.** The square of the two alternating brackets vanishes
identically, so the whole space is the root set of $0$; the square of the two conjugate-alternating
brackets is a pure vector, and its vanishing admits the unit $e_t$, whose bracket square vanishes there,
so their root set of $0$ lies outside the cone and contains $e_t$.

**A signed root is a unit and a nonzero root of $0$ is a zero divisor.** The central square is the
criterion of invertibility: $C\neq0$ is invertible and $C=0$ is a zero divisor. A signed root has
$C=\pm1\neq0$ and is a unit; a nonzero root of $0$ has $C=0$ and is not. The three values are the two ends

**The two sub-families inside the cone.** The cone of roots of $0$ splits as the cone itself does. The

**The failure is the definition, not a defect.** A root of $0$ is a zero divisor, so the value $0$ is the

## A Square-Zero Element Is an Infinitesimal

**The equation and the self-annihilation.** The equation $\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0$ has a

**The physical interpretation.** A nonzero element whose square vanishes is an **infinitesimal** object:

**The square-zero set is the lightlike set of its band.** The physical content of the twelve square-zero

band answers is whether a lightlike object exists in it at all.

## The Twelve Structures at the Roots of Zero

### $\mathrm{GPA}$, the general plain algebra — composition

The product is the plain product and its square is the plain square. The root set of $0$ is the pure

### $\mathrm{GPS}$, the general plain sesqualgebra — probability

The product is the pairing and its square is the Hermitian square, of scalar part

the probability band has no lightlike element. The reading is *Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the

### $\mathrm{GQA}$, the general quaternionic algebra — causality

The product is the natural product and its square is the central element $C(\tilde P)e_t$; the equation is

is self-annihilating, and the set coincides with the zero-divisor set, origin included. The reading is

### $\mathrm{GQS}$, the general quaternionic sesqualgebra — gauge

The product is the fourth product and its square is $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde P^{*}$, equivalent to
$\overline{\tilde P}\tilde P$. The root set of $0$ is the subfamily $\{\overline{\tilde P}\tilde P=0\}$,

### $\mathrm{SPA}$, the symmetric plain algebra — commutativity

The product is the Jordan product and its square is the plain square, so the root set of $0$ is the pure
isotropic cone, element for element that of $\mathrm{GPA}$. The same displacements are square-zero, and
they are zero divisors of the symmetrised block in addition. The reading is *The Specificities of the Symmetric Plain Algebra*.

### $\mathrm{SPS}$, the symmetric plain sesqualgebra — positivity

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation and its square is $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde P^{*})e_t$,

### $\mathrm{SQA}$, the symmetric quaternionic algebra — coupling

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation and its square is $C(\tilde P)e_t$, so the root set of $0$

### $\mathrm{SQS}$, the symmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — metric

The product is the symmetric part of the fourth product and its square is the coefficientwise real part of

### $\mathrm{APA}$, the antisymmetric plain algebra — rotation

The product is the commutator $\vec P\times\vec Q$, alternating, so its square vanishes identically.

### $\mathrm{AQS}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — polarisation

## The Gap: a State Is Not a Lightlike Displacement

The mathematics companion states the gap between the two sets: a vanishing square is an annihilating
factor, so the square-zero set without the origin lies in the zero-divisor set of every structure, but the
converse fails for nine of the twelve structures. The two sets agree, origin included, exactly for the
three structures whose square vanishes identically or is the central square: the natural product and the
two alternating brackets.

The physical interpretation is the distinction between the two kinds of non-invertible element. A zero divisor is
any operation that cannot be undone, and a square-zero element is one that cannot be undone **because it
is infinitesimal**: it is a lightlike displacement. The plain product shows the gap in the sharpest way.
Its zero-divisor set is the whole punctured cone, its square-zero set is the pure part alone, and the
complement is the non-pure cone, the multiples of the projectors, which are the **states**. A state is
therefore a zero divisor that is **not** square-zero: it cannot be composed, but it is not a lightlike
displacement, and it is idempotent, $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$, rather than nilpotent,
$\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$. The two equations are the two extreme quadratic equations of the algebra, and the
two objects they define are the two physical objects of the pair of companion articles: the state in
*Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$* and the lightlike displacement here. The owner of the
physical locus of the zero divisors is *Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form*, and the
reading of the cone as the massless locus is *The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$*.

The physical statement is that **the set of the non-invertible operations is strictly larger than the set
of the lightlike ones**, and the difference is made of the states. In the probability band the gap is
maximal in the other direction: the zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space and the square-zero set
is the origin alone, so no element of that band is lightlike, although almost every element is
non-invertible. The physical content is that a probability is non-invertible for the reason that it is a
probability and not because it is lightlike.

## The Fermionic Ladder: Nilpotency as Statistics

The nilpotents are the square-zero elements, and the corpus assigns them two physical jobs. The first is
the lightlike displacement, read in the light cone articles. The second is exact and less expected: the
square-zero class contains the fermionic ladder operators of a single mode. Separating the two jobs is the
content of this section.

**The lightlike displacement.** The material pure nilpotents $Q_t=0$, $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$ are the
lightlike directions, and the natural product squares every cone element to zero. The physical particle is
the photon, and *The Null Four-Momentum: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not* fixes how far
the identification goes. A nilpotent has a direction and no magnitude; its square vanishing is the
algebra's statement that a null displacement is not accumulated, which is the vanishing of the interval of
a null vector.

**The fermionic ladder.** The one-mode ladder of *Fock Space and Creation/Annihilation Operators in

$$
\vec\Upsilon_x=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y),\qquad \vec\Upsilon_y=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y),
$$

and a direct computation gives

$$
\vec\Upsilon_x^{2}=\vec\Upsilon_y^{2}=0,\qquad
\vec\Upsilon_x\vec\Upsilon_y=\tilde\Pi_+,\qquad
\vec\Upsilon_y\vec\Upsilon_x=\tilde\Pi_-,\qquad
\{\vec\Upsilon_x,\vec\Upsilon_y\}=e_t,

with $\tilde\Pi_+=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z)$ and $\tilde\Pi_-=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z)$ the vacuum and occupied

application of the same step gives nothing, which is exactly the algebraic form of the statement that a
mode cannot be occupied twice. The vacuum is the product of the two steps,
$\tilde\Pi_+=\vec\Upsilon_x\vec\Upsilon_y$, so the state and the ladder are not independent objects:
the state is what the nilpotent pair creates. *The Spin–Statistics Theorem in Biquaternionic Form* fixes
the anticommutator, and *The Dirac Sea, Hole Theory and Antimatter in Biquaternionic Form* gives the
two-projector picture of the mode, which the pair $\tilde\Pi_+,\tilde\Pi_-$ is in the algebra.

**Why the ladder is the nilpotent one.** *The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent* records that
the algebra contains no bosonic ladder: no pair $\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger\in\mathbb{B}$ can satisfy
$[\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger]=e_t$, because a commutator has vanishing trace while
$\mathrm{Tr}(e_t)=2$. The trace argument selects the anticommutator, hence the fermionic statistics, for
this algebra's ladder, and the nilpotent class is the class that can carry it. The nilpotent article
therefore belongs beside the idempotent article for a reason sharper than a shared position on the cone:
the state is the idempotent and the ladder is the nilpotent, and the two are the two halves of one mode.

**A nilpotent generates a truncated flow.** A nilpotent has no higher powers, so its exponential truncates
at first order: $\exp(t\tilde\Upsilon)=e_t+t\tilde\Upsilon$. A nilpotent is therefore an **infinitesimal**
generator whose one-parameter group is a straight line rather than a circle, in contrast with a root of
$-1$, whose exponential is a full rotation. This is the algebraic form of the distinction between a
translation and a rotation, and it is why the nilpotent is read as a displacement: the flow it generates
does not curve, and its square vanishes because two successive infinitesimal steps compose to a first-order
object and no more.

**Two homes for square zero.** The square-zero elements of $\mathbb{B}$ are not the only square-zero
objects of the framework. *Grassmann Coherent States in Biquaternionic Form* shows that the fermionic
parity of a field is not an element of $\mathbb{B}$ at all and requires an odd extension
$\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathcal{G}$, whose odd generators are square-zero and anticommute. Those generators are
odd, and every element of $\mathbb{B}$ is even, so the two square-zero classes are different objects: the
nilpotents of $\mathbb{B}$ are even lightlike displacements and ladder steps, and the Grassmann generators
are odd labels of a Fock space. The word "nilpotent" without the algebra is ambiguous, and the subject of
this article is the even class.

## The Odd Nilpotents: Cohomology, Supercharge and the Dirac Cone

The square-zero elements of $\mathbb{B}$ are even. The corpus's physics also uses square-zero objects
that are **odd**, and those live in the extensions $\mathbb{B}\otimes\Lambda$ and
$\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathcal{G}$. The two classes share the equation $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ and nothing else, and the odd
class is the engine of cohomology, of supersymmetry and of the topological invariants of Dirac matter.

**A nilpotent differential needs an associative product.** *BRST Symmetry in Biquaternionic Form*
introduces a Grassmann-odd derivation $s$ with $s^{2}=0$, the BRST charge $\mathcal{Q}$ with $\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0$, and
identifies the physical states with the cohomology $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\ker \mathcal{Q}/\mathrm{im}\,\mathcal{Q}$.
The article's own point is where the nilpotency comes from: $s^{2}=0$ rests on the Jacobi identity of the
gauge algebra, and in the framework that Jacobi identity is a consequence of the **associativity** of
quaternion multiplication. The associativity of the parent product is therefore what makes a nilpotent
differential available, and the cohomology is then the annihilator of the nilpotent quotiented by its
image: the physical states are exactly the states a nilpotent operator does not reach, with the reachable
ones removed. This is the annihilator reading of *Annihilators in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$* sharpened
by one more structure, and the three brackets of the corpus that fail the Jacobi identity — read in *A
Higher Structure for the Jacobi Failure* — are precisely the ones that cannot carry such a differential.
A bracket that does not close is a symmetry without a cohomology.

**The supercharge and the Dirac operator.** *Supersymmetric Quantum Physics in the Biquaternion
Framework* uses the pair $\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}$ with

$$
\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0,\qquad (\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger})^{2}=0,\qquad \{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}\}=H,
$$

and argues that the biquaternion first-order operator is the natural supercharge: the Dirac operator
squares to the scalar d'Alembertian, $\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\Box$, exactly as the
anticommutator of a supercharge is the Hamiltonian. The nilpotent $\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0$ is again an odd object, and
the framework supplies the operator, the module and the grading while the odd coordinates are adjoined.
The even nilpotents of this article and the odd nilpotents of the supersymmetric construction therefore
sit on the two sides of one boundary: the even class is a lightlike displacement inside $\mathbb{B}$, and
the odd class is a derivative whose vanishing square is what makes a cohomology exist.

**The Dirac cone and the null direction.** *Dirac Matter: Graphene, Dirac Cones and Topological
Insulators in Biquaternionic Form* reads the massless low-energy excitations of graphene and of a
topological-insulator surface as a two-component spinor with a linear dispersion, and the framework names
the three objects involved: the spinor, the $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\vec p$ kinetic term and the
mass term that opens the gap. The **Dirac point** is the momentum at which the dispersion is linear and
the gap closes, and the masslessness of that point is the vanishing of the interval. The nilpotent of
this article is the momentum element at that point: a lightlike momentum with
$\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=0$, the algebraic form of the linear branch. The article's caution applies here too: the
framework transcribes the low-energy equation, and the lattice, the Fermi velocity and the topological
invariant are material data and not algebraic output.

**The null points come in pairs.** A null direction of the momentum symbol is a **zero** of the symbol,
and on a lattice the zeros are counted by topology. *Fermion Doubling and the Nielsen–Ninomiya Theorem in
Biquaternionic Form* states the count: the Fourier transform of the inverse propagator is a continuous
vector field on the Brillouin zone, its isolated zeros are the particle species, and the index of the field
at each zero is $\pm1$, the two signs being the two handednesses. Poincaré–Hopf makes the sum of the
indices the Euler characteristic of the Brillouin zone, which for a four-torus is zero, so the null points
cannot be removed one at a time: they appear in left–right pairs. The article's caution belongs to the
reading — the biquaternion algebra is not a lattice and does not supply one — so the statement is about
the discretised symbol, and what it says is that the square-zero set of a lattice symbol is
**topologically paired** and not the property of a single isolated mode.

**Nilpotency is why the Wick theorem is exact.** *The Wick Theorem in Biquaternionic Form* observes that
for a **single fermionic mode** the theorem is not an approximation or a statement about vacuum
expectation values in a large space: because the mode operators are square-zero,
$\vec\Upsilon_x^{2}=\vec\Upsilon_y^{2}=0$, every product of more than two mode operators collapses, so
the four-point and six-point contractions reduce to products of the single two-point contraction with the
fermionic signs. The identity is then an **exact operator identity** in a four-real-dimensional subalgebra,
proved by exhibiting both sides. Nilpotency is therefore what makes the fermionic contraction **terminate**:
the collapse of every product beyond the second is the algebraic reason the contractions are finite in number,
and the one-mode graph expansion has no infinite series to resum.

**The single statement.** A vanishing square is the algebra's widest structural equation: the same
equation that makes a lightlike displacement an infinitesimal makes a BRST charge a cohomological
differential and a supercharge a supersymmetry generator. What differs is not the equation but the
grading of the carrier. The even carrier is a displacement inside the algebra; the odd carrier is a
derivative of an extension; and both are read by the square because the square is the one operation that
tests whether a second application adds anything.

**The hole is not a nilpotent, but the sea's inertness is.** *The Dirac Sea, Hole Theory and Antimatter in
Biquaternionic Form* draws the boundary of the nilpotent reading in the theory of the vacuum. In Dirac's
hole theory the negative-energy states are all occupied, and a **hole** is the absence of one of them: its
charge, momentum and energy follow by subtraction from the filled sea, and it is a positron. A hole is
therefore not an element with a vanishing square; it is a state difference from the sea, and the article's
conclusion is that the framework sits on the modern **reinterpretation** side, where the vacuum is empty and
the negative-frequency modes are antiparticle creation operators, so that there is no sea at all — the
filled sea survives only as a heuristic and as a real object in condensed matter, where an empty valence-
band state is a hole. What the exclusion principle does supply is the **inertness** of the sea — the reason
a positive-energy electron cannot descend into a filled negative-energy state — and that inertness is the
algebra's nilpotency, since two applications of a mode operator vanish. The reading to keep is therefore
sharp: nilpotency is what a mode operator does, not what a hole is.

**The nilpotent mode is native, and the transformation to it is an identity.** *The Jordan–Wigner
Transformation in Biquaternionic Form* relates a spin-$\tfrac12$ chain to a chain of fermionic modes, and its
framework finding is that at one site there is nothing to transform. The spin ladder and the fermionic mode
are the **same element** of $\mathbb{B}$: the parents' operators
$\tilde a=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y)$, $\tilde a^\dagger=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y)$ satisfy $\tilde a^{2}=0$ and
$\{\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger\}=e_t$ inside the algebra, so the square-zero mode is not adjoined but found.
The Jordan–Wigner string — the product of the other sites' fermion parities — has an empty product at one
site, and the article states the consequence exactly: the transformation is the **choice of a square root of
the spin algebra**, and the string is its branch cut. The reading is that nilpotency is the algebra's native
one-site fact, and the spin–fermion map is the extension of that fact over sites; the string, and with it the
grading that the map fails to preserve, appears only when a second site exists.

**The eikonal avoids the cone.** *The WKB Approximation and the Hamilton–Jacobi Equation in Biquaternionic
Form* gives a sharp negative result, and it is worth recording because it bounds the nilpotent's reach. In
the semiclassical limit the phase is a central element $\exp(iS/\hbar)$ and the momentum is the real quaternion
$\nabla S=\sum_k(\partial/\partial Q_k\,S)\vec e_k$; the Hamilton–Jacobi equation is the **biquaternion norm** of that momentum
equated to the kinetic energy, $\langle \nabla S\rangle_{\natural}=2m(E-V)$. The article's own statement is then exact: "the null
(zero divisor) cone is not reached by the phase gradient, whose biquaternion norm is fixed by the energy
defect and vanishes only where the classical particle stops". So the semiclassical momentum is never a
**nonzero** nilpotent: its norm is pinned to the energy defect, and the only way it vanishes is the trivial
one, $\nabla S=0$ at the turning point. The lightlike element is the eikonal's asymptotic boundary, not a
value the approximation attains, which is the same distinction the corpus draws between the null direction
and the null vector.

**The ladder stops at two levels.** *The Qutrit Problem: Three-Level Systems Outside the Biquaternion
Algebra* turns the square-zero identity into a ceiling. Since $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, a nilpotent
element has square zero, so the algebra's own raising operator **cannot climb more than one step**: the
spectrum it generates has exactly two levels, and there is no unital three-dimensional module. Two further
faces of the same fact are stated there: the identity splits into at most two orthogonal idempotents, so the
algebra cannot host a three-outcome projective measurement, and the three-dimensional adjoint space carries
an action of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ but not of the algebra, because the adjoint action is a
derivation and not a multiplication. The sentence that belongs in this companion's ledger is the article's:
**the algebra has two levels, and the third is not inside it.** A three-level ladder is therefore not a
module of $\mathbb{B}$; a qutrit exists only in the tensor square $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$, whose four
levels split three-plus-one, at the price of an extra level to be projected out. The reading is a boundary
rather than a coincidence: nilpotency is not only the mechanism of the ladder, it is the reason the ladder
has length two, and the same square-zero identity that creates the raising operator forbids a third rung.

**The null direction makes the unit group infinite.** *Biquaternion Orders and Finite Groups of Units* shows
the same vanishing square doing arithmetic. For a null element $P$ (so $\langle P\rangle_{\natural}=0$, hence $P^2=0$), the element
$e_t+P$ is a **unit**, with inverse $e_t-P$, and $(e_t+P)^n=e_t+nP$: a nilpotent direction generates an
infinite cyclic subgroup of the unit group. The article's own reading is exact and is the one to keep: the
real orders have finite unit groups, the complex order does not, and the difference is the **light cone** —
the null direction is the nilpotent one, it lies on $c^2t^2=x^2+y^2+z^2$, and it generates a
translation-like one-parameter group without bound, while the finite unit groups sit in the real sector,
where the norm is definite and no null direction exists. So the zero of the norm is what makes a unit group
infinite, and the algebraic statement of the light cone's presence is the infinite unit group of the complex
order.

**Nilpotency is the endpoint of a contraction.** *Squeezing Beyond the Biquaternion Algebra* shows where
the nilpotent comes from as a limit. A **contraction** rescales generators so that some commutators and some
norms go to zero; for $E_2=\epsilon \vec e_y$, $E_3=\epsilon \vec e_z$ the article verifies numerically that
$[E_2,E_3]=2\epsilon^2E_1$ and $\langle E_2\rangle_{\natural}=\epsilon^2e_t$ **vanish together** as $\epsilon\to0$ (at
$\epsilon=1,0.1,0.01$ the values are $2,2\times10^{-2},2\times10^{-4}$ and $1,10^{-2},10^{-4}$). The limit
is an algebra in which $E_2$ and $E_3$ are nilpotent and mutually annihilating, and the article states the
mechanism exactly: "zero divisors are what survive when a quadratic structure is squeezed until its metric
collapses", with the zero-divisor cone as the limiting locus of that degeneration. The reading is a third
home for square zero, beside the lightlike displacement and the fermionic mode: **the nilpotent is what an
invertible element becomes when the norm degenerates**, so the square-zero set is also the singular limit of
a nondegenerate quadratic structure. The article's other boundary fixes what the algebra cannot absorb —
q-deformations, octonionic doublings and dissipative completions lie outside $\mathbb{B}$, each for a
definite structural reason — so the contraction limit is the one degeneration that stays inside.

## Physical Interpretation

  direction and no magnitude, while the signed values set a magnitude, and the root of $0$ is the
  non-reversible value of the invertibility criterion: a signed root is a unit and a nonzero root of $0$
  is a zero divisor.
- The value $0$ is the one that carries the **direction** of the photon, and the root of $0$ **is** the
  massless element: the corpus reads it as a point of the celestial sphere together with a phase, the
  phase being the little-group $\mathrm{U}(1)$. The lightlike displacement, the null four-momentum and the Dirac
  point are the same algebraic object in the three bands that reach it.
- The branch-point reading of the value $0$ — the merging of the two roots of a nonzero value at the
  massless limit — belongs to *Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$* and is cited, not restated.

- A square-zero element kills itself: it is an element of its own annihilator. Physically it is an
  infinitesimal object, of which two successive applications give nothing, and the lightlike displacement

  massless case rather than choosing it.
- The plain composition band has the pure isotropic cone as its square-zero set, of real dimension four:
  the pure lightlike directions. The non-pure cone elements are the states, and they are not square-zero.
- The interval band has the whole cone: every lightlike element is self-annihilating there, and that band
  knows masslessness completely.
- The probability band has the origin alone: no nonzero amplitude squares to zero, so masslessness is
  absent from the band that measures probability. A probability is non-invertible because it is a
  probability, not because it is lightlike.
- The gauge band has a proper subfamily of the cone, so it reads only part of the lightlike family as
  square-zero.
- The two brackets have the whole space: an infinitesimal rotation is square-zero as a value, and the
  bracket has no proper notion of a lightlike displacement. The conjugate brackets annihilate the
  identity, so their square-zero set leaves the cone.
- The set of the non-invertible operations is strictly larger than the set of the lightlike ones, and the
  difference is made of the states. A state is a zero divisor that is idempotent, a lightlike displacement
  is a zero divisor that is nilpotent.
- The one-mode fermionic ladder is a pair of nilpotents, with
  $\vec\Upsilon_x^{2}=\vec\Upsilon_y^{2}=0$ and
  $\{\vec\Upsilon_x,\vec\Upsilon_y\}=e_t$, whose product is the vacuum projector. Nilpotency is Pauli
  exclusion, and the trace obstruction is why the algebra's ladder is fermionic.
- The same equation $\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0$ governs the odd nilpotents of the corpus: the BRST charge, whose cohomology

  multiplication is what makes the BRST charge nilpotent, and the three corpus brackets that fail the
  Jacobi identity are the ones that cannot carry a cohomology.
- The hole of Dirac's hole theory is not a nilpotent. A hole is the absence of a filled negative-energy
  state, a difference from the sea, and the framework sits on the modern reinterpretation side, where the

- The nilpotent mode is **native**, and the map to it is an identity at one site. *The Jordan–Wigner
  Transformation in Biquaternionic Form* finds that the one-site spin ladder is the fermionic mode itself,
  $\tilde a^{2}=0$, so the transformation is the choice of a square root of the spin algebra and the string

- Nilpotency at the level of a **graded extension** is where the algebra stops. In *Grassmann Coherent States
  in Biquaternionic Form* the coherent-state labels generate a Grassmann algebra by one odd variable per mode,

- The Dirac point of graphene and of a topological-insulator surface is a lightlike momentum,

- Nilpotency is the endpoint of a contraction. *Squeezing Beyond the Biquaternion Algebra* degenerates the

## Summary

A nilpotent of index two, or square-zero element, is a nonzero element whose square in the product at hand
vanishes, and it is an element that kills itself: it lies in its own annihilator. Physically it is an
infinitesimal object, the lightlike displacement, and the corpus writes it $\tilde\Upsilon$. The
mathematics companion computes the square-zero sets of the twelve structures and orders them by the cone.
The plain composition band has the pure isotropic cone of real dimension four, the interval band the whole
cone, the probability band the origin alone and the gauge band a proper subfamily of the cone, while the
two brackets have the whole space and the two antisymmetric sesquilinear structures have a subfamily
outside the cone that contains the unit. Read physically, the order is the order of the bands by their
lightlike content: none in the probability band, the pure ones in the composition band, all of them in the
interval band, and no proper lightlike notion in the brackets. The square-zero set lies in the zero-divisor
set of its structure and contains the cone, but the two sets agree for only three of the twelve
structures, and the gap for the other nine is the distinction between the two kinds of non-invertible
element. The plain product shows it: its zero divisors include the states, which are idempotent and not
square-zero, and its square-zero elements are the pure lightlike displacements alone. A state is a zero
divisor that is idempotent; a lightlike displacement is a zero divisor that is nilpotent; and the two
equations $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ and $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ are the two extremes of the quadratic
element theory of the algebra.

Møller Scattering in Biquaternionic Form

Introduction

Møller scattering is electron–electron scattering, $e^-e^-\to e^-e^-$, in quantum electrodynamics. Christian Møller derived it in 1932, in the same paper that treated the passage of fast electrons through matter. It is the paradigmatic two-electron process: the electron repulsion inside the helium atom is its non-relativistic face, and electron–electron colliders were built around it. At the tree level it receives two contributions, the $t$-channel and $u$-channel diagrams in which the two electrons exchange a photon. The two diagrams differ by the exchange of the two final electrons and therefore enter with a relative minus sign, the Pauli exchange sign of identical fermions. By crossing symmetry the Møller amplitude and the Bhabha amplitude, $e^+e^-\to e^+e^-$, are the same amplitude read with different particle labels; the companion article Bhabha Scattering in Biquaternionic Form uses that correspondence.

This article treats Møller scattering in the biquaternion framework. The framework's contribution is structural. Its minimal-coupling and quantization articles supply the ingredients — a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ coupling localised in the centre, a quantized Maxwell field, a Fock space with creation and annihilation operators, and a propagator — and the Møller amplitude is then a product of two material-sector currents contracted by the photon propagator. The relative minus sign between the two diagrams is the antisymmetry of fermion exchange, which the framework's canonical quantization already carries as the identity $(a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger})^2=0$ on the mode algebra. And crossing symmetry is the framework's charge conjugation on the doubled module, read backwards. What the framework does not do is evaluate loops: the corpus's scattering content is tree-level, and the present article stays there.

Every vertex below is one action, written out. A vertex acts on an element $Q$ of $\mathbb{B}$ by $Q\mapsto aQb$, with $a$ and $b$ elements of the algebra, and each action is printed in full where it enters. The five actions the amplitude uses are written out in §Kinematics; the closing section writes the same five in the older display, for comparison only, and nothing in the body depends on it. Throughout, $i$ is the central imaginary of $\mathbb{B}$, so every $i$ in an action is that one central unit and no separate imaginary of a display enters.

The article is organised as follows. The next section sets up the two diagrams, the kinematics, the mass equation of the external elements and the minus sign. The third computes the spin-summed matrix element. The fourth gives the cross section and its two limits. The fifth treats the weak correction and the parity-violating asymmetry. The sixth is the biquaternion interpretation. The seventh is the closing comparison, and the remaining sections are the open questions, the summary, the notation table and the literature.

The Process and Its Two Diagrams

Kinematics

Let the incoming electrons carry four-momenta $p_1,p_2$ and the outgoing electrons $p_3,p_4$, with $p_i^2=m^{2}c^{2}$ and $m=m_e$. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}$, with the scalar unit $e_t$, the three vector units and the central imaginary $i$, as Algebra and Element Conventions fixes them.

The four insertions. The four vertex insertions are the four actions

$$ Ξ_t:\ Q\mapsto iQ\vec e_x,\qquad Ξ_k:\ Q\mapsto i\vec e_kQ\vec e_y\quad(k=1,2,3), $$

with $(\vec e_1,\vec e_2,\vec e_3)=(\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$. Each insertion carries exactly one factor of the central $i$ and one or two vector units, so its square is read from the unit squares and the unit products of Algebra and Element Conventions: $Ξ^{2}_t$ is the identity, $Ξ^{2}_k$ is minus the identity, and $Ξ_\muΞ_\nu+Ξ_\nuΞ_\mu$ is $2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}$ times the identity, with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$. Those three derived forms are what every identity below uses. The time insertion multiplies on the right alone, while the three spatial insertions multiply on both sides. The fifth action, the chirality action, is the right multiplication $Q\mapsto-iQ\vec e_z$; it also multiplies on the right alone and anticommutes with the four insertions.

The momentum insertion. The insertion of a four-momentum $p=(p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3)$ is the action

$$ P(p):\ Q\mapsto p_0\,iQ\vec e_x-\sum_{k=1}^3 p_k\,i\vec e_kQ\vec e_y , $$

which is the four-momentum contracted with the insertions of the preceding paragraph. Its square is $(p_0^2-\vec p^2)$ times the identity, and the mass-shell condition is $p_0^2-\vec p^{\,2}=m^{2}c^{2}$.

The Mandelstam variables are

$$ s = (p_1+p_2)^2 = (p_3+p_4)^2, \qquad t = (p_1-p_3)^2 = (p_4-p_2)^2, \qquad u = (p_1-p_4)^2 = (p_3-p_2)^2, $$

and for equal masses they satisfy the identity

$$ s + t + u = 4m^2 . $$

The centre-of-momentum configuration used below is

$$ p_1 = (E,0,0,p),\quad p_2 = (E,0,0,-p),\quad p_3 = (E,p\sin\theta,0,p\cos\theta),\quad p_4 = (E,-p\sin\theta,0,-p\cos\theta), $$

with $E^2 = m^2+p^2$ and $E_{\mathrm{CM}} = 2E$, so that

$$ s = 4E^2 = E_{\mathrm{CM}}^2, \qquad t = 2p^2(\cos\theta-1) = -4p^2\sin^2\tfrac{\theta}{2}, \qquad u = -2p^2(\cos\theta+1) = -4p^2\cos^2\tfrac{\theta}{2}. $$

Both $-t$ and $-u$ are positive and vanish at one end of the angular range, $-t$ at $\theta=0$ and $-u$ at $\theta=\pi$; this is what makes $1/t$ and $1/u$ the two photon propagators and $y:=-t/s$ the inelasticity, equal to $\sin^2(\theta/2)$ up to $m^2/E^2$.

The two Dirac equations of the external states

The external elements satisfy the massive Dirac equation, but in the biquaternion algebra that equation is not a single line.

The chiral pair. It is first a pair of one-sided equations

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}, \qquad \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}, $$

where $\tilde\nabla$ is the biquaternionic gradient and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$ its quaternion conjugate, in the sense of Algebra and Element Conventions. The two subscripts are the two values of the chirality of the declared frame: $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ is the part of value $L$, the minus one, and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ the part of value $R$, the plus one, and only the zero element carries both. A product takes the letter of its right factor, and a left multiplication keeps it. At $m=0$ the two lines decouple into the two Weyl equations $\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=0$ and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=0$, one for each value. The mass is the only term that couples them, and it couples them off the diagonal. Both gradients act from the left, so this pair is one-sided. Here $k_m=mc/\hbar$ is the mass wave number, the inverse of the mass length.

The mass on the right. Read on the whole algebra rather than on the carrier of the field, the same physics is the single equation

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}F=-i\,m\,\bar F\vec e_z , $$

with $\bar F$ the coefficient conjugate. The derivative acts on the left, the mass acts on the right, and the conjugation supplies the antilinear part; the second element of the pair is the conjugate of the first, $-i\bar F\vec e_z$. The two readings differ in kind: the pair is linear in the field, this equation is antilinear in $F$ because the mass term carries $\bar F$. The pair is what the external elements below solve; this equation is the algebra's own reading of the same mass.

The momentum-space form. In momentum space the pair is the single action equation

$$ P(p)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} $$

on an element of momentum $p$: the insertion $P(p)$ applied to $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ gives back $m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$. It is the pair written once, its two lines being the two parts $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, and the mass is here the scalar $m$ and not a right multiplication. The two positive-frequency elements of a momentum are written $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{(1)}$ and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{(2)}$, and their spin sum is the action $D(p)$ of §The Spin-Summed Matrix Element.

The two amplitudes and the minus sign

The vertex current of an electron of incoming momentum $p_{\mathrm{in}}$ and outgoing momentum $p_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the scalar

$$ J_\mu(p_{\mathrm{out}},p_{\mathrm{in}}):=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\bar{\tilde{Ξ}}_{\mathrm{sp}}(p_{\mathrm{out}})\,Ξ_\mu\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(p_{\mathrm{in}})\bigr), \qquad \bar{\tilde{Ξ}}_{\mathrm{sp}}:=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*i\vec e_x, $$

with $Ξ_\mu$ the insertion of the Lorentz index $\mu$ and $\bar{\tilde{Ξ}}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ the Dirac adjoint, the product of the star of the element with the fixed element $i\vec e_x$, the one factor of the adjoint that lies outside $\mathbb{B}$. The product $\bar{\tilde{Ξ}}_{\mathrm{sp}}\,Ξ_\mu\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is an element of $\mathbb{B}$ and its scalar part is a single complex number, so every $J_\mu$ is a single complex number and the brackets below are ordinary complex products. With this notation the $t$-channel and $u$-channel brackets are

$$ \mathcal M_t=\frac{e^2}{t}\Bigl[J_t(p_3,p_1)J_t(p_4,p_2)-\sum_{k=1}^3 J_k(p_3,p_1)J_k(p_4,p_2)\Bigr], $$

$$ \mathcal M_u=\frac{e^2}{u}\Bigl[J_t(p_3,p_2)J_t(p_4,p_1)-\sum_{k=1}^3 J_k(p_3,p_2)J_k(p_4,p_1)\Bigr]. $$

The two brackets differ by the exchange of the two electron lines: $\mathcal M_u$ is obtained from $\mathcal M_t$ by swapping the initial momenta, $p_1\leftrightarrow p_2$, or equivalently the final momenta, $p_3\leftrightarrow p_4$. Because the two electrons are identical, the exchange costs a minus sign, and the total amplitude is

$$ \boxed{\;\mathcal M=\mathcal M_t-\mathcal M_u\;}, $$

where the amplitude $\mathcal M$ is the invariant matrix element, normalised so that $i\mathcal M$ times the momentum-conservation delta is the $S$-matrix element. This single sign is the whole of the interference structure: it is why the $u$-channel is a subtraction rather than an addition, and it is the sign that distinguishes the identical-fermion cross section from the distinguishable-particle one.

The Spin-Summed Matrix Element

Squaring and averaging over the two initial spins and summing over the two final spins — a factor $\tfrac14$ — gives the following.

The spin sum. The two positive-frequency elements of a momentum $p$ satisfy the operator identity

$$ \sum_{r=1}^{2}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{(r)}(p)\,\bar{\tilde{Ξ}}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{(r)}(p)=D(p), $$

the sum of the two rank-one products being the action

$$ D(p):\ Q\mapsto p_0\,iQ\vec e_x-\sum_{k=1}^3 p_k\,i\vec e_kQ\vec e_y+m\,Q , $$

that is, the momentum insertion $P(p)$ plus the scalar $m$. The mass term is a scalar multiple of the unit and therefore acts on the right and on the left alike. This identity is what turns a sum over the spins of a product of two vertex currents into a trace of the corresponding composite, and it is the only place where the two external elements enter the squared amplitude.

The trace. The traces below are the operator traces of composites of these actions, in the normalisation of Matrix Representations Conventions, where the identity action has trace four. The factorisation is a derived form, read off the two slots: with $Ξ_i$ the action $Q\mapsto a_iQb_i$, the trace of the composite $Ξ_1Ξ_2\cdotsΞ_n$ is four times the product of the scalar parts of the two multiplier strings,

$$ \operatorname{tr}\bigl[Ξ_1Ξ_2\cdotsΞ_n\bigr] =4\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(a_1a_2\cdots a_n\bigr)\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(b_n\cdots b_2b_1\bigr), $$

the left string read in the order of the factors and the right string in reverse, and the trace is additive over sums of actions. With $Ξ^{t}=Ξ_t$ and $Ξ^{k}=-Ξ_k$, the three trace combinations are

$$ L_t:=\operatorname{tr}\bigl[Ξ^{\mu} D(p_1)Ξ^{\nu} D(p_3)\bigr]\, \operatorname{tr}\bigl[Ξ_\mu D(p_2)Ξ_\nu D(p_4)\bigr], $$

$$ L_u:=\operatorname{tr}\bigl[Ξ^{\mu} D(p_2)Ξ^{\nu} D(p_3)\bigr]\, \operatorname{tr}\bigl[Ξ_\mu D(p_1)Ξ_\nu D(p_4)\bigr], $$

$$ L_{tu}:=\operatorname{tr}\bigl[D(p_3)Ξ^{\mu} D(p_1)Ξ^{\nu} D(p_4)Ξ_\mu D(p_2)Ξ_\nu\bigr], $$

with the sum over the two repeated Lorentz indices, and the trace of an odd number of insertions vanishes. Then

$$ \frac14\sum_{\mathrm{spins}}|\mathcal M|^2 =\frac{e^4}{4}\left(\frac{L_t}{t^2}+\frac{L_u}{u^2}-\frac{2L_{tu}}{tu}\right). $$

The traces are evaluated with the standard four-dimensional identities, which follow from the anticommutation relations of the insertions alone,

$$ \operatorname{tr}(Ξ_\muΞ_\nu)=4\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu},\qquad \operatorname{tr}(Ξ_\muΞ_\nuΞ_\rhoΞ_\sigma) =4\bigl(\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}\varepsilon_\rho\delta_{\rho\sigma}-\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\rho}\varepsilon_\nu\delta_{\nu\sigma}+\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\sigma}\varepsilon_\nu\delta_{\nu\rho}\bigr), $$

with odd traces vanishing; The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation records them with the rest of the dictionary. Evaluating the three traces gives the closed form, in which the factor $\tfrac14$ of the spin average is already included:

$$ \begin{aligned} \overline{|\mathcal M|^2} = 2e^4\Bigl\{& \frac{s^2+u^2-8m^2(s+u)+24m^4}{t^2}\\ &+\frac{s^2+t^2-8m^2(s+t)+24m^4}{u^2} +\frac{2(s^2-8m^2s+12m^4)}{tu}\Bigr\}. \end{aligned} $$

Each of the three terms has the same anatomy: the two trace factors are the two electron currents contracted with each other through the photon propagator's $1/t$ or $1/u$, and the cross term is the interference. The interference carries the exchange sign squared, so its sign is not fixed by the amplitude but by the kinematics: since $tu>0$, the cross term has the sign of $s^2-8m^2s+12m^4$, whose roots are $s=2m^2$ and $s=6m^2$, so on the physical range it is negative for $4m^2\le s<6m^2$ and positive above, that is, negative while $p^2m^2/2$. The interference is therefore destructive in the non-relativistic region and constructive in the ultrarelativistic one, and the change of sign is one reason the two limits below have such different angular shapes. In Bhabha scattering the cross term, in the same normalisation $\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=2e^4\{\ldots\}$, is $+\,2u^2/(st)$ with $u$ the crossed invariant of the companion article; once the overall $2e^4$ is distributed it reads $4u^2/(st)$, and since $st<0$ it is negative at every energy, so there the annihilation interference is destructive throughout.

The Cross Section and Its Limits

The unpolarized differential cross section follows from the standard two-body flux factor, with equal masses so that the momentum ratio is one,

$$ \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2 E_{\mathrm{CM}}^2}\,\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}, \qquad \alpha=\frac{e^2}{4\pi}, $$

inserting the centre-of-momentum Mandelstam variables. In the two limits it takes instructive forms.

Non-relativistic limit ($m\gg p$, to leading order in $p^2/m^2$, with $E_{\mathrm{CM}}\simeq2m$):

$$ \boxed{\; \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{m^4\alpha^2}{E_{\mathrm{CM}}^2\,p^4\sin^4\theta}\,(1+3\cos^2\theta)\;}, $$

a Rutherford-like $1/\sin^4\theta$ law modulated by the $(1+3\cos^2\theta)$ factor. That factor is the spin average of two Rutherford amplitudes. Write $f(\theta)\propto\sin^{-2}(\theta/2)$ for the direct one and $f(\pi-\theta)\propto\cos^{-2}(\theta/2)$ for the exchanged one; the three triplet spin states go with the antisymmetric spatial combination and the singlet with the symmetric one, so

$$ \tfrac34\bigl|f(\theta)-f(\pi-\theta)\bigr|^2+\tfrac14\bigl|f(\theta)+f(\pi-\theta)\bigr|^2 =|f|^2\left[\frac{1}{\sin^4\tfrac\theta2}+\frac{1}{\cos^4\tfrac\theta2}-\frac{1}{\sin^2\tfrac\theta2\cos^2\tfrac\theta2}\right] =\frac{4\,|f|^2\,(1+3\cos^2\theta)}{\sin^4\theta}, $$

which is the $(1+3\cos^2\theta)$ of the box. A spinless electron would instead give the antisymmetric combination alone, and its cross section would vanish at $\theta=\pi/2$. It is the quantum-mechanical electron–electron repulsion behind the helium atom, with the exchange factor.

Ultrarelativistic limit ($m\ll p$, to leading order in $m^2/p^2$):

$$ \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{E_{\mathrm{CM}}^2\,\sin^4\theta}\,(3+\cos^2\theta)^2 . $$

Both ends of the angular range carry a $1/\sin^4\theta$ factor under the exchange factor $(3+\cos^2\theta)^2$, and both ends are genuine Coulomb singularities: $t=0$ at $\theta=0$ is the direct photon, $u=0$ at $\theta=\pi$ is the exchanged one, since backward scattering of one electron is forward scattering of the other. The electron mass regulates neither — $u=-4p^2\cos^2(\theta/2)$ vanishes at $\theta=\pi$ for every $p$ — so only a detector's angular acceptance makes the cross section finite, and with a fixed cut the total cross section falls as $\alpha^2/s$ up to logarithms from the interference.

The Weak Correction and Parity Violation

At energies below the $Z$ mass the process is described by QED, but the full electroweak theory adds two further tree-level diagrams with $Z$-boson exchange. Because the weak interaction is left-handed while the electromagnetic one is parity-symmetric, the $Z$ contribution makes the cross section different for left- and right-handed incoming electrons. For a polarized electron beam on an unpolarized target the parity-violating asymmetry is

$$ A_{\mathrm{PV}}\equiv\frac{\sigma_L-\sigma_R}{\sigma_L+\sigma_R}, $$

where $\sigma_L$ and $\sigma_R$ are the cross sections for left- and right-handed incident electrons.

The $Z$ couples to the electron with $g_V^e=-\tfrac12Q_W^e$ and $g_A^e=-\tfrac12$. At $Q^2\ll M_Z^2$ its propagator collapses to $-1/M_Z^2$, so the $Z$ amplitude is $-\sqrt2G_{\mathrm F}$ times the same two currents with the insertion replaced by the combination $g_V^eΞ_\mu$ minus $g_A^e$ times the same insertion followed by the chirality action $Q\mapsto-iQ\vec e_z$: the vector term keeps both values of the chirality, and the axial term carries one value into the other. The asymmetry is twice the interference of the $Z$ amplitude with the photon amplitude, divided by the photon amplitude squared. Three facts follow at once, and they are the anatomy of the boxed result below: the interference carries exactly one power of $G_{\mathrm F}$ and one factor $1/\alpha$ from the two photon vertices; it is proportional to the product $g_V^eg_A^e=\tfrac14Q_W^e$, so a parity-symmetric $Z$ coupling would give nothing; and the kinematic factor is $Q^2$, which in a fixed-target experiment is $Q^2=2m_eE_{\mathrm{beam}}y$, hence the $m_eE_{\mathrm{beam}}$ in front and the $y$-dependence behind.

At tree level the asymmetry is therefore

$$ \boxed{\; A_{\mathrm{PV}} = -\,m_eE_{\mathrm{beam}}\,\frac{G_{\mathrm F}}{\sqrt2\,\pi\alpha}\, \frac{4\sin^2\theta}{\left(3+\cos^2\theta\right)^2}\, Q_W^e\;}, \qquad Q_W^e=1-4\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm W}, $$

with $Q_W^e$ the weak charge of the electron, $E_{\mathrm{beam}}$ the beam energy in the laboratory — not the centre-of-mass energy $E$ of the kinematics above, since $m_eE_{\mathrm{beam}}=Q^2/(2y)$ in a fixed-target experiment — and $\theta$ the centre-of-mass scattering angle. The same formula in terms of the inelasticity $y=-t/s$, equal to $\sin^2(\theta/2)$ up to $m^2/E^2$, is

$$ A_{\mathrm{PV}} = -\,m_eE_{\mathrm{beam}}\,\frac{G_{\mathrm F}}{\sqrt2\,\pi\alpha}\, \frac{2y(1-y)}{1+y^4+(1-y)^4}\,Q_W^e, $$

and the two forms agree by $4\sin^2\theta/(3+\cos^2\theta)^2=2y(1-y)/(1+y^4+(1-y)^4)$. With the definition adopted here, $A_{\mathrm{PV}}$ is negative for $Q_W^e>0$; MOLLER quotes the same number with the opposite convention $(\sigma_R-\sigma_L)/(\sigma_R+\sigma_L)$ and the opposite overall sign. The asymmetry is of order $10^{-7}$, a hundred parts per billion, because $m_eE_{\mathrm{beam}}\sim10^{-2}$ GeV$^2$ and $G_{\mathrm F}/(\sqrt2\pi\alpha)\sim4\times10^{-4}$ GeV$^{-2}$.

It was measured at SLAC (E158), which finds $A_{\mathrm{PV}}=-131\pm14\pm10$ parts per billion and determines $\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm W}$; the approved MOLLER experiment at Jefferson Laboratory is designed for $E_{\mathrm{beam}}=11$ GeV, where the same formula gives $A_{\mathrm{PV}}\approx-4\times10^{-8}$, forty parts per billion. The first discussion is due to Zel'dovich (1959). It is the cleanest experimental reason Møller scattering is still studied, and it is the physical content of the framework's statement that the photon coupling is parity-symmetric while a left-handed coupling is not.

The Biquaternion Reading

The amplitude as a product of two material-sector currents

The framework's minimal-coupling article writes the electromagnetic coupling of the biquaternion Dirac field through the four-potential and identifies the conserved current as the pairing

$$ j_\mu=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*i\vec e_x\,Ξ_\mu\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\bigr), \qquad \bar{\tilde{Ξ}}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*i\vec e_x, $$

where $Ξ_\mu$ are the four insertions of §Kinematics. The current is the material-sector four-current $\tilde J_{\Phi}\in\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ of Algebra and Element Conventions, but its Dirac adjoint uses the fixed element $i\vec e_x$, which lies outside $\mathbb{B}$; the naive product $i\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*$ is in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and is not conserved. The Møller amplitude is the second-quantized version of that structure. Each vertex $J_\mu(p_{\mathrm{out}},p_{\mathrm{in}})$ is one electron current, and the two currents are contracted by the photon propagator $1/t$ or $1/u$. The framework's Maxwell field supplies the photon line: its canonical-quantization article constructs the quantized Maxwell field, and the photon propagator $1/t$ or $1/u$ is the standard two-point function of that field. The amplitude is the framework's current, twice, joined by the framework's photon. Nothing in this is a new prediction; it is the identification of the components.

The relative minus sign is the mode algebra's antisymmetry

The single structural feature the framework owns here is the exchange sign. The corpus's canonical-quantization article of the Dirac field obtains Pauli exclusion as the algebraic identity $(a_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger})^2=0$, together with the $\mathbb Z/2$ grading of the mode algebra by fermion parity. That identity is exactly the statement that two fermionic mode operators anticommute, and the relative minus sign between the $t$- and $u$-channel amplitudes is its momentum-space consequence: exchanging two identical external fermions costs a sign. The framework therefore does not merely transcribe the minus sign; the sign is the shadow of the graded structure the corpus establishes on the operator algebra. This is the same $\mathbb Z/2$ grading that the corpus's KMS and fermion-parity articles treat.

Crossing symmetry is charge conjugation

The Møller and Bhabha amplitudes are related by crossing symmetry: moving an incoming electron to an outgoing positron, with reversed four-momentum, changes one process into the other. In the framework this operation is the charge conjugation real structure $\mathcal C$ on the doubled module $\Delta = S\oplus\bar S$ of the companion article Real Spinors and Reality Conditions with Inner Conjugation; a particle line is replaced by an antiparticle line, and the amplitude's analytic continuation in the Mandelstam variables is the momentum-space face of the same relabelling. The numerical check reported in the companion Bhabha article — the two amplitudes agreeing once the Mandelstam roles are permuted — is therefore a check of the framework's particle–antiparticle relabelling as much as of the algebra of the insertions. The companion Bhabha article states the correspondence from the other side.

What the framework supplies and what it does not

The framework supplies the two vertex currents, the photon propagator, the Fock space in which the external states live, and the exchange sign as the antisymmetry of its mode algebra. It does not supply the numerical evaluation of the traces as a derivation from its own structure — the trace algebra is the dictionary of The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation — nor does it supply the $Z$-exchange diagrams, which belong to the framework's electroweak research agenda rather than to its established content. The parity-violating asymmetry above is quoted as the experimental fact that motivates the left-handed coupling; the corpus's chiral-fermion article discusses the framework's treatment of handedness, and the electroweak article collects the open questions. No framework-specific prediction is claimed.

Open Questions

  1. The traces as algebra. Can the three trace combinations be reorganised as products of the framework's own currents in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, so that neither the insertions nor the trace appear — and does the $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ restriction of the currents reproduce the standard results identically?

  2. The exchange sign from first principles. The corpus obtains Pauli exclusion on the mode algebra. Is there a corresponding statement on the classical biquaternion field (a graded-commutativity of the field's components) from which the momentum-space minus sign follows without quantizing?

  3. Crossing and $\mathcal C$. Is the Mandelstam analytic continuation of the framework's amplitude literally the action of $\mathcal C$ on the external states, or only the same relabelling at the level of the on-shell elements?

  4. The weak asymmetry. The corpus's electroweak agenda lists the framework's internal $SU(2)\times \mathrm{U}(1)$ questions; the Møller $A_{\mathrm{PV}}$ is a concrete target. Does the framework's left-handed coupling reproduce the $(1/4-\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm W})$ coefficient, or only the general form?

  5. Loops. The corpus's Ward–Takahashi and Schwinger–Dyson articles treat the identities a loop computation must satisfy, but no loop amplitude is evaluated. Whether Møller scattering at one loop can be organised in the framework without new structure is open.

  6. The two chiral sums. The spin sum $D(p)$ is the momentum-space reading of the chiral pair. Can the positive- and negative-frequency sums be written as two separate actions, one for each value of the chirality, without passing through the momentum-space equation?

  7. Empirical contact. The tree-level result is standard electrodynamics in the framework's variables; the parity-violating asymmetry is the one place the framework meets a measured number, and it is not a framework-specific prediction.

The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices. The generators carry no frame label: $\gamma_\mu$ is one set of matrices, and the frame is named on the coordinate.

This section fixes the dictionary that the traces above use, and nothing here is needed outside it.

The five $\gamma$'s are a frame of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, so they are elements of the same algebra as the insertions, not of a larger one. Here $L_a$ and $R_b$ are the left and right multiplications, $L_a\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=a\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and $R_b\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} b$, and $c(M)$ is the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products.

$$ \gamma_0=iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_x},\quad \gamma_1=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_x}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_2=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_3=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_5=-iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z} . $$

The same in the axis-free display:

The body's five actions are these: the time insertion is $\gamma_0$, the three spatial insertions are $\gamma_1$, $\gamma_2$ and $\gamma_3$, and the chirality action is $\gamma_5$.

A single product is a pure tensor; in the sixteen-dimensional coefficient space the products form the seven-dimensional cone of coefficient rank at most one. A general element is a sum of at most four products; its length $\ell(M)$ is the least number of products in such a sum. The matrix rank of $M$ on the regular module is a different number: for a single basis operator $L_QR_P$ it is $\operatorname{rank}\mathsf M_2(Q)\operatorname{rank}\mathsf M_2(P)$, and it lies in $\{0,1,2,4\}$. The two numbers are independent.

$\gamma_5$ is the chirality action itself, $-iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z}$, so the fifth action and the fifth gamma are one object seen in two readings. The central grading $\xi_{\mathrm{ad}}:=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ — the difference of the two central idempotents of the complexified algebra, so $\xi_{\mathrm{ad}}^2=e_t$ — is a different object; it merely has the same representative $\gamma_5$ on the Dirac module, so that the two central idempotents correspond to the two chiral projectors and $\xi$ is the module representative of $\xi_{\mathrm{ad}}$.

The dictionary $M\mapsto c(M)$ is complex-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the basis operators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

Each $\gamma$ is a basis operator with one factor of $i$; the squares use $i^2=-1$.

The contraction sums are fixed by the metric, because the sign of a basis operator's square is. With the summed index raised, $\gamma^\mu=-\varepsilon_\mu\gamma_\mu$,

$$ \sum_\mu\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu=-4I_4,\qquad \sum_\mu\gamma^\mu\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\mu=+2\gamma_\alpha, $$

$$ \sum_\mu\gamma^\mu\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma_\mu=-4\varepsilon_\alpha\delta_{\alpha\beta}I_4,\qquad \sum_\mu\gamma^\mu\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma_\gamma\gamma_\mu=-2\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma_\gamma . $$

With every index low only the first keeps a closed form, $\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu\gamma_\mu=-2I_4$; the other three are index-dependent and are computed, not transcribed.

The display. Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. The two are carried into one another by $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}=C_{\Pi}L_{\mathrm{pau},e_\mu}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and a conjugation by $C_{\Pi}$ changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant, so every identity of the section holds in both. An array is a statement about one display; a basis operator named in the running text keeps the Peirce label, and its axis-free array is the conjugate. The label names the array and not the operator.

Summary

Møller scattering, $e^-e^-\to e^-e^-$, is the tree-level exchange of a photon between two electrons, contributing through $t$-channel and $u$-channel diagrams that combine with the relative minus sign of identical-fermion exchange. The Mandelstam variables satisfy $s+t+u=4m^2$; the spin-summed matrix element is three terms, two of them products of traces over the photon propagators and one the interference, whose sign flips from destructive to constructive at $p^2=m^{2}c^{2}/2$; the unpolarized cross section reduces to the Rutherford-like $(1+3\cos^2\theta)/\sin^4\theta$ law at low energy and to $(3+\cos^2\theta)^2/\sin^4\theta$ at high energy. The electroweak $Z$-exchange adds a parity-violating asymmetry of order one hundred parts per billion, measured at SLAC and used to extract the weak mixing angle.

In the biquaternion framework the amplitude is the contraction of two material-sector currents — the same current the minimal-coupling article identifies — by the framework's quantized photon propagator. Every vertex is one action written out, $Q\mapsto aQb$; the five actions are the four insertions and the chirality action, and the chirality takes the two values $L$ and $R$, which the external elements carry as subscripts. The mass is a scalar multiple of the unit in the momentum-space equation, and a right multiplication by $-i\vec e_z$ on the conjugate when the same physics is read on the whole algebra. The two structures the framework genuinely owns are the exchange sign, which is the antisymmetry of the mode algebra established by the canonical-quantization article as $(a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger})^2=0$ and the $\mathbb Z/2$ grading, and crossing symmetry, which is the charge-conjugation real structure $\mathcal C$ on the doubled module. The body uses no array: the traces are traces of composites of the actions, computed from the two multiplier strings, and the older display appears once, at the end, with the dictionary and no derivation. The framework does not evaluate loops, does not supply $Z$ exchange, and makes no framework-specific prediction; the parity-violating asymmetry is quoted as the experimental target that the framework's left-handed coupling must ultimately meet.

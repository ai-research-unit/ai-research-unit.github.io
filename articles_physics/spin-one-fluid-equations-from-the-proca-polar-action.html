Spin-One Fluid Equations from the Proca Polar Action: A Divergence, a Projection and a Stiff Equation of State

Introduction

The Spin-One Fluid reads the Proca equation and finds that the Lorenz condition is the continuity equation, that the mass term is a stiff fluid with $\epsilon=p=\tfrac12m^{2}\varrho^{2}$, and that the vorticity has no baroclinic source. The Lagrangian and the Proca Equation in Polar Variables writes the action in the four parameters of the material sector and finds the scale equation and the frame transport.

This article varies that action and reads the fluid's two equations. Neither of them is a coordinate's equation. Continuity is the divergence of the four Euler–Lagrange equations, which is the identity that the divergence of an antisymmetric field strength vanishes; the Euler equation is the part of the field equation orthogonal to the flow. The scale's variation is the fourth component, and it is not a fluid equation at all.

Why this is the rung's structural mark. At the three rungs above the fluid's two equations are two different coordinates' equations: the phase's and the scale's, or the phase's and the rotor's. Here there is no phase. The field is the current, $A_{\imath}=\varrho u_{\imath}$, so the four parameters are a density and a direction, and continuity is not the density's equation — it is a divergence taken across all four. A fluid whose continuity is a divergence and whose Euler equation is a projection is a fluid read through two operations on the equations, and neither operation is a variation. That is the rung's mark, and the rest of the article is its consequence.

The equation of state, and where it comes from. The fluid article derives the stiff equation of state twice and checks that the two agree: from the stress and from the density-gradient force. The action route gives it in one line, and the line is the article's cleanest result. The only term of the action that is not a derivative is the mass term, and it is a pure power of the modulus. So the pressure is a function of the density alone, the equation of state is barotropic, and the baroclinic torque vanishes identically. The stiffness and the absence of vorticity generation are the same fact, and the fact is that the mass term has no derivatives.

The strongest objection. A four-parameter chart cannot carry a fluid, because a fluid needs a density, a pressure and a velocity, and the pressure is not a parameter. The objection is the reason the vector rung is the interesting one. The pressure here is not a parameter: it is the mass term read through the stress, and it is a function of the density because the action says so. A fluid whose pressure is a function of its density is a barotropic fluid, and a barotropic fluid needs exactly the density and the direction. The count closes, and it closes because of the equation of state and not in spite of it.

Position in the block. This is the variational companion of The Spin-One Fluid and the free member of the pair whose coupled member is The Charged Spin-One Fluid from the Polar Action. Its action is the polar companion's; its carrier is the material sector, and the word has four parameters and two branches.

Conventions

The conventions are the polar companion's, restated so that every sign is checkable from this page alone. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with $e_t=1$ and $\vec e_{\imath}^{2}=-e_t$; $i$ central; $\mathrm{Sc}$ the coefficient of $e_t$; the material sector $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ is anti-Hermitian; $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}$ is the quaternionic norm, real on $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$. The word is piecewise,

$$ \tilde A_\Phi=\varepsilon\,i\,\varrho\,\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\quad(\langle\tilde A_\Phi\rangle_\natural<0), \qquad \tilde A_\Phi=\varrho\,\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\,\hat u\quad(\langle\tilde A_\Phi\rangle_\natural>0), $$

with $\varrho>0$, $\varepsilon=\pm1$, and $\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}$ the boost of rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ about the unit axis $\hat u_{\mathrm b}$. The branch followed here is the timelike one; the spacelike branch is named where it matters.

The letters. The measure is $\mathrm{d}^{4}r$, $\partial/\partial R_t=-i\,\partial/\partial r'_t$, $\Box_\Phi=\Delta_\Phi-\partial^{2}/\partial r^{\prime 2}_{t}$, and $\nu_m=\frac{mc}{h}$. The fluid's velocity is the unit four-vector $u_{\imath}$, the field is $A_{\imath}=\varrho u_{\imath}$, and the derivative of the direction is $\Omega_{\imath\jmath}=D_{\imath}u_{\jmath}-D_{\jmath}u_{\imath}$ with $D$ the projected gradient. The field strength splits algebraically,

$$ F^{\mathrm{gco}}=r\Omega+\mathrm{d}r\wedge u $$

in the corpus's notation for the split, which is the identity $F(D)(ru)=r(Du)+\mathrm{d}r\wedge u$ read in the chart.

The Action in the Word

The element form. The polar companion's action is

$$ S[\tilde A_\Phi]=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}r\;\Bigl[\tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde F\tilde F\bigr)-\tfrac12\,4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\,\langle\tilde A_\Phi\rangle_\natural\Bigr]. $$

The field term is a full quadratic minus a square. Because the conjugate gradient splits additively into a scalar and a pure vector,

$$ \tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde F\tilde F\bigr) =\tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Sc}\Bigl[\bigl((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi\bigr)^{2}\Bigr]-\tfrac12 S^{2}, \qquad S=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi\bigr), $$

so the curl term carries the propagation, the square of the scalar carries the constraint, and the mass carries the scale.

The mass term is the branch. $\tfrac12\,4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\varrho^{2}$ on the timelike branch and its negative on the spacelike one. The sign of the mass term in the chart is the causal character of the four-vector, and that is the polar companion's cleanest new statement.

Three properties, and each is used below. The field term depends on the parameters only through first derivatives, so it supplies the metric and no potential. The mass term depends on the parameters algebraically, so it supplies a potential and no metric. And the split identity is algebraic, so the field strength is a sum of a term in the direction's derivative and a term in the density's gradient, with no cross term that mixes them at second order.

The Hessian and its rank. The chart's Hessian has rank three and not four, and the missing direction is the one that changes the scalar potential at fixed vector potential. That is the primary constraint of a massive vector field, and the Lorenz condition is its secondary partner. The chart relabels both and removes neither.

The Equations, and the Divergence Across Them

The four equations. Varying $ arrho$ and the three frame parameters gives four Euler–Lagrange equations: one for the scale, and three for the rapidity and the two angles of the axis. The frame's three are the polar companion's frame transport; the scale's is the scale equation with the frame's quadratic source.

Continuity is the divergence of all four, and the divergence is an identity. The field strength is antisymmetric, so the divergence of its divergence vanishes:

$$ \partial_{\imath}igl( ext{the field equation}igr)_{\imath}=0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad m^{2}\sum_{\imath} arepsilon_{\imath}\partial_{\imath}A_{\imath}=0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \sum_{\imath} arepsilon_{\imath}\partial_{\imath}igl( arrho u_{\imath}igr)=0, $$

and the last display is the Lorenz condition. Since the field is the current, $A_{\imath}= arrho u_{\imath}$, the Lorenz condition is the continuity equation. So continuity is not one of the four equations: it is what the four equations say when they are contracted with the gradient, and the contraction is an identity of the field strength rather than a variation.

Why the identification needs no step. At the three rungs above, the conserved current is a bilinear or a product of two factors, and identifying it with the density times the velocity takes a computation. Here the field is the current and the velocity is the field's direction, so the divergence of the current and the divergence of the field are one expression. The identification is a substitution and not a theorem.

The objection. If continuity is a consequence of the other four equations, the fluid's count of two is wrong: it is four equations, not two. The count of equations is four and the count of fluid equations is two, and the difference is the two operations. The four split into the divergence, which is continuity, and the part orthogonal to it, which is the Euler equation — and that split is four into one and three. Nothing is lost and nothing is added: one scalar is continuity and three components carry the flow's acceleration, of which two are independent because the velocity is a unit vector.

What the scale's equation is for, then. It supplies the density's rate. It is the component of the field equation along the flow, and its content is the statement that the four-vector is on the branch. A reader who calls it "the density equation" is not wrong about its content and is wrong about its role: it is not continuity, and continuity does not need it.

The Variation: The Transverse Part Is the Euler Equation

The two transverse components of the frame's equation are the fluid's Euler equation, and the article states its structure rather than redisplaying the polar companion's algebra.

The form. The part of the field equation orthogonal to the flow is the acceleration law: the flow's own derivative along itself equals a coefficient times the gradient of the logarithm of the density, up to the corpus's signs. That is the Euler equation of a fluid whose only force is a density gradient.

Where the force comes from. The field term is the curl's square, and the curl is $r\Omega+\mathrm{d}r\wedge u$. Varying the direction at fixed density leaves $r\Omega$, whose variation is the acceleration; the density's gradient enters through the $\mathrm{d}r\wedge u$ piece and through the overall factor $r$. So the force is a gradient of the density and no other force exists. The fluid article reads the same force off the stress and finds it agrees with the pressure gradient, and that agreement is the reason the two readings are one.

Why there is no baroclinic torque, and this is the article's second result. A force that is a gradient of a function of the density alone cannot generate vorticity from rest: the torque is $\vec\nabla\varrho\times\vec\nabla p/\varrho^{2}$, and if $p=p(\varrho)$ the two gradients are parallel and the wedge vanishes. The action says $p=p(\varrho)$ in one line, because the only non-derivative term is $\propto\varrho^{2}$. So the vorticity's conservation is not a separate theorem about the flow; it is the statement that the mass term has no derivatives.

What the Euler equation is not. It is not an independent variation. There is no coordinate whose equation is the Euler equation, and a reader who looks for one will find the frame's three and have to project. That is the price of a chart in which the velocity is a direction rather than a gradient.

The Variation: The Scale Equation, and What It Is Not

Varying $\varrho$. The scale appears in the field term through the overall factor and in the mass term as $\varrho^{2}$, and its equation is the polar companion's scale equation with the frame's quadratic source.

It is not a fluid equation, and the article says why. A fluid's equations are continuity and Euler, and both are in the frame's three. The scale's equation is the fourth component of the field equation in the chart's basis, and its content is the longitudinal part: it fixes the density's rate in terms of the direction's divergence, which is to say it is the Lorenz condition read the other way round. A reader who counts "density equation" and "direction equation" gets two and two, and the count is wrong: the split is one and three, and the fluid's is one scalar of the three plus two of the three.

What it does supply. The mass shell. The scale's equation is what remains of the field equation when the constraint is used, and it is the statement that the four-vector is on the branch. The polar companion's "scale equation with the frame's quadratic source" is that statement.

The check. On the Lorenz surface the four equations reduce to three, which is the count of helicities of a massive spin-one field. The reduction is the count's check, and the fluid reads it as: continuity removes one parameter and the three that remain are the direction's.

The Equation of State from the Mass Term

The argument, in three steps.

First, the stress's mass part. The mass term is $-\tfrac12 4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\langle\tilde A_\Phi\rangle_\natural$, which on the timelike branch is $+\tfrac12 4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}\varrho^{2}$. Its contribution to the stress is proportional to the metric with a coefficient that is a function of $\varrho$ alone.

Second, the identification. A stress of that form is a perfect fluid with $\epsilon$ and $p$ both equal to that coefficient, because the term is a pure potential with no derivative and its stress is isotropic. So $\epsilon=p=\tfrac12 m^{2}\varrho^{2}$, which is the stiff equation of state, and the sound speed is $c$ because $\mathrm{d}p/\mathrm{d}\epsilon=1$.

Third, the consequence for the flow. A stiff fluid's pressure is a function of the density, so the flow is barotropic, so the vorticity has no source. The three statements — the equation of state, the sound speed and the irrotationality of a flow started from rest — are one statement about one term of the action.

What is not claimed. The article does not redisplay the stress's computation; The Lagrangian and the Spin-One Fluid owns it and this article cites the result. What the article claims is the reason: the equation of state is barotropic because the mass term is algebraic, and it is stiff because the algebraic term is quadratic.

The contrast with the scalar rung. There the mass term is $-\varrho^{2}\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}$, which is algebraic but carries the frame's rapidity. It is therefore not a function of the density alone, and the scalar fluid's equation of state is not barotropic in the same sense. The vector rung's chart has no rapidity in the density, because the field is the current and the density is its modulus. That is why the vector rung is the one with a clean barotropic equation of state.

The Two Branches, the Charge and the Count

The branch and the sign. The two branches differ by the sign of the mass term and by the sign of the charge density, which is $\gamma\varepsilon\varrho$ on the timelike branch. The fluid's proper density is $\varrho$, positive by construction, and the charge density is signed. The two are different objects, and conflating them is the standard error at this rung.

The current is the field. $J_{\imath}=A_{\imath}$ is conserved on the Lorenz surface, and its conservation is continuity. That is the sharpest difference from the three rungs above, where the current is a bilinear or a product of factors and continuity is a separate statement about it.

The count of conserved densities. One, the charge, and no spin. The spin of the massive vector is not in the chart: the material sector's word carries the direction of the flow and not an orientation, and the corpus says so. So the vector rung is the one at which the fluid has a charge and no spin density, while the spinor rung has both and the scalar rung has the charge of a signed density and no spin.

What the Alternative Route Adds and What It Relabels

Adds. The location of the fluid's two equations in two operations on the four variations — a divergence and a transverse projection — rather than in two coordinates. The one-line derivation of the barotropic equation of state from the mass term's algebraicity, and with it the reason the vorticity has no source. The exact statement of what the four-parameter count can carry: a barotropic fluid and nothing else.

Does not add. No new equation and no new solution. The polar companion's two displays are the article's premise and the fluid article's identifications are its conclusion. The spacelike branch is named and not followed.

Relabels. "The Lorenz condition is the continuity equation." On the equation route this is an identification of two formulas. On the action route it is the statement that the field is the current, so that the divergence of the field and the divergence of the current are one object, and the trace of the frame's equation is that divergence.

Open Questions

The transverse projection's explicit form. The Euler equation is stated by structure. The polar companion's frame transport carries the display, and the two are the same equation in different letters; whether the projection makes the density-gradient force's coefficient transparent is not settled here.

The spacelike branch. The chart's second branch has a negative mass term and a different sign of the charge. Whether the fluid reading is the same read with the opposite pressure sign, or whether the branch is not a fluid at all, is not answered in the corpus.

Summary

The Proca polar action varies into four equations, and the fluid's two are the divergence across them and the transverse part of the frame's transport. The divergence is the Lorenz condition, which is continuity because the field is the current. The part orthogonal to the flow is the Euler equation, with a density-gradient force and no other. The scale's equation is the fourth and is not a fluid equation. The mass term is algebraic and quadratic, so the pressure is a function of the density alone, the equation of state is stiff with sound speed $c$, and the vorticity has no baroclinic source. One divergence, one projection, and an equation of state read off the only non-derivative term of the action.

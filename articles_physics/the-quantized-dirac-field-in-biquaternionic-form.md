# __The Quantized Dirac Field in Biquaternionic Form__

## Introduction

The companion article *Canonical Quantization of the Biquaternion Dirac Field* promotes the classical biquaternion Dirac field to an operator-valued field. It fixes the Lagrangian of the one-sided pair, the conjugate momentum with its second-class constraint, the equal-time anticommutators, the expansion in the parent's plane-wave solutions, the mode algebra, the normal-ordered Hamiltonian and its constant, and the $\mathbb{Z}/2$ grading of the mode algebra. It closes with an explicit list of what is standard and what is open, and it names the present article's subject as one of the open items: the quantized field was handled through a representative rather than as an object in its own right, and the question of what kind of object the operator-valued biquaternion field is was left standing.

**Units.** Natural units $\hbar=c=1$ are used throughout this article: a four-momentum is $(p_0,\vec p)$ with $p_0=E$, the mass shell is $p^2=\vec p^{\,2}+m^2$, and every mass $m$ is an inverse length.

This article takes up that question. It does not repeat the quantization, which is the parent's; it asks what the resulting object *is* in the biquaternion framework, and it develops the four structures that make the operator field more than a rewrite:

- **The field as an operator-valued biquaternion.** The quantized field is a map from spacetime into the tensor product of the fermionic operator algebra and the algebra $\mathbb{B}$. It is an element of $\mathbb{B}$ at every point, multiplied by an operator. The four conjugations of $\mathbb{B}$ lift to operations on the field, three antilinear and one complex-linear, and their action has to be computed together with the operator ordering.
- **The field as the odd generator.** Under fermion parity the field is odd and a bilinear is even. The observable algebra is the even part, and the field is the object whose square generates it. This is the operator-level content of the grading the parents supply on the modes.
- **The operator-valued observables.** The energy–momentum, the charge and the current are operator-valued; the energy–momentum sits in the material sector $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, the charge in the centre $\mathbb{B}_{C}$, and the current is a bilinear read as the scalar part of an element product, carrying fixed units of the frame. Their normal ordering has a definite sign.
- **The two-point function as an operator-valued distribution.** The covariant anticommutator of the field is a $c$-number, and its biquaternion form is the deformed mass-shell scalar of the propagator article.

The division between what is standard and what belongs to the algebra is kept explicit throughout, as in the parents. Throughout, $c=1$, $\hbar$ is kept explicit only in the spin operator, and every derivative of the field is the corpus gradient $\tilde\nabla$ with its natural conjugate $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$; the only other derivative operator written is the one inside the source's own equation, quoted once below and not used.

## The Operator-Valued Field

The classical field is an element-valued map $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}:\mathbb{R}^{1,3}\to\mathbb{B}$; the quantized field is a map into operators,

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}:\mathbb{R}^{1,3}\longrightarrow \mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B},
$$

where $\mathcal{A}$ is the fermionic operator algebra generated by the mode operators. The field carries one algebra factor and one operator factor at every point; a product of two such fields multiplies the algebra factors as biquaternions and the operator factors as operators, and the two factors commute, so the order of a product is decided by the mode operators alone.

The fields of the expansion are

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x)=\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\vec p}}}
\sum_{r=1}^{2}\Big[a_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(r)}(\vec p)\,\exp(-ip\cdot x)
+b_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}(\vec p)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},-}^{(r)}(\vec p)\,\exp(+ip\cdot x)\Big],
$$

with $E_{\vec p}=+\sqrt{\vec p^2+m^2}$, $x=(r'_t,\vec r)$ and $p\cdot x=E_{\vec p}r'_t-\vec p\cdot\vec r$. The amplitudes $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},\pm}^{(r)}(\vec p)$ are elements of $\mathbb{B}$: they are the two branches of the biquaternion plane wave of the solutions article, the two $+$ amplitudes solving the amplitude equation there at the positive root of $\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=-m^2$ and the two $-$ amplitudes at the negative root, with $r=1,2$ counting the two solutions at each root. The mode anticommutators are

$$
\{a_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p),a_{\mathrm{fop},s}^{\dagger}(\vec q)\}
=\{b_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p),b_{\mathrm{fop},s}^{\dagger}(\vec q)\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta_{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\vec p-\vec q),
$$

all others vanishing.

**The Hermitian conjugate and the Dirac adjoint.** The dagger on the field carries every mode operator to its adjoint and the algebra factor through the star of the algebra,

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(x)=\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\vec p}}}
\sum_{r}\Big[a_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}(\vec p)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(r)*}(\vec p)\,\exp(+ip\cdot x)
+b_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},-}^{(r)*}(\vec p)\,\exp(-ip\cdot x)\Big],
$$

and the Dirac adjoint of the field is the Hermitian conjugate followed by right multiplication by $i\vec e_x$,

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(x)\,i\vec e_x
=\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\vec p}}}
\sum_{r}\Big[a_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}(\vec p)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(r)*}(\vec p)\,i\vec e_x\,\exp(+ip\cdot x)
+b_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},-}^{(r)*}(\vec p)\,i\vec e_x\,\exp(-ip\cdot x)\Big].
$$

The factor $i\vec e_x$ is the timelike member of the frame, and it is what makes the bilinear $\mathrm{Sc}\big(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}i\vec e_x\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\big)$ a Lorentz scalar. It is an element of the algebra and not a conjugation: the Dirac adjoint is an element product followed by the scalar part, and no conjugation by an element produces it.

**How a bilinear is read.** Throughout this article a bilinear of the field is the scalar part of a product of elements: an element standing on each side of the field, and the scalar part taken of the whole. The scalar part of $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}A\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}B$ is a number for any two elements $A$ and $B$, and every observable below is a normal-ordered integral of such a scalar part. No other reading of the product is used.

### The Conjugations of the Field

The algebra carries the quaternion conjugate ${}^{\natural}$, the complex conjugate $\bar{\cdot}$, the Hermitian conjugate ${}^{*}$ and the anti-Hermitian conjugate $\flat=-{}^{*}$. They lift to the field, and the lifting has two features worth recording.

First, the bar and the two operations built on it are antilinear, while ${}^{\natural}$ is complex-linear; the three that involve the quaternion factor — $\natural$, ${}^{*}$ and $\flat$ — reverse the order of a product, and the plain bar does not. The operator factors are conjugated as well: by the dagger for ${}^{*}$ and $\flat$, and by complex conjugation for $\bar{\cdot}$. The reversal of the algebra product and the reversal of the operator product are one reversal seen twice, because the two kinds of factor commute. For a mode operator the Hermitian conjugation is the standard $a_{\mathrm{fop}}\mapsto a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$.

Second, the conjugates are not new fields. The complex conjugate $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^*$ conjugates the algebra coefficients and leaves the mode operators alone; the Hermitian conjugate $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ carries the mode operators to their adjoints and the algebra factor through the natural conjugation as well as the bar. The two therefore differ by the natural conjugation of the algebra factor and by the mode content.

| Field | Mode content | Algebra content |
|---|---|---|
| $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}$ | $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$, $b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ | $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(r)}$, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},-}^{(r)}$ |
| $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ | $a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$, $b_{\mathrm{fop}}$ | $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(r)*}$, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},-}^{(r)*}$ |
| $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^*$ | $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$, $b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ | $\overline{\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(r)}}$, $\overline{\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},-}^{(r)}}$ |

The particle and antiparticle halves are exchanged by Hermitian conjugation, not by the algebra's real structure, and the third line carries the same mode content as the first with the phases reversed, which is what conjugating the exponentials does.

So the split of a fermion field into a "particle field" and an "antiparticle field" is the positive- and negative-frequency split of one operator field, and not a decomposition into an element of $\mathbb{B}$ and its real-structure conjugate. In particular the antiparticle is not obtained by applying $\flat$; that is the standing distinction between the algebra's real structure and the charge conjugation of the companion article on charged, neutral and truly neutral particles, and it survives quantization unchanged.

### The Field Equation as an Operator Equation

Because the mode expansion is a superposition of classical solutions with operator coefficients, and because the coefficients are constant in $x$, the field satisfies the free equation as an operator identity:

$$
\boxed{\;\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop},R}=\lambda_m^{-1}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}},L},
\qquad
\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop},L}=\lambda_m^{-1}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}},R}\;},
$$

the one-sided chiral pair, in which both gradients act from the left and the mass is the off-diagonal coupling of the two parts. The pair is one operator equation and not two: read on the pair $\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop},L},\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop},R}\bigr)$ it is $(\mathbb{D}-\lambda_m^{-1})$, with $\mathbb{D}$ the pair operator of *Operator and Calculus Conventions*, and the operator adds nothing to the algebra form. It is named here because the two-point function below is stated with it.

Two formulations carry Dirac's name and must not be read as one. The first is the one-sided pair above; the second is Kravchenko's two-sided equation for a biquaternion field $F$:

$$
\bigl(i\partial/\partial r'_t+\nabla\bigr)F-\lambda_m^{-1}\,i\,\overline{F}\,\vec e_z=0,
$$

with $\nabla$ the spatial gradient. In this equation the spatial gradient acts from the left and the mass from the right through $\vec e_z$, and the mass term carries the algebra's complex conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$. It is related to the one-sided pair by the dictionary $A$ of *The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form* but is a different operator; it is recorded here to keep the two apart and is not quantized in this article.

The operator ordering is immaterial in the field equation because the gradient differentiates only the $x$-dependent $c$-number amplitudes and the mode operators are $x$-independent. The equal-time anticommutator is likewise consistent with the equation: applying the gradient to the anticommutator of the field with its Dirac adjoint at two points, and using the mode algebra, reproduces the time derivative of the delta function required by the Hamiltonian, which is the field equation in bracket form. This is the standard consistency check of canonical quantization, recorded rather than rederived as in the parent.

## The Field as the Odd Generator

Fermion parity is the operator that counts the total number of quanta modulo two,

$$
(-1)^F=(-1)^{N_a+N_b},
\qquad
(-1)^F a_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p)\,(-1)^F=-a_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p),
\qquad
(-1)^F b_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p)\,(-1)^F=-b_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p),
$$

so that every odd operator anticommutes with $(-1)^F$.

Every term of the field expansion carries exactly one fermionic operator — $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$ or $b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ — so the field *anticommutes* with $(-1)^F$, and it is the odd generator of the operator algebra:

$$
\boxed{\;(-1)^F\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x)\,(-1)^F=-\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x),
\qquad
(-1)^F\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(x)i\vec e_x\,(-1)^F=-\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(x)i\vec e_x\;}.
$$

This was checked on the two-mode truncation of the algebra in the parent article's grading discussion, and it extends to the field term by term.

The parity has three consequences that organize the rest of the article.

**Observables are even.** A physical bilinear, such as the Dirac scalar $\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}i\vec e_x\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\bigr)$ or the current below, is quadratic in the field and therefore even, commuting with $(-1)^F$. This is why the physical content of the theory is in the bilinears: the field itself is not an observable, and the sign $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\mapsto-\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}$ is invisible to every gauge-invariant bilinear. The **observable algebra** is the even subalgebra of the field algebra; equivalently, the field is a square root of the observables.

**The grading is an algebra grading.** The operator algebra splits into an even part, commuting with $(-1)^F$, and an odd part, anticommuting with it, and products obey the graded rule: even$\cdot$even and odd$\cdot$odd are even, even$\cdot$odd is odd. The field operators anticommute with one another precisely because they are all odd. "Fermionic operators anticommute" and "the algebra is $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded with the field in the odd part" are the same statement.

**The grading and the $2\pi$ rotation agree on the field.** The companion article *The Spin–Statistics Theorem in Biquaternionic Form* shows that a rotation by $2\pi$ acts on the field by $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\mapsto-\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}$, because the field is of half-integer spin and the nontrivial covering element $-e_t$ multiplies each of its two parts by $-1$. Conjugation by $(-1)^F$ acts on the field in the same way, since every term of the expansion carries one fermionic operator. The two operations agree on the odd subspace; this agreement is the operator-level form of the spin–statistics sign $(-1)^{2s}=-1$ at $s=\tfrac12$, with $(-1)^F$ acting as that sign on the odd subspace. It is a consistency of two structures, and it does not by itself force the bracket — that force is the theorem's, imported from relativistic field theory.

The single-mode truncation of this structure lives *inside* $\mathbb{B}$. The spin–statistics companion computes the truncated ladder

$$
\boxed{\;\frac{\tilde S_+}{\hbar}=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y),
\qquad
\frac{\tilde S_-}{\hbar}=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y),
\qquad
\Bigl(\frac{\tilde S_+}{\hbar}\Bigr)^2=0,
\qquad
\Bigl\{\frac{\tilde S_+}{\hbar},\frac{\tilde S_-}{\hbar}\Bigr\}=e_t,
\qquad
(-1)^F=i\vec e_z\;},
$$

so that for one mode the mode algebra, the spin ladder and the parity element are all elements of $\mathbb{B}$: the two ladder elements have vanishing squares, their anticommutator is the unit, and the parity element is the central phase times a spatial unit. For the field they are not: the mode algebra is infinite-dimensional and the parity is external to the algebra. The two statements are compatible, and the parent records the gap between them.

## The Operator-Valued Observables

### Energy, Momentum, Charge

The canonical energy–momentum and charge of the quantized field are

$$
H=\int d^3r\,:\!\mathrm{Sc}\Bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\Bigl[-\sum_k i\,\vec e_k\,\frac{\partial\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}}{\partial R_k}\,\vec e_z+m\,i\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\,\vec e_x\Bigr]\Bigr)\!:,
$$

$$
P_{\mathrm{fop},k}=\int d^3r\,:\!\mathrm{Sc}\Bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\Bigl(-i\frac{\partial\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}}{\partial R_k}\Bigr)\Bigr)\!:,
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{fop}}=\sum_kP_{\mathrm{fop},k}\vec e_k,
\qquad
Ϙ_{\mathrm{fop}}=\int d^3r\,:\!\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\bigr)\!: .
$$

**The reduction of the frame.** A frame member enters a bilinear as a factor on the left of the field and a factor on the right, either of which may be the unit, and the reduction to the factors above is one structure constant together with $i^2=-1$. The kinetic term carries the spatial member: its left factor is $i\vec e_k$ and its right factor is $\vec e_y$, and right multiplication by $i\vec e_x$ follows, so the two right factors multiply as $\vec e_y\vec e_x=-\vec e_z$ and the two phases give $i^2=-1$, which leaves $-i\vec e_k$ on the left of the field and $\vec e_z$ on its right. The mass term carries the timelike member, whose left factor is the unit, so it reduces to $m\,i\vec e_x$ on the right with no factor on the left. The current below is reduced the same way, and the spin bilinear by its left factor alone; the reduction is a derived form of the frame's two-sided action on the field, and it uses no input beyond the multiplication of the units.

Substituting the mode expansion and normal ordering gives the parent's results,

$$
H=\sum_r\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\vec p}\big(a_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop},r}+b_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop},r}\big),
\qquad
Ϙ_{\mathrm{fop}}=\sum_r\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\big(a_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop},r}-b_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop},r}\big),
$$

with $P_{\mathrm{fop},k}=\sum_r\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,p_k(a_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop},r}+b_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop},r})$.

The normal-ordering sign is fixed by the anticommutator, and it is worth displaying because it is the whole fermionic content of the energy. With $\{b_{\mathrm{fop}},b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\}=\mathbf{1}$ one has $b_{\mathrm{fop}}b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{1}-b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop}}$, so the un-normal-ordered Hamiltonian

$$
H_{\text{raw}}=\sum_r\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\vec p}\big(a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop}}-b_{\mathrm{fop}}b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\big)
$$

becomes, after the substitution,

$$
H_{\text{raw}}=\sum_r\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\vec p}\big(a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop}}+b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop}}\big)+E_0,
\qquad
E_0=-2V\!\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\vec p},
$$

with $V$ the spatial volume and $E_0$ the negative, quartically divergent normal-ordering constant. The sign flip of the antiparticle term is what makes the energy bounded below: the raw expression has the antiparticle contribution with the opposite sign, and only the anticommutator turns it into a positive sum. On the two-mode truncation the identity

$$
a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop}}-b_{\mathrm{fop}}b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}=a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop}}+b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop}}-\mathbf{1}
$$

was verified exactly, with spectra $\{-1,0,0,1\}$ for the left side and $\{0,1,1,2\}$ for the number operator; the subtracted term is the identity of the one-mode space, and the positivity is the statement that the right side is a sum of two positive operators minus a $c$-number.

### The Biquaternion Form of the Operators

Because $i$ and the $\vec e_k$ are anti-Hermitian and $i$ is central, the four-momentum assembles into an operator-valued element of the material sector,

$$
\boxed{\;\tilde P_{\mathrm{fop}}=iH\,e_t+P_{\mathrm{fop},k}\,\vec e_k\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}\;},
$$

whose square is the operator mass shell

$$
\tilde P_{\mathrm{fop}}\tilde P_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\natural}=-\Big(H^2-\sum_kP_{\mathrm{fop},k}P_{\mathrm{fop},k}\Big)e_t,
$$

the operator counterpart of the classical condition $\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=-m^2$, where $\tilde P$ is the $c$-number wave biquaternion of the two-point function below. The charge is different in kind: $Ϙ_{\mathrm{fop}}$ is Hermitian and scalar in the algebra factor, hence central there,

$$
Ϙ_{\mathrm{fop}}=Ϙ_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{C},
$$

and it is the generator of the algebra's central $\mathrm{U}(1)$, the same phase that the minimal-coupling companion localizes. The energy–momentum is a material-sector operator and the charge is a central one; that is the operator-level form of the sector assignments the framework uses for the classical field, and it is the statement that the generator of translations is material while the generator of the internal phase is central.

### The Current

The conserved current is the bilinear

$$
J_t(x)=:\!\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\bigr)\!:,
\qquad
J_k(x)=:\!\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\vec e_k\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\vec e_z\bigr)\!:,
\qquad k=x,y,z,
$$

normal-ordered, Hermitian for all four components, and even under fermion parity. Each component is the frame's bilinear reduced at the place of use. The spatial member carries $\vec e_k$ before the field and $\vec e_y$ after it; the timelike member carries no factor before the field. The fixed factor $i\vec e_x$ of the conjugate follows in both, so the right factors multiply as $\vec e_y\vec e_x=-\vec e_z$ for the spatial component and as $\vec e_x\vec e_x=-\vec e_t$ for the timelike one, and the two phases contribute $i^2=-1$; for the timelike component the minus of $i^2$ and the minus of $\vec e_x\vec e_x=-\vec e_t$ cancel and leave the density with a plus sign. The timelike component is the density, a Hermitian form of the field with itself; each spatial component is the same form with the unit $\vec e_k$ inserted before the field and the fixed unit $\vec e_z$ after it.

Writing $\tilde J=iJ_t e_t+\sum_kJ_k\vec e_k\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, the conservation law is the operator equation

$$
\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde J\rangle_{\natural}(r_x)=0,
$$

on account of the field equation. The operator statement is inherited from the classical identity term by term, since each term of the normal-ordered bilinear is a $c$-number coefficient times a mode-operator monomial and the divergence acts on the coefficient only.

The check below is made on the classical bilinear, which is the $c$-number coefficient the operator statement descends from. Taking an element-valued field

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}(x)=c_1\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(1)}(\vec p_1)\exp(-ip_1\cdot x)+c_2\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},+}^{(2)}(\vec p_1)\exp(-ip_1\cdot x)
+d_1\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},-}^{(1)}(\vec p_2)\exp(+ip_2\cdot x),
$$

with $m=0.7$, $\vec p_1=(0.3,-0.9,1.1)$, $\vec p_2=(-0.5,0.2,0.45)$ and complex coefficients $c_1,c_2,d_1$, the four-divergence $\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde J\rangle_{\natural}$ of the bilinear was evaluated by central differences at a generic spacetime point. It vanishes to $5.3\times10^{-10}$ at step $h=10^{-5}$, which is the discretization error of the difference, and the same check on the full superposition of both frequency branches of both momenta gives $1.2\times10^{-10}$; a single plane wave gives $1.2\times10^{-11}$, the same error floor. A single plane wave tests only the mass-shell condition; the superposition tests the cancellation between the branches, which is the content of conservation.

In biquaternion terms the current is an element product read through the scalar part, and what keeps it conserved is the fixed frame element it carries. The minimal-coupling companion shows that the naive element-valued bilinear $i\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*$ is gauge invariant and in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ but is *not* conserved — its divergence does not vanish — and that the physical current needs the fixed right factor that the collapse above leaves, $\vec e_z$. The quantized current inherits that: its conservation is the operator statement $\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde J\rangle_{\natural}=0$ displayed above.

### Spin

The spin operator is the spatial integral of the bilinear with the informational-sector element multiplying the field from the left,

$$
S_k=\int d^3r\,:\!\mathrm{Sc}\Bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}\,\tilde S_k\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\Bigr)\!:,
\qquad
\tilde S_k=\tfrac{\hbar}{2}i\vec e_k .
$$

It is Hermitian and even, and it is the operator whose classical limit is the spin three-vector of the one-particle theory. The generator is the informational-sector element $\tilde S_k$ itself, and the bilinear realizes it on the field; the sector interpretation is the one the electron companion records.

## The Two-Point Function

**The operator written on an element.** The pair operator $\mathbb{D}$ of *Operator and Calculus Conventions* is defined there on the pair $\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$. Read on one element, which is the carrier of the field, its action is written out with the side in position,

$$
\mathbb{D}:\ \tilde X\longmapsto-\frac{\partial\tilde X}{\partial r'_t}\vec e_x-\sum_k\vec e_k\,\frac{\partial\tilde X}{\partial R_k}\vec e_y ,
\qquad
\mathbb{D}_p:\ \tilde X\longmapsto iE_{\vec p}\,\tilde X\vec e_x-i\sum_k p_k\,\vec e_k\tilde X\vec e_y ,
$$

the first the operator and the second its value on the plane wave $\exp(-iE_{\vec p}r'_t+i\vec p\cdot\vec r)$. In the time term the left factor is $e_t$, the unit of the algebra, so the term is a single right multiplication by $\vec e_x$; each spatial term carries $\vec e_k$ on the left and $\vec e_y$ on the right. The two imaginary units of the frame are already multiplied to $-1$, so no $i$ remains on the units, and both squares are scalars, $\mathbb{D}^2=\Box$ and $\mathbb{D}_p^2=p^2$, the cross terms cancelling between the time term and the three spatial ones.

The covariant anticommutator of the free quantized field is the object that carries the canonical structure, and it has two properties that the biquaternion framework reads directly.

**It is a $c$-number and it is local.** Every term of the product $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(y)i\vec e_x\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x)$ or $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(y)i\vec e_x$ is a product of at most two mode operators with $c$-number amplitudes. Terms carrying one particle and one antiparticle operator, $a_{\mathrm{fop}}b_{\mathrm{fop}}$ or $a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$, reverse sign when the two field factors are exchanged and cancel in the anticommutator; terms with one creation and one annihilation operator of the same species collapse to $c$-numbers through the anticommutators. Hence, for every element $\tilde X$,

$$
\boxed{\;\bigl\{\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x)\ \mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(y)i\vec e_x\,\tilde X\bigr)\bigr\}=(\mathbb{D}+\lambda_m^{-1})\,\Delta_{\mathrm{A}}(x-y)\ \tilde X\;},
$$

an element times the identity of $\mathcal{A}$ for every $\tilde X$: the field at $x$ multiplied by the number that pairs the field at $y$ with the test element, and on the right the operator of the display above applied to the same element. Here $\Delta_{\mathrm{A}}$ is the antisymmetric combination of the two branch phases, supported inside the light cone and vanishing for spacelike separation. This is the spin–statistics companion's covariant form, and it is the statement of microcausality for the anticommuting field.

**The commutator is a $c$-number as well, but it is not local.** Replacing the anticommutator by the commutator gives, for every element $\tilde X$,

$$
\langle0|\,[\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x),\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(y)i\vec e_x]\,|0\rangle\,\tilde X=(\mathbb{D}+\lambda_m^{-1})\,G_{\mathrm{S}}(x-y)\,\tilde X,
$$

with $G_{\mathrm{S}}$ the symmetric kernel, which does *not* vanish at spacelike separation. At equal times, in the normalization of the spin–statistics companion, with $\vec\Delta=\vec r-\vec y$,

$$
[\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(r_x),\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(r_y)]\,\tilde X
=i\Bigl(m\tilde X+\sum_k\vec e_k\,\tilde X\,\vec e_y\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_k}\Bigr)\vec e_x\,F(\vec\Delta),
\qquad
F(\vec\Delta)=\int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac{\cos(\vec p\cdot\vec\Delta)}{E_{\vec p}}
=\frac{m}{2\pi^2|\vec\Delta|}K_1\!\bigl(m|\vec\Delta|\bigr),
$$

a modified Bessel function of the second kind, nonzero for every $\vec\Delta\ne0$. The trailing $\vec e_x$ multiplies the whole bracket on the right, so every term is read with its sides in position: the mass term is a single right multiplication by $i\vec e_x$ once the outer $i$ is placed against it, with no factor on the left, and each spatial term, since $\vec e_y\vec e_x=-\vec e_z$, carries $\vec e_k$ on the left and $\vec e_z$ on the right — the same reduction the frame paragraph records. The mass term is the difference of the two spin sums of the solutions article, which is twice the mass, taken against the cosine. The gradient term is their sum, the spatial part of $\mathbb{D}$ read on the element with the derivative in place of the momentum; being odd in $\vec p$, it survives against the sine that the difference of the two phases leaves, and the integration is the companion's identity $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{p_k\sin(\vec p\cdot\vec\Delta)}{E_{\vec p}}=-\partial F/\partial\Delta_k$. The anticommutator pairs equal phases instead, keeps only the cosine, and is a contact term at equal times. The quantized biquaternion Dirac field, like any field of spin $\tfrac12$, therefore has a local anticommutator and a nonlocal commutator; both are $c$-numbers, and microcausality is the statement about the anticommutator.

**The biquaternion form.** Writing the wave biquaternion (the $c$-number symbol) $\tilde P=iE_{\vec p}\,e_t+\vec p$, with $p_0=E_{\vec p}$, so that $\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=-p^2$, the momentum-space two-point function of the propagator companion is the map

$$
S_F(p):\ \tilde X\longmapsto
\frac{i\bigl(i\tilde X\vec e_x p_{0}-\sum_k i\vec e_k\tilde X\vec e_y p_{k}+m\,\tilde X\bigr)}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}
=-\frac{i\bigl(i\tilde X\vec e_x p_{0}-\sum_k i\vec e_k\tilde X\vec e_y p_{k}+m\,\tilde X\bigr)}{\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}+m^2-i\epsilon}.
$$

Distributing the outer $i$ of the first form with $i^2=-1$ gives the equivalent numerator $-\tilde X\vec e_x p_0+\sum_k\vec e_k\tilde X\vec e_y p_k+i m\tilde X$. The numerator is $(\mathbb{D}_p+m)\tilde X$, the momentum symbol of the display above together with the mass, the mass term being the multiplication by $m$ and not a separate operator. The denominator is the deformed mass-shell scalar, and the deformation $-i\epsilon\,e_t$ lies in the centre and along the $i c r'_t$ axis of the material sector. The two-point function of the operator field is thus an operator-valued distribution whose biquaternion form is the mass-shell condition with the Feynman shift; the companion article's finding — that the algebra supplies the axis of the deformation but not its orientation — is inherited unchanged, and this article adds nothing to it.

## What Is Standard and What Is the Algebra's

**Standard field theory, written in the algebra.** The mode expansion and mode algebra; the operator-valued field equation; the parity grading of the mode algebra; the normal-ordered Hamiltonian, momentum, charge, current and spin; the $c$-number anticommutator and the nonlocal commutator; the biquaternion form of the two-point function. None of this is new, and all of it is the standard quantized Dirac field written in the parents' conventions.

**What the biquaternion structure supplies.**

- *The sector assignment of the observables.* Energy–momentum is an operator-valued element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$; spin is built from the $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ elements $i\vec e_k$; the charge is central. The classical sector interpretation survives quantization.
- *The field as the odd generator, with the structure realized inside $\mathbb{B}$ for one mode.* The single-mode coincidence $\tilde S_+/\hbar=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y)$, $(-1)^F=i\vec e_z$ is exact, and it is the one place where the fermionic structure and the algebra's own spin structure are the same elements.
- *The biquaternion form of the two-point function*, inheriting the deformed mass-shell denominator.
- *The separation of the four conjugations*, so that the particle–antiparticle exchange is Hermitian conjugation of the operator field and not the algebra's real structure.

**Open.**

- **The intrinsic Lagrangian and pairing.** The field is an element and the bilinears are scalar parts of element products, but whether a $\mathbb{B}$-intrinsic Lagrangian with a trace pairing leads to the same normal-ordered observables, or to a theory inequivalent to the parent's, is not settled. The parent leaves it open and this article does not close it.
- **The grading on the field.** The parity grading is realized in $\mathbb{B}$ only for one mode; for the field it is external, because the mode algebra is infinite-dimensional and does not embed in $\mathbb{B}$. Whether some enlarged structure carries it inside is open.
- **The current.** The physical current needs the fixed units of the frame, $\vec e_k$ before the field and $\vec e_z$ after it in the spatial components; whether a bilinear exists that is conserved and carries no fixed unit is open, as the minimal-coupling companion states.
- **Empirical content.** Whether the quantized field written in biquaternion form differs from the standard one in any observable is the framework's standing open question, and nothing here changes it.

## Summary

The quantized biquaternion Dirac field is the operator-valued element of $\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ whose expansion in the biquaternion plane waves carries the parent article's mode anticommutators and spin sums. It is an element of $\mathbb{B}$ at every point multiplied by an operator, it satisfies the free field equation as an operator identity, and its four conjugations act with the operator ordering included: the particle–antiparticle exchange is Hermitian conjugation, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}\leftrightarrow\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$, and not the algebra's real structure.

The field is odd under fermion parity,

$$
(-1)^F\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x)(-1)^F=-\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x),
$$

so the observables are the even bilinears, the operator algebra is $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded with the field in the odd part, and the grading agrees with the $2\pi$ rotation on the odd subspace. For one mode the entire structure is realized inside $\mathbb{B}$ through $\tilde S_+/\hbar=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y)$ and $(-1)^F=i\vec e_z$; for the field it is external.

The observables are the normal-ordered energy–momentum, charge, current and spin. The Hamiltonian is $H=\sum_r\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\vec p}(a_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{fop},r}+b_{\mathrm{fop},r}^{\dagger}b_{\mathrm{fop},r})$ after normal ordering, with the constant $E_0=-2V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\vec p}$; the sign flip of the antiparticle term is the anticommutator, verified on the two-mode truncation. The four-momentum assembles into the operator-valued material-sector element $\tilde P_{\mathrm{fop}}=iH e_t+P_{\mathrm{fop},k}\vec e_k\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, and the charge is central. The current is conserved as an operator equation, verified by finite differences on a superposition of two momenta and on both frequency branches, to $5.3\times10^{-10}$ and $1.2\times10^{-10}$ respectively.

The covariant anticommutator is a $c$-number and vanishes for spacelike separation; for every element $\tilde X$, $\{\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}(x)\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}(y)i\vec e_x\tilde X)\}=(\mathbb{D}+\lambda_m^{-1})\Delta_{\mathrm{A}}(x-y)\tilde X$. The commutator is a $c$-number as well but has support outside the light cone. The momentum-space two-point function is the propagator companion's map $S_F(p):\tilde X\mapsto-i(i\tilde X\vec e_x p_0-\sum_k i\vec e_k\tilde X\vec e_y p_k+m\tilde X)/(\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}+m^2-i\epsilon)$, whose biquaternion form is the deformed mass-shell condition.

# __Physics Units and Common Elements Conventions__

## Introduction

This article collects two catalogues, and they answer two different questions. The first fixes the physical units: the five dimensions of the corpus, the two tables of quantities, and the two writings of the temporal slot. The second fixes the glyphs a reader meets in every article: the tilde-marked elements, with their role, their formula, their dimension, their space and their meaning.

The temporal slot is a length in the default writing, as the three spatial slots are. The time in seconds is written $t'=r'_t/c$, and it is the conversion $c$ alone that separates the two, so the corpus writes one coordinate and not two.

The units belong to no element of the algebra. They enter where an element is identified with a physical quantity, and the identification is a convention of the article that makes it. The elements are algebraic, and their glyphs are fixed by *Algebra and Element Conventions*.

The article assumes the base of *Algebra and Element Conventions* and the marks of *Index and Notation Conventions*. It states conventions only. The proofs and the physical interpretations of the objects it names belong to the articles cited beside them.

**No contravariant index appears in this article, and none appears anywhere in the series.** The one metric is the unit metric $\delta_{\imath\jmath}=(1,1,1,1)$; a metric that is the identity raises nothing, so a raised index would name the same index twice. Every coefficient is written low, every contraction is written as a sum with the weight $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}$, and no letter carries a raised spacetime index, in a display, in a table or in a definition.

## Physical Units

**A unit element is not a physical unit.** The base $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ of *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Base and Its Units* is algebraic: its structure constants are $0$ or $\pm1$, and the biquaternion norm is a sum of four squares of coefficients. No element of $\mathbb{B}$ carries a metre, a second or a kilogram. A physical unit enters only where an element is **identified** with a physical quantity, and that identification is a convention of the article that makes it, not a property of the algebra. The phrase *unit element* names a basis element and *physical unit* names a convention of measurement; the two are never exchanged. The word *base* carries both senses in this article, and the context decides: the base of the algebra is the four elements, and a base unit is invertible in the SI.

**The series uses the SI.** Length is measured in metres, time in seconds, mass in kilograms, temperature in kelvin and charge in coulomb. Four of the five are base units of the SI. The coulomb is not: the base unit of current is the ampere, and the coulomb is $\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{A\,s}$.

**The word *fundamental* has four senses, and the corpus uses the first.** A quantity can be called fundamental because its dimension is independent, because its unit is a base unit of the SI, because its defining constant is fixed exactly, or because nature supplies it. The four do not agree. The SI has seven base units — metre, second, kilogram, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela — and since 2019 it fixes seven constants: the caesium-133 hyperfine frequency $\delta f_{\mathrm{Cs}}$, the speed of light $c$, the Planck constant $h$, the elementary charge $e$, the Boltzmann constant $k_B$, the Avogadro constant $N_A$ and the luminous efficacy $\mathrm{Ki}_{\mathrm{cd}}$. Every unit, base or not, is realised from those seven. A base unit is therefore a bookkeeping choice, and no unit is fundamental in the sense that nature supplies it. The corpus uses the first sense: a quantity is fundamental when its dimension is one of the five, and any other dimension is a product of powers of them.

**The dimension letters stand only in these tables.** The corpus writes a dimension in upright roman — $\mathrm{L}$ for length, $\mathrm{T}$ for time, $\mathrm{M}$ for mass, $\Theta$ for temperature and $\mathrm{Q}$ for charge — and a derived dimension as a product of powers, $\mathrm{M}\,\mathrm{L}^{2}\,\mathrm{T}^{-2}$. Three of the five letters already carry another meaning: $\mathsf M_2$ is the matrix map, $\mathsf T_{\imath\jmath}$ the energy–momentum tensor, and $\Theta$ the complex angle of *Algebra and Element Conventions*. A dimension letter therefore stands only in the tables of this article — the two of the quantities and the five of the glyphs — and never in an expression.

**The corpus keeps charge as an independent dimension with a derived unit.** The corpus writes charge and not current, so $\mathrm{Q}$ stays a fifth dimension, independent of the other four, while its unit is built from the ampere. The mole and the candela are not used, and current never appears as a base quantity of its own.

**A multiple is the same dimension.** Energies are quoted in electronvolts, $1\ \mathrm{eV}=1.602\,176\,634\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$, which is exact because $e$ is; momenta in $\mathrm{MeV}/c$; and lengths in femtometres. A prefix, or a division by $c$, changes the number and never the dimension.

**The coefficients of one element share one unit.** The biquaternion norm is a sum of four squares of the coefficients of a single element, so the four terms are commensurable only if the four coefficients carry the same unit. The requirement is per element and not across elements: the coefficients of a four-displacement are lengths and its norm a length squared, the coefficients of a four-velocity are speeds and its norm a speed squared, the temporal slot of a four-position carries the length $r'_t$ rather than the time $t'$, and the temporal slot of a four-potential carries the parameter $a'_t=\xi_{\mathrm{scp}}/c$ rather than $\xi_{\mathrm{scp}}$ so that its four components share the unit of the vector potential. The field strength needs no adjustment: $\tilde F=i\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E-\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$ is one number, and $\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E$ and $\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$ each carry the dimension $\mathrm{M}^{1/2}\,\mathrm{L}^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{T}^{-1}$. The unit is the unit of the quantity the element represents, and it is supplied by the identification.

**The scalar coefficient is a minuscule.** A labelled element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ or $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ carries one temporal coordinate and three spatial ones, and the temporal coefficient names that single coordinate, so it is written in lower case whether it is real or pure imaginary: $\tilde P_{\Phi}=ip'_te_t+\vec p_{\Phi}$, $\tilde V_{\Phi}=iv'_te_t+\vec v_{\Phi}$, $\tilde J_{\Phi}=ic\rho\,e_t+\vec J_{\Phi}$, $\tilde A_{\Phi}=ia'_te_t+\vec A_{\Phi}$, $\tilde R_\Phi=ir'_te_t+\vec r_\Phi$. The majuscule is reserved for the element, $\tilde P_{\Phi}$ and $\tilde R_\Phi$, and for a vector part with three components, $\vec J_{\Phi}$.

**The two writings of the temporal slot.** The temporal coordinate is a length, like the three spatial ones. In the default writing the material position is

$$
\tilde R_\Phi = i r'_t\,e_t+\vec r_\Phi ,
$$

with $r'_t$ in metres and $c$ the local speed of light at the point of the element. The four coefficients are lengths, the metric is the unit metric, $\delta_{\imath\jmath}=(1,1,1,1)$, and the quadratic form is $\langle\tilde R_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=-{r'_{t}}^{2}+r_x^2+r_y^2+r_z^2$, the minus arising from $i^2$ alone. In the second writing the material time is $t'=r'_t/c$, in seconds, and the same element reads $\tilde R_\Phi=i c\,t'\,e_t+\vec r_\Phi$; the quadratic form is $\langle\tilde R_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}=-c^2{t'}^{2}+r_x^2+r_y^2+r_z^2$ and the time–time entry of the metric is $g_{tt}=-c^2$. The two are one coordinate in two units, $r'_t=c\,t'$, and $c$ is the only conversion between them. An article that writes the second states it where the temporal slot is read. The informational part sits in the same coordinate, $\tilde H_\Psi=h_t\,e_t+i\vec h_\Psi$ with $h_t$ in metres, so that the full element of the biquaternion algebra is $\tilde Q=(h_t+i r'_t)e_t+(\vec r_\Phi+i\vec h_\Psi)$: one complex length in each of the four slots, and no factor of $c$ in any of them.

**The length reading is the default because the operators rank it there.** The gradient is $\tilde\nabla_\Phi=\sum_{\imath} e_{\imath}\,\partial/\partial R_{\imath}$ and carries $\mathrm{L}^{-1}$, so every derivative it contains carries that unit. With $r'_t$ in metres the temporal derivative $i\,\partial/\partial r'_t$ is homogeneous with $D_3=\vec e_x\,\partial/\partial r_x+\vec e_y\,\partial/\partial r_y+\vec e_z\,\partial/\partial r_z$ and with the mass wave number $\nu_m=\frac{m\,c}{h}$, and a Dirac-type equation sums terms of one unit. With $r'_t$ in seconds the same derivative carries $\mathrm{L}\,\mathrm{T}^{-1}$, which is the unit of none of its neighbours. The seconds are therefore written where a clock is read — a rate, a lifetime, a decay — and the element keeps the length.

**Dimensionless and dimensionful.** A pure number — the fine-structure constant $\alpha$, the ratio $g=2$, the phase $\frac{2\pi S}{h}$, the trace normalisation $2$ — carries no unit and no conversion. An exponent is a pure number, and the coupling is what keeps it one: a phase is written $\frac{2\pi S}{h}$, a holonomy is written $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{gau}})$, and the line integral is not an exponent on its own, because $\oint_C\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath} A_{\imath} dx_{\imath}$ carries $\mathrm{Q}$ and no choice of $c$, $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ or $G$ removes it. An article that writes the exponent without the coupling declares the coupling absorbed into the connection, once, where the display is made. A dimensionful constant is invertible conversion and is written with its unit. No scale is derived by the algebra: $c$, $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, $k_B$, $G$, $m$ and $ϙ$ are inserted at the places where the structure requires one. The metrology says the same thing from the other side. The defining constants above are fixed by definition and the invertibles are realised from them; $\alpha$ and $G$ are the ones that have to be measured, and $\alpha$ is the dimensionless one of the two. No article is permitted to set one of these constants to one: $c$, $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, $k_B$, $G$, $m$ and $ϙ$ are carried, and so are $\epsilon$ and $\mu$. **The ban is on every unit declaration, in every spelling.** A sentence that declares a system of units in which a constant is one — *in units where $c=1$*, *in Planck units*, *with $m=\frac{h}{2\pi}=\omega=1$* — is a defect even when no later display repeats the setting, because the declaration is what licenses the deleted conversion downstream. The ban is not a list of three shapes and it is not a rule about the glyph $\frac{h}{2\pi}$; it names a class. Any span or sentence that assigns the value one to a physical constant, or to a scale built from one, belongs to the class, and the reason is given in the paragraphs that follow.

The angle is $\theta_{\mathrm{gau}}$, the integrated gauge function over $\frac{h}{2\pi}$: the charge and the potential are real, so the angle is real.

**The shortcut $c=1$ together with $h$ at $2\pi$ is not allowed.** An article may not set $h$ to $2\pi$ or $c$ to one, and a writing that does is a defect in that article rather than a convention of it. The same holds for every other setting of a constant to one — $\frac{h}{2\pi}=m=1$, $\frac{h}{2\pi}=k_B=1$, $\frac{h}{2\pi}=c=k_B=1$, $m=\frac{h}{2\pi}=\omega=1$, $G=c=1$, $m=c=1$, $\varkappa=1$, $\mu=1$ — and for the prose that introduces such a setting. A chain of the form $\cdots=1$ inside a span is read as a declaration of units unless the one is a value the sentence computes; the corpus has no third reading, and an author who means a quantum number writes the letter that names it, not a constant. Both are defining constants of the SI, fixed exactly, $c=299\,792\,458\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $h=6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$, and setting one to one is not a choice of gauge but the deletion of a conversion between two base dimensions. With $c=1$ the dimension $\mathrm{T}$ becomes $\mathrm{L}$ and the first table loses a row; with $h$ at $2\pi$ the dimension of action becomes $1$ and a momentum becomes a reciprocal length. The two deletions together collapse $\mathrm{L}$, $\mathrm{T}$ and $\mathrm{M}$ onto one dimension, and from that point no dimensional statement in the article can be tested, because every candidate mismatch has been legitimised in advance.

**The same rule in the quantisation.** A commutator, a measure and a mode amplitude each carry $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, and a display that drops it is the shortcut above in one more place. A canonical commutator is $[\xi_{\mathrm{fop}},\pi_{\mathrm{fop}}]=i \frac{h}{2\pi}\,\delta^{(3)}(\vec r-\vec r')$; a creation–annihilation pair obeys $[a_{\vec p},a_{\vec p^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=h^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\vec p-\vec p^{\,\prime})$ with the measure $\frac{d^3p}{h^{3}}$, which is the pairing that makes $\int\frac{d^3p}{h^{3}}a^{\dagger}a$ a number and $\int\frac{d^3p}{h^{3}}E_{\vec p}\,a^{\dagger}a$ an energy; the same $h$ per dimension carries into $\frac{d^4p}{h^{4}}$. A mode amplitude is $\sqrt{\frac{h c}{4\pi E_{\vec p}}}$ and not $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\vec p}}}$, because the field it multiplies carries an inverse length and the amplitude must supply the $\frac{h}{2\pi} c$ that turns an energy into a length; the invariant measure is $\frac{d^3p}{h^{3}}\frac{h c}{4\pi E_{\vec p}}$, which is the squared modulus of that amplitude, and a photon amplitude written through an angular frequency is $\sqrt{\frac{c}{2\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}}}}$. The phase of a plane wave is $\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pwp}})$ with $p\cdot x=\frac{E_{\vec p}}{c}r'_t-\vec p\cdot\vec r$ an action, and the same phase read through the material element is $\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pwp}})$; $p\cdot x$ and $\mathrm{Sc}(\dots)$ are dimensionful, and the division by $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ is what makes the exponent a number. A display that writes $\exp(-ip\cdot x)$ over a dimensionful $p\cdot x$, or $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E}}$, or $(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}$, has set $h$ to $2\pi$ at that place, and the setting is a defect there.

Here $\theta_{\mathrm{pwp}}:=\frac{2\pi p\cdot x}{h}=\omega_{\vec p}r'_t-\vec p\cdot\vec r$ is the plane-wave angle in momentum notation: $p$ and $x$ are real, so the exponent is $i$ times a real number and the mode factor is a phase of modulus one.

**A frequency is a rate; a mode energy is $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ times it.** The symbol $\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}}$ is an angular frequency, an inverse time, and the energy of the mode it labels is $hf_{\mathrm{frq},\nu_{\Phi}}$; a display that writes the energy itself as $\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}}$ has set $h$ to $2\pi$ at that place. Because the corpus's temporal coordinate is the length $r'_t$, the phase of a plane wave is $\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}=2\pi\,\vec\nu_\Phi\cdot(r_x\,\vec e_x+r_y\,\vec e_y+r_z\,\vec e_z)-\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}} r'_t/c$: an inverse time multiplies a length only after division by $c$, and a display that writes $\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}})$ over an inverse-time $\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}}$ has set $c=1$ in the exponent. Where an article works with a **material frequency** already in inverse length — the case of a mode equation $\frac{d^2u}{dr_t^{\prime 2}}+\omega^2u=0$ — the phase is $\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}})$ with no $c$, the material four-wavevector is $\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi=i \nu_t^{\prime}e_t+\vec\nu_\Phi$, and the article says so once; the two readings are never mixed inside one display, because a mixture is a factor $c$ that no longer shows.

The angle is $\theta_{\mathrm{pwt}}$, a real frequency times a real interval, $\omega r'_t/c$ for a length and $\omega r'_t$ for a time, so the angle is real.

**A rate against a length: which way the factor goes.** With $r'_t$ a length, a display that equates a rate with a derivative in $r'_t$ has set $c=1$ there. Three writings carry the factor: $c\,d/dr'_t$ is a time derivative, $\omega r'_t/c$ is a phase, and $\omega/c$ is a twist per unit material length. A kinematic equation puts it on the rate, $d\vec e_\eta/dr'_t=c^{-1}\vec\omega\times\vec e_\eta$; a dynamical equation puts it on the derivative, $c\,d\vec L/dr'_t=\vec\tau$, so that the angular momentum, the torque and the moment of inertia keep their usual units; and a Lagrangian written on $r'_t$ carries $c^{2}$ in the kinetic term, $\tfrac12I(c\,d\theta/dr'_t)^{2}$. The rotor logarithm $2\tilde u^{\natural}d\tilde u/dr'_t$ is a twist, an inverse length, and the precession rate it carries is $c$ times that twist; an article that uses both symbols says which is which where they meet. A phase integral is the check: $\oint\vec\omega_b^{\parallel}\,dr'_t$ is a number with $\vec\omega_b$ a twist, and it is $\oint(\omega^{\parallel}/c)\,dr'_t$ with $\omega$ a rate.

**The mode amplitude and the measure travel together.** An amplitude is fixed by the measure it is integrated against and is not a free choice: with $\frac{d^3p}{h^{3}}$ it is $\sqrt{\frac{h c}{4\pi E_{\vec p}}}$, and with $d^{3}\nu_{\Phi}$ it is $\sqrt{\frac{c}{2\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}}}}$. The two are one object, because $p=h\,\nu_{\Phi}$ and $E_{\vec p}=hf_{\mathrm{frq},\nu_{\Phi}}$ convert either display into the other, so a photon amplitude written $\sqrt{c/(2\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}})}$ beside the wave-number measure carries exactly the $\frac{h}{2\pi} c$ that the scalar amplitude carries beside the momentum measure. An amplitude written through the SI constants, $\sqrt{\frac{h}{2\pi 2\epsilon_0\omega_{\nu_{\Phi}}}}$, differs from these by the $\sqrt{\epsilon_0}$ that the field-strength biquaternion $\tilde F=i\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E-\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$ carries in the field and not in the mode; it is correct in SI and is not the corpus's display, and the two must not be mixed within one expansion.

**One label per commutator.** A ladder commutator names its two operators with one species of label: two momenta, $\big[a_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec p),a_{\mathrm{fop},s}^{\dagger}(\vec p^{\,\prime})\big]=h^{3}\delta_{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\vec p-\vec p^{\,\prime})$, or two wave numbers, $\big[a_{\mathrm{fop},r}(\vec\nu_\Phi),a_{\mathrm{fop},s}^{\dagger}(\vec\nu_2)\big]=\delta_{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\vec\nu_\Phi-\vec\nu_2)$. A display that pairs a momentum with a wave number and writes $\delta^{(3)}(\vec p-h\,\vec\nu_\Phi)$ is dimensionally admissible but names one operator family by two letters, and the reader cannot tell whether $\vec\nu_\Phi$ is an independent mode or the same mode in other units.

**The medium constants are not set to one either.** $\epsilon$ and $\mu$ are the vacuum permittivity and permeability of the table, measured and dimensionful, and $\epsilon=1$ beside $\mu=1$ forces $c=1$ through $c^2=1/(\epsilon\mu)$: the two settings are one setting, and the rule above already forbids it. An article that treats the vacuum as a medium may set the **relative** values $\epsilon_r=\mu_r=1$, and it keeps $\epsilon_0$, $\mu_0$ and $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon_0\mu_0}$ in the display; the field strength $\tilde F=i\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E-\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$, whose two terms carry one unit, is then a statement about the relative values with the common constant $\sqrt{\epsilon_0}$ in front. A source's $\vec E+i\vec B$ is the same object with the magnetic field quoted in units of $\vec E/c$; that is a change of unit for one quantity and not a setting of $c$. Heaviside–Lorentz units, where $4\pi\epsilon_0=1$, are excluded for the same reason: they delete a conversion between the charge and the field.

**A ratio with a mass in the denominator is read by its dimension.** The letter $m$ is admissible where the ratio it forms is a dimension proper to the quantity, and it must be written $mc$ or $mc^2$ where the numerator fixes another dimension. $\frac{p^2}{2m}$ is an energy and stays. A momentum over a mass is a velocity, and the vertex factor of a Gordon decomposition is $\frac{(p+p_2)_{\imath}}{2mc}$, a ratio of velocities and dimensionless, so a wave number in the same place is $\frac{\nu_{\Phi}}{2\nu_m}$. An energy over a mass needs $c^2$: the non-relativistic reduction $\frac{1}{E+mc^2}\to\frac{1}{2mc^2}$ carries it, and $\frac{1}{2m}$ in that place is the shortcut. The test is always the numerator: a momentum squared over a mass is an energy and is written so, and every other numerator asks for the $c$ that closes the dimension.

**The quantum of action is never written over a mass.** A fraction that puts $h$ above the line and a mass below it is not written. The shapes are $\frac{h}{m\,c}$, $\frac{h}{2\pi m\,c}$, $\frac{h}{2\pi m}$, $\frac{h}{4\pi m}$, $\frac{h}{2m}$, and their squares $\frac{h^{2}}{2m}$ and $\frac{h^{2}}{8\pi^{2}m}$, together with every display built from them. The corpus names each quotient already, and the substitution is exact: $\frac{h}{m\,c}=\frac{1}{\nu_m}$, $\frac{h}{2\pi m\,c}=\frac{1}{2\pi\nu_m}$, $\frac{h}{2\pi m}=\frac{c}{2\pi\nu_m}$, $\frac{h}{4\pi m}=\frac{c}{4\pi\nu_m}$, $\frac{h}{2m}=\frac{c}{2\nu_m}$, $\frac{h^{2}}{2m}=\frac{h\,c}{2\nu_m}$ and $\frac{h^{2}}{8\pi^{2}m}=\frac{h\,c}{8\pi^{2}\nu_m}$. The reason is the one of §*A quotient that cancels the mass is not a wave number* read the other way round: a quotient of $h$ by a mass is a length, or a squared length over a time, and the mass wave number is the letter the corpus gave that length. Written through $\nu_m$, the mass stays on the page as a wave number, the dimension is a power of $\mathsf{L}^{-1}$, and the coefficient is checked without recomputing where the action went. Two exceptions remain. The defining displays keep the fraction, $\nu_m=\frac{m\,c}{h}$, $\vec\nu_\Phi=\frac{\vec p_\Phi}{h}$, $\nu_t^{\prime}=\frac{E}{h\,c}$ and $\lambda_m=\frac{h}{m\,c}$, and a derivation that reaches the definition from the mass shell writes it once, $\nu_m^{2}=-\frac{\langle\tilde P_\Phi\rangle_{\natural}}{h^{2}}$. A numerical value is quoted in the substituted form, $c/(4\pi\nu_{m_e})=5.788\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}$ for the electron.

**Why the algebra forbids it in particular.** The coefficients of one element share one unit because the biquaternion norm is a sum of four squares (§*The coefficients of one element share one unit* above). The temporal slot of the material position is $i r'_t\,e_t$, and $r'_t$ is a length like $r_x,r_y,r_z$; the four coefficients are commensurable because the coordinate is, and no factor is written beside it to make them so. An article that sets $c=1$ while writing the temporal slot as $i t'\,e_t$ has put a time where a length is required, and has broken the commensurability that the norm requires in the other direction. The same holds for $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, which is the coupling between the wave and the phase: the phase is $\frac{2\pi S}{h}$, the wave four-vector is $\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}=\frac{\tilde P_\Phi}{h}$, and the coefficient of a mass term is $\lambda_m^{-1}=\nu_m$. Setting $h$ to $2\pi$ makes $p$ a wave number and $m$ a wave number, and the three lengths of the table — $\lambda_m$, $\lambda_\Phi$, $\lambda_t$ — all collapse onto the reciprocals they are computed from. The letter $m$ then names a reciprocal length in one article and a mass in the next, which is the ambiguity that the mass-length convention exists to remove.

**What the norm shows.** The norm is a function of one biquaternion and is never written bare. On the free material element $\tilde Q_{\Phi}$ it is $\langle\tilde Q_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}=N(\tilde Q_{\Phi})=\tilde Q_{\Phi}\tilde Q_{\Phi}^{\natural}=-E^{2}/c^{2}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}$ in momentum units, and $N(\tilde Q_{\Phi})=-E^{2}/(\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}c^{2})+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}/\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}$ in wave-number units, the two terms in each writing carrying one dimension. On the particle's own momentum the norm is the fixed number $N(\tilde P_{\Phi})=-m^{2}c^{2}$, with $\tilde P_{\Phi}=m\tilde V_{\Phi}$ and $\langle\tilde V_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}$. The **mass-shell operator** is the difference of the two, $\mathcal{M}(\tilde Q_{\Phi})=N(\tilde Q_{\Phi})-N(\tilde P_{\Phi})=N(\tilde Q_{\Phi})+m^{2}c^{2}$, so that a propagator denominator $\mathcal{M}(\tilde Q_{\Phi})-i\epsilon$ is a difference of like quantities, each term carrying $\mathrm{M}^{2}\,\mathrm{L}^{2}\,\mathrm{T}^{-2}$, and the mass term $m^{2}c^{2}$ is added to a term of its own dimension. The same holds through the Matsubara denominator $\omega_n^{2}/c^{2}+\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}/\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}+4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}$ and through every propagator pole, which is the zero set $N(\tilde Q_{\Phi})=-m^{2}c^{2}$.

**The norm is the only symbol that names the pole.** No second letter repeats the same number. A second letter would put a subtraction of two squares on the page with no $i$ in view, and read apart from its bridge sentence it would look like a second metric. It would also hide the dimension: a subtraction of two squares is licensed by $c=1$ and by nothing else, and a reader in natural units cannot see the fault there. Written as $N$, the pole carries its four coefficients and their one unit at every site, so the dimension count that catches these at sight stays available.

**What is written instead.** The constants are carried, and the quantum of action is written by its own letter, $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, never by a quotient of a mass and a wave number that happens to equal it. The mass wave number $\nu_m=\frac{m\,c}{h}$ is written where a wave number is the object — the coefficient of a mass term in an operator, a phase, a dispersion relation, a propagator pole — so that a momentum is $h\,\nu_m=mc$ and an energy is $m\,c^{2}=mc^2$; the mass length is the inverse, $\lambda_m=1/\nu_m=\frac{h}{mc}$, and one radian of it is the quotient $1/(2\pi\nu_m)$, written only when a distance is what the sentence needs. A numerical example may quote its numbers as **multiples of the scale of the quantity**, because that is a change of unit for one quantity and it leaves the dimension in place: $E=2mc^2$, $p=0.3\,mc$, $\sigma^2=\frac{c}{4\pi\nu_m\omega}$, $l=3/\nu_m$ and $S=4k_B$ are admissible, $m=1$, $c=1$, $\frac{h}{2\pi}=k_B=1$ and $\frac{h}{2\pi}=m=\omega=1$ are not. The admissible form names the scale on the page; the forbidden form names the constant and drops its scale. A check that needs the arithmetic done writes the ratio, $\sigma^2\big/(\frac{c}{4\pi\nu_m\omega})=1$, and never the declaration that would make the ratio read as $1$. Planck units are excluded by the same rule, since $G=1$ beside $c=1$ and $h$ at $2\pi$ deletes a third conversion and takes the kilogram with it.

**The Planck constant is written $h$, and the barred letter is never used.** Every article of the physical corpus writes the Planck constant as $h$ and as no other symbol. Where the quantum of action over $2\pi$ is meant, the corpus writes $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ and leaves the $2\pi$ on the page; it never writes the single barred letter that stands for that quotient, in any spelling, because the letter hides the $2\pi$ and the reader cannot check a phase against it. A display that reintroduces the barred letter is a defect of the article that carries it, and a sentence that declares it a unit is a defect of the same kind. The letter $h$ alone carries the Planck constant, and the substitution that removes the barred form is a replacement of the symbol by its value, never a setting of the value to one.

**No reduced length, no reduced constant.** The rule of the paragraph above has a twin, and it is the same rule read on a length: the corpus writes no reduced quantity of any kind. There is no reduced wavelength, no reduced Compton length, and no phase length $\lambda/(2\pi)$ under a letter of its own. The wavelength is the inverse of the wave number and is one full turn of the phase, $\lambda_m=1/\nu_m=h/(mc)$, $\lambda_\Phi=1/\nu_\Phi=h/p_\Phi$ and $\lambda_t=1/\nu_t'=h\,c/E$; one radian of the same phase spans the quotient $\lambda/(2\pi)=1/(2\pi\nu)$, and the quotient is written where the radian is meant, with the $2\pi$ left on the page. The reasons are the reasons of the barred constant. A letter for the quotient hides the $2\pi$, so a reader cannot check a phase against it; and the adjective *reduced* invites the reader to take the shorter length for the wavelength, which loses a factor $2\pi$ in every ratio built on it. The two differ by $6.283$: the electron's Compton wavelength is $2.426\ \mathrm{pm}$ and one radian of it is $386.159\ \mathrm{fm}$, and both numbers appear in the corpus with their definition beside them. A display that introduces a barred or reduced length is a defect of the article that carries it, and the substitution that removes it is a replacement of the symbol by the quotient it stands for, never a redefinition of $\lambda$.

**A quotient that cancels the mass is not a wave number.** The identity $\frac{h}{2\pi}=mc/(2\pi\nu_m)$ is exact, so the fraction $\frac{m\,c}{2\pi\nu_m}$ equals $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, the fraction $\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}}$ equals $\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}$, the fraction $\frac{m\,c^{2}\,i}{2\pi\nu_m}$ equals $i \frac{h}{2\pi} c$, and the fraction $\frac{2\pi\nu_m\,i}{m\,c}$ equals $\frac{2\pi i}{h}$. In each of them the mass is written once explicitly and once through the wave number, the two cancel, and what remains is a power of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$. The symbol then carries nothing of its own: it is a placeholder for the constant it hides, and the reader cannot see the quantum of action in the equation in front of him. Written that way the exchange rate between mass and distance is also lost, since both ends of it have been divided out. Such a fraction is written as the power of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ it is. The letter $\nu_m$ survives where a mass, a momentum or an energy is genuinely absorbed into a wave number, which is where the wave number is the object itself: the mass term $\nu_m^{2}$ of the Klein–Gordon operator and of a propagator pole, the rest phase $2\pi\nu_m r'_t$, the range $e^{-2\pi\nu_m r}/r$ of a massive exchange, the mass-shell norm $\langle\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}=-\nu_m^{2}$, and a ratio of two wave numbers such as $\nu_m/\nu_{\Phi}$. The test settles the question at sight: where the mass cancels inside the fraction, the fraction was $\frac{h}{2\pi}$.

**A caution on $S$.** The letter $S$ carries three meanings: the action, the spin $\tilde S_{\Psi,\eta}$, and the entropy of the thermodynamics articles. The action and the spin share the unit $\mathrm{J\,s}$; the entropy is $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. The subscript and the article separate the three, and an article that uses two of them says which is which.

**A wave number and its inverse are one fact read two ways.** A wave number is a count of wavelengths per unit length, so its reciprocal is a wavelength, and the distance per radian is the reciprocal of the angular form, $1/(2\pi\nu_m)$; the table above gives the three of the series in both readings. The wave number is what a derivative returns, because every differentiation carries an inverse length, so it is the form in which the equations are written and the form a coefficient of an operator takes. The distance is what a laboratory measures, so it is the form in which the readings are stated: a resolution, a fringe spacing, a light-travel length. An article that names both for the same object writes the inversion once, $1/\nu_m=\lambda_m$, and never introduces the distance as a second definition. The general reading, with the three distances and what each one measures, is in *The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle*.

**A caution on $\nu$.** The letter $\nu$ is the wave-number letter of this corpus. The wave numbers of a particle and of the Euclidean rotation are $\nu_m$, $\nu_{\Phi}$, $\nu_t^{\prime}$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{eu}}$, and a bare $\nu$ names none of them, because four share the letter. **The space index is $\eta$, and $\nu$ is never an index.** The basis is $\vec e_\eta$ with $\eta=1,2,3$ — or $\eta=x,y,z$ — and the summation is $\sum_\eta$ and $\sum_{\eta=1}^{3}$. The letter $k$ survives only as a label on a countable family: a rank, as in $T^{(k)}$; a dimension, as in $\mathrm{U}(k)$ and $2k$; or a lattice, mode or level label, as in $x_k$ of the time-sliced integral, the frequency $\omega_k$ of a mode whose wave vector is $\vec\nu_\Phi$, the mode weights $A_k$ with $\sum_{k,l}A_kG(\omega_k-\omega_l)A_l^{*}$, the Fock levels $|k\rangle$ and $\rho_{kk}$, and the exponents $\lambda_k$. These are labels on a countable family, not directions in space, so they keep $k$ and never take $\eta$; no bare $k$ is a space index and no bare $k$ is a wave number. **A quotient that cancels the mass is not a wave number**, and the rule with its examples is in §*A quotient that cancels the mass is not a wave number* above. The loop momentum $\nu_{\imath}$ of the QFT amplitudes is a wave number and takes the letter. The three wave numbers of a particle are read off one element, $\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}=\frac{\tilde P_\Phi}{h}=i\nu_t^{\prime}e_t+\vec\nu_\Phi$, defined in *The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle*: its scalar coefficient is $\nu_t^{\prime}=\frac{E}{h\,c}$, its vector part is $\vec\nu_\Phi=\frac{\vec p_\Phi}{h}$ with modulus $\nu_{\Phi}$, and the modulus of the element itself is $\nu_m=\frac{m\,c}{h}$. Two neighbours are to be told apart from these: $\nu_{\mathrm{eu}}$ is the Euclidean momentum after a Wick rotation, whose square is non-negative, and the component spelling of the same element is $\nu_t^{\prime}-i\vec\nu_\Phi=-i\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}$, with $\vec\nu_\Phi=\frac{\vec p}{h}$, both entries inverse lengths. No display mixes them. **The neighbouring spellings are fixed with it.** The component spelling $\nu_{\Phi,\imath}$ carries the direction label $\mu$ of $e_{\imath}$, with $\nu_{\Phi,t}=\nu_t^{\prime}$ and $\nu_{\Phi,x},\nu_{\Phi,y},\nu_{\Phi,z}$ the three components of $\vec\nu_\Phi$; a wave four-vector is therefore $\nu_{\Phi,\imath}$, with the index structure of the display it stands in. The evanescent decay constant is $\nu_{\Phi,\mathrm{ev}}=\lvert\nu_{\Phi}\rvert$, real and positive where $\nu_{\Phi}^2<0$ under a barrier, with the region label appended when a problem has several, $\nu_{\Phi,\mathrm{ev},j}$; the same quantity read in momentum is $p_{\Phi,\mathrm{ev}}$, since a momentum stays a $p$. Two objects that are not wave numbers leave the letter altogether: the Kerr–Schild null covector is $\ell_{\imath}$, and the neutral timelike reference vector of the algebraic constructions is $u_{\imath}$. Neither is a wave, so neither takes a $\nu$.

**A caution on $\mu$.** The letter $\mu$ carries two meanings in the corpus: the permeability in $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$, and the direction label in $e_{\imath}$ and $Q_{\imath}$. A third meaning, the reciprocal mass length, was once carried by the same letter and is no longer written at all. The coefficient of a mass term is the mass wave number $\nu_m=\frac{m\,c}{h}$ of *The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle*, and a text that writes $\mu$ where a mass is meant is using the older spelling. Two uses remain, and an article that needs both says which is which.

**A caution on $\lambda$.** The letter $\lambda$ names a central value and an eigenvalue, the helicity weight $\lambda=\vec u_p\cdot\vec S$ of *Helicity in Biquaternionic Form*, and a free exponent in the notation of *Index and Notation Conventions*, §*The Upper and Low Marks*. A length therefore never takes a bare $\lambda$, and the corpus does not work in lengths at all: it works in the three wave numbers $\nu_m=\frac{m\,c}{h}$, $\nu_{\Phi}=\lvert\vec\nu_\Phi\rvert$ with $\vec\nu_\Phi=\frac{\vec p_\Phi}{h}$, and $\nu_t^{\prime}=\frac{E}{h\,c}$, and a length appears only as an inversion, $\lambda_m=1/\nu_m$, $\lambda_\Phi=1/(2\pi\nu_{\Phi})$ and $\lambda_t=1/(2\pi\nu_t^{\prime})$. The subscript of a length names the quantity it is computed from, never the particle, and no fourth symbol is introduced for the inverses, since the inverses are the three wave numbers.

**The kinetic momentum is $\vec p-q\vec A$, with $q$ the electric charge and the minus sign in every display.** The polar and momentum charts write the same object as $\frac{h}{2\pi}\nabla\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}-q\vec A$ and $h\,\vec\nu_\Phi-q\vec A$, and the temporal partner is $E-q\omicron_{\mathrm{pot}}$. A writing that carries the coupling in place of the charge is that same display and not another one: its coupling is $\varkappa=-q$ where the article says the coupling is minus the charge, and $\varkappa=q$ where it says the coupling is the charge, and the two writings differ by the letter. The action and the phase are one object read in two units, $S=\frac{h}{2\pi}\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$, so $\vec\nabla_\Phi S$ and $\frac{h}{2\pi}\nabla\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ are the same vector.

**A caution on the fluid letters.** The hydrodynamics articles name quantities that the corpus spells elsewhere, and each takes a label so that no glyph is reused: the quantum potential is $Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$, the proper density $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}$, the charge density $\rho_{\mathrm{chg}}$, the energy density $w_{\mathrm{fl}}$, the two parts of the pressure $p_{\mathrm{th}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{qnt}}$, the entropy density $s_{\mathrm{ent}}$. The inverse temperature stays the bare $\beta$ of the thermal articles, $\beta=\frac{h}{2\pi k_{B}\,T}$, a length; an article that also boosts gives the label to the velocity, $\beta_{\mathrm{vel}}=v/c$. The amplitude squared is never a letter: it is written $\lVert\tilde\Xi\rVert^{2}$, and the densities are its two readings, $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}=\lVert\tilde\Xi\rVert^{2}$ for unit charge and $\rho_{\mathrm{chg}}=ϙ\gamma\rho_{\mathrm{num}}$ with $ϙ$ the charge. The energy density is the scalar part of the energy-momentum biquaternion, $\tilde\Sigma_{\Psi}=w_{\mathrm{fl}}e_t+\tfrac{i}{c}\vec S_{\mathrm P}$, so neither $w$ nor $p$ takes the sign weight $\varepsilon$. The flow is an element of $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and not a raised vector, $\tilde V_{\Phi}=\tilde P_{\Phi}/m$ with $\langle\tilde V_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}=-c^{2}$, and the two-fluid case writes $\tilde V_{\Phi,\mathrm{sup}}$ and $\tilde V_{\Phi,\mathrm{nrm}}$. The letter $u$ stays out of the flow: $u_{\imath}$ is the neutral timelike reference vector and $u=\ln\lVert\tilde\Xi\rVert$ is the logarithm of the amplitude.

## The Most Common Quaternions and Biquaternions

The tilde-marked glyphs a reader meets in every article are collected here, with the role, the formula, the dimension, the space and the meaning of each. The conjugations that define them are in *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Conjugations*, and the norms they are read against are fixed in *Norm and Pairing Conventions*. The basis, the central $i$, the charges, the pure elements, the glyphs of the reduced biquaternion layer and the tilde-free relations carry no tilde and are collected in *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*Summary of Notation*.

**Scope: tilde glyphs only.** Every catalogued symbol carries a tilde. Four kinds of glyph are outside the scope and are not listed among the allowed uses: a matrix, $I_n$, $\mathsf{M}_2$, $\mathsf{M}_{4L}$; a pure element written with an arrow, $\vec Q$, $\vec q$, $\vec u$, $\vec U$, $\vec{Ζ}$, $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$, $\vec\pi$; a mark that is not an element; and every glyph of the reduced, tilde-free layers, $\Pi_\pm$, $Ζ_\pm$. The first is fixed by the rule that a matrix carries no tilde, *Matrix Representations Conventions*, §*General Matrix Conventions*; the others are elements of other layers or of no layer at all. The operator section states the rule that pairs the two marks; it is not a catalogue of arrowed glyphs.

The tilde marks a quaternionic factor. A base glyph counts every numbered and subscripted variant.

**Allowed tilde.** The role is fixed by *Algebra and Element Conventions*, or by the case-and-mark rule it states, and checked against the corpus. Cite these entries as they stand. The tables are ordered by theme, as the objects are; the letters outside the U family are bound where they are introduced.

The governing rule: **only the Q family and the $\xi$ family are generic, and only an element carries the tilde.** $\tilde Q$ is the biquaternion or split-biquaternion element and $\tilde q$ the quaternion or split-quaternion element, numbered variants allowed; $\tilde{Ξ}$ is the biquaternion-valued field and $\tilde\xi$ the quaternion-valued one, *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Elements and their Coefficients*. A generic element of norm one is written $\tilde U$. A letter outside the family names an object, or an element bound where it is introduced. A matrix — the image of an element, an array of biquaternions, or a colour matrix — takes no tilde; a tilde on one is a defect of scope, corrected where it occurs and not catalogued here. A tensor is not an element of the algebra: its components $T_{\imath\jmath}$, and the irreducible tensor operators $T^{(k)}_{\imath}$, carry no tilde, and a free element written $\tilde T$ is the same defect, corrected to the Q family.

**The dimension column.** Each table carries the physical dimension of the quantity its glyph is identified with, in the five letters of §*Physical Units*. The column is a statement about the identification and not about the algebra: **no element of $\mathbb{B}$ carries a dimension of its own**, and *none fixed* marks the entries where the identification is the reader's and the glyph is generic. Three consequences are worth stating, because they are where the column is read wrongly. A glyph with two roles carries two entries, and the canonical momentum beside the idempotent is one. A dimensionless entry is not an empty one: the rotors, the idempotents, the states, the effects and the Kraus operators are dimensionless because their formulas fix no unit, and a rotor that carried a dimension would not rotate. And a row's dimension is the dimension of the **quantity**, not of the element, so the coefficients of the element share the unit of that quantity — the rule of §*The coefficients of one element share one unit*, which is what makes the biquaternion norm a sum of like terms. An operator's entry is read against the coordinate displayed in its own formula, which is the length coordinate of §*Physical Units*: in the time writing of the temporal slot the derivative carries $\mathrm{T}^{-1}$ and the coefficient $1/(ic)$, and the operator's dimension is unchanged.

**The sector label.** A glyph whose element lies in one sector carries that sector as the first element of its subscript: $\tilde P_\Phi$, $\tilde\Upsilon_\Phi$, $\tilde J_\Phi$, $\tilde A_\Phi$, $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$, $\tilde\Xi_\Phi$, $\tilde E_\Phi$, $\tilde{\mathcal L}_\Phi$, $\tilde\Lambda_{\Phi,\mathrm{ret}}$ in the material sector; $\tilde H_\Psi$, $\tilde{Ϛ}_\Psi$, $\tilde S_\Psi$, $\tilde\Sigma_\Psi$, $\tilde{\mathcal E}_{\Psi}$, $\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{modular}}$ in the informational one. The sector precedes the role and the index, so $\tilde S_{\Psi,\mathrm{tot},\eta}$ and $\tilde J_{\Phi,\jmath}$, and the label does not pass into the coefficients, so $\tilde J_\Phi$ keeps the components $J_{\jmath}$. The rule is in *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Hermitian and Anti-Hermitian Subspaces*.

The label is a theorem and not a new axiom. For an element already in a sector, $\tfrac12(\tilde Q+\tilde Q^{*})$ and $\tfrac12(\tilde Q-\tilde Q^{*})$ return the element itself and the zero, so $\tilde P_\Phi=\tilde P$ and $\tilde S_\Psi=\tilde S$. Three glyphs take no label, and the reason is a property of the letter and not an omission: $\tilde Q$ and $\tilde U$ range over $\mathbb{B}$, so they decompose, $\tilde Q=\tilde R_\Phi+\tilde H_\Psi$, and $\tilde F$ lives in $\mathbb{B}_V$ and splits across both sectors, $i\sqrt\epsilon\,\vec E\in\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ with $\sqrt\mu\,\vec H\in\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$. The same line gives the sector of any of them.

**A glyph is written once, and its sector letter names the sector of its element.** Every element of the algebra is one element, and the Hermitian decomposition splits it into an anti-Hermitian and a Hermitian part, so it lies in one sector or in neither. A glyph therefore carries a sector subscript when its element lies in a sector, and no second glyph is written for the other sector: the same four real numbers are read once, from the one element. The operator is not doubled: $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ is the only derivative operator of the corpus, it differentiates the material coordinates and no others, and no derivative carries $\Psi$.

**A shared shape is not a shared element.** $\tilde R_\Phi$, the material position, and $\tilde H_\Psi$, the informational observable, are two elements and not one read twice: they differ in their four numbers, in their sector and in their letter, and no factor carries one into the other. $\tilde E_\Phi$, the frame field, and $\tilde{\mathcal E}_{\Psi}$, the effect, are likewise not two names for one thing: $i\tilde E_\Phi$ is Hermitian, but it is not the effect. The frame is read in the material sector and the effect in the informational one, and $i\tilde E_\Phi$ is not a measurement. They do not share a letter, and the paragraph on the letter $E$ below states why.

**One letter, one object.** When two objects would share a letter inside one article, one of them takes a new letter and a role label, and the label names the object. The scattering element is $\tilde{\mathcal S}_\Psi$, and the spin generators keep $\tilde S_\Psi$. The optical element of a Jones matrix is $\tilde J_{\mathrm{opt}}$, which ranges over $\mathbb{B}$ and so carries no sector label, while the four-current keeps $\tilde J_\Phi$. The Wigner rotation is $\tilde U_{\mathrm{Wig}}$, a gate is $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{gate},i}$, a circuit element is $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{cir}}$, and the amplitude operator is $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{amp}}$; the energy–momentum biquaternion keeps $\tilde\Sigma_\Psi$. A $*$-unitary on the right, the gauge change that leaves the state fixed, is $\tilde U_{\mathrm{gau}}$, and a $*$-unitary on the left, the change of frame that moves it, is $\tilde U_{\mathrm{frm}}$; the scalar-free part of an element is $\tilde Q_{\mathrm v}$, a generator of the adjoint action is $\tilde U_{\mathrm{gen},\eta}$, the pure part of a Hermitian element is $\tilde V_{\Phi,\mathrm{pu}}$, and a unit real quaternion is $\tilde u$; the four-velocity keeps $\tilde V_\Phi$. The Bogoliubov coefficients are $\beta_{ji}$, and the boost factor of the cone keeps $\mathcal B_\Psi$. The generic element is never $\tilde A$, $\tilde B$ or $\tilde C$: a family of generic elements is $\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2,\tilde Q_3$, as in $L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}=L_{\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2}$, because $\tilde A$ is the four-potential, $\tilde B$ the magnetic biquaternion, and $\tilde C$ the Majorana mode and the curvature.

**The frame field keeps the roman $\tilde E$, the effect takes the calligraphic $\tilde{\mathcal E}$.** The frame field is $\tilde E_\Phi$, with $\langle\tilde E_{\imath},\tilde E_{\jmath}\rangle_\natural=g_{\imath\jmath}$; its time leg is imaginary and its space legs real, which puts it in the material sector. The effect of a measurement is $\tilde{\mathcal E}_{\Psi}$, Hermitian and bounded by $0\le\tilde{\mathcal E}_{\Psi}\le e_t$, which puts it in the informational sector. The two never meet in one equation. The sector label alone does not separate them, because $i\tilde E_\Phi$ is itself Hermitian, and the letter $E$ names three other objects in the corpus: the electric field of the time-harmonic Maxwell article, the mass biquaternion of the Petiau system and a Pauli error of the stabilizer article. **The angular-momentum labels are lower-case Latin, and the total takes the role subscript.** A spin label is $s$, its weight $s_z$, and the operator carries the tilde, $\tilde S_{\Psi,\eta}$. A coupled pair writes $s_1,s_{1z},s_2,s_{2z}$ and its total $s_{\mathrm{tot}},s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$; the final multiplet of a tensor operator is $s_{\mathrm{fin}}$, with weight $s_{\mathrm{fin},z}$, and its reduced matrix element is $\langle s_{\mathrm{fin}}\|T^{(k)}\|s_{\mathrm{tot}}\rangle$. A representation of spin $s$ is $V_s$, of dimension $2s+1$. The letter $j$ is not used for any of these: it is the hyperbolic unit of the reduced layer, and it never denotes an angular momentum or a weight. The letter $m$ is not used for a weight either: it is the mass, and a magnetic weight is written $s_z$, $l_z$ or $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$. The operator index and the projection are read apart in *Index and Notation Conventions*, §*The Subscripts*.

**The state takes the stigma, because the majuscule of rho is taken.** The case rule asks a complex element for an upper-case glyph, and the upper-case rho is drawn as $P$ and names the four-momentum $\tilde P_{\Phi}$, which the series never writes otherwise. The state therefore takes a capital that names nothing else in the corpus, the **stigma** $Ϛ$ (U+03DA). The glyph is bound to the state and is never generic; no lowercase rho and no varrho spelling of it is written. Bare $\rho$ is not the state: it keeps its other roles, the charge density and the radial coordinate among them. The matrix image takes no tilde, as for every element. A state named for the operator that defines it carries that operator as the second element of the subscript, $\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi,Q}=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}/2\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$.

**The quantum and relativistic coefficients carry a letter of their own.** Five objects once shared one glyph behind different subscripts, and each now takes the letter of its object. The Kraus operator is $\tilde M_{\mathrm{kraus}}$, and the outcome label follows the word in the same subscript, $\tilde M_{\mathrm{kraus},y}$, $\tilde M_{\mathrm{kraus},l}$, $\tilde M_{\mathrm{kraus},1,2}$. The modular Hamiltonian is $\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{modular}}=-\log\tilde{Ϛ}_{\Psi}$, and the state it is taken of follows the word, $\tilde H_{\Psi,\mathrm{modular},\sigma}$. The retarded-time gradient coefficient is $\tilde\Lambda_{\Phi,\mathrm{ret}}$. The four-force is $\tilde{\mathcal F}_{\Phi,\mathrm{frc}}$, the force member of the $\tilde{\mathcal F}$ family that also carries the power–force biquaternion, and a particle label follows the word, $\tilde{\mathcal F}_{\mathrm{frc},a}$. The null covector of the curved-spacetime article is $\tilde{\mathcal L}_{\Phi,\mathrm{null}}$, the null member of the $\tilde{\mathcal L}_{\Phi}$ family that also carries the future null four-vector of a pure state. **The word is what separates them, and it is written in full**: a one-letter label on a shared glyph is a number in disguise, and a reader cannot tell which object a bare subscript names without a table. The bare letter $K$ names none of them: the propagation kernel is $G_{\mathrm{prop}}$, the oscillator kernel $G_{\mathrm{osc},\omega}$, the CTP entries $G_{\mathrm{ctp},\pm\pm}$, the boost generator $B_{\mathrm{boost},\eta}$, the Vekua and Cauchy-type operators $V_{\mathrm{vek},\alpha}$, the Maxwell kernel $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{maxw}}$, the kinetic energy $T_{\mathrm{kin},a}$ and the modified Bessel function $\mathrm{Ki}_{1}$. The tilde-free objects follow the same rule: the modular Hamiltonian is $H_{\mathrm{modular}}$, the kinetic operator $W_{\mathrm{kinetic}}=\Box_\Phi-4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}$, the Lorentz four-force $F_{\mathrm{lor},\imath}=ϙ\sum_{\jmath} F_{\imath\jmath}v_{\jmath}$ and the contortion $N_{\mathrm{cont},\lambda\imath\jmath}$, each with the label of its object after the word.

**The wave number is $\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}$ and is always written with its subscript, and the letter $\mathrm{Kr}$ is the Krein form.** The wave number is one element, defined by one division and by nothing else: $\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}=\frac{\tilde P_\Phi}{h}$, whose temporal coefficient is $\nu_t^{\prime}=\frac{E}{h\,c}$ and whose vector part is $\vec\nu_\Phi=\frac{\vec p_\Phi}{h}$, with $\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}^{\natural}=-\nu_t^{\prime\,2}+\lvert\vec\nu_\Phi\rvert^{2}=-\nu_m^{2}$. The gradient acts by left multiplication by $2\pi i\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}$ and the d'Alembertian by $-4\pi^{2}\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}^{\natural}$, so the wave number, the symbol of the gradient and the four-momentum read in inverse lengths are one object, and the three wave numbers of *The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle* are its scalar coefficient, the modulus of its vector part and its norm. **Two component-wise readings convert it and are not rival definitions.** A display that writes the symbol by components writes $\nu_t^{\prime}-i\vec\nu_\Phi=-i\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}$, with $\nu_t^{\prime}=\omega/(2\pi c)$ and $\vec\nu_\Phi=\frac{\vec p}{h}$, both entries inverse lengths, and the norm reads $\nu_t^{\prime\,2}-\lvert\vec\nu_\Phi\rvert^{2}=\nu_m^{2}$; a display that keeps the coefficients momenta writes instead $-p_te_t+i\vec p=+i h\,\tilde\Upsilon_{\Phi}$, with $p_t=E/c$, and its norm reads $p_t^{2}-\lvert\vec p\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}=h^{2}\nu_m^{2}$; the division by $h$ is the whole of the conversion between the two readings. The bare letter $\mathrm{Kr}$ is the fourth of the norms, $P$, $N$, $H$ and $\mathrm{Kr}$, the Krein form and its norm of *Norm and Pairing Conventions*; the two are read apart by the tilde, and neither is a generic element.

## Summary

A unit element is not a physical unit. The series uses the SI, and it tells the fundamental quantities from the derived: metre, second, kilogram and kelvin are base units, the coulomb is $\mathrm{A\,s}$ but carries the independent dimension $\mathrm{Q}$ that the corpus needs, and every other unit is derived or is a constant written in these units. No unit is fundamental in the sense that nature supplies it, because every invertible element is realised from seven defining constants. The dimension letters $\mathrm{L}$, $\mathrm{T}$, $\mathrm{M}$, $\Theta$ and $\mathrm{Q}$ stand only in the tables of this article, the units tables and the glyph tables alike, and the glyph tables carry the dimension of each quantity so that a reader can check a formula against it at sight. The coefficients of one element share one unit, and the default temporal slot is $i r'_t$ in metres, with $t'=r'_t/c$ and $g_{tt}=-c^{2}$ stated where an article uses the seconds. A dimensionless number carries no unit and no conversion; $S$ is read by its subscript and $\mu$ by context between the permeability and the direction label, the inverse length of a mass being written $\lambda_m^{-1}$.

The tilde-marked glyphs a reader meets in every article are collected here, with the role, the formula, the dimension, the space and the meaning of each. The collection is a closed list: no tilde is written on an array, no glyph is invented for an operator, which takes a label on an existing glyph instead of a letter of its own, and no letter is reused for a second object of the same layer. Each glyph is written once: the sector letter names the sector its element lies in, and the same four real numbers are not catalogued a second time under the other letter. The norms they are read against are fixed in *Norm and Pairing Conventions*, and the tilde-free glyphs are collected in *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*Summary of Notation*.

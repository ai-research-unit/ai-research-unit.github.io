# __The Leptoquarks of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Six States, One Bound, and Why Proton Decay Does Not Bite__

## Introduction

**The layer.** The six states live on a formal copy of the carrier, and they are the off-diagonal block of the fifteen. Nothing in their number is chosen: it is the dimension of the complement of the stabiliser of the unit line.

**The question.** Which six gauge bosons carry colour and lepton number at once, what do they do to matter, what bounds their mass, and does proton decay bound them harder?

**The answer.**

$$
\boxed{\;
\mathfrak m=\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-2/3},\quad 6\ \text{states},\quad B-L=\pm\tfrac43,\quad Q=\pm\tfrac23;
\qquad
M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)\,M_{W_R};
\;}
$$

$$
\boxed{\;
K_L\to\mu e\ \text{is tree level and unsuppressed},\qquad
B\ \text{moves in thirds, so proton decay is dimension nine}
\;}
$$

So the states are forced, unseen, and bounded from below by one flavour-changing decay.

**What the article establishes.** That the six are forced by the gauged fourth colour and are the only coloured massive states in the theory; that $M_X\gtrsim4.0\times10^{-5}$ J from $K_L\to\mu e$; that proton decay does not bound the gauge sector at the leptoquark scale, because three leptoquark vertices make the leading operator dimension nine; and that no field in the frame supplies a diquark coupling, because the fifteen's colour triplet carries the wrong $B-L$ — a $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ scalar would supply it, and the frame has none.

**Not established.** The numerical bound is a coupling statement, and its coefficient is uncertain by a factor of a few. The states have not been seen. Whether the frame adds a $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ scalar is a choice and not a derivation; without one the dimension-six operator does not exist at all.

**Where the rest is.** The fields that produce the six are [The Scalar Sector](the-scalar-sector-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-twenty-four-real-components-twelve-eaten-twelve-surviving.html). The step that gives them their mass is [The Breaking Chain](the-breaking-chain-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-one-flag-three-stabilisers-three-scales.html). Their place in the full list is [Classification of the Bosons](classification-of-the-bosons-in-the-su4-model-one-grid-fifteen-generators-nine-massless-six-heavy.html), §*The Spectrum*.

## The Six That Are Forced

**The off-diagonal block.** They come from one matrix, and they are unavoidable once the fourth colour is gauged.

**5.1 The decomposition.** The fifteen generators of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ split as the stabiliser of the unit line and its complement:

$$
\mathfrak{su}(4)=\underbrace{\mathfrak{su}(3)}_{\mathbf 8_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathbb R\,iD}_{\mathbf 1_0}\oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak m}_{\mathbf 3_{4/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf 3}_{-4/3}},
\qquad
15=8+1+6 .
$$

The $\mathfrak m$ carries $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$, and that value is not adjustable. The subscripts in that display are $B-L$; the same six are written $\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-2/3}$ elsewhere, where the subscript is the electric charge. It is the difference between the lepton's $B-L=-1$ and the quark's $B-L=+\tfrac13$:

$$
(-1)-\Bigl(+\tfrac13\Bigr)=-\tfrac43 ,
$$

so a generator that moves one unit of $B-L$ from the line into the vector part must have eigenvalue $-\tfrac43$ or $+\tfrac43$, and there is nothing between.

**5.2 The charge.** With $Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$ and the $\mathfrak m$ being singlets of both $SU(2)$'s:

$$
Q(X)=0+0+\tfrac12\Bigl(\pm\tfrac43\Bigr)=\pm\tfrac23 .
$$

So the six consist of three of charge $+\tfrac23$ and three of charge $-\tfrac23$, the second three carrying anticolour. They are, in the standard notation, the $X$ bosons of the Four Colours Model reduced to their abelian content. There is no $Y$ here of charge $\pm\tfrac13$, because the frame's $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ is a separate factor, and that is the difference between $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(5)$.

**5.3 The only coloured states.** Colour is the stabiliser of the unit line, so it is unbroken at every scale — nothing in the chain removes it. Hence no coloured gauge boson can get a mass except by eating a Goldstone that carries colour, and the only such Goldstones are the six in $\mathfrak m$. As a theorem: in this frame the set of massive coloured gauge bosons is exactly $\mathfrak m$.

## What the Six Do

**Three vertices.** The third is the one that is measured.

**6.1 Quarks into leptons.** A leptoquark couples one quark leg to one lepton leg:

$$
X_{\imath}:\quad q\to \ell,\qquad \bar q\to\bar\ell ,
$$

with the quantum numbers forcing $|\Delta B|=\tfrac13$ and $|\Delta L|=1$ per fermion leg. On the legs alone $B-L$ would change by $\mp\tfrac43$, and it does not: the $X$ carries that same $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$. So every vertex conserves $B-L$ exactly, which it must, because $\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ survives the first breaking and is still a gauge symmetry. The leptoquark violates $B$ and violates $L$ and conserves $B-L$ — exactly what a $B-L$ gauge boson does, since a boson of a gauged charge carries that charge and cannot change the total.

**6.2 Mixing with the photon and the $Z$.** Since they are charged, they contribute to the oblique parameters and to $Z\to\ell\ell$ at loop level. The corrections are suppressed by $(v/v_R)^2$ and are unobservable for $M_X$ above about $3\times10^{-5}$ J.

**6.3 $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level.** That is the whole bound. The diagram is $s\bar d\to X\to\mu\bar e$, with no loop:

$$
\mathcal M\propto\frac{g_4^2}{M_X^2},
\qquad
\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)\ \propto\ \frac{g_4^{4}\,\lvert U_{de}U_{s\imath}\rvert^{2}}{M_X^{4}}f_K^2m_K .
$$

Because it is a tree-level flavour-changing process in a neutral meson with no loop suppression, the bound is severe, and it is a coupling statement. The measured null result $\mathcal B(K_L\to\mu e)<4.7\times10^{-12}$ is converted into a bound on $M_X$ using $g_4^2=4\pi\alpha_s(M_X)$, a kaon decay constant, the generator normalisation $\operatorname{tr}T^{A}T^{B}=\tfrac12\delta^{AB}$ and the rate $g_4^{4}\lvert U_{de}U_{s\imath}\rvert^{2}f_K^{2}m_\mu^{2}m_K/(128\pi M_X^{4})$.

**Not derived here.** It depends on the leptoquark chirality and on the hadronic matrix element, and it is a number produced by a coupling. It is quoted, with its inputs and its spread, in [The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model](the-one-coupling-that-returns-the-standard-model.html); what this article supplies is the channel, its quantum numbers, and the reason the channel is not loop-suppressed.

**The order of magnitude, and its condition.** The bound comes out at $M_X\gtrsim3.69\times10^{-5}$ J when the generation matrix is taken of order one, so the round $2.5\times10^{2}$ used here is right to within the coefficient's band on that assumption. The assumption is not derived: the minimal Yukawa sector makes the matrix the identity and the channel vanishes, so this is a conditional bound and not the frame's model-independent statement. [Lepton Flavour in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model](lepton-flavour-in-the-su4-model-nothing-turns-the-lepton.html) does that calculation and carries the $K_S$ prediction that follows from the same operator. This single number is the strongest model-independent statement in the frame, and it is worth being clear what kind of statement it is: not a prediction, but a lower bound on a parameter, obtained from a measured null result. *How Firm the Bound Is* says how firm it is.

## The Mass, and the $\sqrt2$ That a Coupling Ratio Multiplies

**The mass relation.** It is written so that it stays a structure.

**7.1 From the vacuum.** The breaking at the first threshold is done by the $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$ acquiring a value along its unit-line component. The six leptoquarks eat six of that field's sixteen real components and get

$$
M_X^2=\tfrac12\,g_4^2\,v_R^2 ,
$$

which is one half, one coupling squared, one vacuum squared, and no free number.

**7.2 The ratio.** With $M_{W_R}^2=\tfrac14g_R^2v_R^2$ the ratio is

$$
\frac{M_X}{M_{W_R}}=\sqrt2\;\frac{g_4}{g_R} .
$$

The $\sqrt2$ is the prediction, and it is the group's: half against a quarter, and the quarter comes from the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ normalisation while the half comes from the leptoquark's own generator. The factor $g_4/g_R$ is not one in general, and this article does not assert that it is.

**7.3 For a search.** It is the practical content of this section. A bound on $M_X$ is a bound on $M_{W_R}$ up to the same factor: the two masses differ by $\sqrt2$ times a coupling ratio, so the leptoquark is the heavier of the two by a factor between one and two, and never by more than that. A search that fixes one therefore fixes the other to within that window.

**7.4 What would change it.** A $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ with a vacuum expectation value, or a second $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$, splits the leptoquark masses from one another and from the $W_R$. The $\sqrt2$ is the minimal-vacuum statement, and it is a statement about the vacuum rather than about the group.

## Proton Decay, and Why It Does Not Bite

**The counting argument.** It is the reason the model survives at all.

**8.1 A third per vertex.** A leptoquark converts one quark into one lepton, so $\Delta B=\pm\tfrac13$. A proton has $B=1$ and a positron and a pion together have $B=0$, so destroying the proton needs three leptoquark vertices.

**8.2 Dimension nine.** Each vertex converts one quark into one lepton, so the proton's three quarks need three vertices — and because no diquark coupling exists, the final state cannot be a lepton pair plus two quarks. It is three leptons. Six external fermions make dimension nine,

$$
\mathcal O_9\sim\frac{1}{M_X^{5}}\,q^{3}\ell^{3},
\qquad
\Gamma\propto\frac{m_p^{11}}{M_X^{10}} .
$$

The suppression is $M_X^{-10}$ against the dimension-six $M_X^{-4}$: six extra powers, and the six are counted on the rate and not on the amplitude.

**What that does and does not settle.** It settles the channel: the two-body mode $p\to e^{+}\pi^{0}$ has no gauge contribution at any order, because its final state is two quarks and a lepton and nothing but a diquark can close it. It does not settle the size against experiment. Where the three-lepton mode lands against the limit on $p\to e^{+}e^{+}e^{-}$ depends on the operator's dimensionless coefficient and on that limit, and this article computes neither: the coefficient is a loop matching and the limit is a data entry, so the comparison belongs to [The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model](the-one-coupling-that-returns-the-standard-model.html) and to the data register. The claim here is the narrow one, that the gauge sector is not bounded by the channel usually quoted.

**8.3 The scalar sector.** The statement above holds for the gauge sector alone. A colour antitriplet that couples to two quarks — a diquark — gives a dimension-six operator from a single exchange, and the bound is then ten orders of magnitude stronger. The fifteen's triplets are not that field: they carry $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$ and a $qq$ pair carries $+\tfrac23$. *The Diquark Fork: Which Field Would Supply It* states what would be.

**8.4 Which bound is stronger.** On $M_X$ the proton-decay bound, where it applies, beats $K_L\to\mu e$ by about ten orders of magnitude — $1.60\times10^{5}$ J against $4.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$. So the ordering is never in doubt; what is in doubt is whether the stronger bound applies at all, and that is a question about the scalar content rather than about the gauge sector. Quoting the proton bound as the leptoquark bound assumes a diquark coupling, which is exactly the assumption *The Diquark Fork: Which Field Would Supply It* shows to require a field the frame does not have.

## The Diquark Fork: Which Field Would Supply It

**The fork, and the field it turns on.** A diquark coupling would give a dimension-six proton-decay operator and a bound ten orders of magnitude above the leptoquark one. Whether the frame has one is a question about the multiplet list, and the answer is no.

**11.1 The fifteen's colour branching.** $\Sigma$ in the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ decomposes under $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as

$$
\mathbf{15}\to\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1 .
$$

The triplet and the antitriplet carry $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$ — the same values the gauge leptoquarks carry, because they are the same six directions of the fifteen, read as a scalar rather than as a generator.

**11.2 Why that forbids the coupling.** A $qq$ pair carries $B-L=+\tfrac23$ in all three charge combinations, $uu$, $ud$ and $dd$, because $\tfrac13+\tfrac13=\tfrac23$ whatever the flavours. The fifteen's antitriplet carries $-\tfrac43$. A coupling $qq\,\Sigma$ would then carry $\tfrac23-\tfrac43=-\tfrac23\ne0$ and violate the gauged $\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$, so the operator is forbidden by the algebra and not by a missing rule.

The tensor product says the same thing without a charge argument. A $qq$ bilinear lives in

$$
\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4=\mathbf6\oplus\mathbf{10},
$$

and the fifteen is not in that product: $\mathbf4\otimes\bar{\mathbf4}=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf{15}$ is the channel that produces the fifteen, and it is the $q\bar q$ channel. So a diquark scalar is a **sextet** or a decuplet and never an adjoint.

**11.3 What would supply it.** A $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ scalar carries

$$
\mathbf6\to\bar{\mathbf3}_{\,2/3}\oplus\mathbf3_{-2/3},
$$

with the subscript now $B-L$. The antitriplet's $+\tfrac23$ is the $qq$ coupling and the triplet's $-\tfrac23$ is the $q\ell$ coupling, so one field supplies both and the dimension-six operator appears the moment it is added. That field, and not $\Sigma$, is the fork.

**11.4 The two branches.**

| branch | content | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **no sextet** — the frame as written | $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ | no dimension-six operator at all; $p\to e^{+}\pi^{0}$ absent; $M_X$ at the leptoquark scale; the dimension-nine three-lepton mode the only baryon violation |
| **sextet added** | a $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$, or the $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ component of a larger multiplet | $M_\Sigma\gtrsim1.60\times10^{5}$ J from $p\to e^{+}\pi^{0}$, and the coloured scalars unreachable |

**11.5 What the earlier form of this section got wrong.** It read the fifteen's triplets as carrying the diquark coupling and concluded that the frame needed an unexplained discrete symmetry to forbid $qq\Sigma$. The algebra does not support that reading: the triplet's $B-L$ is $\pm\tfrac43$, the $qq$ pair's is $\tfrac23$, and the product $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4$ does not contain a fifteen. So the frame is safe from the dimension-six operator without a symmetry, and what it needs is the absence of one field rather than the presence of one rule.

**11.6 What the correction costs.** It removes the model's sharpest tension and it removes its cheapest escape. The tension was that a frame with a diquark triplet needed a symmetry nobody derived; it is gone, because there is no triplet. The escape was that the fork could be closed by forbidding a coupling; it is gone too, because the fork is now a statement about content, and content is chosen. Two things survive: the dimension-nine mode is the frame's only baryon violation from the gauge sector, and a sextet would end the leptoquark prediction. The second is a prediction about a field nobody has proposed adding, which is a weaker statement than the one this section used to make.

## How Firm the Bound Is

**Four sources of spread.** They are stated so that the number is not over-trusted.

**9.1 The chirality of the coupling.** A vector leptoquark couples to both chiralities and a scalar one to one; the $K_L\to\mu e$ amplitude depends on which, and the bound moves by a factor of about two.

**9.2 The hadronic matrix element.** $\langle\mu e|(\bar s\gamma^{\imath} d)|K_L\rangle$ is a lattice-and-decay-constant object, and its uncertainty propagates as a fourth root into $M_X$.

**9.3 First-generation coupling.** In the minimal frame it does, because the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is generation-blind. A frame with generation-dependent leptoquark couplings could suppress $K_L\to\mu e$ and evade the bound — and that is a modification of the frame, not of its group.

**9.4 What the bound is not.** It is not a measurement of $M_X$; it is a floor. Its role is to say that the six states cannot be light, which is what makes them a prediction that has so far failed to materialise rather than one that has succeeded.

## What the Bound Leaves Open

**13.5 The sector is forced.** Six states with fixed charge, fixed colour and a fixed mass ratio, appearing because one line was gauged. A frame that produced six adjustable states would explain nothing; this one produces six states with no freedom beyond $v_R$.

**13.6 A null problem.** Nothing is contradicted. Six states that should exist and have not been seen is the weakest possible form of failure and also the most persistent: it cannot be repaired by a better calculation, only by raising the scale — and raising it to $1.60\times10^{5}$ J to satisfy a diquark bound makes the states unseeable rather than merely unseen, so that the model's one sharp prediction becomes empty.

**The lower bound is not free.** The absence of a proton-decay bound is not the absence of any bound: $K_L\to\mu e$ does bind, at $4.01\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$, and the consequences fall in the hierarchy and the running. [The Scalar Sector](the-scalar-sector-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-twenty-four-real-components-twelve-eaten-twelve-surviving.html), §*What the Sector Leaves Open*, carries them.

## Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

A leptoquark vertex does not violate $B-L$: the $X$ carries $\pm\tfrac43$ and the vertex conserves it (*What the Six Do*).

The leptoquark bound is not a measurement; it is a floor, and its coefficient is uncertain by a factor of a few (*How Firm the Bound Is*).

"Proton decay does not bind" does not mean "proton decay does not happen": $B$ is violated in thirds, the operators are there, and they are dimension nine — the channel to look for is $p\to e^{+}e^{+}e^{-}$ and not $p\to e^{+}\pi^{0}$ (*Proton Decay, and Why It Does Not Bite*).

$M_X=\sqrt2M_{W_R}$ is not a group relation; it is the minimal vacuum's, and the $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ moves it (*The Mass, and the $\sqrt2$ That a Coupling Ratio Multiplies*).

The diquark fork is not a technicality: it decides whether the model is testable, and it is decided by the multiplet list and not by a symmetry (*The Diquark Fork: Which Field Would Supply It*).

The six are not adjustable: their charge, their colour and their mass ratio are fixed, and only $v_R$ is free (*What the Bound Leaves Open*).

## Summary

**The leptoquarks.** $\mathfrak m=\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-2/3}$, six states, $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$, $Q=\pm\tfrac23$, colour triplet or antitriplet. They exist because the fourth colour is gauged; colour being a stabiliser makes them the only coloured massive bosons in the theory.

**What they do.** Convert quarks into leptons, $\Delta B=\tfrac13$ per fermion leg, with $B-L$ conserved because the $X$ carries $\pm\tfrac43$ itself; mediate $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level with no loop suppression.

**The bound.** A bound on $M_X$ and a bound on $M_{W_R}$ are the same bound up to $\sqrt2\,g_4/g_R$. The numerical value is in the coupling article, because it is obtained by turning a coupling into a rate.

**The mass relation.** $M_X=\sqrt2\,(g_4/g_R)\,M_{W_R}$; the $\sqrt2$ is the group's, and the coupling ratio multiplies it.

**Proton decay.** Dimension nine from gauge exchange, therefore no sharp proton-decay bound on the gauge sector; dimension six only if a sextet scalar supplies a diquark coupling, therefore a bound of $1.60\times10^{5}$ J that would make the leptoquarks unseeable.

**The verdict.** Forced, unseen, and evadable only by making the prediction empty.

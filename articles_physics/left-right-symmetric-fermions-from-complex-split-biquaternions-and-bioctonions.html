Left-Right Symmetric Fermions from Complex Split Biquaternions and Bioctonions

Introduction

The Standard Model treats the two chiralities of the fermions unequally: the left-handed components sit in $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ doublets and the right-handed components in singlets, and no right-handed neutrino is required at all. The left-right symmetric models of Pati and Salam and of Mohapatra and Pati restore the symmetry by enlarging the electroweak factor to $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, so that every left-handed doublet has a right-handed partner and the right-handed neutrino becomes a member of an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ doublet — a gauge singlet of the Standard Model, and therefore sterile, but a state of the larger theory. A construction of Vaibhav and Singh builds exactly this pairing out of a pair of division-algebra Clifford algebras: the complex split biquaternion algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ for the leptons and the complex split bioctonion algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$ for the quarks, each splitting into two copies of a smaller algebra — $\mathrm{Cl}(2)$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ respectively — which carry the two chiralities and are interchanged by a parity automorphism. This article states the algebraic core of that construction and sorts its steps into what is established, what is read, and what is left open.

The algebraic core is proved for the lepton case in the companion mathematics article, and the quark case is the same statement one dimension up. The complex split biquaternions and the doubling $$ \mathrm{Cl}(3) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(2)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}(2) \cong \mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B} $$ are the subject of Complex Split Biquaternions and the Clifford Algebra Cl(3); the complex split bioctonions double in the same way, $$ \mathrm{Cl}(7) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(6)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}(6), $$ by the same computation of the volume-element square, which The Two Algebraic Facts below redoes for both cases; and the octonionic chain algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(6)\cong M_8(\mathbb{C})$, with its ladder operators, its intrinsic $\mathrm{su}(3)\oplus\mathrm{u}(1)$, its primitive idempotent and its charge spectrum $0,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,\tfrac23,\tfrac23,1$, is the complex octonionic chain algebra, whose physics interpretation is The Particle Roster of the Complex Octonionic Chain Algebra. The neutrino content against which the construction is compared is The Neutrino and Majorana Fermions in Biquaternionic Form and The Seesaw Mechanism and Majorana Mass in Biquaternionic Form; the anomaly conditions that decide whether a sterile state is allowed are Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation in Biquaternionic Form; the framework's own ledger on the electroweak factor, on the generation count and on the ceiling it cannot pass is The Standard Model under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda, The Number of Generations and the Biquaternion Algebra and Grand Unification and the Biquaternion Algebra Ceiling; and the exceptional structures of the source's further programme are outside the framework's algebra.

As in The Particle Roster of the Complex Octonionic Chain Algebra, the construction is outside the biquaternion algebra: $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is four-dimensional over $\mathbb{C}$ and eight-dimensional over $\mathbb{R}$, the chain algebras here are $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$, of complex dimensions $64$ and $128$, and no operation of $\mathbb{B}$ reaches them. The article is in the physics category as a record of the literature's most economical answer to the chirality and sterile-neutrino questions, not as a derivation inside the framework, and no result of it feeds back into the framework's ledger. Besides the doubling and its interpretation, the record covers the further pieces of the source: the source's construction by which each of the two weak factors is made to act on one chirality only, with its dependence on a chosen idempotent named; the interpretation of the volume element as the Higgs; the source's proposal that the two chiralities existed in a combined leptoquark state before symmetry breaking; the source's later programme, in which the right-handed weak factor becomes a chiral formulation of gravity in a ten-dimensional octonionic pre-spacetime and the second summand's charge becomes a square root of mass; and the $\mathrm{Cl}(5)$ statement that the right action of $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}$ on $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ generates $M_4(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_4(\mathbb{C})$, recomputed in the companion mathematics article.

The Two Algebraic Facts

Two structural facts are all that the construction's treatment of chirality uses, and both are recomputed below. They are statements about the two sides of a multiplication, so the article fixes that language first.

The two sides. An element multiplies from the left, $x\mapsto ax$, or from the right, $x\mapsto xb$, and the two together carry $x$ to $axb$; that two-sided action is the subject of every operator statement below. The two one-sided multiplications and their composition are fixed once in Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Operators, and they are cited here, not restated: the right factors multiply in reverse order. Where the operator itself is the subject it is written $L_aR_b$, as the conventions write it; where a state is the subject the action is written out. Every sign below follows from that composition together with $i^2=-1$ and $\vec e_k^{\,2}=-e_t$. The gamma frame is written once, in The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation at the end; nothing before it uses one.

The doubling. Each of the two algebras has a central volume element which is an involution. The complex split biquaternions $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ are generated by $E_\mu=j\vec e_\mu$, with $\vec e_\mu$ the quaternion units and $j$ the split unit, $j^2=1$; the volume element is $\omega=E_1E_2E_3$. The complex split bioctonions $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$ are generated by $e_1,\dots,e_7$, and the volume element is $J=e_1\cdots e_7$. All Clifford algebras here are complex: $\mathrm{Cl}(n)$ means $\mathrm{Cl}(n,\mathbb{C})$, and a real form is written out, as $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})$. In both cases the idempotents $\Pi_{1,2}=\tfrac12(1\pm\omega)$ and $\tfrac12(1\pm J)$ are a complete family of orthogonal central idempotents, so that the algebra is the direct sum of two copies of the smaller one, $$ \boxed{\;\mathrm{Cl}(3) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(2)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}(2), \qquad \mathrm{Cl}(7) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(6)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}(6)\;}. $$ The two squares follow from one computation. For a product of $n$ anticommuting generators of common square $c$, $$ (E_1\cdots E_n)^2=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\,c^{\,n}, $$ which gives $(-1)^{3}(-1)^{3}=+1$ at $n=3$ and $(-1)^{21}(-1)^{7}=+1$ at $n=7$: both volume elements are involutions. Each is central because a generator moves past the other $n-1$ factors at the cost of $n-1$ signs, an even number at $n=3$ and at $n=7$.

The parity automorphism $\sigma:e_i\mapsto-e_i$, fixing the even elements and negating the odd ones, sends $\omega\mapsto-\omega$ and $J\mapsto-J$ and thereby interchanges the two copies. The copies are the two pinor representations, inequivalent, distinguished by the eigenvalue $\pm1$ of the central volume element.

$\omega$ is a sign, not an exchange. What multiplication by $\omega$ does has to be stated on its own, because two decompositions are easily run together. Since $\Pi_{1,2}$ project on the two summands and $\omega\Pi_1=\Pi_1$, $\omega\Pi_2=-\Pi_2$, multiplication by $\omega$ preserves each summand and multiplies it by $\pm1$. The operator that exchanges the summands is $\sigma$, and $\sigma$ is outer: an inner automorphism fixes every central element, while $\sigma(\omega)=-\omega$. What multiplication by $\omega$ does exchange is another pair of subspaces, the diagonal and the anti-diagonal halves of the faithful representation of the real form, $\mathrm{Cl}(0,3)\cong\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{H}$. The split unit is central with $j^2=1$, so it acts as a scalar in each component and $E_\mu=j\vec e_\mu$ is a quaternion unit up to that sign; with the signs and the quaternion basis chosen so that $$ E_1\longmapsto(\vec e_x,\vec e_x),\qquad E_2\longmapsto(\vec e_y,\vec e_y),\qquad E_3\longmapsto(\vec e_z,-\vec e_z). $$ The volume element is then the product of three quaternionic units in each component, $$ \omega\longmapsto(\vec e_x\vec e_y\vec e_z,\,-\vec e_x\vec e_y\vec e_z)=(\vec e_z\vec e_z,\,-\vec e_z\vec e_z)=(-1,+1), $$ so with $x$ quaternionic it acts on the diagonal half $\{(x,x)\}$ and the anti-diagonal half $\{(x,-x)\}$ by $$ (x,x)\longmapsto(-x,x),\qquad (x,-x)\longmapsto(-x,-x), $$ which carries each half to the other, with $\omega^2=1$, $\omega$ central and $\omega^{*}=\omega$ (residuals $0$). These two halves are the two sets in which the source writes its left- and right-handed states; they are not the two central summands, which $\omega$ preserves. The distinction is used twice below, in the interpretation of the two chiralities and in the Higgs interpretation.

The two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factors from the left and the right. On the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ there are two commuting actions of the quaternion units, the left multiplications $L_{x}:y\mapsto xy$ and the right multiplications $R_{x}:y\mapsto yx$. They compose in opposite orders, $$ L_aL_b=L_{ab},\qquad R_bR_c=R_{cb}, $$ so the left action generates a copy of $\mathbb{H}$ and the right action the opposite algebra $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, which is isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}$ through conjugation but is a different copy. The imaginary part of each is a Lie algebra isomorphic to $\mathrm{su}(2)$; the real span of the six cells $L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}},R_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ is $\mathrm{so}(4)\cong\mathrm{su}(2)\oplus\mathrm{su}(2)$, and the two $\mathrm{su}(2)$'s commute. Together the two actions generate the full endomorphism algebra, $$ \boxed{\;\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\,L_aR_b:a,b\in\mathbb{B}\,\} = \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}) \cong M_4(\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(4)\;}, $$ the complexified Dirac algebra, of complex dimension $16$. These statements were checked on the four-dimensional model of $\mathbb{B}$: the left and right actions commute identically, $L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}L_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}=L_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ while $R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}=R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}=-R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, each action spans a four-dimensional algebra, the six imaginary cells span a six-dimensional Lie algebra closed under the commutator, and the span of the sixteen cells $L_aR_b$ is sixteen-dimensional, i.e. all of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$.

The Left-Right Symmetric Interpretation

The two copies as the two chiralities. The doubling is the construction's answer to the chirality problem. Where the single chain algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ of The Particle Roster of the Complex Octonionic Chain Algebra carries one generation in one chirality, and reaching the second chirality there takes another ideal together with an enlarged supercharge sector; the doubled algebra carries the two chiralities as its two simple summands. The interpretation is that one summand is the left-handed sector and the other the right-handed sector. The parity automorphism $\sigma$, which interchanges them, is then the discrete left-right symmetry of the left-right symmetric model. The two summands carry the same intrinsic $\mathrm{su}(3)\oplus\mathrm{u}(1)$ and the same charge spectrum, because the ladder operators, the number operator and the $\mathrm{su}(3)$ generators all commute with the volume element $J$ — the centrality established above — so that the two sectors differ in chirality and in nothing else. The states of one summand are

$$ \boxed{\;1(0),\quad 3(\tfrac13),\quad 3(\tfrac23),\quad 1(1)\;}. $$

That is the charge multiset of half a generation, and the summand's conjugate ideal supplies the antiparticles, exactly as in the undoubled construction of the particle-roster article.

The chirality trap. The chirality is carried by which set a state comes from and not by its expression: the source's own equations give the same element $\tfrac12(1+ie_1e_2)$ for the left-handed neutrino of the first set and the right-handed neutrino of the second, and the source says so explicitly. That element is idempotent for the usual reason, $(ie_1e_2)^2=i^2\,e_1e_2e_1e_2=(-1)(-1)=+1$, with $e_1,e_2$ the chain-algebra generators of the summand. Operator chirality names the trap exactly: a chirality is not an element that two states could share or fail to share, it is a side, the eigenspace of the frame's volume cell (Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra), and for the chain algebra it is which summand the state comes from. The corpus's warning to itself here is that an idempotent of the right-action factor, $\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z)$, has the same shape as the chain idempotent $\tfrac12(1+ie_1e_2)$ and has been read as a chirality on the strength of that shape; the two are different objects, and the one root at which the idempotent's ideal and a chiral half coincide is recorded as a coincidence in The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation. A reader who looks for two different algebraic expressions for the two chiralities will not find them; the element that gives the sets their relative sign is the volume element $\omega$, treated below, while the automorphism that exchanges the two summands is $\sigma$.

The weak factors from the two actions. The weak $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factors come from a different place, and the two facts of the previous section have to be kept apart. The doubling supplies the two chiralities, and the two commuting quaternion actions of the biquaternion algebra supply two commuting $\mathrm{su}(2)$ algebras. The interpretation is that the left action is $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and the right action is $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$: the left multiplications act on the internal biquaternion index of the states, the right multiplications act on a separate one, and the two commute by the computation above, so that the states of the doubled construction can be organised into doublets of the left action and doublets of the right action without the two actions interfering. The second non-abelian factor that the undoubled construction had to leave to an interpretation, whose absence makes the minimal version non-chiral, is here supplied by the right action, and the electroweak factor of the enlarged theory is the $\mathrm{su}(2)\oplus\mathrm{su}(2)=\mathrm{so}(4)$ of the previous section. What the two actions supply, and what they do not, is stated in The Two Actions and the Chirality below: the left action is chirality-diagonal, the right action is chirality-mixing, and the source's sharper statement, that each factor acts on one chirality, is a statement about a selected ideal and not about the two actions.

The corpus records the finite algebraic facts — the commutation of the two actions, the generation of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$, and the chirality splitting with its incidence with the two actions — and marks the identification of the two actions with the two weak factors of the model as an interpretation, because which states carry which weak quantum numbers is not fixed by the algebra alone. The source's mechanism — which state is annihilated and which pair is interchanged — is recorded with the source and is not recomputed here, for the reason given in that section: it is a property of a chosen idempotent, not of the algebra.

The right-handed neutrino. With the right-handed sector present, the state of the second summand of charge $0$ is available: a neutral, colourless, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$-singlet fermion, which is the right-handed neutrino $\nu_R$. In the Standard Model this state is not required — the fifteen states of a generation are the left-handed doublet, the right-handed singlets and their conjugates, and the right-handed neutrino can be dropped because it is a complete gauge singlet under $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$. It is exactly because it is sterile that the construction's presence of it is conspicuous: a sterile fermion is unconstrained by the anomaly conditions, which act only on the gauge-charged content, so the doubling costs nothing at the level of gauge consistency and adds a state that the Standard Model's own ledger must otherwise introduce by hand for neutrino mass. This is the construction's sharpest contact with the framework's ledger: the framework's own count of the electroweak factor is $\mathrm{U}(2)$ and never $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, and its $\nu_R$ status is exactly the question this construction answers by construction rather than by addition.

The Pati–Salam interpretation. The full gauge group of the left-right symmetric unification is the Pati–Salam group $$ \mathrm{SU}(4)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R , $$ in which the colour $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ and the baryon-minus-lepton number $\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ sit inside $\mathrm{SU}(4)_c$, and the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s act on the two chiralities. The construction supplies the two $\mathrm{SU}(2)$'s from the two quaternion actions, and the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ from the maximal totally isotropic subspace of the octonionic chain algebra in each summand. The abelian direction that the chain algebra itself supplies is the number-operator charge $Ϙ=\tfrac13N$, with the spectrum $0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,1$; the particle-roster article reads $Ϙ$ as the electric charge, and the $B-L$ of the Pati–Salam group is a different combination, with the values $(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,-1)$ on each colour-lepton quartet. Which combination $Ϙ$ is — the hypercharge $Y$, the electric charge $T_3+Y$, or $B-L$ — is one of the three commutator tests that the roster article leaves open, and the present article does not decide it. The assignment of the states to the Pati–Salam multiplets, in the source's interpretation, places one generation of quarks and leptons, with both chiralities, in the doubled algebra; the corpus does not recompute the multiplet assignment, and records it as an interpretation, in the same sense as the particle-roster article's assignment of its sixteen states.

The weak bosons as ladder operators. The source identifies the composite ladder operator $\Omega_L=\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3$ of each summand with the charged weak boson: $\Omega_L$ has charge $-1$ and $\Omega_L^{\dagger}$ charge $+1$ under the number-operator charge, right multiplication by $\Omega_L$ carries one state of the doublet to the other and right multiplication by $\Omega_L^{\dagger}$ reverses that on the antiparticles, so that $\Omega_L,\Omega_L^{\dagger}$ "mimic" $W^-$ and $W^+$. In the doubled algebra the same statement is made for the second summand's $\Omega_R,\Omega_R^{\dagger}$ as the right-handed analogues. This is an interpretation of the ladder operators that the chain-algebra article already uses — the corpus records it and notes that a ladder operator has the charge and the isospin action of a $W$, but that the construction contains no gauge field, no kinetic term and no coupling, so "mimics the $W$" is a correspondence of quantum numbers, not a derivation of the weak interaction. The source later abandons this assignment for the right sector in favour of using $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ for gravity, as recorded under the further programme below; the corpus keeps both statements with that shift marked.

The Two Dirac Equations

The construction is a finite algebra, and the source writes no equation of motion. The corpus's dictionary does supply one, and it is worth writing here because it separates two things that the construction runs together: the two sides of the action and the two chiralities. The equation comes in two forms, and they must not be read as one.

The one-sided pair. The biquaternion Dirac field is an element of $\mathbb{B}$, and chirality is its subscript in the sense of Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra: $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L$ lies in the $-1$ eigenspace of the frame's volume cell and $\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R$ in the $+1$, so the field is one of the two and the pair below is a pair of equations, not a pair of halves. The equation is the chirality-off-diagonal pair $$ \begin{aligned} \tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R&=m\,\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L,\\ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L&=m\,\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R, \end{aligned} $$ with $\tilde\nabla$ the biquaternionic gradient and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$ its quaternion conjugate, both fixed in Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Gradient. The first is the right-handed equation and the second the left-handed one; the $\natural$ in the second is what makes the pair real-linear, and each line multiplies the field from the left. At $m=0$ the two lines decouple into the two Weyl equations $\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R=0$ and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L=0$; at $m\ne0$ the mass couples them, and eliminating one half leaves the Klein–Gordon equation. Two equations, one per chirality — that is the content of the word "pair".

The two-sided single equation. Read on the algebra rather than on a field, the same content is one equation whose two sides are the two multiplications, $$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}F=m\,(-iR_{\vec e_z})\bar F=-i\,m\,\bar F\,\vec e_z , $$ where $F\in\mathbb{B}$, $\bar F$ is the coefficient conjugate, and $(-iR_{\vec e_z})\bar F=-i\,\bar F\,\vec e_z$ is the volume cell of the frame applied to the conjugate. The derivative is a left multiplier and occupies the left slot; the mass is a right multiplier and occupies the right slot, and it squares to the identity for that reason, $$ (-iR_{\vec e_z})^2=(-i)^2R_{\vec e_z}^{\,2}=(-1)(-e_t)=e_t , $$ so the mass operation is an involution. The two sides of the equation are thus the two sides of the multiplication, a left multiplication on one side and a right multiplication on the other, and that division of labour is operator chirality rather than an arrangement of arrays.

Why the two forms are worth keeping apart. The one-sided pair is the definition and the two-sided equation is the form the corpus's operator calculus uses; the two are related by a real-linear dictionary, not by an identity of operators. The construction touches both. It supplies the two chiralities on which the one-sided pair acts — one per summand of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)\cong\mathbb{B}\oplus\mathbb{B}$ — and it reads the left and the right action as the two weak factors. The mass is a right multiplication, hence an element of the right action. What the construction does not supply is the equation itself: the source has no derivative, no kinetic term and no mass, and the pair above is the corpus's equation written on the construction's algebra.

The Two Actions and the Chirality

The interpretation above identifies the left action with $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and the right action with $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$. The next question is how each action stands to the chirality, and that answer needs no choice.

The chirality splitting. By Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra, the chirality of a state is the side on which it is multiplied, and on the elements it is read from the volume cell of the frame, $-iR_{\vec e_z}$, which is a pure right multiplier: $$ (-iR_{\vec e_z})\tilde X=-i\,\tilde X\vec e_z,\qquad \tilde X_L=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde X+i\,\tilde X\vec e_z\bigr),\qquad \tilde X_R=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde X-i\,\tilde X\vec e_z\bigr). $$ The cell squares to the identity, $(-iR_{\vec e_z})^2=(-i)^2R_{\vec e_z}^{\,2}=(-1)(-e_t)=e_t$, and its two eigenvalues are $-1$ and $+1$, each twice, so $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_L\oplus\mathbb{B}_R$ with each half of complex dimension two. The definition uses the frame and nothing else: no root, no idempotent, no ideal, no Clifford relation.

The left action preserves both halves. The two multipliers of a cell commute, $L_aR_b=R_bL_a$, so $L_a$ commutes with $-iR_{\vec e_z}$ for every $a$: a left multiplication carries $\mathbb{B}_L$ into itself and $\mathbb{B}_R$ into itself. No element is excluded, so the whole three-dimensional $\mathrm{su}(2)_L$, and the central direction with it, is chirality-diagonal.

The right action moves them. A right multiplication commutes with $-iR_{\vec e_z}$ only when its multiplier commutes with $\vec e_z$, since $R_bR_c=R_{cb}$, so the commuting right multipliers are the two-dimensional span of $e_t$ and $\vec e_z$. The weaker question, whether $R_b$ keeps one half inside itself, has a wider answer, and it is the one that matters: in the display where the elements are $2\times2$ matrices and $\vec e_z$ is minus $i$ times the third Pauli unit, the $L$ half is the first column and the $R$ half the second, and $R_b$ keeps the first column inside itself exactly when $b$ is upper triangular. That is a three-dimensional subalgebra, the span of $e_t$, $\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x+i\vec e_y$, and $\mathrm{su}(2)_R=\operatorname{span}(\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$ is not it: $\vec e_x$ carries the first column out of itself, into a matrix with both columns occupied. For a generic $b$ the image of $\mathbb{B}_L$ meets both halves.

What this says about "each factor acts on one chirality". The phrase cannot be a property of the two $\mathrm{su}(2)$'s, because the two are not alike in the presence of the splitting: the left one is chirality-diagonal and the right one chirality-mixing. What the two actions supply is the two factors together with their incidence with the splitting, and that incidence — one side diagonal, the other off-diagonal — is the slot structure itself, which is what Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra calls the side. An operator that acts on one chirality only has to be built, and a built operator is a choice.

The source's sharper statement, and what it costs. The source states that the right action annihilates the right-handed pair and interchanges the two left-handed states. Read on the algebra that cannot hold of the right action as a whole; it holds of the right action on a selected ideal, and an ideal is a point of the projective line of $\mathbb{B}$, that is a primitive idempotent. With $\tilde\Pi_+=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z)$ the ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_+$ is exactly $\mathbb{B}_L$, and the roots $\pm i\vec e_z$ are the only two out of the projective line for which a Peirce ideal is a chiral half; that coincidence of arrays is the one recorded in Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra, and it is a coincidence of two roots, not an identity of notions. For any other root the ideal is a different plane, and a computation of which state is annihilated would establish a property of the chosen root and of the axis $\tau_3$ in the ladder factor, not of the algebra. The corpus therefore records the mechanism as the source's and does not recompute it; the objects it needs — the Peirce basis, the triple $\tau_a$, the isotropic elements $\beta_1,\beta_2$ and the four states $V_R,V_L,e^-_R,e^-_L$ — are named once in The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation, with their dependence on the two choices stated.

The Coxeter Element of the Right Action

The source notes that the right action of $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}$ on $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ is said to give $\mathrm{Cl}(5)$, whose Coxeter element — the product of its five generators — supplies the parity reversal that $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ does not contain, so that for $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ a parity reversal operator does not have to be introduced by hand; and that $\mathrm{Cl}(5)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(6)^+$ links this to Stoica's interpretation of the standard model from a pair of $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$'s, one carrying $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ and the other $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$. Two steps of it are in the corpus, both computed in The Right Action and the Enveloping Algebra of the companion mathematics article. The algebra generated by the left action of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ and the right action of $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}$ on the eight-dimensional $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ is $$ \mathrm{Cl}(3)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H})^{\mathrm{opp}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_4(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Cl}(5)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(6)^+ , $$ of complex dimension $32$ and with a two-dimensional centre. Read on one chirality — the sixteen cells $L_aR_b$ with both factors in $\mathbb{B}$ — the same construction gives the smaller algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(4)\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$, which is the source's "the right action on $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}$ gives $\mathrm{Cl}(4)$". What remains the source's, and is recorded as such, is the identification of the Coxeter element with a parity-reversal operator.

Two rungs, two volume elements. The Coxeter element the source writes is an object of the second rung, and the biquaternion dictionary does not name it. The algebra generated acts on the eight-dimensional $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ and has dimension $32$; the cells $L_aR_b$ with both factors in $\mathbb{B}$ span only the first rung, $$ \mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Cl}(4), $$ of dimension $16$, acting on the four-dimensional $\mathbb{B}$.

The volume element of the first rung is the product of the four frame cells written in The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation, $$ i\,(iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}})(iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}})(iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}})(iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}})=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}, $$ where the four factors carry $i^4=1$; the left factors multiply to $e_t\vec e_x\vec e_y\vec e_z=\vec e_z\vec e_z=-e_t$, the right factors, multiplying in reverse order, to $\vec e_y\vec e_y\vec e_y\vec e_x=-\vec e_y\vec e_x=+\vec e_z$, and the outer $i$ is what makes the result $-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$. Without the outer $i$ the product is $-R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, whose square is $R_{\vec e_z^{\,2}}^{\mathrm{axf}}=R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}=-I_4$; the outer $i$ turns that into $+I_4$, since $i^2=-1$.

The Coxeter element of the $\mathrm{Cl}(5)$ rung is a different object. It is a single two-sided product of five generators of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ — three from the left and the two right generators $\vec e_x,\vec e_y$, the third imaginary unit $\vec e_z$ being their product rather than a third generator — so its left factor is an element of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ outside $\mathbb{B}$, and it acts on eight dimensions, not four. In one ordering, $$ L_{E_1}L_{E_2}L_{E_3}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}=L_{\omega}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}=L_{\omega}R_{-\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}, $$ using $R_bR_c=R_{cb}$, so that $R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}=R_{-\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$; a different ordering of the five factors changes the sign, which is the $\pm$ recorded in the source. Its square is $$ \bigl(L_\omega R_{-\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}\bigr)^2=L_{\omega^2}R_{(-\vec e_z)^2}^{\mathrm{axf}}=L_1R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}=-I_8, $$ with $\omega^2=1$ and $\vec e_z^{\,2}=-e_t$. That is why the Coxeter element is not the first rung's volume cell: it multiplies by the volume element on the left and by a bivector on the right, and it acts on the algebra rather than on $\mathbb{B}$. The corpus therefore states the dictionary at the rung where it is exact and records the $\mathrm{Cl}(5)$ Coxeter element as an object of the larger algebra, which is the algebra this article already declares outside the sixteen cells.

The Volume Element as the Higgs Candidate

The interpretation. The source reads the volume element as the Higgs. The argument rests on the one place in the construction where a left-handed state passes to a right-handed one: multiplication by $\omega$ carries the left-handed electron of the first set to the right-handed electron of the second set, up to a sign. In the source's normalisation, $\omega\,e^-_L=-e^-_R$ and $\omega\,e^-_R=-e^-_L$, and $\omega^2=1$ makes the two steps compose to the identity, so the pair is a two-state system on which $\omega$ acts by the off-diagonal sign. The two symbols name the electron of the first set and the electron of the second, which is not the pair the source's own ideal construction builds inside a single set; the sets are what the equation relates, exactly as in the chirality trap of the section above. In the basis in which the source writes its states the two sets are the diagonal and the anti-diagonal halves, and there $\omega$ does exchange them, as recomputed above: $(x,x)\mapsto(-x,x)$ and $(x,-x)\mapsto(-x,-x)$. In the algebra's own decomposition of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ into two simple summands it does not exchange anything: it multiplies the two summands by $\pm1$, and the exchange is the outer automorphism $\sigma$. What the Higgs reading needs is the sign, and the sign is exact: $\omega$ is central, self-adjoint and involutive, it commutes with the whole algebra, and it separates the two chiralities by the eigenvalue $\pm1$. In the standard mechanism the Higgs is what supplies the hypercharge shift turning a right-handed fermion into a left-handed one; the source proposes the pseudoscalar $\omega$ of a Clifford algebra as the algebraic object in that role, for $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ and, with the quarks added, for $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$, where it notes in passing that for the up quark $\omega$ "gets squared to one" and the assignment is therefore not uniform across a generation.

Where the interpretation stops. The corpus records this as an interpretation, and names the boundary. What is exact is $\omega$'s centrality, self-adjointness, square and its action as the relative sign of the two sets, all recomputed; what is not in the algebra is a coupling. The construction is a finite algebra with no field content, no hypercharge multiplet structure and no Yukawa operator, so "$\omega$ is the Higgs" names an element whose algebraic role resembles the Higgs's role in the standard mechanism, and is not a derivation of a Higgs field, of a Higgs potential, or of a coupling. It is recorded alongside the framework's own scalar-sector ledger rather than as a result of it.

Two claims cited to the standard literature. The source also states, as properties of the left-right symmetric model as such, that the model explains the strong CP problem and that the Higgs coupling vanishes before electroweak symmetry breaking, citing Hall and Harigaya. These are claims about the left-right model and not about the division algebras; the corpus records them as attributed statements of the source's introduction and conclusion, and takes no position on them.

The Sterile Neutrino, Its Mass and the Seesaw

Sterility. The anomaly conditions on a gauge theory with a single $\mathrm{U}(1)$ are $\sum Y=0$, $\sum Y^3=0$ and $\sum YT_3^2=0$ over the fermions, and they constrain only the gauge-charged content. A right-handed neutrino that is a complete gauge singlet carries $Y=0$, $T_3=0$ and colour $0$, so it contributes nothing to any of the three sums; the corpus's anomaly article establishes the same conclusion in the biquaternion framework, that sterile fermions are anomaly-unconstrained. The doubling therefore adds a state that no consistency condition of the gauge sector forbids, and the construction's inclusion of it is not a claim about anomaly cancellation but a claim about the pattern of the fermions.

Mass and the seesaw. A sterile fermion is allowed a Majorana mass $M_R$ as a renormalisable term, unlike the gauge-charged fermions; through the type-I seesaw of The Seesaw Mechanism and Majorana Mass in Biquaternionic Form, a Dirac mass $m_D$ linking $\nu_L$ to $\nu_R$ and a large Majorana mass $M_R$ give a light neutrino of mass $m_\nu\simeq m_D^2/M_R$, so the construction's $\nu_R$ is exactly the state that the seesaw needs and the smallness of the observed neutrino masses follows from the largeness of $M_R$. The corpus's seesaw article works the mechanism in the framework's own conventions: the Dirac mass is the linear chirality-off-diagonal pair, the Majorana block is the self-conjugate pairing on the module real structure, and the renormalisable-level obstruction is the statement that a mass term for a chiral fermion requires a compensating scalar. The construction supplies the second chirality whose presence the seesaw's Dirac mass requires; the mechanism and the scale are the cited standard treatment and are not recomputed here.

Dark matter. A keV-scale sterile neutrino is a standard warm dark matter candidate, produced by oscillation from the active neutrinos in the early universe and detected only gravitationally. The source proposes the construction's right-handed neutrino as this candidate. The corpus records the proposal and its status: the mass scale and the production mechanism are the standard ones of the sterile-neutrino dark-matter literature, the construction's contribution is the presence of the state in the algebra, and the dark-matter identification is an interpretation in the same sense as the rest.

The proposed probe, and the Majorana requirement. The source names neutrinoless double beta decay as the experiment proposed to infer a right-handed sterile neutrino, and notes that both the seesaw and its own mass-ratio programme require the neutrino to be a Majorana particle. The corpus records both as the standard statements they are — the seesaw needs a Majorana mass for the singlet, and $0\nu\beta\beta$ is the standard lepton-number-violating probe of it — and records that the construction contributes the presence of the state and not the decay rate, the effective mass or any nuclear matrix element. The Neutrino and Majorana Fermions in Biquaternionic Form carries the corpus's treatment of the Majorana condition.

Three statements deserve emphasis.

  • What is an interpretation. The Further Programme of the Source

The construction is presented by its authors as one step of a larger programme, and the corpus records the further steps in the same spirit, as the source's programme rather than as results.

Mass as a square root and a second $\mathrm{U}(1)$. The source proposes that the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ number operator of the right-chiral fermions — the second summand's charge operator, not the hypercharge — be identified with the square root of mass, $\pm\sqrt{m}$, which like electric charge comes with both signs, one sign for matter and the other for antimatter. On that identification the square root of mass is quantised in the ratio $(\tfrac13,\tfrac23,1)$, assigned respectively to the electron, the up quark and the down quark, so that relative to the electric-charge assignment of the first $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ the positions of the electron and the down quark are interchanged in the second $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ while the up quark keeps its place. This is what makes the second abelian factor a mass factor rather than a second electric charge, and the source calls it $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\text{grav}}$ and associates it with gravitation; in the pre-theory of the next paragraph the two invariants merge into a single "charge-root-mass", quantised in units $0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,1$ with both signs. The shift from the earlier sections is worth marking: there the two summands carry the same $\mathrm{U}(1)$ charge, and the reassignment of the second copy's $\mathrm{U}(1)$ from electric charge to square-root mass is a step of the source's later programme, not a property of the doubling. The corpus records the whole chain as a proposal and notes that its own mass articles read mass differently: The Petiau System and the Quantisation of Mass in Biquaternionic Form reads mass from the eigenvalues of $\tilde E\tilde E^{*}$ and from the double-periodic Petiau modulus, and does not use a square root.

Leptoquarks before the breaking. The source puts one further speculation, as a question, at the end of its sixth section: whether before electroweak symmetry breaking the left-handed and the right-handed particles existed in a combined state of leptoquarks, carrying both the electric charge and the square-root-mass charge. In its account the leptoquarks are bosons, described by the doubled algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$ — two copies of $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ and the split bioctonions — which the source gives as its reason for the study of the split bioctonions. On the merged identification the two quantum numbers become one, the charge-root-mass, quantised in units $0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,1$ with both signs, the negative sign for antiparticles. The corpus records the proposal as the source's programme and as a question rather than a result: the word leptoquark occurs nowhere else in the corpus, the construction exhibits no leptoquark field, no coupling to the states of the two summands and no decay, and the bosonic character that the source assigns to the combined states is a statement of its pre-theory, not a property of the two Clifford algebras.

The square-root mass ratios and the exceptional Jordan algebra. The $1:2:3$ statement of the source's fourth and sixth sections is about the square roots of the masses of the electron, the up quark and the down quark — one generation, leptons and quarks together, not the charged leptons alone — and is quoted as the motivation for the mass-ratio programme rather than derived in this paper. The separate three-generation statement is that the eigenvalues of the exceptional Jordan matrices $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ with fermionic entries give the mass ratios of the three Standard Model generations, summarised in the source's Figure 2 and worked out in its own reference on the fine-structure constant and mass ratios; the source proposes obtaining the three generations themselves as rotations of the fermionic representation of any one generation, a transformation of $J_3(\mathbb{O})$. The classical theory treats $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ as the carrier of the exceptional structures, and the corpus records both statements as the source's proposals: it does not recompute the eigenvalue fit, the fermionic-entry construction or the three-generation rotation. The $1:2:3$ pattern is not a charged-lepton ratio, and it is not the eigenvalue statement for $J_3(\mathbb{O})$; the source makes two different claims, and they are kept apart here.

$\mathrm{E}_6$, $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ and the exceptional subgroups. $\mathrm{F}_4$ is the automorphism group of the Albert algebra $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ and sits inside $\mathrm{E}_6$ as a maximal subgroup, the quotient $\mathrm{E}_6/\mathrm{F}_4$ being a compact symmetric space of dimension $26$; the two are not related by complexification, their complex dimensions being $52$ and $78$. The source uses the two maximal subgroups of $\mathrm{F}_4$ — $H_1=[\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)]/\mathbb{Z}_3$, of dimension $16$, and $H_2=\mathrm{Spin}(9)$, of dimension $36$ — which are not isomorphic, their dimensions differing — whose intersection $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$, of dimension $12$, is the Standard Model's gauge group; and their counterparts in $\mathrm{E}_6$: the subgroups $\tilde{R}_{\psi,1}=[\mathrm{SU}(3)]^3/\mathbb{Z}_3$ and $\tilde{R}_{\psi,2}=\mathrm{Spin}(10)$, whose intersection $$ \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1) $$ is the left-right symmetric gauge group. These subgroup statements are standard Lie theory, cited by the source from the literature of Boyle and of Dubois-Violette and Todorov; the corpus records them as standard and not as results of the construction.

$\mathrm{E}_8\times\mathrm{E}_8$, trace dynamics and pre-spacetime. The source's further programme proposes an $\mathrm{E}_8\times\mathrm{E}_8$ structure, one factor for each chirality, as the carrier of a pre-gravitational interaction; a passage from that structure to quantum theory and gravity through Adler's trace dynamics, in which quantum physics emerges as the statistical mechanics of a deterministic matrix mechanics; and a pre-spacetime in which the algebra precedes and generates the spacetime manifold. Two consequences are stated sharply enough to record separately. The first is what happens to $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$: rather than being the gauge factor of a right-handed weak interaction, it is used for a chiral formulation of gravity, and in place of a spin-$2$ graviton the programme proposes three spin-$1$ Lorentz bosons, on an equal footing with the other gauge bosons — a departure from the left-right model as usually written, which the corpus marks. The second is the arena: before symmetry breaking the spacetime is taken to be ten-dimensional and labelled by the octonions, its symmetry being the action of $\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb{O})$ on the Hermitian $2\times2$ octonionic matrices $J_2(\mathbb{O})$, whose determinant gives a $(1,9)$ signature, so that the Lorentz–weak symmetry is unified with the electro-colour symmetry through an eight-dimensional octonionic Kaluza–Klein structure; the $J_3(\mathbb{O})\supset J_2(\mathbb{O})\supset J_2(\mathbb{C})$ stabiliser chain of the preceding paragraph is read as the chain in which the stabiliser of $4$-dimensional spacetime sits inside the stabiliser of the $10$-dimensional one, giving the Standard Model group at the bottom. These are the programme's most speculative steps. The corpus records them as such: they are not derived from the two Clifford algebras of this article, and the corpus's ledger already records that the framework's ceiling is the reason such structures have to be supplied outside the biquaternion algebra. The Coleman–Mandula theorem, which the source invokes, forbids the combination of spacetime and internal symmetries of the ordinary kind; the programme argues that in a pre-spacetime with no background manifold the distinction between internal and spacetime symmetries has not yet arisen, so the theorem does not apply, and the corpus records the argument without endorsing it.

The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices. The generators carry no frame label: $\gamma_\mu$ is one set of matrices, $\gamma_0^2=I_4$ and $\gamma_k^2=-I_4$. The frame belongs to the coordinate, not to the array.

Every section but this one is written on the algebra; here the same objects are written as gamma matrices, for readers who want the standard notation. The gammas are a frame of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, not a different algebra.

The cells are those of the setup, $L_aR_b$ with $x\mapsto axb$ and the right factors multiplying in reverse order; $c(M)$ is the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products, and $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$ its cell rank. The same in the axis-free display:

$$ \begin{aligned} \gamma_0&=iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}, & \gamma_1&=iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}, & \gamma_2&=iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\\ \gamma_3&=iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}, & \gamma_5&=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}} . \end{aligned} $$

Every gamma is a single cell. The single cells are the pure tensors; they form the seven-dimensional cone $\ell(M)\le1$, and a general element of the algebra is a sum of at most four of them.

Chirality. $\gamma_5=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ is the chirality cell of The Two Dirac Equations, and the product of the four frame cells returns it, $$ \gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3=-R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}},\qquad\text{so}\qquad i\,\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3=-iR_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}=\gamma_5 , $$ with the four factors carrying $i^4=1$ and the left and right products evaluated as in Two rungs, two volume elements. So $\omega$, $\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ and $\gamma_5$ are three different objects: a central element, its root, and the cell that carries it on the module. $\gamma_5$ represents $\xi_{\mathrm{ad}}$ on the Dirac module, as $\pm1$ on the two chiral halves, and the sign of the representation — hence which summand is called left — is the only freedom left.

Traces. With $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$, $$ \mathrm{tr}(\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu)=4\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu},\qquad \mathrm{tr}(\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\gamma_\rho\gamma_\sigma)=-4i\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}. $$

The dictionary. It is complex-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

The display. Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. A remark on the idempotent construction. A display labelled Peirce in this section is a frame, a choice of basis for the arrays; a Peirce decomposition is what a primitive idempotent produces on the algebra. The two are different kinds of object, and it is the second that this remark is about. The source builds its isospin mechanism from two choices, and the corpus keeps them in view because they are easy to mistake for structure. The first is a primitive idempotent of the right-action factor, $\tilde\Pi_+=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z)$; a primitive idempotent is a point of the projective line of $\mathbb{B}$, so choosing one chooses a direction in the algebra. The second is the axis $\tau_3=1-2N$ of the ladder factor, which is what makes interchange and annihilate meaningful for a pair of states. With those two choices the source writes the isospin triple $\tau_a$, the totally isotropic elements $\beta_1=\tfrac12(-\vec e_x+i\vec e_y\tau_3)$ and $\beta_2=\Omega_L^{\dagger}i\vec e_y$, and the four states $V_R$, $V_L$, $e^-_R$, $e^-_L$ built from them, and obtains its statement that the generators annihilate the right-handed pair and interchange the left-handed one. Every step of it is correct and none of it is adopted here, because a Peirce basis is a coordinate choice attached to one idempotent: change the root and the four states change, while the chirality splitting of Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra does not. The rule that follows is that chirality is a side, read from the frame, and that a Peirce decomposition is a coordinate system laid on top of it. The two agree at exactly one root, up to sign: the one aligned with the volume cell, where the Peirce sign is $P=R_{i\vec e_z}=iR_{\vec e_z}=-\gamma_5$ and the projector on the ideal is $\tfrac12(\mathrm I-\gamma_5)$, the projector on the $L$ half. That agreement is a coincidence of arrays at one root out of the projective line, and it is the origin of every statement that a Peirce idempotent is a chirality.

Summary

The left-right symmetric construction of Vaibhav and Singh rests on two exact algebraic facts and a set of interpretations.

The first fact is the doubling. The central volume element of each algebra is an involution, $\omega^2=J^2=1$, so $\mathrm{Cl}(3)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(2)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}(2)$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(7)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(6)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}(6)$: the idempotents $\tfrac12(1\pm\omega)$ and $\tfrac12(1\pm J)$ cut each algebra into two summands on which the volume element acts by $\pm1$, the two inequivalent pinor representations, and the parity automorphism $e_i\mapsto-e_i$ exchanges them. The second fact is the pair of commuting quaternion actions of the biquaternion algebra, whose combination generates $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$ and whose imaginary parts span the $\mathrm{su}(2)\oplus\mathrm{su}(2)$ of $\mathrm{so}(4)$.

The interpretations are the identification of the two summands with the two chiralities and of the two quaternion actions with $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, the identification of the neutral state of the right-handed summand with the sterile right-handed neutrino, the assignment of the states to the Pati–Salam multiplets, and the interpretation of the volume element $\omega$ as the Higgs.

The equation side is written on the algebra and not on arrays: the chirality-off-diagonal pair $\tilde\nabla\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R=m\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L$ and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_L=m\tilde{Ξ}^{\mathrm{sp}}_R$, one equation per chirality, together with the single two-sided equation $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}F=m(-iR_{\vec e_z})\bar F$, in which the mass applies the frame's volume cell to $\bar F$ as a right multiplication, and that cell squares to $e_t$.

The construction's contact with the framework's ledger is that the second chirality and the sterile state are supplied by the algebra rather than added by hand, and that a sterile fermion is unconstrained by the anomaly conditions. Its open end is the same as that of the undoubled construction: the three commutator computations that would decide whether the abelian charge is a physical gauge charge, together with the choice of the supercharge sector that would fix which states carry which weak quantum numbers.

Beyond the doubling, the article records what the two actions do to the chirality — the left action preserves both halves and the right action mixes them, so that the source's mechanism for making each factor act on one chirality needs an idempotent and an axis and is recorded as such — and the source's further programme: the proposal of a combined leptoquark state before symmetry breaking; the later reassignment of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ from a weak factor to a chiral formulation of gravity on a ten-dimensional octonionic pre-spacetime, with the second summand's $\mathrm{U}(1)$ read as a square root of mass; and the exceptional structures — $J_3(\mathbb{O})$, $\mathrm{F}_4\subset\mathrm{E}_6$, $\mathrm{E}_8\times\mathrm{E}_8$ — that the programme needs and the biquaternion algebra cannot supply.

The $\mathrm{Cl}(5)$ rung belongs to the construction of the weak factors rather than to the programme. There the left action of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$ and the right action of $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}$ together generate $$ \mathrm{Cl}(3)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_4(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Cl}(5), $$ which is the algebra in which the source's Coxeter-element parity reversal lives.

The construction, like the particle-roster one, is outside the biquaternion algebra, and it is recorded here for the same reason: it is the literature's most economical answer to the chirality and sterile-neutrino questions, and its successes and its interpretations are best kept apart.

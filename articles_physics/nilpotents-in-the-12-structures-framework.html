Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework

Introduction

A nilpotent of index two of a product is a nonzero element whose square vanishes, $\tilde\Upsilon\star \tilde\Upsilon=0$, and the corpus calls it a **square-zero** element and writes it $\tilde\Upsilon$ where the nilpotency is the point, following Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe. The square-zero set of a structure is the solution set of the equation, the origin included. A square-zero element is an element that annihilates itself, so the square-zero set is the self-annihilation part of the zero-divisor theory, and its physical object is the lightlike displacement: the algebraic form of an infinitesimal object, of which two successive applications give nothing beyond the first. The photon is the physical member, and the reading is The Photon as a Null Element: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not together with The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone. This article is the sixth and last physics companion of the mathematics comparison group, and its mathematics companion is Nilpotents (articles_maths/nilpotents.md).

The mathematics companion proves two facts that organise the physics. First, the square-zero sets of the twelve structures are ordered by the cone: the general plain sesquilinear structures have the origin alone, the plain structures have the pure isotropic cone, the quaternionic bilinear structures have the whole cone, and the two alternating structures have the whole space, while the two antisymmetric sesquilinear structures have a subfamily outside the cone that contains the unit. Second, the square-zero set is the self-annihilation part of the zero-divisor set: it lies inside the zero-divisor set of its structure and the cone lies in every one of them, but the converse fails for nine of the twelve structures, and it fails there because of a property of the structure and not of the element.

Those two facts are two physical statements. The order of the sets is the order of the bands by their massless content: the probability band has no massless element, the composition band has the pure null directions, the interval band has all the lightlike elements, and the brackets have no proper square-zero notion at all. The gap between the square-zero set and the zero-divisor set is the statement that a state is not a lightlike displacement: in the composition band a state is a zero divisor that is not square-zero, and the two notions must be kept apart. The companion article Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures Framework owns the degeneracy, the companion article Idempotents in the 12 Structures Framework owns the states, and the present article owns the lightlike displacements.

Conventions. We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_k^{2}=-e_0$; $i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The central square is $C(\tilde Q)e_0=\tilde Q^{\natural} \tilde Q$ with $C(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$, and the **cone** is $\mathcal{C}={C=0}$. A square-zero element is a nonzero element with $\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0$ in the product at hand; the corpus writes it $\tilde\Upsilon$. The material coordinate is $\tilde Q=i c t\,e_0+\mathbf x$. The twelve structures are cited by their three-letter names.

A Square-Zero Element Is an Infinitesimal

The equation and the self-annihilation. The equation $\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0$ has a direct reading: the element kills itself. In the language of the companion article Annihilators in the 12 Structures Framework the square-zero set is the set of elements lying in their own annihilator, $\tilde\Upsilon\star\tilde\Upsilon=0$ exactly when $\tilde\Upsilon\in A_\star(\tilde\Upsilon)$ and $\tilde\Upsilon\in B_\star(\tilde\Upsilon)$, the annihilator being read in the same product.

The physical reading. A nonzero element whose square vanishes is an infinitesimal object: applied once it moves, applied twice it gives nothing beyond the first order, and that is the defining property of a first-order (infinitesimal) generator. The physical members are the lightlike displacement and the photon, read in The Photon as a Null Element: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not and The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone. The same square-zero algebra appears a second time, as the algebra of the Grassmann generators of the corpus, read in Grassmann Coherent States in Biquaternionic Form; the two occurrences are distinct, and the closing section of this article separates them. The nilpotent convention $\tilde\Upsilon$ marks the elements of $\mathbb{B}$ in Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

The square-zero set is the lightlike set of its band. The physical content of the twelve square-zero sets is thus a classification of the lightlike objects of the twelve bands, and the physical question each band answers is whether a lightlike object exists in it at all.

The Four General Products: Four Sizes of the Lightlike Set

The non-pure cone elements, the multiples of the projectors, are not square-zero: they are the states, and the separation of the two families of the cone is the physical content of the next section.
  • The interval band. The natural square is the norm, so the square-zero set is the whole cone: every lightlike element of the interval band annihilates itself. This is the largest square-zero set among the non-alternating structures, and the physical reading is that the band whose value is the interval knows masslessness completely.
  • The probability band. The third square has the scalar part $H=\sum_\mu\lvert P_\mu\rvert^{2}$, definite, so its square-zero set is the origin alone. The physical reading is that the probability band has no lightlike element: a nonzero amplitude never composes to nothing. Masslessness does not live in the band that measures probability, and the owner of the positivity is Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone.
  • The gauge band. The fourth square vanishes on the subfamily $\overline{\tilde P}\tilde P=0$, which contains $e_0+ie_1$ and misses $ie_0+e_1+e_2+ie_3$. It is a proper subfamily of the cone, so the gauge band has lightlike elements that are not square-zero: the fourth product distinguishes a part of the lightlike family. The owner is The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric.

The order of the four sets. The mathematics companion proves $Z_3=\{0\}\subsetneq Z_1\subsetneq Z_2$, $Z_3\subsetneq Z_4\subsetneq Z_2$ and $Z_1\cap Z_4=\{0\}$. The physical reading is the ranking of the bands by their lightlike content: the probability band has none, the composition band has the pure ones, the interval band has all of them, and the plain and the gauge square-zero sets are complementary inside the cone, meeting only at the origin. A lightlike element that is square-zero in the composition band is not square-zero in the gauge band, and conversely: the two bands read two different kinds of lightlike object.

  • The whole space, for the two alternating structures. The square of the plain and the quaternionic bracket vanishes identically, so every element is square-zero. The physical reading is the antisymmetry of a bracket: the bracket of an element with itself is zero, so an infinitesimal transformation is square-zero as a value. The bracket has no proper notion of a lightlike displacement; the owner of the bracket is The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket.

The antisymmetric sesquilinear structures have a square-zero set that is not inside the cone and that contains the unit $e_0$; the physical reading is that the conjugate bracket annihilates the identity, so the global phase is an infinitesimal object of that bracket. The square-zero set of those two rows is not a lightlike set at all, and the corpus reads the same rows as the ones the bracket is invisible on in Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form.

The Gap: a State Is Not a Lightlike Displacement

The mathematics companion states the gap between the two sets: a vanishing square is an annihilating factor, so the square-zero set without the origin lies in the zero-divisor set of every structure, but the converse fails for nine of the twelve structures. The two sets agree, origin included, exactly for the three structures whose square vanishes identically or is the central square: the natural product and the two alternating brackets.

The physical reading is the distinction between the two kinds of non-invertible element. A zero divisor is any operation that cannot be undone, and a square-zero element is one that cannot be undone because it is infinitesimal: it is a lightlike displacement. The plain product shows the gap in the sharpest way. Its zero-divisor set is the whole punctured cone, its square-zero set is the pure part alone, and the complement is the non-pure cone, the multiples of the projectors, which are the states. A state is therefore a zero divisor that is not square-zero: it cannot be composed, but it is not a lightlike displacement, and it is idempotent, $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$, rather than nilpotent, $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$. The two equations are the two extreme quadratic equations of the algebra, and the two objects they define are the two physical objects of the pair of companion articles: the state in Idempotents in the 12 Structures Framework and the lightlike displacement here. The owner of the physical locus of the zero divisors is Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form, and the reading of the cone as the massless locus is The Cone and the Massless Particles in the 12 Structures Framework.

The physical statement is that the set of the non-invertible operations is strictly larger than the set of the lightlike ones, and the difference is made of the states. In the probability band the gap is maximal in the other direction: the zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space and the square-zero set is the origin alone, so no element of that band is lightlike, although almost every element is non-invertible. The physical content is that a probability is non-invertible for the reason that it is a probability and not because it is lightlike.

The Fermionic Ladder: Nilpotency as Statistics

The nilpotents are the square-zero elements, and the corpus assigns them two physical jobs. The first is the lightlike displacement, read in the light cone articles. The second is exact and less expected: the square-zero class contains the fermionic ladder operators of a single mode. Separating the two jobs is the content of this section.

The lightlike displacement. The material pure nilpotents $Q_0=0$, $(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)=0$ are the lightlike directions, and the natural product squares every cone element to zero. The physical particle is the photon, and The Photon as a Null Element: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not fixes how far the identification goes. A nilpotent has a direction and no magnitude; its square vanishing is the algebra's statement that a null displacement is not accumulated, which is the vanishing of the interval of a null vector.

The fermionic ladder. The one-mode ladder of Fock Space and Creation/Annihilation Operators in Biquaternionic Form is the pair

$$ \tilde\Upsilon_1=\tfrac12(ie_1-e_2),\qquad \tilde\Upsilon_2=\tfrac12(ie_1+e_2), $$

and a direct computation gives

$$ \tilde\Upsilon_1^{2}=\tilde\Upsilon_2^{2}=0,\qquad \tilde\Upsilon_1\tilde\Upsilon_2=\tilde\Pi_1,\qquad \tilde\Upsilon_2\tilde\Upsilon_1=\tilde\Pi_2,\qquad \{\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\}=e_0, $$

with $\tilde\Pi_1=\tfrac12(e_0+ie_3)$ and $\tilde\Pi_2=\tfrac12(e_0-ie_3)$ the vacuum and occupied projectors of The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent. The creation and annihilation operators of a single fermionic mode are therefore nilpotents of the algebra itself, and the canonical anticommutator $\{\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger\}=e_0$ is the statement that a square-zero pair multiplies to the identity. Pauli exclusion is the vanishing square: $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ says that a second application of the same step gives nothing, which is exactly the algebraic form of the statement that a mode cannot be occupied twice. The vacuum is the product of the two steps, $\tilde\Pi_1=\tilde\Upsilon_1\tilde\Upsilon_2$, so the state and the ladder are not independent objects: the state is what the nilpotent pair creates. The Spin–Statistics Theorem in Biquaternionic Form fixes the anticommutator, and The Dirac Sea, Hole Theory and Antimatter in Biquaternionic Form gives the two-projector picture of the mode, which the pair $\tilde\Pi_1,\tilde\Pi_2$ is in the algebra.

Why the ladder is the nilpotent one. The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent records that the algebra contains no bosonic ladder: no pair $\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger\in\mathbb{B}$ can satisfy $[\tilde a,\tilde a^\dagger]=e_0$, because a commutator has vanishing trace while $\mathrm{Tr}(e_0)=2$. The trace argument selects the anticommutator, hence the fermionic statistics, for this algebra's ladder, and the nilpotent class is the class that can carry it. The nilpotent article therefore belongs beside the idempotent article for a reason sharper than a shared position on the cone: the state is the idempotent and the ladder is the nilpotent, and the two are the two halves of one mode.

A nilpotent generates a truncated flow. A nilpotent has no higher powers, so its exponential truncates at first order: $\exp(t\tilde\Upsilon)=e_0+t\tilde\Upsilon$. A nilpotent is therefore an infinitesimal generator whose one-parameter group is a straight line rather than a circle, in contrast with a root of $-1$, whose exponential is a full rotation. This is the algebraic form of the distinction between a translation and a rotation, and it is why the nilpotent is read as a displacement: the flow it generates does not curve, and its square vanishes because two successive infinitesimal steps compose to a first-order object and no more.

Two homes for square zero. The square-zero elements of $\mathbb{B}$ are not the only square-zero objects of the framework. Grassmann Coherent States in Biquaternionic Form shows that the fermionic parity of a field is not an element of $\mathbb{B}$ at all and requires an odd extension $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathcal{G}$, whose odd generators are square-zero and anticommute. Those generators are odd, and every element of $\mathbb{B}$ is even, so the two square-zero classes are different objects: the nilpotents of $\mathbb{B}$ are even lightlike displacements and ladder steps, and the Grassmann generators are odd labels of a Fock space. The word "nilpotent" without the algebra is ambiguous, and the subject of this article is the even class.

The Odd Nilpotents: Cohomology, Supercharge and the Dirac Cone

The square-zero elements of $\mathbb{B}$ are even. The corpus's physics also uses square-zero objects that are odd, and those live in the extensions $\mathbb{B}\otimes\Lambda$ and $\mathbb{B}\otimes \mathcal{G}$. The two classes share the equation $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ and nothing else, and the odd class is the engine of cohomology, of supersymmetry and of the topological invariants of Dirac matter.

A nilpotent differential needs an associative product. BRST Symmetry in Biquaternionic Form introduces a Grassmann-odd derivation $s$ with $s^{2}=0$, the BRST charge $Q$ with $Q^{2}=0$, and identifies the physical states with the cohomology $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\ker Q/\mathrm{im}\,Q$. The article's own point is where the nilpotency comes from: $s^{2}=0$ rests on the Jacobi identity of the gauge algebra, and in the framework that Jacobi identity is a consequence of the associativity of quaternion multiplication. The associativity of the parent product is therefore what makes a nilpotent differential available, and the cohomology is then the annihilator of the nilpotent quotiented by its image: the physical states are exactly the states a nilpotent operator does not reach, with the reachable ones removed. This is the annihilator reading of Annihilators in the 12 Structures Framework sharpened by one more structure, and the three brackets of the corpus that fail the Jacobi identity — read in A Higher Structure for the Jacobi Failure — are precisely the ones that cannot carry such a differential. A bracket that does not close is a symmetry without a cohomology.

The supercharge and the Dirac operator. Supersymmetric Quantum Physics in the Biquaternion Framework uses the pair $Q,Q^{\dagger}$ with

$$ Q^{2}=0,\qquad (Q^{\dagger})^{2}=0,\qquad \{Q,Q^{\dagger}\}=H, $$

and argues that the biquaternion first-order operator is the natural supercharge: the Dirac operator squares to the scalar d'Alembertian, $\tilde\nabla\tilde\nabla^{\natural}=\Box$, exactly as the anticommutator of a supercharge is the Hamiltonian. The nilpotent $Q^{2}=0$ is again an odd object, and the framework supplies the operator, the module and the grading while the odd coordinates are adjoined. The even nilpotents of this article and the odd nilpotents of the supersymmetric construction therefore sit on the two sides of one boundary: the even class is a lightlike displacement inside $\mathbb{B}$, and the odd class is a derivative whose vanishing square is what makes a cohomology exist.

The Dirac cone and the null direction. Dirac Matter: Graphene, Dirac Cones and Topological Insulators in Biquaternionic Form reads the massless low-energy excitations of graphene and of a topological-insulator surface as a two-component spinor with a linear dispersion, and the framework names the three objects involved: the spinor, the $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p$ kinetic term and the mass term that opens the gap. The Dirac point is the momentum at which the dispersion is linear and the gap closes, and the masslessness of that point is the vanishing of the interval. The nilpotent of this article is the momentum element at that point: a lightlike momentum with $\tilde k\tilde k^{\natural}=0$, the algebraic form of the linear branch. The article's caution applies here too: the framework transcribes the low-energy equation, and the lattice, the Fermi velocity and the topological invariant are material data and not algebraic output.

The null points come in pairs. A null direction of the momentum symbol is a zero of the symbol, and on a lattice the zeros are counted by topology. Fermion Doubling and the Nielsen–Ninomiya Theorem in Biquaternionic Form states the count: the Fourier transform of the inverse propagator is a continuous vector field on the Brillouin zone, its isolated zeros are the particle species, and the index of the field at each zero is $\pm1$, the two signs being the two handednesses. Poincaré–Hopf makes the sum of the indices the Euler characteristic of the Brillouin zone, which for a four-torus is zero, so the null points cannot be removed one at a time: they appear in left–right pairs. The article's caution belongs to the reading — the biquaternion algebra is not a lattice and does not supply one — so the statement is about the discretised symbol, and what it says is that the square-zero set of a lattice symbol is topologically paired and not the property of a single isolated mode.

Nilpotency is why the Wick theorem is exact. The Wick Theorem in Biquaternionic Form observes that for a single fermionic mode the theorem is not an approximation or a statement about vacuum expectation values in a large space: because the mode operators are square-zero, $\tilde\Upsilon_1^{2}=\tilde\Upsilon_2^{2}=0$, every product of more than two mode operators collapses, so the four-point and six-point contractions reduce to products of the single two-point contraction with the fermionic signs. The identity is then an exact operator identity in a four-real-dimensional subalgebra, proved by exhibiting both sides. Nilpotency is therefore what makes the fermionic perturbation series terminate: the collapse of every product beyond the second is the algebraic reason the contractions are finite in number, and the graph expansion of a fermionic theory has no infinite series to resum at the level of one mode.

The single statement. A vanishing square is the algebra's widest structural equation: the same equation that makes a lightlike displacement an infinitesimal makes a BRST charge a cohomological differential and a supercharge a supersymmetry generator. What differs is not the equation but the grading of the carrier. The even carrier is a displacement inside the algebra; the odd carrier is a derivative of an extension; and both are read by the square because the square is the one operation that tests whether a second application adds anything.

Physical Interpretation

  • A square-zero element kills itself: it is an element of its own annihilator. Physically it is an infinitesimal object, of which two successive applications give nothing, and the lightlike displacement is the member; the photon is the physical particle.
  • The plain composition band has the pure isotropic cone as its square-zero set, of real dimension four: the pure lightlike directions. The non-pure cone elements are the states, and they are not square-zero.
  • The interval band has the whole cone: every lightlike element is self-annihilating there, and that band knows masslessness completely.
  • The probability band has the origin alone: no nonzero amplitude squares to zero, so masslessness is absent from the band that measures probability. A probability is non-invertible because it is a probability, not because it is lightlike.
  • The gauge band has a proper subfamily of the cone, so it reads only part of the lightlike family as square-zero.
  • The two brackets have the whole space: an infinitesimal rotation is square-zero as a value, and the bracket has no proper notion of a lightlike displacement. The conjugate brackets annihilate the identity, so their square-zero set leaves the cone.
  • The set of the non-invertible operations is strictly larger than the set of the lightlike ones, and the difference is made of the states. A state is a zero divisor that is idempotent, a lightlike displacement is a zero divisor that is nilpotent.
  • The one-mode fermionic ladder is a pair of nilpotents, with $\tilde\Upsilon_1^{2}=\tilde\Upsilon_2^{2}=0$ and $\{\tilde\Upsilon_1,\tilde\Upsilon_2\}=e_0$, whose product is the vacuum projector. Nilpotency is Pauli exclusion, and the trace obstruction is why the algebra's ladder is fermionic.
  • The same equation $Q^{2}=0$ governs the odd nilpotents of the corpus: the BRST charge, whose cohomology $\ker Q/\mathrm{im}\,Q$ is the physical state space, and the supercharge, whose anticommutator is the Hamiltonian that the biquaternion Dirac operator realises. The even carrier is a lightlike displacement inside $\mathbb{B}$ and the odd carrier is a derivative of an extension.
  • A nilpotent differential exists only where the bracket closes: the associativity of quaternion multiplication is what makes the BRST charge nilpotent, and the three corpus brackets that fail the Jacobi identity are the ones that cannot carry a cohomology.
  • The Dirac point of graphene and of a topological-insulator surface is a lightlike momentum, $\tilde k\tilde k^{\natural}=0$: the nilpotent is the algebraic form of the linear, gapless branch.
  • On a lattice the null points of the momentum symbol are counted by topology: the index sum is the Euler characteristic of the Brillouin zone, so they come in left–right pairs and cannot be removed one at a time (Nielsen–Ninomiya). Nilpotency of the discretised symbol is a paired, topological property.
  • Nilpotency makes the fermionic Wick theorem an exact operator identity rather than an approximation: the collapse of products beyond the second leaves only the two-point contraction, so the graph expansion of a single mode has no infinite series to resum.

Summary

A nilpotent of index two, or square-zero element, is a nonzero element whose square in the product at hand vanishes, and it is an element that kills itself: it lies in its own annihilator. Physically it is an infinitesimal object, the lightlike displacement, and the corpus writes it $\tilde\Upsilon$. The mathematics companion computes the square-zero sets of the twelve structures and orders them by the cone. The plain composition band has the pure isotropic cone of real dimension four, the interval band the whole cone, the probability band the origin alone and the gauge band a proper subfamily of the cone, while the two brackets have the whole space and the two antisymmetric sesquilinear structures have a subfamily outside the cone that contains the unit. Read physically, the order is the order of the bands by their lightlike content: none in the probability band, the pure ones in the composition band, all of them in the interval band, and no proper lightlike notion in the brackets. The square-zero set lies in the zero-divisor set of its structure and contains the cone, but the two sets agree for only three of the twelve structures, and the gap for the other nine is the distinction between the two kinds of non-invertible element. The plain product shows it: its zero divisors include the states, which are idempotent and not square-zero, and its square-zero elements are the pure lightlike displacements alone. A state is a zero divisor that is idempotent; a lightlike displacement is a zero divisor that is nilpotent; and the two equations $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ and $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$ are the two extremes of the quadratic element theory of the algebra.

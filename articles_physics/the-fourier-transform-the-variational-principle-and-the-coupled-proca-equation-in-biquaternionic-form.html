The Fourier Transform, the Variational Principle and the Coupled Proca Equation in Biquaternionic Form

Introduction

The coupled variational parent ends with a precise complaint and this article answers the part of it that frequency space can answer. The Variational Principle and the Coupled Proca Equation in Biquaternionic Form states the action, proves the mass term's exact invariance, states the field term's exact non-invariance, and records that the symmetric pairing $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde F\tilde F)$ is invariant under the phase "only when the phase's square is one". The complaint is that the algebra's single field strength cannot carry a complex bivector's Lagrangian. The article's first finding is the frequency-space form of that complaint, and it is one number.

The two terms of the action transform under a constant phase in two different ways, and the difference is measurable. At a mode the mass term is $\tfrac12k_m^{2}A^{\dagger}A$, a sesquilinear form, invariant under $A\mapsto e^{i\alpha}A$: measured at $0.169050$ for every $\alpha$ in $\{0,\,0.7,\,1.9,\,3.1\}$. The field term is $\tfrac12[kA]^{2}$, a bilinear form, and it acquires $e^{2i\alpha}$: the ratio's argument is $1.400000$, $-2.483185$ and $-0.083185$, which is $2\alpha$ modulo $2\pi$. So the mismatch that the parent states as "the phase's square must be one" is a phase factor of $2\alpha$ on one term and $1$ on the other, and it is exact.

The second finding is that the constraint is a determination and not a residue. The parent's coupled reading is explicit: on the coupled carrier the mass term does not break the gauge symmetry, so the constraint is not the residue of a broken symmetry; it says that the longitudinal part is determined by the transverse part through the mass. In frequency space the determination is a division: $a_t'=\vec k_\Phi\cdot\vec a/k_t'$, verified to $8\times10^{-17}$ on two test amplitudes. The constraint is one amplitude expressed through three.

The third finding is that the longitudinal coefficient survives the coupling unchanged. Along the shifted four-momentum the form is $\tfrac12k_m^{2}(A\!\cdot\!A)$, so its magnitude is $\tfrac12k_m^{2}$ — $0.245000000$ at $k_m=0.7$ — at every momentum tested, and the coupling's only effect is the sign, which follows $\pi^{2}$. The coupling cannot change what the constraint costs, and that is the frequency-space form of the parent's "the constraint is a consequence and not an imposed condition".

The fourth finding is that the primary constraint survives the coupling as an absent power. The coefficient of $A_0^{2}$ is $\tfrac12(\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2})$, with no $\pi_t^{2}$: measured at $1.065000000$ for $\pi_t=0.9,\,1.6,\,2.4$ while the first spatial coefficient moves from $0.060$ to $0.935$ to $2.535$. A covariant derivative cannot put a time derivative on $A_0$, because $F_{0\eta}$ has none.

The fifth finding is the asymmetry between the two conserved quantities. The charge current carries no mass, because it is built from $i\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco}}$ and the mass is not in the gradient; the energy carries $k_m^{2}$, because the trace is the mass term. The charge is the phase's and the energy is the scale's, and the article states the two as one sentence about which term of the action each one comes from.

The denial is stated plainly. No sector is exchanged. The action is real, the form at a mode is a complex number, and the phase is a coordinate. There is no new equation and no new current; the action, the equation and both currents are the parent's.

The parent's action is quoted once and not re-derived:

The momentum-space form is the action at one wave number, with two terms of different kinds. For a constant connection,

$$ S=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\;\Bigl[\mathcal{F}(A)+\mathcal{M}(A)\Bigr] , \qquad \mathcal{F}=\tfrac12\,[\,\pi A\,]^{2} , \qquad \mathcal{M}=\tfrac12\,k_m^{2}\,A^{\dagger}A , $$

where $[\,\pi A\,]=\pi_t a_t'+\vec\pi\cdot\vec a$ is the shifted Lorenz form and $A^{\dagger}A=\lvert a_t'\rvert^{2}+\lvert\vec a\rvert^{2}$ the positive amplitude norm of the complex carrier. $\mathcal{F}$ is bilinear in $A$ and $\mathcal{M}$ is sesquilinear, and every asymmetry in the article descends from that one fact.

The stationarity is the coupled symbol. $\partial S/\partial A^{\dagger}=-\sigma_{\mathrm{gco}}A$ with $\sigma_{\mathrm{gco}}=-\pi^{2}\delta+\frac{i\varkappa}{\hbar}F^{(\Phi)}-\pi^{\mu}\pi_{\nu}$, so the momentum-space variational principle is the coupled Proca equation. The form companion's machinery applies with $\pi$ in place of $k$, and the article uses it without repeating it.

The pairing is the parent's and the article's, and they agree. The parent varies $\tilde A$ and $\tilde A^{*}$ independently with $\delta\tilde A^{*}$ on the left, which is the pairing the field term's reality dictates; at a mode that is the derivative with respect to $A^{\dagger}$. The modulus pairing would give the energy and not the equation, and the parent states the distinction and the article inherits it.

The constant-connection licence. The form is the action at a mode for a constant connection. A varying connection has no form at a mode, and the element companion shows why: the symbol is not a function of the momentum alone.

The Form on a Mode

The two terms have different algebraic characters and that is the article's engine. $\mathcal{F}$ is a square of a linear form in $A$; $\mathcal{M}$ is a Hermitian square. A square of a linear form is bilinear; a Hermitian square is sesquilinear, and the whole of the phase behaviour follows: the first picks up the transformation's factor twice and the second cancels it.

The split is the parent's and it is the parent's complaint in one line. The parent's §The Field Term and the Charge observes that a complex bivector has twelve real components and the algebra's field strength has six, so the field term cannot be stored in one field strength. At a mode the same observation is the statement that $\mathcal{F}$ depends on $A$ only through the four numbers $[\,\pi A\,]$ and its square, so the field term of a complex amplitude is one complex number and its conjugate is not in the term.

The mass term sees the amplitude and the field term sees only a projection of it. $\mathcal{M}$ is a function of all four components and their conjugates; $\mathcal{F}$ is a function of one linear combination. That asymmetry is why the mass term is the one that fixes the normalisation and the field term is the one that leaves a direction free, and it is the frequency-space form of the parent's "the field term's status is stated precisely rather than assumed".

The form is complex and the action is its real part. $\mathcal{F}$ is complex for a complex amplitude and real for a material one; $\mathcal{M}$ is real always. So the action at a mode is $\mathrm{Re}(\mathcal{F})+\mathcal{M}$, and the imaginary part of $\mathcal{F}$ is the part the action discards — the same discard the parent records at $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Sc}$.

What the imaginary part does is carry the phase's mismatch. A bilinear form's complex value has an argument that rotates at twice the phase and a modulus that does not; discarding the imaginary part therefore discards the information that would have made the term invariant. That is the article's sharpest statement about the parent's complaint, and §The Two Terms Transform Differently measures it.

The Two Terms Transform Differently

Under a constant central phase the mass term does not move and the field term rotates at twice the angle. With $\alpha$ a constant and $A\mapsto e^{i\alpha}A$,

$$ \mathcal{M}\bigl(e^{i\alpha}A\bigr)=\mathcal{M}(A) , \qquad \mathcal{F}\bigl(e^{i\alpha}A\bigr)=e^{2i\alpha}\,\mathcal{F}(A) , $$

the first because $e^{-i\alpha}e^{i\alpha}=1$ and the second because the two factors of the square each carry one power. Measured on a random complex amplitude at $\pi_t=1.2$, $\vec\pi=(0.3,0.2,0.1)$, $k_m=0.7$:

$0$ $0.169050$ $0.158799$ $0.000000$ $0.000000$
$0.7$ $0.169050$ $0.158799$ $+1.400000$ $+1.400000$
$1.9$ $0.169050$ $0.158799$ $-2.483185$ $+3.800000$
$3.1$ $0.169050$ $0.158799$ $-0.083185$ $+6.200000$

Four rows, one invariant column, one rotating column. The mass term's value is identical in every row to six digits, and the field term's modulus is identical in every row while its argument advances by exactly twice the phase. So the field term is invariant in modulus and not in argument, which is the precise statement the parent makes in prose.

The parent's "only when the phase's square is one" is the case $\alpha\in\{0,\pi\}$. At $\alpha=\pi$ the factor is $e^{2i\pi}=1$ and the field term returns to itself; at every other angle it does not. So invariance holds on a discrete two-element set and nowhere else, and the article's table is that sentence with numbers.

The consequence for the action is that the phase is a symmetry of one term and not of the other. The mass term alone would give a conserved charge for every constant $\alpha$; the sum gives one only at $\alpha\in\{0,\pi\}$, which is no continuous symmetry at all. So the coupled action's phase symmetry is not a symmetry of the action as written, and the parent's record of the field term's exact non-invariance is the same statement.

What an invariant field term would need is a sesquilinear structure, and the algebra's single field strength does not supply one. A sesquilinear term would pair $\tilde F$ with its conjugate, which is the parent's $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde F\overline{\tilde F'})$, and in frequency space that pairing needs a second field strength to conjugate. The missing second half is the parent's twelve-minus-six, and the article's display of $e^{2i\alpha}$ is the arithmetic of the same deficit.

The Constraint Is a Determination

The parent's coupled reading of the constraint is a determination and not a residue, and frequency space makes the determination a division. On the coupled carrier the mass term does not break the gauge symmetry, so the free article's "the mass breaks it" reading is unavailable; what remains is that the equation's longitudinal content determines the temporal amplitude from the spatial ones:

$$ [kA]=0 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a_t'=\frac{\vec k_\Phi\cdot\vec a}{k_t'} . $$

Verified on two test amplitudes, $\vec a=(0.5,-0.2,0.8)$ and $(0.1,0.3,-0.4)$: the determined temporal amplitudes are $0.239377500$ and $0.062994079$, and the resulting amplitudes satisfy $[kA]=6.7\times10^{-18}$ and $3.6\times10^{-18}$ and $\lvert\sigma A\rvert=8.0\times10^{-17}$ and $3.3\times10^{-17}$.

So the constraint is one equation and it is not a vanishing of the field. The amplitude is not zero, not lightlike and not in any special branch: it is one of four components expressed through the other three. That is what "determined by the transverse part through the mass" means when the mass is finite, and the division shows the mass's role: at $k_m=0$ the same computation gives $[kA]=0$ as a free condition on the three spatial components, and the temporal one is undetermined.

The coefficient of the longitudinal direction is the mass and the coupling leaves it alone. Along $\pi^{\mu}$ the form is $\tfrac12k_m^{2}(A\!\cdot\!A)$, so its magnitude is $\tfrac12k_m^{2}=0.245000000$ at $k_m=0.7$ for every momentum tested, the sign following $\pi^{2}$: $-0.245000000$ at $\pi_t=0.9$ and $+0.245000000$ at $\pi_t=1.6$ and $2.4$, the sign change being the crossing of the shifted cone. The translation moves the cone and does not move the coefficient's magnitude, which is the frequency-space form of "the mass is the only scale in the constraint".

The constraint's symbol is free of curvature and its coefficient is free of the coupling. The trace of $\sigma_{\mathrm{gco}}$ is $-4\pi^{2}$ and carries no $F$; the longitudinal coefficient carries no $\varkappa$ except through the sign. So the constraint is the one part of the coupled theory whose structure is exactly the free one's, and the parent's "the constraint holds to machine precision in a background with constant field strength" is that statement's numerical form.

What the constraint is not is the gauge-fixing condition of the free theory. The free article's constraint was the vanishing of a coefficient that the mass supplied, and it removed the direction along the wave number; here the same equation removes the gradient part of the amplitude and leaves the mass's longitudinal polarisation. The two readings are the same equation and different bookkeeping, and the parent says so: "the same statement in both cases with a different bookkeeping".

The Temporal Coefficient Has No Frequency

The primary constraint survives the coupling as an absent power of $\pi_t$. Expanding the form's diagonal,

$$ \frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A_0^{2}}=\tfrac12\bigl(\pi_t^{2}-\pi^{2}+k_m^{2}\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2}\bigr) , \qquad \frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A_\eta^{2}}=\tfrac12\bigl(\pi_\eta^{2}+\pi^{2}-k_m^{2}\bigr) , $$

so the temporal coefficient has no $\pi_t^{2}$ and the three spatial coefficients each carry $\pi_t^{2}$ through $\pi^{2}$. Measured with $\varkappa=1$, $\vec A=(0.9,0.2,0.1)$ and $\vec k_\Phi=(0.3,0.2,0.1)$, so $\vec\pi=(1.2,0.4,0.2)$ and $\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}=1.64$:

$1.6$ $1.065000000$ $1.065000000$ $+0.935000$

One fixed column and one moving column. The temporal coefficient does not move with the frequency, and the spatial ones do, exactly as in the free case. The coupling cannot add a $\pi_t^{2}$ to $A_0$, because the curvature's temporal-spatial components are $\partial_0A_\eta-\partial_\eta A_0$ and neither contains $\partial_0A_0$.

So the count is four minus one in both theories, and the reason is the field strength's antisymmetry and not the mass. The free article's absent power and the coupled article's absent power are the same absent power. That is the article's cleanest agreement with its free companion, and it is worth stating because the coupling changes almost everything else.

What the coupling changes is the coefficient's value and not its character. $\tfrac12(\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2})$ moves with the background potential through $\vec\pi$ and with the mass; it never vanishes for a massive field in a bounded background. So the primary constraint is never a dynamical equation, in the free theory or the coupled one.

The Charge Has No Mass and the Energy Has Mass-Squared

The two conserved quantities come from the two terms and they carry different constants. The parent's charge current is a Noether current of the phase, and the phase is the mass term's symmetry; the parent's stress tensor is a Noether current of translations, and its trace is the mass term. In frequency space the charge current's symbol carries $\pi_\mu$ and is linear in one power of the amplitude, so it carries no $k_m$; the trace carries $k_m^{2}$ and nothing else on the material slice.

The charge's mass-independence is structural and not a cancellation. $j_\mu=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A^{*}\,i\tilde\nabla_{\mathrm{gco},\mu}\tilde A)$ has the coupling in the gradient and the amplitude in both slots; the mass appears in neither. So the charge's symbol is $-i\pi_\mu$ times the amplitude's bilinear, and the mass cannot enter because the mass is not in the operator.

The energy's mass-dependence is the trace's. The parent's material-slice trace is $T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}\propto k_m^{2}\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A^{*}\tilde A^{\natural})$ exactly, and the form companion's symbol trace is $3\pi^{2}-4k_m^{2}$, which at $\pi^{2}=k_m^{2}$ is $-k_m^{2}$. So the two traces agree at the shell, and the agreement is the reason the article can state one sentence for both.

The asymmetry is the theory's and not the article's. A field with a mass has a conserved charge that does not carry the mass and an energy that does. The reason is that the charge is the phase's Noether current and the phase is a coordinate of the field, while the energy is the scale's and the scale is the mass's own coordinate — the parent's chart makes that division visible, and the article states it as the ledger's last row.

What the asymmetry forbids is a statement of the form "the charge measures the mass". It does not; the charge measures the phase's flow and the mass measures the scale's. A frequency-space statement about the mass is a statement about the trace or about the longitudinal coefficient, and those are the two places the article has looked.

The Legendre Inversion Is Two-Valued

The form is quadratic in the frequency, so solving it for the frequency gives two roots. $\mathcal{F}$ is a square of a form linear in $\pi_t$ and $\mathcal{M}$ is independent of $\pi_t$; the resulting polynomial in $\omega$ is quadratic. So every value of the other variables corresponds to two frequencies, and the pair is the particle–antiparticle pair.

With the connection the reflection's axis is translated and not moved. The form depends on $\pi_t=\hbar\omega/c+\varkappa\varphi$ quadratically, so it is even under

$$ \omega\longmapsto-\omega-\frac{2\varkappa c\,\varphi}{\hbar} , $$

a reflection about $\omega=-\varkappa c\varphi/\hbar$ rather than about zero. A constant scalar potential therefore moves the axis of the two-valued inversion, which is the frequency-space form of "a constant potential shifts every energy".

The two-valuedness is a property of the form's degree and not of the Proca field. A first-order theory's form is linear in the frequency and its inversion is single-valued; the Dirac variational companion's inversion is of that kind. The Proca form is degree two, so its inversion is degree two, and the article states the degree rather than the analogy.

What the two roots mean for the constraint is nothing, and that is worth stating. The constraint is the vanishing of the temporal coefficient's $\pi_t$ dependence, which is absent at both roots; the two roots are the two branches' frequencies and both satisfy it. A constraint that does not depend on the frequency cannot select a root, and the article's last sentence about the inversion is that the two structures are independent.

Limits

The constant-connection licence bounds the form. The form is the action at a mode for a constant connection; a varying connection has no form at a mode, and the element companion's argument is the reason.

The field term's status is the parent's and the article does not improve it. The action is written in the free article's form, its field term is not phase-invariant, and the invariant pairing's normalisation is open. Every statement above about $\mathcal{F}$ is a statement about the action as written, and the article says so because the temptation to read the $e^{2i\alpha}$ as a defect of the field rather than of the term is strong.

The Abelian case is the one treated. The phase's centrality is what makes the transformation a constant multiplication, and the corpus's coupling is Abelian.

The denial, once more. No sector is exchanged. The action is real, the form is a complex number per mode, and the phase is a coordinate. None of the three is a statement about $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ and $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$.

Open Questions

Whether a sesquilinear field term exists whose normalisation is the free one's is open, and it is the parent's first open item. The frequency-space form of the question is sharp: a sesquilinear term is a function of $A$ and $A^{\dagger}$, so it is invariant under the phase; whether the algebra supplies a second field strength whose pairing with $\tilde F$ is sesquilinear and reduces to the Maxwell density is the question. The article's $e^{2i\alpha}$ is the arithmetic of the deficit and not a solution of it.

Whether the constraint's determination has a chart reading is open. The division $a_t'=\vec k_\Phi\cdot\vec a/k_t'$ is a statement about amplitudes. Whether it is the frequency-space image of the chart's one real equation among eight is a question the polar companion raises jointly, and neither article answers it.

Whether the charge's mass-independence survives a dynamical connection is open. The article's argument is that the mass is not in the operator, which holds for a background connection; with a dynamical connection the mass term would have a second variation and the argument would need redoing.

Whether the two-valued inversion has a reading in the currents is open. The charge is linear in the amplitude and the energy is quadratic, so the inversion's two roots give opposite charges and equal energies. Whether that is a statement about antiparticles or about the form's degree is a question the article raises and does not answer.

Summary

At a wave number the coupled Proca action is $\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{M}=\tfrac12[\,\pi A\,]^{2}+\tfrac12k_m^{2}A^{\dagger}A$ with $\pi_\mu=\hbar k_\mu+\varkappa A_\mu$, and its stationarity is the coupled symbol.

The two terms have different algebraic characters and transform differently under a constant phase. $\mathcal{M}$ is sesquilinear and does not move: $0.169050$ at $\alpha=0,\,0.7,\,1.9,\,3.1$. $\mathcal{F}$ is bilinear and rotates at twice the angle: the ratio's argument is $1.400000$, $-2.483185$, $-0.083185$, which is $2\alpha$ modulo $2\pi$, while its modulus stays at $0.158799$. Invariance holds only at $\alpha\in\{0,\pi\}$, which is the parent's "only when the phase's square is one" as a table.

The constraint is a determination: $[kA]=0$ gives $a_t'=\vec k_\Phi\cdot\vec a/k_t'$, verified to $8\times10^{-17}$ on two amplitudes, so the temporal component is expressed through the three spatial ones and the mass is what makes the division possible. The longitudinal coefficient's magnitude is $\tfrac12k_m^{2}=0.245000000$ at every momentum tested, the sign following $\pi^{2}$, so the coupling moves the cone and not the cost.

The temporal coefficient is $\tfrac12(\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}+k_m^{2})$, with no $\pi_t^{2}$: $1.065000000$ at $\pi_t=0.9,\,1.6,\,2.4$ while the first spatial coefficient moves from $0.060$ to $2.535$. The primary constraint survives the coupling, because $F_{0\eta}$ contains no $\partial_0A_0$, and the count is four minus one in both theories.

The charge carries no mass and the energy carries $k_m^{2}$: the charge's symbol is $-i\pi_\mu$ times a bilinear and the mass is not in the operator; the trace is $3\pi^{2}-4k_m^{2}$, which is $-k_m^{2}$ on the shell. The form is quadratic in the frequency, so the Legendre inversion is two-valued, and with a connection the two roots are reflections about $\omega=-\varkappa c\varphi/\hbar$ rather than about zero.

# __The Products, Their Forms and Their Norms__

## Introduction

The general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ is the multiplication of the biquaternion algebra
$\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, and it acts on the whole algebra: every element,
in either sector and in every remarkable subspace, is multiplied by it. Of the four general products of
the space it is the only one that is associative and two-sidedly unital, so it is the product that makes
$\mathbb{B}$ an associative unital algebra over $\mathbb{C}$; the other three are built from it and from
the conjugations (*The 12 Products of the Biquaternioid Complex Space*). This article states the products of the space — the four general products and their symmetric and
antisymmetric parts, which are the twelve — and reads them on the whole algebra through the scalar form, the
algebraic norm, the composition law and the reversibility.

The **law** is that the product is associative, has the unit $e_t$, and is not commutative, and the three
properties are read as the composition of operations. The **scalar part** is the **general plain bilinear
form** of this block,

$$
\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_t-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q,
$$

symmetric and non-degenerate, with Gram matrix $\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ in the coefficient
basis. It is neither the metric of spacetime nor the interval: the metric is the interval,
$\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q)$, the general quaternionic bilinear form of signature
$(-,+,+,+)$ on the material sector, and it belongs to the companion quaternionic block and to
*The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars*. The two forms sit on the same algebra with opposite types, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$
definite on each sector where the interval is indefinite, and the place where that opposition is read
physically is the material sector, in the companion article *The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector*.

**Placement and boundaries.** This article opens the biquaternion-algebra group. It states the products of the space and their scalar forms on the whole algebra; the
restriction of the pair to the material sector, the sector sign and the absence of a material idempotent
are *The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector*; the multiplication operators, one-sided and two-sided,
and the sandwich are *The Operators of the Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich*; the
invertibility criterion, the group of units and the cone are the mathematics of *The Group of Units of the Biquaternion Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone* and the physical level data of
*The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars*, which owns the norm, the interval, the light cone and the mass
shell; the properties of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ — its symmetry, its non-degeneracy, its Gram matrix and its restriction to
the remarkable subspaces — are *The 4 Forms over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space* and *Remarkable
Subspaces under the General Plain Algebra of Biquaternions*; and the comparison of the four general
products is *The Four General Products and Their Physical Readings*. None of those is reproved here.

**Conventions.** $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis
$e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ with $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$, $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^{2}=-1$; a
general element is $\tilde Q=Q_te_t+Q_x\vec e_x+Q_y\vec e_y+Q_z\vec e_z$ with complex coefficients; the scalar part is
$\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)=Q_t$ and the vector part is $\vec Q=Q_x\vec e_x+Q_y\vec e_y+Q_z\vec e_z$. The conjugations are
the complex conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, the natural (quaternion) conjugation ${\natural}$, and the
Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{\natural}$. The material sector is the anti-Hermitian half,
$\mathbb{M}_-=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q\}$, with the real basis $\{ie_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$; the
informational sector is the Hermitian half, $\mathbb{M}_+=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=+\tilde Q\}$, with the
real basis $\{e_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$.

## The Products

The biquaternion space carries **four general products**, and no more, obtained from the plain bilinear product by letting the first slot carry the identity or the natural conjugation and the second the identity or the star conjugation. Each of them splits in its two arguments into a **symmetric part** and an **antisymmetric part**, so the four general products give the **twelve products**; the dot and the wedge rebuild the four. The algebra of the twelve, their forms and their algebraic norms is *The Products, Their Forms and Their Algebraic Norms*; their naming and their physical job is *The Twelve Structures Compared: Product, Law and Form* and *The Four General Products and Their Physical Readings*.

### The Four General Products

**Definition.** The four general products are the operations obtained from the plain bilinear product by applying, independently, the identity or the natural conjugation in the first slot and the identity or the star conjugation in the second:

$$
\tilde P\tilde Q,\qquad \tilde P\tilde Q^{*},\qquad \tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q,\qquad \tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*},
$$

the **general plain bilinear** ($\mathrm{GPA}$), the **general quaternionic bilinear** ($\mathrm{GQA}$), the **general plain sesquilinear** ($\mathrm{GPS}$) and the **general quaternionic sesquilinear** ($\mathrm{GQS}$) product. The first slot decides whether the product is linear or conjugate-linear in the first argument, the second whether it is bilinear or sesquilinear. Only $\mathrm{GPA}$ is associative and two-sidedly unital; it is the multiplication of $\mathbb{B}$, and the other three are built from it and the two conjugations.

### The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts

**Definition.** Each general product is split in its two arguments into a **symmetric part**, the half-sum, written with the full dot $\bullet$, and an **antisymmetric part**, the half-difference, written with the wedge $\wedge$; the subscript names the slot as it does for the forms. The exchange is the interchange of the arguments for the two bilinear products and the conjugate transpose for the two sesquilinear ones, the exchange that keeps the class of the product. The four symmetric parts carry the four scalar forms; the four antisymmetric parts have zero scalar part, so they are invisible to the forms and it is in them that the order dependence sits.

**The symmetric parts.**

**The symmetric plain bilinear product**, the **Jordan product** $\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q$:

$$
\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q+\tilde Q\tilde P\bigr)=\bigl[P_tQ_t-(\vec P,\vec Q)\bigr]+P_t\vec Q+Q_t\vec P .
$$

It is the symmetrised product under which the observables close: two Hermitian elements have a Hermitian symmetrised product, and the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$ is a special Jordan algebra under it (*The Bilinear Product on the Informational Sector*).

**The symmetric quaternionic bilinear product**, $\tilde P\bullet_{\natural}\tilde Q$, central-valued; its scalar part is the interval:

$$
\tilde P\bullet_{\natural}\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q+\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P\bigr)=\bigl[P_tQ_t+(\vec P,\vec Q)\bigr] .
$$

**The symmetric plain sesquilinear product**, the **Hermitian form**, central-valued; it is the Born pairing:

$$
\tilde P\bullet_{*}\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}+(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})^{\natural}\bigr)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})=\bigl[P_t\overline{Q_t}+(\vec P,\overline{\vec Q})\bigr] .
$$

**The symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product**:

$$
\tilde P\bullet_{\natural*}\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural}\bigr)=\bigl[P_t\overline{Q_t}-(\vec P,\overline{\vec Q})\bigr]-P_t\overline{\vec Q}-\overline{Q_t}\vec P .
$$

**The antisymmetric parts.**

**The antisymmetric plain bilinear product**, the **outer product** $\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q$:

$$
\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P\bigr)=\vec P\times\vec Q .
$$

It is the **Lie bracket** of the product: the vector parts with the cross product form $\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)$, the algebra of the rotations.

**The antisymmetric quaternionic bilinear product**:

$$
\tilde P\wedge_{\natural}\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q-\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P\bigr)=P_t\vec Q-Q_t\vec P-\vec P\times\vec Q .
$$

**The antisymmetric plain sesquilinear product**, the vector part $\mathrm{Vect}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})$:

$$
\tilde P\wedge_{*}\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}-(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})^{\natural}\bigr)=\mathrm{Vect}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})=-P_t\overline{\vec Q}+\overline{Q_t}\vec P-\vec P\times\overline{\vec Q} .
$$

**The antisymmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product**, the cross product $\vec P\times\overline{\vec Q}$:

$$
\tilde P\wedge_{\natural*}\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}-\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural}\bigr)=\vec P\times\overline{\vec Q} .
$$

**Proposition.** The dot and the wedge rebuild the four general products:

$$
\tilde P\tilde Q=\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q+\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q,\qquad
\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q=\tilde P\bullet_{\natural}\tilde Q+\tilde P\wedge_{\natural}\tilde Q,\qquad
\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}=\tilde P\bullet_{*}\tilde Q+\tilde P\wedge_{*}\tilde Q,\qquad
\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}=\tilde P\bullet_{\natural*}\tilde Q+\tilde P\wedge_{\natural*}\tilde Q .
$$

The eight coordinate formulas and the four reconstructions are verified by recomputation on one hundred random pairs. Only the plain product is associative, so only its split names a classical algebra: $\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q$ is the Jordan product and $\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\vec P\times\vec Q$ the Lie bracket.

### The Plain Product and Its Laws

The **general plain bilinear product** $\tilde P\tilde Q$ is the multiplication of the algebra. Of the four general
products it is the only one that is associative and two-sidedly unital, so it is the product that makes
$\mathbb{B}$ an associative unital algebra over $\mathbb{C}$; the others are built from it and from the
conjugations. Its scalar part is the **general plain bilinear form** of this block,

$$
\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_t-P_1Q_x-P_2Q_y-P_3Q_z=P_0Q_t-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q,
$$

which is symmetric, since the scalar part is invariant under cyclic permutation
$\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde P)$, and non-degenerate, with Gram matrix
$\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ in the coefficient basis. It is the scalar part of the product of
the two underlying quaternions, and those three properties are cited from the mathematics articles named
in the introduction.

**Proposed interpretation, labelled as such.** The general plain bilinear product is read as the **composition of two
operations**: $\tilde P\tilde Q$ is "the operation $\tilde P$, then the operation $\tilde Q$", the
written order of the factors being the order of the operations, as in the polar word $\exp(i\alpha)B\tilde q$
of *The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions* (*The Twisted Spinor Operator Representation of
Biquaternions*). Three of its properties then carry their physical names.

- Associativity is composability: $\tilde P(\tilde Q\tilde{Q}_1)=(\tilde P\tilde Q)\tilde{Q}_1$, so a chain of
operations can be bracketed at will.
- The unit $e_t$ is the identity operation: $e_t\tilde Q=\tilde Qe_t=\tilde Q$.
- Non-commutativity is order dependence: two operations need not commute, and the commutator
$[\tilde P,\tilde Q]=\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P$ measures the failure.

What is proved is the associativity, the unit and the non-commutativity; what is proposed is that they be
read as the composition, the identity and the order of operations. The operation acts on the whole
algebra and not on one sector; which subspace it moves is read sector by sector in the companion
articles — on the material one by *The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector*, and on the compact and
unitary slices by *The Operators of the Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich* and
*Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action*.

**The product and the two involutions.** The two conjugations act on a composite by reversing the order,
$(\tilde P\tilde Q)^{\natural}=\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P^{\natural}$ and
$(\tilde P\tilde Q)^{*}=\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{*}$, so the Hermitian conjugation is an anti-automorphism of
the general plain bilinear product, and an element and its conjugate give the same scalar
$\langle\tilde P^{*},\tilde Q^{*}\rangle=\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle$, the scalar part being cyclically invariant and each
conjugation reversing the order. The two identities are algebra and are used throughout the block; the
second is the reason a product inherits the sector of its factors in the way the material article reads.

## The Forms

### The Scalar Form on the Algebra

The algebra carries four forms and no more, the scalar parts of the four general products: the two
bilinear $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and the two sesquilinear $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ (*The 4 Forms over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space*). $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ are complex-valued, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ is positive definite and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is indefinite, and
of the four only $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is multiplicative. The defining property of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ among them is that it is the scalar part
of the general plain bilinear product itself and the **plain** pairing, while $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the same product with the natural
conjugation inserted in the first slot, $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q)$.
The two differ by twice the coefficient square of the vector part,

$$
\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}-2\,\vec Q\!\cdot\!\vec Q,\qquad \vec Q\!\cdot\!\vec Q=Q_x^{2}+Q_y^{2}+Q_z^{2},
$$

so they agree only when the vector part is isotropic; the difference is the whole distinction between the
sector sign and the interval, and it is displayed on the two sectors in the companion material article.
The definiteness of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and the indefiniteness of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ are also what separates the form that measures from
the form that carries a metric, and that division is the physical job of the plain row.

### The Form on the Remarkable Subspaces

Besides the two sectors the algebra carries four other remarkable real subspaces, and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ has a different
signature on them. On the **centre** $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and the **vector subspace**
$\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, which are complex lines in each coordinate, the pairing of a real direction
with its imaginary companion is purely imaginary, and it is the real part of the form that is read. On all
four four-dimensional subspaces the form is real-valued. The restriction of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is then

| subspace | $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}$ | $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ | $2$ | $(1,1)$ |
| $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ | $6$ | $(3,3)$ |
| $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ | $4$ | $(1,3)$ |
| $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ | $4$ | $(3,1)$ |
| $\mathbb{M}_+$ | $4$ | $(4,0)$ |
| $\mathbb{M}_-$ | $4$ | $(0,4)$ |

Two features of the table carry physical content. First, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is **indefinite on the two quaternionic
subspaces** — $(1,3)$ on $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and $(3,1)$ on $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ — so it has
a real cone there, unlike on the sectors; that cone is **not** the light cone of the framework. Second,
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is **neutral on the centre and on the vector subspace**, $(1,1)$ and $(3,3)$: as many positive as
negative directions, so the form is definite on neither and carries no sector sign there.

The null set of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the complex cone $\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=0$, with
$\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, of real dimension six. It is a third cone, distinct from both the ones the
framework reads physically: the light cone is the null set of the interval read inside the material
sector, the zero-divisor cone is the null set of the biquaternion norm, and the null set of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is
neither. On the sectors, the sharpest case, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is definite and has no null element beyond the origin; the
material light cone is the cone of the interval read inside $\mathbb{M}_-$ and is not a null set of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$.
The restriction of the two forms to the remarkable subspaces is tabulated in *Remarkable Subspaces under
the General Plain Algebra of Biquaternions*.

## The Norms

### The Norm, the Units and the Cone

An element of the general plain bilinear product is invertible, and the criterion is not the product's own scalar form
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. The form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not multiplicative and supplies no criterion: two elements of non-vanishing $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ can
multiply to an element of vanishing $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, so $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ decides no inverse. The criterion is the norm of the
quaternionic bilinear form, which is multiplicative,

$$
\langle\tilde P\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde P\rangle_{\natural}\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural},\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}Q_\mu^{2},
$$

so that the invertible elements are the elements of non-vanishing norm, forming the **group of units**

$$
\mathbb{B}^{\times}=\{\tilde Q:\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0\},\qquad \tilde Q^{-1}=\frac{\tilde Q^{\natural}}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}},
$$

and the non-invertible ones are the elements of the **zero-divisor cone** $\mathcal{C}=\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$. The
algebra is the disjoint union of the units and the cone, the origin lying in the cone and being the one
element that is neither a unit nor a zero divisor, a zero divisor being required to be nonzero. The norm,
the group, the cone and the partition are the mathematics of *The Group of Units of the Biquaternion Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone* and the physical level data of *Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra*; what is read here is that they are read on the whole algebra at once, the criterion
being the same in every remarkable subspace, and the interior of the group being the off-cone part.

**Proposed interpretation, labelled as such.** *Invertible* reads **reversible**: the inverse is the
operation that undoes the operation, the group of units is the set of the reversible operations, and the
cone is the wall of the reversible region. The reading is stated on its own sector in *The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector*, where the cone is the light cone, the group is the set of the
off-light-cone four-vectors and the mass shell $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=-m^{2}c^{2}$ lies inside the group.

## Composition on Both Sides

An operation can be attached to the left of an argument or to its right, and the two possibilities are
the left and the right multiplications

$$
L_{\tilde Q}(\tilde{Q}_1)=\tilde Q\tilde{Q}_1,\qquad R_{\tilde P}(\tilde{Q}_1)=\tilde{Q}_1\tilde P,
\qquad L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde P}=R_{\tilde P}L_{\tilde Q},
$$

which commute. The composition that the framework actually uses is the **sandwich**

$$
\Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde{Q}_1)=\tilde Q\,\tilde{Q}_1\,\tilde Q^{*}=L_{\tilde Q}R_{\tilde Q^{*}}(\tilde{Q}_1),
$$

the parameter entering once on each side. It is the one action form of the framework: with a parameter of
unit norm, $\tilde U\tilde U^{\natural}=e_t$, it is a Lorentz transformation of the material
sector; with a unitary parameter, $\tilde A\tilde A^{*}=e_t$, the unitary evolution of the informational
one; and with a Hermitian idempotent parameter the measurement update. **Proposed interpretation, labelled
as such.** The commutation of the two multiplications is read as the independence of attaching an
operation to the left of an argument and attaching one to its right, and the sandwich as the composition
in which the parameter is attached on both sides, conjugate on the two. What is proved is the commutation
and the placement of the sandwich; the interpretation is a naming. The operators, their composition laws,
their adjoints and their symmetries are *The Operators of the Bilinear Product: One-Sided, Two-Sided and the Sandwich*; the adjoint of the sandwich and its two kinds of operator are *Observables, Gauge
Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action*, and the Lorentz action is *Biquaternion Rotations
and Lorentz Transformations*.

## The Bound

The article states the product and its scalar form on the whole algebra, and the boundary of what it must
declare is the same as the boundary of the form.

- **$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not the metric.** It is definite on each sector and has no cone there, so it cannot define the
causal structure. The metric is the interval, of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$.
- **$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not the interval.** The interval, the mass shell, the four-velocity and invertibility are the
level data of the general quaternionic bilinear form, owned by *The Four Forms, the Four Algebraic Norms and the Change of Scalars*; the
worked case of the companion material article shows the two forms giving different numbers on the same
element.
- **$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not the reversibility.** The criterion of invertibility is the norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ and not the scalar form
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$: the element $e_t+\vec e_x$ is a unit with $\langle e_t+\vec e_x,e_t+\vec e_x\rangle=0$ and the element $e_t+i\vec e_x$ is a zero divisor with $\langle e_t+i\vec e_x,e_t+i\vec e_x\rangle=2$,
so $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ decides no inverse. The group of units is the off-cone part of the algebra, and the reading of the
reversible operations is *The Group of Units of the Biquaternion Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone*.
- **$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not positivity.** The positive definite form of the state space and of the Born pairing is the
Hermitian form of the sesqualgebra block, which is $+\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on $\mathbb{M}_+$ and $-\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$;
positivity is blind to the sector.
- **$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is not the Krein form.** The indefinite metric of the fourth corner restricts to $(1,3)$ on all
four four-dimensional subspaces and so does not separate them.

**What the framework does not claim.** The interpretation does not say which physical operation an element
represents, does not derive the Lorentz transformation or the measurement update from the general plain bilinear product, and does not claim that every physical process is a product. The Lorentz action is the sandwich
of the section above, and the measurement update is an idempotent projection; neither is re-derived here.

## The Ledger

**Proved.** The general plain bilinear product is associative and two-sidedly unital, and its scalar part
$\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_t-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q$ is a symmetric
non-degenerate $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear form with Gram matrix $\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the
form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ restricts to $(1,1)$ on the centre, $(3,3)$ on the vector subspace, $(1,3)$ on
$\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, $(3,1)$ on $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, $(4,0)$ on $\mathbb{M}_+$ and $(0,4)$ on
$\mathbb{M}_-$, and its null set is a complex cone of real dimension six; the Hermitian conjugation is an
anti-automorphism of the product, $(\tilde P\tilde Q)^{*}=\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{*}$; the algebraic norm
$\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$ is multiplicative, its
non-vanishing set $\mathbb{B}^{\times}$ is the group of invertible elements of the general plain bilinear product with
the inverse $\tilde Q^{\natural}/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$, its vanishing set is the zero-divisor cone, and the algebra
is their disjoint union; and the left and right multiplications commute, so the sandwich is their
composition.

**Interpretations.** That the general plain bilinear product is the **composition of operations**, with associativity
read as composability, $e_t$ as the identity and non-commutativity as order dependence; that $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the
**plain** form that measures and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ the form that carries the metric; and that *invertible* reads
**reversible**, the group of units being the reversible operations and the cone the wall of the reversible
region. Each is the framework's naming of a proved algebraic fact and is labelled as such.

## Summary

The biquaternion space carries four general products — $\tilde P\tilde Q$, $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$, $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q$ and $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ — and each splits in its two arguments into a symmetric part $\bullet$ and an antisymmetric part $\wedge$, so the four give the twelve products and the dot and the wedge rebuild the four. Only the plain split names a classical algebra: its symmetrisation is the Jordan product of the observables and its antisymmetrisation the Lie bracket $\vec P\times\vec Q$ of the rotations.
The general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ — the only one of the four general products that is associative
and two-sidedly unital — acts on the whole biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$, and this article reads it
there. Its law is associativity, the unit $e_t$ and non-commutativity, read as the composition of
operations with the written order of the factors the order of the operations. Its scalar part, the
general plain bilinear form $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=P_0Q_t-\vec P\!\cdot\!\vec Q$,
is symmetric and non-degenerate with Gram matrix $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$; its restriction is $(1,1)$
on the centre, $(3,3)$ on the vector subspace, $(1,3)$ and $(3,1)$ on the two quaternionic subspaces, and
$(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$ on the two sectors, so it is neutral on the first two, indefinite with a cone on the
quaternionic pair, and definite on the sectors. Being definite there it has no light cone and cannot be
the metric; the metric is the interval, the general quaternionic bilinear form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ of the companion
quaternionic block, indefinite of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ on the material sector. The two forms differ by
twice the coefficient square of the vector part and agree only when the vector part is isotropic. The
invertibility of the general plain bilinear product is decided by the multiplicative norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ and not by $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$: the
invertible elements are the elements of non-vanishing norm, forming the group $\mathbb{B}^{\times}$ with
the inverse $\tilde Q^{\natural}/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ and bounded by the zero-divisor cone, and the Hermitian
conjugation is an anti-automorphism of the product. The left and right multiplications commute, so the
sandwich $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde{Q}_1)=\tilde Q\tilde{Q}_1\tilde Q^{*}$ is the composition on both sides. The
distinctions are labelled throughout: what is proved is the algebra on the whole space, and the reading of
the sector sign, of the sector of a composite and of the reversibility on the material sector is the
companion article *The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector*; what is proposed is the naming.

# __The Discrete Lattice and the Thermal Circle__

## Introduction

The corpus has two disciplines of discreteness, and they have never been compared. The **algebraic**
discipline is the discrete lattice: the integral structures of the algebra, the Lipschitz and Hurwitz orders,
and the finite groups of units attached to them — the quaternion group of order eight, the binary tetrahedral
group of order twenty-four and the binary polyhedral groups — all owned by *Biquaternion Orders and Finite
Groups of Units*. The **analytic** discipline is the thermal circle: the imaginary-time compactification and
the discrete Matsubara frequencies, owned by *The Matsubara Formalism in Biquaternionic Form*. The lattice is
static and algebraic, the circle is thermal and analytic, and this article states the one principle that
produces both.

The article owns the comparison and the principle, and no theorem. The orders and their unit groups are the
orders article; the frequencies, the circle and the KMS condition are the Matsubara article, *The KMS
Condition and the Biquaternion Framework* and *Thermal Time and the Modular Flow in the Biquaternion
Framework*; the central phase whose compactification the thermal circle realises is *The Central
Rotation: Phase, Duality and the Wick Rotation as One Generator*; the clock structure is *Each Sector Is the
Other's Clock: the Sector Exchange as Relational Time*; and the discrete charge is *Particle Types, Discrete
Charge and Three-Particle Couplings*.

**Conventions.** $U=\{\tilde{U}:\tilde{U}\tilde{U}^{*}=e_0\}\cong U(2)$ is the unitary slice,
$Sp(1)\cong SU(2)$ its real-quaternion part; the thermal circle is the compact imaginary-time direction of
circumference $\beta\hbar$, and the Matsubara frequencies are $\omega_n=2\pi n/\beta\hbar$.

## One Principle

The principle is a single sentence and it covers both disciplines.

> **Every discreteness in the corpus is the Pontryagin dual of a compactness.**

A discrete abelian group is the character group of a compact abelian group, and conversely. The corpus's
discrete data all sit on compact groups, and the discrete datum is the labelling of a representation of that
compact group — equivalently, of a character of a compact dual. Four instances, one per discipline and two
that bridge them:

| compact object | its discreteness | the corpus's reading |
|---|---|---|
| the central phase $U(1)$ | the integer phase winding | the discrete charge label (*Particle Types*) |
| the internal group $U(2)$, compact | its irreducible labels | internal quantum numbers, the discrete charge |
| the thermal circle, circumference $\beta\hbar$ | the Matsubara frequencies $\omega_n=2\pi n/\beta\hbar$ | the thermal spectrum (*The Matsubara Formalism*) |
| the unit sphere $Sp(1)\cong SU(2)$, compact | its finite subgroups and the orders above them | the finite unit groups and the discrete lattice (*Biquaternion Orders*) |

The first two rows are the same mechanism read at two sizes: the compactness of the phase is why angular
momentum and charge are discrete, and the internal group being compact is why its labels are discrete. The
third row is thermal: the imaginary-time direction is a **circle**, a compact direction, and its character
group is $\mathbb{Z}$, the Matsubara lattice. The fourth row is algebraic: the unit sphere is compact, and a
discrete subgroup of a compact group is finite, so the lattice of units is finite and the orders are the
integral lattices inside the compact group.

## The Lattice and the Circle as Two Duals

The principle sharpens when the two disciplines are set side by side, because they are duals of **one**
compact object and **two different** ones at once.

**One object.** The thermal circle is the central phase at finite temperature. The Matsubara analytic
continuation $t\mapsto-i\tau$ turns the time direction into the imaginary direction, and the imaginary
direction is the central phase: the KMS condition is the statement of periodicity in that phase, and the
modular flow it generates is the central rotation. So the circle whose dual is the Matsubara lattice is the
central phase compactified, and the discrete thermal spectrum and the discrete charge label are two
characters of one compact phase. That is the unification the article's title names: **the lattice and the
circle are dual readings of the compact central phase, one in the temperature and one in the charge.**

**Two objects.** The comparison also shows where the disciplines differ, and the difference is exact. The
algebraic lattice is a lattice of **invertible elements** inside the **real compact group** $Sp(1)$: it is
discrete because the group it sits in is compact and its elements are separated. The thermal lattice is
discrete because its dual — the circle — is compact, and it is a lattice of **frequencies**, not of
elements. So the two discretenesses are dual in the same sense and are not the same lattice: one is a
subgroup of a compact group, the other is the character group of a compact group. The first is **finite**;
the second is **infinite and periodic**. The corpus has a finite lattice and an infinite lattice, related to
two compactnesses of different origins, and the article states both rather than conflating them.

## Where the Two Meet: The Orders at Finite Temperature

The comparison suggests exactly one meeting point, and the article states it as a programme. The orders
article's finite unit groups are the discrete subgroups of the compact unit sphere, and the Matsubara
article's lattice is the dual of the compact imaginary-time circle. A finite-temperature reading of the
orders would require the discrete unit group to be a **spectrum** — the set of excitations the imaginary-time
circle supports — and the corpus supplies no such statement; the identification of the order's units with a
thermal partition function is not made anywhere, and the article records the gap rather than proposing a
formula. What is available is the shape: both are "discreteness from compactness", so a thermal reading of
the lattice would be a partition function whose spectrum is the unit group, and whether such a function is
the algebra's is open.

## Physical Readings

- **Discreteness is a consequence of compactness, never a postulate.** The reading is that every discrete
  label of the framework — charge, angular momentum, Matsubara frequency, unit lattice — is discrete because
  some group in its construction is compact, so the framework never has to impose a discretisation. Owner:
  the four articles of the table.
- **The thermal circle is the central phase.** The reading is that the imaginary time of the Wick rotation and
  the central phase of the algebra are one compact direction, so the Matsubara formalism is the central
  rotation at finite temperature. Owner: *The Matsubara Formalism in Biquaternionic Form* and *The Central
  Rotation*.
- **A finite lattice and an infinite lattice, one principle.** The reading is that the finite unit groups and
  the infinite Matsubara lattice are the two dual faces of the compactness principle, one a subgroup and one
  a character group, so the corpus's two disciplines of discreteness are one principle with two duals.
  Boundary: a statement of the principle, and the thermal reading of the orders is left open.

## Summary

The corpus's discrete data fall into two disciplines: the algebraic lattice of orders and finite unit groups
in the compact real unit sphere, and the thermal lattice of Matsubara frequencies dual to the compact
imaginary-time circle. This article states the one principle that produces both — every discreteness is the
Pontryagin dual of a compactness — and tables its four instances: the compact central phase, the compact
internal group, the thermal circle and the compact unit sphere. The two disciplines meet in the central
phase, which is the thermal circle at finite temperature, and they differ in that the algebraic lattice is a
finite subgroup while the thermal lattice is an infinite character group. A thermal reading of the orders is
named as a gap and not proposed as a formula.

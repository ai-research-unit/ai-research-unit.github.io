# __The Chiral Condensate and Dynamical Symmetry Breaking in Biquaternionic Form__

## Introduction

A **chiral condensate** is a nonzero vacuum expectation value of a fermion bilinear, $\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle\neq0$, in a theory whose Lagrangian contains no such term. It is the order parameter of a symmetry that the Lagrangian respects and the vacuum does not: the fermion field is massless as written, the vacuum pairs its two chiralities, and the fermion propagates with a mass it was never given. The mechanism is **dynamical symmetry breaking**, and it is why the pion is light: the pion is the Goldstone boson of a symmetry that the Lagrangian has and the vacuum does not.

In the biquaternion framework the fermion is the Dirac field of the companion articles and its mass is the **linear, chirality-off-diagonal pair**

$$
\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} = k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} , \qquad \tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} = k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} .
$$

Here $k_m=mc/\hbar$ is the mass wave number, the inverse of the mass length.

The framework carries two first-order writings of one mass coupling, and this article keeps them apart from the first line. The **gradient equation** is that pair on the algebra: every derivative acts by left multiplication, the two members are conjugate, and no chirality term is one of its terms, because no right multiplication is one. The **two-sided equation** is $(\mathcal{D}-k_m)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$, in which every derivative sits between a left multiplier and a right multiplier, and its letter is $\mathcal{D}$ and not the retired pair glyph, because it is one operator on one element and not a pair; the basis operator that carries it is the frame's fifth basis operator $\xi:\ Q\mapsto-iQ\vec e_z$, and that basis operator multiplies on the **right**, which is where the chirality is: the side, not the basis operator, is the notion. The condensate, its bilinears and the axial rotation are read from the two-sided equation; the gradient equation supplies the mass shell. The section *The Pair, the Two-Sided Equation and the Chirality* states both, and every later section says which one it uses.

This article asks what the framework supplies for the condensate. The answer is a short chain of algebraic facts and one large missing physical input. The categories are the corpus's: **established** (and recomputed here), **transcribed** from the standard literature, and **gap** (left visible).

- **Established, and recomputed below.** The massless two-sided equation $\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$ carries the two phase symmetries, the vector phase $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\mapsto\exp(i\alpha)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ on the left by a central element and the axial phase $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\mapsto\exp(i\alpha\xi)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\exp(\alpha\vec e_z)$ on the right, generated by the chirality operator; the massive linear chiral pair breaks the second. A condensate $\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$ is the vacuum expectation of exactly the bilinear that the mass term multiplies, so a nonzero condensate is a dynamically generated coefficient of the *same* linear chiral pair — not of the algebra's antilinear real structure $\flat$, which is a different object.

**The actions.** An **action** of the algebra is a map of the algebra into itself, and it is written by what it does. The four **generator actions** are the maps

$$
G_\mu:\ Q\ \longmapsto\ i\,e_\mu\,Q\,\vec e_{\nu(\mu)} ,
$$

with $e_t=1$ the unit of the algebra, $e_\eta=\vec e_\eta$, $\nu(t)=x$ and $\nu(\eta)=y$; the **chirality** is the frame's fifth basis operator, the operator

$$
\xi:\ Q\ \longmapsto\ -i\,Q\,\vec e_z .
$$

Each generator carries one factor of $i$, and the factor is structural: without it the time action would square to minus the identity action and the three spatial ones to plus it. The relations are

$$
G_\mu^{2}=\varepsilon_\mu\,\mathrm{id},\qquad
\{G_\mu,G_\nu\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{id},\qquad
\xi^{2}=\mathrm{id},\qquad
\{\xi,G_\mu\}=0\qquad(\mu=t,x,y,z),
$$

with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, the products read as actions and $\mathrm{id}$ the identity action. There is no root, no idempotent and no split behind these five maps: each is a product by units, and nothing else is introduced to hold it.

**The side, which is what chirality is.** A basis operator is the operator $L_aR_b$, acting $Q\mapsto aQb$, and it has two slots: the left one, where $a$ multiplies, and the right one, where $b$ multiplies. An operator multiplies on the left, on the right, or on both, and that is the whole of the corpus's notion of operator chirality ([Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra](../articles_maths/operator-chirality-on-the-biquaternion-algebra.html)): a property of the operator, not a sign carried by an element. The five maps above are read on that scale and they are not alike. The time generator is $G_t=iR_{\vec e_x}$, a **pure right multiplier**, because $L_{e_t}$ is the identity and a basis operator with the left slot empty is a right multiplication. The three spatial generators occupy **both slots**, $G_\eta=iL_{\vec e_\eta}R_{\vec e_y}$. The chirality is a **pure right multiplier** too,

$$
\xi=-iR_{\vec e_z},\qquad \xi Q=-i\,Q\,\vec e_z ,
$$

and it is the frame's fifth basis operator: four generators plus this one, declared together with the frame. Three consequences follow, and each is a statement about the slot rather than a computation about phases.

*It commutes with every left multiplication.* A left multiplier and a right multiplier never fail to commute, $L_aR_b=R_bL_a$, so $\xi$ commutes with the gradient $\tilde\nabla$, which multiplies on the left; and it commutes with the mass $m$, which is central and therefore occupies both slots. The value of a field is stable under every left multiplication, and hence under both members of the gradient equation; the reason is the side, and no computation about phases is needed.

*Its sign comes from the right slot alone.* Conjugating by $\xi$ does not touch a left multiplier at all, and on the right it reverses the multiplier, because right multiplications compose in reverse, $R_aR_b=R_{ba}$: it sends $R_{\vec e_x}$ and $R_{\vec e_y}$ to their negatives and leaves $R_{\vec e_z}$ alone. The right slot of every one of the four generators is $\vec e_x$ or $\vec e_y$, so

$$
\xi\,G_\mu\,\xi^{-1}=-G_\mu\qquad(\mu=t,x,y,z),
$$

that is $\{\xi,G_\mu\}=0$, and the left slot contributes nothing to the sign because $\xi$ has none. A reader who expects chirality to be *defined* by anticommuting with the frame cannot have it: the frame's time basis operator carries $L_{e_t}=\mathrm{id}$ in its left slot, and an operator that anticommuted with the identity would be zero. The sign in this algebra is not produced by an anticommutator; it is produced by the side.

*It is not a functional on the algebra.* A sign attached to elements would have to be a multiplicative map into $\{\pm1\}$, and none separates the two sides, because the two families of multipliers overlap in the scalars and a scalar has no side. The sign is one operator, the fifth basis operator of the frame, whose two eigenspaces are of equal size; the subscript below is what it does, and not what the algebra is.

**The chirality is a side, and the subscript reads it off.** The subscript takes two values, $L$ and $R$: $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ for a state, $Q_L$ and $Q_R$ for a general element. The letters are the Dirac ones. The value $L$ is the minus one and the value $R$ the plus one, as the corpus fixes them (*Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra*, §*What Operator Chirality Is Not*); this article uses the convention and does not restate it. The value is read from the one operator, $\xi Q_L=-Q_L$ and $\xi Q_R=+Q_R$, and the two eigenspaces are of equal size, so every element is one part of each value and only the zero element is both. A product takes the letter of its right factor, $(\tilde X\tilde Y)\vec e_z=\tilde X(\tilde Y\vec e_z)$, and left multiplication keeps the letter. The value is a property of the element and of the declared frame, and no second object is needed to carry it.

**The collapse of the imaginary unit.** Because $i^{2}=-1$, the product of the central imaginary with an action is a plain product by units, and the two collapses used below are

**The adjoint and the bilinears.** The **Dirac adjoint** is a pairing of two states and not an action of the algebra, written $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}$ — the Hermitian conjugate of the state, followed by the multiplication by $\vec e_x$. \quad\text{and}\quad
i\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z),

## The Pair, the Two-Sided Equation and the Chirality

**The gradient equation.** The gradient is $\tilde{\nabla}=e_t\partial/\partial R_t+\sum_\eta\vec e_\eta\partial/\partial R_\eta$, and its quaternion conjugate squares with it to the scalar d'Alembertian, $\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}=\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{\nabla}=\Box$. The chiral pair

$$
\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L},\qquad
\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}
$$

is the first-order dynamics of the algebra: each member acts by **left** multiplication, the two are conjugate, and the mass is the one term with no side, which is why it is the term that sets one value beside the other. No right multiplication appears in either member, so no chirality term appears either. This is the equation whose square gives the mass shell, and it is the one the article uses for that purpose.

**The two-sided equation.** The same mass coupling has a second writing, in which every derivative sits between a left multiplier and a right multiplier. The operator is a single two-sided contraction on one element, and it is written $\mathcal{D}$ and never with the retired pair glyph:

$$
\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=-\sum_\mu e_\mu\,\frac{\partial\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}}{\partial x_\mu}\,\vec e_{\nu(\mu)} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{D}^{2}=-\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_\mu^{2}}
=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r'_t^{2}}+\sum_\eta\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_\eta^{2}}=\Box ,
$$

a sum of four products — one element, one derivative of the field, one unit — and the square is the d'Alembertian: the first equality is the anticommutator $\{G_\mu,G_\rho\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\rho}$ with the derivatives commuting with the constant multipliers, the last is the definition $\Box=\partial^2/\partial R_\mu\partial R_\mu$ read on the $i r'_t$ slice, where $Q_\eta=x_\eta$ and $\partial^2/\partial R_t^2=-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2$, so that $\Box=-\partial^2/\partial {r'_t}^2+\sum_\eta\partial^2/\partial x_\eta^2$; the minus in front of the sum is the one of that convention, and it is taken once, here. The equation is $(\mathcal{D}-k_m)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$. It is one equation and not four, and because $\xi$ anticommutes with every generator it splits by value into two members, $\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ and $\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, which are the two lines of the pair read on the module: the derivative carries each value into the other and the mass carries it back. Every bilinear and every rotation below is an object of this equation.

**Chirality, and why it belongs to the two-sided equation.** On the algebra the statement is the side and nothing else: $\xi$ is a pure right multiplier and the gradient is a pure left multiplier, so they commute, and each member of the pair acts inside one value. The two-sided writing is where the derivative occupies **both** slots, and there the same operator anticommutes with it:

$$
\xi\,\mathcal{D}\,\xi^{-1}=-\mathcal{D},\qquad [\xi,\mathcal{D}^{2}]=0 .
$$

The two readings are two displays of one fact, and neither contradicts the other: commutation says the operator **preserves** the two halves of the algebra, anticommutation says it **exchanges** the two halves of the space the operator acts on. For a solution the conclusion is the same in both, because $\xi$ is its own inverse: if $\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$ then $\mathcal{D}(\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})=0$. And even here the reason is the slot: $\xi$ has no left slot, so no left multiplier can obstruct it, and the sign is produced by the right slot $\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}$, which is $\vec e_x$ or $\vec e_y$. The gradient equation carries no chirality *term*: neither member contains a right multiplication, and a left multiplication keeps the value, so each member stays inside one value and the mass is the only term that crosses. This is the precise sense in which the framework carries two first-order writings: the mass and the mass shell are statements of the algebra, and the bilinears and the axial rotation are statements of the two-sided equation, which is the writing in which a pairing exists at all.

## The Chiral Symmetry of the Linear Mass Term

**The massless equation and its two phases.** The massless two-sided equation is $\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$, and two one-parameter phase symmetries act on it. The **vector** (central) phase

$$
V:\ \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\ \longmapsto\ \exp(i\alpha)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}
$$

is multiplication on the left by the central element $\exp(i\alpha)\in\mathbb{B}_{C}$; it rotates the two values together. The **axial** phase

$$
A:\ \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\ \longmapsto\ \exp(i\alpha\xi)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\,\exp(\alpha\vec e_z)
$$

is multiplication on the **right**, and it has no left slot, as $\xi$ has none: its generator is the chirality operator itself, and the collapse $i\xi=R_{\vec e_z}$ turns the exponential into the right multiplication by $\cos\alpha\,e_t+\sin\alpha\,\vec e_z$. It rotates the two values oppositely, since $\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=-\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ on the $L$ part and $\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=+\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ on the $R$ part:

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}\longmapsto \exp(-i\alpha)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} ,\qquad \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}\longmapsto \exp(+i\alpha)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} .
$$

The rotation is an element and not a symbol: its two phase factors are read off from the two parts named by the subscript, and the relative phase of the two values is what it supplies.

**Why both are symmetries.** The central phase is constant, so it occupies both slots and commutes with $\mathcal{D}$ at any mass. The axial phase is a right multiplication and has no left slot: on the algebra it commutes with every left multiplier, hence with the gradient, and with the mass, which is central; in the two-sided writing the derivative occupies **both** slots, so there the same rotation does not commute with $\mathcal{D}$, and the amount by which it fails is exactly the phase $\exp(2i\alpha\xi)$. Hence

$$
V:\ [\mathcal{D},\exp(i\alpha)]=0 ,\qquad
A:\ U_\alpha\,\mathcal{D}\,U_\alpha^{-1}=\exp(2i\alpha\xi)\,\mathcal{D} ,
\qquad U_\alpha=\exp(i\alpha\xi) .
$$

The central relation is a commutation and the axial one is a conjugation by a phase, and either of them maps solutions of $\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$ to solutions: $\mathcal{D}U_\alpha=U_\alpha\exp(-2i\alpha\xi)\mathcal{D}$ annihilates the kernel. The two may be applied independently, and the massless theory has the flavour-chiral symmetry

$$
\mathrm{U}(1)_V\times \mathrm{U}(1)_A ,
$$

which for $N_f$ massless flavours enlarges to $SU(N_f)_L\times SU(N_f)_R\times \mathrm{U}(1)_V\times \mathrm{U}(1)_A$. The axial abelian factor is a classical symmetry: the anomaly breaks it, and the companion articles on the anomaly treat that breaking; only the classical pattern is used here.

**The mass term and which phase it breaks.** The massive linear chiral pair is

$$
\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} = k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} , \qquad \tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} = k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} ,
$$

whose two-sided writing is $(\mathcal{D}-k_m)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$. The two transformations act on it as follows.

Under the **vector** phase both values acquire the same factor, so the two sides of the first equation acquire $\exp(i\alpha)$ and $\exp(i\alpha)$: the equation is invariant. Under the **axial** phase the two halves acquire opposite factors, and the first equation becomes

$$
\exp(+i\alpha)\,\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}
=\exp(-i\alpha)\,k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} ,
$$

which is the original equation only when $m=0$; the second member fails in the same way. The reason is the one the side gives, and it is worth stating plainly, because it is not a failure of commutation: every term of the pair is a left multiplication, and the rotation is a right multiplication, so the rotation commutes with the gradient *and* with the mass. What it does is multiply an $R$-valued element by $\exp(+i\alpha)$ and an $L$-valued one by $\exp(-i\alpha)$, and the mass is the only term of the theory that sets one value beside the other. A term that pairs the two values cannot survive a rotation that moves them oppositely.

The same fact on the two-sided equation is a conjugation, and it is one line:

$$
U_\alpha\,(\mathcal{D}-k_m)\,U_\alpha^{-1}
=\exp(2i\alpha\xi)\,\mathcal{D}-k_m ,
\qquad U_\alpha=\exp(i\alpha\xi) ,
$$

since the rotation reaches the derivative, which occupies both slots, and leaves the mass alone, which has no side. The rotated field therefore solves the massive equation only at $m=0$ or at $\alpha\in\pi\mathbb{Z}$, while the central phase is a symmetry at every mass. The statement is exact, and it is again a statement about the slots: the mass is the one term with no side, so it is the one term the chirality cannot move.

This is the framework form of the standard fact that a Dirac mass breaks the axial symmetry while preserving the vector one. It is also the statement recorded in the companion articles on Noether's theorem and on chiral fermions, and it fixes what a condensate can do: a nonzero vacuum value of a chirality-odd bilinear will break $\mathrm{U}(1)_A$ and nothing else at this level.

**The axial current.** The axial current is the four-vector

$$
j_{5\mu} = \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\bigl(e_\mu\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\,\vec e_z\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}\bigr),
$$

one element between the two slots of the pairing: the left unit of the generator, the state, and the product $\vec e_z\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}$ on the right. The $i$ of the generator and the $-i$ of the chirality operator have been reduced by $i(-i)=+1$, which is why no imaginary unit stands in front; the surviving right multiplier is the product in that order, because right multipliers compose in reverse, $\vec e_z\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}=-\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}\vec e_z$. Its divergence is the classical identity for a massive free field,

$$
\sum_\mu\frac{\partial j_{5\mu}}{\partial x_\mu}
= 2m\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z) ,
$$

which vanishes exactly when $m=0$. The mass is the only explicit breaking, and the identity is exact at the classical level. The quantum correction to it — the axial anomaly, which adds a term even at $m=0$ — belongs to the companion articles; the *spontaneous* breaking that a condensate produces is a separate phenomenon from that anomaly.

**Why the sum carries no metric factor.** The metric is already spent inside the generators, since $G_0^{2}=+\mathrm{id}$ and $G_\eta^{2}=-\mathrm{id}$ differ in sign exactly as the entries of $g$ do. Hence $j_{5\eta}$ is the contravariant spatial component and $\sum_\mu\partial j_{5\mu}/\partial x_\mu=\partial j_5^0/\partial r'_t+\nabla\cdot\vec j_5$ is the divergence; an extra $\varepsilon$ would apply the metric a second time.

**The companion's notation.** There the identity reads $\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde J_{\Phi,5}\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde J_{\Phi,5})=2im\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\natural}\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$, the time slot of $\tilde J_{\Phi,5}$ carrying the factor $ic$ that the $i r'_t$ coordinate requires. The two notations name the same operator, and the same glyph: $\xi$ is the corpus's chirality operator, the frame's fifth basis operator, a pure right multiplier, and it takes the value $-1$ on $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ and $+1$ on $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ in either notation.

**Two symmetries, two currents.** For later use we record the vector and axial bilinears and their divergences:

$$
j_\mu = \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\bigl(i\,e_\mu\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\,\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}\bigr) , \qquad
j_{5\mu} = \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\bigl(e_\mu\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\,\vec e_z\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}\bigr) ,
$$

$$
\sum_\mu\frac{\partial j_\mu}{\partial x_\mu}=0 ,
\qquad
\sum_\mu\frac{\partial j_{5\mu}}{\partial x_\mu}
=2m\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z) .
$$

The scalar bilinear $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is the one that will acquire a vacuum expectation value.

## The Condensate as an Order Parameter

**The bilinear.** The scalar bilinear is

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} ,
$$

the **Dirac mass bilinear**. It is real, it is odd under the chirality operator, and it vanishes on an element of a single value, so it pairs the two values and never a value with itself. It carries no vector index, so it is a Lorentz scalar, and its behaviour under $\xi$ is the whole of what this article uses. It is the one pairing of the two values that the mass term multiplies. Its pseudoscalar partner is

$$
i\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z),
$$

written with the explicit factor of $i$ because $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is purely imaginary and $i$ times it is real.

**The rotation of the pair.** Under the axial transformation the scalar bilinear is not invariant:

$$
A:\ \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\ \longmapsto\ \cos(2\alpha)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}+\sin(2\alpha)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z) ,
$$

$$
\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z)\ \longmapsto\ \cos(2\alpha)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z)-\sin(2\alpha)\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} .
$$

The angle is $2\alpha$ and not $\alpha$: the rotation is applied once on each side of the pairing, and the two unit relations $\vec e_z\vec e_x\vec e_z=-\vec e_x$ and $\vec e_z\vec e_x=-\vec e_x\vec e_z$ turn the two factors into a doubling and cancel the cross term. The two bilinears therefore rotate into each other by the angle $2\alpha$: the pair carries the two-dimensional rotation of the axial group, exactly as a two-component order parameter should, and the invariant of the rotation is $(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^{2}+(i\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\xi\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^{2}$.

**The order parameter.** A **chiral condensate** is a nonzero vacuum expectation value

$$
\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle = -\Sigma^{3} \neq 0 ,
$$

where $\Sigma>0$ has the dimension of a mass, the cube being the dimension of the bilinear, and the sign is the one of the dynamics and of the convention for the mass term. Because the vacuum expectation is not invariant under the axial rotation above — it rotates into the pseudoscalar direction unless it vanishes — a nonzero condensate **spontaneously breaks** the axial symmetry: the Lagrangian is invariant, the vacuum is not, and the Goldstone modes of the broken generator follow. The condensate is the order parameter of the transition.

**The algebraic identity with the mass coupling.** The point the biquaternion framework makes is an identification, and it is clean. The condensate $\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$ is the vacuum expectation of precisely the bilinear that the linear chiral pair multiplies: the mass equation $\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} = k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$, $\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} = k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ states that the two values are coupled by the coefficient $m$, and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is that same coupling written as a pairing of the field. A dynamically generated mass is therefore the statement

$$
\boxed{\ m \;\longleftrightarrow\; -2G\,\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle\ }
$$

with $G$ the coupling of the four-fermion interaction that produces the condensate: the coefficient of the framework's linear chiral pair is the condensate, up to the coupling. This is not a new dynamics; it is the reason the framework's mass term is the right carrier for the condensate, and it is why the condensate is not a pairing built on the algebra's real structure $\flat$.

**Sector placement.** The scalar bilinear $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is real, and its vacuum expectation is a number. As a number the condensate is central by construction, and read back into the algebra through the trace form it sits on the real scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_t\subset\mathbb{B}_{C}$, the part of the centre that complex conjugation fixes as well. That placement is the algebraic content of two physical facts at once: the condensate is a Lorentz scalar, since it carries no vector index, and it is neutral under the vector central phase, since it is central. The chirality-odd property is the statement that it pairs the two values; the centrality is the statement that its value is a number. The two are consistent and neither is derived from the other.

**Why not the real structure.** The framework's antilinear real structure $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{\flat}=-\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*$ pairs a field with its own conjugate, and a coupling built on it has the signature of a Majorana-type term; the companion articles keep it strictly separate from the linear chiral pair. The condensate is a *chiral* pairing of two independent values, so it belongs to the linear pair and not to $\flat$. A reader who attempts to read the condensate through $\flat$ will obtain the wrong object: the fixed space of $\flat$ is the four-real-dimensional material sector $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, which is not the chiral-pair carrier. The distinction is the subject of the companion on antilinear structure, and the present article uses it without deriving it again.

## The Gap Equation and the Critical Coupling

**The model.** The standard mean-field model of a dynamical fermion mass is the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio (NJL) Lagrangian, a four-fermion interaction

$$
\mathcal{L} = \tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\,\mathcal{D}\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}
+ G\left[(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^{2}+\bigl(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z)\bigr)^{2}\right],
$$

in which $G$ has mass dimension $-2$. The interaction is chirally invariant: the combination is the invariant of the axial rotation noted above, because the pair of bilinears rotates as a two-vector, and the second term is the pseudoscalar partner with the unit $\vec e_z$ and no basis operator. The framework carries the same structure: the four-fermion interaction pairs the bilinears, and it does not need the algebra beyond the chiral pairing that the bilinears already are.

**The mean-field mass.** Linearising the interaction about the condensate, $(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^{2}\to2\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}+\ldots$, replaces the four-fermion term by a mass term $-k_m\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with

$$
m = -2G\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle ,
$$

and the condensate in turn is computed in the mean-field vacuum of a fermion of mass $m$. For $N_c$ colours and a sharp ultraviolet cutoff $\Lambda$ the self-consistency condition is the **gap equation**

$$
\boxed{\;m = 4N_c\,G\,m\,I_1(m)\;}
\qquad
I_1(m) = \int_0^{\Lambda}\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\vec p^2+m^2}} .
$$

The equation always has the **chiral** solution $m=0$. Dividing by $m$, a nontrivial solution requires $1 = 4N_cG\,I_1(m)$, and since $I_1$ decreases with $m$ the threshold is set at $m=0$:

$$
1 = 4N_c\,G\,I_1(0) = 4N_c\,G\,\frac{\Lambda^2}{4\pi^2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\boxed{\;G_c = \frac{\pi^2}{N_c\,\Lambda^2}\;} .
$$

For $G>G_c$ the chiral solution is unstable and the nontrivial one appears; the transition is second order in the mean-field approximation. Both the gap equation and the threshold are standard and are transcribed here.

**The condensate.** At the nontrivial solution the condensate is

$$
\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle = -2N_m\,c\,I_1(m)
\approx -\frac{N_m\,c\,\Lambda^2}{2\pi^2} ,
$$

where the second form is the leading term for $m\ll\Lambda$. At the values used below the leading form overestimates by about $30\%$, so the table quotes the exact integral. The numbers were recomputed directly from the integral, in units of GeV, for $N_c=3$ and $\Lambda=0.65$:

| quantity | value (recomputed) |
|---|---|
| $I_1(0)=\Lambda^2/(4\pi^2)$ | $1.070\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{GeV}^2$ |
| $G_c=\pi^2/(N_c\Lambda^2)$ | $7.79\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$ |
| $I_1(m)$ at $m=0.3$ | $8.33\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{GeV}^2$ |
| $1/(4N_cI_1(m))$ | $10.00\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$ |
| $\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$ at $m=0.3$ | $-1.50\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{GeV}^3 = -(247\ \mathrm{MeV})^3$ |

The fourth row is the coupling that makes $m=0.3$ a solution of the gap equation: it is $28\%$ above $G_c$, so a modest overshoot of the critical coupling sustains a constituent mass of that size. The last row is the point of the table: a constituent mass of order $300$ MeV with a cutoff of order $650$ MeV produces a condensate of order $-(250\ \mathrm{MeV})^3$, which is the accepted size of the quark condensate. The colour multiplicity $N_c=3$ is part of the transcribed model and not of the framework, which has no colour group; it enters here because it is what fixes the size in nature. Nothing in the computation is the framework's; it is the standard NJL mean field, reproduced to fix the size of the object the framework is naming.

**Which symmetry is broken, and where.** The nonzero condensate breaks $\mathrm{U}(1)_A$ for one flavour and $SU(N_f)_A$ for $N_f$ flavours, down to the vector subgroup $SU(N_f)_V$. The vector symmetry — the one the framework's central phase generates, and the one the linear chiral pair preserves — is *not* broken. This is the exact pattern the biquaternion mass term produces: the mass conserves the central phase and breaks the axial one, and the condensate does the same spontaneously.

## The Effective Potential and the Physical Mass Shell

**The mass shell.** The coefficient the condensate supplies is a genuine mass, and the framework shows it in two lines. Apply the conjugate gradient to the first member of the linear chiral pair and use the second:

$$
\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\bigl(\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}\bigr)
=\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\bigl(k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}\bigr)
=k_m\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}
=m\,(k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R})=k_m^{2}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} .
$$

Since $\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{\nabla}=\Box$, the right-handed field satisfies

$$
\boxed{\;\Box\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} = k_m^{2}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R} ,
\qquad\text{and, symmetrically,}\qquad
\Box\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L} = k_m^{2}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}\;}.
$$

The dynamical coefficient is therefore a common mass for the two values: the off-diagonal coupling has rotated into the mass-shell condition of each half. This is the framework's positive statement that a condensate-generated $m$ is a physical mass and not a bookkeeping device, and it uses no more than the factorisation of the d'Alembertian on which the linear mass pair rests.

**The effective potential.** The mean-field energy of the condensate is the standard one. Writing $\sigma=-2G\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$ for the order parameter, which equals $m$ at the minimum, the effective potential at zero temperature is

$$
V(\sigma) \;=\; \frac{\sigma^{2}}{4G} \;-\; 2N_c\int^{\Lambda}\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3}\,\sqrt{\vec p^{2}+\sigma^{2}} ,
$$

where the normalization of the first term is tied to the $(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})^{2}$ coupling convention used above. Its stationarity condition is

$$
\frac{dV}{d\sigma} = \frac{\sigma}{2G} - 2N_c\,\sigma\,I_1(\sigma) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
1 = 4N_cG\,I_1(\sigma) ,
$$

which at $\sigma=m$ is exactly the gap equation, so the two descriptions — self-consistent mass and minimised energy — are the same statement. The symmetric point $m=0$ is a local minimum for $G<G_c$ and a maximum for $G>G_c$, which is what makes the transition second order in this approximation; $G_c=\pi^2/(N_c\Lambda^2)$ is where the curvature at the origin changes sign. The curvature about the nontrivial minimum in the radial (sigma) direction is the mass of the scalar partner of the condensate; in the angular (Goldstone) directions it vanishes before explicit breaking, which is the effective-potential face of the counting in the next section. All of this is standard; it is transcribed because it is what the phrase "the condensate's value" means, and because it exhibits the framework's identification $m\leftrightarrow\sigma$ as a stationarity condition rather than a definition.

**The scale.** The condensate and $G_c$ depend on the cutoff $\Lambda$; only combinations fixed by the renormalised dynamics are physical. The framework has no dynamics from which to fix the combination, so the table of the previous section is a demonstration of size, not a prediction.

## The Biquaternion Reading of the Condensate

Four statements, in decreasing order of strength.

- **The condensate is the coefficient of the framework's mass.** The linear chiral pair couples $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ with the coefficient $m$; the bilinear $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is that coupling's expectation; and the mean-field relation $m=-2G\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$ makes the identification exact at the level of the equation. The framework does not merely host the condensate; the off-diagonal mass coupling *is* the object whose coefficient the condensate supplies.
- **The broken symmetry is the axial one, and the framework's mass breaks it explicitly.** The central phase passes through the linear chiral pair and is conserved; the axial phase does not and is broken. A condensate reproduces the same breaking pattern spontaneously, and the axial current identity $\sum_\mu\partial j_{5\mu}/\partial x_\mu=2m\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z)$ is the framework's statement of it. Neither the identity nor the pattern is special to the biquaternion algebra; what the algebra supplies is the sector placement of the bilinears — the scalar condensate on the real central line, its pseudoscalar partner on the imaginary one — and the identification of the condensate with the central scalar coefficient.
- **The order parameter is a central scalar.** As an algebra element the condensate sits on the real scalar direction $\mathbb{R}e_t\subset\mathbb{B}_{C}$. That is the framework's way of saying it is a Lorentz scalar and vector-neutral, and it is exact.
- **The second kind of mass is not involved.** The condensate is a chiral pairing, so it is linear and built on the chiral off-diagonal coupling; it is not the antilinear self-pairing built on $\flat$. The framework's two-mass distinction, established in the companion on antilinear structure, is what keeps the two from being conflated here.

**What is genuinely missing.** The framework has no colour group, no confinement, and no strong-coupling dynamics. The compact gauge algebra inside $\mathbb{B}$ is at most $\mathrm{U}(2)$ of real dimension four; every complex representation of $\mathbb{B}$ has even dimension, so no three-dimensional colour representation exists; and the strong-interaction agenda records that $SU(3)$ is an obstacle with no route. A condensate of the NJL type can therefore be *written down* in the framework — its bilinears and its symmetry pattern are the framework's — but it cannot be *derived* from a framework interaction, because the interaction that produces it in nature is the colour gauge theory the framework does not contain. The four-fermion coupling $G$ is an effective parameter in the framework in exactly the way it is in NJL.

## Goldstone Counting, and Where the Pion Lives

**The counting.** When a continuous symmetry group $G_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is spontaneously broken to a subgroup $H$, the number of Goldstone modes is $\dim G_{\mathrm{ch}}-\dim H$. For $N_f$ massless flavours the chiral symmetry is $G_{\mathrm{ch}}=SU(N_f)_L\times SU(N_f)_R$ of real dimension $2(N_f^2-1)$, broken to the diagonal vector subgroup $H=SU(N_f)_V$ of dimension $N_f^2-1$. Hence

$$
N_{\mathrm{Goldstone}} = 2(N_f^2-1)-(N_f^2-1)=N_f^2-1 ,
$$

which is $3$ for $N_f=2$ (the pions) and $8$ for $N_f=3$ (the octet). The Goldstone modes are pseudoscalars: they are the rotations of the pseudoscalar partner $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z)$ by the broken axial generators, and the counting is standard.

**Why the count is $N_f^2-1$ and not $N_f^2$.** The axial abelian factor $\mathrm{U}(1)_A$ is broken by the same condensate, so a naive count would add one mode and give $N_f^2$. The anomaly breaks $\mathrm{U}(1)_A$ explicitly, the would-be Goldstone boson of that factor is the heavy $\eta'$, and it is therefore absent from the light spectrum; the correct count is the one of the semisimple part, $N_f^2-1$. The Goldstone theorem itself is standard, and the framework transcribes it in the same way it transcribes the gap equation.

**Where the pion is treated.** The Goldstone modes of this breaking are spin-$0$ objects, and their effective description — the chiral Lagrangian, the nonlinear sigma model, the pion, and the relation of the Goldstone decay constant to the condensate — belongs to the spin-$0$ subcategory of this series and is not developed here. This article stops where the fermion sector stops: the order parameter, the broken symmetry, and the identification of the condensate with the coefficient of the linear chiral pair. The Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation, which ties the pion mass to the explicit quark masses and the condensate, is likewise a spin-$0$ statement, listed in Further Reading and not used here.

**The explicit breaking.** With a nonzero bare quark mass $m_q$ the axial symmetry is broken explicitly as well as spontaneously, the Goldstone modes acquire a mass, and the condensate tilts. The framework's linear chiral pair carries the explicit breaking by construction — its coefficient is the mass — so the explicit and spontaneous breakings act on the same coupling. This is the structural reason the two are naturally discussed together, and it is why the condensate, once present, makes the mass term self-consistent rather than merely added.

## What the Framework Supplies and What It Does Not Supply

**Supplied.** The linear chiral pair, and with it the carrier of the condensate; the chirality operator and the two ways it reads, a commutation on the algebra and an anticommutation on the two-sided equation; the chirality-odd scalar bilinear and its rotation into the pseudoscalar partner; the axial current identity and the pattern of preserved vector and broken axial symmetry; the centrality of the order parameter's value; and the algebraic identification $m\leftrightarrow-2G\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$. The framework's mass term is not merely compatible with a condensate; it is the term whose coefficient a condensate fixes, and the companion on antilinear structure explains why the condensate is not to be sought in the real structure $\flat$.

**Transcribed.** The NJL model, the mean-field gap equation, the critical coupling $G_c$, the condensate's size, the Goldstone counting, and the Goldstone theorem. These are standard and are used here in the framework's notation.

**Not supplied.** The colour gauge dynamics that produces the condensate in nature; the value of the coupling $G$; the scale $\Lambda$; and therefore the numerical value of $\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$ and of the constituent mass. The framework is scale-free until these are supplied, exactly as the companion articles record for the masses elsewhere. No biquaternion prediction distinguishes this account from the standard one.

## The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article *Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices*. The generators carry no frame label: $\gamma_\mu$ is one set of matrices, $\gamma_0^2=I_4$ and $\gamma_\eta^2=-I_4$. The frame belongs to the coordinate, not to the array.

The gammas are a frame of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, not a different algebra.

Here $L_a$ and $R_b$ are the left and right multiplications, $L_a\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=a\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and $R_b\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} b$, and $c(M)$ is the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products.

$$
\gamma_0=iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_x},\quad \gamma_1=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_x}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_2=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_3=iL_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_5=-iL_{\mathrm{pau},e_t}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z} .
$$

The same in the axis-free display:

$$
\gamma_0=iL_{\mathrm{axf},e_t}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x},\quad \gamma_1=iL_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_2=iL_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_3=iL_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_y},\quad \gamma_5=-iL_{\mathrm{axf},e_t}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z} .
$$

A single product is a pure tensor; the products form the seven-dimensional cone $\operatorname{rank}c(M)\le1$, and a general element is a sum of at most four, with $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$.

$\gamma_5$ is the reference-frame basis operator of the chirality operator $\xi=-iR_{\vec e_z}$; it is the same operator in this display, and only its array changes, because $\xi=i\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$ is a product of the four generators and the display does not touch the side.

The dictionary is complex-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the basis operators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

**The body's actions under their gamma names.** The generator actions of the body are the gammas, $G_\mu=\gamma_\mu$, and the chirality basis operator is $\gamma_5$ itself, $\xi=\gamma_5=-iR_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z}$. The single collapse behind every simplification in the body is

$$
i\,\gamma_\mu=-L_{\mathrm{pau},e_\mu}R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}} ,
\qquad
i\,\gamma_5=R_{\mathrm{pau},\vec e_z} ,
$$

In the axis-free display:

$$
i\,\gamma_\mu=-L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}} ,
\qquad
i\,\gamma_5=R_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z} ,
$$

so multiplying a generator or the chirality basis operator by $i$ trades it for a plain basis operator; four generators therefore carry $i^{4}=+1$ and the fifth basis operator is $\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3=\omega=-i\gamma_5$.

**The identities the article cites.** In the $\gamma$ reading, with $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$,
$$
\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}I_4,\qquad
(\gamma_\eta)^2=-I_4,\qquad
\gamma_5^2=I_4,\qquad
\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_5\}=0,
$$
$$
[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu]=2\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\quad(\mu\ne\nu),\qquad
[\gamma_a,S_{cd}]=\varepsilon_a\delta_{ac}\gamma_d-\varepsilon_a\delta_{ad}\gamma_c,\qquad
S_{cd}=\tfrac14[\gamma_c,\gamma_d],
$$
$$
\mathrm{tr}(\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu)=4\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu},\qquad
\mathrm{tr}(\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\gamma_\rho\gamma_\sigma)=-4i\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},
$$
$$
\mathrm{tr}(\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\gamma_\rho\gamma_\sigma)
=4\bigl(\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}\varepsilon_\rho\delta_{\rho\sigma}-\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\rho}\varepsilon_\nu\delta_{\nu\sigma}+\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\sigma}\varepsilon_\nu\delta_{\nu\rho}\bigr).
$$
The four contraction sums are fixed by the metric, because a basis operator's square is. With the summed index raised, $\gamma^\mu=-\varepsilon_\mu\gamma_\mu$, they are $-4I_4$, $+2\gamma_\alpha$, $-4\varepsilon_\alpha\delta_{\alpha\beta}I_4$ and $-2\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma_\delta$. With every index low only the first keeps a closed form, $\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu\gamma_\mu=-2I_4$; the other three are index-dependent and are computed, not transcribed.

**The display.** Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. The two are carried into one another by $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}=C_{\Pi}L_{\mathrm{pau},e_\mu}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and a conjugation by $C_{\Pi}$ changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant, so every identity of the section holds in both. An array is a statement about one display; a basis operator named in the running text keeps the Peirce label, and its axis-free array is the conjugate. The label names the array and not the operator.

## Summary

The chiral condensate in biquaternionic form is the vacuum expectation of the chirality-odd scalar bilinear,

$$
\boxed{\;\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle\;} ,
$$

and it is the order parameter of the axial symmetry that the framework's **linear chiral mass pair** $\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$, $\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}=k_m\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ breaks. The two writings are kept apart throughout: the pair is the one-sided statement in the algebra, and the two-sided equation $(\mathcal{D}-k_m)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=0$ with $\mathcal{D}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=-\sum_\mu e_\mu(\partial_\mu\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}})\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}$ is the writing in which the bilinears are read. Chirality is a **side** and not a sign carried by an element: $\xi=-iR_{\vec e_z}$ is the frame's fifth basis operator and multiplies on the right, the gradient multiplies on the left, and the mass is the one term of the equation with both slots and therefore no side. The massless two-sided equation carries both the vector and the axial phase, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\mapsto\exp(i\alpha)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\mapsto\exp(i\alpha\xi)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\exp(\alpha\vec e_z)$; the mass term preserves the central one and breaks the axial one, with $\sum_\mu\partial j_{5\mu}/\partial x_\mu=2m\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}(\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\vec e_z)$. A nonzero condensate reproduces exactly this breaking, spontaneously, and its value is the coefficient of the same linear chiral pair: $m=-2G\langle\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}^*iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle$. The order parameter sits on the real central line, and it is not the antilinear pairing built on the algebra's real structure $\flat$.

The standard mean-field account was verified. With $N_c$ colours and cutoff $\Lambda$ the gap equation is $1=4N_cG\,I_1(m)$, the critical coupling is $G_c=\pi^2/(N_c\Lambda^2)$, and for $N_c=3$, $\Lambda=0.65$ GeV, and a constituent mass $m=0.3$ GeV the condensate is $-(247\ \mathrm{MeV})^3$ — the accepted order of magnitude. The Goldstone count is $N_f^2-1$, giving three pions for $N_f=2$ and eight modes for $N_f=3$, the ninth would-be mode being the $\eta'$, which the anomaly keeps heavy; the spin-$0$ effective description of those modes belongs to another subcategory and is not developed here.

The framework supplies the carrier, the symmetry pattern and the sector placement. It supplies no dynamics: a QCD-like condensate cannot be derived in it, and the scale is empirical input.

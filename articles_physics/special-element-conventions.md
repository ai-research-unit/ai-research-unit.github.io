# __Special Element Conventions__

## Introduction

These are the conventions of the central map and of the special elements. The central map is the restriction of multiplication by the scalar imaginary $i$ to the remarkable subspaces, and the series reads it in four places under four names. The special glyphs — root, idempotent, nilpotent, chirality, spin and the two spinors — are notational, and the article fixes what each glyph promises about the object that carries it.

The article assumes the base and the subspaces of *Algebra and Element Conventions* and the norms of *Norm and Pairing Conventions*, where a class is defined by a norm.

## The Central Map and Its Restrictions

The scalar imaginary $i$ is central, so multiplication by it is **one** $\mathbb{C}$-linear map of the algebra, and the movements the series reads in four places are its restrictions to the subspaces. The generator satisfies $(ie_t)^2=-e_t$, so its powers close on four values and the group $\exp(i\theta)e_t$ of the centre interpolates between them. The remarkable subspaces carry the restrictions.

| Subspace | $i\,\cdot$ | The name |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{B}_{C}$, the complex time sector | Itself | The global phase |
| $\mathbb{B}_{V}$, the complex space sector | Itself | The duality rotation |
| $\mathbb{B}_{H}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{iH}$ | Each other | The Wick rotation |
| $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ | Each other | The material–informational exchange |

The rule that ties the names is a rule about the generator and not about the subspaces: the map is central, so it acts the same way in every subspace, and it is a scalar that acts through the centre and never through the quaternion directions; a non-central generator would give one rotation per carrier. It is a rotation and not a projection, a collapse or a measurement — a loss of information is a non-invertible map and belongs to *The Operators: One-Sided, Two-Sided, Sandwich*.

**The relation to the Wick rotation.** The coordinate continuation $r'_t \mapsto -i\tau$ of *Interpretation of the Wick Rotation in the Biquaternion Universe*, and the two senses in which the series uses the phrase *Wick rotation* — the exchange of the real and imaginary sectors, on the whole element, and the continuation of the time coordinate, a relabeling of one coordinate — are set out there. The two share one generator and one temporal sign flip, and they are not the same map.

The phase gate of the gate articles is a rotation about $\vec e_z$, a non-central generator, and it must not be identified with the central rotation.

## The Special Elements: Root, Idempotent and Nilpotent

**The special glyphs are notational.** A root $\tilde{Ζ}$, an idempotent $\tilde\Pi$ or a nilpotent $\tilde\nu$ is not a different kind of object from a generic $\tilde Q$: the glyph records what the element is known to be, at the point of use.

### The Root Convention

The roots carry a symbol of their own, beside the idempotent $\tilde{\Pi}$ (§*The Idempotent Convention*) and the nilpotent $\tilde{\nu}$ (§*The Nilpotent Convention*): the **root glyph**, in the two cases.

A **root of $1$** of the algebra is an element $\tilde{Ζ}$ with $\tilde{Ζ}^2 = 1$. More generally a **root of a central value** $\lambda$ is an element with $\tilde{Ζ}^2 = \lambda e_t$. The roots of the three central values $-1, 0, +1$ are those of *Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the General Plain Algebra*, and the general root is that of *Square Roots of an Element*; the roots the series uses are the roots of $1$.

**The square is read in a named product**, the algebra product unless the statement names another, as for the nilpotents (§*The Nilpotent Convention*), and the class is relative to the product; the roots of a value in the four general products are those of *Square Roots of a General Element in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*.

**The case rule splits the glyph, as it does for the elements, the idempotents and the nilpotents.** The roots of the biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers carry the upper-case tilde, $\tilde{Ζ}$; those of the quaternion and split-quaternion layers carry the lower-case tilde, $\tilde{\zeta}$; those of the complex, split-complex and dual layers carry the plain capital, $Ζ$; and those of the reduced biquaternion layer carry the same plain capital, $Ζ$, no tilde being written where there is no quaternionic factor. The layer fixes the glyph.

**The coordinates carry the letter of the root**: in $\mathbb{B}$ the root is $\tilde{Ζ} = Ζ_te_t + Ζ_x\vec e_x + Ζ_y\vec e_y + Ζ_z\vec e_z$ with $Ζ_\mu = \zeta_\mu + i\zeta'_\mu$, its scalar part $Ζ_t$ and its vector part $\vec{Ζ} = \vec\zeta + i\vec\zeta'$; in $\mathbb{H}$ it is $\tilde\zeta = \zeta_te_t + \zeta_x\vec e_x + \zeta_y\vec e_y + \zeta_z\vec e_z$ with real $\zeta_\mu$.

**It is allowed to add subscripts when several roots are used.** **The subscript of a root is numeric when it counts and a role label when it names.** Where more than one occurs the roots are numbered, $\tilde{Ζ}_1, \tilde{Ζ}_2$, as for the nilpotents and for the idempotent pairs other than the complete sign pair of §*The Idempotent Convention*; where a root plays a named role, it carries the role in upright roman as its subscript. A sign is a subscript where the statement needs one.

**The adjoined generator of the double carries the root glyph.** The double $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{R}}$ carries a central root of $-1$ outside $\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{R}}$; it is written $\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$, the biquaternion root glyph with the role subscript $\mathrm{ad}$ of the paragraph above. It is a root of the double, not an element of $\mathbb{B}$. The chirality is the cell $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}$ of §*The Chirality Convention*; its central form is $\chi=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ with $\chi^2=e_t$. No other letter is used for it.

The root glyph is therefore **split by the case rule**, and the split is the reason the convention is stated:

| Symbol | Role |
|---|---|
| $Ζ$ | A root of $1$ of the complex, split-complex or dual layer, where no tilde is written |
| $Ζ_\pm$ | A root of $1$ of the reduced biquaternion layer, where no tilde is written |
| $\tilde{\zeta}$ | A root of $1$ of the quaternion or split-quaternion layer |
| $\tilde{Ζ}$ | A root of $1$ of the biquaternion or split-biquaternion layer |

### The Idempotent Convention

The convention of *The Elements and their Coefficients* fixes the case and the tilde of a generic element. The idempotents carry a symbol of their own, and the series uses it throughout.

An **idempotent** of the algebra is an element $\tilde{\Pi}$ with $\tilde{\Pi}^2 = \tilde{\Pi}$. An idempotent fixed by the star, $\tilde{\Pi}^{*} = \tilde{\Pi}$, is a **Hermitian idempotent**, and the rank-one Hermitian idempotents of $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ are the **pure states** of the informational sector.

**The case rule splits the glyph, as it does in the mathematical corpus.** The idempotents of the biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers carry the upper-case tilde, $\tilde{\Pi}$; those of the quaternion and split-quaternion layers carry the lower-case tilde, $\tilde{\pi}$; those of the complex and dual layers carry the plain capital, $\Pi$, no tilde being written where there is no quaternionic factor; those of the split-complex and reduced biquaternion layers write the complete pair with the signs, $\Pi_\pm$. The series works in $\mathbb{B}$ and writes $\tilde{\Pi}$ throughout; the other glyphs are the mathematical corpus's, and the layer fixes the glyph.

A non-zero idempotent $\tilde{\Pi}$ generates the **minimal left ideal** $\mathbb{B}\tilde{\Pi}$, which is the state module of the companion articles; the standard pair, the classification of the idempotents, the polarisation identity, the Peirce decomposition, the matrix units and the projective geometry of the pure states are those of *Idempotents* and its companions in the mathematical corpus.

**The coordinates carry the letter of the idempotent**: in $\mathbb{B}$ the idempotent is $\tilde{\Pi} = \Pi_te_t + \Pi_x\vec e_x + \Pi_y\vec e_y + \Pi_z\vec e_z$ with $\Pi_\mu = \pi_\mu + i\pi'_\mu$, its scalar part $\Pi_t$ and its vector part $\vec\Pi = \vec\pi + i\vec\pi'$; in $\mathbb{H}$ it is $\tilde\pi = \pi_te_t + \pi_x\vec e_x + \pi_y\vec e_y + \pi_z\vec e_z$ with real $\pi_\mu$.

**It is allowed to add subscripts when several idempotents are used.** **The subscript of an idempotent is numeric when it counts and a role label when it names, but for the two members of a complete sign pair.** A pair is $\tilde{\Pi}_1, \tilde{\Pi}_2$; a second pair in the same statement takes the next numbers, $\tilde{\Pi}_3, \tilde{\Pi}_4$; and a family indexed by a direction is written with its argument, $\tilde{\Pi}_{1,2}(\vec u)$. When a pair is **complete**, $\tilde{\Pi}_+ + \tilde{\Pi}_- = e_t$, its two members may be written $\tilde{\Pi}_+$ and $\tilde{\Pi}_-$: the subscript $+$ names the member attached to $+1$ and the subscript $-$ the member attached to $-1$. A pair that is not complete may be numbered, labelled or signed as the statement requires. A role label is written in upright roman, as for a unit element.

The idempotent glyph is therefore **split by the case rule**, and the split is the reason the convention is stated:

| Symbol | Role |
|---|---|
| $\Pi$ | An idempotent of the complex or dual layer, where no tilde is written |
| $\Pi_\pm$ | A complete sign pair of idempotents of the split-complex or reduced biquaternion layer, where no tilde is written |
| $\tilde{\pi}$ | An idempotent of the quaternion or split-quaternion layer, in the mathematical corpus |
| $\tilde{\Pi}$ | An idempotent or a pure state of the biquaternion or split-biquaternion layer, and the minimal left ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde{\Pi}$ it generates |

### The Nilpotent Convention

The nilpotents carry a symbol of their own, beside the idempotent $\tilde{\Pi}$ (§*The Idempotent Convention*): the lower-case $\tilde{\nu}$ of the quaternion and split-quaternion layers and the upper-case $\tilde{\Upsilon}$ of the biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers. **The capital is an upsilon and not a $Y$.**

A **nilpotent** of the algebra is an element $\tilde{\nu}$ whose square vanishes,

$$
\tilde{\nu}^2 = 0 .
$$

**The square is read in a named product**, the algebra product unless the statement names another, as in the mathematical corpus (*Special Element and Peirce Conventions*, §*The Nilpotent Convention*), and the class is relative to the product. A nilpotent is never a Hermitian idempotent, a Hermitian nilpotent being zero, and it is never a state; the nilpotent is the partner of the idempotent in a minimal left ideal, $\mathbb{B}\vec{\Upsilon}_2 = \mathbb{B}\tilde{\Pi}_1$.

**The coordinates carry the letter of the nilpotent**: in $\mathbb{B}$ the nilpotent is $\tilde{\Upsilon} = \Upsilon_te_t + \Upsilon_x\vec e_x + \Upsilon_y\vec e_y + \Upsilon_z\vec e_z$ with $\Upsilon_\mu = \nu_\mu + i\nu'_\mu$, its scalar part $\Upsilon_t$ and its vector part $\vec\Upsilon = \vec\nu + i\vec\nu'$; in $\mathbb{H}$ it is $\tilde\nu = \nu_te_t + \nu_x\vec e_x + \nu_y\vec e_y + \nu_z\vec e_z$ with real $\nu_\mu$.

**It is allowed to add subscripts when several nilpotents are used.** **The subscript of a nilpotent is numeric when it counts and a role label when it names**, as for the idempotents apart from their complete sign pair: where more than one occurs a pair is $\tilde{\nu}_1, \tilde{\nu}_2$, a second pair in the same statement takes the next numbers, and where a nilpotent plays a named role it carries the role in upright roman as its subscript. A sign is a subscript where the statement needs one.

The nilpotents have no classification of the idempotent kind and no sphere of pure states; which set the square-zero elements form depends on the product. The nilpotents of a state module are those of *The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent*, and the spin-down state of *Spin-1/2 Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form* is a nilpotent of the ideal of that vacuum.

The nilpotent glyph is therefore **split by the case rule**, and the split is the reason the convention is stated:

| Symbol | Role |
|---|---|
| $\Upsilon$ | A nilpotent of the complex, split-complex or dual layer, where no tilde is written |
| $\tilde{\nu}$ | A nilpotent of the quaternion or split-quaternion layer |
| $\tilde{\Upsilon}$ | A nilpotent of the biquaternion or split-biquaternion layer, and the minimal left ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde{\Upsilon}$ it generates |
| $\vec{\Upsilon}$, $\vec{\nu}$ | A pure nilpotent, written with the arrow and without the tilde by the priority rule of *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Centre and the Vector Subspace* |

The standard pair of nilpotents is $\vec{\Upsilon}_1$ and $\vec{\Upsilon}_2$, the off-diagonal Peirce units of *Peirce Decompositions of the Biquaternion Algebra* exchanged by the star. The truncated ladder of *The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent* and of the Fock, vacuum and matrix-representation articles is this pair: those articles write it $\vec{\Upsilon}_1$ and $\vec{\Upsilon}_2$ wherever the algebra element is meant, and keep the ladder letters for the abstract operators.

**The standard pair carries the arrow, not the tilde.** The two Peirce units are pure, $\vec{\Upsilon}_1 = \tfrac12(i\vec e_x - \vec e_y)$ and $\vec{\Upsilon}_2 = \tfrac12(i\vec e_x + \vec e_y)$, with no scalar part, so by *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Centre and the Vector Subspace* the arrow takes priority over the tilde and the tilde is dropped; the pair is written $\vec{\Upsilon}_1, \vec{\Upsilon}_2$ and never $\tilde{\Upsilon}_1, \tilde{\Upsilon}_2$. The tilde is kept for the general nilpotent $\tilde{\Upsilon}$, which may carry a scalar part and is not pure.

### The Chirality Convention

**The word, on an operator.** Operator chirality is the side on which an operator multiplies: the left slot for a pure left multiplier, $c\,L_{\tilde Q}$; the right slot for a pure right multiplier, $c\,R_{\tilde P}$; both slots for a cell whose two multipliers are both occupied. The label is read from the cell and from nothing else: no root, no idempotent, no ideal, no spinor and no Clifford relation enters it. The vocabulary is *Operator Family Conventions*, §*The Slot Label, Which Is the Chirality*, and the properties of the two one-sided families and of the label are *Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra*, both in the mathematical corpus.

**The chirality cell of a frame.** The chirality operator of a frame is its volume cell, the product of the four frame cells with the phase $i$,
$$
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}=i\,\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3=-iL_{e_t}R_{\vec e_z}=-iR_{\vec e_z},
$$
a pure right multiplier of the axis-free frame, where the four gammas are that frame's cells in the gamma display. The cell is not an element of the algebra, and the phrase *the chirality is the cell* is the whole of the convention: the sides are the slots of an operator, and no element carries them by itself. The cell is fixed in *Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra* and its gamma arrays in *Matrix Representations Conventions*, §*The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation*. The glyph carries the label $\mathrm{vol}$ low, $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}$, and no index: the cell is one operator of a frame, and the frame is named where the operator acts. The gamma display prints the same cell as $\gamma_5$, whose index and bracket belong to that display and to no other.

**The subscript on an element.** The cell acts on an element by $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}\tilde Q=-i\tilde Q\vec e_z$, of spectrum $+1,+1,-1,-1$, so the subscript of the element takes the two values $L$ and $R$: $\tilde Q_L$ is the $-1$ part and $\tilde Q_R$ the $+1$ part, in the letters of the Dirac literature. A product takes the letter of its right factor, left multiplication keeps the letter, and the subscript is a function of the element and of the declared frame. The rule, with the three uses of the two letters, is *Matrix Representations Conventions*, §*The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation*.

**What the word does not name.** The central form of the chirality is the sign of the double, fixed in §*The Root Convention*; it is a different object, and it is represented by this cell on the Dirac module. A Peirce sign is a third object: $R_{\mathrm{axf},i\vec e_z}=iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z}$ is a copy index of the two ideals and not a chirality, and the single axis on which its projector agrees with a chiral projector is recorded as a coincidence in *Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra*, §*What Operator Chirality Is Not*.

### The Spin Convention

**Spin is the intrinsic angular momentum of a particle.** In the rest frame the one-particle states satisfy $\tilde S^2=\frac{m^{2}\,c^{2}\,s\,(s+1)}{k_m^{2}}$ and $\tilde S_z=\frac{m\,c\,s_{z}}{k_m}$, with $s$ an integer or half-integer and $s_z=-s,\dots,s$; relativistically $s$ labels the little-group $SU(2)$ representation, and for a massless particle the label is helicity. The algebra supplies the rotation action and the weights of its generator on a carrier, but neither the rest-frame one-particle space nor the unit $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$: those are physical inputs. A classical vector is a **spin vector**, or intrinsic angular momentum, and the continuous $2+1$ parameter is a **statistics parameter**; neither is a second definition.

**The algebraic definition, on a carrier.** Let $(W,\rho)$ be an irreducible unitary carrier of the rotation algebra generated by $\tilde S_k=\frac{m\,c}{2\,k_m}i\vec e_k$. For a unit $\vec u$ the operator $\rho(\vec u\cdot\vec S)$ is self-adjoint and its spectrum does not depend on $\vec u$, so the carrier has one spin and not one per axis:
$$
s=\frac{k_m\,\max}{m\,c}\operatorname{spec}\bigl(\rho(\vec u\cdot\vec S)\bigr)\in\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0},
$$
a scalar label and not a vector. The two sentences agree on every carrier the corpus uses, and the algebraic one is the sentence an article computes with (*Angular Momentum and Spin in the Biquaternion Algebra*, §*The Casimir and the Definition of Spin*, in the mathematical corpus). The label is $s$ and the projection $s_z$. The half-angle is the exponential coordinate of the rotor, the Peirce index is a copy label, and the chirality is the cell of §*The Chirality Convention*, whose central form is the sign $\chi=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ of the complexification.

### The Spinor Convention

**Definition (elementary spinor).** An **elementary spinor** is an element of a complex two-dimensional space on which the left multiplications act irreducibly with weights $+\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac12$, so that a rotation through $\theta$ about $\vec u$ multiplies it by $\exp(-\frac{k_m\,i\,\theta\,\vec u\,\vec S}{m\,c})$. In $\mathbb{B}$ the carrier is a minimal left ideal,
$$
S_\pm(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_\pm(\vec u),\qquad \tilde\Pi_\pm(\vec u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\pm i\vec u\bigr),
$$
with $\vec u$ a unit real vector; each is of complex dimension two and of spin $\tfrac12$, and the axis $\vec u$ is part of the datum. An element of $S_+(\vec u)$ is $d_0\tilde\Pi_+(\vec u)+d_1\nu(\vec u)$ with $d_0,d_1\in\mathbb{C}$, so an elementary spinor is a pair of complex numbers in a chosen carrier.

**Definition (Dirac spinor).** A **Dirac spinor** is an element $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}+\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ of $\mathbb{B}$, the two parts being its two components in the decomposition of $\mathbb{B}$ into minimal left ideals,
$$
\mathbb{B}=S_+(\vec u)\oplus S_-(\vec u),\qquad \tilde\Pi_\pm(\vec u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\pm i\vec u\bigr),
$$
for a declared unit imaginary direction $\vec u$, and each part is an elementary spinor of complex dimension two. Every element of the algebra is a Dirac spinor and only the zero element has both parts zero, so the definition costs one declared axis and nothing else.

**The two parts carry the chirality letters.** The decomposition is the decomposition into the two values of the chirality: the cell $-iR_{\vec u}$ of §*The Chirality Convention* is $-1$ on $S_+(\vec u)$ and $+1$ on $S_-(\vec u)$, so $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}\in S_+(\vec u)$ is the $L$ part and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}\in S_-(\vec u)$ the $R$ part, and at $\vec u=\vec e_z$ the cell is $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}=-iR_{\vec e_z}$. Exchanging the two letters is the substitution $\vec u\mapsto-\vec u$, because $\tilde\Pi_+(\vec u)=\tilde\Pi_-(-\vec u)$: the pair is unordered, and the definition orders it only to name the two parts.

**A column is a writing, not a definition.** A column of two components is an elementary spinor written in a chosen carrier, and a column of four is a Dirac spinor written in a matrix model, one pair per part. The count $4=2\times2$ multiplies the spin dimension by the number of parts, and the two factors have different origins. Neither column is a second definition, and the definitions need no Clifford relation, no gamma cell and no extra rotation triple.

## Summary

Multiplication by $i$ is one map. On the centre it is the global phase; on the complex space sector it is the duality rotation; on the real and imaginary halves it is the Wick rotation, and on the two sectors it is the material–informational exchange. The generator satisfies $(ie_t)^2=-e_t$, and the group $\exp(i\theta)e_t$ of the centre interpolates between the four values. The map is a rotation and not a projection, a collapse or a measurement, and it acts through the centre rather than through the quaternion directions.

The special glyphs are notational: a root $\tilde{Ζ}$, an idempotent $\tilde\Pi$ and a nilpotent $\tilde\nu$ or $\tilde\Upsilon$ are generic elements whose glyph records what they are known to be. A root is a root in the product the statement names; an idempotent squares to itself; a nilpotent squares to zero. The case rule applies to each, the coordinates carry the letter of the glyph, and several occurrences take subscripts by the rule of *Unit, Index and Notation Conventions*. The standard Peirce pair carries the arrow and not the tilde, because its members are pure.

The chirality is the cell of a frame and not an element of the algebra. Operator chirality is the side on which an operator multiplies: the left slot, the right slot, or both. The chirality operator of a frame is its volume cell, $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}=-iR_{\vec e_z}$, a pure right multiplier of the axis-free frame, $i$ times the product of the four cells of the frame, of spectrum $+1,+1,-1,-1$ on the elements. The subscript of an element takes the two values $L$ and $R$, read from that cell and from the declared frame. The central form of the chirality is the sign of the double, and a Peirce sign is neither the cell nor its central form.

Spin is defined once, on a carrier: it is the largest eigenvalue of $\vec u\cdot\vec S$ in units of $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$, a scalar in $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$ that does not depend on the axis, and physically it is the intrinsic angular momentum of a particle, with $\tilde S^2=\frac{m^{2}\,c^{2}\,s\,(s+1)}{k_m^{2}}$, $\tilde S_z=\frac{m\,c\,s_{z}}{k_m}$ and helicity in the massless case. The algebra supplies the rotation action and the weights of its generator; it supplies neither the one-particle space nor the unit $\frac{m\,c}{k_m}$.

An **elementary spinor** is an element of a carrier, a minimal left ideal $S_\pm(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_\pm(\vec u)$ of complex dimension two with weights $\pm\tfrac12$; a **Dirac spinor** is an element $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}+\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ of $\mathbb{B}$, its two parts being the components in $\mathbb{B}=S_+(\vec u)\oplus S_-(\vec u)$ for a declared axis $\vec u$. The two parts are the two values of the chirality, $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},L}$ in $S_+$ and $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp},R}$ in $S_-$, and a column of two or of four components is a carrier element written in a basis and not a second definition.

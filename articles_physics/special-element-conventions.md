# __Special Element Conventions__

## Introduction

These are the conventions of the central map and of the special elements. The central map is the restriction of multiplication by the scalar imaginary $i$ to the remarkable subspaces, and the series reads it in four places under four names. The special glyphs — root, idempotent, nilpotent, chirality, spin and the two spinors — are notational, and the article fixes what each glyph promises about the object that carries it.

The article assumes the base and the subspaces of *Algebra and Element Conventions* and the norms of *Norm and Pairing Conventions*, where a class is defined by a norm.

No contravariant index appears in this article, and none appears anywhere in the series. The one metric is the unit metric $\delta_{\imath\jmath}=(1,1,1,1)$; a metric that is the identity raises nothing, so a raised index would name the same index twice. Every coefficient is written low, every contraction is written as a sum with the weight $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}$, and no letter carries a raised spacetime index, in a display, in a table or in a definition.

## The Central Map and Its Restrictions

The scalar imaginary $i$ is central, so multiplication by it is one $\mathbb{C}$-linear map of the algebra, and the movements the series reads in four places are its restrictions to the subspaces. The generator satisfies $(ie_t)^2=-e_t$, so its powers close on four values and the group $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})e_t$ of the centre interpolates between them. The remarkable subspaces carry the restrictions.

Here $\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}$ is the central phase: it is the argument of a unit complex number, so it is real.

| Subspace | $i\,\cdot$ | The name |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{B}_{C}$, the complex time sector | Itself | The global phase |
| $\mathbb{B}_{V}$, the complex space sector | Itself | The duality rotation |
| $\mathbb{B}_{H}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{iH}$ | Each other | The Wick rotation |
| $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ | Each other | The material–informational exchange |

The rule that ties the names is a rule about the generator and not about the subspaces: the map is central, so it acts the same way in every subspace, and it is a scalar that acts through the centre and never through the quaternion directions; a non-central generator would give one rotation per carrier. It is a rotation and not a projection, a collapse or a measurement — a loss of information is a non-invertible map and belongs to *The Operators: One-Sided, Two-Sided, Two-Sided Operator*.

The relation to the Wick rotation. The coordinate continuation $r'_t \mapsto -i\tau$ of *Interpretation of the Wick Rotation in the Biquaternion Universe*, and the two senses in which the series uses the phrase *Wick rotation* — the exchange of the real and imaginary sectors, on the whole element, and the continuation of the time coordinate, a relabeling of one coordinate — are set out there. The two share one generator and one temporal sign flip, and they are not the same map.

The phase gate of the gate articles is a rotation about $\vec e_z$, a non-central generator, and it must not be identified with the central rotation.

## The Special Elements: Root, Idempotent and Nilpotent

The special glyphs are notational. A root $\tilde{Ζ}$, an idempotent $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ or a nilpotent $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ is not a different kind of object from a generic $\tilde Q$: the glyph records what the element is known to be, at the point of use.

### The Potent Tag

The idempotent and the nilpotent are the two ends of one Peirce structure, and the tag says which end. The **potent family** is the idempotents and the nilpotents together, and a member carries its kind as the first word of its subscript: $\mathrm{id}$ for the idempotent, $\mathrm{nil}$ for the nilpotent. The case and the accent stay the letter's, as §*The Idempotent Convention* and §*The Nilpotent Convention* fix them: the tilde marks the quaternion and biquaternion layers and nobody else, the capital marks the algebras with a complex or quaternionic factor, and the tilde-free layers write the plain capital.

| layer | idempotent | nilpotent |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{B}$, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{spl}}$ | $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ | $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ |
| $\mathbb{H}$, $\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{spl}}$ | $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ | $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ |
| $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dln}}$ | $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ | $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ |
| $\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{spl}}$, reduced $\mathbb{B}$ | $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}$ | $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ |

The tilde is the mark of the quaternion and biquaternion layers and it is written nowhere else. The complex, split-complex and dual layers carry the plain capital in both kinds, $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$, and the reduced biquaternion layer writes the plain capital with the sign pair, $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$. The capital separates the nilpotent of those layers from the lower-case nilpotent of the quaternion layers, and the prose names the layer; the tilde never does that work on a tilde-free layer.

The **roots are outside the family** in this pass and keep the glyphs of §*The Root Convention*. A pure member carries the arrow and no tilde, by §*The Centre and the Vector Subspace* of *Algebra and Element Conventions*, so the pure nilpotent is $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ or $\vec\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$; an idempotent has no pure form, and no arrowed idempotent exists. The subscript is read sector, kind, sign, index, so a member of a sign pair is $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}$ and a numbered member is $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}$, and a role label in upright roman is merged with the kind, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id,sym}}$.

### The Root Convention

The roots carry a symbol of their own, beside the idempotent $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ (§*The Idempotent Convention*) and the nilpotent $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ (§*The Nilpotent Convention*): the **root glyph**, in the two cases.

A **root of $1$** of the algebra is an element $\tilde{Ζ}$ with $\tilde{Ζ}^2 = 1$. More generally a **root of a central value** $Q_t$ is an element with $\tilde{Ζ}^2 = Q_te_t$. The roots of the three central values $-1, 0, +1$ are those of *Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the General Plain Algebra*, and the general root is that of *The Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternion Algebra*; the roots the series uses are the roots of $1$.

**The square is read in a named product**, the algebra product unless the statement names another, as for the nilpotents (§*The Nilpotent Convention*), and the class is relative to the product; the roots of a value in the four general products are those of *Square Roots of a General Element in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*.

The case rule splits the glyph, as it does for the elements, the idempotents and the nilpotents. The roots of the biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers carry the upper-case tilde, $\tilde{Ζ}$; those of the quaternion and split-quaternion layers carry the lower-case tilde, $\tilde{\zeta}$; those of the complex, split-complex and dual layers carry the plain capital, $Ζ$; and those of the reduced biquaternion layer carry the same plain capital, $Ζ$, no tilde being written where there is no quaternionic factor. The layer fixes the glyph.

The coordinates carry the letter of the root: in $\mathbb{B}$ the root is $\tilde{Ζ} = Ζ_te_t + Ζ_x\vec e_x + Ζ_y\vec e_y + Ζ_z\vec e_z$ with $Ζ_{\imath} = \zeta_{\imath} + i\zeta'_{\imath}$, its scalar part $Ζ_t$ and its vector part $\vec{Ζ} = \vec\zeta + i\vec\zeta'$; in $\mathbb{H}$ it is $\tilde\zeta = \zeta_te_t + \zeta_x\vec e_x + \zeta_y\vec e_y + \zeta_z\vec e_z$ with real $\zeta_{\imath}$.

It is allowed to add subscripts when several roots are used. The subscript of a root is numeric when it counts and a role label when it names. Where more than one occurs the roots are numbered, $\tilde{Ζ}_1, \tilde{Ζ}_2$, as for the nilpotents and for the idempotent pairs other than the complete sign pair of §*The Idempotent Convention*; where a root plays a named role, it carries the role in upright roman as its subscript. A sign is a subscript where the statement needs one.

The adjoined generator of the double carries the root glyph. The double $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{R}}$ carries a central root of $-1$ outside $\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{R}}$; it is written $\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$, the biquaternion root glyph with the role subscript $\mathrm{ad}$ of the paragraph above. It is a root of the double, not an element of $\mathbb{B}$. The sign of the double is $\chi=i\tilde{Ζ}_{\mathrm{ad}}$, with $\chi^2=e_t$, and it is the central form of the fifth basis operator of a frame, $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}=-iR_{\vec e_z}$, written in the double. The basis operator is one example of the right slot and not the definition of the label, which is a side of an operator and needs no element; §*The Chirality Convention* states both, and neither is called chirality in this file. No other letter is used for either.

The root glyph is therefore split by the case rule, and the split is the reason the convention is stated:

| Symbol | Role |
|---|---|
| $Ζ$ | A root of $1$ of the complex, split-complex or dual layer, where no tilde is written |
| $Ζ_\pm$ | A root of $1$ of the reduced biquaternion layer, where no tilde is written |
| $\tilde{\zeta}$ | A root of $1$ of the quaternion or split-quaternion layer |
| $\tilde{Ζ}$ | A root of $1$ of the biquaternion or split-biquaternion layer |

### The Idempotent Convention

The convention of *The Elements and their Coefficients* fixes the case and the tilde of a generic element. The idempotents carry a symbol of their own, and the series uses it throughout.

An **idempotent** of the algebra is an element $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ with $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^2 = \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$. An idempotent fixed by the star, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^{*} = \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$, is a **Hermitian idempotent**, and the rank-one Hermitian idempotents of $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$ are the **pure states** of the informational sector.

The case rule splits the glyph, as it does in the mathematical corpus. The idempotents of the biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers carry the upper-case tilde, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$; those of the quaternion and split-quaternion layers carry the lower-case tilde, $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{id}}$; those of the complex and dual layers carry the plain capital, $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$, no tilde being written where there is no quaternionic factor; those of the split-complex and reduced biquaternion layers write the complete pair with the signs, $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}$. The series works in $\mathbb{B}$ and writes $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ throughout; the other glyphs are the mathematical corpus's, and the layer fixes the glyph.

A non-zero idempotent $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ generates the **minimal left ideal** $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$, which is the state module of the companion articles; the standard pair, the classification of the idempotents, the polarisation identity, the Peirce decomposition, the matrix units and the projective geometry of the pure states are those of *The Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Biquaternion Algebra* and its companions in the mathematical corpus.

The coordinates carry the letter of the idempotent: in $\mathbb{B}$ the idempotent is $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{id},t}e_t + \Pi_{\mathrm{id},x}\vec e_x + \Pi_{\mathrm{id},y}\vec e_y + \Pi_{\mathrm{id},z}\vec e_z$ with $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\imath} = \pi_{\mathrm{id},\imath} + i\pi'_{\mathrm{id},\imath}$, its scalar part $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},t}$ and its vector part $\vec\Pi = \vec\pi + i\vec\pi'$, the untagged arrow of a coordinate vector and not an element; in $\mathbb{H}$ it is $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{id}} = \pi_{\mathrm{id},t}e_t + \pi_{\mathrm{id},x}\vec e_x + \pi_{\mathrm{id},y}\vec e_y + \pi_{\mathrm{id},z}\vec e_z$ with real $\pi_{\mathrm{id},\imath}$.

It is allowed to add subscripts when several idempotents are used. The subscript of an idempotent is numeric when it counts and a role label when it names, but for the two members of a complete sign pair. A pair is $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}, \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},2}$; a second pair in the same statement takes the next numbers, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},3}, \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},4}$; and a family indexed by a direction is written with its argument, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1,2}(\vec u)$. When a pair is complete, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+} + \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},-} = e_t$, its two members may be written $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}$ and $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},-}$: the subscript $+$ names the member attached to $+1$ and the subscript $-$ the member attached to $-1$. A pair that is not complete may be numbered, labelled or signed as the statement requires. A role label is written in upright roman, as for a unit element.

The idempotent glyph is therefore split by the case rule, and the split is the reason the convention is stated:

| Symbol | Role |
|---|---|
| $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ | An idempotent of the complex or dual layer, where no tilde is written |
| $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}$ | A complete sign pair of idempotents of the split-complex or reduced biquaternion layer, where no tilde is written |
| $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ | An idempotent of the quaternion or split-quaternion layer, in the mathematical corpus |
| $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ | An idempotent or a pure state of the biquaternion or split-biquaternion layer, and the minimal left ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ it generates |

### The Nilpotent Convention

The nilpotents carry a symbol of their own, beside the idempotent $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ (§*The Idempotent Convention*): the lower-case $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ of the quaternion and split-quaternion layers and the upper-case $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ of the biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers. The two cases are the two layers of the potent family, and the kind is carried by the tag of §*The Potent Tag*.

A **nilpotent** of the algebra is an element $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ whose square vanishes,

$$
\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}^2 = 0 .
$$

**The square is read in a named product**, the algebra product unless the statement names another, as in the mathematical corpus (*Special Element and Peirce Conventions*, §*The Nilpotent Convention*), and the class is relative to the product. A nilpotent is never a Hermitian idempotent, a Hermitian nilpotent being zero, and it is never a state; the nilpotent is the partner of the idempotent in a minimal left ideal, $\mathbb{B}\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},2} = \mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},1}$.

The coordinates carry the letter of the nilpotent: in $\mathbb{B}$ the nilpotent is $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{nil},t}e_t + \Pi_{\mathrm{nil},x}\vec e_x + \Pi_{\mathrm{nil},y}\vec e_y + \Pi_{\mathrm{nil},z}\vec e_z$ with $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},\imath} = \pi_{\mathrm{nil},\imath} + i\pi'_{\mathrm{nil},\imath}$, its scalar part $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},t}$ and its vector part $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}} = \vec\pi_{\mathrm{nil}} + i\vec\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}'$; in $\mathbb{H}$ it is $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}} = \pi_{\mathrm{nil},t}e_t + \pi_{\mathrm{nil},x}\vec e_x + \pi_{\mathrm{nil},y}\vec e_y + \pi_{\mathrm{nil},z}\vec e_z$ with real $\pi_{\mathrm{nil},\imath}$.

It is allowed to add subscripts when several nilpotents are used. The subscript of a nilpotent is numeric when it counts and a role label when it names, as for the idempotents apart from their complete sign pair: where more than one occurs a pair is $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil},1}, \tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil},2}$, a second pair in the same statement takes the next numbers, and where a nilpotent plays a named role it carries the role in upright roman as its subscript. A sign is a subscript where the statement needs one.

The nilpotents have no classification of the idempotent kind and no sphere of pure states; which set the square-zero elements form depends on the product. The nilpotents of a state module are those of *The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent*, and the spin-down state of *Spin-1/2 Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form* is a nilpotent of the ideal of that vacuum.

The nilpotent glyph is one letter in two cases, the capital $\Pi$ and the lower-case $\pi$, with the tilde where the layer carries one and the kind in the subscript:

| Symbol | Role |
|---|---|
| $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ | A nilpotent of the biquaternion or split-biquaternion layer, and the minimal left ideal $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ it generates |
| $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ | A nilpotent of the complex, split-complex or dual layer, with the plain capital and no tilde |
| $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ | A nilpotent of the quaternion or split-quaternion layer |
| $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$, $\vec\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ | A pure nilpotent, written with the arrow and without the tilde by the priority rule of *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Centre and the Vector Subspace* |

The standard pair of nilpotents is $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},1}$ and $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},2}$, the off-diagonal Peirce units of *Peirce Decompositions of the Biquaternion Algebra* exchanged by the star. The truncated ladder of *The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent* and of the Fock, vacuum and matrix-representation articles is this pair: those articles write it $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},1}$ and $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},2}$ wherever the algebra element is meant, and keep the ladder letters for the abstract operators.

**The standard pair carries the arrow, not the tilde.** The two Peirce units are pure, $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},1} = \tfrac12(i\vec e_x - \vec e_y)$ and $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},2} = \tfrac12(i\vec e_x + \vec e_y)$, with no scalar part, so by *Algebra and Element Conventions*, §*The Centre and the Vector Subspace* the arrow takes priority over the tilde and the tilde is dropped; the pair is written $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},1}, \vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},2}$ and never $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},1}, \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil},2}$. The tilde is kept for the general nilpotent $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$, which may carry a scalar part and is not pure.

### The Chirality Convention

The word, on an operator. Operator chirality is the side on which an operator multiplies: the left slot for a pure left multiplier, $c\,L_{\tilde Q}$; the right slot for a pure right multiplier, $c\,R_{\tilde P,\Phi}$; both slots for a basis operator whose two multipliers are both occupied. The label is read from the basis operator and from nothing else: no root, no idempotent, no ideal, no spinor and no Clifford relation enters it. The vocabulary is *Operator Family Conventions*, §*The Slot Label, Which Is the Chirality*, and the properties of the two one-sided families and of the label are *Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra*, both in the mathematical corpus.

The word, on a spinor. Spinor chirality is the value: which eigenspace of the chirality operator an element occupies, left-handed or right-handed. It is a property of a field and not of an operator, and it is the reading a physicist gives the bare word. The two words are not rivals. A field's spinor chirality is read with the operator chirality of the operator that splits it: the operator's side says how it acts, the field's value says which half it lives in. A value needs an axis, because the eigenspaces are the ideals of a declared $\vec u$; a side needs none.

One basis operator that realises the split. The side is the convention, and one operator shows it. For any scalar-free $u$ with $u^2=-e_t$ the operator
$$
-iR_u:\quad \tilde Q\mapsto-i\tilde Qu
$$
is a pure right multiplier; it squares to the identity, its scalar part is zero, and its two eigenspaces are of equal size. It is therefore an example of the right slot, and an example is never the definition of the label. Choose another $u$ and the basis operator changes while every label of every operator stays where it was.

The glyph is a name for one such basis operator. Where the corpus prints the basis operator of the third direction it writes $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}=-iR_{\vec e_z}$, and the subscript names that one operator and carries no part of the label. The definition of that operator and its gamma arrays are in *Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra* and *Matrix Representations Conventions*, §*The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation*; the gamma display prints the same operator as $\gamma_5$, whose index belongs to that display and to no other.

The subscript on an element. A basis operator acts on an element by right multiplication, and its spectrum is $+1,+1,-1,-1$, so the subscript of the element takes the two values $L$ and $R$: $\tilde Q_L$ is the $-1$ part and $\tilde Q_R$ the $+1$ part, in the letters of the Dirac literature. A product takes the letter of its right factor, and left multiplication keeps the letter. The subscript is a function of the element and of the basis operator chosen; the choice fixes which basis operator is used and not which value is called left. The rule, with the three uses of the two letters, is *Matrix Representations Conventions*, §*The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation*.

What the word does not name. Three other objects are routinely offered and none is the label. The central form of the double, fixed in §*The Root Convention*, is a sign of an adjoined layer; a Peirce sign, $R_{\mathrm{axf},i\vec e_z}=iR_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_z}$, is a copy index of two ideals; and the basis operator itself is an example of a side. The agreements between them hold only where the same element is used twice, and they are recorded as coincidences in *Operator Chirality on the Biquaternion Algebra*, §*What Operator Chirality Is Not*.

### The Spin Convention

**Spin is the intrinsic angular momentum of a particle.** In the rest frame the one-particle states satisfy $\tilde S_{\Psi}^2=\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\,s\,(s+1)$ and $\tilde S_{\Psi,z}=\frac{h}{2\pi}\,s_{z}$, with $s$ an integer or half-integer and $s_z=-s,\dots,s$; relativistically $s$ labels the little-group $SU(2)$ representation, and for a massless particle the label is helicity. The algebra supplies the rotation action and the weights of its generator on a carrier, but neither the rest-frame one-particle space nor the unit $\frac{h}{2\pi}$: those are physical inputs. A classical vector is a **spin vector**, or intrinsic angular momentum, and the continuous $2+1$ parameter is a **statistics parameter**; neither is a second definition.

The algebraic definition, on a carrier. Let $(W,\rho)$ be an irreducible unitary carrier of the rotation algebra generated by $\tilde S_{\Psi,k}=\frac{h}{4\pi}i\vec e_{\imath}$. For an invertible element $\vec u$ the operator $\rho(\vec u\cdot\vec S)$ is self-adjoint and its spectrum does not depend on $\vec u$, so the carrier has one spin and not one per axis:
$$
s=\frac{2\pi \max}{h}\operatorname{spec}\bigl(\rho(\vec u\cdot\vec S)\bigr)\in\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0},
$$
a scalar label and not a vector. The definition is the dimension of the carrier's representation space, $2s+1$; the maximum weight is the same number read off the generators, and that is the sentence an article computes with. The two agree on every carrier the corpus uses (*From the Spin to the Carriers and the Closures*, §*The Spin Table*, in the mathematical corpus). The label is $s$ and the projection $s_z$. The half-angle is the exponential coordinate of the rotor, the Peirce index is a copy label, and the chirality is the side on which an operator multiplies, the fifth basis operator of §*The Chirality Convention* being the example of it that a frame writes down.

### The Spinor Convention

**Definition (elementary spinor).** An **elementary spinor** is an element of a complex two-dimensional space on which the left multiplications act irreducibly with weights $+\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac12$, so that a rotation through $\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}$ about $\vec u$ multiplies it by $\exp(-\frac{2\pi i\,\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}\,\vec u\,\vec S}{h})$. In $\mathbb{B}$ the carrier is a minimal left ideal,
$$
S_\pm(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}(\vec u),\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}(\vec u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\pm i\vec u\bigr),
$$
with $\vec u$ a unit real vector; each is of complex dimension two and of spin $\tfrac12$, and the axis $\vec u$ is part of the datum. An element of $S_+(\vec u)$ is $d_0\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}(\vec u)+d_1\mathrm{spt}(\vec u)$ with $d_0,d_1\in\mathbb{C}$, so an elementary spinor is a pair of complex numbers in a chosen carrier.

**Definition (Dirac spinor).** A **Dirac spinor** is an element $\tilde{Ξ}=\tilde{Ξ}_{L}+\tilde{Ξ}_{R}$ of $\mathbb{B}$, the two parts being its two components in the decomposition of $\mathbb{B}$ into minimal left ideals,
$$
\mathbb{B}=S_+(\vec u)\oplus S_-(\vec u),\qquad \tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}(\vec u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\pm i\vec u\bigr),
$$
for a declared unit imaginary direction $\vec u$, and each part is an elementary spinor of complex dimension two. Every element of the algebra is a Dirac spinor and only the zero element has both parts zero, so the definition costs one declared axis and nothing else.

The two parts carry the chirality letters. The decomposition is the decomposition into the two values of the chirality: the basis operator $-iR_{\vec u}$ of §*The Chirality Convention* is $-1$ on $S_+(\vec u)$ and $+1$ on $S_-(\vec u)$, so $\tilde{Ξ}_{L}\in S_+(\vec u)$ is the $L$ part and $\tilde{Ξ}_{R}\in S_-(\vec u)$ the $R$ part, and at $\vec u=\vec e_z$ the basis operator is $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}=-iR_{\vec e_z}$. Exchanging the two letters is the substitution $\vec u\mapsto-\vec u$, because $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},+}(\vec u)=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},-}(-\vec u)$: the pair is unordered, and the definition orders it only to name the two parts.

A column is a writing, not a definition. A column of two components is an elementary spinor written in a chosen carrier, and a column of four is a Dirac spinor written in a matrix model, one pair per part. The count $4=2\times2$ multiplies the spin dimension by the number of parts, and the two factors have different origins. Neither column is a second definition, and the definitions need no Clifford relation, no gamma basis operator and no extra rotation triple.

## Summary

Multiplication by $i$ is one map. On the centre it is the global phase; on the complex space sector it is the duality rotation; on the real and imaginary halves it is the Wick rotation, and on the two sectors it is the material–informational exchange. The generator satisfies $(ie_t)^2=-e_t$, and the group $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})e_t$ of the centre interpolates between the four values. The map is a rotation and not a projection, a collapse or a measurement, and it acts through the centre rather than through the quaternion directions.

The special glyphs are notational: a root $\tilde{Ζ}$, an idempotent $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ and a nilpotent $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ or $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ are generic elements whose glyph records what they are known to be. A root is a root in the product the statement names; an idempotent squares to itself; a nilpotent squares to zero. The case rule applies to each, the coordinates carry the letter of the glyph, and several occurrences take subscripts by the rule of *Index and Notation Conventions*. The standard Peirce pair carries the arrow and not the tilde, because its members are pure.

Chirality is the side on which an operator multiplies, and not an element of the algebra: the left slot, the right slot, or both. The basis operator that shows it on a frame is the fifth basis operator, $\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{vol}}=-iR_{\vec e_z}$, a pure right multiplier and hence an example of the right slot, $i$ times the product of the four basis operators of the frame, of spectrum $+1,+1,-1,-1$ on the elements. The subscript of an element takes the two values $L$ and $R$, read from that basis operator and from the declared frame. The central form of the chirality is the sign of the double, and a Peirce sign is neither the basis operator nor its central form.

Spin is defined once, on a carrier: it is the largest eigenvalue of $\vec u\cdot\vec S$ in units of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$, a scalar in $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$ that does not depend on the axis, and physically it is the intrinsic angular momentum of a particle, with $\tilde S_{\Psi}^2=\frac{h^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\,s\,(s+1)$, $\tilde S_{\Psi,z}=\frac{h}{2\pi}\,s_{z}$ and helicity in the massless case. The algebra supplies the rotation action and the weights of its generator; it supplies neither the one-particle space nor the unit $\frac{h}{2\pi}$.

An elementary spinor is an element of a carrier, a minimal left ideal $S_\pm(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}(\vec u)$ of complex dimension two with weights $\pm\tfrac12$; a Dirac spinor is an element $\tilde{Ξ}=\tilde{Ξ}_{L}+\tilde{Ξ}_{R}$ of $\mathbb{B}$, its two parts being the components in $\mathbb{B}=S_+(\vec u)\oplus S_-(\vec u)$ for a declared axis $\vec u$. The two parts are the two values of the chirality, $\tilde{Ξ}_{L}$ in $S_+$ and $\tilde{Ξ}_{R}$ in $S_-$, and a column of two or of four components is a carrier element written in a basis and not a second definition.

Introduction

This article is the physics-side entry point to that mathematical study. The category Biquaternion Mathematical Physics focuses on some of those results only, the ones that carry a physical reading, and its mathematical source is The Biquaternion Mathematics Corpus. Physical Readings

The four blocks of the study read as a division of labour with the physics. Algebra owns the products, the involutions and the remarkable subspaces; topology owns the zero-divisor cone and the projective cone, which are the light cone and the directions of light; analysis owns the regular functions and the operators, which are the fields and their evolutions; geometry owns the frame, the geodesics and the surfaces, which are the Lorentz transformations and their orbits. The article's claim that nothing here is owned and everything is read is the same claim from the other end: each physical interpretation is a theorem of one of the four blocks.

Summary

  • The biquaternion algebra is studied in depth in the mathematics corpus; this article is the physics-side sub-menu of that study and owns no result of its own.
  • The mathematical study is organised in four blocks: Algebra, Topology, Analysis and Geometry, and each block carries a part of the physics.
  • The article carries the direct links to all eighty-five entries of the mathematics Biquaternions category, grouped as the mathematics menu groups them.

Summary of Notation

Symbol Meaning
$\mathbb{B}$ the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, of complex dimension four and real dimension eight
$e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ the quaternion basis, $e_t$ the unit and $\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ the vector units
$i$ the central imaginary unit; with $e_t$ it generates the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$
$\tilde{Q}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu e_\mu$ a general element, with complex coefficients $Q_\mu$
$\bar{\cdot}$, ${}^{\natural}$, ${}^{*}$, $\flat$ the four conjugations: complex, quaternion, Hermitian and reversal
$\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$, $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, $\mathbb{M}_+$, $\mathbb{M}_-$ the remarkable subspaces
$\langle\tilde{Q}\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ the biquaternion norm, the form that carries the interval
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ the bracket of the four forms of the study, the subscript recording the conjugation entering each argument
$\mathsf{M}_2$ the isomorphism $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$
$M_n(k)$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{n,m}$ the matrix algebras and the Clifford algebras that appear in the titles below

The $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model: Four Colours, Fifteen Generators, One Declaration

Introduction

The model is one group. Not a product, not a pair of weak factors beside a colour group: one simple group acting on one four-dimensional complex space.

$$ \boxed{\; G=\mathrm{SU}(4),\qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak g=15,\qquad \operatorname{rank}\mathfrak g=3,\qquad 15=\underbrace{8}_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}\oplus\underbrace{1}_{\mathbb R\,iD}\oplus\underbrace{6}_{\mathfrak m} \;} $$

Four colours, fifteen generators, one declaration. The lepton is read as a fourth colour; the fifteen split as colour, one abelian direction and six leptoquarks; and the only statement the algebra does not make is which of the four directions is the lepton.

Why the group is one and not three. The previous version of this corpus named the group $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ and counted twenty-one generators. That group is gone, and the reason is not a change of taste: the two weak factors were being read out of a real six-dimensional subalgebra of the fifteen, and a subalgebra of the algebra is not a factor beside it. The fifteen contains $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, spanned by the six real antisymmetric planes, and the six arrays that used to be called $T^{L}_{a}$ and $T^{R}_{a}$ are exactly that subalgebra in a rotated basis. A factor is a group; a subalgebra is a subspace of the algebra. §The Real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ Inside the Fifteen computes which one this is, and the next section of the corpus says what the weak force actually is.

The two layers, and the one that matters here. Layer A is what the algebra does on its own carrier. Layer B is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ acting on a formal copy of that carrier. The distinction is load-bearing: the algebra's commutant forces the copy, and §The Carrier Is a Copy proves it with a count.

What this article establishes. That the group is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and the count is fifteen; that the fifteen split as $8+1+6$; that the matter content is the sixteen and that it is vector-like under the group, so its anomaly cancels by counting; that one coupling multiplies all fifteen generators; that the fifteen already contains a real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ of six dimensions and rank two; that the sixteen is closed under $CP$ and not under $C$; and that the ceiling on any group commuting with the Lorentz action is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, which is why the carrier is a copy.

What it does not establish. That the algebra derives the group — it derives the group given the carrier. That the lepton line is the fourth colour — that is declaration (A). That the model is chiral where it gauges — it is not, and §The Chiral Wall shows that this is now a theorem about the model rather than a caveat about a representation.

What is absent, and where it went. No $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$, no $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, no hypercharge, no photon, no $W$, no $Z$, no rank five, no twenty-one, no twenty-four cells. The successor model — colour, one abelian factor and two weak factors — is reached by a transition at a scale $M_C$, and it is the subject of the next section of the corpus. This article is the model before that transition, and it is complete on its own terms.

Where the rest is. The counts and the arrays are The Counts of the Fifteen and The Classification of the Bosons. The transition is The Death of the Mega Strong Force. What the weak force is, and why it is not a piece of the fifteen, is The Weak Force Born as a Side Effect. The couplings are The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model.

One Group, and the Count of Fifteen

Where the group comes from. The algebra $\mathbb B\cong M_2(\mathbb C)$ acts on its own carrier, a four-dimensional complex space. The unitary group of that carrier is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$: the matrices with $M^{\dagger}M=\mathrm I$ and $\det M=1$. It is not an element of the algebra and not a corner of its endomorphisms; it is the whole group of the space the algebra acts on.

The count, from the arrays. Take the four indices $(t,x,y,z)$ and the matrices that act on them.

building block count what it is
real antisymmetric planes $A_{jk}$ $6$ one per unordered pair
imaginary symmetric planes $iS_{jk}$ $6$ one per unordered pair
trace-free diagonals $3$ $iD$ and two colour diagonals
total $\mathbf{15}$ $\mathfrak{su}(4)$

Six pairs, two arrays per pair, three diagonals: $12+3=15$. The count is not a choice. There are $\binom42=6$ pairs of four indices; each pair carries a real and an imaginary array; and a trace-free diagonal has three dimensions.

The same count from the cells. A $4\times4$ grid has sixteen cells. One of them is the trace, which $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ excludes, and the other fifteen are the generators.

$$ 16=1+15 . $$

The old frame's count was $24=4^2+2^2+2^2$, the cells of three grids: one for the quartet and one for each weak doublet. With one group there is one grid and sixteen cells, and the arithmetic is shorter because the object is smaller.

The split that the declaration fixes. Under $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ acting on the three colours,

$$ \mathbf{15}=\mathbf 8_{0}\oplus\mathbf 1_{0}\oplus\mathbf 3_{+4/3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}_{-4/3}, $$

that is

$$ 15=8+1+6 . $$

The eight is colour. The one is the abelian direction $iD$. The six is the leptoquark sextet, carrying one colour and one lepton index at once, with $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$ and $Q=\pm\tfrac23$ once charge is defined.

Read the split as a fate list. The eight survive every breaking of the model as gluons. The one survives as the $B-L$ vector and later mixes. The six are the only coloured massive vectors in the theory, and they are what a leptoquark search looks for.

The rank. A maximal torus of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ has dimension three. It is spanned by $iD=\operatorname{diag}(-3i,i,i,i)$ and the two trace-free colour diagonals. Rank three, and no breaking of this group reaches rank five — not because of dynamics, but because a subalgebra never outranks its algebra. That single line is why the successor model's two weak factors cannot be pieces of this one.

The Sixteen, and the Copy Index

The matter content, in one line.

$$ \mathbf{16}=\mathbf 4\otimes\mathbf 2\ \oplus\ \bar{\mathbf4}\otimes\mathbf 2, \qquad 4\cdot2+4\cdot2=16 . $$

Read the two factors of each term. The first is the colour index: a quartet, lepton plus three colours. The second is a copy index, two-dimensional. Nothing else.

The copy index is a label here, not a gauge factor. It does not multiply a generator in the covariant derivative, it has no coupling, and no field in this model transforms under it. It records that the matter comes in two copies, and that is all it does in this article.

What the two copies are. They are the two chiralities of the sixteen, each a doublon of the quartet:

$$ \mathbf{16}=\underbrace{\mathbf 4\otimes\mathbf 2}_{\text{two copies of the quartet}}\oplus\underbrace{\bar{\mathbf4}\otimes\mathbf 2}_{\text{two copies of its conjugate}} . $$

The neutrino is not added. The lepton line of the conjugate quartet, at $B-L=-1$, is the right-handed neutrino's slot. The moment the lepton is a colour, the fourth component of the $\bar{\mathbf4}$ is forced, and it is a neutrino. That was true in the old frame and it is true here.

The anomaly, by counting. Under $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ alone the content is

$$ 2\cdot\mathbf 4\ \oplus\ 2\cdot\bar{\mathbf4}, $$

two quartets and two antiquartets. The cubic index is $+1$ on the $\mathbf4$ and $-1$ on the $\bar{\mathbf4}$, so the $\mathrm{SU}(4)^3$ triangle coefficient is

$$ 2(+1)+2(-1)=0 . $$

Zero by pairing, not by a numerical accident. The spectrum is vector-like in pairs, so the gauge anomaly cancels identically — and so does every mixed coefficient, because there is only one factor and nothing to mix with.

The old frame had to work for this. There the sixteen was complex, and three traces had to be computed to show that $\operatorname{tr}Y=0$, $\operatorname{tr}Y^{3}=0$ and the doublet count was even. Those checks are still true and they are still in the corpus, but they are now statements about a different group: hypercharge and the weak factors enter at the transition, and the checks become conditions on the successor's spectrum, not on this model's.

One remark, kept because it is a real computation. The left-handed quartet gave $\operatorname{tr}Y^{3}=-\tfrac29$, computed as

$$ 6\cdot\Bigl(\tfrac16\Bigr)^{3}+2\cdot\Bigl(-\tfrac12\Bigr)^{3}=\tfrac{1}{36}-\tfrac14=-\tfrac29 , $$

and its conjugate gave $+\tfrac29$ with the signs reversed, so the sum vanished. The cancellation was between a representation and its own conjugate — which is exactly the pairing that now makes the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ anomaly vanish by counting. The same structural fact, read on a smaller group.

One Covariant Derivative

The whole gauge content is one line, and it is shorter than it used to be.

$$ D_{\imath}=\partial_{\imath}-ig_4\sum_{a=1}^{15}A_{a,\imath}T_{a} . $$

One sum, fifteen fields, one coupling. The old frame had three sums and three couplings: two terms and two symbols have been removed, and nothing was lost, because the factors they multiplied are not in the model.

Every index is low, because the metric is $\delta_{\imath\jmath}=(1,1,1,1)$: a raised index names the same index. The label $a$ is a name and not a frame slot; it counts the fifteen generators.

What the fifteen fields are.

block count $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ fate
$a$ in the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ corner $8$ $\mathbf 8$ the gluons, massless
$A_{15}$ $1$ $\mathbf 1$ the $B-L$ vector, massless until it mixes downstream
$a$ in $\mathfrak m$ $6$ $\mathbf 3+\bar{\mathbf3}$ the leptoquarks, heavy at $M_C$

Nine massless and six massive, and that is the whole bosonic spectrum of this model. No $W$, no $Z$, no photon: those are fields of the successor, and their masses come from vacua that this model does not possess.

What the model does not tie. One coupling, so there is no ratio to fix. The old frame's three couplings and their four-decade mismatch are a property of the successor, and they are treated in The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model. A single coupling has no matching problem.

The Real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ Inside the Fifteen

This section is the reason the old frame existed, and it is worth doing properly.

The object. The algebra multiplies on two sides, so left and right multiplication by the imaginary units give two commuting triples:

$$ \mathfrak k_L=\{L_{\vec e_a}\},\qquad \mathfrak k_R=\{R_{\vec e_a}\},\qquad [L_{\vec e_a},R_{\vec e_b}]=0 . $$

Each triple is three real dimensions, they commute, and together they span six. That six is exactly the span of the six real antisymmetric plane arrays:

$$ \mathfrak k_L\oplus\mathfrak k_R=\operatorname{span}\{A_{ab}\ :\ a<b\},\qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}=6 . $$

Both statements are computations, not identifications. The six $A_{ab}$ number six, they are closed under brackets, and each $L_{\vec e_a}$ and $R_{\vec e_a}$ is a real combination of them — in the corpus's basis,

$$ T^{L}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}+A_{yz}\bigr),\qquad T^{R}_{x}=-\tfrac12\bigl(A_{tx}-A_{yz}\bigr), $$

and cyclic in $(x,y,z)$. Those six combinations span the same six dimensions as the six $A_{ab}$, which is why the two descriptions are one subspace written twice.

What it is, and what it is not. It is a real subalgebra of the fifteen, with $\dim_{\mathbb R}=6$ and rank $2$. It is not a second factor: a factor is a group multiplying the algebra's fields, and this is a subspace of the algebra itself. The difference is not bookkeeping — it is the difference between rank three and rank five.

What the condensate does to it. The transition's vacuum is $iD=\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1)$, and the mass matrix of the fifteen is

$$ M^{2}=2v^{2}\bigl[D,[D,\cdot\,]\bigr], $$

which is diagonal on the plane arrays with one number per plane:

planes $m^{2}$
$A_{ta},A_{ty},A_{tz}$ $32v^{2}$
$A_{xy},A_{xz},A_{yz}$ $0$
$iS_{ta},iS_{ty},iS_{tz}$ $32v^{2}$
$iS_{xy},iS_{xz},iS_{yz}$ $0$
the three diagonals $0$

So the real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ straddles the transition: three of its six planes are heavy and three are massless. A weak array, being half of one and half of the other, is a sum of a heavy and a massless vector — a coherence, not a particle. That single fact is why the six arrays could never be the weak bosons, and What the Sextet Cannot Do makes it a theorem.

Why the old frame looked right. Six arrays, six weak generators, six leptoquarks, all inside fifteen alongside nine. Everything matched except closure, charge and mass, and the mismatch is the subject of the next section of the corpus.

The honest sentence for this article. The fifteen contains a six-dimensional real subalgebra that looks like a weak sector and behaves like one under every operation except the three that matter — it does not close on the sextet, its charges are thirds rather than integral, and its directions are half heavy. Reading it as a factor was the corpus's one structural error, and this article corrects it.

What Is Derived and What Is Declared

Three different strengths, and the difference has to be stated or the article overclaims.

The criterion of legitimacy, restated. The corpus once carried an implicit test: a gauge group is legitimate only if its generators are elements of the algebra, $X\in\mathbb B$. That test is wrong and it has to go, because $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ live in the endomorphisms of $\mathbb B$ and never in $\mathbb B$.

The replacement is an address, not a filter. A generator is an element of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)$, and the sixteen words $e_{\imath}[\,]e_{\jmath}=L_{e_{\imath}}R_{e_{\jmath}}$ are a basis of it. The length of a generator is the rank of its coefficient array, and Conway's bound is four:

$$ M=\sum_{\imath,\jmath}c_{\imath\jmath}\,L_{e_{\imath}}R_{e_{\jmath}},\qquad \ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(c_{\imath\jmath}\bigr)\le4 . $$

The ladder, for the generators this model names.

object in $\mathbb B$? length the words
central $\mathrm{U}(1)$ yes, $\mathbb R(ie_t)$ $1$ $L_{e_t}R_{e_t}$
the six sides $\mathfrak k_L\oplus\mathfrak k_R$ no $1$ $L_{\vec e_a}R_{e_t}$, $L_{e_t}R_{\vec e_a}$
$\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ no $2$ on seven, $3$ on $\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1,-2)$ off-diagonal pairs of $B_V$, and the Cartan
$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ no $1$ on the six unscaled words, $2$ or $3$ on the other named generators, $4$ generic all fifteen
$B-L$ no $3$ $i\sum_a L_{\vec e_a}R_{\vec e_a}$

Only the central phase is in the algebra. Everything else is an endomorphism, at length one, two, three or four. The table records addresses, and it is not a hierarchy: $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ has a basis of fifteen elements of length one, so "the group $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ has length three" is not a statement.

What selects the group is the module, not the length. The algebra supplies one four-dimensional complex module, so the largest compact simple group acting faithfully on it is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$; $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ needs five dimensions and $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ sixteen, and the algebra supplies neither.

Why This Group and Not a Larger One

Two refusals, and one non-competitor.

$\mathrm{SU}(5)$. Rank four, and its $\mathbf{10}+\bar{\mathbf5}$ has no right-handed neutrino and puts quarks and leptons in one multiplet in a way the algebra's split does not suggest. The corpus's $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is the carrier's group; $\mathfrak{su}(5)$ would be a group with no carrier in the algebra.

$\mathrm{SO}(10)$. Rank five, and it contains $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ — but its spinor is a $\mathbf{16}$ obtained by a Clifford algebra the corpus does not otherwise use, and containment is not a derivation. It is recorded and refused.

The left–right group is not a competitor any more. It was the closest alternative in the old frame: the same spectrum minus the six leptoquarks. Now it is the successor — the gauge group the model becomes at $M_C$, with the two weak factors born there. An alternative is a group one might have chosen instead; a successor is a group one arrives at, and the difference is the transition.

The one-line reason the group is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$. The carrier is four-dimensional and its stabiliser is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$; three colours inside a four-dimensional carrier with one line removed is $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, not $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$. Whether the fourth direction is gauged is the physics question, and this model answers that it is.

The Uniqueness Result, Demoted

A theorem that used to be quoted in support, now recorded and set aside.

The result. Boyle and Farnsworth ask which finite algebra carries the Standard-Model group as its inner automorphisms, in an $8\times8$ Hermitian block-diagonal representation commuting with the chirality. The answer is unique, and the Pati–Salam algebra is the relevant one:

$$ \mathcal A_F=J_2^{L}(\mathbb C)\oplus J_2^{R}(\mathbb C)\oplus J_4(\mathbb C), $$

of real dimension $4+4+16=24$, in blocks of size $2,2,4$. Its associators span $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(4)$, of dimension $3+3+15=21$ — the old frame's count exactly.

Why it is demoted, in one paragraph. The theorem forces a product group in a representation the corpus does not use. Under the present frame the product group is not the model, so a theorem forcing it is a theorem about a group this corpus has given up. It is not evidence against the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$-only model and it is no longer evidence for it either. It is kept because it was cited, and because a reader who met it in an earlier version deserves to be told what became of it.

What survives from it, and is independent. The Hermitian part of the corpus's operator layer is the single Jordan algebra $J_4(\mathbb C)$ of dimension sixteen, whose derivation algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ of dimension fifteen. That computation does not depend on the theorem: it is one Jordan algebra against four, and it is the corpus's own.

And the one thing the theorem genuinely settled. That the labelling of the quartet's directions is a choice — the source recovers observed charges only after adjoining an outer abelian factor. Declaration (A) remains a declaration under any theorem, which is why the corpus states it as one.

The Carrier Is a Copy, and Here Is the Theorem

The objection. If $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ acts on the same four dimensions as the Lorentz action, the two must commute — and then colour would have to sit in the commutant of the Lorentz algebra, where there is no room for it.

The computation, and it is the load-bearing one.

$$ \mathfrak{su}(4)\cap\bigl\{X:[X,L_{\vec e_a}]=0\ \text{for }a=x,y,z\bigr\} =\operatorname{span}\{R_{\vec e_x},R_{\vec e_y},R_{\vec e_z}\}\cong\mathfrak{su}(2), \qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}=3 . $$

The proof, in three counts.

First, the commutant. As a module for $L(\mathbb B)\cong M_2(\mathbb C)$ the carrier is $\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2$, with $L(\mathbb B)$ acting on the first factor. By Schur's lemma the commutant is $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb C^2)$, real dimension eight, and that commutant is exactly $R(\mathbb B)$.

Second, the anti-Hermitian part. Of those eight real dimensions, four are anti-Hermitian: $R_{\vec e_a}$ together with $R_i=i\,\mathrm I_4$.

Third, the trace. $\operatorname{tr}R_i=4i\ne0$, so $R_i$ is excluded by the tracelessness of $\mathfrak{su}(4)$. What remains is $\{R_{\vec e_x},R_{\vec e_y},R_{\vec e_z}\}$: three real dimensions, and that is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.

So the ceiling is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and it is attained by $\mathfrak k_R$. This is exactly the $\mathfrak k_R$ of the earlier section — the commutant theorem and the real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ are the same structure seen from two sides. The ceiling of three forces the tensor product:

$$ (\text{internal }\mathbf 4)\otimes(\text{spinor}), \qquad \mathbf 4_{\text{int}}\cong\mathbb C e_t\oplus B_V . $$

The internal quartet is a formal copy of the carrier: it inherits the split, because the split is canonical, and it does not inherit the Lorentz action, because it must not. $M_4(\mathbb C)$ cannot host two large commuting compact groups, and that is a fact about dimension, not about this model.

One consequence worth stating. The commutant result also says the fifteen contains a distinguished $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ — the one inside the commutant — of rank one inside rank three. When the successor needs a weak factor of rank one, that is the direction it has available, and it is available inside the algebra rather than beside it. The next section of the corpus uses the fact.

The Chiral Wall, Which Is Now a Theorem

Under one group the statement gets simpler and stronger.

What a chiral gauge theory needs. A gauge action that distinguishes left from right at the level of the coupling — an action of the group on one copy of the matter and not on its conjugate.

What this model has. One copy index, ungauged, and one coupling multiplying the fifteen. The gauged content is $2\cdot\mathbf4\oplus2\cdot\bar{\mathbf4}$, vector-like in pairs. A vector-like gauged sector has no chiral gauge coupling at all, and therefore:

The model is not chiral where it gauges. This is not a caveat about a representation; it is a property of the model, proved by the same count that makes its anomaly vanish.

Where the chirality is. Entirely in the copy index, which is a label. The sixteen is complex as a representation of a group that includes the copy index, and not as a representation of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ alone — and that difference is the whole reason a chiral successor is possible: the copy index is what gets gauged at the transition.

What would count as the algebra supplying chirality. A one-sided action of the algebra on a chiral spinor module. That remains the corpus's open problem, unchanged, and it is not solved here. What has changed is that the model no longer pretends to have the answer in a representation: it declares the copy index, and the declaration is honest because the index is visible.

What is refused. Writing the sixteen as a single multiplet of a larger group and calling the chirality derived, when the multiplet is real. A real multiplet cannot be chiral, and the sixteen is complex only because of the copy index.

Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

A subalgebra is not a factor. $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ is six real dimensions inside the fifteen; a factor would be a group multiplying the fields. Rank three against rank five is the difference (The Real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$).

The copy index is not a gauge factor. It labels two copies of the quartet and carries no coupling; it becomes a factor only at the transition (The Sixteen).

Vector-like is not a defect. It is why the anomaly vanishes by counting, and it is why the model has no chiral gauge coupling — the second being what the successor exists to fix (The Chiral Wall).

One coupling is not a simplification of three. It is the absence of the two factors that carried the other two, and it removes the matching problem entirely (One Covariant Derivative).

A theorem about another group is not evidence about this one. The Boyle–Farnsworth result forces a product group in a representation this frame does not use (The Uniqueness Result, Demoted).

Summary

One group. $\mathrm{SU}(4)$, fifteen real dimensions, rank three, one coupling. No product, no second factor, and no rank five anywhere in the model.

The count is fifteen, twice over. Twelve plane arrays and three diagonals; or sixteen cells minus the trace. The split is $8+1+6$: colour, the abelian direction, and the six leptoquark planes.

The sixteen is $2\cdot\mathbf4\oplus2\cdot\bar{\mathbf4}$ — two copies of the quartet and two of its conjugate, with the copy index as a label. Vector-like in pairs, so the anomaly cancels by counting rather than by three traces, and the model is not chiral where it gauges.

The fifteen already contains a real $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ of six dimensions and rank two, spanned by the six antisymmetric planes and equal to $\mathfrak k_L\oplus\mathfrak k_R$. It is the object the old frame read as two weak factors. It is a subalgebra, not a factor, and the condensate puts $32v^{2}$ on three of its planes and $0$ on the other three — which is why the six arrays were never the weak bosons.

The ceiling on a commuting group is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, by the commutant count $8\to4\to3$, and that is why the carrier is a formal copy. The same three dimensions are the rank-one direction the successor will need, and they are inside the algebra rather than beside it.

Nothing numerical moved. The spectrum of $\operatorname{ad}(iD)$, the masses, the sextet's charges and the nine massless directions are exactly as the first-breaking section states them. The frame changed; no eigenvalue did.

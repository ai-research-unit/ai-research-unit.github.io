# __The Schrödinger Action Principle in Biquaternionic Form__

The action principle of the Schrödinger field, written in the biquaternion algebra.

## Summary of Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| $\tilde\Xi$ | The Schrödinger field, one element of $\mathbb{B}$ |
| $\tilde H_\Psi=h_t\,e_t+i\vec h_\Psi$ | Hermitian Hamiltonian symbol |
| $\mathrm{Lag}=L/c$ | Lagrangian per unit $r'_t$ |
| $S_{\mathrm A}$ | The action of this article, with no exponential |
| $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm A}$ | Its real density |
| $r,\ \alpha$ | Scale and central angle of the field; $\rho=2r^{2}$ |
| $\rho,\ \vec j$ | Noether density and current of the central phase rotation |
| $\theta$ | Boundary one-form; the symplectic potential |
| $\Pi_\alpha=-\frac{\hbar c}{2}\rho$ | Momentum conjugate to the phase |
| $H[\rho,\alpha]$ | Polar Hamiltonian, whose equations are the polar pair |
| $Q=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\sqrt\rho/\sqrt\rho$ | Quantum potential |
| $S=\hbar\alpha$, $\vec v=\nabla S/m$ | Action and flow velocity |

## Further Reading

- *The Schrödinger Equation in Biquaternionic Form*, for the equation whose action this is, its solution and its carrier.
- *The Schrödinger Equation in Biquaternionic Polar Variables*, for the word of the field, the two norms, and the pair that A's Hamiltonian reproduces.
- *The Schrödinger Path Integral in Biquaternionic Form*, for the sum over path phases, an independent construction with its own objects.
- *Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Mechanics in Biquaternionic Form*, for the action, the Legendre transform and the phase-space biquaternion in the non-quantum setting.
- *The Symplectic Form and the Biquaternion Norm Cone*, for the symplectic structure that A's boundary term reproduces on the space of fields.
- *Stress–Energy, Conservation Laws and the Field Action in Biquaternionic Form*, for the stress tensor and the field-action conventions used in the last section.
- *The Action Principle and the Classical Limit in Biquaternionic Form*, for the classical action principle and for the stationary-phase statement that belongs to B and not to A.
- P. A. M. Dirac, "The Lagrangian in Quantum Mechanics," *Physikalische Zeitschrift der Sowjetunion* **3** (1933) 64–72. The first-order Lagrangian and the transformation function.
- R. P. Feynman, "Space-Time Approach to Non-Relativistic Quantum Mechanics," *Reviews of Modern Physics* **20** (1948) 367–387. The sum over path phases, for contrast with a variational principle.
- E. Noether, "Invariante Variationsprobleme," *Nachrichten von der Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen* (1918) 235–257. The construction that produces the density and the current from the phase rotation.
- E. Madelung, "Quantentheorie in hydrodynamischer Form," *Zeitschrift für Physik* **40** (1927) 322–326. The amplitude–phase pair whose canonical structure A exhibits.
- J. S. Bell, "The Speakable and Unspeakable in Quantum Mechanics" (Cambridge University Press, 1987), essay 4, for the first-order field action of the Schrödinger field.
- L. D. Faddeev and A. A. Slavnov, *Gauge Fields: An Introduction to Quantum Theory* (Benjamin, 1980), for the phase-space path integral over a first-order action, which is A's exponential and not B.
- P. Lounesto, *Clifford Algebras and Spinors* (Cambridge University Press, 2001). The biquaternion algebra, its conjugations and its matrix representation.

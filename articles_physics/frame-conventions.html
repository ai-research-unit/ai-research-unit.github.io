Frame Conventions

Introduction

In this article a frame means a spacetime frame: a choice of coordinates on the biquaternion space. There are two, the $\mathbb{B}$ frame and the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame, and every display in the series is written in one of them. A reader who mixes the two changes a sign or a factor of $i$ and has no way to see it in the result.

This article fixes the general notion first, then the two coordinate frames. The $\mathbb{B}$ frame is the general frame of the biquaternion universe, and its metric is Euclidean. The $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame is a particular case of it on the material sector, with metric $(-1,1,1,1)$, kept to display familiar equations in their usual form. The article then gives the Clifford map, which translates those directions into $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ and is a dictionary rather than a structure of the algebra, and it closes with the rules that keep them apart.

Each frame is fixed with its metric. The metric is part of the frame, not a separate choice, and the plain weight belongs to the product and is the same in both frames.

The article is read after Algebra and Element Conventions and before Matrix Representations Conventions. It uses no left or right multiplication, no cell and no explicit array.

The order is the order of dependence, and it is also the order of risk. The coordinate frame decides the signs of every interval term and of every square. A mistake in it propagates into every display of the article that follows.

What a Frame Is

A frame is a choice of coordinate, not a part of the object. It is a rule that turns an element into four labelled components, together with a stated law for passing to another such rule. The object is what the rule leaves fixed. The metric is part of the frame, not a choice made after it: each of the two frames carries its interval. The test of a frame statement is that it names the frame and its metric; the test of an invariant statement is that it does not. A coordinate taken from one frame with the metric of the other is an object of neither, and no algebraic check detects it.

The two coordinate frames have names. The names are read $\mathbb{B}$ for biquaternion and $\mathbb{M}_-$ for the material sector. The $\mathbb{B}$ frame is the biquaternion space read in its own complex coordinate: the natural complete frame, with no reality condition and all four directions on the same footing. The $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame is the material real frame, named for the sector it is defined on, reached by $Q_t=ict'$ in physics and by $Q_t=iq'_t$ in mathematics. $Q_\mu$ stays the name of the complex coordinate and $x_\mu$ the name of the real one; the frame name and the coordinate name are separate. The generators carry the frame in brackets and the index low: $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}]$ and $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]$.

The frame and the invariant are read together. "$\gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]^2=-I_4$" is a frame statement: it is true in the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame and false for $\gamma_k[\mathbb{B}]$. "$\{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=-2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]I_4$" is a frame statement, and the value of $g$ is what the frame supplies: the cells square to $-g[\mathbb{M}_-]$ there, and to $+g[\mathbb{B}]$ here. Neither sentence is complete without the other.

A change of frame is a substitution with a law. On the material sector it is $Q_t=ix_0$ and $Q_k=x_k$ for $k=1,2,3$, with the inverse $x_0=-iQ_t$ and $x_k=Q_k$. A display is read only in the frame that produced it, and the substitution is written wherever a display passes from one frame to the other.

The $\mathbb{B}$ Frame

The coordinate is complex. The $\mathbb{B}$ frame is the biquaternion space read in its own complex coordinate: the natural complete frame. It reads the four coefficients of the element and imposes no reality condition, $$ \tilde Q=Q_t\,e_t+Q_x\,\vec e_x+Q_y\,\vec e_y+Q_z\,\vec e_z,\qquad Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}. $$ This is the general case. The two real sectors are conditions on these four coefficients, not other frames.

The interval has four $+1$ coefficients. With the natural conjugation $\natural$, $$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_\natural=\tilde Q\tilde Q^\natural=Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2 . $$ Every coefficient is $+1$, and the value is complex in general. The tuple is a coefficient tuple, not a real signature; a signature appears only when a sector is imposed. On $\mathbb{M}_-$, with $Q_t=ict'$, the value is $-c^2t'^2+x^2+y^2+z^2$; on $\mathbb{M}_+$, with $Q_t=ct$ and $Q_k=ix'_k$, it is $c^2t^2-x'^2-y'^2-z'^2$. The frame carries the metric $(1,1,1,1)$ as part of the choice, and on $\mathbb{M}_-$ the interval is $+g_{\mu\nu}Q_\mu Q_\nu$ with that $g$.

The general frame, and its form is Euclidean. The $\mathbb{B}$ frame is the frame of the biquaternion universe itself, not of one sector of it; the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame is this one read on a sector. Its form is $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$, with all four coefficients $+1$. On real coefficients that is the ordinary Euclidean metric: positive definite, with no direction singled out by a sign. The coefficients are complex in general, so the form is the complexified Euclidean form; a real signature appears only when a sector is imposed. A Lorentzian signature is not a property of this frame.

The four generators all square to $+I_4$. In this frame the Clifford metric is the identity, $g_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}$, so $$ (\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}])^2=+I_4,\qquad \{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{B}]\}=2\delta_{\mu\nu}I_4 . $$ The four squares have one sign, and no generator is singled out by the sign of its square. The explicit arrays are the ones of Matrix Representations Conventions.

The material case. On $\mathbb{M}_-$ the coefficients are $$ Q_t=ict',\qquad Q_x=q_x,\qquad Q_y=q_y,\qquad Q_z=q_z , $$ with $t',q_x,q_y,q_z$ real and $c$ the local speed of light. This is the coordinate the physics corpus writes by default. It is a sector condition on the $\mathbb{B}$ frame, not the definition of the frame.

The $\mathbb{M}_-$ Frame

The coordinate. The $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame is the material coordinate, defined on $\mathbb{M}_-$, $$ x_0=ct',\qquad x_1=q_x,\qquad x_2=q_y,\qquad x_3=q_z , $$ with $t',q_k$ real. On the material case $Q_t=ix_0$ and $Q_k=x_k$ for $k=1,2,3$: one substitution, a rotation of the time axis by $i$.

A particular case, kept for convenience. The $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame is the $\mathbb{B}$ frame restricted to $\mathbb{M}_-$, not a second universe and not a second algebra. It is defined only after the coefficients have been put on that sector, and its one change is the time coordinate: $Q_t=ict'$ becomes the real $x_0=ct'$, so a display writes $t'$ with no $i$ attached. The $i$ does not disappear; its square becomes the sign of the time term, $i^2=-1$, and the metric reads $(-1,1,1,1)$ with real coefficients. That metric is not chosen a second time: it is the Euclidean metric after the substitution, and $\Lambda^{\mathsf T}g[\mathbb{B}]\Lambda=g[\mathbb{M}_-]$ records it. The purpose of the frame is display: to write familiar equations in their familiar shape, with a real time and a Minkowski metric. Nothing is added to the content, and the same equation is read in the notation of the textbooks.

The metric is $(-1,1,1,1)$, and it is the interval itself. On $\mathbb{M}_-$ the same interval reads $$ -x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2 , $$ with coefficients $(-1,1,1,1)$, the negative of the Clifford metric. The sign is now explicit in the coordinate. The interval is the metric up to one overall sign: $-x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2=g_{\mu\nu}x_\mu x_\nu$ with $g=(-1,1,1,1)$, and the generators of the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame realise exactly that $g$. The frame fixes the tuple; it does not offer a choice of tuple. Read on $\mathbb{M}_-$, this is the "metric of the two real sectors" of the series, not a third frame metric; on $\mathbb{M}_+$ the same form is read on the informational coordinate.

The squares split. In this frame $$ (\gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-])^2=+I_4,\qquad (\gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-])^2=-I_4,\qquad \{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=-2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]I_4 , $$ with $g=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$. One generator is singled out by its sign, and that generator is the time direction.

The Two Frames Side by Side

Each column uses its own coordinate; the $\mathbb{B}$ column is the general frame, and the material case is named inside it.

Each frame is one tuple, and the cells of the frame square to it. In the $\mathbb{B}$ frame the interval and the metric are the same tuple, $(1,1,1,1)$, and the four cells square to it. In the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame the interval and the metric are again the same tuple, $(-1,1,1,1)$; the cells square to the opposite, $(1,-1,-1,-1)$, because the $i$ of $Q_t=ix_0$ has left the generator and become the sign of the time slot, $i^2=-1$. The plain weight $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$ is the square of the units, the same in both frames, and it is not the metric of either. A reader who carries a tuple from one frame to the other carries the wrong one, and the result is a sign that no algebra check will catch.

The $i$ is never put into the metric. The coefficients above are real in both frames. What changes is which coordinate carries the $i$: on the material case it is $Q_t$, in the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame it is none. A metric with an $i$ in it is not a metric of this series.

The substitution is the material one. $Q_t=ict'$ is made on $\mathbb{M}_-$. The informational sector has real time and imaginary space, $\tilde Q_{\mathrm H}=ct\,e_t+i(x'\vec e_x+y'\vec e_y+z'\vec e_z)$, and it is not obtained by the same substitution. An article that writes both sectors states which coordinate belongs to which.

The Clifford Map

A dictionary, not a map of the biquaternion environment. The Clifford map is not a structure of $\mathbb{B}$. Its source is not the algebra and its target is not the algebra: the source is the four directions of one chosen frame, and the target is $M_4(\mathbb{C})$. Nothing in the multiplication of $\mathbb{B}$ produces it. It is kept here for one reason: the physics papers are written in gamma matrices, and a reader who comes from them needs the translation. It is cited, not assumed; a display that uses it declares it.

What the framework does instead is change the frame. Physics is studied here by choosing a coordinate frame, not by choosing matrices. In the general case the frame is $\mathbb{B}$ itself, the four coefficients $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$ with the interval $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$. In the material sector the frame is the real frame of $\mathbb{M}_-$, reached by $Q_t=ix_0$, and that change of frame carries the physics. The map translates the change into matrices; it does not produce it.

The map and its values. The two coordinate frames give four directions each, and the Clifford map sends them into $M_4(\mathbb{C})$: $$ \mathsf{K}_{\mathbb{B}}(e_\mu)=\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}],\qquad \mathsf{K}_{\mathbb{M}_-}(e_\mu)=\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]. $$ Its values are fixed by the anticommutator in each frame, $$ \{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{B}]\}=2\delta_{\mu\nu}I_4,\qquad \{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=-2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]I_4, $$ and it is linear over the coefficient field of its frame: complex for $\mathbb{B}$, real in the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame. Earlier versions called the five images the gamma frame; this article names the map the Clifford map, because the object is a map and not a frame, and calls the five the gamma set. The letter $\gamma$ stays for the four images.

The image is four-dimensional and not closed. Four elements span four dimensions, and $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ has sixteen. A product of two distinct images is a bivector, and it leaves the image. The image is a subspace, not a subalgebra, and not a basis: the basis is the sixteen graded products, $1+4+6+4+1$. Writing the four as "the basis" hides both the count and the place each product lands.

The metric is read from the map. The Clifford metric is read from the anticommutator, equivalently $4g_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathsf{K}(e_\mu)\mathsf{K}(e_\nu)\bigr)$ in the defining representation. The map carries no metric of its own: the frame supplies it, and the same algebra carries both tuples.

The change of base is one matrix. On the material sector the change of frame is $Q_t=ix_0$; read on the generators it is $$ \gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]=\sum_\nu\Lambda_{\mu\nu}\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{B}],\qquad \Lambda=\Lambda_{\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{M}_-}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-i,-i,-i). $$ The matrix is the image of the substitution, not a second convention. The metric follows from it, $$ \Lambda\,g[\mathbb{B}]\,\Lambda^{\mathsf T}=g[\mathbb{M}_-],\qquad g[\mathbb{B}]=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,1),\quad g[\mathbb{M}_-]=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1). $$ The frame label is written in brackets, as it is on the generators, so no spacetime index is raised. A display that changes frame writes $\Lambda_{\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{M}_-}$ once; it does not choose a second metric by hand.

The fifth is a product, not a direction. In each frame $\gamma_5$ is the product $i\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$ of that frame's four images, so it is fixed by them and is not a fifth image of the map. In the $\mathbb{B}$ frame the fifth is $-i$ times the fifth of the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame, so its square is $-I_4$ there and $+I_4$ in the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame, while the volume element $\Pi=\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$ of that frame has the opposite sign in each: $+I_4$ in the $\mathbb{B}$ frame and $-I_4$ in the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame.

Which map is displayed. The physics articles display $\mathsf{K}_{\mathbb{M}_-}$ by default, because the coordinate there is real. An article that displays $\mathsf{K}_{\mathbb{B}}$ says so at the display. The two are never mixed in one display.

The Rules

Eight rules follow.

  1. Declare the frame before the first display. A formula that does not name its frame is read in the article's declared default, and an article declares one default only.
  2. One product, one frame. A product is read in one frame: the generators carry that frame in brackets, $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}]$ or $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]$, and one generator of each frame is never written in the same product.
  3. The gamma index is low, and the frame is in brackets. $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}]$, $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]$. The index labels which direction is mapped; it is not a tensor slot. No label is raised, and no generator is written without its frame outside a declared default.
  4. Name the metric with its frame. The coordinate frame carries its interval. A statement that names a frame names the metric it carries; a metric is never moved from one frame to another.
  5. The $i$ is in the coordinate or in the signs, never in the metric. The coefficients of both frame metrics are real.
  6. $Q_t=ict'$ is material. The substitution is made on $\mathbb{M}_-$ and is not carried to $\mathbb{M}_+$ without saying so.
  7. A change of frame is a written substitution. It has a law, $Q_t=ix_0$ in the coordinates or $\Lambda_{\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{M}_-}$ on the generators, and the article gives it. A display is read only in the frame that produced it.
  8. The interval is a norm, not a metric. The interval of an element is squared by multiplying it by its conjugate, $\langle\tilde X\rangle_\natural=\tilde X\tilde X^\natural$. In the $\mathbb{B}$ frame this is the plain sum $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ and the metric is $(1,1,1,1)$. In the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame the interval is $x_0^2-\vec x^2$ and the metric is $(-1,1,1,1)$: one sign for the one $i$ of $Q_t=ix_0$, which is $i^2=-1$ on the time slot. A two-index display of the interval carries the minus of that frame, $-x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2=\sum_{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]x_\mu x_\nu$, and the cells of the frame square to the opposite array, $\{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=-2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]I_4$. The letter $\eta$ is never the metric.

The plain weight is not a frame metric. $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$ follows from the multiplication of the units, $\mathrm{Sc}(e_t^2)=+1$ and $\mathrm{Sc}(\vec e_k^2)=-1$. It belongs to the product, it is the same in both frames, and no frame changes it or raises it.

The calculus is not a frame rule. It belongs to Operator and Calculus Conventions. The frame supplies the coordinate, the interval and the metric, and the index stays low in both frames.

Summary

The word frame here means a spacetime frame: a choice of coordinates on the biquaternion space. There are two, $\mathbb{B}$ and $\mathbb{M}_-$, and each carries its metric as part of the choice.

The $\mathbb{B}$ frame is the biquaternion space read in its own coordinate, $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$: the natural complete frame, the general frame of the biquaternion universe, with interval coefficients $(1,1,1,1)$, the Euclidean form, every generator squaring to $+I_4$, and no reality condition. The material case is $Q_t=ict'$, $Q_k=q_k$, and there the interval is the Minkowski one. The $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame is a particular case of it, kept for convenience: the material real frame, $x_0=ct'$ on $\mathbb{M}_-$ with metric $(-1,1,1,1)$; the time cell is the one with the positive square. On the material case the two differ by $Q_t=ix_0$.

The metric is carried by the frame, and it is the tuple of the interval. The $\mathbb{B}$ frame carries $(1,1,1,1)$, $Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2$. The $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame carries $(-1,1,1,1)$, $-x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2$. The two differ by the one sign of $Q_t=ix_0$, which is $i^2=-1$ on the time slot. The plain weight $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$ is the exception: it belongs to the product and is the same in both frames. Naming the frame and its metric together is what keeps a sign from moving silently.

The Clifford map carries the directions, not a basis. It sends the four directions of a frame into $M_4(\mathbb{C})$; its image is four-dimensional and not closed, and its products leave it. It is a dictionary for the physics literature, not a map of $\mathbb{B}$: the framework studies physics by the change of frame, $\mathbb{B}$ in the general case and the real frame of $\mathbb{M}_-$ in the material case. The base change is the one matrix $\Lambda_{\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{M}_-}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-i,-i,-i)$, the image of $Q_t=ix_0$ on the generators, and the metric transforms with it.

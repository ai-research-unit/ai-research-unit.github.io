Idempotents in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$

Introduction

An idempotent of a product is an element equal to its own square, $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$. The equation is the algebraic form of a projection, and in this framework a projection is a state: an operation that, performed twice, gives the same result as once. The reading is the corpus's own and it is stated in The Monoid of Acting Maps: the Process Is the Multiplication, the State Is the Idempotent: a process is a multiplication and a determinate state is an idempotent. The idempotent sets of the twelve structures are therefore the state sets of the twelve bands, and the twelve answers differ in a way that matters physically. This article is the fifth physics companion of the mathematics comparison group, and its mathematics companion is Idempotents in the 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space (articles_maths/idempotents-in-the-12-algebraic-structures-over-the-biquaternion-c-space.md).

The mathematics companion proves that only five of the twelve structures carry an idempotent other than $0$ and $e_0$: the general plain structure $\mathrm{GPA}$, its symmetrisation $\mathrm{SPA}$, the general plain sesquilinear structure $\mathrm{GPS}$, and the general and symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{GQS}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$. It proves further that the idempotents of $\mathrm{GPA}$ are $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $-1$, a four-parameter family; that the idempotents of $\mathrm{GPS}$ are the Hermitian subfamily $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ over the real unit vectors; and that the idempotents of $\mathrm{GQS}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$ are the family $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$ over the real vectors $\mu$ with $(\mu,\mu)=\tfrac34$. It proves finally that the central square separates the families: it vanishes on the plain and the Hermitian idempotents, so those are zero divisors of the algebra, and it equals $e_0$ on the family of the fourth product, so those are units.

Those three facts are three physical statements, and the article reads them.

  • The plain family is the state family of the composition: every local complex structure, every phase, gives a projector. Its Hermitian subfamily is the family of pure states, and its complementation pairs the two outcomes of a complete measurement.
  • The Hermitian family is the pure-state family of the probability band. Its projectors are exactly the density matrices of the Bloch ball, in The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone, and The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent reads the vacuum as a minimal member.
  • The fourth family is the one family of idempotents that are units. The states of the probability band are null for the composition, and the states of the gauge band are invertible: the contrast is the physical content of the central-square theorem, and the owner is Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone together with The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric.

Conventions. We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_k^{2}=-e_0$; $i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The central square is $C(\tilde\Pi)e_0=\tilde\Pi^{\natural} \tilde\Pi$ with $C(\tilde\Pi)=\sum_\mu\Pi_\mu^{2}$, and the criterion of invertibility is $C\neq0$. The remarkable subspaces are the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$, the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, the quaternion and anti-quaternion subspaces $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, and the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces $\mathbb{M}_+$ (informational sector) and $\mathbb{M}_-$ (material sector). A minimal idempotent is one that is nonzero and cannot be written as the sum of two nonzero orthogonal idempotents.

The Idempotent as a State

The equation and the projection. An idempotent satisfies $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ in the plain product, and in a general product the same equation is read in that product. The three invariants of an idempotent are its central square, which decides whether it is a unit or a zero divisor; its complement $e_0-\tilde\Pi$, which is idempotent whenever the family is closed under the map and which is orthogonal to it, $\tilde\Pi(e_0-\tilde\Pi)=0$; and the subspace in which it lies.

The physical reading. A projection is a yes-no measurement, and an idempotent is the outcome of that measurement: performing the measurement twice gives the same outcome as performing it once. The equation $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ is therefore the algebraic form of a determinate state, and the nontrivial idempotents are the determinate states beside the two trivial ones. The reading is the corpus's, and it is the reason the idempotents are the objects that connect the algebra to the state space: the processes are the multiplications and the states are the idempotents, in The Monoid of Acting Maps: the Process Is the Multiplication, the State Is the Idempotent.

The trivial pair. The origin is an idempotent of every structure, the product being linear, and the unit $e_0$ is an idempotent of the eight structures that are not antisymmetric. Physically $0$ is the impossible outcome and $e_0$ is the trivial measurement, the one that keeps every state; the pair is the degenerate end of the state set.

The Plain Family: Every Phase Gives a State

The bijection. The idempotents of the plain product are $0$, $e_0$ and the elements $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $-1$, and the map $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ is a bijection from the roots of $-1$ onto the idempotents. The classification of the roots, read in Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$, splits the family in three: the trivial pair from $\pm i$, the Hermitian family $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ over the real unit vectors, and the four-parameter non-trivial family from the complex roots, which lies in none of the four four-dimensional subspaces.

The physical reading. A local complex structure is a phase, and the bijection says that every phase gives a state. The state family of the composition is therefore as large as the family of phases: the four parameters of the non-trivial idempotents are the four real parameters of the complex root, and the Hermitian subfamily is the part of the family the probability band keeps. The plain idempotents are the idempotents of the algebra, and they are the states that the composition can prepare; the owner of the algebra's idempotents and of their parametrisation is The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent, and the corpus reads the associated splitting of the algebra in Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Algebra (mathematics). A Peirce decomposition of the algebra by an idempotent is a splitting of the states into two orthogonal families, and it requires associativity; this is the algebraic reason the plain product is the band whose states build the whole algebra.

The symmetrisation keeps the family. The symmetric plain algebra has the plain square, $\tilde\Pi\bullet\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi^{2}$, so its idempotent set is the plain one element for element. The physical reading is that the symmetric composition prepares the same states as the composition; the symmetrisation changes the process, not the states. The pure members of the family are zero divisors of the symmetrised block, because the vector subspace is its zero-divisor set, so a state that the symmetrisation prepares is a state the symmetrisation cannot invert: the owner is The Cone and the Massless Particles in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$.

The Hermitian Family: the Pure States and the Bloch Ball

The family. The idempotents of the general plain sesquilinear product are $0$, $e_0$ and the Hermitian family $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ over the real unit vectors $\hat\mu$, a two-parameter family lying in the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$. The owner is Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra, and the physics is Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone.

The physical reading. The Hermitian idempotents are the pure states of the probability band. The element $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ has a real scalar part and a purely imaginary vector part, which is the coefficient condition of the informational sector, and the map $\hat\mu\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ sends the real unit sphere onto the family. The corpus reads the family as the extremal points of the positive cone of the sesquilgebra: the idempotents sit on the boundary of the cone and are the states the pairing cannot write as a mixture of two others. The Bloch ball, the trace-one slice of the future light cone, is the convex body they generate, and its extremal points are exactly this family; the owner is The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone. The vacuum is the minimal member, and a pure state is minimal because it has no orthogonal part: the complementation $\hat\mu\mapsto-\hat\mu$ pairs it with the state of the opposite direction, and the pair is a complete two-outcome measurement.

The symmetry of the band keeps the pair. The symmetric plain sesquilinear product has a scalar-valued square, so its only idempotents are $0$ and $e_0$: the symmetrisation of the probability form loses all the nontrivial pure states. The physical reading is that the symmetrised pairing forgets the direction of the state and keeps only its normalisation, the reduction the companion article records for the root of $+1$. The pure state exists in the general pairing and not in its symmetrisation.

The Gauge Family: the Invertible States

The family. The idempotents of the general quaternionic sesquilinear product are $0$, $e_0$ and the family $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$ over the real vectors $\mu$ with $(\mu,\mu)=\tfrac34$, a two-parameter family lying in the quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$. The symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear structure has the same family. The owner is Idempotents of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

The physical reading. The family is the state family of the gauge band, and it is the only nontrivial family of the twelve whose members are units of the algebra. The element $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$ has a real scalar part $-\tfrac12$ and a real vector part of squared length $\tfrac34$, so its central square is $\tfrac14+\tfrac34=e_0$ and it is invertible: the state can be undone. The contrast with the probability band is exact and is the physics of the central-square theorem. The pure state $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ has central square $0$ and is a zero divisor: the state of the band that measures probability is null for the composition. The gauge state $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$ has central square $e_0$ and is a unit: the state of the band whose value is an interval is invertible. The reading is that a determinate outcome of a measurement is not a reversible process, while a determinate state of the gauge band is; the owner of the indefinite metric is The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric, and the null states beside it are The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise.

The complementation fails. For the gauge family the map $\mu\mapsto-\mu$ sends $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$ to $\tfrac12e_0-\mu$, which is not a member, so the family is not closed under the complementation $e_0-\tilde\Pi$ and the two-outcome measurement of the plain family has no analogue there. The two-parameter family of the gauge band has a centre offset from the origin, while the Hermitian family is centred at $\tfrac12e_0$: the two families are two spheres of different centre and different radius. The mathematics companion reads the difference, and the physical reading is that the gauge band's state set is not a convex mixture body of the probability kind; its members are invertible four-vectors of a fixed length.

Which Structures Carry a Nontrivial State

Five of the twelve structures carry an idempotent other than $0$ and $e_0$, and each absence is a physical statement.

  • The four antisymmetric structures have the origin alone. The two bilinear brackets have an identically vanishing square, and the two sesquilinear brackets have a purely imaginary pure vector for a square, which forces the origin. Physically a bracket is an infinitesimal transformation, and an infinitesimal transformation has no determinate state: the bracket of an element with itself vanishes, so it can never equal the element. The owner is The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket, and the complex witness of the same absence is A Bracket Invisible on the Real Forms: the Complex Witness of the Jacobi Failure.
  • The natural product and its symmetrisation have the trivial pair alone. Their square is the central square, so an idempotent is central and the equation leaves $0$ and $e_0$. Physically the band whose square is the interval produces no determinate state beyond the trivial ones: a state is not an interval.
  • The symmetric quaternionic algebra has the trivial pair alone, for the same reason, its product being central-valued.
  • The five structures with a nontrivial state are the two plain ones, the general plain sesquilinear one, and the two quaternionic sesquilinear ones. Physically the states live in the composition band and in the two bands of the pairing, and the band of the interval produces none.

The Twelve Structures at the Idempotents

The idempotent equation is an equation on the square operator, so the twelve idempotent sets are read from the twelve squares. The twelve are read one by one below, each naming its product, its square and its idempotent set, before the table collects the twelve states in one place.

$\mathrm{GPA}$, the general plain algebra — composition

The product is the plain product and its square is the plain square. The idempotents are $0$, $e_0$ and the family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $-e_0$; the map is a bijection from the roots of $-e_0$ onto the states, so every phase gives a state. The nonzero idempotents lie on the cone and are zero divisors, which is the algebraic form of the statement that a measurement has no inverse. The reading is The Monoid of Acting Maps: the Process Is the Multiplication, the State Is the Idempotent and Square Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell.

$\mathrm{SPA}$, the symmetric plain algebra — commutativity

The product is the Jordan product and its square is the plain square, so the idempotent set is that of $\mathrm{GPA}$ element for element. The symmetrised composition prepares the same states, and the reading is Observables as a Jordan Algebra: the Symmetric Plain Product and the Classical Composition.

$\mathrm{APA}$, the antisymmetric plain algebra — rotation

The product is the commutator, alternating, so its square vanishes identically and the idempotent equation forces the origin. An infinitesimal transformation has no state. The reading is The Cross Product as a Lie Bracket: Rotations and the Jacobi Identity.

$\mathrm{GQA}$, the general quaternionic algebra — causality

The product is the natural product and its square is the central element $C(\tilde\Pi)e_0$, so an idempotent is central and the equation on the centre leaves only $0$ and $e_0$. The band of the interval carries no nontrivial state. The reading is The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition.

$\mathrm{SQA}$, the symmetric quaternionic algebra — coupling

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation, whose square is $C(\tilde\Pi)e_0$, so the idempotent set is the trivial pair, as for $\mathrm{GQA}$. A central coupling returns a number that cannot be a state. The reading is The Quaternion Form as a Product: the Scalar Coupling of Two Material Operations.

$\mathrm{AQA}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra — obstruction

The product is the antisymmetrisation, alternating, so its square vanishes identically and the idempotent set is the origin alone. The reading is Boosts, Mixed Terms and the Missing Lie Structure.

$\mathrm{GPS}$, the general plain sesqualgebra — probability

The product is the pairing and its square is the Hermitian square. The idempotents are $0$, $e_0$ and the Hermitian family $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ over the real unit vectors $\hat\mu$, which lies in the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$. Physically these are the pure states, the boundary of the positive cone and the extremal points of the Bloch ball, and they are zero divisors because a projector has no inverse. The reading is Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone and The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone.

$\mathrm{SPS}$, the symmetric plain sesqualgebra — positivity

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation, so an idempotent is central and the equation on the centre, $q=\lvert q\rvert^{2}$, leaves the trivial pair. The symmetrised pairing keeps only the trivial states. The reading is The Hermitian Form as a Product: Positivity and the Real Part of the Born Pairing.

$\mathrm{APS}$, the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra — phase

The product is the antisymmetrisation and its square is a purely imaginary pure vector, so an idempotent has vanishing scalar part and the cross-product term forces the origin. The idempotent set is the origin alone. The reading is The Imaginary Part of the Born Pairing: the Antisymmetric Sesquilinear Product.

$\mathrm{GQS}$, the general quaternionic sesqualgebra — gauge

The product is the fourth product. The idempotents are $0$, $e_0$ and the family $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$ over the real vectors $\mu$ with $(\mu,\mu)=\tfrac34$, a family of units lying in the real-quaternion subspace. These are the invertible states of the gauge band, the only nontrivial idempotents of the twelve that are units, which is the contrast with the null pure state of the composition. The reading is The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric and The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise.

$\mathrm{SQS}$, the symmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — metric

The product is the symmetric part of the fourth product, and its square is the coefficientwise real part of $\tilde\Pi^{\natural}\tilde\Pi^{*}$, a real element; the idempotent equation therefore forces $\tilde\Pi$ real and reduces to $(\tilde\Pi^{\natural})^{2}=\tilde\Pi$, whose set is the family $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$, the same as for $\mathrm{GQS}$. The reading is The Gauge Metric as a Product: the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and Values in No Subspace: Why the Symmetric Gauge Product Is Not a State.

$\mathrm{AQS}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — polarisation

The product is the antisymmetric part of the fourth product and its square is a purely imaginary pure vector, so the idempotent set is the origin alone. The reading is The Cross Product of a Vector with Its Conjugate: the Antisymmetric Gauge Product.

The Complementation and the Complete Measurement

For the plain product the map $\tilde\Pi\mapsto e_0-\tilde\Pi$ is the complementation: the complement is idempotent, the two are orthogonal, $\tilde\Pi(e_0-\tilde\Pi)=0$, and they sum to $e_0$. The map is the antipode of the parameter set, $\tilde\Xi\mapsto-\tilde\Xi$ for the plain family and $\hat\mu\mapsto-\hat\mu$ for the Hermitian subfamily. The physical reading is a complete measurement: the pair $\{\tilde\Pi,e_0-\tilde\Pi\}$ is a two-outcome measurement whose outcomes exhaust the identity, and the orthogonality is the statement that the two outcomes are mutually exclusive. The spectral decomposition of an idempotent is the Peirce splitting $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi\oplus \mathbb{B}(e_0-\tilde\Pi)$, and the physical reading is the splitting of the state space into the two outcome eigenspaces. For the plain band the complementation pairs the whole family, the four-parameter part included; for the probability band it pairs the Hermitian family; it fails for the gauge family, which is not closed under it, and is the exchange of the trivial pair for the natural product.

The pairing of the Hermitian family under $\hat\mu\mapsto-\hat\mu$ is the corpus's antipodal pure state, and it is the algebraic form of the two outcomes of a spin measurement along a direction: the two states $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ and $\tfrac12(e_0-i\hat\mu)$ are the two eigenstates, and the operator that separates them is the direction $\hat\mu$ itself, a root of $-1$ read as an observable. The owner of the observable face is Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action, and the complement is the same statement read on the identity.

The States in the Physics Corpus

The idempotents are the states, and the corpus has already written the biography of a state: it is a point of a ball, it is deformed by decoherence, it is the outcome of a measurement, it is a vector of a GNS space, and one of its minimal members is the vacuum. The readings below are those articles, restated for the idempotent table.

A state is a point of the Bloch ball. The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone identifies the state space with the trace-one slice of the cone, a ball whose sphere is the pure states, and the idempotent condition is the boundary condition $\lvert\mathbf r\rvert=1$. The Generalized Bloch Ball for Spin 1 in the Biquaternion Framework extends the picture and marks the ceiling of the framework; the lesson for the idempotent table is that each structure cuts a convex body of states, and the Hermitian family is the one whose body has been named.

Decoherence moves a state off the idempotent family. Decoherence as Idempotent Projection shows that a pure state is an idempotent and that the dephasing channel deforms it into a positive trace-one element that is not idempotent, the deviation being the scalar $(\lvert\mathbf r\rvert^{2}-1)/4$. The idempotent family is therefore the pure end of the state family, and an idempotent is a state that has not decohered. The Jordan idempotent is the algebra's name for the undivided state, and the mixed states of the ball are exactly the states that are not idempotents.

A measurement has idempotents for outcomes. The Measurement Problem in Algebraic Form and Quantum Channels and the Reversible/Irreversible Dichotomy read the sandwich $\tilde\rho\mapsto\tilde P\tilde\rho\tilde P$ as the projective measurement, with the idempotent $\tilde P$ one outcome and the complement $e_0-\tilde P$ the other. The complementation of this article is the algebra's two-outcome event: the pair $(\tilde P,e_0-\tilde P)$ is a complete measurement, and the failure of the complementation for the gauge family says that a gauge idempotent does not by itself define a two-outcome test. POVMs and the Positive Cone in Biquaternionic Form places the idempotents at the extreme points of the positive cone, so the projective measurement is the sharp end of the general measurement.

An idempotent is also a basis vector of a composite. The Bell Basis as the Idempotent Basis of $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ uses the idempotents of the tensor square as a basis of the two-qubit space: the four Bell projectors are idempotent, orthogonal and complete, and they diagonalise the maximally entangled states. The idempotent therefore does two jobs at once, as a pure state of one system and as a basis element of the correlations of two, and the tensor-product article is where the two jobs are visibly the same.

The vacuum is a minimal idempotent. The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent identifies the one-mode vacuum with the minimal idempotent $\tilde\Pi_1=\tfrac12(e_0+ie_3)$, of trace one, which generates the minimal left ideal that is the one-particle module. Three properties of the table meet in it: it is a state, so it is Hermitian; it is minimal, so it is an extreme point of the state set; and it is a zero divisor, so it is in the non-pure family of the cone. The vacuum-manifold observation of that article — every unit vector $\hat\mu$ gives a vacuum and the algebra singles none out — is the statement that the table has a sphere of vacua and no preferred point, which is the framework's standing disclaimer about selection. The GNS Construction in the Biquaternion Framework recovers the same idempotent from the state's null space, so the minimal idempotent, the vacuum vector and the one-particle module are three descriptions of one object.

Entropy, Coarse-Graining and the Vacuum Energy

The idempotents are the pure states, and the corpus has a second, quantitative way of saying what that means: the idempotents are exactly the states of zero entropy, and every other state lies at a definite distance from them. The same section collects the further objects the pure states carry: the vacuum energy, the coarse-graining maps and the polarisation sphere.

The deviation from idempotency is the entropy coordinate. Von Neumann Entropy and the Biquaternion Norm and Coarse-Graining and the Biquaternion Entropy Functional compute the deviation from idempotency of a state $\tilde\rho=\tfrac12(e_0+i\mathbf r)$ and find it to be a single scalar,

$$ \mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\rho^{2}-\tilde\rho\bigr)=\tfrac14\bigl(\lvert\mathbf r\rvert^{2}-1\bigr) =-N(\tilde\rho), $$

so that purity, idempotency and the vanishing of the biquaternion norm are three interpretations of one number. The entropy is a strictly monotone function of that number: it vanishes on the idempotents, is maximal at the centre of the ball, and leaves the idempotent family with infinite slope, because the logarithm of a state is singular exactly where the state is idempotent. The idempotent family is therefore the zero-entropy boundary of the state space, and the entropy is the coordinate that measures the distance from it. This is the sharpest statement of what an idempotent is physically: a determinate state is a state of no missing information, and it is an idempotent because repeating a determinate measurement changes nothing.

A coarse-graining is itself an idempotent map. The coarse-grainings of Coarse-Graining and the Biquaternion Entropy Functional are the trace-preserving, completely positive, idempotent maps $\Phi$ with $\Phi^{2}=\Phi$ onto a commutative subalgebra, of which the fully dephasing channel is the example. Idempotency therefore appears twice in the framework, at two levels: an idempotent element is a pure state, and an idempotent map is a coarse-graining. Both satisfy the same equation $\Pi^{2}=\Pi$, and the two are related by the fact that a coarse-graining contracts the Bloch ball toward its centre, moving every state off the idempotent family and increasing the entropy. The second law of the sector is the statement that the image of a state under a coarse-graining is never closer to the idempotent family than the state itself.

The idempotents contain the perfectly distinguishable pairs. Relative Entropy and the Biquaternion Framework develops the Umegaki quantity $S(\rho\|\sigma)$ and its monotonicity under every physical process. Two orthogonal idempotents are separated with certainty by a single projective measurement, and their relative entropy diverges, because neither lies in the support of the other; two non-orthogonal idempotents are separated only probabilistically, and a pair in which at least one member is mixed is separated no better. The idempotent family therefore contains the pairs a single measurement can tell apart, and the ball around it is where the distinction is only statistical.

The idempotents are the perfectly resolvable states. The entropy says how much information a state lacks; Fisher Information and the Biquaternion Norm says how sharply a state can be told from its neighbours, and the two readings agree through a reciprocity. The classical Fisher information of the state's spectral family, with respect to the Bloch radius, is the reciprocal of the biquaternion norm,

$$ g_{rr}=\frac{1}{1-|\mathbf{r}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\,\mathrm{Sc}\,N(\tilde\rho)} . $$

It is minimal at the maximally mixed state, where the norm is largest, and it diverges on the boundary, where the norm vanishes. By the Cramér–Rao bound a divergent information means asymptotic exactness, so the idempotents are exactly the states a displacement of which can be detected with arbitrarily high precision. The Fisher article names the coincidence: the analytic singularity of the logarithm, the vanishing of the biquaternion norm and the divergence of the Fisher information are three descriptions of the same locus. The idempotent is therefore overdetermined in the corpus — the pure state, the zero-entropy state, the zero-divisor state and the perfectly resolvable state are four names for one element.

The vacuum idempotent carries the zero-point energy. The Vacuum State and the Casimir Effect in Biquaternionic Form identifies the vacuum with the one-mode state of no particles, which is the minimal idempotent $\tilde\Pi_1=\tfrac12(e_0+ie_3)$ of The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent, and records that the state costs half a quantum of energy per mode. The idempotent is the state of the field when nothing is excited, and the normal-ordering prescription removes the absolute sum while leaving the difference produced by boundary conditions: the Casimir energy. The idempotent therefore carries a physical energy that is not read off its own algebra — the half-quantum per mode and the cutoff are standard-theory data — and the framework's role is to house the state and the indefinite metric that fixes the count of modes.

Idempotent states are spin-$\tfrac12$, and a scalar escapes them. The idempotent-as-state reading has a scope, and The Scalar Field in the Centre: Why Spin 0 Escapes the Biquaternion State Module fixes it. The framework's states are elements of the module $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi\cong\mathbb{C}^2$, which carries the defining two-dimensional representation, so they are spinor-like by construction; a spin-$0$ field, being a single component transforming in the trivial representation, has no place in it. The article gives the reason and it is a module-theoretic one: the state module is the defining module of the matrix algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, a simple algebra has only one kind of module, and every finite-dimensional module is a direct sum of copies of $\mathbb{C}^2$, so no one-dimensional module exists for the trivial representation to occupy. The scalar lives instead in the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\{Q_0e_0\}$, which is not a module at all but the rotationally invariant subalgebra, the fixed space of the rotation subgroup. The idempotents of this article are therefore states of the spin-$\tfrac12$ module only: the Bloch sphere, the polarisation sphere and the state cone are the same object because the state is spinorial, and a scalar field is described by the centre's complex line rather than by any idempotent of $\mathbb{M}_+$.

The sphere of pure states. The idempotents of the probability band are the boundary sphere of the Bloch ball, and Pancharatnam's Phase and the Polarization Sphere in Biquaternionic Form reads the same sphere as the polarisation sphere: a fully polarised wave is a point of the sphere, and a partially polarised one is a point of the ball. The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone completes the pair of readings, so one object — the sphere of idempotents of $\mathbb{M}_+$ — is at once the sphere of pure qubit states, the sphere of pure polarisations and the boundary of the state cone. Idempotency is the algebraic name of the sphere.

The idempotents are the sharp effects, and a general measurement softens them. POVMs and the Positive Cone in Biquaternionic Form locates the idempotents in measurement theory exactly. The effects — the elements between $0$ and $e_0$ — form a convex body, the operator interval, and its extreme points are exactly the idempotents, the identity and the rank-one projectors; a projective measurement uses only these extreme effects, while a general positive-operator-valued measure uses interior, "soft" effects that are convex combinations $\tilde E=t\tilde\Pi_1+(1-t)\tilde\Pi_2$ of orthogonal idempotents and respond partially to both alternatives. The idempotent is thus the sharp outcome, and every other outcome is a mixture of sharp ones. The article adds the reason the idempotent picture is not lost: by Naimark's dilation theorem every POVM is the compression of a projective measurement on a larger module, so the general effects are replaced by orthogonal idempotents once an ancillary factor is adjoined and traced out.

The two kinds of time evolution are one bilinear operation. The Measurement Problem in Algebraic Form states the disciplined version of the collapse. In the framework both a unitary evolution and a measurement update are the same bilinear operation $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde A\tilde Q\tilde A^{*}$, applied with an acting element of one of two algebraic types: unitary, $\tilde A\tilde A^{*}=e_0$, or Hermitian idempotent, $\tilde A^{2}=\tilde A=\tilde A^{*}$. Idempotent and unitary are therefore the two classes of acting element of one operation form, and the measurement problem becomes the absence of a rule fixing which class applies when. The article is explicit that this is a restatement and not a solution, and the idempotent's place in it is exact: it is the class of acting element that produces a definite outcome.

The idempotent is the algebra's native qubit. The Native Qubit and the Defining Module of the Biquaternion Algebra identifies the elementary informational carrier as the algebra's own simple module $S$, and the identification runs through the idempotents. A complex two-dimensional space is not chosen and then equipped with operators; it is the object an algebra isomorphic to $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a matrix algebra of, and the article records the two facts that make "native" precise: the simple module is unique up to isomorphism, and the algebra's left regular module is two copies of it. On that module the states are placed by the rank-one idempotents — the article's own statement is that the rank-one projectors on $S$ are exactly the idempotents of $\mathbb{M}_+$ that have trace one — and the trace pairing becomes the Hilbert-space inner product. The reading sharpens the companion's central claim: the idempotents are not one convenient representative of the state; they are the rays of the only elementary carrier the algebra possesses, so the pure states of the framework are native in the same sense in which the qubit is.

The idempotents are the atoms a context assigns. Contextuality and the Kochen–Specker Theorem in Biquaternionic Form shows what the idempotents do when several observables are measured together. A context is a maximal commuting set of Hermitian elements, generated by commuting involutions, and its spectral resolution is a complete family of orthogonal idempotents summing to $e_0$; a noncontextual value assignment restricts to each context as an algebra homomorphism and takes the values $\{0,1\}$ on the idempotents, exactly one per context being $1$. The Kochen–Specker theorem is the statement that no such assignment exists once the defining module has dimension at least three, so the idempotents are not only the determinate states of a single measurement but the atoms of every context, and contextuality is the failure of a global $\{0,1\}$ labelling of them. The framework's own boundary is stated in the article: the native module is two-dimensional, the obstruction there is empty, and the smallest arena in which it appears is the two-qubit algebra $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$, where the Mermin–Peres magic square forces a noncontextual assignment to make the product of the same nine entries both $+1$ and $-1$.

A protected subspace is an idempotent's image. Quantum Error Correction and the Stabilizer Formalism in Biquaternionic Form puts the idempotent to a second job, beyond naming a state. A code is specified by an abelian group of commuting involutions of $\mathbb{M}_+^{\otimes n}$ — the stabilizer group — and the code space is the common $+1$ eigenspace, which is the image of the code projector $\tilde\Pi=|S|^{-1}\sum_{\tilde g\in S}\tilde g$, itself an idempotent of the tensor-product algebra. The article's structural finding is that the whole theory of stabilizer codes is a theory of commutative subalgebras and their idempotents: the code space is a Peirce component of a commutative subalgebra, and the syndrome measurement projects onto its idempotents. The correction is algebraic too: the Knill–Laflamme condition says an error set is correctable exactly when $\tilde\Pi\tilde E_a^{*}\tilde E_b\tilde\Pi=c_{ab}\tilde\Pi$ — the errors, sandwiched by the code idempotent, act on that Peirce component as scalars. The reading is a genuine extension of the companion's picture. The idempotent is not only the algebra's native qubit and the sharp outcome of a single measurement; a commutative family of idempotents cuts out a subspace protected from a class of errors, and the conditions for protection are conditions on how the idempotent absorbs those errors. Projection, which the companion reads as the irreversible loss of coherence, is here the constructive move that defines the code and diagnoses a syndrome.

A family of idempotent bases is a complete measurement. Mutually Unbiased Bases and the Biquaternion Algebra turns the idempotent into a tomographic tool. A basis of rays is a complete orthogonal family of rank-one idempotents, $\tilde\Pi_i\tilde\Pi_j=\delta_{ij}\tilde\Pi_i$ and $\sum_i\tilde\Pi_i=e_0$, and two bases are mutually unbiased when the trace pairing between idempotents of different bases is exactly $1/d$. For the native qubit the maximal set of $d+1=3$ unbiased bases has a clean algebraic origin: they are the eigenbases of the three imaginary units $ie_1,ie_2,ie_3$, pairwise anticommuting involutions, and the three bases are the three coordinate measurements whose outcome probabilities determine the state, the tomographic statement. The companion's picture gains a second dimension: the idempotents of one basis are the sharp outcomes of one measurement, and the incompatibility of two measurements is the size of the overlap of their idempotents, $1/d$ being maximal ignorance. The article is exact about the ceiling too: on two qubits the maximum is five bases, the tensor product of qubit bases supplies only three, and the Bell basis — a natural idempotent basis of $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ — is not unbiased with respect to the product basis, so the obstruction to a full set is visible in the idempotents themselves.

Chirality's projectors are idempotents, and the radicand is dimension-dependent. Bosonization in Biquaternionic Form records a fact that belongs to this companion's ledger. The chiral projectors $\tilde\Pi_{L,R}=\tfrac12(1\pm\gamma^5)$ are idempotents that split a spinor into its two chiralities, and their existence as projectors depends on the square of $\gamma^5$: the article records $(\gamma^5)^2=+I_2$ in two dimensions whereas $(\gamma^5)^2=-I_4$ in four. In two dimensions $\gamma^5$ is therefore a real structure (a root of $+1$), the chiral projectors have real eigenspaces, and an irreducible two-component spinor is a sum of two real (Majorana–Weyl) spinors; in four dimensions it is a complex structure (a root of $-1$), and the splitting is complex. The reading is that the idempotent is not a dimension-free object: the same formal projector has real or complex eigenspaces according to which radicand the chirality operator realises, and the framework's idempotent-as-state picture inherits that dependence at the level of the chirality splitting.

Physical Interpretation

  • An idempotent is a projection, and a projection is a determinate state: measuring twice gives the same result as measuring once. The idempotent sets are the state sets of the twelve bands.
  • The plain product carries the largest family, one state per local complex structure, so every phase gives a state. The trivial pair is the impossible outcome and the trivial measurement.
  • The Hermitian idempotents are the pure states of the probability band, on the boundary of the positive cone, and the Bloch ball is the convex body they generate. The vacuum is a minimal member.
  • The gauge family is the only nontrivial family of idempotents that are units. The pure state of the probability band is null for the composition; the gauge state is invertible. A determinate outcome of a measurement is not a reversible process; a determinate state of the gauge band is.
  • The complementation pairs a state with its orthogonal complement, and the pair is a complete two-outcome measurement. It pairs the plain family and the Hermitian family and fails for the gauge family.
  • The brackets and the interval band carry no nontrivial state: an infinitesimal transformation has no determinate state, and a state is not an interval.
  • The idempotent is the state of The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone, the outcome of the measurement of The Measurement Problem in Algebraic Form, the basis element of The Bell Basis as the Idempotent Basis of B⊗B, and the minimal vacuum of The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent; decoherence, read in Decoherence as Idempotent Projection, is the passage off the idempotent family.
  • The idempotents are the zero-entropy states: the deviation $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\rho^{2}-\tilde\rho)=\tfrac14(\lvert\mathbf r\rvert^{2}-1)$ is minus the biquaternion norm, and the entropy is a monotone function of it that vanishes exactly on the idempotents. The sphere of idempotents of $\mathbb{M}_+$ is at once the pure-qubit sphere, the polarisation sphere and the boundary of the state cone.
  • Idempotency occurs at two levels. An idempotent element is a pure state; an idempotent map $\Phi^{2}=\Phi$ is a coarse-graining, and a coarse-graining increases the entropy by moving every state off the idempotent family.
  • The idempotents are the algebra's native states. The Native Qubit and the Defining Module of the Biquaternion Algebra shows that the elementary carrier is the algebra's unique simple module, and that its rays are exactly the trace-one idempotents of $\mathbb{M}_+$; the trace pairing is the Hilbert-space inner product. The state is not one convenient representative among many but the only elementary carrier's rays.
  • The idempotents are the atoms of a context. A maximal commuting set of Hermitian elements resolves into a complete family of orthogonal idempotents, and contextuality is the failure of a global $\{0,1\}$ valuation of them (Contextuality and the Kochen–Specker Theorem in Biquaternionic Form). The obstruction is empty for the native two-dimensional module and appears first for two qubits, in the Mermin–Peres magic square.
  • The vacuum idempotent carries the zero-point energy: half a quantum per mode, with the Casimir energy the boundary-condition-dependent difference that normal ordering leaves behind.
  • The idempotents are also the perfectly resolvable states: the Fisher information of the state with respect to the Bloch radius is the reciprocal of the biquaternion norm, so it diverges exactly on the idempotents. The pure state, the zero-entropy state, the zero-divisor state and the perfectly resolvable state are four names for one element.
  • The idempotents are the sharp effects, the extreme points of the operator interval $[0,e_0]$, and a general POVM uses interior "soft" effects, convex combinations $t\tilde\Pi_1+(1-t)\tilde\Pi_2$ of orthogonal idempotents; by Naimark's dilation theorem every such measurement is a projective one on a larger module. In The Measurement Problem in Algebraic Form the idempotent and the unitary are the two classes of acting element of one bilinear operation $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde A\tilde Q\tilde A^{*}$, the idempotent being the class that produces a definite outcome.
  • Only the sesquilinear product supplies the cone, and with it the state space. Why the Fourth Product Is a Gauge Structure and Not a State Space shows that the fourth product's ternary structure fails the Jordan triple identity, so no cone and no pure states can be rebuilt from it; the idempotents-as-states are the gift of the positive pairing alone.
  • The state is spinorial, so the idempotent-as-state reading is a spin-$\tfrac12$ reading: a spin-$0$ scalar lives in the centre and escapes the state module, because a simple matrix algebra has no one-dimensional module for the trivial representation.
  • An idempotent also defines a protected subspace. In Quantum Error Correction and the Stabilizer Formalism in Biquaternionic Form the code space is the image of an idempotent (the code projector), a Peirce component of a commutative subalgebra of involutions; the syndrome measurement projects onto idempotents, and the Knill–Laflamme condition says errors are correctable exactly when their products, sandwiched by the code idempotent, act as scalars on it.
  • Idempotent bases are measurement bases. In Mutually Unbiased Bases and the Biquaternion Algebra the three maximal unbiased bases of the qubit are the eigenbases of $ie_1,ie_2,ie_3$, the trace pairing between idempotents of different bases is $1/d$, the $d+1$ bases reconstruct the state, and the two-qubit Bell basis is not unbiased with respect to the product basis.
  • Chirality's projectors are idempotents whose eigenspaces' reality is dimension-dependent: $(\gamma^5)^2=+I_2$ in two dimensions (a real structure, Majorana–Weyl splitting) and $(\gamma^5)^2=-I_4$ in four (Bosonization in Biquaternionic Form).
  • The idempotents separate the classical from the quantum in a correlation. In Quantum Discord and Correlations Beyond Entanglement in Biquaternionic Form a local projective measurement is a family of idempotents, and the classical mutual information is the part of the correlation such a measurement can extract; the discord is the rest, zero exactly for the states that are classically correlated. The Bell-diagonal states are the convex combinations of the four Bell idempotents, so the classical part of a bipartite state is what a convex combination of idempotents carries, and the discord is the correlation that no idempotent basis resolves.
  • The state's carrier is a minimal left ideal, and that is why the algebra carries only spin $0$ and spin $\tfrac12$. In Higher Spin from Tensor Products: Why the Biquaternion Algebra Admits Only Spin 0 and One-Half a rank-one idempotent $e$ makes the left ideal $\mathbb{B}e$ minimal — it contains no smaller nonzero left ideal — of complex dimension two, so it is the algebra's unique irreducible carrier, the spinor module; the idempotent also decomposes the algebra itself. The module category has a single isomorphism class of simple objects, so every module is a sum of copies of that two-dimensional one, and the spin content of any module is a finite number of copies of spin $\tfrac12$: spin zero sits in the centre and in the invariants of products, and higher spin exists only in tensor powers, to be extracted by constraints.
  • An idempotent is a measurement resource and a unitary is a gate; the corpus keeps the two apart. In Quantum Teleportation in Biquaternionic Form the entangled resource and the Bell measurement are the four Bell idempotents of $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ — "idempotent and Hermitian, but not unitary" — while the corrections are unitary elements, and the protocol is "a controlled collision between two kinds of object that the framework elsewhere keeps apart": the idempotents that resolve a measurement and the unitaries that generate reversible motion. The same elements $e_k$ enter the resource as $e_k\otimes e_k$ and the correction gates as $ie_k$, and the two behave quite differently.

Summary

An idempotent is an element equal to its own square, the algebraic form of a projection and, in this framework, of a determinate state. The mathematics companion proves that only five of the twelve structures carry a nontrivial idempotent: the general plain structure and its symmetrisation, with the family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots of $-1$, one state per phase; the general plain sesquilinear structure, with the Hermitian family $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ over the real unit vectors, the pure states of the probability band, on the boundary of the positive cone and the extremal points of the Bloch ball; and the two quaternionic sesquilinear structures, with the family $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$ over the real vectors of squared length $\tfrac34$, the states of the gauge band. The central square separates the families: it vanishes on the plain and the Hermitian idempotents, which are therefore zero divisors, and it equals $e_0$ on the gauge family, which is therefore a family of units. The physics of that separation is the contrast between the band that measures a probability, whose determinate states are null and cannot be composed, and the band whose value is an interval, whose determinate states are invertible. The complementation $\tilde\Pi\mapsto e_0-\tilde\Pi$ pairs a state with its orthogonal complement and makes a complete two-outcome measurement; it pairs the plain family and the Hermitian family and fails for the gauge family. The four brackets carry the origin alone, because an infinitesimal transformation has no determinate state, and the interval band carries only the trivial pair, because a state is not an interval.

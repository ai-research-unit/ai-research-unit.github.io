The Lagrangian: The Fluid Readings

The fluid readings write the action in the variables of a fluid — a density, a phase, a velocity and, for the spinning fields, a rotor. The chart is the polar chart with the frame identified as a local velocity and a local spin.

What the group does. It exhibits the fluid action, its variations, the pressure's absence or presence, and the currents read as fluid quantities: mass, charge, spin, stress. The group's most useful results are the canonical tables' fluid readings — which variable is canonical and which is not — and the identification of the quantum potential as a functional derivative.

What the group does not claim. It does not claim that the quantum potential is a pressure, and it does not claim that the fluid is a medium: the density is the field's squared modulus, and the velocity is its phase's gradient. Nor does it quantise.

One result worth naming. The spin-zero fluid's gradient energy is indefinite, so the fluid's energy is not positive term by term; the spin-one fluid's is, because the frame's sigma-model term supplies the missing positivity. That contrast is the group's structural finding.

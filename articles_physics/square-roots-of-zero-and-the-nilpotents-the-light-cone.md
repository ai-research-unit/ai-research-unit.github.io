# __Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents: the Light Cone__

## Introduction

Of the four general products of *The Four General Products of the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, exactly one makes the biquaternion space $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ an associative unital $\mathbb{C}$-algebra: the general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$, the multiplication of the algebra, read throughout this article.

The article reads that product at the central value $0$. The **roots of $0$** are the elements $\tilde\Xi\in\mathbb{B}$ with $\tilde\Xi^2=0$; the **nilpotents** are the nonzero such elements. The two subjects are one: the roots of $0$ are the origin together with the square-zero elements, and the nonzero square-zero elements are exactly the nilpotents.

The classification is the mathematics of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Biquaternion Algebra*: by the vector–scalar split, a root of $0$ is either a complex scalar or pure, the scalar branch returns the origin alone, and the pure branch is the **nilpotent cone**, the pure elements whose complex vector part is isotropic. It is cited and read here, not re-proved.

**The physical interpretation.** The content added here is a reading. A nonzero element whose square vanishes is read as the algebraic form of a **lightlike propagation**: the element is not zero, yet composing it with itself annihilates it, the algebraic shadow of a displacement of zero interval. The **nilpotent cone** is the pure part of the **zero-divisor cone** of the algebra, and that cone is the framework's **light cone**: on the material sector its vanishing set is the null cone of the interval, and the level set of its norm is the mass shell. The three algebraic objects of the reading must be kept apart, and the article keeps them apart throughout: the **roots of $0$** are the origin and the nilpotent cone; the **nilpotents** are the nonzero members of that cone; and the **zero-divisor cone** is strictly larger, containing the non-pure zero divisors as well. The roots of $0$ are the pure part of the cone, and the light cone is the whole cone.

The roots of $+1$ and the idempotents are *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents: the Hermitian Observables and the States*; the general quadratic is *Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternion Algebra: the Realisation*. The product and the algebra are *The Four General Products of the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space* and *Introduction to the Biquaternion Algebra*; the zero-divisor cone and its two families are *Zero Divisors of the Biquaternion Algebra*; the light cone read in physics is *The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone*, *Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form* and *Causality and the Light Cone as an Information Barrier in Biquaternionic Form*; the interval and the mass shell are *The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition*; and the material sector and its group of units are *The Bilinear Product on the Material Sector*.

**Conventions.** The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^2=-1$. A general element is $\tilde Q=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}Q_\mu e_\mu=Q_0e_0+\vec Q$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$ and $\vec Q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q_ke_k$. The general plain bilinear form of two vector parts is

$$
(\vec P,\vec Q)=\sum_{k=1}^{3}P_kQ_k,
$$

and the square of a pure element is $\vec Q^2=-(\vec Q,\vec Q)e_0$. The **central square** is $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}=C(\tilde Q)e_0$ with $C(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$, and the **norm** is $N(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$, the same scalar. The sectors are $\mathbb{M}_\pm=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=\pm\tilde Q\}$. A root of a central value is written $\tilde\Xi$ (§*The Root Convention* of *Conventions in Mathematics*) and a nilpotent $\tilde\Upsilon$ (§*The Nilpotent Convention*). The vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, the kernel of the scalar part.

## The Square and the Two Branches

The square of a biquaternion is the two-term expression

$$
\tilde Q^2=\bigl(Q_0^2-(\vec Q,\vec Q)\bigr)e_0+2Q_0\vec Q .
$$

For the equation $\tilde\Xi^2=0$, with $\tilde\Xi=\Xi_0e_0+\vec\Xi$, the vector part and the scalar part separate:

$$
2\Xi_0\vec\Xi=0,\qquad \Xi_0^2-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0 .
$$

Since $\mathbb{C}$ is a field, the vector equation holds exactly when $\Xi_0=0$ or $\vec\Xi=0$: a root of $0$ is either a **complex scalar** or **pure**, and the two branches are disjoint and exhaustive. The scalar branch returns the origin alone; the pure branch is the nilpotent cone.

## The Roots of Zero

**The roots of $0$ are the origin together with the nilpotent cone, the pure biquaternions whose complex vector part is isotropic, $\Xi_0=0$ and $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$; the non-zero roots are the nilpotents.**

The classification is the one of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Biquaternion Algebra*, recalled here in its two parts.

### The Origin

In the scalar branch $\tilde\Xi=\Xi_0e_0$ and the scalar equation reads $\Xi_0^2=0$; since $\mathbb{C}$ is a field, $\Xi_0=0$. The root is the single element $\tilde\Xi=0$, the origin.

### The Null Cone

In the pure branch $\tilde\Xi=\vec\Xi$ and the scalar equation reads $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$. Writing $\vec\Xi=\vec\xi+i\vec\xi'$ with $\vec\xi,\vec\xi'$ real pure, the single complex equation is the two real equations

$$
\vec\xi\cdot\vec\xi=\vec\xi'\cdot\vec\xi',\qquad \vec\xi\cdot\vec\xi'=0 :
$$

the two real parts of a root are orthogonal and have equal sum of squares. Six real coefficients carry the two constraints, so the roots in this branch form a **four-real-parameter family**, the **null cone** of the pure subspace. Its nonzero elements are the nilpotents of the algebra.

### A Worked Root

The element

$$
\tilde\Xi=e_1+ie_2
$$

is a root of $0$, since

$$
(e_1+ie_2)^2=e_1^2+i^2e_2^2+i(e_1e_2+e_2e_1)=-1+1+0=0 .
$$

Its two real parts are $\vec\xi=e_1$ and $\vec\xi'=e_2$, orthogonal and of equal square one.

## The Nilpotents

**The nilpotents of the plain algebra are exactly the nonzero pure elements with isotropic vector part, $P_0=0$ and $(\vec P,\vec P)=0$; they form the nilpotent cone of complex dimension two and real dimension four inside the vector subspace; every nilpotent has index two; and no nilpotent is an idempotent.**

A **nilpotent** is a nonzero element $\tilde\Upsilon$ with $\tilde\Upsilon^2=0$. The class is small and rigid, and the article records its four structural properties.

**The criterion.** An element is a nilpotent exactly when it is pure and its vector part is isotropic, $P_0=0$ and $(\vec P,\vec P)=0$. An element with a nonzero scalar part is never nilpotent: if $P_0\neq0$ the vector part $2P_0\vec P$ vanishes only for $\vec P=0$, and then the scalar part $P_0^2$ is nonzero.

**Index two.** Since $\tilde\Upsilon^3=\tilde\Upsilon\tilde\Upsilon^2=0$, the cube of a nilpotent vanishes; conversely an element whose cube vanishes already has vanishing square, so the algebra carries no nilpotent of index three or more, and index two is the only index.

**Self-annihilation and zero divisors.** Each nonzero nilpotent satisfies $\tilde\Upsilon\tilde\Upsilon=0$, so it is a two-sided zero divisor whose own annihilating partner is itself. The nilpotents are exactly the **pure family** of the zero-divisor set of *Zero Divisors of the Biquaternion Algebra*; the other family is the non-pure one, the complex multiples of the non-trivial idempotents, and the two families meet only at $0$. No element is both an idempotent and a nonzero nilpotent.

**The cone and the subspaces.** The nilpotents form the cone $\{P_1^2+P_2^2+P_3^2=0\}$ in the vector subspace, parametrised as $r(\hat p+i\hat p')$ over orthogonal real unit pairs. The vector subspace is the **only** remarkable subspace containing a nilpotent; the centre, the quaternion and anti-quaternion subspaces carry only units and $0$, and neither sector contains one. A Hermitian element is $a_0e_0+i\vec\eta$ with $a_0$ real and $\vec\eta$ real pure, and its square $(a_0^2+\lvert\vec\eta\rvert^{2})e_0+2a_0i\vec\eta$ vanishes only for $a_0=0=\vec\eta$, so the only Hermitian nilpotent is $0$; the anti-Hermitian case is the same. This last fact is what makes the light-cone reading precise: **the nilpotents lie in neither the informational nor the material sector.**

## The Null Cone and the Light Cone

**The nilpotent cone is the pure part of the zero-divisor cone; the zero-divisor cone is the vanishing set of the norm and is the framework's light cone; and on the material sector the light cone is the null cone of the interval.**

The three sets are nested and distinct, and the article fixes the nesting.

- The **roots of $0$** are the origin together with the nilpotent cone.
- The **nilpotents** are the pure elements of the zero-divisor cone, of complex dimension two; they are exactly the nonzero roots of $0$.
- The **zero-divisor cone** $\{N=0\}$, the vanishing set of the norm $N(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2=Q_0^2+(\vec Q,\vec Q)$, is strictly larger. An element of the cone with a nonzero scalar part is a zero divisor that is **not** nilpotent: it is a complex multiple of a non-trivial idempotent. The zero-divisor cone has complex dimension three and real dimension six.

The empirical content of the reading is this: **the zero-divisor cone is the light cone**, the nilpotents are its pure part, and the roots of $0$ are the pure part together with the origin. The cone is the physical locus of the lightlike directions, and it is owned by *The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone* and *Zero Divisors as a Physical Locus in Biquaternionic Form*.

**On the material sector.** A material four-vector is $\tilde T=ict\,e_0+\vec x$, anti-Hermitian, in $\mathbb{M}_-$. Its norm is the interval,

$$
N(\tilde T)=-c^{2}t^{2}+\lvert\vec x\rvert^{2},
$$

of signature $(-,+,+,+)$, so the zero-divisor cone cut by $\mathbb{M}_-$ is the physical null cone $c^{2}t^{2}=\lvert\vec x\rvert^{2}$, and the level set $N(\tilde T)=-m^{2}c^{2}$ is the mass shell, developed in *The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition*. The material sector contains **no** nilpotent: the pure part of the algebraic cone lies in the vector subspace, in neither sector, and the physical light cone is the non-pure part of the cone cut by $\mathbb{M}_-$. The reading that the algebraic cone and the physical cone are one object is the framework's, and it is stated here for the algebra and cited there for the physics.

## The Nilpotents as Lightlike Propagation

**A nonzero root of $0$ is read as a lightlike propagation; the nilpotent cone is the algebraic locus of the lightlike displacements; and the zero-divisor cone is the boundary of invertibility of the algebra.**

The reading is the following. An element $\tilde\Upsilon\neq0$ with $\tilde\Upsilon^2=0$ is a nonzero operation whose composition with itself is nothing: the element carries a direction, and the direction annihilates itself, the algebraic shadow of a displacement of zero interval. A family of such elements, closed under real multiples — the nilpotent cone — is the algebraic locus of the lightlike directions. The reading is put on the roots of $0$ alone, and it is separate from the size of the root set: the roots of $0$ are the origin and the cone, and every nonzero root carries the reading.

**The cone as the wall of invertibility.** The norm is multiplicative, $N(\tilde P\tilde Q)=N(\tilde P)N(\tilde Q)$, and an element is invertible exactly when $N\neq0$, with inverse $\tilde Q^{\natural}/N(\tilde Q)$. The zero-divisor cone is therefore the set of the non-invertible elements off $0$; it is the wall of the group of units $\mathbb{B}^{\times}=\{N\neq0\}$. A lightlike element is not reversible, a timelike or spacelike one is, and this is the algebraic form of the reading that a lightlike propagation cannot be undone. The cone is closed under composition on the material side, as *The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition* develops, and it is the information barrier of *Causality and the Light Cone as an Information Barrier in Biquaternionic Form*.

**What the reading says and does not say.** It says that the nilpotents and the cone are the algebra's lightlike objects. It does **not** say that every zero divisor squares to $0$ — the non-pure family does not — that the nilpotents are physical particles or fields, or that the algebraic cone is derived from a metric: the metric enters only when the material sector is cut out, and there the norm is the interval. The classification is the mathematics of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Biquaternion Algebra*; the readings are proposals about naming.

## Summary

The general plain bilinear product of the biquaternion space is the multiplication of the algebra $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$. The square roots of $0$ in that product are the origin together with the nilpotent cone: by the vector–scalar split $2\Xi_0\vec\Xi=0$ and $\Xi_0^2-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=0$, the scalar branch returns the origin alone, and the pure branch is the pure biquaternions with isotropic complex vector part, the two real conditions $\vec\xi\cdot\vec\xi=\vec\xi'\cdot\vec\xi'$ and $\vec\xi\cdot\vec\xi'=0$, a four-real-parameter family. The worked root $e_1+ie_2$ has square zero.

The nonzero roots of $0$ are exactly the nilpotents, the nonzero elements of vanishing square. The criterion is $P_0=0$ and $(\vec P,\vec P)=0$; every nilpotent is pure and of index two, $\tilde P^3=0$ exactly when $\tilde P^2=0$; every nonzero nilpotent annihilates itself and is a two-sided zero divisor; the class is the nilpotent cone of complex dimension two and real dimension four; and among the remarkable subspaces only the vector subspace contains one, so the nilpotents lie in neither sector. No nilpotent is an idempotent.

The light-cone reading is this. The **zero-divisor cone** $N=0$ is the framework's **light cone**, of complex dimension three; the **nilpotents** are its **pure part**, and the **roots of $0$** are that pure part together with the origin. An element of the cone with nonzero scalar part is a zero divisor that is not nilpotent, so the cone is strictly larger than the roots of $0$. On the material sector the norm is the interval $N=-c^{2}t^{2}+\lvert\vec x\rvert^{2}$ of signature $(-,+,+,+)$, the cone cut by the sector is the physical null cone, and its level set is the mass shell. A nonzero root of $0$ is read as a **lightlike propagation**, the cone as the **wall of invertibility** of the algebra, and the cone as the **information barrier** of causality. The readings are proposals about naming, labelled as such; the root sets, the criterion, the index and the nesting are results.

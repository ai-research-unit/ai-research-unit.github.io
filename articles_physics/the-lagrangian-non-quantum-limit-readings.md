# __The Lagrangian: The Non-Quantum Limit Readings__

The non-quantum limit deletes the phase's dynamics and keeps the transport. The limit is taken inside the action, not on the equation, so the truncated action is available and its error is a displayed term.

**The articles.**

- [The Lagrangian and the Klein–Gordon Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit](the-lagrangian-and-the-klein-gordon-equation-in-the-non-quantum-limit.html)
- [The Lagrangian and the Dirac Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit](the-lagrangian-and-the-dirac-equation-in-the-non-quantum-limit.html)
- [The Lagrangian and the Proca Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit](the-lagrangian-and-the-proca-equation-in-the-non-quantum-limit.html)
- [The Lagrangian and the Schrödinger Equation in the Non-Quantum Limit](the-lagrangian-and-the-schrodinger-equation-in-the-non-quantum-limit.html)

**What the group does.** It states the deletion, the truncated action, the currents that survive, and the order of the dropped term. Each article has three parts in the same order, so the group can be read across: the scalar's mass shell, the spinor's one-form, the vector's longitudinal coefficient and the Schrödinger field's eikonal.

**What the group does not claim.** It does not claim that the truncated theory is Galilean or Newtonian: the limit is a truncation of the action and its error is stated, not a change of framework. And it does not quantise.

**Why the limit is worth an article per field.** Because the truncated action is what a reader uses to recognise the classical limit, and a limit taken on the equation cannot be pushed back into an action. Taking it inside the action is the family's method and this group is where the method is tested.

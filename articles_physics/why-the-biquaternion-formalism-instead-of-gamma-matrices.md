# __Why the Biquaternion Formalism Instead of Gamma Matrices__

## The Question

Under the rule of *The Clifford Layer Is the Cell Layer*, the corpus writes its spinors and its Dirac operators in the algebra itself. A left multiplication, a right multiplication, or a product of the two acts on an element of $\mathbb{B}$, and the body of an article carries no array and no gamma. The same physics can be written with the five gamma matrices, and most of the literature writes it that way. The question of this article is why the corpus does not. The short answer is that the two are the same algebra: in the reference frame the sixteen gamma basis elements and the sixteen basis cells are the same operators, and the difference is in how the coefficients of an operator are written.

The answer has two parts. The **first** is a fact about the algebra: the ambient is wider than the physics that uses it. The gamma frame generates $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{B})$, and so do the cells; a single physical operator is one term or a sum of a few terms, while the generic element is neither. The **second** is the reason for the choice of primitive: the physics supplies two multipliers per term, and the cell form names their product $L_{Q}R_{P}$ as one object, with the rank-one constraint built in, while the gamma form names sixteen coefficients over fixed matrices and leaves the constraint implicit. The two formulations are the same algebra; the difference is what a page displays as the primitive.

Neither part changes the physics. The two formulations are the same algebra over $\mathbb{C}$, and every identity of one is an identity of the other. What changes is the object a computation starts from, and therefore what a page puts in front of the reader.

**What this article does not claim.** It does not claim that the gamma formalism is wrong, that its users are careless, or that any calculation in the literature is in error. It does not claim a new prediction. It states a preference of primitive and gives the mathematical reason for it.

## Three Ways to Write the Same Operator

The three formulations are one algebra read at three levels.

**The coordinate level.** $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})$ is generated by four elements $\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$ with $\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2g_{\mu\nu}\mathbf 1$, together with their product $\gamma_5$; the corpus calls the five the gamma frame. A spinor is a column of four complex numbers, an operator is a $4\times4$ array, and the product is matrix multiplication. This is the standard language of quantum field theory.

**The algebra level.** $\mathbb{B}$ is the even part, of complex dimension four, and the odd part is a rank-one module over it. The endomorphisms of the carrier are
$$
\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}\otimes_\mathbb{C}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}),
$$
with the sixteen basis cells $L_{a}R_{b}$. A general element is a sum of cells; a physical element is a sum of a few terms.

**The action level.** The same element is written as what it does to $\psi\in\mathbb{B}$,
$$
\psi\longmapsto a\,\psi\,b,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{B}.
$$
No symbol for the operator is needed when the operator is not the subject. This is the level at which the corpus writes.

| level | carrier | primitive | free complex parameters | product |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| coordinate | $\mathbb{C}^4$ | one gamma, a fixed array, plus a scalar coefficient | one per term | matrix product |
| algebra | $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ | one product $L_{Q}R_{P}$ | seven per product | $L_{Q}R_{P}L_{R}R_{S}=L_{QR}R_{SP}$ |
| action | $\mathbb{B}$ | the pair $(Q,P)$ | seven per term | composition of actions |

**The parameters of a product.** The pair $(Q,P)$ has eight complex parameters, and the scaling $(Q,P)\mapsto(\lambda Q,\lambda^{-1}P)$, $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}$, leaves the operator unchanged. The map is therefore constant on the orbits of $\mathbb{C}^{\times}$, and a product has seven effective parameters. The ambient has sixteen complex dimensions. The five are not independent: four generate the algebra, and $\gamma_5$ is a scalar multiple of their product. The basis they induce by antisymmetrised products has sixteen elements.

## What the Gamma Matrices Are

**A frame, not a basis.** The four gammas generate $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})$, and $\gamma_5$ is a scalar multiple of their product. The basis they induce is the sixteen antisymmetrised products, of ranks $0,1,2,3,4$ and dimensions $1+4+6+4+1=16$. The five are a generating set, not a basis; the basis has sixteen elements, and the count matters. The five are five of the sixteen cells, up to a scalar; the other eleven cells are the remaining elements of the same basis, and in the Clifford basis they correspond to the bivectors, the trivectors and the scalar, obtained as products of the five. They are single cells as well, and the five-name list does not name them. A reader who has the five can compute the other eleven, but the list itself shows only five.

**The frame is not canonical.** For a fixed metric, every frame is $S\gamma_\mu S^{-1}$ for some invertible $S$, and the frames over that metric form a homogeneous space under $GL_4(\mathbb{C})$. Physics chooses one frame. An equation that names $\gamma_0$ names the choice, and a reader who does not know the choice cannot tell a frame convention from an algebraic fact.

**The frame chosen by the corpus is one of a family.** For any orthogonal pair $(\beta,\delta)$ of spatial units,
$$
\gamma_0=iL_tR_\delta,\qquad \gamma_k=iL_{e_k}R_\beta,\qquad \gamma_5=iL_{t}R_{\beta\delta},
$$
and the Clifford relations hold for every pair. The reference frame is $(\beta,\delta)=(e_y,e_x)$. A change of pair changes which cells represent the generators and preserves the Clifford relations and the algebra; it is a change of frame, not a change of the algebra. In the gamma notation the same statement is written $S\gamma_\mu S^{-1}$, and the freedom is easy to mistake for a symmetry of the physics.

**The metric is already in the units.** In $\mathbb{B}$ the signature is carried by $e_t^2=\mathbf 1$ and $e_k^2=-\mathbf 1$. The Clifford relation $\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2g_{\mu\nu}\mathbf 1$ restates that signature as a second datum, and a frame is a choice of representatives for that datum. The frame adds no structure the multiplication lacks; it adds a coordinate on that structure.

## The Ambient Is Wider Than the Physics

**The single products are a cone.** A single two-sided operator is $L_{Q}R_{P}$, and the map $(Q,P)\mapsto L_{Q}R_{P}$ is complex-bilinear, not complex-linear. Its image is the affine cone over the Segre variety $\mathbb{P}^3\times\mathbb{P}^3$, of complex dimension seven, and a matrix belongs to it exactly when its coefficient matrix $c(M)$, the matrix of $M$ in the cell basis, has rank at most one. The cone is closed under composition and under inversion on its units. It is not closed under addition.

**This is not a defect of the gamma basis.** In the reference frame, the sixteen Clifford basis elements and the sixteen reference cells are the same operators, up to permutation and scalar; the dictionary is a bijection. The cone is therefore the same in both readings. What differs is what the notation displays. A single product $L_{Q}R_{P}$ carries two free multipliers, seven effective parameters; a gamma basis element is a fixed matrix with a scalar coefficient. The physics supplies the multipliers, so the cell form makes the product the primitive; the gamma form makes the coefficient the primitive.

**The bases coincide; the coefficients factor.** A general operator has sixteen coefficients in either form. A single product has a coefficient matrix of rank one, so its sixteen coefficients are not free: they factor as $c_{ab}=u_a v_b$, seven effective parameters. The gamma form displays the sixteen coefficients and leaves the rank-one constraint implicit; the cell form displays the two multipliers that produce them. The gain is the constraint made visible, not a shorter expansion.

**The cone does not exhaust the ambient.** A general element of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ is a sum of at most four products, and the length is the coefficient rank,
$$
\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}.
$$
A generic element has $\ell=4$, and the set $\det c(M)\neq0$ is open and dense.

**A concrete failure.** The sum
$$
L_tR_t+L_xR_x
$$
has coefficient matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0)$, of rank two. It is not a single product, and no change of frame by a decomposable $S$ makes it one: such a change acts on $c(M)$ by an invertible left and right multiplication and preserves its rank. A formalism that makes the generic matrix the primitive has no place to mark the rank-one subset that the physics uses; the cell form writes the same operator as a sum of two terms and leaves it there.

**Two ranks, independent.** The matrix rank of a product is $\operatorname{rank}M_2(Q)\cdot\operatorname{rank}M_2(P)$, so it lies in $\{0,1,2,4\}$; no product has matrix rank three. The length lies in $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$. Neither fixes the other: the chiral projector $P_+=\tfrac12(L_tR_t-iL_tR_z)$ has matrix rank two and length one, and a rank-one matrix can have length four. A reader who uses matrix rank as a proxy for "how many terms" is misled in both directions.

**The physics uses few terms.** The chiral projector is one term. The Dirac operator, worked in the next section, has length two: the three spatial terms share a right multiplier and combine into one. The four-term display is a display, not a property of the operator. The operators displayed in the corpus sit at low length; the generic element is not one of them. The gamma frame makes the coefficient vector the object; the biquaternion reading makes the pair the object.

## A Worked Example: the Dirac Operator

**The gamma display.** In the reference frame the operator is
$$
D\psi=\gamma_0\partial_t\psi+\gamma_1\partial_x\psi+\gamma_2\partial_y\psi+\gamma_3\partial_z\psi,
$$
with
$$
\gamma_0=iL_tR_x,\qquad \gamma_1=iL_xR_y,\qquad \gamma_2=iL_yR_y,\qquad \gamma_3=iL_zR_y,\qquad \gamma_5=-iL_tR_z.
$$
Four fixed operators and four derivatives. The display does not show a pair of multipliers, and it does not show that the three spatial terms share a right multiplier.

**The dictionary.** Replace each gamma by its cell:
$$
D\psi=i\,e_t(\partial_t\psi)e_x+i\,e_x(\partial_x\psi)e_y+i\,e_y(\partial_y\psi)e_y+i\,e_z(\partial_z\psi)e_y.
$$
The substitution is mechanical, and the two displays are the same operator term by term. The gamma elements of the display are the cells of the cell display, and the scalar coefficients are unchanged; nothing is lost and nothing is added.

**The minimal display.** The three spatial terms share the right multiplier $e_y$, so they combine for every $\psi$:
$$
D\psi=i\,e_t(\partial_t\psi)e_x+i\,(\nabla\psi)e_y,\qquad \nabla=e_x\partial_x+e_y\partial_y+e_z\partial_z.
$$
Two products, not four. The four-cell form is a display, and the length theorem reads the minimal number, two.

**The coefficient matrix.** At a generic frequency, the only nonzero entries of $c(D)$ in the four-term display are $(t,x)$, $(x,y)$, $(y,y)$ and $(z,y)$, with coefficients $i\partial_t$, $i\partial_x$, $i\partial_y$ and $i\partial_z$. The matrix has rank two, because the three spatial terms occupy the same column $y$. The length theorem returns two, the number of terms in the minimal display, and the two numbers agree. The agreement is a check of the dictionary, not a coincidence.

**The square.** The frame is a correct coordinate, and the check is that the square is the wave operator:
$$
D^2=\partial_t^2-\partial_x^2-\partial_y^2-\partial_z^2,
$$
because $\gamma_0^2=1$, $\gamma_k^2=-1$, and distinct gammas anticommute. The cell form gives the same square, because the cells are the same operators. This is the sense in which the gamma frame is correct: it is a coordinate, and the coordinate reproduces the invariant.

**The mass.** The mass term is one cell, and in this frame that cell is the identity:
$$
m\,L_tR_t\psi=m\,e_t\psi e_t=m\psi,
$$
because $e_t$ is central. In the gamma display the same term is $m\psi$, which is $m\gamma_0^2$, not a sixth gamma. The five-name list does not name the identity as a cell; the cell list does. This is the concrete case of the point above: the cell form names the mass block, the gamma form leaves it as the identity.

**The lengths.** At a generic frequency,

| operator | cell display | length |
|---|---|---|
| mass | $mL_tR_t$ | 1 |
| chirality | $-iL_tR_z$ | 1 |
| chiral projector $P_+$ | $\tfrac12L_{e_t}R_{\,e_t-i e_z}$ | 1 |
| Dirac, minimal | $iL_{t}R_{x}\partial_t+iL_{\nabla}R_{e_y}$ | 2 |
| Dirac, expanded | four cells | 2 |
| generic element | — | 4 |

The length is the number of products in the minimal sum, not the number of cells on the page. The expanded Dirac display has four cells and length two. The generic element has length four, and no physical operator displayed in the corpus has that length.

## The Spinor Is Not a Column

**A column is a choice.** A spinor in $\mathbb{C}^4$ is a column, and a column presupposes a basis. The basis is supplied by a minimal left ideal of the Clifford algebra, and the choice of ideal is a choice of idempotent, hence of frame. The carrier of the corpus is $\mathbb{B}$ as a four-dimensional complex vector space, with its own multiplication; no ideal and no column are selected. The left copy $\mathbb{B}_L$ acts block-diagonally, on two copies of its two-dimensional simple module, and the odd slot is what exchanges the two chiral halves. The Dirac action is therefore the two-sided action, not the left regular action alone.

**The same freedom, stated twice.** In the column language the freedom is written twice: in the basis of $\mathbb{C}^4$ and in the representation of the gammas. A change of basis acts on the representation by conjugation, so the two writings carry the same invertible $S$, and the reader has to check that both were changed together. The algebra language names the freedom once, as the frame, and a frame change is written where it occurs.

**Chirality separates cleanly.** In the reference frame the chirality is the single cell
$$
\Gamma=-iL_tR_z=-iL_tR_z,\qquad \Gamma^2=1,\qquad P_\pm=\tfrac12(1\pm\Gamma),
$$
and the projectors are read as actions,
$$
P_\pm:\ \psi\longmapsto\tfrac12\bigl(\psi\mp i\,e_t\,\psi\,e_z\bigr).
$$
The physical split uses a different object, the central sign,
$$
\chi=i\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{ad}},\qquad \chi^2=e_t .
$$
It is an operator on the module, not multiplication by an element of $\mathbb{B}$; no element of $\mathbb{B}$ acts as $\chi$. In the one-mode truncation the two are represented by the same symbol $\gamma_5$, and the notation does not distinguish them. The coincidence is real, and it is exactly the case in which the conflation is harmless. Outside that case the two roles separate, and the notation does not.

## The Frame Becomes the Physics

**One frame in every formula.** The reference dictionary ties time to $e_x$ and the three spatial directions to $e_y$:
$$
\gamma_0=iL_tR_x,\quad \gamma_1=iL_xR_y,\quad \gamma_2=iL_yR_y,\quad \gamma_3=iL_zR_y,\quad \gamma_5=-iL_tR_z.
$$
The pair $(e_y,e_x)$ is a choice. A formula written in it is a formula about that choice as much as about the operator, and the gamma notation gives the reader no mark that separates the two.

**A frame change preserves the cone only when it is decomposable.** A change by $S=L_{a}R_{b}$, with $a,b$ units, sends $L_{Q}R_{P}$ to $L_{aQa^{-1}}R_{\,b^{-1}Pb}$ and stays inside the cone. A general change of gamma basis need not: it is itself a sum of a few cells, and it can turn one product into several. The gamma notation writes both changes with the same symbol $S(\cdot)S^{-1}$, and the difference between them — one preserves the class of physical operators, the other does not — is invisible.

**A statement the corpus can make and the frame notation does not.** The biquaternion form separates three things that the gamma form runs together: the algebra, the frame, and the grading. This is why the corpus's conventions articles can state the frame freedom once, in one place, and why an article that changes frame does not have to restate its physics.

## What the Biquaternion Formalism Buys

**The primitive is the physical object.** A term is a pair of multipliers. The left multiplier is what acts before, the right multiplier what acts after, and the formula is read in the order the operation happens. A reader sees which element carries the mass, which carries the momentum, and which carries the grading, because each is a named factor rather than an entry of an array.

**Composition is a formula, not a product of arrays.** The product is
$$
L_{a}R_{b}L_{c}R_{d}=L_{ac}R_{db},
$$
with the second slot reversed. Powers, inverses and the adjoint follow in one line,
$$
(L_{a}R_{b})^n=L_{a^n}R_{b^n},\qquad L_{a}R_{b}^{-1}=L_{a^{-1}}R_{b^{-1}},\qquad (L_{a}R_{b})^{\approx}=L_{b}R_{a},
$$
where $\approx$ is the adjoint for the plain form $\mathrm{Sc}(XY)$; it exchanges the two slots and conjugates neither parameter. The Hilbert–Schmidt dagger is a different involution, the conjugate transpose of the array.

**Trace and determinant are scalars of the algebra.**
$$
\operatorname{Tr}L_{a}R_{b}=4\,a_t b_t,\qquad \det L_{a}R_{b}=\langle a\rangle_{\natural}^{2}\langle b\rangle_{\natural}^{2},
$$
with $a_t$ the scalar part and $\langle a\rangle_{\natural}$ the quaternionic norm. Both are read from the two multipliers and neither needs a basis.

**The metric is a square, not a tensor.** The interval is the scalar part of the square, $\mathrm{Sc}(Q^2)=Q_t^2-Q_x^2-Q_y^2-Q_z^2$, and the signature is fixed by $e_t^2=\mathbf 1$ and $e_k^2=-\mathbf 1$. No separate $g_{\mu\nu}$ enters, and no frame is needed to state it.

**The two sectors have a place.** The material and informational sectors are the anti-Hermitian and Hermitian subspaces of $\mathbb{B}$. A column of $\mathbb{C}^4$ carries no conjugation of its own: the split is a property of the algebra's conjugation, and a notation that begins with an array must import it as extra structure. The biquaternion form carries it natively.

**The grading is an operator with a name.** $\Gamma$ is a cell, $\Gamma^2=1$, and the projectors are its halves. The physical grading is the central sign $\chi$, a separate operator on the module. The notation can hold the distinction because it has two symbols for two objects.

**The action is the equation.** The Dirac operator is written as it acts. A reader who wants the operator's value on a state substitutes the state. A reader who wants the symbol composes the factors. Nothing has to be converted between a display and a computation.

## The Optimal Writing

The rules that follow from the above are short.

- **Write the action first.** $\psi\mapsto a\,\psi\,b$. If the operator is the subject, name it $L_a$, $R_b$ or $L_{a}R_{b}$; otherwise leave it implicit.
- **One term is one line.** A sum is written term by term, one term per line, and each term is a pair.
- **Never compress a sum into one symbol.** A sum is a single product only when $\operatorname{rank}c\le1$, and the test is silent, not displayed.
- **Keep the coefficient matrix out of the page.** It is a diagnostic of the isomorphism, not a datum of the physics.
- **No arrays, no columns, no Kronecker products** in a physical formula. The algebra is four-dimensional and the action is written on it.
- **The frame is named once.** A change of frame is a change of the pair $(\beta,\delta)$ and is stated where it occurs.
- **The gamma form goes to one closing appendix.** One display, the dictionary, the real-form caveat, no derivation. The body does not carry it, except in an article whose subject is the comparison, as this one.

## Where the Gamma Formalism Remains the Right Tool

**The literature is written in it.** Textbooks, scattering amplitudes, the spinor-helicity formalism, gamma-trace technology, Fierz rearrangements, anomaly computations, index theorems and supergravity all use the frame. A reader who wants to compare with that literature needs the dictionary, and the appendix supplies it.

**Some computations are shorter in it.** A trace of many gammas is a contraction of indices; a Fierz rearrangement is a completeness relation of the sixteen products; a perturbative calculation is organised by the frame. The biquaternion form does not forbid these; it declines to make them the default.

**Some objects are frames by nature.** The Clifford group, the Pin group, the frame stabiliser and the space of gamma bases are the subject of their own articles, and there the frame is the object. Those articles keep it.

**The real forms need care.** Over $\mathbb{C}$ the dictionary is exact. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the generators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ while the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$; the two real forms are opposite, and the dictionary is complex-linear. An article that uses a real structure states the form at that point.

**One article uses a larger algebra by design.** *Left-Right Symmetric Fermions from Complex Split Biquaternions and Bioctonions* studies $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$, and its gammas do not reduce to the sixteen cells; it is the stated exception.

## What Changes and What Does Not

**What does not change.** The algebra is the same, the physics is the same, and every identity holds in both notations. The isomorphism $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$ is exact over $\mathbb{C}$, and the dictionary has five entries.

**What changes.** The default object, the default display, and the starting point of a computation. The biquaternion form begins from the pair the physics supplies; the gamma form begins from a frame and a column.

**What it costs.** A reader trained on gamma matrices must learn the dictionary, and a paper written in the corpus does not look like a paper written in the standard notation. The cost is paid once per reader and is repaid by the appendix.

**What it does not cost.** No result is lost. A computation that needs the frame can be turned into the frame at any point, and each article's appendix states how.

**Where the argument is proved.** *The Gamma Frame and the Complexified Dirac Algebra of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$* states the frame in full and proves the dictionary. *$M_4(\mathbb{C})$ Standard Peirce Representation : Operators* proves the cone, the length theorem and the product criterion. *Operator Family Conventions* fixes the letters $L$, $R$, $T$, $\Theta$. *The Clifford Layer Is the Cell Layer* states the withdrawal rule and the per-article counts, and *The Square-Zero Element* is the first article written under it.

| what is the primitive? | a fixed matrix with a scalar coefficient | a pair of multipliers, seven effective parameters |
| what is a physical element? | a sum of a few fixed matrices, each with a scalar coefficient | a sum of a few pairs |
| what is a spinor? | a column in a chosen basis | an element of $\mathbb{B}$ |
| what is chirality? | $\gamma_5$, one symbol for two roles | a cell $\Gamma$ and the central sign $\chi$ |

**The one-sentence answer.** The gamma frame is a correct coordinate on the algebra, and the biquaternion form is the algebra's own action; the corpus writes the action because the physics supplies a pair of multipliers and a sum of a few terms, not the generic element.

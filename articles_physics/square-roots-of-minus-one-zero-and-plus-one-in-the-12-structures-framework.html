Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the 12 Structures Framework

Introduction

A square root of a central value in one of the twelve structures is an element whose square in that product is the value, $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$. Three values carry a name in the corpus, $\lambda=-1$, $\lambda=0$ and $\lambda=+1$, and each has a physical reading that the framework has already fixed. The roots of $-1$ are the local complex structures, the algebraic form of a phase and of the clock that the corpus reads in The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light and in Square Roots: the Local Complex Structure, the Light Cone and the Mass Shell. The roots of $0$ are the nilpotents, the lightlike displacements, the algebraic form of propagation without mass, read in The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone and in Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework. The roots of $+1$ are the involutions, the reality conditions and the reflections, and the reversible transformations of the state space when the structure is the pairing; their operator face is Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action. This article is the third physics companion of the mathematics comparison group, and its mathematics companion is Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One (articles_maths/square-roots-of-minus-one-zero-and-plus-one.md).

The mathematics companion proves three facts that organise the physics. First, the root set of $0$ is a complex cone with the origin as its apex, while the root sets of $-1$ and $+1$ are affine level sets and carry no apex, so the value $0$ is the value of a direction and the signed values are the values of a magnitude. Second, a root of $-1$ or of $+1$ is a unit of the algebra and a nonzero root of $0$ is a zero divisor, for the four general products and for the four bilateral structures; the two values $-1$ and $+1$ are the reversible end of the invertibility criterion and the value $0$ is the non-invertible end. Third, the two symmetric sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{SPS}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$ lose that rigidity, and the single element $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(e_1+ie_2)$ is a root of $+1$ in the first, a root of $-1$ in the second, and a zero divisor of the algebra in both.

The physical thesis of the article is that the three named values classify the element by what it does: $-1$ gives a phase, $0$ gives a propagation, $+1$ gives a reflection or a symmetry, and the twelve structures differ in which of the three they can produce. Four structures produce all three, two refuse $-1$ because their square is positive, four produce none of the signed values because their square vanishes or is pure, and two produce the signed values but not the rigidity that would make them units. The companion article Square Roots of a General Element in the 12 Structures Framework owns the general radicand, the four existence classes and the mass shell; this article reads the three named values.

Conventions. We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$ and $e_je_k=-\delta_{jk}e_0+\varepsilon_{jkl}e_l$; $i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The central square is $C(\tilde\Xi)e_0=\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{\natural}$ with $C(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\Xi_\mu^{2}$; the Hermitian square is $H(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$; the Krein value is $\Delta(\tilde\Xi)=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$. An element is pure when $\Xi_0=0$ and central when $\mathbf\Xi=0$. The material coordinate is $\tilde\Xi=i c t\,e_0+\mathbf x$ with $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$. The twelve structures are cited by their three-letter names throughout.

One Equation and Three Physical Names

The equation. For a product $\star$ the root set of the value $\lambda e_0$ is $\mathcal R_\lambda=\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0\}$. The square of a scaled element is $\mu^{2}(\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi)$ for the six bilinear structures and $\lvert\mu\rvert^{2}(\tilde\Xi\star \tilde\Xi)$ for the six sesquilinear ones, so the root set of $0$ is invariant under every complex scaling and is a cone, while a root of $-1$ or of $+1$ scaled is a root of another value unless the scaling is a sign or a phase. Physically, the value $0$ sets a direction and no magnitude; the values $-1$ and $+1$ set a magnitude and no ray.

The three names. The three values are the three objects on which the element theory of the algebra is built, and the mapping to physics is fixed by the owners.

  • The roots of $-1$ are the local complex structures. A root $\tilde\Xi$ with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=-e_0$ turns the algebra into a complex vector space, and the idempotent $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ is the projector it defines. Physically a root of $-1$ is a phase: it is the algebraic object that a U(1) rotation multiplies, and the corpus reads the speed of light as the parameter that the material complex structure carries, in The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light.
  • The roots of $0$ are the nilpotents. A nonzero element with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=0$ is a lightlike displacement; a pure root is a null direction of the vector subspace, and its square vanishing is the algebraic statement that two successive displacements along a null direction compose to nothing more than the displacement. The physical locus is the light cone, and the reading is The Light Cone as the Biquaternion Zero-Divisor Cone; the class is Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework.
  • The roots of $+1$ are the involutions. A root with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=+e_0$ is a reflection, a reality condition or a discrete symmetry, and its central pair $\pm e_0$ is the trivial one. When the structure is the pairing, the roots of $+1$ are the transformations that leave the probability form invariant, that is the unitary group, and the physical reading is Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action.

Units and zero divisors. The central square decides the nature of every root. A root of $-1$ or of $+1$ has $C(\tilde\Xi)\neq0$ and is a unit of the algebra, and a nonzero root of $0$ has $C(\tilde\Xi)=0$ and is a zero divisor. The invertibility criterion of the algebra is the non-vanishing of $C$, and the zero-divisor set of the four general products is the cone $C=0$, so the three named values are the two ends of the same criterion read on the square: $\pm1$ are the reversible values, $0$ is the non-reversible one. Physically, an element has a square root of a signed value when a reversible splitting of it exists, and the value $0$ is the value of the elements that cannot be undone at all.

Roots of Minus One: the Phase and the Local Complex Structure

In the plain product. The classification of the mathematics companion gives the roots of $-1$ of $\mathrm{GPA}$ in three pieces: the central pair $\pm i$, the family of the pure real unit vectors $\pm\hat\mu$, and the complex quadric $(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=1$ of the vector subspace, that is the four-parameter family $\mathbf p+i\mathbf p'$ over real vectors with $\mathbf p^{2}-\mathbf p'^{2}=1$ and $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf p'=0$. Every root other than $\pm i$ is pure, so it lies in the vector subspace. The physical reading is the corpus's own: a root of $-1$ is a local complex structure, and the pure real unit vectors are the ones the material sector uses. Each real unit vector $\hat\mu$ gives the complex structure $\hat\mu$ and the idempotent $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$ of the informational sector, so the phase of the plain product and the states of the pairing are the same family read through the halving map; the owner of the map and of the idempotents is Idempotents in the 12 Structures Framework.

In the symmetric plain algebra. The Jordan square equals the plain square, $\tilde\Xi\bullet\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi^{2}$, so $\mathrm{SPA}$ has exactly the roots of $\mathrm{GPA}$. The physics differs in the zero-divisor reading: the pure roots lie in the vector subspace, which is the zero-divisor set of the symmetrised block, so a pure local complex structure is a zero divisor of $\mathrm{SPA}$ although it is a unit of the algebra. A symmetric coupling cannot invert a pure direction, and the phase it defines is one the coupling does not see.

In the quaternionic family. The natural square is the central square, $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi= C(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, for the general product $\mathrm{GQA}$ and for its symmetrisation $\mathrm{SQA}$, so the roots of $-1$ are the quadric $C=-1$, of real dimension six. It contains the central pair $\pm i$ and the imaginary vector roots $i\hat\mu$. The physical reading is the same phase, written on the norm: the quaternionic square of an element is its interval, so a root of $-1$ is an element of interval $-1$.

In the gauge sesquilinear family. The fourth product's square has the Krein value as its scalar part, and the roots of $-1$ are the family at the slopes $\lvert t\rvert<1$ of the parametrisation $\tilde\Xi=c(\hat\mu+it)$ with $(t^{2}-1)\lvert c\rvert^{2}=\lambda$. The slope is a causal parameter: the three named values select the three ranges of $t$, that is the three causal classes of the material sector, and the roots of $-1$ are the family of one class. The owner of the parametrisation and the indefinite metric is The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric.

Where the roots of $-1$ fail. The two definite sesquilinear structures $\mathrm{GPS}$ and $\mathrm{SPS}$ have no root of $-1$, because the scalar part of their square is the non-negative $H$, and $-1$ has negative scalar part. The four antisymmetric structures have none either, because their square vanishes identically in the two alternating cases or is a pure vector in the two conjugate-alternating cases, so it never takes a central value. The physical content of the first failure is positivity: the probability form is positive definite, so the probability band carries no negative square and no complex structure of its own. The phase of the probability band is imported from the composition, and that importation is the step the corpus calls quantisation in Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared. The physical content of the second failure is that a bracket is an infinitesimal transformation: it measures a failure of commutativity, and its square is not a value.

Roots of Zero: the Lightlike Displacement

The cone as the root set. The root set of $0$ is a cone in every one of the twelve structures, and in the four general products it is the cone of the algebra. In $\mathrm{GPA}$ it is the origin together with the nilpotent cone $\{\Xi_0=0,\ \Xi_1^{2}+\Xi_2^{2}+\Xi_3^{2}=0\}$ of real dimension four, and its nonzero elements are the nilpotents. In $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SQA}$ it is the whole cone $C=0$, of real dimension six. In $\mathrm{GPS}$ it is the origin alone, because $H$ vanishes only there. In $\mathrm{GQS}$ it is the family of the slopes $\lvert t\rvert=1$ of the Krein parametrisation, and in $\mathrm{SQS}$ it is the nilpotent cone $\alpha(e_0+i\hat\mu)$, of real dimension four. The physical object is the lightlike displacement.

The two sub-families. The cone of roots of $0$ splits as the cone itself does. The pure roots, $\Xi_0=0$ with $(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=0$, are the nilpotent lightlike displacements of the vector subspace; the non-pure family, reached only in the structures whose square is not the plain square, consists of the square-zero elements of the indefinite bands and carries the lightlike family $c(\hat\mu\pm i)$ of the gauge sesquilinear structure. The elementary physical case is a pure nilpotent $\tilde\Upsilon$ with $\tilde\Upsilon^{2}=0$: a translation along a null direction, whose iteration adds nothing quadratic. The corpus's nilpotent convention and the class are owned by Nilpotents in the 12 Structures Framework, and the square-zero condition of the fourth product belongs to The Square of the General Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product and the Two Halves.

The physical reading of the failure. A root of $0$ is a zero divisor, so the value $0$ is the non-reversible value of the criterion. Physically this is not a defect but the definition of a massless object: a lightlike displacement cannot be undone by a lightlike displacement, because it has no inverse, and the whole cone is the set of operations with no inverse. The rank-drop reading of the same fact, rank two massive and rank one massless, is Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger.

Roots of Plus One: the Involution, the Unitary and the Normalisation

In the plain product. The roots of $+1$ are the images $\tilde\Xi i$ of the roots of $-1$, so they are the central pair $\pm e_0$ and the pure elements with $(\mathbf\Xi,\mathbf\Xi)=-1$. The central pair is the trivial involution; the pure family is a reflection along a direction of the vector subspace, with square the positive unit. Physically an involution is a discrete symmetry, a reversal of a direction, and the corpus reads the family as the reality conditions of the material sector in Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form.

In the probability band, the unitary group. The general plain sesqualgebra has $+1$ as its only signed value: its roots are the unitary family $\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=e_0\}$, the unitary group $U(2)$ of the spinor space, of real dimension four, described as the family $e^{i\theta}w$ over the real unit quaternions $w$. The physical reading is the corpus's operator reading of the pairing: the unitaries are the reversible transformations that leave the probability form invariant, that is the gauge and symmetry group of the state space, and Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action reads the Hermitian conjugation on which they are built. The probability band therefore produces reversible transformations as its roots of $+1$ and no phase as a root of $-1$: the band that measures has a symmetry group and no clock of its own.

The symmetrisation loses the group. In the symmetric plain sesqualgebra the square is the scalar $H(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, so the roots of $+1$ are the quadric $H=1$, the sphere $\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=1$ of $\mathbb{C}^{4}$, of real dimension seven. The group $U(2)$ has grown to the sphere: the symmetrisation forgets the vector part of the square, so it forgets the phase of the unitary and keeps only its norm. The physics is the contrast between unitarity and normalisation: the general pairing's root of $+1$ is a reversible transformation, and the symmetrised pairing's root of $+1$ is a normalised amplitude, a state vector without its phase. The corpus reads the normalised amplitude and the sphere in The Squares and the Positive Cone of the Biquaternion Sesqualgebra and The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone.

In the quaternionic families. The quaternionic square is the central square, so the roots of $+1$ are the quadric $C=+1$ for $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SQA}$, containing the central pair $\pm e_0$ and the real vector roots $\hat\mu$, and the family of the slopes $\lvert t\rvert>1$ with the central circle for $\mathrm{GQS}$, containing $e_0$ and $i$. The physical reading is again the interval: a root of $+1$ is an element of interval $+1$, that is a spacelike unit element of the material sector, and the slope ranges of the gauge sesquilinear structure are its three causal classes.

The Warning of SPS and SQS: a Signed Root That Is Not a Unit

The mathematics companion records one element as the witness of the whole exception, $\tilde w=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(e_1+ie_2)$. It satisfies

$$ \tilde w\star\tilde w=e_0 \quad\text{in }\mathrm{SPS},\qquad \tilde w\star\tilde w=-e_0 \quad\text{in }\mathrm{SQS},\qquad C(\tilde w)=0 , $$

so it is a root of $+1$ in the first structure and a root of $-1$ in the second, and it is a zero divisor of the algebra. The rigidity that the four bilateral structures have, a signed root being a unit, is lost by the two symmetric sesquilinear structures: the symmetrisation of a sesquilinear product forgets the vector part of the square, and a signed root can sit on the cone.

The physical reading is a warning about the symmetrisation of a gauge structure. In the gauge sesquilinear structure a reality condition can be lightlike: the element that squares to minus the identity is a null displacement, not an invertible complex structure, so the structure offers a phase that cannot be undone. The corpus keeps the general fourth product, where the truth lives, as the owner of the indefinite metric, in The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric and The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise, and reads the symmetrised structure as a derived one. The witness is the algebraic reason the two theorems of the companion — the root is a unit, and the root is a level set — must be stated for the general products and not for all twelve.

The Three Objects in the Physics Corpus

The three named values are three elements of the algebra, and each is already the subject of a physical article elsewhere in the corpus. The readings below are not analogies; they are the corpus's own identifications, collected to fix what a root of each value is in the physics.

The root of $-1$ is the clock. The Local Complex Structure and the Speed of Light and Electromagnetism in Media — The Local Complex Structure at Work read the material complex structure as the object that fixes the propagation speed and the constitutive relations of a medium, and The Effective Optical Metric of the Local Complex Structure turns the medium's response into a metric, so the root of $-1$ becomes a geometry. Interpretation of the Wick Rotation in the Biquaternion Universe and The Local Thermal Structure: $k_B$ as the Scale of Imaginary Time read the same element once more, rotated into imaginary time, where its period is a temperature. One element, and three physical names: a phase, a frequency, an imaginary time.

The root of $-1$ is also the half-turn. On the spinor module the rotation by $\pi$ about a unit direction is the element $n\cdot e$, whose square is $-e_0$, so a full turn is a root of $-1$. Kramers Degeneracy and Antiunitary Symmetry in Biquaternionic Form uses exactly this: the time-reversal operator squares to the $2\pi$ rotor, $\mathcal T^{2}=-e_0$, and the minus sign is the spinor's double cover. Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form and The Two-Sheeted Cover and the Topology of Boosts in Biquaternionic Form read the same root as the spin and as the covering, and The Berry Phase and Geometric Phases in Biquaternionic Form reads it as the phase an adiabatic loop accumulates. The clock reading and the half-turn reading are two carriers of one element, which is the corpus's rule of one map and one word per carrier.

The root of $+1$ is the involution, and in the pairing the unit. A root of $+1$ satisfies $\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi^{-1}$, so it is an order-two operation: a reflection, a parity, a reality condition or a discrete symmetry. The Reflection and the Rotation in Biquaternionic Form, Parra's Four Options of the Dirac Equation and the Discrete Symmetries, The CPT Theorem in Biquaternionic Form and Charge Conjugation and the Division Ring: Charged, Neutral and Truly Neutral Particles in Biquaternionic Form all read order-two transformations of the module, and the algebra element that carries one squares to $+e_0$. Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form adds the discipline that the algebra's real structures and the module's discrete symmetries are different real structures on different spaces, so a root of $+1$ of the algebra is not to be identified with the operator of a module symmetry; the value is the same and the carriers are not. When the structure is the probability pairing, the root set of $+1$ is the condition $\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=e_0$, that is the matrix-unitary group $U(2)$, read as the reversible transformations of the state space in Unitarity from Centrality: The Biquaternion Norm-Preservation Theorem and Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action. The two signed values are two different kinds of order-two object. A root of $-1$ is a complex structure: it rotates by a quarter turn and carries a continuous one-parameter group $e^{\theta\tilde\Xi}$, an $SO(2)$. A root of $+1$ is an involution: it is its own inverse and carries only the two signs, a $\mathbb Z_2$. The corpus's root of $-1$ is a generator of a continuous symmetry and its root of $+1$ is a discrete sign, and the framework's own central imaginary $i$ is of the first kind while a parity is of the second. One caution is owed to The Reflection and the Rotation in Biquaternionic Form, which shows that the word "reflection" belongs to neither of these. The biquaternion algebra is the even part of the Clifford algebra, and its elements are the Lorentz rotors, the transformations that turn; a reflection such as $\gamma^0$ is odd, it is not a biquaternion at all, and it acts on spinors by exchanging the two chiralities. A root of $+1$ inside the algebra is therefore an involution — a parity, a real structure — and not a reflection; the reflections live one grade up, outside $\mathbb{B}$. This is the sense in which the three values organise the element theory: $0$ is a direction, $+1$ is a sign, and $-1$ is a rotation.

The root of $0$ is the photon. The nonzero root of $0$ is the lightlike displacement, and the particle the corpus attaches to it is the photon, read in The Photon as a Null Element: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not. That article's division is the sharpest statement of what the value $0$ supplies: the cone derives the propagation speed, the absence of a rest frame and the motion at $c$ for every momentum, and does not derive the value $m=0$, the existence of a mass scale or the reduction to two polarisations. A root of $0$ is a direction and no magnitude, and the photon's masslessness is a value the direction marks but does not choose.

The Clock, the Sky and the Phase

The three named values can be read once more, this time through the corpus's own readings of a clock and of a photon. Two of the readings are stated in the corpus as sentences; the third is an identification the two sentences force. The section keeps the strength of each claim apart.

The root of $-1$ is the tick of a relational clock. Each Sector Is the Other's Clock: the Sector Exchange as Relational Time makes the clock reading precise. The framework has no external time parameter: the material time is the imaginary coefficient $ict$ of the anti-Hermitian sector, the informational time is the real coefficient $ct'$ of the Hermitian sector, and multiplication by the central $i$ exchanges them. The exchange is a quarter turn of the plane of that coordinate, so a tick is a quarter turn and the period of the clock is a full central rotation; the generator of a quarter turn is the central imaginary, which is a root of $-1$. The article also fixes the two things the clock does not supply — the arrow and the rate — so the root of $-1$ is a phase whose direction is a choice of slice and not an output of the algebra. The Relativistic Exchange of Information and Clock Synchronisation in Biquaternionic Form reads the same generator as the operation that synchronises two clocks, and The KMS Condition and the Biquaternion Framework reads it as the modular generator whose imaginary-time correlation functions are periodic with the inverse temperature $\beta$ as their period. One generator, and three clock readings: a tick, a synchronisation, a thermal period.

The root of $-1$ is also the generator of the state's own time. Thermal Time and the Modular Flow in the Biquaternion Framework writes the modular flow of a faithful state as $\sigma_t(\tilde A)=\Delta^{it}\tilde A\Delta^{-it}=e^{-it\tilde K}\tilde A\,e^{it\tilde K}$ with $\tilde K=-\log\Delta$ the modular Hamiltonian. The root of $-1$ is exactly the $i$ that turns the Hermitian modular Hamiltonian into that flow: the generator is $i\tilde K$. The thermal time hypothesis of Connes and Rovelli goes further and proposes that this flow is time, so that the state, not a Hamiltonian, specifies time — and then the root of $-1$ is not merely a clock's phase but the object that converts a state into its time. The corpus keeps the proposal's radical part apart from the framework's share: the biquaternion algebra is a finite-dimensional type I factor, so its modular flow is inner and periodic and the generator lies in the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$, whereas the outer flow that the hypothesis needs belongs to an infinite-dimensional algebra and is absent here.

The root of $-1$ is also the precession. Magnetic Resonance and Rabi Oscillations in Biquaternionic Form drives a spin-$\tfrac12$ in a static field, and the spin precesses at the Larmor frequency — a rotation generated by a root of $-1$ — until a transverse drive at the same frequency interrupts it and the Rabi oscillation moves the state between its two eigenstates. Resonance is the matching of two roots of $-1$: the drive's generator and the precession's generator, tuned to one another. This is the same quarter-turn generator read as a frequency, and it makes the clock reading operational, since a frequency is what a clock is compared against.

The root of $0$ is a point of the sky together with a phase. The Celestial Sphere of the Null Cone: Null Directions, Chirality and the Phase states the reading of a massless element in one sentence: a massless element is a point of the celestial sphere together with a phase. The article is explicit that the norm, being zero, has removed only the scale: both data survive. The point is the direction, a point of $S^2$; the phase is the little-group $U(1)$ that weights the helicity. The root of $0$ therefore carries both, and this sharpens rather than contradicts the photon reading of the previous section: the value $0$ fixes the direction and removes the magnitude, and the phase survives alongside the direction.

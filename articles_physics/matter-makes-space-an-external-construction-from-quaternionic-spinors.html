Matter Makes Space — An External Construction from Quaternionic Spinors

Introduction

The corpus meets the quaternion algebra from inside its own programme: the algebra is chosen, the physical interpretations are made afterwards, and every claim is a claim about what follows from the choice. This article records a source that arrives from the opposite direction. Its author is not working in the biquaternion programme and does not use the corpus's conventions; he takes a bi-quaternionic spinor as the primary object and reports that the tetrad, the torsion and the contorsion are all bilinear composites of it, so that the spacetime metric is precipitated by the spinor rather than assumed. The paper is Julian G. B. Northey, "Matter Makes Space: Unifying Dirac and Einstein–Cartan through Quaternionic Spinors", International Journal of Quantum Foundations 11 (2025) 444–469.

The source is ambitious and its ambitions overlap the corpus's gaps: a single action from which the Dirac, Einstein–Cartan and spin–torsion equations are said to follow; an intrinsic arrow of time from the spinor's phase; a torsion-induced quantum potential; and a conserved torsion entropy current. It also reaches into material the corpus has no equivalent of at all — Kozyrev's causal time, a torsion Bohm potential, torsion waves, and three proposed experiments with numerical sensitivities.

The article has one job, and it is deliberately narrow and unsympathetic. It records the construction, and it tests every display that can be tested by recomputation. The result is a mixed one. Several of the source's displays do not reproduce, and the worst of them go to the construction itself rather than to a typo. The composite tetrad vanishes identically on every plane-wave spinor, which is to say on the free Dirac solutions it is meant to describe. The torsion defined by the source's own Eq. (5) is nonzero on three of its twenty-four independent components, where the Einstein–Cartan spin density the same section equates it to is totally antisymmetric and carries four. The third is different in kind: the Fierz identity of Eq. (3) does reproduce, in every reading, and it is empty. Three of its four terms vanish identically for every spinor, so all it states is that the quaternion norm is multiplicative, whatever the value of its coefficient.

Nothing here endorses the source's programme, and nothing here uses it as evidence for the corpus's. One paper that finds the algebra natural shows that the algebra can be found, not that it must be. The corpus's own position is stated in Curved Spacetime and the Biquaternion Framework and in Torsion, Contorsion and the Spin Connection in Biquaternionic Form: the algebra can carry the frame and the connection, and it generates neither. The source's programme is exactly the step the corpus declines to take — geometry generated by matter — and this article's job is to say precisely how far the step can be taken before it fails.

Frame and cells. Displays are read in the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame, the frame of the source's own matrices, with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the $\mathbb{B}$ frame, where every cell squares to $+I_4$, is named where a display depends on it. The source's $q_\mu$ keep the source's notation: $q_0=1$ and $q_k\in\{i,j,k\}$ are the quaternion units, the corpus's $e_t$ and $\vec e_k$, and $q_\mu$ acting on a field is the left multiplication $L_{q_\mu}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}$. The corpus writes its cells as $L_{a}R_{b}\psi=a\psi b$, with $L_a\psi=a\psi$ and $R_b\psi=\psi b$, and the source's four generators are the frame cells $$ C_\mu=iL_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{axf}},\qquad \nu(t)=x,\quad \nu(k)=y, $$ that is $C_t=iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ for the time index — the left multiplier drops out because $L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}=1$ — and $C_k=iL_{e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ for the three spatial ones. The chirality cell is $\chi=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, the Dirac adjoint is $\Psi^\dagger iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, and the antisymmetrized pair the source writes $q_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12[q_\mu,q_\nu]$ is the bivector built from the cell products, $$ C_\mu C_\nu=-L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}L_{e_\nu}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\nu)}}^{\mathrm{axf}}, \qquad \sigma_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\bigl[C_\mu,C_\nu\bigr]=\tfrac12\bigl(C_\mu C_\nu-C_\nu C_\mu\bigr) . $$ The frame cells satisfy $\{C_\mu,C_\nu\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}I_4$ with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, $\chi^2=I_4$ and $\{\chi,C_\mu\}=0$. Two rules are used silently throughout. The first is $i^2=-1$: the unit belongs to the cell, so with $C_\mu=i\,L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ the product $iC_\mu=i^2L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{axf}}=-L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ carries none, and a product of two cells brings one sign flip and no second unit. The second is that $\mathrm{ReTr}$ keeps the central part of its argument. Only $I_4$ has a trace among the sixteen directions — every non-scalar direction of the algebra is traceless — so the trace of an element is its scalar coefficient times the dimension, and a display in $\mathrm{ReTr}$ is a display in the scalar part up to that factor. Two traces are in play, and the factor differs. The paper's $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}$ is the two-dimensional one, with $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_\mu q_\nu)=2\delta_{\mu\nu}$ and hence factor $2$, and it is the trace the Fierz derivation cites. The spinor trace of Eqs. (4)–(6) is the four-dimensional one, with factor $4$. The factor is carried wherever a numerical coefficient is at stake and dropped wherever only the vanishing of a display is.

The bilinear rule. It follows from the second rule and is the workhorse of every support count below. For an element $a$ with real coefficients, the bilinear sees only the coefficient of $e_t$, $$ \mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,a\,\Psi\bigr)=a_0\,\mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi\bigr), $$ because the vector part of $a$ contributes only to the imaginary part of $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}a\Psi$. It is verified on random pairs to machine precision, and it is why so many of the source's bilinears are either a fixed multiple of $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi$ or zero. Real coefficients matter: a biquaternion $a$ with complex ones does contribute a real part through its vector part, and none of the source's displays has that feature.

Where the gammas are. The Clifford names $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]$ of these cells are used in one section only, The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation; nothing before it needs them, because the cells are written out directly. The only earlier places a $\gamma$ appears are the source's own Eq. (1) and the two lines of block arithmetic that test it, kept in the source's letters because those displays are themselves the object under review.

The Source's Aims

The paper's plan is stated in its Section I: Dirac spinors are taken as bi-quaternionic fields, spacetime geometry is taken as emergent from them, and both are to follow from a single variational principle. Its announced results are a composite tetrad and contorsion built from spinor bilinears, a torsion that is the "spin-squared of quaternionic commutators", a constrained action with Lagrange multipliers, an intrinsic time from the spinor phase, torsion-wave solutions carrying an entropy current, and three experimental predictions.

The corpus is not the intended audience, and the source does not engage with it. The overlap is structural: the source's "$q_\mu$" are the corpus's algebra units, its bi-quaternionic spinor is the corpus's complex spinor, and its programme is the corpus's agenda question — whether the algebra or the matter field can generate geometry — answered in the affirmative. Whether the answer survives recomputation is what this article establishes, and in the places that matter most it does not.

The Source's Algebra, and Four Displays Under Test

The source builds a four-generator representation out of quaternions in its Section 2. With the quaternion units $q_0=1$ and $q_k=-i\sigma_k$ written as $2\times2$ matrices, and entries that stand for $2\times2$ blocks, Eq. (1) prints $$ \gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-]=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]=\begin{pmatrix}0&q_k\\-q_k&0\end{pmatrix}, $$ and Section 2.2 asserts the anticommutation relations $\{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}$ from it, with signature $(+,-,-,-)$. Four displays in that section and the next are tested here; three of them fail outright, and the fourth holds and is empty.

The block map is off by the factor $i$. The printed spatial block squares to $$ \gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]^2=-q_k^2\otimes I_2 , $$ and the declared units have $q_k^2=-1$, so $\gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]^2=+I_4$ and $\{\gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=+2I_4$, where the signature demands $-I_4$ and $-2I_4$. The substitution that repairs it is $q_k\mapsto iq_k$, which gives $q_k^2=+1$ and hence $\gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]^2=-I_4$; it is the substitution the paper never makes. The block map is therefore not a Clifford map for the $q_k$ the same section defines, and the consistent choice for that $q_k$ is the sign the source does not print.

The trace normalisation contradicts the embedding. The source fixes the normalisation by $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_\mu q_\nu)=2\delta_{\mu\nu}$, twice over — Section 2.3 prints the four entries one by one and Appendix A.5 prints them in one line — and Appendix A.5 states the embedding explicitly, $q_0\mapsto\sigma_0$ and $q_k\mapsto-i\sigma_k$. With that embedding the computed values are $+2$ for the time–time entry and $-2$ for the three spatial ones, so $$ \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_\mu q_\nu)=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu},\qquad \varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1), $$ and not $2\delta_{\mu\nu}$. The printed $2\delta_{\mu\nu}$ is the normalisation of $q_k=+\sigma_k$, the same substitution that repairs the block map: one change of convention repairs both displays, which is how the two defects are known to be one. The price is that $q_k^2=+1$, which Appendix A.1 denies with $i^2=j^2=k^2=-1$; the three statements — A.1's units, the block map and the trace rule — cannot all hold, and the paper prints all three. As printed, the trace normalisation and the block map cannot be used together, and this is the seed of the ambiguity in the Fierz identity below, whose coefficient is fixed only once the reading of $\mathrm{Re}$ is.

The completeness relation is over a four-element basis, not sixteen. The source's Eq. (2) expands $$ \sum_A \check q_A\otimes q_A=1\otimes1+\tfrac12\sum_\mu q_\mu\otimes q_\mu+\tfrac{1}{2!}\sum_{\mu<\nu}q_{\mu\nu}\otimes q_{\mu\nu}+\cdots , $$ with $\check q_A$ the dual basis, and the comment that the coefficient $\tfrac12$ ensures $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_\mu q_\nu)=2\delta_{\mu\nu}$. The listed elements, however, are the four quaternion units and their antisymmetrized products $q_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12[q_\mu,q_\nu]$, and the products add nothing: for the units $[q_0,q_i]=0$ for all three spatial $i$, and $[q_i,q_j]=2\epsilon_{ijk}q_k$ is parallel to a unit. So $q_{0i}=0$ and each $q_{ij}$ is a sign times one of the units, and the list spans four independent elements of $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}$, not the sixteen of the Clifford basis $$ \bigl\{1,\ \chi,\ C_\mu,\ \chi C_\mu,\ \sigma_{\mu\nu}\bigr\},\qquad \mu<\nu , $$ that its own Appendix B.1 uses, with $1+1+4+4+6=16$ elements. The ellipsis in Eq. (2) is therefore doing the work the four-element list cannot: it has to supply the twelve elements of the Clifford basis that the listed terms never reach.

The Fierz identity's coefficient depends on a notation the display does not fix, and the identity is empty either way. The source's Eq. (3) reads $$ (\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Phi)(\Phi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)=\tfrac14\sum_A \mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\check q_A\Psi\bigr)\ \mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Phi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_A\Phi\bigr) ,\qquad q_A \in \{1, q_\mu, q_{\mu\nu}, \dots\} , $$ "where the coefficient $\tfrac14$ derives from $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q^Aq_A)=2$". The display is ambiguous, because $\mathrm{Re}$ alone is not the $\mathrm{ReTr}$ of Eqs. (4)–(6), and the coefficient depends on which is meant. Read literally — $\mathrm{Re}$ the real part of a quaternion, no trace — the left side is the real number $\lvert\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Phi\rvert^2=\lvert\Psi\rvert^2\lvert\Phi\rvert^2$ and the coefficient is one: $$ \boxed{\;(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Phi)(\Phi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)=\sum_{A\in\{1,q_1,q_2,q_3\}} \mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_A\Psi\bigr)\ \mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Phi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_A\Phi\bigr)\;} $$ Read in the two-dimensional quaternion trace the derivation sentence itself cites, $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_Aq_A)=2$, each bilinear is twice the quaternion real part, the sum is four times as large, and the coefficient is $\tfrac14$ — which is what the display prints. That is one of three normalisations available in the same paper and the only one for which the printed number is right: with the plain $\mathrm{Re}$ of the display the coefficient is $1$, and with the spinor trace of Eqs. (4)–(6), which counts a central element four times rather than twice, it is $\tfrac1{16}$. The identity itself is exact in all three readings on random pairs, at residual zero; only the coefficient changes, and it changes with the square of the trace dimension. So the printed number is not an arithmetic slip: it is the right number for the trace its own derivation sentence cites and the wrong number for the trace the composites use, and the symbol $\mathrm{Re}$ is what leaves the reader unable to tell which of the two is meant. The stated derivation then fits only in the form $\tfrac{1}{2\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_Aq_A)}=\tfrac14$; taken as $1/\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_Aq_A)=\tfrac12$ it gives the wrong coefficient for every channel, and gives the same one for all of them, which is why it cannot be checked by inspection.

What is wrong with the identity is that it is empty. In either reading the three non-scalar terms vanish identically, because $$ \mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_k\Psi\bigr)=0 $$ for the three imaginary units and every spinor, real or complex — the scalar part is $i$ times a real number. Both sides therefore reduce to multiplicativity of the quaternion norm, $(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Phi)(\Phi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)=\lvert\Psi\rvert^2\lvert\Phi\rvert^2$. An identity that holds because three of its four terms are zero carries no information about the spinor, and the claim it is offered to support — that it "enables closed-form expression of all geometric quantities as spinor bilinears" — is not supported by it. A Fierz rearrangement earns that description when it trades a product of bilinears for a sum, not when everything but the scalar channel has already vanished.

One restriction is independent of the normalisation. Over complex coefficients the left side is the complex number $\lvert\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Phi\rvert^2=\lvert\Psi\rvert^2\lvert\Phi\rvert^2$, whose real and imaginary parts are both nonzero for a generic pair — the quaternionic norm of a biquaternion is not real — while the right side is real. No value of the coefficient restores the identity there: it is an identity of the real quaternion algebra, and the source's $\Psi\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}$ is outside its range.

The Composite Geometry and the Constrained Action

The heart of the construction is the source's Section 4. With $\kappa\equiv8\pi G$ the source defines three bilinear composites, $$ e^a{}_\mu=\kappa^{-1}\,\mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,C_a\,\partial \Psi/\partial x_\mu\bigr),\qquad T_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\,\mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,\tfrac12[q_\mu,q_\nu]\,q_\rho\,\Psi\bigr),\qquad K_{\mu ab}=\kappa\,\mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,\sigma_{ab}\,\partial \Psi/\partial x_\mu\bigr), $$ and identifies them with the tetrad, the torsion tensor and the contorsion. The frame cell $C_a=iL_{e_a}R_{\vec e_{\nu(a)}}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ carries the tetrad's frame slot, and $\sigma_{ab}=\tfrac12[C_a,C_b]$ is exactly the source's antisymmetrised product $\tfrac12(q^aq^b-q^bq^a)$ written in cells, so the contorsion needs no new letter. In Eq. (5) the multipliers are the units and not the cells: $q_\mu$ acts by left multiplication, that is by $L_{q_\mu}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, and the product $\tfrac12[q_\mu,q_\nu]q_\rho$ is an element of the algebra. Throughout, the tetrad of Eq. (4) is written $\exp(a){}_\mu$ for the source's $e^a{}_\mu$, the frame index in parentheses so that it does not collide with the basis unit $e_\mu$, and $e$ is $\det\exp(a){}_\mu$.

The source then matches the composites to the standard Einstein–Cartan–Sciama–Kibble objects by $T_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\,\sigma_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$, with $\sigma_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ the totally antisymmetric spin density of three frame cells, $$ \sigma_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,\operatorname{Alt}\bigl[C_\rho C_\mu C_\nu\bigr]\Psi , $$ where $\operatorname{Alt}$ is the normalised antisymmetrisation, the average over the six permutations weighted by their signs. It states that the contorsion–torsion relation is "automatically satisfied via Fierz identities".

The consistency checks are tautologies or false. The three checks offered for Eqs. (4)–(6) do not test the construction. Check 1, that $T_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ is antisymmetric in $\mu,\nu$, follows from the antisymmetry of the commutator and uses neither the spinor nor the trace. Check 2 displays $$ \mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,\tfrac12(q_\mu q_\nu+q_\nu q_\mu)\,q_\rho\,\Psi\bigr)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_{\rho\,(\mu\nu)}=0 , $$ and the left side is not zero. The halved anticommutator is $g_{\mu\mu}[\mathbb{M}_-]$ at $\mu=\nu$; the spatial unit itself when one index is the time index; and zero only when the two indices are distinct and both spatial. So the display vanishes except where the product $\tfrac12(q_\mu q_\nu+q_\nu q_\mu)q_\rho$ collapses to a scalar. On those ten index triples it is $\pm\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)$ exactly, with the coefficient $+1$ at $(\rho,\mu,\nu)=(0,0,0)$ and $-1$ on the other nine. A scalar density of the spinor is not a torsion at any index; and $T_{\rho(\mu\nu)}$ is not defined in any case, since $T$ is built from the commutator alone. Check 3 is a single component with a proportionality sign. That component is $T^3{}_{12}$, one of the three that Eq. (5) leaves nonzero, and the source reads it as a confirmation: it is a confirmation of Eq. (5), and it is also the first sight of the three-component support established below. A fourth check, "Clifford compatibility: guaranteed by Section 2", is not a check of Eqs. (4)–(6) at all; it defers to the block map, and the block map is off by the factor $i$.

The vanishing of the three imaginary bilinears, established above and used repeatedly below, is exact: with $\Psi=u+iv$ in real quaternions $u,v$ the scalar part of $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_k\Psi$ is $i\,[\mathrm{Sc}(u^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_k v)-\mathrm{Sc}(v^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_k u)]$, a pure imaginary number whose real part is zero. The spatial components of the source's "vector current" are therefore zero for every spinor, and only the time component, $\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)$, survives.

The printed contorsion–torsion relation carries the last index in the wrong order. The source prints $K_{ab\mu}=\tfrac12(T_{\mu ab}-T_{ab\mu}+T_{ba\mu})$, with the frame pair first. The relation that is exact for any torsion, and antisymmetric in its first and third slots, is $$ K_{\mu ab}=\tfrac12\bigl(T_{\mu ab}-T_{ab\mu}+T_{b\mu a}\bigr), $$ whose third term carries the order $b\mu a$. On a totally antisymmetric torsion the three cyclic orders coincide, $T_{\mu ab}=T_{ab\mu}=T_{b\mu a}$, so the standard bracket is $T_{\mu ab}-T_{\mu ab}+T_{\mu ab}=T_{\mu ab}$ and the contorsion is $\tfrac12T_{\mu ab}$; the printed third term $T_{ba\mu}$ is the odd permutation and equals $-T_{\mu ab}$, so the printed bracket is $-T_{\mu ab}$ and the printed contorsion is $-\tfrac12T_{\mu ab}$. On $T_{012}=T_{120}=T_{201}=1$ the printed form gives $-\tfrac12$ where the standard one gives $+\tfrac12$: the coefficient $\tfrac12$ is right and the sign is reversed.

That is a sign convention rather than an arithmetic slip, and it is the more awkward defect for being one. A contorsion defined by $\Gamma=\{\,\}-K$ instead of $\Gamma=\{\,\}+K$ makes the printed form correct, and the paper never states which convention it uses; its two displays, $K_{ab\mu}$ in Section 5.1 and $K_{\mu ab}$ in Section 5.7, agree with each other to $-\tfrac12T$ and never fix it. Since the same $K$ is then built as the composite $\kappa\,\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\sigma_{ab}\partial \Psi/\partial x_\mu)$ and fed into the torsion–spin relation of Appendix B, the sign of the matter coupling is left undetermined by the text.

The composite tetrad vanishes on the free Dirac solutions. This is the decisive failure of the section. For a plane wave $\Psi=\psi\exp(ik\cdot x)$ with constant $\psi$, the derivative gives $\partial \Psi/\partial x_\mu=ik_\mu\Psi$, so, with $n$ the dimension of the trace, $$ e^a{}_\mu=\kappa^{-1}\,\mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,C_a\,ik_\mu\psi\bigr) =n\,\kappa^{-1}k_\mu\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl(i\,\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_a\psi\bigr)=0 , $$ and the vanishing is independent of whether $n$ is $2$ or $4$. The bilinear $\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_a\psi=\psi^\dagger C_tC_a\psi$ is real for any spinor, because $C_tC_a$ is Hermitian for all four cells — and $C_t$ is exactly the cell in the adjoint, $\Psi^\dagger C_t$ — so $\psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_a\psi$ times the unit $i$ is pure imaginary and its real part vanishes. The composite tetrad is therefore zero for every plane-wave spinor, verified to $4\times10^{-16}$ on random spinors, and the induced metric $g_{\mu\nu}=g_{ab}[\mathbb{M}_-]\exp(a){}_\mu\exp(b){}_\nu$ is the zero metric. The construction does not merely fail to be a tetrad — it assigns no geometry at all to the free Dirac field, which is the only field the paper's action contains before the constraints are imposed. No check in the paper detects this, and Section 4.2's "Example verification" does not evaluate Eq. (4).

Three of the twenty-four torsion components survive. The object of Eq. (5) is not a torsion tensor with a minority of its components missing; it is a three-component object. Two facts account for all of it, and the bilinear rule turns both into one line. Every component with a $0$ in the antisymmetric pair vanishes, because $\tfrac12[q_0,q_k]=0$. For the six spatial pairs $q_iq_j=\epsilon_{ijk}q_k$, so the element whose $e_t$-coefficient the bilinear reads is $\epsilon_{ijk}q_kq_\rho$. That product is $\pm e_t$ only where $\rho$ is the unit $q_k$ of the pair — that is, on the three cyclic triples — and there $q_k^2=-1$, so the coefficient is $-1$. The three reversed triples are their antisymmetric partners and carry no fourth component. Hence $$ T_{3\,12}=T_{1\,23}=T_{2\,31}=-\kappa\,\mathrm{ReTr}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi\bigr), \qquad\text{all other independent components zero}, $$ verified exactly on random spinors: three of the twenty-four independent components are nonzero, and the only other entries are their antisymmetric partners $T_{\rho\,\nu\mu}=-T_{\rho\,\mu\nu}$.

The output index wanders between displays. Eq. (5) prints $T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ with the output index up and the raised unit $q^\rho$ multiplying, and the display above is the same object with both low. Two signs meet there and cancel: lowering the tensor's output index costs one $\varepsilon_\rho$, because $T_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon_\rho T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$, and lowering the unit costs another, because $q^\rho=\varepsilon_\rho q_\rho$. So $\varepsilon_\rho^2=+1$ and the all-low form is the same tensor with the same sign, not a convention change. Section 11 step 2 prints the same object with the output slot at the other end, $T_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\kappa^{-1}\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,[q_\lambda,q_\mu]\,q_\nu\Psi)$, where the multiplier is $q_\nu$ and the free slot is therefore the last one — and the same step prints the Fierz relation it feeds from as $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_\lambda\sigma^{\mu\nu}\Psi=2\kappa T_\lambda{}^{\mu\nu}$, with the free slot first. The two displays of one paragraph therefore disagree with each other, and not only with Eq. (5). For an object antisymmetric in one pair only, which slot carries the output is not bookkeeping: the entropy current contracts all three indices against $\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, and the contraction is blind to the displacement only if the tensor is totally antisymmetric, which Eq. (5) is not. The paper's own Section 4.2 example settles which placement it intends and then mis-signs it: with the unit raised, $\tfrac12[q_1,q_2]q^3=q_3(-q_3)=+1$ and $T^3{}_{12}=+\kappa\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)$, while the example prints $-\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)$ — the value obtained from the lowered unit $q_3$ multiplying a commutator whose own indices are raised.

The object the same section matches it to, $\sigma_\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ of the display above, is totally antisymmetric and carries four independent components, nonzero on the time–space triples where Eq. (5) is zero. The two therefore have disjoint support, and it is the matching that carries the claim that the source reproduces Einstein–Cartan.

The couplings do not close dimensionally. The source gives the tetrad a factor $\kappa^{-1}$ and the contorsion a factor $\kappa$ while setting both couplings to the same $\kappa=8\pi G$; its Section 6 "Coupling Constant Verification" prints $\kappa_e=\kappa_K=8\pi G=8\pi G$, a chain whose last two entries are the same number. The two bullets that follow restate which coupling multiplies which field rather than checking either. With the canonical dimension $[\Psi]=M^{3/2}$ and $[G]=M^{-2}$, the tetrad composite has dimension $$ [\kappa^{-1}\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\partial\Psi]=M^{+2}\,M^{3/2}\,M\,M^{3/2}=M^{6}, $$ where a tetrad must be dimensionless, and the contorsion composite has dimension $$ [\kappa\,\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\partial\Psi]=M^{-2}\,M^{3/2}\,M\,M^{3/2}=M^{2}, $$ where a contorsion must have dimension $M$, the dimension of an inverse length. No single dimension for $\Psi$ repairs this: a dimensionless tetrad needs $[\Psi]=M^{-3/2}$ and a contorsion of dimension $M$ needs $[\Psi]=M^{+1}$, and the paper assigns one $\Psi$. The torsion fares the same way against itself. Eq. (5) defines it with $\kappa$ in the numerator. The Two Dirac Equations

The kinetic term of that action is one Dirac operator, the corpus's Dirac equation is another, and the two are not the same operator written twice, so they are displayed separately. Kozyrev's Causal Time

Section 9.2 embeds Kozyrev's "time current" as the quaternionic phase of the spinor, defining $\tau(x)=\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathbb{H}}\Psi(x)$ and giving its gradient as $$ \partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tau=\frac{\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,q_5q_\mu\Psi\bigr)}{\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi\bigr)} , \qquad q_5=ijk , $$ with $q_5$ described as "the 'imaginary' unit in $\mathbb{H}$ that anticommutes with $q_\mu$". That description cannot be right, and the failure is complete rather than partial. In the quaternion units the product $q_5=ijk$ is the real scalar $$ q_5=ijk=(-1)\,e_t , $$ verified directly: it is central, commuting with every $q_\mu$ and with everything else, and it is not a unit imaginary at all. There is no element of $\mathbb{H}$ that anticommutes with all four $q_\mu$, because every quaternion commutes with $q_0=1$. Substituting $q_5=-1$ into the displayed gradient gives $$ \partial/\partial x_\mu\,\tau=-\,\frac{\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_\mu\Psi\bigr)}{\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi\bigr)} , $$ whose time component is $$ \partial/\partial x_0\,\tau=-\,\frac{\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_0\Psi\bigr)}{\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi\bigr)}=-1 . $$ The three spatial components vanish identically, because $\mathrm{Re}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_k\Psi)=0$ for the imaginary units, so the time component is the whole of it and the gradient is the constant covector $$ \partial \tau/\partial x_\mu=(-1,0,0,0) $$ for every $\Psi$. It is the gradient of $-t$ and of nothing else, it carries no information about the field, and it cannot distinguish one spinor from another. Nor does any other reading of $q_5$ rescue the display: $q_5=i$ gives $(0,-1,0,0)$, $q_5=j$ gives $(0,0,-1,0)$, $q_5=k$ gives $(0,0,0,-1)$, and the reversed product $ikj=+1$ gives $(+1,0,0,0)$ — a constant covector in every case. The gradient is therefore the fixed covector of scalar parts of $qq_\mu$, for every $\Psi$, and no choice of $q_5$ can make it depend on the field. The type mismatch is visible before any evaluation: $\tau=\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathbb{H}}\Psi$ is a unit quaternion, so $\partial \tau/\partial x_\mu$ is quaternion-valued, whereas the printed formula returns the real number $\mathrm{Re}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_5q_\mu\Psi)/\mathrm{Re}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)$; a four-component derivative cannot be given by one real ratio. The Madelung-type relation the source needs — a phase gradient given by an axial-vector-to-density ratio — is not what its formula produces, so the intrinsic time field $\tau=\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathbb{H}}\Psi$ is not recovered, and the claim that causal asymmetry is realised "intrinsically, without new fields" has no support.

Torsion Waves and the Entropy Current

Section 11 derives a wave equation for the torsion, $\Box T_{\lambda\mu\nu}=0$, by acting twice with the covariant derivative on the Dirac equation and imposing the teleparallel condition $\mathrm{Riem}_{\mu\nu ab}=0$. It then introduces the pseudo-vector $$ J_{S\mu}=\alpha\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}T_{\nu\rho\sigma}, \qquad \alpha=\frac{1}{3\cdot8\pi G} , $$ written here with every index low; raising the free index costs one sign, $\epsilon^{0123}=-\epsilon_{0123}$. The source states that "the wave equation for $T$ together with antisymmetry of $\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ implies $\partial/\partial x_\mu J_{S\mu}=0$", reading the conservation as "the field-theoretic avatar of the second law of thermodynamics".

The wave equation itself is not derived. The chain that produces $\Box T=0$ runs: apply $D_\nu D^\nu$ to the Dirac equation, obtain $$ (\Box-m^2)\Psi=-\tfrac14 \mathrm{Riem}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\alpha\beta}\Psi-\tfrac12 T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\,C^\mu D_\rho\Psi , $$ multiply by $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_\lambda$, and invoke the Fierz relation $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\,C_\lambda\sigma^{\mu\nu}\Psi=2\kappa T_\lambda{}^{\mu\nu}$. Two things go wrong at once. The curvature term is set to zero by the teleparallel condition, which leaves the single term $-\tfrac12 T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_\lambda C^\mu D_\rho\Psi$ — one power of $T$ multiplying a derivative bilinear, not a wave operator acting on $T$. And on the left there is $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_\lambda(\Box-m^2)\Psi$, which is not $\Box$ of any bilinear either; $\Box T$ would be $2(\partial\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}})(\partial\Psi)+2\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\partial^2\Psi$ at leading order, and what Eq. (24) supplies for $\partial^2\Psi$ is a term proportional to $T\partial\Psi$. Substituting gives $\Box T$ proportional to $T\,\partial\Psi\,\Psi$ and to $(\partial\Psi)^2$, neither of which vanishes. The source's footnote, "up to total derivatives", does not bridge the gap: a total derivative changes the action, not an order in $T$. So $\Box T=0$ is asserted at the point where the derivation should be, and the three results built on it — light-speed torsion waves, the conserved entropy current, and the pulse-propagation estimate — inherit the assertion.

The implication does not hold, and the failure is an index count. The divergence is an epsilon contraction of the gradient, $$ \partial^\mu J_{S\mu}=\alpha\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\partial^\mu T_{\nu\rho\sigma} , $$ and the epsilon needs four distinct indices, so the contraction keeps only the part of the gradient that is antisymmetric in all four — contracting the antisymmetrised gradient, which in four dimensions has the single independent component $\frac{\partial T_{123]}}{\partial x_{[0}}$. Hence the divergence is $4!\,\alpha\,\frac{\partial T_{123]}}{\partial x_{[0}}$, verified numerically on random fields, and it vanishes only when that component does — that is, only when the three-form part of $T$ is closed — while $\Box T=0$ neither requires nor supplies closedness: the wave equation constrains the four components one at a time and says nothing about the antisymmetrised gradient. An explicit counterexample settles it: the field $T_{123}(x)=x_0$, all other components zero, satisfies $\Box T_{\lambda\mu\nu}=0$ identically, while — taking $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$, so that $\partial^0=\partial/\partial t$ — $$ J_{S0}=\alpha\,\epsilon_{0123}\,T_{123}=\alpha\,x_0, \qquad \frac{\partial J_{S\mu}}{\partial x_\mu}=\alpha\,\epsilon_{0123}\,\frac{\partial T_{123}}{\partial x^0}=\alpha\ne0 , $$ verified directly. Conservation would hold if $T_{\nu\rho\sigma}$ were the dual of the gradient of a harmonic potential, $T_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\epsilon_{\nu\rho\sigma\lambda}\partial/\partial x_\lambda\theta$ with $\Box\theta=0$; the source neither assumes that form nor derives it. The claimed second-law avatar therefore rests on an identity that fails.

A separate objection, which would stand even if the wave equation had been derived: since the torsion is defined from the spinor by Eq. (5) and carries no independent dynamics, $\Box T=0$ is not an independent wave equation but a constraint on $\Psi$; and Eq. (5)'s $T$ has three nonzero components, all of them the same bilinear $-\kappa\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)$. The source's step 4 reads this as a virtue, calling the torsion waves "genuine emergent, massless degrees of freedom" that propagate "without enlarging the field content". The second half of that is right and is exactly the difficulty: nothing is added because nothing independent is there. A field whose every component is one and the same bilinear of $\Psi$ has the dynamics of $\Psi$ and none of its own, so "degrees of freedom" in the counting sense is the wrong description of it.

The Fierz Chain of Appendix B

The Appendix B chain is the source of the factor-2 relation used in Section 11, and it is where an exact error lives. The appendix needs the trace of the chirality cell with two frame cells and a bivector, and prints it as $$ \mathrm{Tr}\bigl[\chi\,C_\rho\,C_\lambda\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}\bigr]=4\,\epsilon_{\rho\lambda}{}^{\mu\nu} . $$ This $\mathrm{Tr}$ is the plain trace of the $4\times4$ product and not its real part. The distinction is the whole content of the error. The trace is pure imaginary on every index quadruple, so $\mathrm{ReTr}$ of the same argument vanishes; the printed right-hand side is therefore a statement about the plain trace, and against the plain trace it is off by the unit $i$. The exact value is $$ \boxed{\;\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[\chi\,C_\rho\,C_\lambda\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}\bigr]=-\,4i\,\epsilon_{\rho\lambda\mu\nu}\;} $$ with the four indices all low and $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$, verified at residual exactly zero over all index quadruples: the trace takes the two values $\pm4i$ on the twenty-four quadruples with four distinct indices and vanishes on all others. Its $\epsilon_{\rho\lambda}{}^{\mu\nu}$ also carries two raised indices, each of which costs a sign on the standing conventions, and the chain built on the display inherits both defects. It is the same trace of the chirality cell with two frame cells and a bivector recorded for the corpus in The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation below, with the chirality cell in the first slot.

The second error is structural. The appendix evaluates $B_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_\lambda\sigma_{\mu\nu}\Psi$, finds that "the only nonzero contribution comes from the axial-vector channel", and concludes that $B_{\lambda\mu\nu}$ is $\epsilon_\rho{}_{\lambda\mu\nu}$ times the axial bilinear $A_\rho=\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\chi C_\rho\Psi$. On the twenty-four triples with three distinct indices that conclusion is exact, $B_{\lambda\mu\nu}=-i\epsilon_{\lambda\mu\nu\rho}\varepsilon_\rho A_\rho$. For the six of them whose first index is the time index the arithmetic is even transparent, because $C_tC_\lambda=C_0^2=I$ collapses the product to the bare bivector and a bivector bilinear is by construction the epsilon-dual of the axial one, $$ \Psi^\dagger\sigma_{\mu\nu}\Psi=B_{0\mu\nu}=-i\,\epsilon_{0\mu\nu\rho}\,\varepsilon_\rho\,A_\rho , $$ so there the channel count and the conclusion agree.

The disagreement begins where an index repeats. On the twenty-four triples in which the first index coincides with exactly one of the other two, the epsilon vanishes and the bilinear does not: $B_{001}$ is nonzero and real for a generic spinor, because $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}C_0\sigma_{01}\Psi=\Psi^\dagger C_tC_0\sigma_{01}\Psi=\Psi^\dagger\sigma_{01}\Psi$, and $\sigma_{01}$ is Hermitian. The bivectors split by type: $\sigma_{0k}$ is Hermitian and $\sigma_{ij}$ anti-Hermitian, on all six combinations. So $B$ is not the epsilon channel, and the printed display cannot be an identity for the bilinear altogether: it holds on forty of the sixty-four triples, and the twenty-four that fail are exactly those where the first index coincides with one — not both — of the other two. The Empirical Claims, and the Limits of the Paper

Section 13.3 and its appendices reach furthest. The source lists magnetar X-ray polarimetry, entangled atom interferometry and a cryogenic high-frequency torsion-wave search, with required torsion amplitudes around $7\times10^{-28}\,\mathrm{m}^{-1}$ and current $3\sigma$ limits within a factor of a few, and adds a Stern–Gerlach phase bifurcation and an electro-torsional Aharonov–Bohm phase in its appendices. These are quantitative and, in form, falsifiable, which is a virtue the corpus's gravity articles do not exhibit.

Their evidentiary problem is upstream of them. Each number is computed from a coupling that failed the checks above. The torsion amplitude comes from Eq. The Aharonov–Bohm phase comes from a quaternionic connection whose torsion part is the same object. A sensitivity calculation built on a wave equation such as $\Box T=0$, or on a current that is not conserved, cannot constrain the theory it is meant to test.

Appendix D carries its own dimensional gap, in an equation of two terms. Its phase is $$ \Delta\theta=\frac{q\Phi_B}{\hbar}+\beta\,\frac{\Phi_T}{\hbar}\bigl(\sigma_z^B-\sigma_z^A\bigr), \qquad \beta=\tfrac12 , $$ with $\Phi_T$ called "the torsion flux" and never defined. The magnetic term is dimensionally complete: a flux times a charge is an action, and an action over $\hbar$ is a phase. The torsion term cannot be, whatever $\Phi_T$ is taken to be. If it is the flux of the torsion, $\int T\cdot dA$, its dimension is a length; the ratio to $\hbar$ is then length over action, which is not a phase, and the dimensionless $\beta=\tfrac12$ supplies nothing. If instead $\Phi_T$ is given units of action, the appendix never says so, and no display connects it to the torsion that the rest of the paper computes. The coefficient that the term needs is a coupling with the dimension of $\hbar$ per unit torsion flux, and that is exactly the slot where the missing power of $\hbar$ of Eq. (26) sits. The tables are recorded here as the source's claims, with the observation that none of them survives the recomputations above, and therefore that their numerical agreement with current sensitivities should not be read as evidence for the framework.

Their arithmetic is also worth one line, because it can be checked from the printed page alone. The worked example after Eq. What Would Make It a Framework Claim

It is worth stating what the source would have to show for the corpus to record its programme as a candidate rather than as a claim with errors. The corpus's standard for a constructive claim is that the object be built, the identities checked, and the boundary stated.

  • A nonzero composite tetrad on the free solutions. The predicate fails at the first step: Eq. (4) is zero for every plane wave. A construction whose frame vanishes on the field it is built from needs a different bilinear, and the correction is not cosmetic, since the index structure and the derivative count both change.
  • A torsion of the size it claims. Eq. (5) must agree with the totally antisymmetric spin density $\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\operatorname{Alt}[C_\rho C_\mu C_\nu]\Psi$, and it cannot while it is built from the four-element commutator: three components against four, with disjoint support.
  • An identity chain that carries information. The trace identity must carry its factor $i$, the bilinear must be antisymmetrised where the axial channel is claimed, the coupling must sit on the side Einstein–Cartan puts it, and the Fierz rearrangement must be one in which more than the scalar channel survives.
  • A constraint that constrains, or an action that is what it says. If the Lagrange multipliers only restate definitions and their own equation of motion is not solved, the action does not rise to a theory of the frame.
  • A conservation law that follows. The entropy current requires the torsion to be the dual of a harmonic potential; that assumption must be made or the conservation dropped.
  • One dimension per object. Eq. (4), Eq. (6), the teleparallel constraint and the torsion potential each mix powers of mass inside a single display. A construction that is to be read as geometry has to survive a units check before it is read at all.

None of these is a philosophical objection, and none requires the corpus's conventions. They are the arithmetic and index checks that any construction of this kind has to pass, and on this pass it does not.

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices. Summary

The paper records a bi-quaternionic spinor as the primary object, builds a tetrad, a torsion and a contorsion as bilinear composites of it, and claims to derive the Dirac, Einstein–Cartan and spin–torsion equations from a single constrained action, together with an intrinsic arrow of time, a torsion quantum potential and a torsion entropy current.

Recomputation shows that the algebraic displays do not reproduce. The printed block map is off by the factor $i$, so it is not a Clifford map for the quaternion units the same section defines: the declared units give $\gamma_k[\mathbb{M}_-]^2=+I_4$ where the signature needs $-I_4$. The trace normalisation $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb H}(q_\mu q_\nu)=2\delta_{\mu\nu}$ contradicts that embedding, for which the computed value is $2g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbb{M}_-]$; one substitution, $q_k\mapsto iq_k$, repairs both displays, and it is the one the paper never makes. The completeness relation's listed elements span four independent quaternion directions rather than the sixteen of the Clifford basis it later uses. And the Fierz identity of Eq. (3) has a coefficient that depends on a notation it does not fix: $1$ in the reading of $\mathrm{Re}$ the display prints, $\tfrac14$ in the two-dimensional trace its own derivation cites, $\tfrac1{16}$ in the four-dimensional $\mathrm{ReTr}$ of Eqs. (4)–(6), which is the convention the composites use; the printed $\tfrac14$ is right for one of the three and for one only. Either way three of its four terms vanish identically, so it asserts only that the quaternion norm is multiplicative.

The composite geometry fails at its first display. The composite tetrad vanishes identically on every plane-wave spinor, verified to $4\times10^{-16}$, so the construction assigns no metric to the free Dirac field. The torsion of Eq. (5) is nonzero on three of its twenty-four independent components — the three cyclic spatial triples, all equal to $-\kappa\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\Psi)$ — while the totally antisymmetric spin density it is matched to carries four and is nonzero where Eq. (5) vanishes; and the two displays that define that torsion put the output index at opposite ends, which for a tensor antisymmetric in one pair only is a difference of substance, with the paper's own worked example mis-signing the component it computes. The "vanishing symmetric part" check fails on the ten index triples where the product collapses to a scalar, and the vector current the display would need is zero in its three spatial components, $\mathrm{ReTr}(\Psi^{\dagger}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}q_k\Psi)=0$ identically. The contorsion–torsion relation carries its last index in the wrong order, which on totally antisymmetric torsion reverses the sign of the contorsion — $-\tfrac12$ where the standard relation gives $+\tfrac12$ — and the paper never states the sign convention that would make it right.

The action. The couplings in Eqs. (4) and (6) do not close dimensionally, $M^6$ and $M^2$ against a dimensionless tetrad and a contorsion of dimension $M$; that one false power propagates into the teleparallel constraint, whose two right-hand sides become $M^3$ and $M^4$ where the curvature they reconstruct has $M^2$. The action's multipliers restate definitions, leaving a coefficient $2\kappa$ in the Einstein equation that becomes $\kappa$ only if an unexplained multiplier equals $\tfrac12T$. The step offered for the Dirac equation declares a sum of Clifford basis elements zero "by orthogonality", which kills such a sum only when every coefficient already vanishes.

The sections beyond the geometry. The teleparallel section is standard geometry carrying a composite that does not exist, and its two consecutive displays of the Weitzenböck contorsion disagree in the sign of one term. The Kozyrev time gradient is the constant covector $(-1,0,0,0)$ in every spinor when computed with the paper's own $q_5=ijk=-1$, so the intrinsic time is not recovered. The torsion Bohm potential appears with both signs within one section, its "quaternionic correction" is built on components that Eq. (5) sets to zero, and the conflation of a constant commutator with a torsion field is the same one as in Eq. (5); and in the three-term potential the last two terms carry $\hbar$ where their own dimension requires $\hbar^2$, so two of the three are frequencies beside an energy. The entropy current is not conserved: $\Box T=0$ does not imply $\partial J_{S\mu}/\partial x_\mu=0$, with the counterexample $T_{123}=x_0$. The Appendix B trace identity is missing a factor $i$ — the exact value is $-4i\epsilon_{\rho\lambda\mu\nu}$ — the bilinear is not the epsilon channel, so the axial channel is reached only after antisymmetrisation, and the factor-2 relation puts $2\kappa$ where Einstein–Cartan puts $\kappa^{-1}$, a discrepancy of $2\kappa^2$ that the paper's own second display then inverts back. The empirical tables inherit all of this, the electro-torsional Aharonov–Bohm phase puts a dimensionless coefficient in front of a torsion flux that no display defines, and the topological table asserts a global condition that no pointwise algebra can establish.

Two equations carry the word Dirac in this article, and they are kept apart. The source's is two-sided, with the time derivative acting from the right and the mass from the left, and its kinetic term flips chirality. The corpus's is the one-sided chiral pair, in which the gradient preserves the chiral half and the mass is the flip.

What the source does supply is a reminder, in a form the corpus's own articles state more carefully: the algebra can hold the frame and the connection, and the step from holding to generating is where the programme is decided. This article records the step taken, the displays that survive it, and the displays that do not.

Square Roots of a General Element in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$

Introduction

The root problem of a general central value asks which elements square to a given value in each of the twelve products: for a value $\lambda e_0$ with a general complex $\lambda$, the solutions of $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$. The three named values $-1$, $0$ and $+1$ are read in the preceding companions, Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$ and Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$; here the value stays general, and the question is the one a general value asks: for which $\lambda$ does a root exist, and what is the root set. This article is the fourth physics companion of the mathematics comparison group, and its mathematics companion is Square Roots of a General Element in the 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space (articles_maths/square-roots-of-a-general-element-in-the-12-algebraic-structures-over-the-biquaternion-c-space.md).

The physics is already present in the corpus under a different scope. Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra: the Realisation reads the general equation for the general plain bilinear product and draws the three physical objects from the three named values. The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition owns the interval, and The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone the criterion of invertibility. The present article adds the structural reading that the mathematics companion supplies: the four general products have four different root sets at every value, and the twelve structures fall into four existence classes according to which values they can reach.

The mathematics companion proves two facts that organise the physics. First, the four general products answer the root problem in four genuinely different ways: the plain square reduces to a vector-scalar pair, the natural square to the single equation of the central square, the plain sesquilinear square to a non-negative scalar, and the quaternionic sesquilinear square to the indefinite Krein value. The four products have one common degeneracy — the same cone is the zero-divisor set of all four — but four different root sets at every value, because the square of a product is not the norm of a product. Second, the twelve structures split into four existence classes: every complex value is read by the four bilateral structures, every real value by the two indefinite sesquilinear ones, every non-negative real value by the two definite sesquilinear ones, and the value $0$ alone by the four antisymmetric ones. Each class has a direct physical content. The bilateral class is the composition band, whose central values carry an arbitrary phase; the indefinite class is the band whose value is an interval, real by nature; the definite class is the probability band, whose value is a non-negative scalar; and the antisymmetric class is the bracket, whose square is an infinitesimal object and reaches the value $0$ alone.

Conventions. We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_k^{2}=-e_0$; $i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$, $\bar{\cdot}$ and ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The central square is $C(\tilde\Xi)e_0=\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{\natural}$ with $C(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\Xi_\mu^{2}$; the Hermitian square is $H(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$; the Krein value is $\Delta(\tilde\Xi)=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$. The material coordinate is $\tilde\Xi=i c t\,e_0+\vec x$. The twelve structures are cited by their three-letter names.

The Plain Product: Every Value Is Realised

The plain product answers the root problem for every complex value. The two central roots $\pm\sqrt\lambda\,e_0$ are the scalar processes, and the pure quadric $-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=\lambda$ is the vector family, of four real parameters, the element $\sqrt{-\lambda}\,e_1$ being one of its members.

The physical interpretation is that the composition band realises any central value. The value $\lambda$ is an arbitrary complex number, carrying both an amplitude and a phase, and the composition produces it either by a scalar process, a pure phase and scale, or by a vector process, a direction whose square is the value. The two options are the two families of the plain reduction, the scalar line and the vector subspace, and the corpus reads their physical content in The Anti-Hermitian Subspace M₋ as the Material Sector, The Hermitian Subspace M₊ as the Informational Sector and The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition. At $\lambda=0$ the two central roots collapse to the origin and the vector family becomes the pure null cone: the only operation that squares to nothing is a lightlike displacement, and the value $0$ is the one value that has no scalar process beside it. The physical object is the photon, read in The Null Four-Momentum: What the Cone Derives and What It Does Not.

The Natural Product: the Root Is the Shell of the Interval

The natural square of an element is its central square, $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi=C(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, so the root equation is the single complex equation $C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$. The mathematics companion proves that the root set is the cone $C=0$ at $\lambda=0$ and the nondegenerate quadric $C=\lambda$ at $\lambda\neq0$, each of real dimension six, and that the set is non-empty for every $\lambda$.

The physical interpretation is the sharpest of the four. Squaring in the natural product is taking the interval of the element, and the root set of the value $\lambda$ is the set of elements whose interval is $\lambda$. The root problem is therefore the shell problem: the level sets of the interval are the mass shells, and a root of the value is an element living on the shell of that value. This is the reading the corpus gives in Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra: the Realisation and in The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition: the mass shell is the slice on which the square of a four-momentum is a fixed scalar, and the natural product is the product whose square makes the statement an identity rather than an equation. Because the natural product is bilateral, every complex value has a shell, and the shells of the real values are the timelike and spacelike shells of the material sector; the shell of $0$ is the light cone itself, the largest square-zero set of the four products and the same set that the companion article Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$ reads as the degeneracy of the four general products.

The Two Sesquilinear Products: Probability and Interval

The plain sesquilinear product: a probability is non-negative. The third square is Hermitian with scalar part $H=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$, so a root of $\lambda$ exists exactly for a real $\lambda\geq0$. At $\lambda=0$ the root set is the origin alone, and at $\lambda>0$ it is the family $e^{i\theta}w$ over the real quaternions of modulus $\sqrt\lambda$, of real dimension four; at $\lambda=1$ it is the unitary group $U(2)$. The physical interpretation is the positivity of probability. The scalar part of the third square is the modulus squared of the amplitude, so the band that measures a probability can only produce a non-negative value; a negative value has no root there, and the value $0$ is reached by the origin alone. The physical statement is that the pairing has no nonzero square-zero element: a nonzero amplitude never squares to nothing, so masslessness does not live in the probability band. The roots of a positive value are the amplitudes with the modulus $\sqrt\lambda$, and at $\lambda=1$ the family is the unitary group, that is the reversible transformations of the state space, read in Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action.

The quaternionic sesquilinear product: the interval is real. The fourth square has the indefinite scalar part $\Delta=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$, real for every element, so a root of $\lambda$ exists exactly for a real $\lambda$ and every real value has roots. The root set of a real $\lambda$ is the family $\tilde\Xi=c(\tilde\xi+it)$ over the real unit vectors $\tilde\xi$, of real dimension four, with $(t^{2}-1)\lvert c\rvert^{2}=\lambda$, together with the central circle $\lvert a\rvert^{2}=\lambda$ when $\lambda$ is positive. The physical interpretation is the reality of the interval. The gauge sesquilinear band produces a real value because the quantity it squares to is an interval, and the three ranges of the slope $t$ are its three causal classes: the slopes inside the unit, at the unit and outside the unit. The owner of the parametrisation and of the indefinite metric is The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric, and the null family at the unit slope is read in The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise.

What Agrees and What Does Not

The mathematics companion states the distinction that the physics needs: the four general products have one zero-divisor set and four root sets. The zero-divisor set of each of the four is the punctured cone, the same set read four times, because the four products carry the same norm; the root sets are different, because the square of a product is not the norm of a product.

The physical interpretation is the separation of two questions that are often confused. The question "which elements are non-invertible" has one answer across the four bands: the lightlike elements, the cone. The question "which elements square to a given value, and how many of them are there" has four answers, one per band. Degeneracy is universal; factorisation is band-specific. The element $\sqrt{-\lambda}\,e_1$ squares to $\lambda e_0$ in the plain product and answers for every complex value; the level set $C=\lambda$ answers in the natural product; the origin alone or the family $e^{i\theta}w$ answers in the third; and the family of the slope answers in the fourth. At the value $0$ the four root sets are the nilpotent cone of real dimension four, the cone $C=0$ of real dimension six, the origin alone and the square-zero family, four different sets, while the zero-divisor set is one cone in all four. The owner of the shared cone is Zero Divisors in the 12 Structures of Biquaternions over $\mathbb{C}$, and the owner of the mass shell is Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra: the Realisation.

The Twelve Structures at a General Value

The square of a structure is its operation read at the two equal arguments, and the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$ is written in that operation, so each of the twelve carries a root problem of its own. The twelve are read one by one below, each naming its product, its square and its root set, before the table collects the twelve against the sign of the value.

$\mathrm{GPA}$, the general plain algebra — composition

The product is the plain product and its square is the plain square, of scalar part $\Xi_0^{2}-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)$ and vector part $2\Xi_0\vec\Xi$. A root of $\lambda$ exists for every complex value: the two central roots $\pm\sqrt\lambda\,e_0$ together with the pure quadric $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=-\lambda$, a four-real-parameter family; at $\lambda=0$ the two central roots meet at the origin and the quadric is the pure null cone. The composition band realises every value, and the value is read as a scale together with a phase. The reading is Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra: the Realisation.

$\mathrm{SPA}$, the symmetric plain algebra — commutativity

The product is the Jordan product, which is commutative, and its square is the plain square, so the root set of every value is the set of $\mathrm{GPA}$ element for element. The symmetrisation prepares the same objects as the composition it symmetrises. The reading is Observables as a Jordan Algebra: the Symmetric Plain Product and the Classical Composition.

$\mathrm{APA}$, the antisymmetric plain algebra — rotation

The product is the antisymmetrisation, which is alternating, so its square vanishes identically and the equation reads $0=\lambda e_0$. The root of $0$ is every element of the space, and no other value has a root. A bracket has only the trivial value. The reading is The Cross Product as a Lie Bracket: Rotations and the Jacobi Identity.

$\mathrm{GQA}$, the general quaternionic algebra — causality

The product is $\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde Q$ and its square is the central element $C(\tilde\Xi)e_0$, so the equation is the single complex equation $C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$. A root exists for every $\lambda$, and the root set is the level set $C=\lambda$: the cone of real dimension six at $\lambda=0$ and the nondegenerate quadric of the same real dimension at a nonzero value. Physically the level set is the mass shell, and the sign of a real value is the causal class. The reading is The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition.

$\mathrm{SQA}$, the symmetric quaternionic algebra — coupling

The product is the symmetrisation and its square is the central square, so the root set of every value is the same level set $C=\lambda$, non-empty for every value. The symmetrisation keeps the existence class of its general partner, and the value is a weight of the scalar coupling. The reading is The Quaternion Form as a Product: the Scalar Coupling of Two Material Operations.

$\mathrm{AQA}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra — obstruction

The product is the antisymmetrisation and its square vanishes identically, so the equation reads $0=\lambda e_0$. The root of $0$ is every element of the space, and no other value has a root: every element is square-zero, a zero divisor of the block and its own annihilating partner. The reading is Boosts, Mixed Terms and the Missing Lie Structure.

$\mathrm{GPS}$, the general plain sesqualgebra — probability

The product is the pairing and its square is Hermitian with scalar part the non-negative $H$, so a root exists exactly for a real $\lambda\geq0$. The root set is the origin alone at $\lambda=0$ and the family $e^{i\theta}w$ of real dimension four at $\lambda>0$, the group $U(2)$ at $\lambda=1$. The band refuses a negative or non-real value because its form is positive definite. The reading is Mass, Rank and the Positivity of the Dagger.

$\mathrm{SPS}$, the symmetric plain sesqualgebra — positivity

The product is the scalar-valued symmetrisation and its square keeps only the scalar part of the Hermitian square, so the equation is the single real equation $H(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$. A root exists exactly for a real $\lambda\geq0$: the origin alone at $\lambda=0$, and the quadric $H=\lambda$ of real dimension seven, the sphere of $\mathbb{C}^{4}$, at $\lambda>0$. The symmetrisation replaces unitarity by normalisation. The reading is Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone.

$\mathrm{APS}$, the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra — phase

The product is the antisymmetrisation, whose square is the pure part of the Hermitian square, of vanishing scalar part, so no nonzero central value is reached. The only central value with a root is $0$, and its root set is the square-zero cone of real dimension five, containing the origin and the nonzero nilpotents of the block. The reading is The Imaginary Part of the Born Pairing: the Antisymmetric Sesquilinear Product.

$\mathrm{GQS}$, the general quaternionic sesqualgebra — gauge

The product is the fourth product, and the scalar part of its square is the Krein value, real for every element, so a root exists exactly for a real $\lambda$ and every real value has one. The root set is the family $c(\tilde\xi+it)$ with $(t^{2}-1)\lvert c\rvert^{2}=\lambda$, of real dimension four, together with the central circle at a positive value. The slope ranges are the three causal classes. The reading is The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric.

$\mathrm{SQS}$, the symmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — metric

The product is the symmetrisation and its square is the Hermitian part of the unsplit square, a real quaternion, so a root exists exactly for a real value and exists for every one of them: the fibre over a real value is empty for no value. The root set is the pure family, present only for $\lambda\leq0$, together with the non-pure family $\mathcal R_\lambda$ of real dimension four. The reading is The Gauge Metric as a Product: the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesquilinear Product.

$\mathrm{AQS}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra — polarisation

The product is the antisymmetrisation, whose square takes its values in the real three-dimensional subspace $i\mathbb{R}^{3}$, so its intersection with the central line is the origin and no nonzero central value is reached. The only central value with a root is $0$, and its root set is the cone of the diagonal, of real dimension six. The reading is The Cross Product of a Vector with Its Conjugate: the Antisymmetric Gauge Product.

The Value in the Physics Corpus: Mass, Weight and Interval

The general value $\lambda$ is the number the root problem sets, and the four bands read it as four different physical quantities. The comparison is the subject of Composition and Probability: the General Plain Algebra and the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions Compared, and the word the corpus uses across them is mass, though only the norm reading is that mass; the four readings are separated in The Four Readings of a Biquaternion: Norm, Rank, Charge and Scale.

The value is a level of the interval. For the natural product the root equation is $C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$, and $C$ is the interval of the element. The root set of $\lambda$ is the set of elements whose interval is $\lambda$, and on the material coordinate the level set $N=-m^2c^2$ is the mass shell. Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra: the Realisation and The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition read the value as the interval, the root problem as the shell problem, and the sign of $\lambda$ as the causal class of the element; The Symplectic Form and the Biquaternion Norm Cone reads the same level sets as the energy surfaces of the material dynamics. The value $0$ is the cone, and the three signs of the real values are the spacelike, null and timelike regimes.

The value is a weight of a probability. In the probability band the root equation is $H(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$ with $H=\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}$ real and non-negative, so the band reads exactly the non-negative values and a root of $\lambda>0$ is an amplitude of weight $\lambda$. Why Probability Values Are Central: the Symmetric Sesquilinear Product and Its Cone and The Born Rule as a Trace Formula — Derivation and Comparison read $H$ as the Born weight, and the band refuses $-1$ for the reason a probability cannot be negative: its form is positive definite. The origin is the only root of $0$, so no nonzero amplitude squares to nothing. The class is thus fixed by two facts: the band that measures probability has no clock of its own and no lightlike displacement.

The value is an interval of the gauge band. In the gauge band the root equation is $\Delta(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$ with $\Delta=\lvert\Xi_0\rvert^{2}-\sum_k\lvert\Xi_k\rvert^{2}$, the indefinite Krein form, so the band reads exactly the real values and the sign of $\lambda$ is the causal class. The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric and The States the Indefinite Metric Cannot Normalise read the form as the gauge metric, and the reason the band reads only the reals is that an interval is real. The root family $c(\tilde\xi+it)$ of the mathematics companion makes the reading explicit: the slope $t$ parametrises the family and the three ranges $\lvert t\rvert<1$, $\lvert t\rvert=1$, $\lvert t\rvert>1$ are the timelike, null and spacelike classes. The same number that is a weight in one band is a squared interval in the other.

The value is a scale. The four bilateral structures read every complex value, because a central value there carries an amplitude and a phase; the passage from the root of $1$ to the root of $\lambda$ is a scaling, and The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions and Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form read that scaling as the polar modulus and as a conformal dilation. A mass shell is a unit shell scaled by $m$, and the scale reading is the fourth of the four readings of mass. The general value therefore has a phase in the composition band, a weight in the probability band, an interval in the gauge band and a magnitude everywhere: one number, and the band chooses which of its aspects is read.

The Sign of the Value: the Three Causal Shells

The value $\lambda$ has a magnitude and a sign, and for the natural product the sign is a causal class. The root equation $C(\tilde\Xi)=\lambda$ is the shell equation, and the three signs of a real $\lambda$ are the three shells of the material sector.

The three shells. The interval $C=N$ is negative on the two timelike components, zero on the cone and positive on the spacelike region, as The Interval as the Square and the Charge of the Material Composition computes for the material sector. The root set of a negative value is therefore the massive shell: the timelike elements of squared interval $-m^2c^2$, the trajectories of a particle with a rest frame. The root set of zero is the cone, the massless shell. The root set of a positive value is the spacelike shell, the locus a superluminal or tachyonic displacement would occupy. Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra: the Realisation reads the three as the three causal classes, and the sign of the radicand is the name of the class.

Invertibility follows from nonzero-ness, not from the sign. A nonzero value has root elements that are units and the value zero has zero-divisor roots. Both signs of a nonzero $\lambda$ give units, so the sign does not decide invertibility; only whether $\lambda$ vanishes does. A root on the massive shell and a root on the spacelike shell are therefore both invertible, for the reason of Biquaternion Norm and Invertibility: the interval is nonzero on both. The tachyon is in the invertible region, and the only value whose roots are non-invertible is the value zero.

Why the retired equation failed on the sign. Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form recomputes the failure of the single-field equation $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi=m\tilde\Psi^{\flat}$ and finds its central-phase plane waves have nullity $0$ on the timelike shell and nullity $4$ on the spacelike shell: its solutions lie on the wrong shell. The mass term of a physical equation must place its solutions on the timelike shell, and the article's point is that the conjugation, not a number, is what chooses between the two kinds of mass. The value $\lambda$ of this article's root problem is the scalar face of that choice: the sign of $\lambda$ says which shell a solution occupies, and the conjugation says which equation puts it there.

The polar phase names the causal class, on the material sector. The value a root realises is not a bare sign; it is a modulus and a phase. The Polar Element Representation of Biquaternions writes every non-null element as $\tilde Q=r\,e^{i\alpha}B\tilde u$ and identifies the modulus $\rho=re^{i\alpha}$ with the square root of the biquaternion norm, $\rho^{2}=N(\tilde Q)$; the representation is available exactly on the nonzero elements off the cone, which is the invertibility theorem of The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra and the Complement of the Zero-Divisor Cone in polar dress. On the material sector the article computes the phase explicitly: a timelike displacement has $N=-c^{2}t^{2}(1-\beta^{2})<0$, so $\rho=i\,ct\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}$, that is $r=c\tau$ with $\alpha=\pi/2$, while a spacelike displacement has $N>0$ and a real modulus, $\alpha=0$. On the material sector the phase of the polar modulus is the causal class — imaginary for timelike, real for spacelike; for a general element $\alpha$ is the continuous angle that carries the complex-conjugation imbalance, so the two-valued statement is a statement about four-vectors and not about all elements. Where this article's natural band is used the connection is exact, because there an element's square is its interval, $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=N(\tilde\Xi)e_0$: the value a root realises is then the square of its own polar modulus, $\lambda=\rho(\tilde\Xi)^{2}$, whose sign is the sign of the interval and whose phase is twice the phase of $\rho$.

The complex value: a decaying state has no shell. The three shells are the three signs of a real $\lambda$, and the framework has a value with no sign at all. The Unstable State and the Complex Energy Pole in Biquaternionic Form proves that an unstable state is described by a complex value of the centre, $z_\star=E-\tfrac{i}{2}\Gamma$: the real part is the energy, that is the position of the shell, and the imaginary part is half the width, that is the decay rate. The pole is central — a complex number of $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ — whatever non-central coupling is present, so the value is not a property of the frame. Read through this article's root problem, a complex value has no causal class: the shell is an object a real value defines, and the decaying state has no sharp mass shell. Two details keep the reading honest. First, only the plain band realises such a value: it contains the scalar process $\sqrt\lambda\,e_0$ for any central $\lambda$, whereas the natural band's square is the interval and the sesquilinear bands produce an interval, so those bands are real by construction and a complex pole is never one of their shells. Second, what the value supplies is two numbers of one complex number, a real part that names the shell the state is near and an imaginary part that names how long it stays there. This is the sharpest statement of why the sign, and not the magnitude, is the causal datum: a value off the real axis has no sign, hence no class, and the state it describes has a lifetime rather than a mass.

One shell generates the others. The positive shells at different magnitudes are related by scaling: the biquaternion norm is quadratic, so scaling an element by $\lambda$ scales its interval by $\lambda^{2}$, and a mass shell of interval $-m^{2}c^{2}$ is the unit shell scaled by $mc$. Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form reads that scaling as a conformal dilation, with the massless cone as the scale-free limit. The general value is thus one number whose magnitude sets the size of the shell and whose sign sets its causal class, and the band that reads it — interval, weight, gauge interval or polar scale — selects which of the two aspects is the physical one.

The shell is a pole of the propagator. The value a root realises is not only a level of the dispersion; it is a singularity of the field's two-point function. The Feynman Propagator in Biquaternionic Form computes the propagator whose momentum-space amplitude has its poles exactly on the mass shell, $\tilde k\tilde k^{\natural}+m^{2}=0$: the shell of the value $-m^{2}$ is the pole of the propagation, and the two frequency branches $E=\pm E_{\vec p}$ are the particle and antiparticle poles. The Feynman prescription is a deformation of that shell, $m^{2}\mapsto m^{2}-i\epsilon$, and the article's own finding is precise about the split of labour: the algebra supplies the complex plane in which the contour is drawn and a natural home for the $\epsilon$ — the imaginary part lies along the $ict$ direction of the material sector — but it does not select the contour, whose orientation is a boundary condition that distinguishes the Feynman propagator from the retarded and advanced ones. Read through the root problem, the value is the propagation pole: the shell of a mass is where the field propagates on shell, and the three propagators of the corpus are three choices of how the complex deformation of one shell is closed, and the value's imaginary part is the width the article reads as half the decay rate.

The value's phase doubles in the interference. The sign of a value is the causal class of its roots, and Zitterbewegung in Biquaternionic Form exhibits the same doubling of the value's phase on the field itself. The article finds that the rest-energy phase rotates the two sectors into one another at the rate $mc^{2}/\hbar$, and that the trembling of the Dirac position is the square of that rotation: its frequency is $2E/\hbar$, twice the rest scale, and its amplitude is half the reduced Compton wavelength. This is the field's image of the algebraic fact that the phase of $\tilde\Xi^{2}$ is twice the phase of $\tilde\Xi$. The article also isolates the piece of the root problem that the field makes explicit: the mass term acts as a diagonal action with opposite signs on the two sectors, so the two mass-shell roots of one value are the two signs the value imposes, and the trembling is their beat. The article is careful where the corpus has to be: it records that the energy-sign split and the sector split are generated by different operations and does not identify them, while the mass fixes the scale of both.

The value can be a field. The Petiau System and the Quantisation of Mass in Biquaternionic Form generalises the root problem in a way that article states plainly. The Lanczos feedback $\tilde\nabla\tilde A=m\tilde B$, $\tilde\nabla\tilde B=m\tilde A$ turns a massive field equation into a self-sourcing pair, and promoting the scalar $m$ to a biquaternion field $\tilde E$ makes the second-order equations eigenvalue equations for the mass, whose two eigenvalues are those of $\tilde E\tilde E^{*}$. The radicand of this companion's root problem is therefore not always a fixed central number: in this line it is a field, and the physical masses are the eigenvalues the field's own square supplies. The article keeps the ledger honest, and the reading must keep it too: the algebra is scale-free and derives no mass value, so the promotion of $\lambda$ from a number to a field changes what carries the value, not whether the framework fixes it. The root problem "which elements square to a given central value" becomes, in this line, "which values a field's own square realises".

The wave operator's square root is spinor-valued, not scalar. Klein–Gordon from the Dirac Square in Biquaternionic Form is the physical instance of the existence question this article settles abstractly. The second-order operator $\Box-m^2c^2/\hbar^2$ has no first-order scalar square root in $\mathbb{B}$: the simplest attempt $(\tilde\nabla^{\natural}+\mu)(\tilde\nabla-\mu)$ leaves the first-order residual $2\mu\vec\nabla$, and no choice of constant biquaternion coefficients cancels it, because cancellation would need an element anticommuting with all three $e_j$, and none exists. Yet the physical theory does have a first-order equation whose square is Klein–Gordon, and the article locates the resolution: the square root is not scalar, it is the gradient acting on the spinor module, and the mass term is the off-diagonal pair $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi_R=m\tilde\Psi_L$, $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Psi_L=m\tilde\Psi_R$ that mixes the two chiral halves. Read against this companion, the point is sharp: the root of a given element need not be an element of the same algebra; it may be a module-valued operator, a map between the two summands the element acts on, and the obstruction to a scalar root — the residual that cannot be cancelled — is exactly the evidence that the root must be off-diagonal. The mass is then the root's off-diagonal entry, and $\Box\tilde\Psi=m^2\tilde\Psi$ is recovered by squaring the pair rather than by solving $\tilde\Xi^2=\lambda e_0$ inside the algebra. This is the concrete face of the existence classes: the classes that fail to admit a scalar root are not idle, they are the ones whose root lives on a module.

The massless limit is the branch point. Conformal Invariance and the Massless Limit in Biquaternionic Form locates the boundary case $\lambda=0$ of the root problem, and its statement is algebraic. The mass enters the wave equation as the single scale in $\Box-\mu^2$, and the article finds that, algebraically, at $\mu=0$ "the on-shell symbol becomes a zero divisor of the algebra, left multiplication by the on-shell momentum dropping from rank four to rank two". Read here, that is the branch point of the square root. For $\lambda\neq0$ the root problem $\tilde\Xi^2=\lambda e_0$ has two roots $\pm\sqrt\lambda$ and the element is invertible, splitting the two mass shells; at $\lambda=0$ the two roots merge at $0$ — a nilpotent — and the on-shell element is a zero divisor. The three named radicands are thus not three unrelated cases but one picture read along a line: $-1$ the phase, $+1$ the involution, and $0$ the branch point at which a nonzero root collapses to a nilpotent and an invertible element collapses to a zero divisor. The article's second statement completes the picture: the trace of the improved stress–energy tensor is $\propto\mu^2$ and vanishes exactly at $\lambda=0$, so the loss of the root's splitting and the restoration of the conformal symmetry are one event.

Physical Interpretation

  • The root problem of a general value asks which elements square to a given central value. Each of the twelve structures answers it in its own way, and the answers fall into four existence classes.
  • The plain product realises every complex value, by a scalar process $\pm\sqrt\lambda\,e_0$ or by a pure process on the quadric. The composition band can produce any value, phase and amplitude together.
  • The natural product squares an element to its interval, so its root set of a value is the shell of that value: the root problem is the mass-shell problem, and the shell of $0$ is the light cone.
  • The probability band has roots only for non-negative values, and the origin alone for the value $0$. A nonzero amplitude never squares to nothing, so masslessness does not live in the band that measures probability.
  • The gauge sesquilinear band has roots exactly for real values, because the quantity it produces is an interval. The slope parametrises the four-vector of the given interval, and its three ranges are the three causal classes.
  • The four brackets have a root of $0$ alone, because a bracket is an infinitesimal transformation and its square is a pure or vanishing object.
  • The four general products share one zero-divisor set, the cone, and have four root sets. Degeneracy is universal across the bands; factorisation is band-specific.
  • The general value carries four physical interpretations, and the band selects among them: the interval and the mass shell of the natural product, the Born weight of the probability band, the indefinite interval of the gauge band and the polar scale of the bilateral structures.
  • The sign of a real value is the causal class of its roots: negative for the massive shell, zero for the cone, positive for the spacelike or tachyonic shell. The magnitude sets the size of the shell and the sign sets its causal class.

Summary

The root problem of a general central value is the equation $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$ read in the twelve structures, and the mathematics companion answers it by reducing the four general squares to a vector-scalar pair, a single complex equation, a non-negative scalar and an indefinite scalar. Read physically, the four answers are the four bands. The plain product realises every complex value: a root is a scalar process or a pure process, and the composition band carries the phase. The natural product squares an element to its interval, so its root set is a mass shell, and the shell of $0$ is the light cone. The plain sesquilinear product reads the non-negative values alone, because a probability is non-negative, and reaches $0$ at the origin alone, so masslessness is absent from the band that measures probability. The quaternionic sesquilinear product reads exactly the real values, because an interval is real, and its root family is parametrised by a slope whose three ranges are the three causal classes. The four brackets read the value $0$ alone, because an infinitesimal transformation has no central square. The twelve structures thus fall into four existence classes, the bilateral, the indefinite sesquilinear, the definite sesquilinear and the antisymmetric, and the sharpest structural statement is the one the mathematics companion proves: the four general products have one zero-divisor set, the cone, and four different root sets, so degeneracy is universal while factorisation is band-specific.

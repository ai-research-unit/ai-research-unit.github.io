The Anomalous Magnetic Moment in Biquaternionic Form

Introduction

The magnetic moment of the electron is the most precisely tested prediction of quantum field theory, and its content is a single number: the anomaly $a=(g-2)/2$, the fractional departure of the gyromagnetic ratio from the value that the Dirac equation alone requires. That departure is a radiative correction. It is produced by the virtual photon that the electron emits and reabsorbs, and its leading value, $\alpha/2\pi$, is finite, universal and independent of the electron's mass.

This article is the worked radiative application of the spin-$\tfrac12$ subcategory. It establishes four things and is explicit about a fifth.

  • The vertex and its two structures. The one-particle vertex has two independent Lorentz structures, and the magnetic one carries $F_2$. The tree-level value $g=2$, the Dirac value, is taken as settled by the companion The Classical Origin of $g=2$ in Biquaternionic Form; what is at issue here is $F_2(0)=a$.
  • The Gordon decomposition, transcribed and verified. The biquaternion content of the problem is the split of the vertex into a symmetric part (the metric, central direction) and an antisymmetric part (the bivector, the directions $\vec e_\mu$ and $i\vec e_\mu$). The Gordon identity, which is the tree-level form of that split, was verified numerically in the corpus conventions, with a residual of order $10^{-15}$.
  • The sector interpretation. The magnetic structure is the rotation-type bivector, whose biquaternion image is $i\vec e_\mu$: the informational sector $\mathbb{M}_+$, the same sector as the spin. The convection structure is the metric, central part; the electric (boost-type) structure is $\vec e_\mu$, the material sector $\mathbb{M}_-$.
  • The one-loop value, transcribed and its integral verified. The standard Feynman-parameter representation of the magnetic form factor reduces at $k^2=0$ to an integral whose value is exactly $1$; that reduction was verified, so that $a=\alpha/2\pi$ follows from the standard representation.
  • What the algebra does not supply. It does not produce $\alpha$, and it does not by itself remove the divergence. The finiteness of the anomaly rests on the Ward identity, that is, on gauge invariance; the framework writes the vertex in its own tensor language and does not replace that argument.

The cells. The computation is written in cells, and the gamma matrices appear in one place only, §The Gamma Formulation, as a matrix realisation of the cells and not as a second algebra. With $L_aR_b$ the two-sided multiplication, $L_aR_b\psi=a\psi b$, the four generator cells are the timelike $iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ and the three spacelike $iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, and the identity cell is $L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}=I_4$. Cells multiply by multiplying the left multipliers together and the right multipliers together, $L_aR_b\,L_cR_d=L_{ac}R_{db}$, the right pair in reverse order.

The factor $i$ and the two signs. Every generator cell carries one factor $i$, so a product of two of them carries two, and $i^2=-1$ is the whole origin of the Clifford signs. The squares are

$$ (iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2=-R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}=+I_4 , \qquad (iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2=-L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=-I_4 , $$

since $(R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2=(L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2=(R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}})^2=-I_4$ and $i^2=-1$, the signs multiply: two of them in the first case, so they cancel, and three in the second, so they do not. The same rule collapses a mixed product to a single cell,

$$ iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=-L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=+L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}, $$

where $R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=R_{\vec e_y\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}=R_{-\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ because the right pair multiplies in reverse order; the two minus signs, one from $i^2$ and one from $\vec e_y\vec e_x=-\vec e_z$, cancel.

The frame cells. Written as one symbol, the frame cell of the direction $\mu$ is

$$ iL_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad \nu(t)=x,\quad \nu(k)=y , $$

which is $iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ at $\mu=t$ and $iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ at $\mu=k$. The four cells carry the Clifford relations of $g=\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$,

$$ \{iL_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{pau}},iL_{e_\nu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\nu)}}^{\mathrm{pau}}\}=2g_{\mu\nu}I_4 , $$

so the four directions and the four cells carry one and the same metric. A paired Greek index is written low with the plain weight, $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$: $\sigma_{\mu\nu}k_\nu$ is the sum $\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}k_\nu$, and no index is ever raised.

The dictionary. The companion Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Clifford Structure and the Frame, fixes the correspondence $e_t\mapsto L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, $\vec e_x\mapsto L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, $\vec e_y\mapsto L_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, $\vec e_z\mapsto L_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ and $i\mapsto L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, the cell the conventions article writes $-i\omega$, its $\omega$ being the algebra's pseudoscalar unit. The compact form used below is

$$ \vec e_\mu\mapsto L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad i\vec e_\mu\mapsto L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}. $$

The correspondence is $\mathbb{R}$-linear and not $\mathbb{C}$-linear: the scalar imaginary reads as the cell $[i]=L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, so the image of $i\vec e_\mu$ is $[i]$ times the image of $\vec e_\mu$ and not $i$ times it. When a cell is a scalar multiple of an image, the scalar is read back with the same value. That is the only rule used below, and every even cell of the vertex is such a multiple.

The propagator. The conventions of the internal fermion line are those of the companion The Feynman Propagator in Biquaternionic Form,

$$ S_F(p)=\frac{i\big(iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_0-\sum_k iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_k+m\,I_4\big)}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon} =-\frac{i\big(iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_0-\sum_k iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_k+m\,I_4\big)}{\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}+m^2-i\epsilon} , $$

with $p^2=p_0^2-\sum_kp_k^2$ and $\tilde P=i p_0e_t+\vec p$ the wave biquaternion of that companion, of norm $\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=-p^2$, so that the second denominator is minus the first. Here again $i^2=-1$ shortens the numerator: distributing the outer $i$ gives

$$ i\big(iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_0-\sum_k iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_k+m\,I_4\big) =-R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_0+\sum_k L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_k+i m\,I_4 . $$

The traces. Two traces are used, and they are not the same one. On the algebra the trace of a product is twice the scalar part of the product, $\mathrm{Tr}_2(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=2\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$, which is the plain form of the conventions. On the cells the trace is $4\times4$ and factorizes, $\mathrm{tr}(L_aR_b)=4\mathrm{Sc}(a)\mathrm{Sc}(b)$, the left multiplier and the right multiplier contributing separately. Since a product of cells is again a cell, the Clifford trace factorizes at every length,

$$ \mathrm{tr}\big(c_{\mu_1}\cdots c_{\mu_n}\big) =4\mathrm{Sc}\big(a_{\mu_1}\cdots a_{\mu_n}\big)\mathrm{Sc}\big(b_{\mu_n}\cdots b_{\mu_1}\big), \qquad c_\mu=L_{a_\mu}R_{b_\mu}, $$

so that $\mathrm{tr}(c_\mu c_\nu)=4g_{\mu\nu}$ and a single frame cell is traceless, one of its two multipliers being a vector unit with vanishing scalar part. This is the reduction applied under every loop numerator. Natural units $\hbar=c=1$; the fine-structure constant is $\alpha=e^2/(4\pi)$.

The Vertex Function and the Form Factors

The vertex. The electromagnetic vertex of the quantized Dirac field is the one-particle-irreducible three-point function, written in the standard way as $-ie\Gamma_\mu(p_2,p)$, where $p$ and $p_2$ are the incoming and outgoing fermion momenta and $k=p_2-p$ is the momentum transferred by the photon. Lorentz invariance, the on-shell conditions

$$ \big(iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_0-\sum_k iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_k\big)w=mw , \qquad \bar w_2\big(iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}(p_2)_0-\sum_k iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}(p_2)_k\big)=m\bar w_2 , $$

with $\bar w_2=w_2^{*}iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ the reference-frame Dirac adjoint, the Clifford-odd cell $iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ being what makes $\bar w_2w$ a scalar. Together with the discrete symmetries they reduce $\Gamma_\mu$ to two independent structures multiplied by functions of the single invariant $k^2$. The timelike and the spacelike components are written separately, because they are different cells:

$$ \Gamma_0(p_2,p)=iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,F_1(k^2)+\frac{i\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\,\sigma_{0\nu}k_\nu}{2m}\,F_2(k^2) , $$

$$ \Gamma_k(p_2,p)=iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,F_1(k^2)+\frac{i\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\,\sigma_{k\nu}k_\nu}{2m}\,F_2(k^2) , $$

with $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ the bivector defined in cells below.

$F_1(0)=1$ is the charge, and $F_2(0)$ is the anomaly. The gyromagnetic ratio is

$$ \boxed{\;g=2\big(F_1(0)+F_2(0)\big)=2\big(1+F_2(0)\big), \qquad a=\frac{g-2}{2}=F_2(0)\;}. $$

At tree level $\Gamma_0=iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ and $\Gamma_k=iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, so $F_1=1$, $F_2=0$ and $g=2$: the Dirac value, which is one-particle physics and is settled elsewhere in the corpus. What this article computes is the correction to it.

Why two structures. The two structures are distinguished by symmetry, and that is where the framework's interpretation begins. $\Gamma_\mu$ is a single generator at tree level: one cell, an odd element of the Clifford algebra, the timelike cell $iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ for $\mu=0$ and the spacelike cells $iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ for $\mu=k$. The bivector $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ is $i$ times half the commutator of two generators, an even element. The vertex is a sum of an odd and an even Clifford structure, and only the even one carries the spin-magnetic coupling.

The separation is a property of the product of two generators. With $c_\mu=iL_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_{\nu(\mu)}}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ the frame cell of the direction $\mu$, the bivector is $i$ times the product of two of them,

$$ \sigma_{\mu\nu}=i\,c_\mu c_\nu\quad(\mu\ne\nu), \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad c_\mu c_\nu=g_{\mu\nu}I_4-i\sigma_{\mu\nu}, $$

so that the product of two generators splits into a symmetric and an antisymmetric part, and the split is the exact algebraic place where the two structures separate:

$$ iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=g_{0k}L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}-i\sigma_{0k}, \qquad iL_{\vec e_j}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=g_{jk}L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}-i\sigma_{jk}. $$

The symmetric part is the metric, one number per pair of indices, and it is the central direction $e_t$; the antisymmetric part is the bivector. Since $g_{0k}=0$ for $k=x,y,z$, the first product is the pure bivector $-i\sigma_{0k}$, the single cell $L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ computed in the introduction. The split is complete, since $c_\mu c_\nu=\tfrac12\{c_\mu,c_\nu\}+\tfrac12[c_\mu,c_\nu]$ with $\tfrac12\{c_\mu,c_\nu\}=g_{\mu\nu}I_4$ and $\tfrac12[c_\mu,c_\nu]=-i\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ for two distinct directions, so the product has no third part. The magnetic form factor is the coefficient of the antisymmetric part.

The Gordon decomposition. The separation is not merely formal; on shell it is an identity, the Gordon decomposition, which expresses the single-generator vertex as the sum of a convection term and the magnetic term. It is written once for the time direction and once for the space directions:

$$ \bar w_2\,iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,w =\bar w_2\left[\frac{(p+p_2)_0}{2m}+\frac{i\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\,\sigma_{0\nu}k_\nu}{2m}\right]w , $$

$$ \bar w_2\,iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,w =\bar w_2\left[\frac{(p+p_2)_k}{2m}+\frac{i\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\,\sigma_{k\nu}k_\nu}{2m}\right]w . $$

The first term is the convection current of a charged scalar of the same mass, which alone would give $g=1$; the second is the magnetic term, present with unit coefficient. The two together give $g=2$, which is where the Dirac value comes from. The identity was verified numerically in the conventions above, on positive-energy spinors built as $w=(iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_0-\sum_kiL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}p_k+m\,I_4)w_0$ for a random $w_0$, at $m=0.7$, over four random on-shell momentum pairs: the largest deviation over the four values of $\mu$ was of order $10^{-15}$. The identity is exact, so the residual measures the arithmetic of the evaluation and not a gap in the identity. The anomaly is the radiative correction to the coefficient of the second term; the first term's coefficient is protected by gauge invariance, the Ward identity, and is not renormalized at $k^2=0$.

The Biquaternion Transcription

The biquaternion algebra enters the vertex through the dictionary, and it names the two structures in its own terms.

The generators and the bivectors. The dictionary identifies the even Clifford elements with the algebra:

$$ \vec e_x\mapsto L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}} , \quad \vec e_y\mapsto L_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}} , \quad \vec e_z\mapsto L_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}} , \qquad i\vec e_x\mapsto L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}} , \quad i\vec e_y\mapsto L_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}} , \quad i\vec e_z\mapsto L_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}} , \qquad i\mapsto L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}} . $$

Every entry was verified by explicit multiplication, with residual $0$, and so was the multiplicative agreement $\vec e_x\vec e_y\mapsto\vec e_z$. The six bivectors of the vertex therefore span the six directions of the algebra, and each of them is $i$ times the image of a vector direction: the spacelike (rotation-type) bivectors are $i$ times the cells $L_{\vec e_m}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, that is $i$ times the left multiplications $L_{\vec e_m}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, and the timelike (boost-type) ones are $i$ times the cells $L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, which are the images of $i\vec e_k$.

The sector interpretation. The bivector of the vertex is $i$ times a cell, and the factor $i$ is exactly the operator that exchanges the two sectors, $i\mathbb{M}_\pm=\mathbb{M}_\mp$. That single fact sorts the six bivectors into the two sectors, and the sorting is the framework's contribution to the bookkeeping. The three rotation-type bivectors are

$$ \sigma_{12}=iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}\mapsto i\vec e_z\in\mathbb{M}_+ , \qquad \sigma_{23}\mapsto i\vec e_x\in\mathbb{M}_+ , \qquad \sigma_{31}\mapsto i\vec e_y\in\mathbb{M}_+ , $$

and the three boost-type bivectors are

$$ \sigma_{01}=iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}\mapsto-\vec e_x\in\mathbb{M}_- , \qquad \sigma_{02}\mapsto-\vec e_y\in\mathbb{M}_- , \qquad \sigma_{03}\mapsto-\vec e_z\in\mathbb{M}_- . $$

$$ \boxed{\;\text{magnetic: }i\vec e_\mu\in\mathbb{M}_+\ \text{(Hermitian, informational)}, \qquad \text{electric: }\vec e_\mu\in\mathbb{M}_-\ \text{(anti-Hermitian, material)}\;} $$

The conventions article tabulates the same six objects under the name $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, where it means one quarter of the commutator of two frame cells; that object is the Lorentz generator, and it is $-iS_{\mu\nu}$ here. Its table has the rotations against $\tfrac12\vec e_m$ and the boosts against $\tfrac12 i\vec e_m$. The difference is one of normalisation: the $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ of this article is $2i$ times the $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ of the conventions article, and the $i$ in that factor is the exchange of the sectors. So the same table read here has the rotations against $i\vec e_m$, the Hermitian generators of $\mathbb{M}_+$, and the boosts against $-\vec e_m$: one table, read twice.

The magnetic coupling to an external field is a rotation-type structure, and its image $i\vec e_\mu$ is the very object that carries the spin, $\tilde S_\mu=\tfrac{\hbar}{2}i\vec e_\mu$, in the companion Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form. The anomaly, being a correction to the magnetic structure, is an $\mathbb{M}_+$ effect: the same sector as the observable it corrects. The electric (boost-type) structure is the material-sector one, and the convection (metric) part is central, in neither sector.

The bivector algebra and its two sectors. The Lorentz generators are half the bivectors, $S_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, and the factor $i$ in $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ is what makes the rotation generators Hermitian and the boost generators anti-Hermitian. They close on the Lorentz algebra, with residual $0$:

$$ \big[S_{\mu\nu},S_{\rho\sigma}\big] =i\big(g_{\nu\rho}S_{\mu\sigma}-g_{\mu\rho}S_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\nu\sigma}S_{\mu\rho}+g_{\mu\sigma}S_{\nu\rho}\big). $$

The Hermiticity split was verified too, and it is the sector split:

$$ \big(S_{\mu\nu}\big)^\dagger=S_{\mu\nu}\in\mathbb{M}_+ \quad\text{(rotations, magnetic)}, \qquad \big(S_{0k}\big)^\dagger=-S_{0k}\in\mathbb{M}_- \quad\text{(boosts, electric)}. $$

The bivector is also a matrix on the spinor module. In the canonical frame of the conventions article, verified entry by entry,

$$ \sigma_{12}=i\,\big(iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}\big)\big(iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}\big) =i^3\,L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}L_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}} =-i\,L_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}\big(-R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}\big) =iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}} =\mathrm{diag}(\sigma^3,\sigma^3)=\Sigma^3 . $$

Reading the factors: $i^3=-i$, the left multipliers give $L_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}L_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=L_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, and the right ones give $R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}=R_{-\vec e_t}=-R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, so the two minus signs cancel and one factor $i$ survives. The same matrix is the left multiplication by the biquaternion of the spin generator, $iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}=L_{i\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, twice $\tilde S_3/\hbar$ of the companion Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form; the bivector acts on the module as the doubled spin generator itself. Two sectors, one bivector algebra, and the anomaly's structure is the informational one.

The Clifford reduction. The algebraic work behind the loop computation is the reduction of the numerator, and it rests on the four contraction identities of §The Gamma Formulation, re-evaluated in the cell basis with residual $0$. With $c_\mu$ the frame cell of the direction $\mu$ and the plain weight $\varepsilon$ in the sum, they are

$$ \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,c_\mu c_\mu=4I_4 , \qquad \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,c_\mu c_\alpha c_\mu=-2c_\alpha , $$

$$ \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,c_\mu c_\alpha c_\beta c_\mu=4g_{\alpha\beta}I_4 , \qquad \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,c_\mu c_\alpha c_\beta c_\rho c_\mu=-2\,c_\rho c_\beta c_\alpha . $$

Read off in order: the four cells square to the metric; sandwiching one cell gives $-2$ times that cell, the minus being the reversal of a product of odd length; sandwiching two gives the metric and nothing else; sandwiching three gives the three in reverse order.

The weight $\varepsilon$ is what carries the metric into the sum: it is $+1$ on the timelike direction and $-1$ on each spacelike one. In the $\mathbb{B}$ frame, where every cell squares to $+I_4$, the weight is $+1$ and the same four identities hold with $g$ replaced by $\delta$. The four identities are what convert any one-loop numerator written in the generators into sums of metric factors and bivectors, that is, into the two structures of the vertex. The framework does not change them; it gives them a home, since the metric factor is the central direction $e_t$ and the bivector is one of the six directions.

What this transcription does and does not do. It identifies which algebraic object the anomaly multiplies, $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, hence a direction $\vec e_\mu$ or $i\vec e_\mu$, hence a sector, and it exhibits the Gordon split as the symmetric-versus-antisymmetric split of a product of generators. It does not compute the loop integral, and it does not fix $\alpha$.

The One-Loop Correction

The diagram. The order-$\alpha$ correction to the vertex is the one-loop diagram in which the fermion line exchanges a virtual photon with itself. Its value is standard: the numerator is reduced with the contraction identities of the previous section, and the result is

$$ F_1(k^2)=1+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\Big[\text{terms vanishing at }k^2=0\Big] , $$

$$ F_2(k^2)=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\int_0^1\!dx\,dy\,dz\;\delta(x+y+z-1)\; \frac{2m^2z(1-z)}{m^2(1-z)^2-xy\,k^2} , $$

in the standard Feynman-parameter representation. The sign of the $k^2$ term is fixed by analyticity: the denominator vanishes first at $k^2=4m^2$, the pair-production threshold, and with the opposite sign the branch point would sit at $k^2=-4m^2$, where there is no threshold. Two features make the anomaly the clean test it is.

  • The divergence stays in $F_1$. The Ward identity $k_\mu\Gamma_\mu=0$ is gauge invariance at the vertex, and it forces the divergent part of $\Gamma_\mu$ to be proportional to the tree vertex, that is to the $F_1$ structure. The divergence of the vertex is the same one the fermion self-energy carries, $Z_1=Z_2$, and the same counterterm removes both. Nothing divergent reaches $F_2$, so the anomaly is finite: no counterterm is needed for it, and it is a prediction of the theory rather than a parameter of it. This argument is standard and is not supplied by the algebra.
  • The mass drops out at leading order. At $k^2=0$ the parameter integral is independent of $m$ after the $m^2$ factors cancel, so the leading anomaly is universal: every charged lepton has the same leading $a=\alpha/2\pi$, and every charged fermion does after the appropriate QCD corrections.

The $k^2=0$ integral. At $k^2=0$ the denominator is $m^2(1-z)^2$, the mass cancels, and the parameter integral collapses:

$$ \int_0^1\!dz\int_0^{1-z}\!dy\;\frac{2z(1-z)}{(1-z)^2} =\int_0^1\!dz\;\frac{2z}{1-z}\big[(1-z)-0\big] =\int_0^1\!2z\,dz=1 , $$

the inner parameter running over an interval of length $1-z$.

This reduction was verified numerically, and it is exactly what makes the leading value

$$ \boxed{\;a=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}=1.1614097329\times10^{-3}\;} $$

for $\alpha=1/137.035999084$. The number is Schwinger's, the first radiative correction ever computed, and it sits above the measured electron anomaly

$$ a_e^{\mathrm{exp}}=1.15965218073\times10^{-3} $$

with a relative excess of $1.5156\times10^{-3}$, of the order of the two-loop correction.

The higher orders. The perturbative series is conventionally written

$$ \boxed{\;a=\sum_{\lambda\ge1}A_\lambda\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\Big)^\lambda , \qquad A_1=\frac12=0.5 , \qquad A_2=\frac{197}{144}+\frac{\pi^2}{12}-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\ln2+\frac34\zeta(3)\;}. $$

The two-loop coefficient was recomputed from this closed form:

$$ A_2=-0.328478965579\ldots , $$

in agreement with the standard value. With $A_3=1.181241456\ldots$ the electron prediction becomes

$$ a_e=1.1596522320\times10^{-3} $$

against the measured $1.15965218073\times10^{-3}$: agreement to $5\times10^{-11}$ in $a_e$, nine significant figures. That comparison tests the standard calculation and not this framework; it is reproduced here because it is the numerical reference the conventions are checked against. With the two-loop term alone the prediction is $1.1596374278\times10^{-3}$, short by about $1.5\times10^{-8}$, which is the size of the three-loop term. Beyond QED, the same decomposition organizes the muon anomaly into its QED, electroweak and hadronic parts; that is particle physics and lies outside this subcategory.

What the Algebra Supplies and What It Does Not

Standard, transcribed. The vertex decomposition into $F_1$ and $F_2$; the tree-level $g=2$; the Gordon decomposition; the one-loop diagram and its Feynman-parameter representation; the Ward-identity confinement of the divergence to the $F_1$ structure; the value $a=\alpha/2\pi$; the higher-order coefficients; the comparison with the measured electron anomaly; the Clifford trace and contraction identities; and the explicit frame cells with their anticommutators.

The algebra's own.

  • The sector placement of the two vertex structures. The magnetic (bivector, rotation-type) structure is the $\mathbb{M}_+$ direction $i\vec e_\mu$, the same sector as the spin generators $\tilde S_\mu=\tfrac{\hbar}{2}i\vec e_\mu$; the electric (boost-type) structure is the $\mathbb{M}_-$ direction $\vec e_\mu$; the convection (metric) structure is central.
  • The Gordon split as the symmetric/antisymmetric split of a generator product, done in the algebra's own basis.
  • The dictionary verification of the six bivectors against the six algebra directions.

It fixes the conventions. The Clifford metric, the sign of $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, the dictionary and the propagator's $i\epsilon$ are fixed by the companion articles, and the standard loop integral is transcribed into them without translation error.

It does not supply the numbers. The value of $\alpha$, the coefficient $A_2$, and the finiteness of the anomaly are not consequences of the algebra. The finiteness is the Ward identity's; the coefficients are the theory's; $\alpha$ is empirical. The position is exactly the one the propagator companion records for the $i\epsilon$ and the mass article records for the masses: the algebra names the axis, and physics fixes its magnitude and orientation.

Open Questions

  • The intrinsic field. As the companion propagation and quantization articles record, an intrinsic $\mathbb{B}$-valued three-point function is not constructed; the vertex here is the spinor-module object transcribed into the algebra's tensor language.
  • Whether the algebra constrains the coefficients. No mechanism is offered by which $\mathbb{B}$ would fix $A_1$, $A_2$, or $\alpha$; the algebra's role is structural.
  • The electroweak and hadronic contributions to the muon anomaly are outside this subcategory and are not treated.
  • The axial anomaly, the anomaly-cancellation conditions and the Fujikawa method belong to the particle-physics subcategory; the trace anomaly and the functional determinant belong to the generalities subcategory. This article is the magnetic moment only, and it does not borrow their arguments.

The Gamma Formulation

The gammas are a frame of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, not a different algebra.

Here $L_a$ and $R_b$ are the left and right multiplications, $L_a\psi=a\psi$ and $R_b\psi=\psi b$. The cells below are the frame cells of §The Clifford Structure and the Frame of the conventions article, with the chirality cell added.

$$ \gamma_0=iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad \gamma_1=iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad \gamma_2=iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\qquad \gamma_3=iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}, $$

$$ \gamma_5=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}} . $$

The five are generators, not a basis: the basis is the sixteen products. A product of cells is again a cell, and that closure is what makes the reduction of a loop numerator terminate.

The reference-frame cell of the chirality operator is $\gamma_5$. The central grading $\chi=i\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{ad}}$ — the difference of the two central idempotents of the complexified algebra, so $\chi^2=e_t$ — is a different object, represented by $\gamma_5$ on the Dirac module.

The dictionary is $\mathbb{R}$-linear, not $\mathbb{C}$-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the sixteen products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the sixteen cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$: two real Clifford algebras, each of real dimension $16$.

The bivector and the generator. The vertex is written in the bivector

$$ \sigma_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac i2\big[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\big]=i\,\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\quad(\mu\ne\nu), $$

which carries the factor $i$; the Lorentz generator is half of it,

$$ S_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\sigma_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac i4\big[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\big], $$

and the conventions article writes that generator as one quarter of the commutator, $\tfrac14[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu]$, which is $-iS_{\mu\nu}$. The two symbols $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, the one here and the one of the conventions article, differ by the factor $2i$, the $i$ in it being the exchange of the sectors; that is why the article states which one it uses at every display. The product identity that separates the two vertex structures is

$$ \gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu=g_{\mu\nu}I_4-i\sigma_{\mu\nu}\qquad(\mu\ne\nu), $$

and the commutators of the generators are

$$ \big[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\big]=2\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\quad(\mu\ne\nu), \qquad \big[\gamma_a,S_{cd}\big]=i\big(g_{ac}\gamma_d-g_{ad}\gamma_c\big). $$

The identities the body cites. In the $\gamma$ realisation, with $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$,

$$ \{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2g_{\mu\nu}I_4,\qquad (\gamma_k)^2=-I_4,\qquad \gamma_5^2=I_4,\qquad \{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_5\}=0, $$

$$ \mathrm{tr}(\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu)=4g_{\mu\nu},\qquad \mathrm{tr}(\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\gamma_\rho\gamma_\sigma)=-4i\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}, $$

$$ \mathrm{tr}(\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\gamma_\rho\gamma_\sigma) =4\bigl(g_{\mu\nu}g_{\rho\sigma}-g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}+g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho}\bigr). $$

The four contractions. They are frame statements, since a product of cells is again a cell, and the weight $\varepsilon$ is what carries the metric into the sum:

$$ \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,\gamma_\mu\gamma_\mu=4I_4,\qquad \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,\gamma_\mu\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\mu=-2\gamma_\alpha, $$

$$ \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,\gamma_\mu\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma_\mu=4g_{\alpha\beta}I_4,\qquad \sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\,\gamma_\mu\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma_\rho\gamma_\mu=-2\gamma_\rho\gamma_\beta\gamma_\alpha . $$

In the $\mathbb{B}$ frame every cell squares to $+I_4$, the weight is $+1$, and the same four sums give $4I_4$, $-2\gamma_\alpha$, $4\delta_{\alpha\beta}I_4$ and $-2\gamma_\rho\gamma_\beta\gamma_\alpha$. The unweighted sum $\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu\gamma_\mu$ equals $-2I_4$ in the frame used here and $+4I_4$ in the $\mathbb{B}$ frame; it is a third object, not one of the four.

Summary

The anomalous magnetic moment in biquaternion form is the coefficient $F_2(0)=a$ of the bivector structure of the electromagnetic vertex,

$$ \Gamma_0=iR_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,F_1+\frac{i\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\,\sigma_{0\nu}k_\nu}{2m}\,F_2 , \qquad \Gamma_k=iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}\,F_1+\frac{i\sum_\nu\varepsilon_\nu\,\sigma_{k\nu}k_\nu}{2m}\,F_2 , $$

$$ g=2\big(1+F_2(0)\big),\qquad a=F_2(0)=\frac{g-2}{2} . $$

with the tree-level $g=2$ taken from the one-particle theory. The two vertex structures are the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of a product of two generators, the metric part and the bivector; the Gordon decomposition exhibits the split on shell and was verified with a deviation of order $10^{-15}$ on on-shell spinors. Under the dictionary the six bivectors are the six algebra directions, $\vec e_\mu\mapsto L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ and $i\vec e_\mu\mapsto L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$; the factor $i$ of $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ sends the three rotation-type bivectors to the informational sector, $i\vec e_\mu\in\mathbb{M}_+$, the same sector as the spin, and the three boost-type ones to the material sector, $\vec e_\mu\in\mathbb{M}_-$. The convection structure is central. In the canonical frame $\sigma_{12}=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma^3,\sigma^3)=\Sigma^3$, so the anomaly multiplies the spin matrix itself.

The one-loop representation of $F_2$ reduces at $k^2=0$ to $\int_0^12z\,dz=1$, giving Schwinger's value $a=\alpha/2\pi=1.1614097329\times10^{-3}$; the two-loop coefficient $A_2=197/144+\pi^2/12-(\pi^2/2)\ln2+(3/4)\zeta(3)=-0.328478965579$ was recomputed, and with $A_3=1.181241456$ the prediction $a_e=1.1596522320\times10^{-3}$ matches the measured $1.15965218073\times10^{-3}$ to $5\times10^{-11}$. The Clifford trace and contraction identities on which the reduction rests were verified with residual $0$; the trace identity was checked at every word length from one to four.

The algebra supplies the tensor structure, the sector placement and the conventions; it does not supply $\alpha$, the coefficients, or the Ward-identity argument that makes the anomaly finite. On this problem, as on the $i\epsilon$ and the masses, the framework names the axis and physics fixes the magnitude.

The Lagrangian Family: A Map of the Forty-Eight Articles

What the family is

The family is one method applied to one field at a time: write the action, vary it, read the structural consequence, compute the currents, and state what the variation adds and what it only relabels. The method is applied to the four one-particle fields — spin zero, spin one-half, spin one, and the non-relativistic spin-zero field — in five readings, and to their minimal-coupling versions in four readings each.

The five readings.

  • The Element Readings, eight articles: the field as one element of $\mathbb{B}$.
  • The Polar Readings, eight articles: the field as the eight parameters of its word.
  • The Momentum-Space Readings, eight articles: the symbol of the action at one mode.
  • The Non-Quantum Limit Readings, eight articles: the action truncated in the phase's rate.
  • The Fluid Readings, eight articles: density, phase, velocity, rotor.

The coupled corner. The Coupled Readings collects the sixteen minimal-coupling articles, which are the same four readings applied to a matter field plus one connection.

The frontier. Beyond the One-Particle Fields collects the eight articles that reach past the one-particle actions: the electromagnetic field, QED, the interacting scalar, the electroweak theory, the Pauli limit, the Rarita–Schwinger action, linearised gravity, and the map of the second quantisation.

The three hubs added in the last pass.

  • The Lagrangian Articles by Object: Four Fields, Nine Articles Each: the family read from the object instead of the reading.
  • The Quantisation Hub: The Sixteen Articles That Import the Promotion: the articles on the far side of the boundary, in the order the operation is performed.
  • The Sector Hub: What the Lagrangian Family Reaches in Each Sector: the four sectors and their four different answers.

The object hubs. Each of the four objects has a page listing every article the corpus has on it, not only the family's: Klein–Gordon, Dirac, Proca, Schrödinger. These are the pages that answer the question the five reading hubs cannot answer: which eight of the sixty-four articles on this object is the family's.

The schema

Every article of the forty carries the same seventeen field slots, numbered 1 to 17, plus four sub-blocks: P1–P2 for the polar readings, M1–M3 for the momentum readings, 11a–11c for the limit readings, and S1–S7 for the sector statements. Ten slots are mandatory in every article — introduction, conventions, action, variation, structural consequence, currents, ledger, summary, notation, reading — and seven are conditional.

The structural consequence is slot 7, and it is the article's reason to exist. It carries one claim the reader can check by hand, in the article's own displays. An article whose slot 7 merely restates its slot 3 has failed, whatever its length.

A slot that is not used is declared. Each article ends its introduction with a sentence naming the conditional slots it omits and why. The declarations are the schema's honesty mechanism: a missing section is visible, and it is not mistaken for an oversight.

Completeness

The forty within-schema articles are complete: every mandatory slot is present in every one, and every conditional slot is either present or declared. The eight frontier articles are complete where their subject permits, and their limits are declared in place.

The two declared absences. The Rarita–Schwinger article names the standard action and states that this family does not write it in the algebra; the reason is a missing contraction, and the article states it. The bridge article names the step to a quantised theory and states that the corpus does not take it.

The boundary: what this family does not have

The family's articles state it in one sentence, and this map states it in a list. This family does not quantise. Its fields are one-particle fields; their actions are quadratic; their brackets have not been promoted; their propagators are Green functions and not Feynman propagators.

What the corpus has elsewhere. A separate quantum-fields category promotes the brackets, writes the commutators and builds the Fock space, and a particle-physics category transcribes the Standard Model. Those are imports: they apply the standard quantisation to the biquaternion form and do not derive it from this family's actions. So the boundary below is the family's, and the corpus's own ledger is articles_internal/internal-note-on-quantum-field-theory-in-the-corpus.md.

Why the boundary is stated rather than implied. Because a reader who has ten actions, their currents, their counts and their constraints will reasonably ask for the next step, and the answer must be a name and not a shrug. The name is the promotion, and the bridge article maps it in four steps.

Two proposed moves, and why both were rejected

Two extensions were considered for the schema and both fail the same test.

The first was an eighteenth slot for the promotion to operators. It fails because slot 7's standard — a claim checkable by hand from the article's own displays — cannot be met by a section whose content is a postulate. A section that says "and then one postulates $[\hat\phi,\hat\pi]=i\hbar$" has no hand check, because the postulate is not derived from the action.

The second was a Fock-space block, S8, for the many-particle sector. It fails for the same reason and for one more: the block's content would belong to a different object. The family's data is one field's action and its variations; a Fock space is a representation of an algebra that the family does not construct.

Both decisions are recorded, with the date, in the family's migration notes. The test is the reason, and the test is reusable: any future slot must own a claim that can be checked from the article's own action.

What the family is for

It is a reference for the question "what does the variational principle say about this field, in these variables?" — with the answer kept short, checkable, and honest about its limits. It is not a textbook, not a quantum field theory, and not a substitute for either. Its value is that each of its forty-eight articles makes one claim, proves it, and says what it does not claim.

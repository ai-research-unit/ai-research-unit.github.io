## The Word of the Enlarged Carrier

**The word factorises into a Hermitian modulus and a unitary phase, and that is the exact statement of the enlargement.** For $H$ Hermitian and $\Omega$ $*$-unitary, the product $H\Omega$ is a general element, and the counting is four parameters for $H$ (a scale and a rapidity's axis and angle) and four for $\Omega$ (a phase and a rotor's three). **Eight for eight: the word is a bijection on its domain, and each factor is a chart of the piece it names.**

**The Hermitian factor's unit property carries the free chart's norm computation.** $\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{\natural}=1$, so $HH^{\natural}=r^{2}$ and $H$ is a $\natural$-unit times $r$. The free field's value is $iH$, and

$$
(iH)(iH)^{\natural}=i^{2}r^{2}=-r^{2},
$$

which is the free article's $\langle\tilde A_\Phi\rangle_\natural=-r^{2}$ recovered from the factorisation. **The timelike branch is $\Omega=i$ with $\varepsilon$ folded into $H$'s sign; the spacelike branch is $H\hat u$, the same construction with the direction carried by $\hat u$ instead of by the boost's axis.**

**The unitary factor is $*$-unitary and that is what makes it the phase.** $\Omega^{*}\Omega=e^{-i\vartheta}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{-1}e^{i\vartheta}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}=1$, verified to $5.6\times10^{-17}$. **A $*$-unitary element of $\mathbb{B}$ is one whose action preserves the algebra's Euclidean norm**, and the check of that is the check of the next section.

**The rotor is a genuine parameter of the coupled carrier and a frozen label of the free one.** On the free slice the rotor is $e_t$ and the only discrete data are the branch and the sign. ## The Mass Term: A Magnitude and a Rotation

**On the free slice the mass term is a real number whose sign is the branch's; on the coupled carrier it is a complex number whose argument is twice the phase.** Computing,

$$
\langle\tilde A\rangle_\natural=\tilde A\tilde A^{\natural}=H\Omega\Omega^{\natural}H^{\natural}
=H\,e^{2i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}}H^{\natural}
=r^{2}e^{2i\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}} .
$$

The rotor has cancelled, because $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}^{\natural}=1$; the phase has doubled, because $\natural$ is $\mathbb{C}$-linear and passes $i$ through unchanged. The check at $r=0.9$ gave $0.66852185+0.45736040\,i$ at $\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}=0.3$, against $r^{2}\cos0.6=0.668522$ and $r^{2}\sin0.6=0.457360$.

**The modulus is the free scale and the argument is twice the phase, and no rotor appears.** That is the chart's reading of the coupled equation article's "the carrier that closes is the carrier on which the mass term is invariant": the closure is the right multiplication by $\Omega$, and the mass term built from it sees $\Omega\Omega^{\natural}$, which is a pure phase. **The mass term is rotor-blind and phase-covariant**, and it is the phase's double that appears.

**The invariant the coupled action uses is the real one, and it is phase-blind.** $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A^{*}\tilde A^{\natural})=\mathrm{Sc}(\Omega^{-1}H^{2}\Omega^{\natural})$ is real and independent of $\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}$: the value $0.4465451904$ was obtained at $\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}=0$ and at $\vartheta_{\mathrm{pha}}=0.6$, identical to ten digits. **So the chart shows why the coupled action must be written with $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A^{*}\tilde A^{\natural})$ and not with $\langle\tilde A\rangle_\natural$ alone**: the latter carries a phase, and only the former is invariant.

**The branch sign has no place on the enlarged carrier, and this is the sharpest difference between the two charts.** The free chart's mass term is $\mp\frac12k_m^{2}r^{2}$, with the sign decided by the branch; the coupled chart's modulus is $r^{2}$ with no sign to decide, and what remains of the sign is the argument. **A discrete label of the free chart becomes a continuous angle of the coupled one**, and the doubling of the carrier is what allows it.

## The Measure

**The coupled chart's Jacobian is $r^{7}$ times an angular factor.** The word is homogeneous of degree one in $r$, and the diffeomorphism's determinant therefore scales as $r^{7}$. **The homogeneity test is exact and decisive**: the Jacobian determinant of the eight-parameter word at $r=0.9$ was $8.821347\times10^{-3}$ and at $r=1.8$ it was $1.129132$, a ratio of $128.000000$, and the fitted exponent is $7.000000$.

**The mass term completes the count, and the completion is the eight-component one.** The mass term is $\propto r^{2}$, so the chart's radial integral is

\int_0^{\infty}r^{7}\mathrm{d}r\,e^{-ar^{2}}=\frac{\Gamma(4)}{2a^{4}}=\frac{3}{a^{4}} ,
$$

**The unitary factor does not change the scale's weight, and the reason is that it is an isometry.** Right multiplication by $\Omega$ preserves the algebra's Euclidean norm: with $\tilde X=(0.3,-0.7,1.2,0.4)+i(-0.5,0.9,0.2,-1.1)$ the norm squared was $4.490000000000$ before and after the multiplication, to the last digit. **So the coupling's extra four parameters are purely angular**, the scale's radial power is set by the carrier's dimension alone, and the measure's weight on $r$ is what the mass term sees.

## What Composes and What Does Not

**Inside $\mathbb{B}$ the product closes, so the word of a product is computable.** For $\tilde A=H\Omega$ and $\tilde A'=H'\Omega'$ the product is $HH'\Omega\Omega'$ up to the non-commutativity of the two factors, and each factor's parameters combine in a definite way: the scales multiply, the phases add, the rotors multiply. **The coupled carrier therefore has a composition law for the algebra's product**, which the free carrier did not have at all, because the free carrier is not closed.

**The integral's composition is a different operation and it does not compose the word.** The composition of two kernels is a convolution: the intermediate field values are summed over, and a sum over intermediate values is not a product of two values. **So the word of a composite kernel is not the product of the words of the two halves**, exactly as in the scalar pair, and for the same structural reason: a sum is not a product.

**The scale is the clearest case.** The scale of a composite amplitude is the modulus of a convolution, and the modulus of a convolution is not the product of the moduli — it is the product only when the two phases are aligned along one intermediate value. **The angle's alignment is the stationary-phase condition, and the scale of the composite is the count of aligned configurations**, which is the free article's van Vleck-like statement in the scalar case and has no Proca counterpart because the Proca kernel is a bivector.

**The free pair had no composition law to offer; this one has one and it is the wrong one.** That is worth stating plainly: the closure that the coupling buys gives the word a product, and the product is not the integral's composition. **The scalar pair's non-composition and this pair's are therefore different failures**, one of arithmetic and one of analysis.

The Breaking Chain of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: One Flag, Three Stabilisers, Three Scales

Introduction

The layer. The chain is a statement about a group breaking on a formal copy of the carrier. Its Layer-A content is arithmetic at one step only: the six generators that leave at $M_{C}$ are the directions that move the canonical line, and that is why they carry no rank.

The question. In what order does $G_{4LR}$ break, at what scales, by which vacuum directions, and how much dimension and rank does each step cost?

The answer.

$$ \boxed{\; \mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R \to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R \to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y \to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}, \;} $$

$$ \boxed{\; 21\to15\to12\to9\ \text{in dimensions},\qquad 5\to5\to4\to3\ \text{in rank},\qquad 6+3+3=12\ \text{newly massive},\quad 9\ \text{massless}. \;} $$

The chain is driven by one point of $\mathbb{CP}^{3}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}$.

The single idea. The chain is not three independent breakings. It is one object, a flag of three lines, and every step is the stabiliser of one line. Written that way the chain has no free choices left except the scales, and the scales are not tied to one another by anything this article states.

What the article establishes. That each step is a stabiliser; that the dimension and rank budgets close; that the massless count equals the dimension of the unbroken group at every stage; that each of the last two steps kills one neutral boson and one pair of charged ones; and that the first step breaks no Cartan direction. The threshold statement itself — $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at one scale, and the number it fixes — is not in this article: it is in The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model: One Equation, One Scale, One Content Statement, with its failure.

Not established. The three scales are not predicted. The minimal matter content does not run correctly: it fails, and the size and sign of the failure are in the coupling article. The three scales of the chain are three parameters, and in the minimal content the first two are not two values that happen to coincide but one breaking at one scale, described twice; splitting them into two thresholds needs a second field. §The Budget and the Thresholds states which of the article's numbers is a scale and which is a factor.

Division of labour. This article owns the budgets, the ranks and the eaten count. Why the order of the steps is forced — the charge chain $B-L$ then $Y$ then $Q$ — belongs to The Order of the Breaking in the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Nested Vacua and the Scale Order $M_C\ge M_R\ge M_W$, and is not re-argued here.

Where the spectrum is. This article owns the chain, the budget and the scales. The spectrum — which of the twenty-one gauge bosons stays massless, which twelve acquire mass, why three neutral states mix and how the photon comes out of the mixing — is Classification of the Bosons in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model: Sixteen Cells, Fifteen Generators, Nine Massless and Six Heavy. The fields that do the breaking are The Scalar Sector of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Twenty-Four Real Components, Twelve Eaten, Twelve Surviving, and the six coloured states the first step creates are The Leptoquarks of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Six States, One Bound, and Why Proton Decay Does Not Bite.

The Flag, Which Is the Chain

Three lines. In three spaces, and the chain is the sequence of their stabilisers.

1.1 The first line. It is the unit line, and it lives in the carrier. $\mathbb C e_t\subset\mathbb C^4$ is the line singled out by the $B-L$ charge: it is the eigenvector on which $B-L$ takes its extremal value, and it is the one line fixed by the whole stabiliser $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$. It is not fixed by $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ — an automorphism that moves it into the colour part does not fix it — which is exactly why fixing it breaks the group. Its stabiliser in $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$, and its stabiliser in the whole frame is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$:

$$ \mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(4)}(\mathbb C e_t)=\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}, \qquad 15=8+1+6, \qquad \mathbf{15}\to\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1 . $$

The nine unbroken directions are the eight of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and the one Cartan, $9=8+1$, and the six that leave are the off-diagonal $\mathfrak m$ of §Why the First Step Is Different. A $\mathbf{15}$ of matter branches into the same four pieces with the $6$ written $\mathbf3\oplus\bar{\mathbf3}$, so the algebra and the multiplet carry the same four labels. The count is a count of generators, and the threshold the minimal content actually has is the one The Budget and the Thresholds states; the difference between them is the field that takes the vev.

1.2 The second line. It lives in the $SU(2)_R$ doublet. The vacuum that breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ does it with a field in the $(\mathbf 4,\mathbf 1,\mathbf 2)$: one $SU(2)_R$ doublet, and the breaking of $SU(2)_R$ is the choice of one of its two components. A doublet has a $\mathbb{CP}^1$ of lines and not one, but every line has a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ stabiliser, and all of those are conjugate in $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$. So the line's stabiliser is $\mathrm{U}(1)_R$ whichever line is chosen, and the choice of line moves no group theory:

$$ \mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(2)_R}(\text{a line})=\mathrm{U}(1)_R, \qquad 3\to 1\ \text{on lines} . $$

What the lines do and what the generators do. A line stabiliser and the kernel of a mass matrix are different objects, and the difference is the whole of what the second step costs. No generator annihilates $v_R\,e_t\otimes e_\uparrow$: $T_{3R}$ gives $+\tfrac12$ on it and $B-L$ gives $-1$, so both are broken. The one combination that annihilates it is hypercharge,

$$ Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L), \qquad \mathfrak{su}(2)_R\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_{B-L}\to\mathfrak u(1)_Y, \qquad (3+1)\to 1\ \text{on generators} . $$

So the step consumes the whole $T_{3R}$ triplet and the $B-L$ boson and leaves one direction: a drop of four, not of two. Both counts are right and they count different things. The Unbroken Subgroup of the Four Colours Left–Right Model owns the kernel statement, and its §The Differences, and Why the Second Step Costs Three is the arithmetic that reconciles the two.

1.3 The third line. It lives in the $SU(2)_L$ doublet, and it is the electroweak one. Its stabiliser is $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$:

$$ \mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y}(\text{a line})=\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}, \qquad 3+1\to 1 . $$

1.4 One object, not three. The three lines are nested in the sense that matters: each is a line in the module of the unbroken factor of the previous step. The chain is therefore a point of

$$ \mathbb{CP}^{3}_{\ \text{(the carrier)}}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{\ \text{(the }R\text{ doublet)}}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{\ \text{(the }L\text{ doublet)}}, $$

and each factor of that product is a choice of vacuum, not a choice of group. The group is fixed; the vacuum is a point; the chain is the stabiliser of that point read off factor by factor. The word flag is used for that nesting of stabilisers and not for its geometric namesake: a geometric flag is a chain of subspaces of one vector space, and these three lines live in three different spaces, each a line in the module of the previous unbroken factor.

Completeness. Rank $5=3+1+1$ at the top; rank $2+1+1+1=5$ after the first step; $2+1+1=4$ after the second; $2+1=3$ at the bottom. One rank is lost at each of the last two steps and none at the first, and Why the First Step Is Different gives the reason.

The Budget and the Thresholds. The table has three rows and the chain has three factors, but the two are not the same count, and the difference is the minimal content.

Read as factors. Three stabilisers of three lines: the carrier line, the right line, the left line. That is what the table's three rows are, and it is exact group theory for the three lines. Each row is a factor of one point of $\mathbb{CP}^3\times\mathbb{CP}^1\times\mathbb{CP}^1$, so the table is the flag read one factor at a time.

Read as thresholds. A threshold is a scale at which the description changes, and that requires the vacuum to change. The minimal content has one field for the first two lines: $\Phi=(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ with $\Phi_0=v_R\,e_t\otimes e_{\uparrow}$ fixes both, so its stabiliser is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ of dimension $12$ and the first threshold lands on the $12$ row, not the $15$. The dimension sequence is then $21\to12\to9$, with $9$ states becoming massive at $v_R$ — the six leptoquarks, $W^{\pm}_R$ and $Z_R$ — and $3$ at $v$.

So the table's $15$ row is a genuine stabiliser (of the carrier line alone) and not a stage of the minimal content. Two thresholds, three factors, four stabilisers counting the top. The order article owns the same distinction and the charge argument that forces the order of the factors.

As masses. Twelve of the twenty-one become massive, nine stay massless, and the nine are the eight gluons and the photon.

$$ 6+3+3=12=21-9 . $$

A frame that predicted any other total would be wrong before any dynamics were computed.

As rank. $5\to5\to4\to3$: the first step loses six dimensions and no rank, the second loses three and one, the third loses three and one. So each of the last two steps kills exactly one neutral boson and two charged ones: one neutral and one pair of $W$'s at $M_{R}$, one neutral and one pair at $M_{W}$.

As a theorem. This is the sense in which the count is not dynamics. The number of massless gauge bosons equals the dimension of the unbroken group, always, because the Higgs mechanism eats exactly one Goldstone per broken generator. The last row's massless count is $9=8+1$, and $8+1$ is $\dim\bigl(\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}\bigr)$. So $9+12$ is a statement about group dimensions, and it would be a scandal if it failed.

Why the count matters. Because the assignment is not a count: which six become massive at $M_{C}$, and which three at $M_{R}$, depends on which vacuum is chosen. A frame could have the right totals and the wrong rows — if a leptoquark stayed massless, colour would be broken and the chain would be a different chain. The rows of Classification of the Bosons in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model: Sixteen Cells, Fifteen Generators, Nine Massless and Six Heavy, §The Spectrum, are the content; the totals are the arithmetic check on them.

The Goldstones. Every massive state is one eaten real scalar, so the twelve massive states consume twelve of the scalar sector's twenty-four real numbers; the other twelve survive as scalars, and $12+12=24$ is The Scalar Sector of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Twenty-Four Real Components, Twelve Eaten, Twelve Surviving's count stated in this article's language. Grouped by broken generator the twelve are $6+2+4$ — six for the leptoquarks, two for the $W_R^{\pm}$, four for the $W^{\pm}$, the $Z$ and the $Z_R$ — and two of those four are neutral, because they are eaten by the $Z$ and the $Z_R$. Grouped by the scale at which each state becomes massive they are $6+3+3$, as in the table. The two groupings agree in total and differ only in where the $Z_R$ is placed, and the reason is worth one sentence.

What each grouping counts. $6+2+4$ counts the eaten scalars by the multiplet that supplies them: six colour components of $\Phi$, two $R$-doublet components of $\Phi$, four of $H$. $6+3+3$ counts the massive states by the scale at which each mass appears: six leptoquarks at $M_{C}$, $W^{\pm}_R$ and $Z_R$ at $M_{R}$, $W^{\pm}$ and $Z$ at $M_{W}$.

Why they differ at the $Z_R$. Its mass eigenvector mixes the $T_{3R}$ direction, which lives in the right doublet, with the $B-L$ direction, which lives in the carrier; and its $T_{3R}$ component is one the bidoublet also carries. So its mass is $v_R$-sized and belongs to the $M_{R}$ row, while the $4$-count books its eaten scalar to $H$. In the minimal content, where the two breakings are one, that booking is a convention of the ledger rather than a statement about which field's vev did the work, because one vev did both. The Scalar Sector of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Twenty-Four Real Components, Twelve Eaten, Twelve Surviving owns the ledger, and this paragraph is the reason its $6+2+4$ and the table's $6+3+3$ are not in conflict.

Why the First Step Is Different

The structural fact. The six that leave at $M_{C}$ are all charged, and that is worth stating.

3.1 The off-diagonal block. They are the off-diagonal block of the fifteen. The six generators mixing $\mathbb C e_t$ with the vector part carry $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$ and are $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$ singlets, so

$$ Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)=\pm\tfrac23 . $$

The $\pm\tfrac23$ is the charge of the gauge boson $X^{a}$, not of a fermion: it is what makes the leptoquark couple a quark to a lepton and so mediate a charge-$\tfrac23$ transition, which is the process the $K_L\to\mu e$ bound constrains. They are the leptoquarks, and they are the only place in the whole chain where a coloured boson acquires a mass. The gluons never do, because $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is a stabiliser at every scale.

3.2 No rank. A Cartan generator is diagonal, and a generator with $B-L=\pm\tfrac43$ is off-diagonal by construction — it moves the unit line into the vector part. So the first step is the only step that breaks a group without breaking a Cartan direction, and that is why rank is untouched while six bosons get mass.

3.3 The neutral sector survives untouched. After the first step there are still three neutral gauge bosons in play — $W_L^3$, $W_R^3$, and the surviving $B-L$ field — and two of them must eventually become $Z$ and $Z_R$. The three neutral states are a consequence of the first step not touching the Cartan. The mixing that produces the photon from them is The Neutral Sector of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Three Ingredients, Rank Two, One Null Direction, which is the second breaking's neutral block.

What the Chain Fixes

Fixed, in four lines.

Colour is a stabiliser at every scale, so it is never broken and never needs explaining.

Hypercharge is a combination, $Y=T_{3R}+\tfrac12(B-L)$, so it is not imported.

The chain's three steps are three stabilisers of three lines, so the vacuum is a point of a product of projective spaces and the group is fixed.

The dimension budget $21=9+12$ and the rank budget $5\to5\to4\to3$ are exact, and they are the model's cheapest tests.

Not fixed, in four lines.

The scales. $M_{C}$ and $M_{R}$ are parameters, bounded but not fixed.

The running of any coupling. It is in the coupling article, with the failure it produces.

The chiral assignment. The chain breaks a vector-like group; the chirality was declared before it, in the sixteen.

The scalar content. Which fields do the breaking is a choice, and The Scalar Sector of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Twenty-Four Real Components, Twelve Eaten, Twelve Surviving counts what it requires.

The Coupling Statements and Where They Live

One home. The tree-level mass ratios with $g_L=g_R=g_4$ imposed, the three scales with their bounds, the three matching relations, and the running that shows the boundary condition failing are all coupling statements. This category keeps its coupling statements in one place, The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model: One Equation, One Scale, One Content Statement.

Why they are not restated here. Each of the four carries a value that moves with a choice: the scale of the first breaking, the ratio $g_4/g_R$, the coefficient of the running. A moving value printed in a geometric article reads as though the geometry produced it, and the geometry does not. The chain fixes the count, the stabilisers, the dimension budget and the rank budget. It fixes no number that a scale can change.

The failure. The equal-coupling condition implies two numbers at once, $\sin^2\theta_W=\tfrac38$ and $g_4=g_L$, and they are met four decades apart — $1.63\times10^{3}$ J and $1.57\times10^{7}$ J — so no single threshold satisfies both. That is the failure; §The Boundary Condition, and Why It Fails of the coupling article gives the arithmetic, and it is neither weakened here nor repeated.

Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

$M_{C}$ is not fixed by unification: nothing in this article fixes it, and the coupling article shows that the running does not either.

$\sin^{2}\theta_{W}=\tfrac38$ is a boundary condition, not a prediction of $0.2312$, and the coupling article records how it fails.

The three lines are not three independent choices: two of the three have nothing to choose, and the only genuine choice is the first.

The count is not a dynamical achievement; it is $\dim G-\dim H$ and would be a scandal if it failed (The Dimension Budget, Read Three Ways).

The first step is not like the other two: it loses six dimensions and no rank, because the six generators it kills are off-diagonal (Why the First Step Is Different).

The chain does not predict the twelve masses; it predicts which twelve states exist and at which step each gets a mass (The Dimension Budget, Read Three Ways).

Summary

The chain. $\mathrm{SU}(4)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y\to\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$.

The object. One point of $\mathbb{CP}^{3}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}$; three stabilisers; three lines; the group fixed.

The budget. $21\to15\to12\to9$ dimensions, $5\to5\to4\to3$ rank, $6+3+3=12$ newly massive and $9$ massless.

The first step. Six dimensions and no rank: the leptoquarks leave, and no Cartan does.

The scales. $M_{C}$ and $M_{R}$ are parameters. They are bounded, they are not fixed here, and they are not tied to each other by the geometry.

The failure. Recorded in the coupling article, in §The Boundary Condition, and Why It Fails.

The strength. The count, the stabilisers and the two budgets are exact and independent of any coupling. Everything numerical is in the coupling article.

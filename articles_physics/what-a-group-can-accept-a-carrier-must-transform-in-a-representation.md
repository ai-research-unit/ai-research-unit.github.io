# __What a Group Can Accept: A Carrier Must Transform in a Representation__

## Introduction

**The claim.** **Each group decides which of the three carriers exist for it.** The algebra fixes the bound — three spins and no fourth — and the group fixes the content inside that bound: $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has a spinor and a vector and **no scalar**, $\mathrm{U}(1)$ has a vector and **nothing else**, $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ has **all three**. **A sector's spin roster is therefore a fact about its group, not a slice of a universal list.** The mechanism is a matching condition — the group must have a representation of the carrier's dimension and reality type — but the output is the sector's carrier content, and that content is what this article exists to state.

**Why it needs stating at all.** Each of the four sectors states this in its own words. The strong sector says it has no scalar gluon; the electromagnetic sector says its roster is the photon alone; the electroweak sector says the fermion is a chiral doublet and that the wall is there; the primordial sector says its scalar is a matrix and not a quartet. **Four sentences, one condition.** This article states the condition once and then reads the four sectors off it.

**What the condition is and is not, stated exactly.** The group does not extend the roster: there are three carriers and no group invents a fourth — that is [the previous article](the-carrier-roster-three-spins-and-no-fourth.html)'s bound, and it holds for every force alike. **What the group decides is which of the three exist in its sector**, and the difference between the two statements is the whole article. The bound is algebra, identical everywhere. The content is the group's, and it differs at every force. **A sector's answer is a subset of three, and which subset is the group's decision** — which is why the table below is not one table but four, and why a sector's spin roster cannot be read off another's.

## The Condition

**A field is a section of a bundle associated to a representation.** Concretely, the carrier has dimension $n$, the force's group $G$ has a representation $\rho$ of dimension $n$, and the field is a map into that representation's space. **If no such $\rho$ exists, there is no field**, and the sector has no action at that spin — not a missing action, an absent object.

**Two things must match, and they are independent.**

- **The dimension.** The carrier's representation-space dimension is $1$, $2$ or $3$. A group with no $n$-dimensional representation cannot accept that carrier.
- **The reality type.** The carrier is anti-Hermitian, Hermitian, or an ideal. The representation must respect that: a real carrier needs a real or pseudo-real representation, and a complex one needs a complex representation. **The corpus's anti-Hermitian material sector is why its connections are anti-Hermitian**, and a group whose generators cannot be taken anti-Hermitian in that module is not admissible however right its dimension.

**The consequence is a finite table, and the table is the sector's spin content.**

| force | group | $n=1$ (spin $0$) | $n=2$ (spin $\tfrac12$) | $n=3$ (spin $1$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| primordial | $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ | accepted, as a **matrix** | accepted, fundamental | accepted, adjoint |
| strong | $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ | **declined** — no fundamental singlet | accepted, triplet | accepted, octet |
| electroweak | $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ | accepted, doublet | accepted, but **chiral** | accepted |
| electromagnetic | $\mathrm{U}(1)$ | **declined** | **declined** in the sector's own right | accepted |

**Gravity is not in the table.** Whether it is a force at all is a separate question; if it is read as one, its carrier is the frame and not an element, and the roster of this section does not apply to it. The table is a table of the four sectors that act on elements.

## The Four Cases, Read Off the Condition

**Strong, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$: the scalar is declined and the spinor is inside the sector action.** The colour group's fundamental is the triplet, dimension $3$; the adjoint is the octet, dimension $8$; the singlet is dimension $1$. **A colour-singlet scalar is not a coloured object at all**, so there is no scalar gluon and the sector cannot carry a fundamental spin-$0$ field. Its four spin-$0$ actions are therefore *effective*: the chiral field is a unit element and not a linear scalar, and a linear scalar cannot be coloured. **The spin-$\tfrac12$ slot is not declined** — the triplet accepts it — and the slot is empty for a filing reason only: the quark term is written inside the QCD display, $\sum_f\bar\Xi_f(i\gamma D-m_f)\Xi_f$, and needed no separate article.

**Electromagnetic, $\mathrm{U}(1)$: one carrier, and it is the vector.** The group is abelian and one-dimensional; its only representation that matters here is the one-dimensional one, carrying the connection. **The photon is the sector's whole roster.** Its matter — spin $0$ and spin $\tfrac12$ — is not declined, it is *external*: the charged scalar and the electron are other sectors' carriers carrying a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ charge, which is why the corpus's electromagnetic sector has one free action and sixteen coupled ones and no free matter action of its own.

**Electroweak, $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$: all three accepted, and the fermion is the problem.** The doublet accepts the spinor, the adjoint and the singlet accept the vector, the doublet accepts the scalar. **Nothing is declined — and the sector is still incomplete**, because the spinor it accepts is a *chiral* doublet and the algebra's connection is one-sided. That is the chiral wall, and it is a different obstruction from this article's: the representation exists, and the action for it cannot be written natively. **A sector can pass the acceptance filter and fail the next one**, and this is the case that shows the filters are independent.

**Primordial, $\mathrm{SU}(4)$: all three accepted, the scalar as a matrix.** The fifteen generators carry the adjoint; the fundamental carries the fermion; the scalar is taken as **the whole matrix** and not as a quartet. The corpus's own reason is a count: "**A vector cannot tell the two apart**", and the matrix's quartics are more numerous than the quartet's. **That is a choice of representation within an accepted carrier**, and it is the one place this filter's output is a choice and not a verdict.

## Two Obstructions This Filter Does Not Explain

**The distinction the article exists to draw.** An empty spin slot in a sector menu has four possible causes, and only one of them is this article's.

| cause | example | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| **the group declines** | no scalar gluon | there is no such field, whatever one writes |
| **the term is not admitted** | gluon mass, photon mass | the field exists and the term is forbidden |
| **the write cannot be made** | the chiral fermion of electroweak | the field and the term exist, and the algebra cannot state them |
| **the filing was not needed** | the quark, inside QCD | the field exists and is written elsewhere |

**Only the first is acceptance.** The second is [which terms survive](which-terms-survive-invariance-as-a-linear-condition-on-a-density.html); the third is the corpus's reach; the fourth is editorial. **A reader who conflates them will take a boundary of the algebra for a boundary of the method, or the reverse** — the error the sector hub is written to prevent.

**And the converse, stated because it is easy to overreach.** Acceptance is necessary and not sufficient. **A group accepting a carrier does not mean the sector has an action for it**: the electroweak spinor is accepted and has only a *named* term, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{matter}}$, and no display. The filter admits the object; it does not write it.

## What the Group Does Not Decide

**It decides acceptance and admission, and nothing about values.** Four statements it does not make.

- *It does not fix the coupling.* That the triplet accepts the quark says nothing about $g_s$; the value is a measurement, and its running is a one-loop object.
- *It does not fix the mass.* Whether a mass term is admitted is the invariance filter's business, and the answer is the same for every group that gauges the carrier.
- *It does not fix the potential's coefficients.* $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ accepts a matrix scalar; the potential's four written quartics and the fifth set to zero are [a declared choice](what-invariance-cannot-decide-declared-coefficients-signs-and-normalisations.html).
- *It does not fix the number of generations.* A representation that can be repeated admits any number of copies, and the corpus does not derive three.

**What it does decide, and this is the article's one structural claim.** The sector menus' three filled bands and one named-only band are this filter's output read as a layout. **Three carriers, four groups, and the pattern of which cell is filled is the acceptance table** — which is why the pattern differs per sector and the roster does not.

## Summary

A carrier becomes a field only when the force's group has a representation of the carrier's dimension and reality type. **The algebra supplies three carriers — dimension $1$, $2$, $3$ — and the group can only decline.** That asymmetry is what makes a sector's spin content a subset of three rather than an open list.

Read off the condition, the four sectors are: $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ declines the scalar and accepts the spinor and the vector, with its spin-$0$ actions effective because the chiral field is not a linear scalar; $\mathrm{U}(1)$ carries the vector alone, its matter being other sectors' fields with a charge; $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ accepts all three and fails on the chiral fermion *after* acceptance; $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ accepts all three and takes its scalar as a matrix by a count.

**An empty slot has four causes and only one is this filter's.** Declined by the group, not admitted by invariance, not writable in the algebra, or not needed as a separate display. Conflating them turns a boundary of the algebra into a boundary of the method.

**The group decides acceptance; it decides no coupling, no mass, no coefficient and no generation count.** The three filled bands and one named-only band of the sector menus are this table read as a layout.

The Biquaternioid General Plain Sesqualgebra (GPS) over $\mathbb{C}$

Introduction

The biquaternioid complex space carries eleven structures beside the ordinary product. This article is about the first of them, the general plain sesqualgebra, written $\mathrm{GPS}$, whose product is the plain product with the star inserted into its second slot, $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$. It is one of the two general sesqualgebras of the space, and its job in the framework is probability: its scalar form is the positive definite Hermitian form $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$, whose squared modulus is the Born weight.

The mathematics of the structure is owned by the group The Biquaternioid General Plain Sesqualgebra (GPS) over $\mathbb{C}$ of the mathematics menu. This article proves nothing. It lists the results, each with the mathematics article that owns it, and it keeps the physical readings the framework gives the structure, every one of them labelled as a reading.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_0=1$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$, $e_1e_2=e_3$, and central imaginary $i$. An element is $\tilde Q=\sum_\mu Q_\mu e_\mu$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$. The conjugations are the natural one $\tilde Q^{\natural}$, the coefficientwise one $\bar{\tilde Q}$ and the star $\tilde Q^{*}=\bar{\tilde Q}^{\natural}$; the sectors are $\mathbb{M}_+=\{Q^{*}=Q\}$ and $\mathbb{M}_-=\{Q^{\flat}=Q\}$ with $\flat=-{}^{*}$; the scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde X)=X_0$. All conventions are those of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe.

The Results

Basic Properties

  • The operation. The product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear and $\mathbb{C}$-sesquilinear — linear in the first slot, conjugate-linear in the second — and it is not associative. Its class among the twelve structures is that of a general sesqualgebra. Owner: Introduction to the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • The unit. $e_0$ is a right identity, $\tilde X\star e_0=\tilde X$, and not a left one, $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$. The structure therefore has no two-sided unit, and this is the algebraic mark that separates it from the ordinary product. Owner: Introduction to the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • The algebraic norm and invertibility. The norm of the structure is $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\sum_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}$, whose diagonal value $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}+\lvert\vec Q\rvert^{2}$ is positive definite of signature $(8,0)$ over $\mathbb{R}$. The invertibility criterion is $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, with the one-sided inverse $\bar{\tilde X}/\overline{\langle\tilde X\rangle_{\natural}}$. Owner: Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • Zero divisors. The zero-divisor set is the cone $\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, the same set as the ordinary product's, so the two structures share their wall of non-invertibility. Owner: Zero Divisors of the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • Annihilators. The left, the right and the two-sided annihilator of an element, and the radical of the product; the two sides are distinguished exactly at an element of vanishing norm. Owner: Annihilators of the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • Square roots of one and the idempotents. The roots of $+1$ form the family $U(1)\cdot S^{3}$, which is the unitary group $U(2)$ of the state space; the Hermitian roots of $+1$ are the involutions, and every idempotent of the structure is Hermitian, so the idempotent set is the pure-state set. Owner: Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • Square roots of zero and the nilpotents. The root set of $0$ is empty: the definite weight refuses a nonzero square-zero element. The nilpotents exist nevertheless, they are the pure isotropic cone of the algebra, of index exactly three, and the square of a nilpotent is a pure state. Owner: Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • Square roots of a general element. The structure admits a root of every non-negative value and of no non-real or negative one, one of the four existence classes of the twelve structures. Owner: Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Sesqualgebra.
  • The squares and the positive cone. The square of an element lands in the Hermitian cone, the image of the structure being the cone of positive elements together with the origin. Owner: The Squares and the Positive Cone of the Biquaternioid Sesqualgebra.
  • Simplicity. The structure is simple: it has no proper two-sided ideal. Owner: The Biquaternioid Sesqualgebra Is Simple.

Physical Interpretation

  • Probability is a product. The amplitude is the scalar part of the plain sesquilinear product and the probability is its squared modulus over the two norms, so the framework's probability is algebraic and not postulated. The boundary: the reading identifies the algebraic pairing with the probabilistic one and derives no frequency.
  • The metric is the norm of the state space, and the polarity of the slots is a physical datum. The two slots are the state and the observable: the linear slot and the conjugate-linear one. On one element the two rows of the framework are not two measurements but one measurement and one metric, since the probability and the interval give different numbers and neither replaces the other.
  • The undefinite row is the composition, and the probability row is the composition with one central sign. On the material sector the star is $-\flat$, so the second slot adds only a central sign and no new operation appears; there the probability of a state is the Euclidean weight of the four-vector, and the product kills exactly the lightlike directions, so the wall of the reversible is the wall of the probability, inherited by a global sign.
  • On the informational sector the probability of a state is the composition of the state with itself. The measurement projection is an idempotent of the product, so a measurement is a statement about the probability product and not about the value; the phase is the vector part of the composite, so the interference of two states is their failure to commute; and the purity is the half-difference of the two metrics, so the information content of a state is the gap between the probability and the interval, maximal for the pure states and vanishing at the maximally mixed element.
  • The state space is doubled and the two copies are orthogonal. The regular module is the orthogonal direct sum of two isometric copies of the state space, the operator coupling them is a right multiplication — a chirality-off-diagonal mass — and a right multiplication is a module operation and not a metric one, so the coupling is invisible to orthogonality. The complement is the conjugate copy under the real structure of the algebra.
  • The band that measures has its group of reversibility and no clock of its own. The composition band obtains its local complex structures by multiplying its roots of $+1$ by the central imaginary; the sesquilinearity of this band makes the central multiplication constant on the square, so the roots of $-1$ are absent here and the clock is owned by the composition band alone.
  • The probability band has no lightlike square, its cone lives at the third power, and each of its nilpotents carries a frame. The nilpotents are the parent's pure isotropic cone, one index higher than the composition band's; the square of a nilpotent is a pure state; and the two squares of a nilpotent are a complete orthogonal pair of pure states whose difference is the observable indexing the frame.
  • The Peirce decomposition is the populations-and-coherences structure in the basis of one of the observables of square one. The four Peirce coordinates of a state are its two populations and its two coherences; the populations sum to the trace, their difference is the mean, the norm is the population product minus the coherence square, and purity is their saturation.
  • The dagger is the operation under which the central phase is conjugated, which is why a reversible evolution is unitary and a mass term is not; rank reads as mass and positivity as the state condition, so mass and probability are two aspects of one dagger. The adjoint rule $\Theta_{\tilde Q}^{\dagger}=\Theta_{\tilde Q^{*}}$ is what makes an observable Hermitian and an evolution unitary, and the reversible elements are the units of the monoid.
  • The reality-condition trichotomy is a particle taxonomy: a spinor whose reality condition holds for no coefficient, for the central one only, and for the whole real part are three physical species fixed by the algebra rather than by an external label. A three-particle vertex is a ternary product of the structure, which is why the couplings are not the structure constants of a Lie algebra.
  • Probability is positive and the interval is indefinite, and the two do not trade places. The form that makes the transform of a measure positive definite is $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$, the probability form; the form that fails at the central element is $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, the interval. A positive measure whose transform is asked for a probability returns one through $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$ and never through $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, and the momentum-space positivity of ordinary theory is the positivity of the central clock.

Summary

The biquaternioid general plain sesqualgebra is the structure $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ over $\mathbb{C}$, the probability band of the framework: sesquilinear, non-associative, of a right identity only, with the positive definite algebraic norm $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$, the shared zero-divisor cone and no root of $0$. Its roots of $+1$ are the unitary group of the state space, its idempotents are the pure states, its nilpotents are the pure isotropic cone at index three, and its complex spinors carry a trichotomy of reality conditions. The mathematics is owned by the mathematics group of the same name; the readings listed above are the framework's, each labelled, and none of them is a proof.

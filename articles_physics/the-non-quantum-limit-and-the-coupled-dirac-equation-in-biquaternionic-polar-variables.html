The limit in one sentence. The coupling is one addition to the phase's bracket, so the limit's order zero is the mechanical mass shell with the boost carrying the kinetic velocity, order one is the three transport laws in the mechanical variables, and the spin's own transport stays the free one.

Applied to the mechanical wavevector it gives $\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}=ik_m\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2}$, so the boost in the word is the kinetic one and $\vec v=c\tanh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ is the velocity of the trajectory. The canonical momentum is then the mechanical one plus the potential, $\tilde K_\Phi=\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}-\frac{ϙ}{\hbar}\tilde A_\Phi$, and the word's frame is a frame for the kinetic variables.

One Addition and Two Frames

The addition. $\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}=\tilde K_\Phi+\frac{ϙ}{\hbar}\tilde A_\Phi$. The temporal slot is $\hbar c\kappa_t=E-q\varphi$ and the spatial one is $\hbar\vec\kappa=\vec\pi$: one element's two slots give the two mechanical variables and nothing else.

The frame. By the parent's proposition,

$$ \tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}=ik_m\,\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \tilde V_\Phi=ic\,\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2}=\gamma\bigl(ic\,e_{t}+\vec v\bigr), \qquad \vec v=c\tanh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}\,\vec u_{\mathrm b}, $$

so the rapidity is the kinetic one and the four-velocity is the trajectory's. The parent's display for the same statement is $\tilde P_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}=m\tilde V_\Phi$, with $\vec\pi=\gamma m\vec v$.

The two frames and their relations. The canonical wavevector is $\tilde K_\Phi=i(E/\hbar c)e_{t}+\vec p/\hbar$; the mechanical one is $i(\mathcal E/\hbar c)e_{t}+\vec\pi/\hbar$; and the two differ by the potential's slots. The word's boost is the mechanical one's inverse square, the canonical one's is not, and the difference is the whole content of the coupled polar chart.

The gauge statement, in the parent's sharpest form. For a pure gauge potential $A_\mu=\partial_\mu\Lambda$, the coupled derivative acts as $i\partial_\mu(\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}+\frac{ϙ}{\hbar}\Lambda)$: the entire effect of the potential is a reparameterisation of one coordinate. That is why the criterion must live on the invariant combination and why the chart's gauge group is a translation.

Order Zero: the Mechanical Shell and the Polarisation

The mechanical mass shell.

$$ \tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}^{\natural}=-k_m^{2} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{\mathcal E^{2}}{c^{2}}-\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}, $$

which is the free shell with the mechanical variables in place of the canonical ones. It is one relation among four components, and it is the statement that the mechanical wavevector over $k_m$ is invertible.

The polarisation. The chiral link is the parent's,

$$ \tilde\Xi_0^{L}=-\mathcal B_\Psi\,\tilde\Xi_0^{R}, \qquad \mathcal B_\Psi=\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2}, $$

and $\mathcal B_\Psi$ is now the kinetic boost, so the relation between the two halves is a relation between the two halves in the frame of the trajectory. In the canonical frame the same relation would carry a boost that is not the velocity's, and the difference is the potential's.

What the phase gradient then is. $\vec p=\vec\pi+q\vec A=\gamma m\vec v+q\vec A$ and $E=\mathcal E+q\varphi=\gamma mc^{2}+q\varphi$: the canonical momentum is the kinetic one plus the potential's contribution, and the two are different objects with different laws. The canonical momentum is conserved only where the potential is; the kinetic one is what the force changes.

Why this is the family's characteristic order zero. A Klein–Gordon field's order zero fixes the mechanical shell and nothing else. Here it fixes the shell and the frame's polarisation, and the frame is the kinetic one. Two objects, one algebraic system, and no derivative taken.

Order One: Three Laws in the Mechanical Variables

The density's law.

$$ \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial r'_{t}}+\vec\nabla_\Phi\cdot(\rho\vec v)=0 , $$

with $\vec v$ the kinetic velocity. The carrier is the trajectory's velocity, which is what makes the continuity equation a statement about the path and not about the chart.

The velocity's law. The boost's connection transported along the ray gives

$$ \frac{d\vec\pi}{dt'}=q\bigl(\vec E+\vec v\times\vec B\bigr), \qquad \vec\pi=\gamma m\vec v , $$

the Lorentz force with the relativistic momentum. The corpus verifies it twice, in Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave and in The Non-Quantum Limit of the Coupled Dirac Equation, and both verifications are of the same identity: the gradient of the mechanical shell is the force.

The spin's law, and the limitation the chart imposes. The rotor's bracket is a real vector part; the potential's contribution to the phase bracket is central imaginary and its contribution to the boost's is Hermitian. So the coupling cannot appear in the rotor's bracket, and the spin's own transport is the free one:

$$ \frac{d\hat s}{dt'}=\vec\Omega_{\mathrm{sp}}\times\hat s , $$

with $\vec\Omega_{\mathrm{sp}}$ the free precession's rate. The magnetic torque does not rotate the rotor directly.

How the observed precession arises, in the parent's own words. The background curves the boost, the spin's transport follows the boost's, and where the trajectory bends the frame in which the rotor's rate is zero bends with it. The observed precession is the composition of the frame's bending with the rotor's zero rate, which is the Thomas term, and the magnetic part enters through the boost's curvature and not through the rotor's bracket.

What follows for the anomalous moment, stated as a limit and not as a defect. An anomalous magnetic moment would have to be a term that rotates the rotor independently of the boost, and the chart has no slot for one: a precession of the spin direction would be a term in the fourth bracket, and the coupling cannot reach it. The corpus does not claim to derive $g-2$, and this article records that the chart's structure is why.

What the Coupling Changes and What It Does Not

It changes the frame. The boost is the kinetic one, so the polarisation, the velocity and the current are all statements about the trajectory. A reader who keeps the canonical frame gets a boost that is off by the potential, and the error is first order in it.

It changes the criterion's slot. The parameter reads $\vec\pi$ and not $\vec p$.

It does not change the order structure. Three orders, the same three, with the same reasons.

It does not reach the rotor. The spin's transport is the free one; the precession is induced.

It does not remove the amplitude's absence. The equation is first order, so no amplitude-curvature term exists to order or to discard. The Dirac family's limit is a constraint-and-flow split in the free case and in the coupled case alike.

Verification

The substitution was checked to be one addition. $\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}=\tilde K_\Phi+\frac{ϙ}{\hbar}\tilde A_\Phi$ has temporal slot $i(E+ϙcA_t)/(\hbar c)$ and spatial slots $(\vec p-q\vec A)/\hbar$, which are $i\mathcal E/(\hbar c)$ and $\vec\pi/\hbar$; the two slots match the mechanical variables slot by slot, with no independent sign.

The shell's form was checked. Applying the parent's proposition to the mechanical wavevector gives $\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}\tilde K_{\Phi,\mathrm{gco}}^{\natural}=-k_m^{2}$, whose real content is $\mathcal E^{2}/c^{2}-\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}=m^{2}c^{2}$; the free case is recovered at $\varphi=\vec A=0$ term for term.

The bracket types were checked against the parent's table. The phase bracket is central imaginary and receives the potential; the boost bracket is Hermitian and receives it; the rotor bracket is a real vector part and does not. The exclusion is by type and not by choice, and it was verified on the four types.

The force was checked independently. With arbitrary $\varphi$ and $\vec A$, the three components of $-\frac{c^{2}}{2\mathcal E}\partial_{i}\left(\mathcal E^{2}/c^{2}-\lvert\vec\pi\rvert^{2}\right)-\left(d\pi_{i}/dt'-q(\vec E+\vec v\times\vec B)_{i}\right)$ vanish identically, with $c\neq1$ so that the factors of $c$ are exercised.

What was not checked. No precession rate was integrated and no anomalous-moment term was constructed. The claims are about structure, identities and types, and all three were verified.

Summary

The coupling is one addition to the phase's bracket, and the parent's mass-shell proposition then makes the word's boost the kinetic boost. Order zero is the mechanical mass shell plus the polarisation relation written in the kinetic frame, and the canonical momentum is the kinetic one plus the potential.

Order one carries three laws in the mechanical variables: the continuity equation with the kinetic velocity, the Lorentz force, and the spin's transport. The first two are the free laws with the mechanical variables; the third is the free law unchanged, because the rotor's bracket is a real vector part and the coupling cannot enter it.

That exclusion is the chart's structural limit and it is stated as one: the magnetic moment does not rotate the rotor in this chart, the observed precession is induced by the boost's curvature, and an anomalous moment has no slot. The corpus does not claim otherwise, and this article records why.

The order structure is the free one, and the equation being first order means there is no amplitude-curvature term to order or to discard.

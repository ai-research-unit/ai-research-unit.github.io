Lepton Flavour in the $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ Model: No Generator Joins the Two Copies

Introduction

No connection vertex changes a lepton's line, and the proof is three matrix statements. The fifteen act on the sixteen as $X\otimes\mathbb 1_{2}$; the lepton is the temporal line $\mathbb C e_{t}$; and every array either annihilates $e_{t}$, multiplies it by a phase, or sends it into the three lines. None sends it to itself with a different label, and none sends it to a different lepton.

Two sentences that are easy to confuse, and the article keeps them apart.

  • In this model, nothing turns the lepton. The statement is exact, it is a computation on the fifteen, and it does not depend on any coupling.
  • Below $M_C$, a matrix decides. The channel $K_L\to\mu e$ needs a vertex that carries a generation index, and this model has no generation index at all: it has one generation, counted twice.

What the model does fix. The six leptoquarks, their charges, their mass, the equality of the two chiralities' coefficients, the group factor of one, the $\Delta F=0$ contact term that certainly exists, the selection rule that picks $K_L$ over $K_S$, and the fourth-power shape of the rate. What it does not fix is whether a lepton can change generation, because it has no generation to change.

Why the distinction matters. The bound $M_X\gtrsim3.7\times10^{-5}$ J is quoted in the literature as the model's strongest statement. It is not a prediction and not a bound of this model: it is a two-generation channel with the mixing matrix set to one, and it should be quoted as an assumption.

What the Model Fixes

The operator. Integrating out the sextet at tree level gives one four-fermion operator per chirality:

$$ \frac{g_4^{2}}{2M_X^{2}}\Bigl[(\bar q\,\gamma^{\imath}P_L\ell)(\bar q'\,\gamma_{\imath}P_L\ell')+(L\to R)\Bigr]. $$

One coupling, so the two chiralities come with equal coefficients, and that equality is exact here: the quartet and the conjugate quartet are made local by the same $g_4$. There is no $LR$ term, because an $LR$ term would need a scalar that carries both chiralities, and $Ξ$ couples $\bar\xi\xi$ and not $\bar\xi\xi$ of the same chirality.

The group factor is one. The quark legs sit on one spatial line, the leptoquark propagator is diagonal in the line index, and there is no sum over the triplet index to make. A group factor of three would need the two vertices on different spatial lines, which is a different diagram.

The mass of the exchanged boson is not free. The sextet's mass is $M_X^{2}=32v^{2}$ from the adjoint mass matrix, that is $16g_4^{2}v_C^{2}$ in the physical normalisation $Ξ_0=v_CD$, so the operator's coefficient is $g_4^{2}/(2M_X^{2})=1/(32v_C^{2})$: the coupling cancels and only the condensate survives. A reader who wants a heavy leptoquark has to want a large $v$.

The $\Delta F=0$ term exists and is unobservable. The diagonal part of the operator shifts the short-distance $d$–$e$ and $s$–$\mu$ couplings by a relative amount of order $(v/M_X)^{2}=1/32$, and no measurement reaches it, because the same operator's off-diagonal part is what the experiments are built to see and the diagonal part is a renormalisation of a coupling.

Why No Generator Joins the Two Copies

The split is temporal against spatial. The quartet splits canonically as

$$ \mathbf 4=\mathbb C e_{t}\oplus B_V , \qquad B_V=\operatorname{span}\{\vec e_{x},\vec e_{y},\vec e_{z}\}, $$

and the fifteen act on the two parts in three ways.

family on $\mathbb C e_{t}$ on $B_V$ can it change a lepton's line
spatial planes, $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ Cartan ($8$) $0$ acts as $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ no
corner $iD$ ($1$) $-3i$ times itself $+i$ times itself no, a phase
mixed planes ($6$) into $B_V$ into $\mathbb C e_{t}$ yes, but into a quark

The third row is the whole of the model's lepton physics. A mixed plane sends a lepton to a quark and never to a lepton, so the only lepton-number-changing vertices are leptoquark vertices, and they change $B-L$ by $\mp\tfrac43$, which is a change of species and not of generation.

The reason is a count of entries, and it is worth stating in that form. Each of the fifteen has at most one entry in the $t$ row and the $t$ column together, except the corner, which has one entry in each and no off-diagonal entry at all. So the space of generators that act within $\mathbb C e_{t}$ is one-dimensional, spanned by $D$, and one dimension cannot produce a second lepton.

The copy index does not help. It is the weak-doublet slot, the algebra is $\mathbb 1$ on it, and no generator joins $\alpha=1$ to $\alpha=2$ — the commutation statement $[\mathbb 1_{4}\otimes\tau_{3},X\otimes\mathbb 1_{2}]=0$ of the vertex article. Two leptons that differ only in the copy index are the two members of a doublet, not two generations.

The Matrix That Would Have To

A flavour-changing channel needs two matrices that disagree, and this model has one.

The Yukawa sector is one matrix $y$ on the copy index, and after diagonalisation the mass matrix is $y_{\alpha\beta}vD_{\imath}\delta_{\imath\jmath}$ — proportional to one matrix in the copy index and diagonal in the quartet index. Two sectors would need two different matrices; one matrix is aligned with itself by construction, so the mixing is the identity and the phase is absent (the Yukawa article).

The prediction that follows is falsified, and that is the honest end of the argument. $m_{\ell}=3m_{q}$ within a copy, against a measured $m_{e}/m_{d}=0.109$: wrong by a factor $27$. So the model's one matrix cannot be the whole story, and a second one is needed below $M_C$. The corpus does not write that second matrix, and an article that pretends otherwise is inventing a coefficient.

What a Mixing Would Change, If the Successor Supplies One

The rate, in the form that shows where the assumption enters.

$$ \Gamma(K_L\to\mu e)=\frac{g_4^{4}\,\lvert U_{de}U_{s\mu}\rvert^{2}f_K^{2}m_\mu^{2}m_K}{128\pi\,M_X^{4}} . $$

At $M_X=4.01\times10^{-5}$ J with $|U_{de}U_{s\mu}|=1$ this is $3.5\times10^{-12}$, against a measured limit of $4.7\times10^{-12}$ — the corner of parameter space where the matrix is maximal sits at the limit. That is why the number $3.7\times10^{-5}$ J is quoted: it is the floor the maximal assumption produces, and it is a floor on an assumption.

If the matrix is the identity, every channel is zero. $K_L\to\mu e$, $K_S\to\mu e$, $\mu\to e\gamma$, $\mu\to eee$ and $\mu$–$e$ conversion would all vanish at tree level, and no kaon or muon measurement would constrain $M_X$ at all.

The four powers are the part that does not depend on the assumption. The rate falls as $M_X^{-4}$ whatever the matrix is, because the operator is dimension six and the exchanged field is one. A reader who remembers the power has remembered the model's contribution; a reader who remembers the bound has remembered an assumption.

What Survives Regardless

The selection rule. For a pseudoscalar meson only the axial part of the operator acts, because the vector matrix element vanishes by parity:

$$ \langle0\rvert\bar d\gamma^{\imath}s\lvert K^{0}\rangle=0 . $$

So the $K_L$ channel is the one that exists and the $K_S$ channel is suppressed by $|\epsilon_K|^{2}\sim10^{-20}$, which is a statement about the operator's Lorentz structure and not about its coefficient.

The group factor of one. Two vertices on one spatial line, a propagator diagonal in the line index, and no sum: the amplitude carries no $N_c$ and no $1/N_c$.

The $\Delta F=0$ contact term. It exists whatever the mixing is, it shifts the $d$–$e$ and $s$–$\mu$ couplings by a relative $(v/M_X)^{2}=1/32$, and it is invisible.

The absence of a lepton–lepton vertex. No operator built from the fifteen joins two lepton lines, at any order in $g_4$, because no product of the fifteen can produce an entry in the lepton line's off-diagonal position — the lepton line is one-dimensional and the generators that act inside it are one.

Summary

Nothing turns the lepton in this model, and the proof is a count of entries. One generator acts inside the lepton line, six send it into the three lines, eight annihilate it, and none joins the two copies.

The channel $K_L\to\mu e$ needs a matrix the model does not have, because the model has one Yukawa matrix and one generation. The bound on $M_X$ that the literature quotes is a two-generation channel with that matrix set to one, and the model's own contribution is the operator's shape: two chiralities with equal coefficients, group factor one, and $M_X^{-4}$.

What the restructuring must supply is named and not invented: a second matrix, a generation index, and a condensate that does not tie the lepton mass to the quark mass by three.

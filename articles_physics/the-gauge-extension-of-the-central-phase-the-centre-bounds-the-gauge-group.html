The Gauge Extension of the Central Phase: The Centre Bounds the Gauge Group

Introduction

The framework's continuous symmetry is the central phase, the group of central unitaries $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})e_t\cong \mathrm{U}(1)$. It is exact and it is global: being central, it multiplies every subspace and every element by the same factor, so it acts the same way at every point, and the corpus records the fact as the interpretation "centrality is globality". A gauge theory needs the opposite — a phase that may differ from point to point, and a compensating connection that makes the difference covariant — and the question this article states is what, exactly, blocks the locality and what a gauge extension of the framework would have to be.

The article owns the statement of the extension problem, the reason the centre caps it, and the two candidate interpretations. It owns no new theorem. The centre and its triviality are The Centre of the Biquaternion Algebra as the Classical Sector; the ceiling $\mathrm{U}(2)$ is Why the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches SU(2) but Not SU(3); the generator is Special Element Conventions, §The Central Map and Its Restrictions; the discrete charge the compact phase carries is The Physical Interpretation of the 12 Products; and the placement of the gauge row among the products is The Heat Map of the Framework: Which Physics Hangs on Which Product.

The Central Phase and the Gauge Principle

The central phase is the one-parameter subgroup $\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}\mapsto \exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})e_t$ of the centre. Three of its properties are the whole of its relevance.

  • It is central: $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})e_t$ commutes with every element, so it acts by the same scalar at every element and in every subspace.
  • It is compact: the parameter is an angle and the group is a circle, so its irreducible labels are integers and the internal charge it carries is discrete.
  • It is the whole continuous symmetry of the algebra that acts uniformly, because the centre is one-dimensional and every uniform continuous symmetry must be central.

The gauge principle asks for the local version: a phase $\theta_{\mathrm{cen}}(x)$ that depends on the point, and a connection $\tilde{A}_{\Phi,\mu}$ that transforms so that the covariant derivative $\partial/\partial x_\mu - iϙ\tilde{A}_{\Phi,\mu}$ is phase-covariant. The framework has the phase and not the connection, and the reason is structural rather than technical: a connection is a field valued in the Lie algebra of the group, and the Lie algebra of the central phase is the one-dimensional span of $ie_t$, which is central. A central generator produces a field that commutes with everything, so the "connection" it generates cannot couple to anything and the covariant derivative collapses to the ordinary one. A gauged central phase is therefore no gauge theory at all: an abelian gauge field needs a matter field that transforms non-trivially, and the framework's matter lives in modules on which a central phase acts by a scalar.

The Centre and the Gauge Group

With the obstruction stated, the reason the gauge group stays at $\mathrm{U}(2)$ follows. The intrinsic gauge group of the algebra is the $*$-unitary slice $U=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}=e_t\}\cong \mathrm{U}(2)$, the central product $(SU(2)\times \mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$, and the abelian factor is exactly the central phase. The structure of the group is the structure of the centre:

piece of the group where it lives what it does
$\mathrm{U}(1)$ the centre, $\{\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})e_t\}$ the global phase, the discrete charge label
$SU(2)$ the unit quaternions, the real slice the internal rotations acting on the module
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ $\{\pm e_t\}$ the identification, the spin double cover

Why the extension cannot be a product, in one rotor sentence. The second row is $SU(2)$ under its biquaternion name, the group $\mathrm{SU}(2)=S^3$, and the first is the central phase, and the difference between them that matters here is that a rotor is not central: $\tilde u\,X\neq X\,\tilde u$ for a general element $X$, since $\tilde u i\tilde u^{*}=i\,\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u}(\vec e_z)$ and the Hermitian pair turns the central $i$ only when the rotor is real. An extension whose new factor commutes with everything is a product; an extension by a factor that does not commute is not, and it is the non-centrality of the rotor that prevents $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ from being the group of the construction. The bound is then arithmetic: the anti-Hermitian sector has four real dimensions, so the compact group it exponentiates to has four parameters, and $\mathrm{U}(2)$ is the only such group that contains a three-parameter simple factor and a circle. The table above is this paragraph written in rows.

A larger group would need generators outside the centre and outside the real-quaternion triple, and there are none: the anti-Hermitian sector of $\mathbb{B}$ has real dimension four, giving $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ exactly, and the Cartan decomposition of that Lie algebra has the compact part $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ and no more. So the ceiling is not a limit of the framework's ambitions but a computation of the algebra, and the route past it is an enlarged carrier — $M_n(\mathbb{B})\cong M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$, whose intrinsic gauge group is $\mathrm{U}(2n)$ and which contains $SU(3)$ for $n\ge2$, as the ceiling article computes — and not a new interpretation of $\mathbb{B}$. What the enlargement changes is the algebra and not its centre: every matrix algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ has centre the scalars, so the additional generators are non-central, and it is their presence, and not a larger centre, that carries the larger group. The centre's role is exact and it is narrower than it may look: it is the whole of the algebra's uniform symmetry, so it fixes the abelian factor of the intrinsic group at $\mathrm{U}(1)$ and forbids a second uniform phase, and it does not by itself bound the group, which is bounded by the dimension of the anti-Hermitian sector.

The parity matching. The central phase has period $2\pi i$, but its shifts are not independent of the module rotations. The kernel of the exponential on the diagonal part is the parity-matched lattice

$$ \boxed{\;L=\pi i m\,e_t+\pi n\,\vec U, \qquad m\equiv n\pmod 2\;}, $$

so a shift by $2\pi i$ in the phase and a shift by $2\pi$ in the rotation are each allowed alone, while a half-shift $\pi i$ must be accompanied by a half-turn $\pi\vec U$. The kernel is therefore not the rectangular lattice $2\pi i\mathbb{Z}e_t\oplus2\pi\mathbb{Z}\vec U$, and the two factors of $\mathrm{U}(1)\times SU(2)$ are not independent at the level of the exponent. The parity matching is the algebraic reason the central phase cannot be gauged by itself: a gauge shift of the phase alone is a kernel element, but a half-phase is not, and the obstruction is the same one that caps the group at $\mathrm{U}(2)$. The lattice is computed in Biquaternion Elementary Functions and read in The Exponential, the Logarithm and the Generator.

The Two Candidate Readings

Two interpretations of the extension problem are consistent with the algebra, and the article states both and chooses neither.

Reading A — the central extension is the whole extension. The gauge structure of the framework is the central product $\mathrm{U}(2)$ and nothing else, and the physically meaningful statement is that the framework's gauge group is the extension of $SU(2)$ by the central phase. On this interpretation the framework is a $\mathrm{U}(2)$-gauge theory in which the abelian factor is the algebra's centre, hypercharge is the central phase and weak isospin is the module action, and the ceiling is a result about the gauge group of the algebra rather than a defect. The interpretation is the framework's own position and is labelled as an interpretation: the algebra fixes the group and not the names of its factors.

Reading B — the central phase is the trace of a local structure. The gauge structure the framework should carry is a local one, and the central phase is the shadow of a connection that the algebra does not contain. On this interpretation the interesting object is the obstruction itself: a local phase gauge theory requires a non-central generator, the algebra has none, and the minimal carrier on which the obstruction lifts is the enlarged one. The interpretation makes the ceiling a constructive programme — which carrier, which module, which connection — and it is the interpretation under which the grand-unification articles work.

The two interpretations are not contradictory: the first states what the framework has, the second what it would need, and the article's position is that both are true and that the disagreement is about which question is being asked.

What Reading A cannot deliver

The hypercharge clause of Reading A is false, and the disproof is one paragraph. Write $\mathfrak u(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$ by the trace decomposition. Any Lie-algebra isomorphism onto that direct sum carries the centre onto the centre, because the centre is an invariant of the algebra and not of a chosen splitting; the centre of $\mathfrak u(2)$ is the line $\mathbb R\,ie_t$, and the line is one-dimensional. A connection valued in $\mathfrak u(1)$ therefore couples to every field through the single number that multiplies $ie_t$. Hypercharge requires six values on one generation, with $Y_L\neq Y_R$ for a single species, and a map that sends every element to a multiple of one generator cannot produce them.

So the two readings survive, with one clause removed. Reading A remains true as a statement about the framework's group: the gauge group is $\mathrm U(2)$, the abelian factor is the central phase, and the ceiling is exact. What it cannot also claim is that this phase is hypercharge. Reading B is untouched, since it already denies that the algebra contains the needed structure.

That article reaches the same conclusion from the other end: the framework supplies the algebra of the group and not the assignment, and one central charge cannot supply two numbers for one species.

What Is Not Claimed

  • The article does not claim that the central phase is gaugeable in the sense of producing a propagating abelian gauge field. It claims that the framework's continuous symmetry is the central phase and that the algebra supplies no non-central generator with which to localise it.
  • It does not claim that the ceiling is permanent. The ceiling is a statement about $\mathbb{B}$; the enlarged carriers of the ceiling article pass it, and the price is stated there and not repeated here.
  • It does not claim that the framework's $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is the electromagnetic one. The identification of the central phase with hypercharge, with electric charge, or with a global phase of the state module is an interpretation of the framework and not a consequence of the algebra, and no experiment is proposed here.

Summary

The framework's continuous symmetry is the central phase $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{cen}})e_t\cong \mathrm{U}(1)$, central, compact and global, and it is the whole of the algebra's uniform symmetry. Its gauge extension is blocked by centrality: a local phase needs a connection valued in a non-central generator, the algebra's only uniform generator is the central $ie_t$, and a central connection couples to nothing. The intrinsic gauge group is the $*$-unitary slice $\mathrm{U}(2)=(SU(2)\times \mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$, whose abelian factor is the central phase and whose size is fixed by the four-dimensional anti-Hermitian sector; a larger group requires an enlarged carrier with non-central generators, not a larger centre. Two interpretations are stated and neither is chosen: that the central extension is the whole extension, and that the central phase is the trace of a local structure the algebra does not contain.

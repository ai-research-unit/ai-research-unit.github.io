# __The Most Common Items with a Tilde__

**Scope: tilde glyphs only.** Every symbol in the tables carries a tilde. Four kinds of glyph are outside the scope and are not listed among the allowed uses: a matrix, $I_n$, $\mathsf{M}_2$, $\mathsf{M}_4$; a pure element written with an arrow, $\vec Q$, $\vec q$, $\vec u$, $\vec U$, $\vec\Xi$, $\vec\Upsilon$, $\vec\pi$; a mark that is not an element, $\tilde\cdot$, $\tilde\star$, $\tilde{\ }$; and every glyph of the reduced, tilde-free layers, $\Pi_\pm$, $\Xi_\pm$, $\Upsilon_\pm$. The first is fixed by the rule that a matrix carries no tilde, §*General Matrix Conventions* of *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe*; the others are elements of other layers or of no layer at all. The operator section states the rule that pairs the two marks; it is not a catalogue of arrowed glyphs.

The tilde marks a quaternionic factor. The last column of every table is the number of articles in which the symbol occurs; a base glyph counts every numbered and subscripted variant. The count covers the 2528 articles of `articles_maths/` (2110) and `articles_physics/` (418) on 18 October 2026; the catalogue itself is excluded. The list is triaged into two sections.

- **1. Allowed tilde** — the role is fixed by *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe*, or by the case-and-mark rule that article states, and checked against the corpus. Cite these entries as they stand. The tables are ordered by theme, as the objects are.
- **2. To be tested** — one specific role, plausible but not yet verified. Test before citing.

The governing rule: **only the Q family is generic, and only an element carries the tilde.** $\tilde Q$ is the biquaternion or split-biquaternion element and $\tilde q$ the quaternion or split-quaternion element; numbered variants are allowed. A generic element that is unitary is written $\tilde U$. A letter outside the family names an object, or an element bound where it is introduced. A matrix — the image of an element, an array of biquaternions, or a colour matrix — takes no tilde; a tilde on one is a defect of scope, corrected where it occurs and not catalogued here.

## 2. To be tested

One specific role per entry, not yet verified. The test is one pass over the articles that use the symbol, checking that every use matches the description. A glyph whose uses are all bound but span several roles is listed separately at the end of the section, with the pass recorded. The section now holds no entry: its entries failed the test or were retired, and the passes are recorded here.

**$\tilde\Gamma_\mu$ is not a tilde glyph.** The connection one-form takes its values in the six-dimensional Lie subspace $\mathrm{span}_\mathbb{R}\{\vec e_k,i\vec e_k\}$, whose elements are traceless; in the matrix image the trace is twice the scalar part, $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q)=2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)$, so tracelessness is purity, and by §*Pure Elements and their Coefficients* of *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe* a pure element carries the arrow and never the tilde. The glyph is $\vec\Gamma_\mu$. The transformation law preserves the mark: a conjugation by a unit rotor preserves the vector subspace, and the inhomogeneous term is pure for a unit element, $(\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{-1}=(\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}$ with $\mathrm{Sc}\bigl((\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}\bigr)=0$ because differentiating $\tilde U\tilde U^{\natural}=e_t$ gives $(\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}=-\bigl((\partial_\mu\tilde U)\tilde U^{\natural}\bigr)^{\natural}$. The row is removed. The seven articles that write the connection — *Curved Spacetime and the Biquaternion Framework*, *The Covariant Derivative and Gauge Connection in Biquaternionic Form*, *Gauge Curvature and the Bianchi Identity in Biquaternionic Form*, *Quantum Gravity under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda*, *Torsion, Contorsion and the Spin Connection in Biquaternionic Form*, *Linearized Gravity in Biquaternionic Form* and *The Einstein Field Equations under the Biquaternion Framework — A Research Agenda* — now write $\vec\Gamma_\mu$. The glyph $\tilde\Gamma_\mu$ no longer occurs in the corpus. The field strength $\tilde F$ is the exception: it is pure, and the conventions keep its tilde in the Maxwell equation $\tilde\nabla\tilde F=-\sqrt{\mu}\,\tilde J$.

**$\tilde\phi$ is retired.** The glyph held two roles, and neither takes the letter. The sector parts of the algebra-valued field in *The Functional Integral in Biquaternionic Form* are $\tilde\Phi_\pm=\tfrac12(\tilde\Phi\pm\tilde\Phi^{\flat})\in\mathbb{M}_\pm$; the earlier Fourier uses in *The Klein–Gordon Path Integral in Biquaternionic Form* and *The Quantized Scalar Field in Biquaternionic Form* were already rewritten in the central coefficient, $\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\omega,\vec k)$ and $\phi(\vec p)$. The classical field of *The Generating Functional and the Effective Action in Biquaternionic Form*, *The Functional Determinant in Biquaternionic Form* and *The Effective Potential and the Coleman–Weinberg Mechanism in Biquaternionic Form* is $\tilde\Phi_{\mathrm{cl}}=\delta W/\delta\tilde J$ on the module, with sector parts $\tilde\Phi_{\mathrm{cl},\pm}$; the classical field is the expectation value of the field, so it takes the field glyph with the label $\mathrm{cl}$. The minuscule was the defect: the object is an element of $\mathbb{B}$ with a complex scalar part, and §*The Elements and their Coefficients* of *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe* asks the majuscule and lists the algebra-valued fields as $\tilde\Psi$, $\tilde\Phi$, $\tilde U$. The glyph $\tilde\phi$ no longer occurs in the corpus, and the row is removed.

**$\tilde\varphi$ is retired.** Both articles wrote a complex central scalar in the minuscule. In *The Effective Potential and the Coleman–Weinberg Mechanism in Biquaternionic Form* the classical field is $\tilde\Phi_{\mathrm{cl}}=\delta W/\delta\tilde J=\varphi_c\,e_t$ for a constant background; in *The Stueckelberg Mechanism in Biquaternionic Form* the central scalar is $\tilde\Phi_{\mathrm{St}}=\rho\exp(i\tilde\sigma)\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$, and the Stueckelberg field itself is its phase $\tilde\sigma$.

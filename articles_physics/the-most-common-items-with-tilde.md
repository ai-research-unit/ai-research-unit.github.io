# __The Most Common Items with a Tilde__

**Scope: tilde glyphs only.** Every catalogued symbol carries a tilde. Four kinds of glyph are outside the scope and are not listed among the allowed uses: a matrix, $I_n$, $\mathsf{M}_2$, $\mathsf{M}_4$; a pure element written with an arrow, $\vec Q$, $\vec q$, $\vec u$, $\vec U$, $\vec\Xi$, $\vec\Upsilon$, $\vec\pi$; a mark that is not an element; and every glyph of the reduced, tilde-free layers, $\Pi_\pm$, $\Xi_\pm$, $\Upsilon_\pm$. The first is fixed by the rule that a matrix carries no tilde, §*General Matrix Conventions* of *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe*; the others are elements of other layers or of no layer at all. The operator section states the rule that pairs the two marks; it is not a catalogue of arrowed glyphs.

The tilde marks a quaternionic factor. Each entry gives the number of articles in which the symbol occurs; a base glyph counts every numbered and subscripted variant. The count covers the 2528 articles of `articles_maths/` (2110) and `articles_physics/` (418) on 18 October 2026; the catalogue itself is excluded.

**Allowed tilde.** The role is fixed by *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe*, or by the case-and-mark rule that article states, and checked against the corpus. Cite these entries as they stand. The tables are ordered by theme, as the objects are; the bound letters outside the U family have a section of their own.

The governing rule: **only the Q family is generic, and only an element carries the tilde.** $\tilde Q$ is the biquaternion or split-biquaternion element and $\tilde q$ the quaternion or split-quaternion element; numbered variants are allowed. A generic element that is unitary is written $\tilde U$. A letter outside the family names an object, or an element bound where it is introduced. A matrix — the image of an element, an array of biquaternions, or a colour matrix — takes no tilde; a tilde on one is a defect of scope, corrected where it occurs and not catalogued here. A tensor is not an element of the algebra: its components $T^\mu{}_\nu$ and $T^{\mu\nu}$, and the irreducible tensor operators $T^{(k)}_\mu$, carry no tilde, and a free element written $\tilde T$ is the same defect, corrected to the Q family.

## 2. Additional tilde to review

### 2.1 Bound Letters

No unbound letter remains. Every letter outside the Q family that names an element is bound in §1 or where it is introduced: $\tilde R$ for the material four-position, with $\tilde R_{\mathrm d}$ and $\tilde R_0$ for the translation displacement; $\tilde\Upsilon$ for the nilpotent partner of $\tilde\Pi_+$; and $\tilde U_T$ for the phase gate. The unbound $\tilde T$ is gone, and no other letter is unbound.

### 2.2 Glyphs without a Tilde

A scan of the 2528 articles finds no tilde outside §1. The reversion of a Clifford element is written $x^{r}$, the anti-automorphism that reverses the order of a product, and the Clifford conjugation is $\hat x=\alpha(x^{r})$; the four Clifford articles that wrote the reversion with a tilde — *Hermitian Clifford Structures*, *The Adjoint of the Left Multiplication on a Clifford Algebra*, *The Signed Adjoint of the Left Multiplication on a Clifford Algebra* and *The Signed Adjoint Sandwich on a Clifford Algebra* — now use this notation.

A tilde on a matrix is forbidden, §*General Matrix Conventions* of *Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe*, and is not catalogued.

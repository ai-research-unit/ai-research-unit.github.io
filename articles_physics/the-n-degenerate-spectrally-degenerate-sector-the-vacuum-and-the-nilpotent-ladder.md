# __The N-Degenerate, Spectrally Degenerate Sector: The Vacuum and the Nilpotent Ladder__

## Introduction

The region $\Sigma_{00}$ is the set of elements for which both invariants vanish: $N=0$ and $B^2=0$. The characteristic polynomial is $X^2$, the element is square-zero,

$$
\tilde Q^2=0,
$$

and the two eigenvalues are both zero. The region is the intersection of the null cone and the spectral cone, the degenerate stratum of the arrangement.

It has two faces. The **origin** is the only semisimple element: the zero element, with no scale, no mode and no direction. The **nilpotent cone** is the set of non-zero pure isotropic vectors: elements that square to zero but are not zero. On the material slice only the origin survives. In the full algebra the nilpotents are the fermionic ladder: a pair of them anticommutes to the identity, and their products are the two orthogonal rungs of a two-level system.

The physical content is therefore the **vacuum and its nilpotent excitations**, and the sector carries a third reading that belongs beside them: the **null electromagnetic field**. That reading is the most direct of the three, because it is a pointwise theorem about a physical object rather than an identification of the zero element. The article reads the region, the origin, the nilpotent, its operator, its field reading, the ladder and the products. It proves nothing; the nilpotent cone, the ladder and the null field are those of *The Square-Zero Element: Nilpotency as Mode, Differential and Supercharge* and *The Zero-Divisor Cone and the Massless Spectrum*, and the sector theorems are those of *The N-Degenerate, Spectrally Degenerate Sector*.

## The Region
The region is

$$
\Sigma_{00}=\{N=0,\ B^2=0\}
=\{Q_t^2+B^2=0,\ B^2=0\}
=\{Q_t=0,\ (\vec Q,\vec Q)=0\}.
$$

The two conditions force the scalar part to vanish, so the region is the set of pure isotropic vectors:

$$
\Sigma_{00}=\{\vec\Upsilon\ :\ \vec\Upsilon^2=0\}.
$$

It is the **nilpotent cone** — the non-zero pure isotropic vectors — with the origin adjoined. It has complex dimension two and real dimension four. The cone is not a manifold at the origin; the origin is its vertex. Off the origin the element is a non-zero nilpotent.

The characteristic polynomial is

$$
p_{\tilde Q}(X)=X^2,
\qquad
\lambda_+=0,
\qquad
\lambda_-=0,
$$

and the minimal polynomial is

$$
m_{\tilde Q}(X)=X
\quad\text{at the origin},
\qquad
m_{\tilde Q}(X)=X^2
\quad\text{off the origin}.
$$

The origin is semisimple; every non-zero nilpotent is defective, with a single Jordan block of size two. The region is the only one in which the two eigenvalues are equal **and** zero; it is the common limit of the rest frame and the light ray.

## The Material Case

**The region's material share is a single point.** With $\tilde Q=i\,q'_t\,e_t+\vec r$ real,

$$
B^{2}=\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \vec r=0,
\qquad
N=-q_t'^{2}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ q'_t=0,
$$

so

$$
\Sigma_{00}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\{0\},
$$

of real dimension zero inside a region of real dimension four. One hundred random material elements gave none in the region, and the only material elements with $B^{2}=0$ are the scalar line, on which the norm vanishes only at the origin.

**The consequence is that the ladder cannot be the value of a momentum.** A momentum chart has a real vector part, the ladder has a complex isotropic one, and a definite form has no non-zero isotropic vector. So **no four-momentum is a nilpotent, and the fermionic reading of the region is a statement about a carrier and not about a momentum** — the same distinction the section on spin makes when it says that an element has no spin. The vacuum is the only material element that the region names, and the point is the whole of the sector's share.

**The other two readings are placed by the same test.** The null field is the one reading of the region that is neither material nor excluded, and it is mixed: its electric half is informational and its magnetic half is material, which is why its norm has two parts and why nullness costs two real conditions rather than one. **So the region's three readings are separated by sectors**: a point in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$, a mixed pair for the field, and neither sector for the ladder.

## The First Carrier: The Centre

**The share is the apex and nothing else.** On $\mathbb{B}_{C}$ the vector square vanishes identically, and the norm $N=q^{2}$ vanishes only at $q=0$, so

$$
\Sigma_{00}\cap\mathbb{B}_{C}=\{0\},
$$

of real dimension zero inside a region of real dimension four. The centre has no nilpotent: a square-zero element with a non-zero vector part is not central, and a central element with a non-zero scalar part is not square zero. **So a scalar has no ladder**, and the fermionic reading of the region is not available to the first carrier at all.

**The reading is consistent with the spin.** The ladder is a two-rung object with an anticommutator, and an anticommutator is a structure on two directions; the centre has one direction, which is the time, and a single direction can be neither ladder nor shear.

## The Second Carrier: The Minimal Left Ideal

**The nilpotent line of the region is the degenerate stratum of the carrier.** In the parametrisation $q_t(e_t\pm i\vec e_z)+q_x(\vec e_x\mp i\vec e_y)$ the condition $B^{2}=-q_t^{2}=0$ is $q_t=0$, so

$$
\Sigma_{00}\cap S_{\pm}=\mathbb{C}(\vec e_x\mp i\vec e_y)
=\mathbb{C}\vec\Upsilon_{y,x},
$$

a complex line, of real dimension two inside the four-dimensional carrier.

**The ladder splits two and two between the ideals.** The four elements of the ladder are distributed by the value they carry:

| element | ideal | region |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\tilde\Pi_{+}=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z)$ | $S_{+}$ | $\Sigma_{01}$ |
| $\tilde\Pi_{-}=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z)$ | $S_{-}$ | $\Sigma_{01}$ |
| $\vec\Upsilon_{y}=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y)$ | $S_{+}$ | $\Sigma_{00}$ |
| $\vec\Upsilon_{x}=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y)$ | $S_{-}$ | $\Sigma_{00}$ |

**Each ideal holds one rung and one nilpotent**, and the rung is in the other region: $B^{2}=-\tfrac14$ for $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$, so the idempotent belongs to $\Sigma_{01}$ and the ladder straddles the two regions that the second carrier reaches.

**The ladder's pair straddles the two values.** The rung's line and the nilpotent's line are the two complex directions of each ideal, so the four ladder elements distribute two and two. The products are exact, and the two sides of the multiplication sort them differently:

$$
\vec\Upsilon_y\tilde\Pi_+=\vec\Upsilon_y,\qquad
\tilde\Pi_+\vec\Upsilon_y=0,\qquad
\tilde\Pi_+\vec\Upsilon_x=\vec\Upsilon_x,\qquad
\vec\Upsilon_x\tilde\Pi_+=0,
$$

with the mirror obtained by exchanging the two values, checked to $10^{-16}$. **Right multiplication by a rung returns the nilpotent of its own ideal; left multiplication by that same rung annihilates it and returns the other ideal's.** So the sorting by value is the right action, which is why the value test of *The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form* multiplies on the right, and the annihilation that §*The Nilpotent* uses, $\vec\Upsilon_x\tilde\Pi_+=0$, pairs a rung of one value with a nilpotent of the other.

**So the fermionic pair needs both ideals.** The ladder is a pair, and the pair straddles the two values, which is why the field is the sum $S_{+}+S_{-}=\mathbb{B}$ and not one ideal. The passage between the two regions inside one carrier is the vanishing of the rung coefficient $q_t$, and there is no other route, because the carrier's two complex directions are exactly the rung's and the nilpotent's.

## The Vacuum

The origin is the zero element:

$$
\tilde Q=0,
\qquad
N=0,
\qquad
B^2=0,
\qquad
Q_t=0.
$$

It is the unique element with no scale, no mode and no direction. On the material slice it is the only point of the region:

$$
Q_t=0,\quad\vec p=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N=0,\quad B=0.
$$

The reading is the **vacuum**: the zero four-momentum, the element that carries neither mass nor motion. It is the additive zero, not the multiplicative identity. The identity $e_t$ is a unit, with $N=1$ and $B^2=0$; it lies on the scalar line of $\Sigma_{10}$, not in $\Sigma_{00}$, and the vacuum is not a state with a norm.

The reading is the weakest of the sector's identifications. Nothing in the algebra distinguishes the additive zero of a momentum chart from the additive zero of any other chart, so "vacuum" names a role and not a structure.

The origin is the apex of the arrangement. Every region has the origin in its closure:

$$
0\in\overline{\Sigma_{11}}\cap\overline{\Sigma_{10}}\cap\overline{\Sigma_{01}}\cap\overline{\Sigma_{00}}.
$$

The reading is the common limit: a massive particle at rest, a light ray, and a nilpotent all degenerate to the zero element when their scale is sent to zero. The limit is not a physical process; it is the geometry of the cones.

## The Nilpotent

### The Element

Off the origin the element is a pure isotropic vector:

$$
\vec\Upsilon=Q_x\vec e_x+Q_y\vec e_y+Q_z\vec e_z,
\qquad
Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2=0,
\qquad
\vec\Upsilon^2=0.
$$

It is defective, with minimal polynomial $X^2$ and a single Jordan block. The centraliser is the dual-number algebra

$$
\mathbb{C}[\vec\Upsilon]=\{a e_t+b\vec\Upsilon\}
\cong\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),
$$

whose only idempotents are $0$ and $e_t$. No non-trivial idempotent commutes with $\vec\Upsilon$, so no non-trivial Peirce decomposition is attached to it and there is no spectral decomposition. A Peirce decomposition still exists relative to an idempotent adapted to the nilpotent; in it $\vec\Upsilon$ lies in the mixed corner. The closest structure is the flag

$$
0\subset\mathbb{C}\vec\Upsilon\subset\ker L_{\vec\Upsilon},
$$

and the flag is the whole of the invariant data.

The powers and the exponential are

$$
\vec\Upsilon^n=0\quad(n\ge2),
\qquad
e^{\theta\vec\Upsilon}=e_t+\theta\vec\Upsilon.
$$

The exponential is a shear, not a rotation. There is no inverse and no logarithm: $\vec\Upsilon$ is a zero divisor, and the exponential is invertible.

### The Operator

Left multiplication by a nilpotent is itself a square-zero operator, and its kernel and its image coincide:

$$
L_{\vec\Upsilon}^2=0,
\qquad
L_{\vec\Upsilon}\ \text{has rank two},
\qquad
\operatorname{im}L_{\vec\Upsilon}=\ker L_{\vec\Upsilon},
\qquad
\mathsf M_2(\vec\Upsilon)\sim\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The kernel is a complex plane, and it is spanned by the nilpotent and by the rung that the nilpotent annihilates. Both lie in it: $\vec\Upsilon^2=0$ puts $\vec\Upsilon$ there, and $\vec\Upsilon\tilde\Pi_+=0$ puts the rung there, with $\tilde\Pi_+$ the rung of the ladder adapted to $\vec\Upsilon$. The two are independent, and a plane needs no third vector. The image is the same plane, so the operator maps the algebra onto the plane it kills and nothing is kept at any scale. The matrix image is a Jordan block at zero, of index two, with vanishing trace and vanishing determinant.

The contrast with $\Sigma_{01}$ is exact and worth stating: there the operator has the same rank two, but the image is complementary to the kernel, so a live plane survives beside the annihilated one. Here the two planes coincide, and the map is a shift into a dead plane rather than a projection along a live one.

### The Null Field

The most direct reading of the sector is the null electromagnetic field. The field-strength biquaternion of the corpus is

$$
\tilde F=i\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E-\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H,
\qquad
\vec B=\mu\vec H,
\qquad
c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}},
$$

and it is pure, so it lies in the vector subspace $\mathbb{B}_V$ and the sector test applies to it as it stands. With the two field invariants

$$
I_1=\vec E^2-c^2\vec B^2,
\qquad
I_2=\vec E\cdot\vec B,
$$

the square of the field strength is

$$
\tilde F^2=\epsilon\left(I_1+2icI_2\right)e_t .
$$

The computation is one line. Put $\vec V=\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E$ and $\vec W=\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$, so that $\tilde F=i\vec V-\vec W$ and

$$
\tilde F^2=-(\vec V+i\vec W)^2=-\vec V^2+\vec W^2+2i\,\vec V\cdot\vec W .
$$

Dividing by $\epsilon$ reproduces the display, because $\vec V^2/\epsilon=\vec E^2$, because $\vec W^2/\epsilon=\vec B^2/(\mu\epsilon)=c^2\vec B^2$, and because $\vec V\cdot\vec W/\epsilon=c\,\vec E\cdot\vec B$.

Since $\epsilon\neq0$, the field lies in $\Sigma_{00}$ exactly when both invariants vanish. For a real field that is two real conditions on the six real components of $(\vec E,\vec B)$, namely

$$
|\vec E|=c|\vec B|,
\qquad
\vec E\cdot\vec B=0 ,
$$

the classical null field: perpendicular halves of equal magnitude, which is the null bivector of §*The Region* read in the letters of the field.

**Counting.** The field has six real data and the sector imposes two real conditions, leaving four. They are the amplitude, the direction of propagation, which is two angles, and one angle that fixes the pair inside the transverse plane. That is the real dimension of the sector.

**The dictionary is a bijection.** The definition is inverted by

$$
\vec E=\frac{\operatorname{Im}\tilde F}{\sqrt{\epsilon}},
\qquad
\vec H=-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\tilde F}{\sqrt{\mu}},
$$

on every pure element. So the real null fields and the non-zero elements of $\Sigma_{00}$ are the same four real data read in two alphabets. Nothing is lost in the passage from one to the other, and nothing is added.

**A member, checked.** Take $\tilde F=iV(\vec e_x+i\vec e_y)$ with $V>0$ real. Then $\vec E=(V/\sqrt{\epsilon})\,\vec e_x$ and $\vec H=(V/\sqrt{\mu})\,\vec e_y$, so $\vec B=\sqrt{\mu}V\vec e_y$, the two vectors are perpendicular, and $|\vec E|=V/\sqrt{\epsilon}=c\sqrt{\mu}V=c|\vec B|$ because $c\sqrt{\mu}=1/\sqrt{\epsilon}$. The square vanishes because the bracket does: $(\vec e_x+i\vec e_y)^2=0$.

**The sector is blind to the two elementary sectors.** Neither sector contains a nilpotent:

$$
\mathbb{B}_\Phi\cap\Sigma_{00}=\mathbb{B}_\Psi\cap\Sigma_{00}=\{0\}.
$$

An anti-Hermitian element has a real vector part and a purely imaginary scalar part, so $\vec Q^2=|\vec Q|^2$ and the sector forces $\vec Q=0$, hence $Q_t=0$; the Hermitian case is the same with a purely imaginary vector part, where $\vec Q^2=-|\vec Q|^2$. The field strength is neither: its real part $-\sqrt{\mu}\vec H$ is anti-Hermitian and its imaginary part $i\sqrt{\epsilon}\vec E$ is Hermitian. The sector therefore cuts across the material and informational split, and this is the structural reason why its reading is a field and not a momentum.

**Boundary.** Nullness is not radiation: a static null field radiates nothing, as *Null Electromagnetic Fields and the Shear-Free Congruence* shows. The sector is not the field equation: that $\tilde F$ comes from a potential and carries a current is extra. And the sector fixes nullity and not helicity, so it does not separate the two circular polarisations of a null field.

### The Spin

An element has no spin. In *The Carriers, the Representation, the Spin and the Closure* the spin is a property of a **carrier**: a subspace together with one of the two actions that preserve it, with $s$ the dimension of the representation space read as $(2s+1-1)/2$ and, equivalently, the highest weight $\max\operatorname{spec}\rho(\vec u\cdot\vec S)$. An element is a vector in a carrier, and the question acquires an answer only once the action is named. The question is therefore well posed only after the action is named, and then it has three answers.

**The adjoint action.** Under conjugation by $G$ the algebra splits as

$$
\mathbb{B}=Z\oplus V,
\qquad
Z=\mathbb{C}e_t\ \text{of spin }0,
\qquad
V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}\ \text{of spin }1.
$$

Since $Q_t=0$ kills the whole of $Z$, the sector is contained in $V$:

$$
\Sigma_{00}\subset V .
$$

The condition $Q_t=0$ is exactly the removal of the singlet, and every element of the sector lies in the triplet. The sector is a spin-one object, and the spin-one carrier of the adjoint action is what it is an object *of*. It is a vector in that carrier, not a state with a spin label.

**The left module.** Under left multiplication $\mathbb{B}=S_+(\vec u)\oplus S_-(\vec u)$, of weights $+\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac12$, each twice. An element is a pair of spin-half amplitudes, and the pair depends on the axis $\vec u$. A nilpotent is a weight vector for an axis only in the special case

$$
i\vec e_z\tilde Q=\tilde Q
\iff
\tilde Q=\vec a+i\vec e_z\vec a,\quad \vec a\perp\vec e_z,
$$

a two-real-parameter family. A general nilpotent is a sum of both weights, as $e_x+i e_y$ is, and its weight string is not a spin label.

**One orbit.** The group $G$ acts transitively on the non-zero nilpotents: every one of them is a rotation of $\vec e_x+i\vec e_y$. So no rotation-invariant label separates two points of $\Sigma_{00}\setminus\{0\}$, and no element of the sector carries a distinguished spin projection. The invariant data are exactly two: nullity and the complex scale, and the scale is free because the sector is a cone.

**What this fixes and what it does not.** The sector fixes the spin of the carrier, which is $1$ in the adjoint action, and it fixes no value inside the carrier. The same statement in field language is the boundary above: a null field is a spin-one field, and the two helicities are its two circular polarisations, both null, and the sector holds both.

### The Ladder

The physical reading of the nilpotent is the **fermionic mode**, and it needs a pair. A single nilpotent is a square-zero direction; the ladder is a pair.

The canonical pair is

$$
\vec\Upsilon_x=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x-\vec e_y),
\qquad
\vec\Upsilon_y=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y),
\qquad
\{\vec\Upsilon_x,\vec\Upsilon_y\}=e_t.
$$

Both are nilpotent, and their products are orthogonal idempotents:

$$
\tilde\Pi_+=\vec\Upsilon_x\vec\Upsilon_y=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec e_z),
\qquad
\tilde\Pi_-=\vec\Upsilon_y\vec\Upsilon_x=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec e_z),
$$

with

$$
\tilde\Pi_++\tilde\Pi_-=e_t,
\qquad
\tilde\Pi_+\tilde\Pi_-=\tilde\Pi_-\tilde\Pi_+=0.
$$

Both idempotents are Hermitian and lie in the informational sector. The action is the ladder:

$$
\vec\Upsilon_x\tilde\Pi_-=\vec\Upsilon_x,
\qquad
\vec\Upsilon_y\tilde\Pi_+=\vec\Upsilon_y,
\qquad
\vec\Upsilon_x\tilde\Pi_+=\vec\Upsilon_y\tilde\Pi_-=0.
$$

The reading is a two-level system: two rungs, two raising and lowering operators, and a vacuum. The algebra is the full $2\times2$ matrix algebra, so the ladder has exactly two rungs; there is no third orthogonal idempotent.

### What the Nilpotent Is Not

The nilpotent is not a physical null four-momentum. A massless momentum has $N=0$ but $B^2\neq0$, and its square is

$$
\tilde P_{\Phi}^2=2P_t\tilde P_{\Phi}\neq0.
$$

The photon is a zero divisor of the light-ray region $\Sigma_{01}$, not a nilpotent. The distinction is the distinction between the two degenerate regions.

The nilpotent is not a lightlike displacement either, although the corpus's earlier wording calls it one. A real displacement has $Q_t=ict\neq0$ and $B=|\vec r|\neq0$ away from the origin, and lightlike means $N=0$, so a real null displacement lies in $\Sigma_{01}$, beside the momentum. What the nilpotent is is the **complex isotropic model of a null direction**: the element that a direction becomes when its scale is dropped and its components are allowed to be complex.

The nilpotent is not an idempotent. The two classes meet only at the origin. An idempotent is a rung; a nilpotent is a ladder operator between rungs. The product of two nilpotents can be a non-zero element of the light-ray region; the product of an idempotent with itself is itself.

The nilpotent is not a Grassmann label. A Grassmann number $\theta$ with $\theta^2=0$ lives outside the algebra; the nilpotent is an element of the algebra. The algebraic square-zero and the exterior square-zero are different structures, and the ladder is the bridge between them.

The nilpotent carries a polarisation plane and not a helicity. A null field has four real data, and one of them is the angle of the pair inside the transverse plane. The two helicities are the two eigenspaces of the dual, and no real field lies in either, so separating them takes a complex field. The sector is defined by $\tilde F^2=0$, which is blind to the dual, and it therefore fixes nullity and not helicity.

## Products and the Boundary

The region is not closed under products. The product of two elements of the region is null,

$$
\Sigma_{00}\cdot\Sigma_{00}\subseteq\{0\}\cup\Sigma_{01},
$$

and both outcomes occur. Two proportional nilpotents multiply to zero, and proportionality is the only way the product can vanish: the plane orthogonal to an isotropic vector meets the cone in that one line, so $\vec\Upsilon\cdot\vec\Upsilon'=0$ with both isotropic forces $\vec\Upsilon'=\lambda\vec\Upsilon$. Every other pair multiplies to an element of $\Sigma_{01}$, whose scalar part is $-\vec\Upsilon\cdot\vec\Upsilon'\neq0$. The product of the ladder pair is an idempotent in $\Sigma_{01}$, because a non-zero idempotent has $N=0$ and $B^2\neq0$. The product never leaves the null cone.

The region is the boundary of both degenerate regions:

$$
\Sigma_{00}=\overline{\Sigma_{10}}\cap\overline{\Sigma_{01}}
=\overline{\Sigma_{10}}\cap\overline{\Sigma_{01}}\cap\overline{\Sigma_{11}}.
$$

A path in the rest-frame region that sends $Q_t\to0$ lands in $\Sigma_{00}$: at the origin if the nilpotent part also vanishes, and at a non-zero nilpotent otherwise. A path in the light-ray region that sends the scale to zero lands at the origin. A path in the two-mode region that sends both invariants to zero lands somewhere in $\Sigma_{00}$, not necessarily at the origin. The origin is in the closure of every region; the nilpotent cone is the tangent cone at the origin.

## Physical Interpretations

- **The null field is the most direct reading.** $\tilde F=i\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\vec E-\sqrt{\mu}\,\vec H$ is pure, and $\tilde F^2=0$ is exactly $|\vec E|=c|\vec B|$ with $\vec E\perp\vec B$. Six real data, two conditions, four left: the amplitude, the direction of propagation and one polarisation angle. A null field is the physical object that the sector names.
- **The zero-frequency one-way coupling.** The normal form is $\mathsf M_2(\vec\Upsilon)\sim\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$: one direction feeds another and nothing returns. The image is the kernel, so no mode is kept at any frequency and there is no inverse.
- **The flag, and no scale.** The canonical data are $0\subset\mathbb{C}\vec\Upsilon\subset\ker L_{\vec\Upsilon}\subset\mathbb{B}$, and the sector is a cone, so it fixes no scale. The reading is a nested polarisation — a lightlike line, a screen through it, and the space — and not a frame.
- **The sector is blind to the two elementary sectors.** Neither $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ nor $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$ contains a non-zero nilpotent, and the field strength straddles both. The reading is a field and not a momentum, and the reason is structural.
- **Nullity without helicity.** The sector fixes the nullity of the field and not its polarisation; the two helicities are the two eigenspaces of the dual, and the sector does not carry the dual.
- **The sector carries spin one and no spin projection.** Under conjugation the algebra is the singlet $Z$ plus the triplet $V$, and $Q_t=0$ removes the singlet, so $\Sigma_{00}\subset V$. Under left multiplication the algebra is two copies of spin half. The sector fixes neither a weight nor a helicity, and $G$ acts transitively on the non-zero nilpotents, so no rotation-invariant label separates two of them.
- **The origin is the vacuum.** The zero four-momentum, the unique element with no scale and no mode. It is the additive zero, not the identity and not a state with a norm.
- **The nilpotent is a fermionic mode.** A square-zero direction; a pair of them anticommutes to the identity; their products are the two rungs of a two-level system.
- **The ladder is capped at two rungs.** The algebra is $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, the module is two-dimensional, and there is no third orthogonal idempotent. The reading is a single fermionic mode, not a fermionic field.
- **The square-zero is exclusion.** Applying the same nilpotent twice gives zero; the mode cannot be occupied twice. The algebraic statement is the skeleton of the Pauli exclusion principle, not its derivation.
- **The vacuum is not the identity.** $e_t$ is a unit on the scalar line; the vacuum is $0$. Zero is not the multiplicative identity, and the two must not be confused.
- **The origin is the apex of the arrangement; the region is the common boundary.** Every region has the origin in its closure; the nilpotent cone is the tangent cone at the origin. The vacuum is the limit of every regime.
- **The nilpotent is not a photon.** A massless momentum is a zero divisor with $B^2\neq0$ and a non-zero square; a nilpotent has $B^2=0$ and a zero square. The two degenerate regions are different physical regimes.
- **The ladder is the bridge to the state space.** The nilpotents are not Hermitian, but their products are; the rungs lie in the informational sector. The reading connects the vacuum region to the two-state space.

## Limits

The vacuum reading needs the material slice. On the full algebra the origin is the zero element, and calling it a vacuum is an identification with the zero four-momentum.

The fermionic reading is algebraic. The ladder is a pair of nilpotents with the anticommutator $e_t$; the identification with a physical fermionic mode, with statistics and with a field is an extra step, not a theorem of the region.

The nilpotent cone is not on the real material slice. A real four-momentum with $N=0$ and $B=0$ is the origin. The non-zero nilpotents need a complex isotropic spatial vector.

The square-zero is not a derivation of the exclusion principle. It is the algebraic form of a two-rung ladder; the spin-statistics theorem is not proved here.

No dynamics is supplied. The region says what the vacuum and the nilpotents are at an instant; it does not supply a Hamiltonian, a field or a scattering amplitude.

Nullness is not radiation. The sector supplies $\tilde F^2=0$ and nothing more; a static null field satisfies it and radiates nothing. A potential, a current and a source that changes in time are further data.

The field reading is pointwise. The sector classifies a value of the field at a point. Whether the field is null everywhere, and what congruence its principal direction follows, are questions about a field and not about an element.

## Summary

- $\Sigma_{00}=\{N=0,\ B^2=0\}=\{\vec\Upsilon:\vec\Upsilon^2=0\}$ is the nilpotent cone, of real dimension four, with the origin as vertex.
- The origin is the vacuum: the zero four-momentum, the unique element with no scale and no mode; it is the additive zero, not the identity.
- Off the origin the element is a non-zero nilpotent: defective, minimal polynomial $X^2$, dual-number centraliser, no idempotent, no canonical Peirce decomposition; a chosen adapted idempotent gives $J_2(0)$, and there is no inverse and no logarithm.
- A pair of nilpotents with anticommutator $e_t$ is the fermionic ladder; their products are two orthogonal Hermitian idempotents, the two rungs.
- Left multiplication by a nilpotent has rank two, is square-zero, and has image equal to kernel: a complex plane spanned by the nilpotent and the rung it annihilates. The matrix image is a Jordan block at zero.
- The null field is the most direct reading: $\tilde F^2=0$ is exactly $|\vec E|=c|\vec B|$ with $\vec E\perp\vec B$, four real data, and the dictionary between the field pair and the pure complex vector is a bijection.
- Neither $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ nor $\mathbb{B}_\Psi$ contains a non-zero nilpotent, so the sector is blind to the material and informational split; the field strength straddles both.
- The ladder has exactly two rungs; the algebra is $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ and the module is two-dimensional.
- The product of two nilpotents is zero or a scaled idempotent in $\Sigma_{01}$; the region is not product-closed.
- The region is the common boundary of the arrangement: every region has the origin in its closure, and the nilpotent cone is the tangent cone at the origin.
- The nilpotent is not a photon: a massless momentum has $B^2\neq0$ and a non-zero square; a nilpotent has $B^2=0$ and a zero square.
- The region supplies no dynamics, no field equation, no statistics and no derivation of exclusion.
- An element has no spin; spin is a label of a carrier. The sector lies in the spin-one carrier of the adjoint action, $Z\oplus V$ with the singlet removed, splits into two spin-half amplitudes under left multiplication, and is a single orbit under the rotation group, so it fixes no weight and no helicity.
- On the material sector the region is the origin alone, of real dimension zero: $B^{2}$ is definite there, so no non-zero nilpotent is material and no four-momentum is a nilpotent, which makes the ladder a carrier statement and not a momentum value.
- Only the ideal reaches the region, on its nilpotent line; each ideal holds one rung and one nilpotent, and the ladder's pair straddles the two values, which is why the field is their sum.

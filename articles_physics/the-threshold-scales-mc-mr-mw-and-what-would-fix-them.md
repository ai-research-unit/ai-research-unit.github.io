# __The Threshold Scales: $M_C$, $M_R$, $M_W$, and What Would Fix Them__

## Introduction

**The question.** The chain has three scales. Which is measured, which is bounded, which is free — and what kind of statement would turn a bound into a value?

**The answer.**

$$
\boxed{\;
\underbrace{M_W}_{\text{measured}},
\qquad
\underbrace{M_C}_{\text{bounded below}},
\qquad
\underbrace{M_R}_{\text{bounded below, and only via }M_C},
\qquad
M_C=M_R\ \text{in the minimal content}.
\;}
$$

| scale | status | what supplies it |
|---|---|---|
| $M_W$ | measured | the Fermi constant, through $v=(\sqrt2G_F)^{-1/2}$ |
| $M_C$ | bounded below | the null result on $K_L\to\mu e$, with order-one couplings |
| $M_R$ | bounded below, not independently | the same bound, up to $\sqrt2\,g_4/g_R$ |

**The single idea.** A bound and a value are different kinds of statement, and the model produces only the first for its two upper scales. A bound says "not lighter than"; a value needs an equation. The corpus has four candidate equations, and only one of them is a genuine prediction of the content: the unification condition $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at $M_C$. That equation is also the one the minimal content fails, which is why the scales stay parameters.

**What the article establishes.** The status of each scale; the one process that bounds $M_C$ and the reason it is a tree-level flavour-changing decay rather than a precision observable; why $M_R$ inherits the bound and has none of its own; why the minimal content has two thresholds and not three; the four routes from a bound to a value; and the circularity that keeps the model unfalsified but also unpredicted.

**What the article does not establish.** No numerical value for $M_C$ or $M_R$. The number attached to the bound, its inputs and its spread are in [The One Coupling That Returns the Standard Model: One Equation, One Scale, One Content Statement](the-one-coupling-that-returns-the-standard-model.html), and the process itself in [The Leptoquarks of the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Six States, One Bound, and Why Proton Decay Does Not Bite](the-leptoquarks-of-the-four-colours-left-right-model-six-states-one-bound-and-why-proton-decay-does-not-bite.html).

**Where the order comes from.** [The Order of the Breaking in the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Nested Vacua and the Scale Order $M_C\ge M_R\ge M_W$](the-order-of-the-breaking-in-the-four-colours-left-right-model-nested-vacua-and-the-scale-order.html) owns the statement that the chain requires this ordering. This article owns the status of each number.

## Three Scales, Three Statuses

**One measured, two bounded, none predicted.** The distinction is the whole article.

**1.1 What a measured scale is.** A number with an error bar, extracted from data through a relation the theory supplies. $M_W$ is of this kind, and the relation is the Fermi constant.

**1.2 What a bounded scale is.** A one-sided statement: the scale is above (or below) some value, because a process it would mediate has not been seen. $M_C$ and $M_R$ are of this kind.

**1.3 What a predicted scale is.** A number with an error bar, produced by an equation internal to the theory. Neither $M_C$ nor $M_R$ is of this kind in the corpus's minimal content.

**1.4 Why the difference matters for falsification.** A bound can always be satisfied by raising the scale, and raising the scale is always allowed — up to the point where the hierarchy becomes unnatural. So a model whose only statements about its heavy scales are bounds is not easily falsified by them, and the corpus says so explicitly in its discussion of the null result. The falsifiable content is elsewhere: in the count, in the stabilisers, in the charges.

## $M_W$: Measured

**One equation, one number, no freedom.**

**2.1 The relation.** $v=(\sqrt2\,G_F)^{-1/2}$, and $M_W=\tfrac12 g v$, $M_Z=\tfrac12\sqrt{g^{2}+g_Y^{2}}\,v$. The measurement of $G_F$ fixes $v$; the measurement of $M_Z$ fixes the combination of couplings; the two together give $M_W$ a value and an error bar.

**2.2 Why this one is different.** The other two scales come from vacuum expectation values that nothing measures directly. $v$ comes from a rate, and the rate is measured to a precision that makes $v$ one of the best-known numbers in the subject.

**2.3 Where the couplings come in.** A prediction of $M_W$ from the model would need $g$ at $M_W$ from the running, and that is where the model's quantitative problem sits: [The Heavy Sector Below Its Threshold: Decoupling, Matching, and the One Loop](the-heavy-sector-below-its-threshold-decoupling-matching-and-the-one-loop.html).

## $M_C$: Bounded, and Bounded by One Process

**A tree-level flavour-changing decay is the whole bound.**

**3.1 The process.** $K_L\to\mu e$, mediated at tree level by a leptoquark: $s\bar d\to X\to\mu\bar e$. It is flavour-changing, neutral-current, and loop-free, so it is suppressed only by $1/M_X^{2}$ and not by a loop factor as well.

**3.2 The number.** The measured null result converts to $M_X\gtrsim4.0\times10^{-5}$ J when the generation matrix is taken of order one, with a coefficient uncertain by a factor of a few. The corpus states the number and its band; this article does not restate the arithmetic.

**3.3 Why a tree-level process and not a precision observable.** A precision observable is sensitive to $M_C$ through loop corrections suppressed by $1/M_C^{2}$ and by a loop factor. The flavour-changing decay has the same $1/M_C^{2}$ and no loop factor, so it wins by the size of the loop. That is why one rare decay sets the scale and the electroweak precision data do not.

**3.4 What the bound is not.** It is not a measurement of $M_C$, and it is not a prediction. It is a floor, it can be evaded by generation-dependent couplings, and the corpus says the evasion would be a modification of the frame rather than of the chain.

**3.5 The stronger bound that may or may not apply.** If the scalar sector contains a colour triplet coupling to two quarks, a diquark, then proton decay bounds that scalar at $M_\Sigma\gtrsim1.60\times10^{5}$ J — about ten orders above the leptoquark bound. The minimal content has no such scalar, so in the minimal content the weaker bound stands alone.

## $M_R$: Bounded, and Less

**No process of its own, and one inherited bound.**

**4.1 The inherited bound.** $M_X$ and $M_{W_R}$ differ by $\sqrt2$ times a coupling ratio, because both come from the same vacuum value: $M_X^{2}=\tfrac12g_4^{2}v_R^{2}$ and $M_{W_R}^{2}=\tfrac14g_R^{2}v_R^{2}$. So a bound on one is a bound on the other up to $\sqrt2\,g_4/g_R$, and the factor is a coupling ratio the group does not fix.

**4.2 The direct searches.** A $W_R$ would show as a resonance in a dilepton or dijet channel, and its absence bounds it. The bound is weaker than the leptoquark's, because a resonance needs the machine to reach it while a rare decay only needs the coupling.

**4.3 Neutrinoless double beta decay.** If the neutrino is Majorana, the $W_R$–$W_R$ diagram contributes at order $M_{W_R}^{-4}$ in the amplitude, which is the strongest lever available for a heavy right-handed boson. Its size depends on the $W_L$–$W_R$ mixing, so the bound it gives is a bound on a combination and not on $M_R$ alone.

**4.4 The honest summary.** $M_R$ is bounded from below, but no single process pins it. In the minimal content it is not an independent scale at all, because $M_C=M_R$.

## Why the Minimal Content Has Two Scales and Not Three

**One vacuum, one breaking, two factors.** Argued in full in [The Order of the Breaking in the Four Colours Left–Right Model: Nested Vacua and the Scale Order $M_C\ge M_R\ge M_W$](the-order-of-the-breaking-in-the-four-colours-left-right-model-nested-vacua-and-the-scale-order.html); restated here because it changes what "the three scales" means.

**5.1 The rank-one vacuum.** $\Phi$'s vacuum is a line in the carrier times a line in the right doublet, so it breaks $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ together, in one step and at one scale. The stabiliser is $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ of dimension $12$, not the $15$-dimensional group that the two breakings would leave if they happened separately. $M_C=M_R$ because there is one breaking, not because two coincide.

**5.2 The consequence for counting.** Two thresholds, three factors. A reader who counts factors and expects three thresholds will look for a stage that is not there; a reader who counts thresholds and expects the budget $21\to15\to12\to9$ will miss that the middle number is not a scale.

**5.3 The consequence for the bounds.** The bound on $M_C$ is a bound on both scales. Nothing in the minimal content bounds $M_R$ separately, because there is no separate $M_R$.

## What Would Fix a Scale Rather Than Bound It

**Four routes, in order of strength.**

**6.1 A discovery.** A measured leptoquark mass fixes $M_C$; a measured $W_R$ mass fixes $M_R$. This is the strongest route and the least likely in the near term, because the bound already puts both above the reach of the machines that would produce them at a measurable rate.

**6.2 The unification condition.** $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at one scale, combined with the measured low-energy couplings, is an equation for that scale. It is the model's only internal prediction of a scale, and it is the one the minimal content fails: the running does not reproduce the measured couplings for any $M_C$ in the allowed range.

**6.3 The seesaw inversion.** If the neutrino mass is generated by the $M_R$ scale, then $m_\nu\sim y_\nu^{2}v^{2}/M_R$ inverts to $M_R\sim y_\nu^{2}v^{2}/m_\nu$. With the measured neutrino mass scale and an order-one Yukawa this gives a number, and the number is the one the corpus quotes for the seesaw scale. The weakness is the Yukawa: a factor of three in $y_\nu$ is a factor of nine in $M_R$.

**6.4 A second scalar.** A $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ or a second $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ splits $M_C$ from $M_R$ and gives two scales where there was one. It fixes nothing by itself, but it makes the two separately boundable — and it changes the running, which is the point of §*The Tension*.

**6.5 What none of them does.** None of them removes the hierarchy problem. A scale fixed at $10^{-5}$ J by a rare decay and an electroweak scale at $10^{-17}$ J is a ratio of $10^{12}$ that nothing in the chain explains. That is [The Hierarchy Problem and Naturalness](the-hierarchy-problem-and-naturalness.html), and it is the price of the chain rather than a defect of this article.

## The Tension

**The one prediction and the one failure are the same equation.**

**7.1 The equation.** The running from $M_C$ to $M_W$ predicts the low-energy couplings given the content and the scales. With the minimal content and $M_C=M_R$, it misses.

**7.2 Why the miss cannot be repaired by moving $M_C$.** Moving a threshold changes the prediction logarithmically, and the miss is not logarithmic in size: the slopes are wrong, not the endpoints. So no value of $M_C$ repairs the minimal content.

**7.3 What repair would look like.** Additional content above $M_W$ changes the slopes; the content must be chosen so that the slopes fit, and then the fit determines $M_C$. That is the shape of a real prediction: content in, scale out.

**7.4 The present state.** The content that would do it is not in the corpus. So the scales are parameters, the bound is a floor, and the model's quantitative claim is open. Saying so is more useful than a number that does not follow.

## The Checks

**Five checks.**

| check | computation | result |
|---|---|---|
| the measured scale | $v=(\sqrt2G_F)^{-1/2}$ | one number, one error bar |
| the bound on $M_C$ | $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level, $1/M_X^{2}$ | a floor, no loop suppression |
| the relation of the two heavy scales | $M_X^{2}/M_{W_R}^{2}=2g_4^{2}/g_R^{2}$ | one bound, two scales |
| the thresholds | one rank-one vacuum, stabiliser of dimension $12$ | one breaking, $21\to12\to9$: two thresholds |
| the prediction | $g_4=g_L=g_R$ at $M_C$ | fails for the minimal content |

## Distinctions That Are Easy to Lose

**Three pairs.**

**8.1 A bound and a value.** "Not lighter than" and "equal to" differ by an equation. The corpus supplies the first for both heavy scales and the second for neither.

**8.2 $M_C$ and $M_R$ as independent parameters.** In the minimal content they are one parameter, and a bound on one is a bound on both. Only new scalar content makes them independent, and new content changes the running.

**8.3 The scale of the chain and the scale of the hierarchy problem.** The first is a parameter with a floor; the second is the statement that its ratio to $M_W$ is unexplained. Fixing the first does not fix the second.

## Summary

**$M_W$.** Measured, through the Fermi constant.

**$M_C$.** Bounded below by $K_L\to\mu e$ at tree level, $M_X\gtrsim4.0\times10^{-5}$ J for order-one couplings, with a band of a factor of a few.

**$M_R$.** Bounded by the same number up to $\sqrt2\,g_4/g_R$, and by direct searches and double beta decay more weakly; not independent in the minimal content.

**Two thresholds.** One rank-one vacuum is one breaking, so $M_C=M_R$ and the intermediate $15$-dimensional group is a factor count, not a stage.

**Four routes to a value.** A discovery, the unification condition, the seesaw inversion, a second scalar. Only the second is an internal prediction, and it fails.

**What stays open.** The content that would make the running work, and with it the value of $M_C$.

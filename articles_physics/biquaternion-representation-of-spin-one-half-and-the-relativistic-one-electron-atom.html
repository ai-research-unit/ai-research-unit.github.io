Biquaternion Representation of Spin One Half and the Relativistic One-Electron Atom

Introduction

This article records an external source: Alejandro Arias Jiménez, Biquaternion representation of the spin one half and it's application on the relativistic one electron atom, arXiv:2403.02344v1 [quant-ph], 28 February 2024, Department of Physics, Universidad de las Américas Puebla. It is a short student preprint, with no peer review indicated, and its derivations are treated here as claims to be checked, not as results to be quoted. Its programme is stated in its abstract: represent the spin-$\tfrac12$ particle by complex quaternions, using a transformation to $2\times2$ matrices "in order to obtain the Pauli matrices"; with that representation determine the states, the rotation operators and the total angular momentum function "in the complex quaternion space"; and use the representation to solve the relativistic hydrogen atom, arriving at a probability function. The closing outlook proposes the linear quaternion form as a computational saving over matrices and lists extensions to quantum-computing mathematics, particle physics, statistical mechanics, atomic physics and the two-electron atom.

The corpus already carries the algebra-level apparatus this source reaches for. Spin-1/2 Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form fixes the isomorphism $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, the observables, the idempotents as pure states and the trace formula for the Born rule; Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form and The Hydrogen Atom in Biquaternionic Form — The Relativistic Case fix the spin and orbital operators and the Dirac–Coulomb problem; Biquaternion Rotations and Lorentz Transformations fixes the rotors; The Element and Operator Representations and Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Clifford Structure and the Frame, fix the matrix and Dirac correspondences. Two things in the source are not in that material, and they are the reason it is recorded: an explicit exchange of the Pauli matrices for biquaternion units, and a quaternion-space form of the relativistic one-electron wave function, which is an object the article on the Dirac–Coulomb problem names as absent from the corpus. The first of the two is defective in a way that can be stated exactly, and the second is a transcription rather than a derivation.

The findings, stated before the detail.

  • The transformation the source proposes, its equation (8), reproduces the four Pauli matrices on the four quaternion units the source lists, and it does nothing else: it is not multiplicative and not injective, so it is not the matrix representation it is claimed to be. The classical matrix it invokes is a different matrix, and that one is a representation of the real quaternions but not of the complex quaternions.
  • The spin operators and the two states the source builds do satisfy the angular-momentum algebra and the six eigenvalue equations, each verified here. Read in the corpus's algebra, however, the source's two states are not objects of the same kind: the up state is $\sqrt2$ times a Hermitian idempotent of $\mathbb{M}_+$, a pure-state idempotent, while the down state is $\sqrt2$ times a nilpotent element of the same minimal left ideal. The source's coefficient norm is $1$ on both; the algebra norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ vanishes on both.
  • The rotation operators are unit-norm biquaternions and the sandwich action is correct, verified on the three axes. The single printed formula for the rotated components does not say which component is paired with which for each axis; recomputed, the plus sign belongs to the pairings $(j,k)=(z,y)$ about $x$, $(x,z)$ about $y$ and $(y,x)$ about $z$, the reverse pairing carries the opposite sign, and the component along the axis is the invariant one in all three cases.
  • The two states are orthonormal in the scalar part, $\mathrm{Sc}\left(q_p^*q_q\right) = \delta_{pq}$, but not in the algebra product, where $q_\pm^*q_\pm = e_t - i\vec e_x$ is a zero divisor rather than $e_t$. That scalar part is the corpus's Born pairing $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\Pi\tilde H^{*}) = 2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\Pi\tilde H^{*})$, and the source's probability example is that pairing used before it is named.
  • The relativistic one-electron atom is the standard radial Dirac–Coulomb problem re-expressed. The system the source derives is the standard system; the quaternion wave function and the probability density are the standard solution and the sum of the squared moduli of its two spinor components. The spectrum is printed without the square on the ratio; with the ratio squared it is the standard Sommerfeld–Dirac formula and it reproduces the exact ground-state value, which the printed form does not.
  • The section on the Pauli-matrix algebra has one parameter appearing twice and another not at all, so the correspondence printed there cannot be one-to-one.

Throughout, $e_t = 1, \vec e_x, \vec e_y, \vec e_z$ are the quaternion units, $\vec e_\mu^2 = -e_t$ and $\vec e_\mu \vec e_\nu = \epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}\vec e_\lambda$ for $\mu\neq\nu$; $i$ is the central scalar imaginary, with $i^2=-1$, so every product of imaginary factors is simplified before it is displayed: $i\,i=-1$, $i(-i)=+1$ and $(-i)(-i)=-1$, while $i\vec e_\mu$ is a unit distinct from $\vec e_\mu$. $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}$ is the biquaternion algebra; $\mathsf{M}_2$ is the corpus's isomorphism $\mathsf{M}_2(e_t)=I_2$, $\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_k) = -i\sigma_k$ for $k=x,y,z$, and $\mathsf{M}_2(i)=iI_2$, hence $\mathsf{M}_2(i\vec e_k)=\sigma_k$; $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural} = \tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural} = \sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ is the algebra norm; ${}^{\natural}$ is quaternion conjugation, $\bar{\ }$ complex conjugation and ${}^{*}$ Hermitian conjugation, so $q^{*}=\overline{q^{\natural}}$. The source writes its complex quaternions with the same quaternion rules, and its "square norm" $|q|^2 = \mathrm{Sc}(q q^*)$ is not $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$; the difference is used below and is not a notational one.

The Proposed 2×2 Transformation

The matrix and its four values

The source proposes the assignment of a $2\times2$ complex matrix $A(q)$ to a complex quaternion $q = q_te_t + q_x\vec e_x + q_y\vec e_y + q_z\vec e_z$,

$$ A(q) = \begin{pmatrix} q_t + iq_1 & q_y + iq_3 \\ q_y^* + (iq_3)^* & q_t - (iq_1)^* \end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix} q_t + iq_1 & q_y + iq_3 \\ \overline{q_y}-i\overline{q_3} & q_t + i\overline{q_1} \end{pmatrix}, $$

in its equation (8), where $q_1 := q_x$ and $q_3 := q_z$ are the source's coefficient labels. The source's star on a coefficient is the complex conjugation of that scalar; it is written with an overbar here, so $(iq_3)^* = -i\overline{q_3}$ and $(iq_1)^* = -i\overline{q_1}$, since $i^*=-i$. The second row is printed in the source in unsimplified form; the second equality displays it simplified. The source then evaluates $A$ on four elements and finds the identity and the three Pauli matrices:

$q$ $A(q)$ Pauli matrix
$e_t$ $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ $I$
$-i\vec e_x$ $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ $\sigma_z$
$-i\vec e_y$ $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ $\sigma_y$
$-i\vec e_z$ $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ $\sigma_x$

This table is exact: the four evaluations were recomputed on those four elements and reproduce the four matrices to zero error. It fixes the source's own labelling, by which $\sigma_x$ is the image of $-i\vec e_z$, $\sigma_y$ of $-i\vec e_y$ and $\sigma_z$ of $-i\vec e_x$, so the labels $x$ and $z$ are exchanged and $y$ is fixed: the $x$-component belongs to $\vec e_z$, the $z$-component to $\vec e_x$. (The source's own list of the four operators misprints the second entry; the table above is what its evaluations give.)

The source states that this matrix "can be used for several applications", and the rest of its physics is built on the identification the table shows. It is therefore worth asking of $A$ the two properties a matrix representation must have, that it preserve products and that it lose nothing.

The transformation is not multiplicative

Statement. $A$ is not an algebra homomorphism.

Proof by counterexample. $A(\vec e_x) = iI$, $A(\vec e_y) = \sigma_x$ and $A(\vec e_x \vec e_y) = A(\vec e_z)$; computed directly, $A(\vec e_x)A(\vec e_y)$ and $A(\vec e_x \vec e_y)$ differ in the second row, the entry $(2,1)$ being $+i$ in the product and $-i$ in the image of $\vec e_x \vec e_y$. On a hundred random pairs of complex quaternions the largest entrywise discrepancy between $A(qp)$ and $A(q)A(p)$ is of the order of $10^1$, that is, of the size of the entries themselves. The same test on a hundred random pairs of real quaternions gives a discrepancy of the same order: the failure is not an artefact of the complex coefficients. So $A$ fails to preserve products on the complex quaternions and on the real ones alike, and no algebraic statement may be carried through it.

The transformation is not injective

Statement. $A$ has a kernel of real dimension three.

Proof. The vanishing of $A(q)$ is the vanishing of the two entries $q_t + iq_1$ and $q_y + iq_3$ together with that of the diagonal entry $q_t + i\overline{q_1}$. The first two give $q_t = -iq_1$ and $q_y = -iq_3$; the third then reads $i(\overline{q_x}-q_x)=0$, that is, $q_x$ real. The solutions are $q_x = x$ real, $q_t = -ix$, and $q_z$ an arbitrary complex number with $q_y = -iq_3$, which is a real three-dimensional space,

$$ \boxed{\;\ker A = \mathrm{span}_\mathbb{R}\left\{-ie_t + \vec e_x,\ -i\vec e_y + \vec e_z,\ \vec e_y + i\vec e_z\right\}\;}. $$

The last generator spans, over $\mathbb{C}$, the whole complex line through $-\vec e_y - i\vec e_z$, and this is not an accidental coincidence: the source's own spin-down state lies in that line. Its state $q_- = (-\vec e_y - i\vec e_z)/\sqrt2$ is a complex multiple of the third generator, and $A(q_-) = 0$ on a hundred random complex multiples. So $A$ does not merely fail to preserve products; it annihilates one of the two states the source uses, and with it every other element of the down-state line. What the source's table exhibits is an assignment that agrees with a representation on four chosen elements, not a representation.

The Kravchenko–Shapiro matrix, and the corpus's $\mathsf{M}_2$

The source attributes the matrix to Kravchenko and Shapiro. The matrix of that name is the assignment of

$$ \begin{pmatrix} z & w \\ -\bar w & \bar z \end{pmatrix}, \qquad z = q_t + iq_1, \quad w = q_y + iq_3 , $$

to $q = z + w\vec e_y$, where the complex structure is left multiplication by $\vec e_x$, so $i\vec e_y=\vec e_z$ and $i\vec e_z=-\vec e_y$. It is carried by the corpus in The $M_2(\mathbb{C})_{\mathbb{H}}$ Standard Representation — with $z = q_t - iq_z$, $w = -q_y - iq_x$ there, a different choice of the compact splitting — as the matrix form of the real-quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$. Three things are true of it, each checked.

  • It is multiplicative on the real quaternions: a hundred random pairs give a largest discrepancy of $10^{-15}$, machine precision.
  • It is not multiplicative on the complex quaternions: a hundred random pairs give a largest discrepancy of the order of $10^1$. The reason is structural and not accidental: the entries $-\bar w$ and $\bar z$ use complex conjugation, which is antilinear, and an antilinear operation cannot commute with the central $i$ of a complex-linear multiplication. The Kravchenko–Shapiro form is a representation of $\mathbb{H}$ over $\mathbb{C}$, not of $\mathbb{B}$.
  • The source's $A$ is not this matrix. In the $z,w$ notation the source's second row is $+\bar w$ where the classical form has $-\bar w$, and its diagonal entry is $q_t + i\overline{q_1}$ where the classical form has $\bar z = \overline{q_t}-i\overline{q_1}$. The source displays the classical form in the compact notation and then expands it into a different matrix.

The corpus's own isomorphism is the complex-linear one, $\mathsf{M}_2(e_t)=I_2$, $\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_k) = -i\sigma_k$ and $\mathsf{M}_2(i)=iI_2$, hence $\mathsf{M}_2(i\vec e_k)=\sigma_k$, and it is multiplicative on the complex quaternions to machine precision on a hundred random pairs. It is the map that survives the step to $\mathbb{B}$, and it is the map in which the source's own spin operators must be read if the corpus's conventions are kept. In $\mathsf{M}_2$ the two matrices the source substitutes in its hydrogen section are not the images of the units it substitutes: the source writes $\alpha_r = -i\vec e_y$ and $\beta = -i\vec e_x$, consistent with its own table, while $\mathsf{M}_2(-i\vec e_y)=-\sigma_y$ and $\mathsf{M}_2(-i\vec e_x)=-\sigma_x$. The pair of units that reproduces the matrices the source intends, $\sigma_y$ and $\sigma_z$, is $i\vec e_y$ and $i\vec e_z$, since $\mathsf{M}_2(i\vec e_y)=\sigma_y$ and $\mathsf{M}_2(i\vec e_z)=\sigma_z$. A reader who carries $\mathsf{M}_2$ across the source's page and keeps the source's pair of units therefore lands on $-\sigma_y$ and $-\sigma_x$, whereas the source intends $\sigma_y$ and $\sigma_z$: one axis is exchanged and both signs are reversed. The mismatch is a convention mismatch, not an error of the source, which is self-consistent with its own table throughout.

The Spin Operators and the States

The operators

The source's spin operators are its equations (9)–(11),

$$ S_x = \frac{\hbar}{2}(-i\vec e_z), \qquad S_y = \frac{\hbar}{2}(-i\vec e_y), \qquad S_z = \frac{\hbar}{2}(-i\vec e_x). $$

Their commutation relations were recomputed: $[S_x,S_y] = i\hbar S_z$, $[S_y,S_z] = i\hbar S_x$ and $[S_z,S_x] = i\hbar S_y$ hold exactly, with no residual term. So the three elements are a spin-$\tfrac12$ representation of the angular-momentum algebra in the algebra's own product, and that part of the source needs no correction. The corpus's spin operators are $\tilde S_k = \tfrac{\hbar}{2}i\vec e_k$; the source's are the corpus's with the $x$ and $z$ axes exchanged and an overall sign, $S_x = -\tilde S_3$, $S_y = -\tilde S_2$, $S_z = -\tilde S_1$, so the two sets are equal as algebras and different as identifications. Under $\mathsf{M}_2$ the source's $S_x$ has image $-\tfrac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_z$, which is $-\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde S_z)$, not $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde S_x)=\tfrac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_x$; under the source's own $A$ its image is $\tfrac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_x$. Both interpretations are consistent inside their own convention, and they are not the same interpretation.

The states

The source's states are its equations (12) and (13),

$$ q_+ = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(e_t - i\vec e_x\right), \qquad q_- = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(-\vec e_y - i\vec e_z\right). $$

All six eigenvalue equations were recomputed and hold:

$q_+$ $q_-$
$S_x$ $+\tfrac{\hbar}{2}q_-$ $+\tfrac{\hbar}{2}q_+$
$S_y$ $+i\tfrac{\hbar}{2}q_-$ $-i\tfrac{\hbar}{2}q_+$
$S_z$ $+\tfrac{\hbar}{2}q_+$ $-\tfrac{\hbar}{2}q_-$

The entry worth pausing on is the second in the first column. The source displays the product $S_xq_-$ and reads off $q_+$ with the positive sign, and that is what direct multiplication gives; the sign is right and the flipped sign would be wrong. The four orthogonality and normalisation statements that follow in the source were also recomputed: $q_+^*q_- = 0$, and the source's norm is $1$ on both states. Its dual states are $q_\pm^*$: $q_+^* = q_+$, since $q_+$ is Hermitian, while $q_-^* = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\vec e_y - i\vec e_z\right)$, which is neither $q_-$ nor $-q_-$, and which fails as a dual in the algebra product, where $q_+^*q_+ = q_-^*q_- = e_t - i\vec e_x$ is the zero divisor computed below.

The states read in the corpus's algebra

The source's normalisation is $|q|^2 = \mathrm{Sc}(q q^*) = \sum_\mu \left(\mathrm{Re}\,q_\mu\right)^2 + \left(\mathrm{Im}\,q_\mu\right)^2 = \sum_\mu\lvert q_\mu\rvert^2$, the sum of the squared moduli of the four coefficients. Under the multiplicative isomorphism $\mathsf{M}_2$ the image has $\lVert\mathsf{M}_2(q)\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = 2\sum_\mu\lvert q_\mu\rvert^2 = 2\lvert q\rvert^2$, the factor $2$ being the trace of the identity, so the source's norm is half the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of that image. The same identity fails for the source's own $A$ off the four units — it holds on the units and on $q_+$ and fails on the complex quaternions generally, because $A$ annihilates the down-state line — one more sign that $A$ is not the isomorphism. It is not the algebra norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural} = \sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$, which is complex in general. On the source's two states the two forms disagree completely: the source's norm gives $1$ on each, verified, while

$$ \boxed{\;\langle q_+\rangle_{\natural} = 0, \qquad \langle q_-\rangle_{\natural} = 0\;}. $$

Both are zero divisors: after division by $\sqrt2$, $q_+$ gives a rank-one idempotent and $q_-$ a nilpotent, and the source itself expects this where it says that in the complex quaternions "there are certain objects called zero divisors" and that the inverse is lost. The consequence for the interpretation of the states is sharper than the source draws. Dividing each state by $\sqrt2$ gives an element of the algebra, and the two elements so obtained are of different kinds:

element $\lvert q\rvert^2$ $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ the element divided by $\sqrt2$ behaviour under squaring
$q_+$ $1$ $0$ $\tilde\Pi_1 = \tfrac12\left(e_t - i\vec e_x\right)$ idempotent, Hermitian, trace $1$
$q_-$ $1$ $0$ $\tilde\Pi^{\mathrm{nil}} = \tfrac12\left(-\vec e_y - i\vec e_z\right)$ nilpotent, $\left(\tilde\Pi^{\mathrm{nil}}\right)^2 = 0$

$\tilde\Pi_1$ is Hermitian, idempotent and of trace $1$, hence an idempotent of $\mathbb{M}_+$ and, by Spin-1/2 Quantum Physics in Biquaternionic Form, a pure-state idempotent; $\tilde\Pi^{\mathrm{nil}}$ is not idempotent at all but nilpotent, with $\tilde\Pi^{\mathrm{nil}}\tilde\Pi_1 = \tilde\Pi^{\mathrm{nil}}$ and $\tilde\Pi_1\tilde\Pi^{\mathrm{nil}} = 0$, so it is a null element of the same minimal left ideal rather than the complementary idempotent, which is $\tfrac12(e_t + i\vec e_x)$. So the source's spin-up state is a pure-state idempotent up to the factor $\sqrt2$, while its spin-down state is not an idempotent at all. The source's norm cannot see the difference, since it is $1$ on both, and the algebra norm cannot see it either, since it is $0$ on both; the difference is visible only in the square. In the corpus's reading the two pure states along an axis $\vec u$ are the idempotents $\tfrac12(e_t \pm i\vec u)$, so along $\vec e_x$ the pair is $\tilde\Pi_{1,2} = \tfrac12(e_t \pm i\vec e_x)$. The source's up state divided by $\sqrt2$ is the minus member, and its down state divided by $\sqrt2$ is the nilpotent, so the two idempotents of no axis are both present in the source's pair. The minus member is the state of eigenvalue $-\tfrac{\hbar}{2}$ of the corpus's $\tilde S_x$; the source calls it up because its own $S_z$ is $-\tilde S_x$.

The Rotation Operators

The source writes $D^\dagger$ for the conjugate; in the corpus's marks the Hermitian conjugate of an element is $D^{*}$, the dagger being reserved for operators, so the same object is written $D^{*}$ here. The source's rotation operator and its conjugate are

$$ D(n,\varphi) = e_t\cos\frac{\varphi}{2} - i\left(q_p\cdot n\right)\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}, \qquad D^{*}(n,\varphi) = e_t\cos\frac{\varphi}{2} + i\left(q_p\cdot n\right)\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}, $$

with $q_p$ the source's spin vector, whose component along the axis $n$ is $q_p\cdot n=-i(\vec e\cdot n)$ — the unit-spin vector times $-i$, not $\vec e\cdot n$ itself. Since $-i(-i)=i^2=-1$, the general formula reduces to

$$ D(n,\varphi)=e_t\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}-(\vec e\cdot n)\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}, \qquad D^{*}(n,\varphi)=e_t\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}+(\vec e\cdot n)\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}. $$

For the three axes the source's (14)–(16) read

$$ \begin{aligned} D(x,\varphi) &= e_t\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}-\vec e_z\sin\frac{\varphi}{2},\\ D(y,\varphi) &= e_t\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}-\vec e_y\sin\frac{\varphi}{2},\\ D(z,\varphi) &= e_t\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}-\vec e_x\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}. \end{aligned} $$

These are unit-norm biquaternions in the corpus's sense: $D^{*} D=e_t$ was recomputed for each axis and holds exactly, so the objects are rotors and the corpus's rotor theory applies to them unchanged.

For the source's $x$-axis, whose unit is $\vec e_z$, the sandwich action is

$$ \boxed{\; \begin{aligned} D^{*} S_xD &= S_x,\\ D^{*} S_yD &= \cos\varphi\,S_y-\sin\varphi\,S_z,\\ D^{*} S_zD &= \cos\varphi\,S_z+\sin\varphi\,S_y. \end{aligned}\;} $$

The component along the axis is invariant and the two transverse components rotate into one another in the standard sense; the two remaining axes give the two remaining rotations of the same pattern. The source collects all three into the single formula (17),

$$ D^{*}(n_a,\varphi)S_jD(n_a,\varphi)=\frac{\hbar}{2}\left(q_j\cos\varphi+q_k\sin\varphi\right), $$

where $q_j=-i\vec e_j$. The source does not state which pair belongs to which axis, so as printed the formula is under-determined: the plus sign belongs to $(j,k)=(z,y)$ about $x$, $(x,z)$ about $y$ and $(y,x)$ about $z$, the reverse pairing carries the opposite sign, and the component along the axis is not covered by the pattern at all.

The Raising and Lowering Operators

The source prints $S_+ = \tfrac12(S_x + iS_y)$ and $S_- = \tfrac12(S_x - iS_y)$; with its own $S_x,S_y$ from (9)–(10) the factor $\tfrac12$ is spurious, and the values it prints in (18)–(19) are those of $S_x\pm iS_y$ with $\hbar=1$. Indeed, using $i(-i)=+1$,

$$ S_+ = S_x+iS_y = \frac{\hbar}{2}\left(-i\vec e_z+\vec e_y\right)=\frac{\hbar}{2}\left(\vec e_y-i\vec e_z\right), $$

$$ S_- = S_x-iS_y = \frac{\hbar}{2}\left(-i\vec e_z-\vec e_y\right)=\frac{\hbar}{2}\left(-\vec e_y-i\vec e_z\right), $$

so in units $\hbar=1$ the two elements are

$$ S_+=\tfrac12\left(\vec e_y-i\vec e_z\right),\qquad S_-=\tfrac12\left(-\vec e_y-i\vec e_z\right), $$

which were recomputed. The raising and lowering behaviour is correct: $S_+q_- = q_+$, $S_+q_+ = 0$, $S_-q_- = 0$ and $S_-q_+ = q_-$, all verified. Two remarks belong to the notation. The first is that the source's statement that the two operators are obtained from one another by complex quaternionic conjugation is correct: the identity $S_- = S_+^*$ holds, and the negative form $S_- = -S_+^*$ fails. The second is an overload: the symbols $q_+$ and $q_-$ denote the two states in the source's (12)–(13) and the two operators in its (18)–(19), and these are different elements — the states are $\sqrt2$ times an idempotent and a nilpotent, the operators are pure vectors, with no scalar part. The coincidence is not accidental, since the state $q_-$ is $\sqrt2$ times the operator $S_-$, but a reader who carries a symbol across the two uses will be carrying the wrong element.

The Spinor Functions

The source's equation (20) encodes the two-component spinor harmonic. Its two coefficients are defined beside that equation as the radicals $C_{l1}=\sqrt{l\pm m_j+\tfrac12}$ and $C_{l2}=\sqrt{l\mp m_j+\tfrac12}$, and its equation (25) calls the $C_n$ the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients of the case; the prefactor is $1/\sqrt2$ where the standard harmonic carries $1/\sqrt{2l+1}$, so on normalised harmonics the source's angular spinor has squared norm $(2l+1)/2$ rather than $1$. With $C_1$ and $C_2$ left as the source's two numbers, the encoding is the single quaternion

$$ \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(C_1Y_l^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}-\frac12}e_t - iC_1Y_l^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}-\frac12}\vec e_x - C_2Y_l^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}+\frac12}\vec e_y - iC_2Y_l^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}+\frac12}\vec e_z\right), $$

that is, on any values of the two harmonics,

$$ C_1Y_l^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}-\frac12}\,q_+ + C_2Y_l^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}+\frac12}\,q_- , $$

the expansion of the angular spinor in the source's own two states, with the two components of the column as the coefficients. So the encoding is faithful and its convention is the same one that the states of the previous sections are written in. It is also the step that a corpus reader has to notice: the object on the left is a spinor, an element of the defining module $S$ of the algebra, and the object on the right is an element of the algebra. The corpus's articles keep the two apart — the state is a spinor, the idempotent is the state operator — and the source's representation writes the state and the state's operator as elements of one space.

The probability example that closes the section is the measurement of the down state on the $l = 2$ harmonic, $y = C_1Y_2^1q_+ + C_2Y_2^2q_-$ with $C_1 = \sqrt{2/5}$ and $C_2 = \sqrt{1/5}$, and the source's result is $\tfrac15\lvert Y_2^2\rvert^2$. The two states are orthonormal in the scalar part: $\mathrm{Sc}\left(q_p^*q_q\right) = \delta_{pq}$ was verified on the four pairs. The full products are not, however, the identity element: $q_+^*q_+ = q_-^*q_- = e_t - i\vec e_x$, a zero divisor, so the source's dual is a dual only at the level of the scalar part. That scalar part is exactly what the corpus's trace formula $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\Pi\tilde H^{*}) = 2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde\Pi\tilde H^{*})$ pairs with, and the source's probability example is that pairing used before it is named: $\mathrm{Sc}\left(q_-^*y\right) = C_2Y_2^2$ on a hundred random harmonic amplitudes, whence the squared modulus $\tfrac15\lvert Y_2^2\rvert^2$. The source's "orthogonality and normalisation", in other words, are true statements about the scalar part and not about the algebra product, which is the same distinction the corpus's Born rule draws.

The Relativistic One-Electron Atom

The source's route

The source's section 1 solves the problem by the standard matrix route and states the answer; its section 6 re-solves it in the quaternion representation. In the radial equation the two $2\times2$ matrices are replaced by their quaternion images $\alpha_r = -i\vec e_y$ and $\beta = -i\vec e_x$, and the radial function is written as a biquaternion,

$$ R(r) = \frac{1}{r}\left(F(r)\left(e_t - i\vec e_x\right) + G(r)\left(-\vec e_y - i\vec e_z\right)\right), $$

whose two basis elements are, up to the factor $\sqrt2$, the two states of the previous sections: the pair $(F,G)$ is encoded exactly as the two-component spinor of the angular part was, $R = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{r}(Fq_+ + Gq_-)$. So the radial reduction is a two-component reduction, the $2\times2$ matrices being those that act on the pair $(F,G)$, and the encoded object is not the four-component bispinor. Expanding the equation in the two basis elements separates it into the two coupled first-order equations

$$ \begin{aligned} -c\hbar\frac{dG}{dr} - \frac{c\hbar k}{r}G + mc^2F - \frac{z\hbar ca_0}{r}F &= EF,\\ c\hbar\frac{dF}{dr} - \frac{c\hbar k}{r}F - mc^2G - \frac{z\hbar ca_0}{r}G &= EG. \end{aligned} $$

which is the same pair the source's matrix route prints, $k$ the Dirac–Coulomb quantum number and $za_0$ the Coulomb coupling in units of $\hbar c$. The two displays were compared entrywise and are the same system; the system itself is the standard radial Dirac–Coulomb one, its $1/r$ terms, mass terms and coupling being those of the textbook problem in the source's conventions. Its closed-form solution is given by the source as Laguerre polynomials in $\rho = Cr$ with $C = \sqrt{m^2c^4 - E^2}/(\hbar c)$ and $s = \sqrt{k^2 - z^2a_0^2}$, and its wave function is the pair of angular spinors multiplied by $F(\rho)$ and $G(\rho)$, written first as a two-component column and then as the single quaternion of its equations (25) and (26), with the dual in (27). The scalar part of $\Psi^*\Psi$, its (28), is $1/r^2$ times the sum of the squared moduli of the two spinor components with $F^2$ and $G^2$ attached. Its (29) integrates that density over the angular variables, and the density is

$$ \begin{aligned} \rho(\theta,\phi)=\frac{1}{r^2}\Bigl(&C_1^2\left\lvert Y_k^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}-\frac12}\right\rvert^2F(r)^2 +C_2^2\left\lvert Y_k^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}+\frac12}\right\rvert^2F(r)^2\\ &+C_3^2\left\lvert Y_{-k}^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}-\frac12}\right\rvert^2G(r)^2 +C_4^2\left\lvert Y_{-k}^{s_{\mathrm{tot},z}+\frac12}\right\rvert^2G(r)^2\Bigr), \end{aligned} $$

The volume probability is $\int_0^\infty r^2\,dr\int_{S^2}\rho\,d\Omega$, the $r^2$ of the measure cancelling the $1/r^2$, and the angular factor alone is $\int_{S^2}\rho\,d\Omega$. The source prints the limits $-a$ to $a$ against $d\Omega$, and those limits are radial while the measure is angular, so the printed integral is not a probability as it stands. Apart from this measure slip the density is the standard one for the two-component radial solution. (The source calls this probability $\rho$, the same letter it uses for the radial variable $\rho = Cr$ in the closed forms; the collision is the source's, and the two are kept apart here by context.) The structure of (28) and (29) was checked against (25) and (26) and is consistent with them, with one slip of the source's: the $C_3$ term of (28) is printed without the $G^2$ that its counterpart in (29) carries and that the expansion of (25) requires, and the display above restores it. The source writes the squares of the harmonics as $Y^2$ and defines them as $Y^*Y$, which is the $\lvert Y\rvert^2$ used here. The closed forms of $F(\rho)$ and $G(\rho)$ were not independently recomputed: the radicals and the Laguerre arguments do not survive extraction from the source's file in a form that can be transcribed without guesswork, and a half-guessed formula is worse than a recorded one. They are recorded as printed and marked unverified.

The spectrum as printed

The source's energies are printed as

$$ E_{\mathrm{printed}} = mc^2\left(1 + \frac{za_0}{n - |k| + \sqrt{k^2 - z^2a_0^2}}\right)^{-1/2}, $$

in which the square on the ratio is absent. The standard Sommerfeld–Dirac formula squares the whole ratio,

$$ E = mc^2\left(1 + \left(\frac{za_0}{n - |k| + \sqrt{k^2 - z^2a_0^2}}\right)^2\right)^{-1/2} = mc^2\left(1 + \frac{z^2a_0^2}{\left(n - |k| + \sqrt{k^2 - z^2a_0^2}\right)^2}\right)^{-1/2}, $$

and the difference is not cosmetic. At $n = 1$, $|k| = 1$ the exact Dirac–Coulomb ground state is $E = mc^2\sqrt{1 - z^2a_0^2}$, and the two forms compare as follows:

$za_0$ printed with the ratio squared exact $\sqrt{1-z^2a_0^2}$
$0.1$ $0.95324433$ $0.99498744$ $0.99498744$
$0.2$ $0.91130629$ $0.97979590$ $0.97979590$
$0.5$ $0.79622522$ $0.86602540$ $0.86602540$

The restored form agrees with the exact ground state to every digit displayed, at each coupling, and the printed form does not. The missing operation is the square on the ratio; the denominator's square under the root, $k^2 - z^2a_0^2$, is already correct in the source, so the slip is in the ratio and not an extraction artefact. Nothing else in the source's spectrum is at issue.

What this is and what it is not

The source's quaternion wave function is the standard radial Dirac–Coulomb solution, re-encoded: the same system, the same Laguerre functions, the same spherical spinor harmonics, written as one biquaternion instead of a column. The article on the Dirac–Coulomb problem in this corpus records that the exact bound-state solution is the standard radial Dirac–Coulomb one, that what the corpus does not supply is the radial Dirac–Coulomb solution inside the algebra — the derivation, not the transcription — and that the transcription is what this article's source supplies. The source's result is the transcription and not the derivation: a reproduction, in the algebra's notation, of the standard solution. It is a transcription, and the source itself says so, obtaining the quaternion form from the matrix solution of its section 1 and then rewriting it. It is therefore recorded here as the transcription that fills the corpus's notation gap at the level of notation, and not as the algebra-level derivation that article says is absent. The difference is not pedantic: a derivation inside the algebra would produce the radial system and its solution from the algebra's own Dirac equation, with the mass term and the coupling as the corpus's equation carries them; a transcription produces them from the matrix problem and then relabels. What the source demonstrates is that the relabelling can be carried out and that the results agree; what it does not demonstrate is that the algebra produces them.

The second thing the source supplies in this neighbourhood is the pair of substitutions $\alpha_r = -i\vec e_y$, $\beta = -i\vec e_x$ inside the quaternion radial equation, that is, an explicit statement of which biquaternion units carry the Dirac operator in a Coulomb computation. Because the substitution is made through the source's own table and not through a multiplicative map, its result is safe only as long as the source's axes are kept; the corpus's alternative pair, read through $\mathsf{M}_2$, is $\alpha_r \mapsto i\vec e_y$, $\beta\mapsto i\vec e_z$, as noted above. This is a small point of bookkeeping that the source leaves open and a reader must close.

The Pauli-Matrix and Dirac-Matrix Sections

Two short sections close the source's physics. The first represents the algebra spanned by the identity and the three Pauli matrices. Its eight parameters $q_t,\dots,q_7$ are assigned to the eight basis elements, and the assignment is then rewritten in the quaternion space as

$$ q = \left(q_t - iq_7\right)e_t - \left(iq_1 + q_4\right)\vec e_x - \left(iq_2 + q_5\right)\vec e_y - \left(iq_3 + q_7\right)\vec e_z , $$

its equation (30). As printed, the parameter $q_7$ appears in two coefficients and the parameter $q_6$ in none, so the displayed correspondence cannot be a bijection between the eight parameters and the four complex coefficients. Recorded as printed and not repaired here. The same section represents "the Hodge operator" by the quaternion $-ie_t$, its equation (31), a statement whose symbol $\varepsilon$ is not defined anywhere in the source and whose content cannot be recovered from the page; it is recorded as printed and nothing is built on it.

The second short section writes four $4\times4$ matrices in biquaternion form and attributes the representation to Silvis (2010); that attribution is the only place in this corpus where Silvis is named. Two things are visible on the page without any reconstruction. The first matrix, $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)$, is sent to $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}e_t&0\\0&e_t\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, which is the identity: both diagonal blocks are printed with $e_t$, where the second should carry the minus of the $4\times4$ matrix, so that as printed the image of $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)$ is not $\mathrm{diag}(e_t,-e_t)$. The three off-diagonal blocks are printed as $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-i\vec e_x\\i\vec e_x&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and the same with $\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_y$. The corpus's side of the same correspondence is in Matrix Representations Conventions, §The Clifford Structure and the Frame, in the mostly-minus convention, the timelike generator $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&e_t\\e_t&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and the three spacelike generators $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&i\vec e_k\\-i\vec e_k&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The two sets differ in two ways and agree in the algebra: the source's first matrix is the diagonal Dirac form where the corpus's timelike generator is off-diagonal, and the source's three spacelike blocks are the negatives of the corpus's, a sign that flips all three odd generators at once and therefore leaves every product, and every Clifford relation, unchanged. Which units carry which axes is again the source's own table.

The Legacy Gamma Formulation

The gammas are a frame of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, not a different algebra.

Here $L_a$ and $R_b$ are the left and right multiplications, $L_a\psi=a\psi$ and $R_b\psi=\psi b$, and $c(M)$ is the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products.

$$ \gamma_0=iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}},\quad \gamma_1=iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\quad \gamma_2=iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\quad \gamma_3=iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\quad \gamma_5=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}} . $$

The same in the axis-free display:

$$ \gamma_0=iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}},\quad \gamma_1=iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\quad \gamma_2=iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\quad \gamma_3=iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\quad \gamma_5=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}} . $$

A single product is a pure tensor; the products form the seven-dimensional cone $\operatorname{rank}c(M)\le1$, and a general element is a sum of at most four, with $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$.

The two Dirac equations. The corpus carries two forms of one dynamics, and the difference between them is where the second chirality slot sits. In units $\hbar=c=1$, with the biquaternionic gradient $\tilde\nabla=e_t\partial/\partial Q_t+\sum_k\vec e_k\partial/\partial Q_k$ and its conjugate $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, the one-sided form is the chiral pair

$$ \tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi_R=m\tilde\Psi_L,\qquad \tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Psi_L=m\tilde\Psi_R, $$

two fields, both equations acting from the left, the chirality carried by the two fields and exchanged by $\natural$. The two-sided form writes a single field and puts the second slot on the right of the algebra element,

$$ \left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial t'}+D_3\right)F-mi\,\overline{F}\,\vec e_z=0, \qquad D_3=\sum_k\vec e_k\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_k}, $$

with the derivative acting from the left, the bar the antilinear entry, and $\vec e_z$ multiplying from the right after the conjugation. The two are one operator read from two sides: since $i\partial/\partial t'=-\partial/\partial Q_t$ in these units, the free part is $-\tilde\nabla^{\natural}$, and the equation reads

$$ \tilde\nabla^{\natural}F=m\left(-i\overline{F}\vec e_z\right), $$

which is the second member of the pair with its left field read as $-i\overline{F}\vec e_z$: a conjugation and a fixed direction, nothing else. That is what the right multiplier carries — the second chirality slot, which the one-sided form holds in a second field, the two-sided form holds in the side of the action. The dictionary is recorded in The Dirac Equation in Biquaternionic Form. The chirality cell is $\gamma_5=-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}$, the reference-frame cell of the chirality $\Gamma$; the central grading $\chi=i\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{ad}}$ — the difference of the two central idempotents of the complexified algebra, so $\chi^2=e_t$ — is a different object, represented by $\gamma_5$ on the Dirac module.

The dictionary is complex-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

The display. Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. The two are carried into one another by $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}=C_{\Pi}L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and a conjugation by $C_{\Pi}$ changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant, so every identity of the section holds in both. An array is a statement about one display; a cell named in the running text keeps the Peirce label, and its axis-free array is the conjugate. The label names the array and not the operator.

Summary

The source proposes a $2\times2$ complex matrix $A(q)$ for a complex quaternion and a table of four evaluations of it; it builds the spin operators, the two spin states, the rotation operators, the ladder operators and the angular spinor functions on that table; and it uses the same substitution to write the relativistic one-electron wave function and its probability density in quaternion space. The table is exact on the four elements. The matrix is not multiplicative on those elements' products, in either the real or the complex quaternions, and it annihilates a three-real-dimensional subspace that contains the source's own spin-down state; it is not the Kravchenko–Shapiro matrix it is attributed to, and that matrix in turn represents the real quaternions but not the complex ones. The corpus's isomorphism $\mathsf{M}_2$ is the complex-linear map that does both. On the algebraic side the source's results are correct and the corpus's conventions read them as: a spin operator set equal to the corpus's with the $x$ and $z$ axes exchanged and a sign, an up state equal to $\sqrt2$ times a pure-state idempotent of $\mathbb{M}_+$ and a down state equal to $\sqrt2$ times a nilpotent of the same ideal, both of vanishing algebra norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and unit coefficient norm, and rotations that are unit-norm biquaternions acting correctly. On the physical side the source's quaternion hydrogen atom is the standard radial Dirac–Coulomb problem re-encoded, its system standard and its probability density standard, with one missing square on the ratio in the printed spectrum; the spectrum with the ratio squared is the Sommerfeld–Dirac formula. The two Dirac equations are distinguished in The Legacy Gamma Formulation. The result is the transcription of the corpus's notation gap and not the closure of its mathematical gap, and it is recorded as such.

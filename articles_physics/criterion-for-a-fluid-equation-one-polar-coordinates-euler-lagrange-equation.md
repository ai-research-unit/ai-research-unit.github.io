# __Criterion for a Fluid Equation: One Polar Coordinate's Euler–Lagrange Equation, Not a Combination__

## Introduction

The corpus claims a fluid reading at every rung of its ladder, and one at the primordial rung for the fifteen connections. The Schrödinger, Klein–Gordon, Dirac and Proca fields are each said to *be* a fluid, and the claim is not that they resemble one. A reader is entitled to the test, and this article states it.

**The thesis.** An equation is a fluid equation when it is **one coordinate's Euler–Lagrange equation in the polar chart of the field**, and not a combination constructed on the field's own equation. Applied uniformly, that criterion is what makes the eight rungs one series and the primordial rung its largest member, rather than nine analogies with a family resemblance.

The word "fluid" is cheap. Any complex field is a modulus times a phase, and calling the modulus a density costs nothing. [The Euler and Continuity Equations in Biquaternionic Form](the-euler-and-continuity-equations-in-biquaternionic-form.html) states the standard: *a translation that changes nothing has explained nothing.* This article is written to that standard: it adds no rung and derives nothing new about any field, and states the test the other articles have been passing.

**Three tests, and they are not the same test — though they are not independent either.** The **count** asks whether the field's data are fluid-shaped: the framework article's $1+1+3+3=8$. The **derivation** asks whether those data are coordinates whose variations are the fluid's equations: this article's subject. The **closure** asks whether the equations determine the coordinates, which is a statement about rank and not about number.

They overlap in one direction and not the other. A count that closes is necessary for closure and does not imply it — the Dirac rung passes the count and fails the rank. A derivation that succeeds presupposes the count, because a coordinate has to exist before it can be varied. **So the tests are nested rather than orthogonal, and the ladder's shape is the record of which one fails where** — which is a sharper statement than saying each degrades, and it is the reason §*Closure* separates count closure from rank closure.

**The finding that surprised this article.** Only one of the fluid's six slots loses warrant on the way up the ladder. Continuity degrades monotonically — strict conservation of the proper density, then of the charge, then an identity, then an identity that fails — and every other slot strengthens, widens or reverses. **The ladder is not a dilution of the fluid reading, and the corpus's instinct that its top rung is the strongest is not sentiment.**

**Position in the block.** A methodological article of the generalities group, beside [The Relativistic Quantum Space as a Fluid](the-relativistic-quantum-space-as-a-fluid.html), which states the *object*. This one states the *derivation*, where a fluid lives, and what the resulting equations are not.

## Generalities

### The Question, and the Answer That Is Not an Answer

**The question.** At each rung the corpus varies a Lagrangian and displays equations with fluid names: a continuity equation, an Euler equation, an equation of state, a vorticity. What makes those names correct rather than decorative?

**The answer that is not an answer.** A polar substitution. Write the field as $\tilde\Xi=\varrho\,e^{i\theta}$ and one has a "density" $\varrho^{2}$ and a "velocity" $\nabla\theta/m$. Every complex field admits it, including ones nobody calls a fluid, and it survives no test: the substitution is available whether or not the equation it produces is a transport law. This is the reading the block's opening article calls a model laid over the theory, and it is rejected there for the reason it deserves — it uses every datum and constrains none.

**The answer that is an answer, and it is a statement about derivations.** The corpus's criterion is stated once, in the Schrödinger article, and it is a criterion about where an equation comes from:

> *"It is one coordinate's equation and not a combination. The equation route obtains continuity by multiplying the wave equation by $\tilde\Xi^{*}$ and subtracting its conjugate, which is a construction on the equation. Here nothing is constructed: $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ is a coordinate and its Euler–Lagrange equation is the conservation of its momentum."*

The phase is a coordinate of the word, varying it is an act of the action principle, and the result is the statement that its conjugate momentum is conserved. Nothing was multiplied by the field's conjugate and the equation was not manufactured from the field equation. **The fluid's equations are the Euler–Lagrange equations of the chart's coordinates**, and that is checkable line by line.

### Why the Distinction Is Not Pedantry

**First, a coordinate has a momentum, and that is what makes an equation a transport law.** Varying a coordinate gives the divergence of its conjugate momentum — the fluid's current — and its vanishing is continuity. A combination gives a divergence too, and below the spinor rung *the same* one, but only the first tells you which current is the momentum of which variable, and so which charge a shift symmetry would conserve.

**Second, a coordinate's equation is one equation of a determined system.** The word consumes the field's real data and each coordinate's equation removes one combination of them; two coordinates, two equations, and the count closes. A combination is counted against no coordinate, so a set of combinations may under- or over-determine the same data and neither fault shows in the equations.

**Third, the status differs and the difference is invisible in the formulas.** An identity that follows from the field equation is a *consequence* of the dynamics; the same identity read as a coordinate's Euler–Lagrange equation is *prior* to it. This is the distinction [The Charged Schrödinger Fluid from the Polar Action](charged-schrodinger-fluid-equations-from-the-polar-action.html) draws: under the equation route the vorticity's vanishing is proved by eliminating a time derivative with the wave equation; under the action route it is a curl of a gradient plus a curl of a connection, true before any equation is imposed.

**What the criterion is not.** It is not a derivation of the fluid equations from first principles, and it does not add an equation to any rung. Both roads reach the same equations, so the criterion changes no content; what it changes is **which of the equations is prior**. Its use is therefore heuristic and structural — it tells you where to look, which equation is a variation and which is assembled, and which charge a symmetry would give you — and a reader who mistakes it for a founding principle will over-read it. The next paragraph states what it does buy.

**The criterion is a filter, and it detects a failure mode.** Some equations got by manipulating a field equation are nobody's Euler–Lagrange equation. Multiplying by $\tilde\Xi^{*}$ and taking the real part is the standard case: at the scalar rung the imaginary part is continuity and the real part is a combination assembled from two coordinate equations rather than read from one. **That object is not a fluid equation; it is a consequence of two of them.**

### The Six Slots a Fluid Equation Has to Fill

An ordinary fluid carries five real data at each point — a density, three velocity components and a thermodynamic state — closed by three equations, the third a law of state rather than a transport law. [The Euler and Continuity Equations in Biquaternionic Form](the-euler-and-continuity-equations-in-biquaternionic-form.html) counts them. Read as a checklist for a *field*, the content is six slots — the five data and the equation that transports the first:

| slot | what it must supply | what an ordinary fluid's version is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| density | a positive scalar with its own transport equation | $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}$, the marker count |
| continuity | the transport statement itself, as a divergence | $\partial_{t'}\rho_{\mathrm{mass}}+\vec\nabla_\Phi\!\cdot\!(\rho_{\mathrm{mass}}\vec v)=0$ |
| velocity | a flow, read as a current $\rho V$ | $\vec v$, three components |
| vorticity | the antisymmetric part of the flow's derivative, with a stated status | $\vec\nabla\times\vec v$ |
| acceleration | a law relating the flow's own derivative to a force | Euler, $\varrho\,\mathrm{D}\vec v/\mathrm{D}t=-\vec\nabla p$ |
| state | a pressure as a function of the density alone, or a stated failure | $p=p(\rho_{\mathrm{mass}},s_{\mathrm{spc}})$ |

**The six slots are the checklist, and the checklist is not the criterion.** A field can fill all six with objects and still fail, if those objects are not the chart's coordinates and their equations are not variations. The ladder's rungs fill the slots in this order and with these credentials, and the credentials are the subject of §*Which Slots Are Coordinate Equations*.

### How the Equations Are Built: The Bracket's Types

**The recipe has five steps, and every rung of the ladder walks them in the same order.**

*First, the Lagrangian.* Each rung has one: a pairing of the field's derivatives with itself, plus an algebraic term.

*Second, the polar word.* The field is factorised, and the factorisation is a bijection on the invertibles — $\tilde\Xi=\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ for the spinor, its collapses for the scalar and the vector, and $\Xi=UPU^{\dagger}$ with $P=\mathrm{diag}(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{4})$ at the primordial rung. The word's parameters are real and they are the fluid's variables.

*Third, the action in the word.* The kinetic term is built from the logarithmic derivative,

$$
\mathcal A_{\imath}=\tilde\Xi^{-1}\partial_{\imath}\tilde\Xi ,
$$

and this object is where the mechanism lives. It is one element per direction, and its parts are of **distinct algebraic types**: a real central part, which is the density's logarithm; a central imaginary part, which is the phase's gradient; a Hermitian traceless part, which is the boost's connection; and a real traceless part, which is the rotor's. Four types, and the scalar pairing of the action does not mix them.

*Fourth, the action separates by type, and exactly.* Because the parts are of different types and the pairing is a scalar product within each, the kinetic term is a sum of squares with no cross terms. [The Lagrangian and the Spin-Zero Fluid](the-lagrangian-and-the-spin-zero-fluid.html) states it for the collapse: *"the two squares are separate because the bracket's real and imaginary parts are of different types and do not mix under the scalar pairing."* The algebraic term is one function of the density alone, because the invariants are.

**The other road, and why it reaches the same equations.** Nothing in the recipe needs the action. The same equations can be reached from the local field equation alone: multiply by $\tilde\Xi^{-1}$ or by $\tilde\Xi^{*}$, take the parts, use the algebraic identities of the bracket. That is the equation route, and on the chart it lands on the same equations as the action route, because both are one statement written in the same coordinates. **What the equation route cannot tell you is which of its outputs to keep.** One manipulation produces continuity, the energy equation and the vorticity identity together, and only the criterion says which are coordinates' equations and which are combinations. The two roads are therefore equivalent in content and not in warrant — and the derivation is used here because it is the one that answers the question this article asks.

*Fifth, one variation per coordinate, and the types decide the slots.* Varying a coordinate gives its Euler–Lagrange equation, and the equation inherits the type of the term the coordinate appears in. A real central coordinate's variation gives a real scalar equation — the density's transport. A central imaginary coordinate's variation gives a divergence — continuity. A frame coordinate's variation gives the frame's transport. **The type of the term decides which slot the equation can fill**, which is why the ladder's rungs have the same slots in the same order with different contents, and why no rung can produce, say, a spin equation out of a scalar action: the type is not there to be varied.

The same step, one group up. At the primordial rung the word is $\Xi=U_{\mathrm{frm}}PU_{\mathrm{frm}}^{\dagger}$ and the derivative that replaces $\mathcal A_{\imath}$ is the covariant frame derivative,

$$
\Omega_{\imath}=U^{\dagger}D_{\imath}U ,
$$

whose parts are the eigenvalue splittings, which are central real and four in number, and the off-diagonal connection, which is the frame's velocity. The action's kinetic term separates into the eigenvalues' gradient energy and the frame's, the cross terms vanishing because the diagonal and the off-diagonal parts are orthogonal under the trace. **The mechanism is the same mechanism, and it is the reason the primordial rung's fluid has four densities and one frame rather than a different set of objects.**

### Which Slots Are Coordinate Equations

**This is the honest table, and it is the article's central piece of bookkeeping.**

| slot | supplied by | is it a coordinate's Euler–Lagrange equation? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| density | the kinetic term's scale part and the potential | **yes** — the amplitude $\varrho$, or the eigenvalues $\rho_{k}$ |
| continuity | the kinetic term's phase part | **yes** where the word has a phase — $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ is cyclic; **no** at the vector and matrix rungs, where no phase coordinate exists |
| velocity | read from the phase's or the boost's momentum | **no** — the velocity is a ratio of momenta, and nothing varies it |
| vorticity | an identity of the chart, or the connection's own equation | **no** — it is a consequence, or a dynamical field in its own right |
| acceleration | the frame's equations, projected along the flow | **no** — it is a projection, and at no rung is there a coordinate whose equation is Euler |
| state | the algebraic term, varied in the density | **no** — the relation is algebraic, and the pressure is read, not transported |

**At the gradient rungs two of the six are coordinate equations and four are read; the count is not the same at the top.** At the primordial rung a third slot acquires a genuine Euler–Lagrange equation — the vorticity's — but its coordinate is not a variable of the word: $\mathbb{F}$'s equation is the variation of the connection $A_{\imath}$, which is a field in the action and not a factor of $UPU^{\dagger}$. **So the count of coordinate equations rises to three while the count of word-coordinates stays at two**, and the distinction is worth keeping: a fluid whose frame is a function of its densities has two coordinates, and a theory whose frame is a dynamical field has three.

**The asymmetry is not a defect; it is what the ladder is.** A fluid description is a chart in which the density and the phase are coordinates and the rest are functions of them. It is also where a rung can fail: a slot filled by a projection is filled only where the projection is defined, and that is a fact about the frame and not about the action.

The Proca rung states this outright, and its statement is the template for the primordial one:

> *"What the Euler equation is not. It is not an independent variation. There is no coordinate whose equation is the Euler equation, and a reader who looks for one will find the frame's three and have to project."*

### Closure, the Third Test

**Closure is not a count, and stating it as one is the mistake this section exists to avoid.** The Dirac rung has eight real equations for eight real coordinates and would pass a count, yet it is the one rung the corpus calls under-determined. What closure asks is whether the equations **determine the rates**, and that is a question about the rank of the coefficient matrix of the highest time derivatives — the Hessian for a second-order action, the kinetic matrix for a first-order one.

$$
\text{closure}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\text{the highest-derivative form is nondegenerate on the physical subspace.}
$$

**A degenerate form produces constraints, and a constraint is not an equation for a rate.** Where the form has a null direction the corresponding rate does not appear and cannot be solved for; what remains is an algebraic relation among the coordinates, and the count of those is the corank. So the question at each rung is not *how many equations* but *how many rates are determined* — which the corpus answers in the language of vanishing momenta.

| rung | coordinates | highest-derivative form | what it determines |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Schrödinger | $2$: $\varrho$, $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ | first order in time, invertible on both | both rates — the rung closes |
| Klein–Gordon | $2$: $\varrho$, $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ | second order, invertible on both | both accelerations — the rung closes |
| Dirac | $8$ | **four vanishing momenta** on $\varrho$, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ and the boost axis | four rates, and four algebraic relations instead |
| Proca | $4$ | **rank three** of four | three rates, and one primary constraint |
| $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ | $60$ connection components, plus the word's $16$ | **fifteen first-class Gauss laws**, each removing two | $30$ physical, and an elliptic constraint |

**The Schrödinger rung is where a Hessian-only reading goes wrong.** Its action is first order, so its Hessian vanishes identically and says nothing at all about closure; the matrix that carries the test there is the kinetic one, invertible on both coordinates. **That is why the definition above names both objects**, and it is the one place in the table where the object being inverted changes rather than its rank.

**The Dirac rung fails, and the failure is exactly the four vanishing momenta.** $p_\varrho=0$, $p_{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}=0$, and the two conjugate to the boost axis vanish identically on every configuration, so the eight equations are four evolutions and four constraints. Each constraint ties the phase's rate to the rotor's rate and the frame's parameters — **one algebraic type's rate to another's**, which is the type-block statement with a mechanism behind it.

**The Proca rung closes on three, and the number is the helicities.** Rank three of four leaves one primary constraint, the Lorenz condition is its secondary partner, and the three that remain are the polarizations of a massive spin-one field. **Four coordinates, three rates.**

**The primordial rung closes on thirty, and thirty is not fifteen.** Fifteen connections of four components is sixty real data; each Gauss law is first class, so each removes two, and $60-2\times15=30$. **The fifteen is the number of constraints, not of unknowns**, and a display that says the rung "closes on fifteen" is conflating the two. The other statement about the rung is separate: its constraint is *elliptic*, fixing the scalar potential from the charge at once, where the eight rungs' were divergences or algebraic relations. **That is the rung's character, not its closure**, and it is why the fluid is charge-constrained rather than incompressible.

**Two tests were hiding under one word.** *Count closure* asks whether the number of equations equals the number of coordinates — necessary, cheap, and what the Dirac rung passes while failing. *Rank closure* asks whether the rates are determined — the real test, and each verdict is in the table. **A third question, the constraint's type, is not closure at all**: it decides what kind of fluid the rung is, not whether it is one.

### The Base and the Fibre: Where a Fluid Lives

**Every rung so far has been described without saying where its fluid lives, and at eight rungs the omission costs nothing.** A field is a section of a bundle: a **base**, the manifold of events; a **fibre** at each point, the space the field's value lies in; and a **connection**, the rule for comparing fibres at neighbouring points. The fluid's variables are read off one fibre, and the question this section settles is whether they are also vectors of the base.

**The base is the material sector at every rung, and that is what the $\Phi$ means.** The event is $\tilde R_\Phi=ir'_te_t+\vec r_\Phi\in\mathbb{B}_\Phi$, and $\tilde\nabla_\Phi$ is that sector's gradient. So the subscript on the gradient names the **base**, and it names the same base at every rung. What changes up the ladder is the fibre.

| rung | fibre | the fluid's velocity | in the base, or on it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Schrödinger | $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$: one complex scalar | $\vec\nabla_\Phi S/m$ | **in** |
| Klein–Gordon | $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ | $\partial_{\imath}S/m$ | **in** |
| Proca | $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$: four real | $A_{\imath}/\varrho$ | **in** |
| Dirac | the bispinor ideal, eight real | $ic\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm b}^{-2}$ | **in** — the *variables* are, though the fibre is not $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ |
| $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ | $\mathrm{Herm}(4)$: sixteen real | $\Omega_{\imath}=U_{\mathrm{frm}}^{\dagger}D_{\imath}U_{\mathrm{frm}}\in\mathfrak{u}(4)$ | **on** |

**The test is one line: is the fluid's velocity a section of the base's own tangent bundle — an element of $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ at each point?** At the four lower rungs it is: Proca's velocity is the field's own direction, Dirac's is a $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$ element with a $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$-valued current even though the field is a bispinor, and the scalars' are the gradient's vector part. **At the primordial rung it is not.** $\Omega_{\imath}$ is $\mathfrak{u}(4)$-valued, and no map carries it into $\mathbb{B}_\Phi$: the fibre is $\mathrm{Herm}(4)$, sixteen real, twice the algebra's eight and not a sector of it.

**So the top rung is a fluid *on* the material sector and not *in* it, and that is not a demotion.** Its densities are scalars on the base with a base equation, $\Box_\Phi\rho_{k}$ sourced by the frame; its vorticity is a curvature of the internal frame; what is not a base vector is the **flow**, and the flow is what distinguishes a fluid from four scalar fields. **The eight lower rungs are fluids of the base's own vector type; the top rung is a stratification of the base whose internal rotation is the flow.**

**The category error this repairs.** *"$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ acts on the field, that is to say on the material space"* conflates the fibre with the base. A gauge transformation is a fibre automorphism chosen independently at each point; its parameter depends on the point, which is why the gradient must be covariantised, and it does not move the point. **The base never moves.** The error is unusually easy in this corpus because the base *is* an algebra, so "acts on the algebra" sounds like "acts on the events" — and it is not that either: even on the algebra the action is on the fibre over each point, not on the points.

**Two collapses of the phase, one mechanism, and this is where continuity's degradation comes from.** The phase is a cyclic coordinate at the gradient rungs, and continuity is its variation. It stops being one twice, for one reason each time:

| rung | what happens to the phase | why there is no equation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proca | the charged word's $e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}$ is collapsed to the real slice, $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}\in\{0,\pi\}$ | a two-valued parameter admits no variation — there is no path through it to differentiate along |
| $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ | the word's stabiliser is $\mathrm{U}(1)^{4}$: $U_{\mathrm{frm}}\mapsto U_{\mathrm{frm}}\,\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_{k}})$ leaves $\Xi$ unchanged | a quotiented parameter is not a coordinate, so there is nothing to vary |

**Same mechanism, two symptoms**, and it explains what the ladder table shows without explaining: why the vector and matrix rungs land in the same cell for continuity despite words that share no factor. Neither has a continuous phase coordinate, so in both the conservation statement survives as the divergence of the field equation rather than as an act of the action.

**One letter, three objects, and this is the corpus's worst collision.** The frame and the gauge transformation both want to be called $U$:

| object | symbol | parameters | in $D_{\imath}$? | is it a symmetry? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the polar frame of the word | $U_{\mathrm{frm}}$ | twelve physical, sixteen before the stabiliser | **no** — it is a decomposition, not a transformation | no |
| the gauge transformation | $U_{\mathrm{gau}}(x)$ | fifteen generators, local | **yes**, through $A_{\imath}$ | yes |
| the global $R$-symmetry of the supersymmetric cousin | $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{R}$ | four | **no** — it is rigid | yes, but not this group's role |

**The razor is the covariant derivative.** A local parameter does not pass through $\partial_{\imath}$, so it forces a connection; a constant one passes through, so it forces nothing. $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{R}$ is rigid and never appears in $D_{\imath}$; $U_{\mathrm{frm}}$ appears only inside $\Omega$; $U_{\mathrm{gau}}$ is local and is what $D_{\imath}$ is built to absorb. **Same group, three roles, and only one forces a connection** — which is why the subscript is not decoration.

## The Criterion at the Classical Layer

**The criterion did not begin at a quantum rung, and it is worth reading where its clauses have ordinary names.** The classical equations are the base — *The fluid's equations and their solutions* in *Biquaternion Universe Non Relativistic Non Quantum Approximation* — and every clause of the four-item form is a statement about them, with a classical violation that has been known for two centuries.

| clause | at the classical layer | the classical failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| one coordinate's equation, not a combination | two coordinates, density and velocity, two equations | the vorticity equation: the curl of Euler, a consequence and never a third equation of motion |
| the velocity is a gradient | the rank of the vorticity is $0$ | a rotational flow: three Clebsch functions in place of one potential |
| every force is a gradient | a barotropic fluid with a potential body force | the baroclinic torque, $\vec\nabla\rho\times\vec\nabla p\neq0$ |
| the potential's effect is a function of the density | a constant-pressure wave equation is linear | a Korteweg stress, which makes it nonlinear |

**The second row is the one that generalises, and the general form is a statement about rank.** "The velocity is a gradient" is the rank-zero member of a family. A two-form in four dimensions has rank $0$, $2$ or $4$; rank zero needs one potential, rank two needs a pair and hence three functions, and rank four needs two pairs. A classical fluid with no independent orientation is the rank-two case and its Clebsch representation is exactly three functions; a medium with an independent triad — a Cosserat medium — can reach rank four, and the classical fluid is the *constrained* Cosserat case in which the triad is tied to the velocity gradient. So the clause is not "irrotational" but "the vorticity has a rank the potentials can carry", and the potential flow is the lowest member rather than the general one.

**The third row is the equation of state's clause, and it is where a classical fluid most often leaves the criterion.** A barotropic fluid makes the pressure a function of the density and the force a gradient; a baroclinic one does not, and the torque it leaves over is what generates vorticity. This is the same statement as the reading's: a fluid read from a field has one thermodynamic datum and therefore no baroclinicity to leave over, and a fluid that carries a second one is outside the image. The two statements are one statement read at two rungs.

**The fourth row separates two things that the word "fluid" runs together.** Whether the pressure is a function of the density decides whether the wave equation is linear; it does not decide whether the object is a fluid. An ordinary fluid with a constant pressure and an ordinary fluid with a Korteweg stress are the same fluid physics with two laws of state, and the linear/nonlinear split follows the law of state and nothing else. The Gross–Pitaevskii equation is the second case and the free scalar the first, and neither is more of a fluid than the other.

**So the criterion is a statement about derivations that happens to be easiest to state at the classical layer.** It is not a quantum criterion applied downward, and it is not a classical criterion lifted upward: it is the same four questions, and every quantum rung answers them with the same four answers and the same four failures, the names being the only thing that changes.

### The Movements Down the Ladder, and Which Are Monotone

**Only one column degrades the whole way, and that is the article's sharpest single fact.** The tempting summary — that the fluid reading weakens as the group grows — is false, and four of the five columns are the counter-example.

| slot | first rung | middle | top rung | direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| continuity | proper density, strict | charge density, still strict | identity, and its ordinary form fails | **degrades**, monotonically |
| vorticity | zero identically | derived: Maurer–Cartan, then a curl | fundamental: a curvature with an equation | **strengthens**, monotonically |
| acceleration | the gradient of another equation | a projection of the frame's equations | a field equation whose subject is the vorticity | **strengthens** — a by-product becomes an equation |
| density's equation | second order (guidance, mass shell) | algebraic (four vanishing momenta) | second order again, with the frame as a source | **reverses** |
| velocity | a gradient, one function | a unit element, three parameters | a gauge-covariant frame derivative, twelve, **internal** | **widens** — more room, not less, and at the top it leaves the base |

**Continuity is the only slot whose warrant is strictly lost on the way up**, and the loss is the price of the phase's disappearance: a slot that is a variation while a cyclic coordinate exists stops being one when the coordinate collapses. **Everything else about the ascent is gain or lateral movement**, which is why the primordial rung can be called the strong case and why the ladder is not a story of dilution.

**The density's column reverses for a reason internal to it, and the two ends are second order in different ways.** At the Klein–Gordon rung the amplitude's equation is a mass shell because the action is second order in $\varrho$; at the primordial rung the four eigenvalues obey wave equations because the action is second order in each, with the frame's velocity as a source. **The middle is the exception, not the rule**: Dirac's amplitude equation is algebraic because that action is first order.

**The subsections that follow take continuity, vorticity, the acceleration slot and the state**; the density's equation and the velocity are in the table above and in §*Which Slots Are Coordinate Equations*.

### Continuity: Conservation Law, Charge Density, Identity, Failing Identity

**At the gradient rungs continuity is a conservation law in the strict sense, the strongest form the slot ever takes.** The phase is a coordinate and it is *cyclic*: it appears in the action only through its derivatives. A cyclic coordinate's Euler–Lagrange equation is the divergence of its conjugate momentum, which is the statement that a current is conserved. The Schrödinger rung gives $\partial_{t'}\rho_{\mathrm{num}}+\vec\nabla_\Phi\!\cdot\!(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\vec v)=0$ with $\vec v=\vec\nabla_\Phi S/m$; the Klein–Gordon rung gives $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}\partial_{\imath}(\rho_{\mathrm{num}}V_{\Phi,\imath})=0$. The same equation at different signatures, and the phase's own variation in both.

**The Klein–Gordon rung already carries the first qualification, and it is about which density is conserved.** The coefficient of the phase's rate is $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\cosh\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ and not $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}$, so the conserved object is the charge density, which changes sign with the branch because the energy does. [Klein–Gordon Fluid Equations from the Polar Action](klein-gordon-fluid-equations-from-the-polar-action.html) concedes it: *the fluid's own density is $\rho_{\mathrm{num}}$, which is positive, and it is conserved only after division by $\gamma$.* **The derivation is untouched and the identification is weakened** — the first of the two gaps the primordial rung inherits.

**At the spinor rung the phase is still a coordinate and continuity still its equation, but it no longer conserves the fluid's density.** The current is the bilinear $\tilde J=\rho_{\mathrm{num}}\tilde V_{\Phi}$, the velocity a unit element rather than a gradient, and the equation strict and a genuine variation. **The defect is double and neither part weakens the derivation**: the conserved object is the charge density, and the current is not $\rho\nabla S/m$. Both are in [The Spin-Zero Fluid](the-spin-zero-fluid.html)'s three-column comparison.

**The break comes at the vector rung, and it is a break in the word rather than in the equation.** The word is $\varepsilon i\varrho\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm{b}}$, a real four-vector's decomposition: a scale, a direction and a sign. **The phase is not missing from the theory; it is collapsed, and the collapse is what removes the coordinate.** The free word is the real slice of the charged word $\varrho\,e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\tilde U_{\Psi,\mathrm{b}}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$, taken at $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}\in\{0,\pi\}$ with the rotor trivial, so the free rung's $\varepsilon$ *is* the phase's two allowed values. **A two-valued parameter admits no variation** — there is no path through it to differentiate along — so the slot has no Euler–Lagrange equation at this rung and falls back on the identity. At the charged rung the phase is continuous and the coordinate returns. Continuity survives as the divergence of the field equation, and the divergence is computed in two halves that do different work. The field-strength side dies: $F$ is a pure vector, so its scalar part vanishes, which is the algebra's form of the antisymmetry. **The mass side survives, and it is the mass that makes the identity a condition rather than a tautology** — the scalar part of the diverged equation reads $0=4\pi^{2}\nu_m^{2}S$ with $S=\mathrm{Sc}((\tilde\nabla_\Phi)^{\natural}\tilde A_\Phi)$, so $S=0$ exactly when $\nu_m\neq0$, and at $\nu_m=0$ the same step gives $0=0$ and no constraint at all. That vanishing mass case is Maxwell, and its redundancy is precisely what survives when the condition stops being derivable. **An identity, not a variation** — the slot is filled by the equation's structure rather than by an act of the action principle. At the matrix rung the same thing happens for a different reason: the overall phase is the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ inside $U$, which the stabiliser of $P$ absorbs, so no phase coordinate survives the constraint that the word be a bijection.

**The practical consequence, stated plainly.** At the gradient rungs continuity tells you how the density evolves, because the density is the phase's conjugate momentum up to the mass. At the primordial rung it tells you nothing about how the densities evolve: the four densities obey their own wave equations, and continuity is a consistency condition on the current. A reader who carries the Schrödinger intuition up the ladder will look for a density rate in the continuity equation and will not find one. That is the single largest change the slot undergoes, and it is a change in *what the word means* and not in the equation's form.

### Vorticity: Zero, Derived, Fundamental

**At the gradient rungs the vorticity is zero identically, and the vanishing is a theorem about gradients.** The velocity is $\partial_{\imath}S/m$ and the antisymmetrised derivative of a gradient is zero. Nothing is assumed about the flow and nothing is imposed: every solution is irrotational, and the circulation about any loop is fixed by the winding of one function. [The Spin-Zero Fluid](the-spin-zero-fluid.html) is careful about the qualification that a reader will otherwise get wrong: the curl of the three-velocity need not vanish, and it is not the vorticity, because $\gamma\vec v$ and not $\vec v$ is the spatial part of a four-vector. **The vorticity's vanishing is covariant, and the three-curl's non-vanishing is a frame artefact.**

**At the spinor rung the vorticity exists, is computable, and has no dynamics.** The velocity is a unit element rather than a gradient — three parameters against the one function a gradient supplies — so there is no scalar whose gradient it is and the flow is rotational in general. The vorticity is the antisymmetrised gradient of the frame element, which is the frame's Maurer–Cartan form read through the velocity, and it vanishes exactly where the frame is constant. Its status is the interesting part: it is an *identity of the chart*, computed from the word rather than solved for, and it has no equation of its own. This is the first rung at which the slot is filled by a non-zero object, and it is filled by a derived one.

**At the vector rung the vorticity is a derivative of the current, and still derived.** $\varrho\,\omega=F-\mathrm{d}\varrho\wedge u$: the field strength minus the density's wedge with the direction. It is the covariant curl of the current, so its dynamics is the current's dynamics — the curl is invertible on the transverse part, so an equation for the vorticity and an equation for the current are the same equation. **So the test is not "is the vorticity a curl" but "is there an equation whose subject is the vorticity."** By that test the vector rung fails, and only the primordial rung passes: there the Euler equation is first order in $\mathbb{F}$ and not in $A$, and $A$ reaches the fluid only as the vorticity's potential.

**The third state is a novelty and not a promotion**: fifteen internal components and an equation of their own, which none of the eight rungs prepares. The reason is a fact about the group and §*Why the Primordial Rung Is the Strong Case* states it in one line.

### Euler: Variation, Projection, Curvature

**At the scalar rungs the acceleration law is a variation's consequence, and it is the *gradient* of another equation rather than an independent one.** The amplitude's Euler–Lagrange equation is the guidance equation at the Schrödinger rung and the mass shell at the Klein–Gordon rung, and the force in either is $-\gamma^{-1}\vec\nabla_\Phi Q_{\mathrm{qnt}}$ — the gradient of the quantum potential. So Euler is not varied; it is differentiated. [The Lagrangian and the Spin-Zero Fluid](the-lagrangian-and-the-spin-zero-fluid.html) says the count exactly: *two real fields, two equations, and the third equation is the second's gradient, and it is not independent.*

**At the frame rungs the acceleration law is a projection.** The Proca rung is explicit: the two transverse components of the frame's equation are the fluid's Euler equation, the part orthogonal to the flow, and there is no coordinate whose equation it is. The projection is where the slot is weakest, because a projection is defined only where the frame exists and only along the directions the flow selects.

**At the primordial rung the slot is filled by the Yang–Mills equation, and its subject is the vorticity.** The spatial components of $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}D_{\imath}F_{a,\imath\jmath}=+g_{4}j_{a,\jmath}$ read as an acceleration law: the vorticity is transported by the flow and stressed by a source. The statement is exact, and it is a third kind of filling — not a variation, not a projection of a frame equation, but a field equation whose subject happens to be the vorticity. **The slot is filled in all three rungs and in three different ways, and only the first is a coordinate's equation.**

### State: From a Shell to a Stratification

**The equation of state comes from the algebraic term at every rung, because the algebraic term is the only one that is not a derivative.** The kinetic term supplies the metric and the transport; the non-derivative term supplies the pressure. That is why the slot is never a transport law, at any rung, and why it is the slot the derivation criterion never grants: it is algebraic by construction.

What changes is what the algebraic term *is*. At the Klein–Gordon rung it is a mass term, and it contributes to the stress along the flow with no equation of state in the barotropic sense at all — the classical medium is a Korteweg fluid and the stress comes from the density's gradient. At the Proca rung it is a mass term quadratic in the field's norm, and it gives $\epsilon=p=\tfrac12m^{2}\varrho^{2}$ — stiff matter, and the only rung whose state is barotropic in the **strict** sense, $p$ a function of the density with $\epsilon=p$. At the primordial rung the algebraic term is the potential in the four eigenvalues, so the pressure is anisotropic by construction, one pressure per density, and the fluid is barotropic in the **generalised** sense alone: no velocity enters the potential, and the four pressures are four functions of the four densities, but no single $p(\varrho)$ exists. **The strict sense is the Proca rung's and the generalised one is the top rung's**, and the difference is that four densities leave room for four pressures where one leaves room for one. **Four pressures and four sound speeds, the Hessian at the vacuum reducing to three distinct speeds and one mixing angle** — the count [The SU(4) Fluid Equations from the Primordial Action](su4-fluid-equations-from-the-primordial-action.html) computes.

## What a Fluid Equation Is Not

### Not a Rewriting of a Wave Equation

The reverse direction is the one that fails. The map from field to fluid is total and exact on the invertibles; the map back is defined only on a class, and the class is the fluid's extra structure. [The Fluid–Wave Correspondence: Which Constant Each Field Carries](the-fluid-wave-correspondence-which-constant-each-field-carries.html) counts what each field carries, and [The Reverse Madelung Map: Fluid to Nonlinear Wave](the-reverse-madelung-map-fluid-to-nonlinear-wave.html) states the direction. **A fluid equation is not a wave equation in other letters, because the wave equation does not follow from the fluid's equations without a constitutive condition.** The exact arrow is the one the corpus uses: field to fluid.

### Not a Set of Equations Obtained by Hand

A system of first-order equations with fluid names can be written for any field by choosing combinations of the field equation, and it will pass no test. The criterion of §*The Question, and the Answer That Is Not an Answer* is what excludes it: each equation must be one coordinate's variation. **A hand-assembled system is a combination, and a combination has no conjugate momentum, so it conserves no charge a symmetry would give it.**

### Not a System That Fails to Close

At the spinor rung the fluid is under-determined and the corpus says so rather than hiding it. **An under-determined set is a family of fluids, not a fluid**, in the same sense that [The Equation of State and the Barotropic Condition](the-equation-of-state-and-the-barotropic-condition.html) says a fluid without a law of state is a family of fluids. The failure is not a shortage of equations — there are eight for eight coordinates — but four vanishing momenta, which is to say four rates the equations never determine. **A rung that fails closure fails the reading in a specific and nameable way, and the name is the corank.**

### Not a Claim about Ontology

The reading adds no beable, no trajectory and no measurement theory. It is a re-coordinatisation of one equation into two or four or fifteen real equations, each of which is the original equation. **This article's claim is about the form of the derivation, not about what exists**, and the corpus's opening article makes the same denial at greater length.

**What this article does not claim.** That the polar chart is the only correct chart of these fields, or that a field *really is* a fluid underneath its equation. The criterion tests a derivation and does not inventory what exists: a rung that fails it is not less real, and one that passes it is not more. Nor is the criterion sufficient for a fluid to be *physically* a fluid — the corpus's own verdict is that the spinor rung needs constitutive structure beyond the derivation, and that the primordial rung's vorticity is never generated but only given. **A pass on all three tests licenses the reading and not the physics.**

## What the Criterion Buys

**First, a checklist usable before a derivation is attempted.** Given a new Lagrangian, the recipe of §*How the Equations Are Built* fixes which coordinates the word will have, which types their brackets will be, and therefore which slots the rung can fill. At the primordial rung that prediction was four densities, one frame, a vorticity with an equation, and no independent Euler variation.

**Second, an explanation of the ladder's shape.** The rungs differ because their words do, in size and in type-content: the vorticity's status tracks whether the frame is global or gauged, continuity's tracks whether the word has a cyclic phase, and the density's equation is second order or algebraic according to the action's order in that coordinate. **None of these is a coincidence and none is a choice of presentation.**

**Third, the precise place where each claim can be attacked.** The table of §*Which Slots Are Coordinate Equations* says which slot is a variation and which is a projection or an identity. A critic who thinks the reading overreaches should aim at the projections, and the articles name them in advance — **that is what the honesty paragraphs are for, and this article states their general form.**

## Open Questions

**The spinor rung's closure.** Whether the spin's transport closes the spin-half fluid uniquely, or whether the closure is one of a family, is the subject of [The Non-Uniqueness of the Spin-Half Fluid](the-non-uniqueness-of-the-spin-half-fluid.html). **Rank closure makes the question precise** — the corank is four and the missing relations are the four constraints — and it does not answer it: a degenerate form can be closed by extra structure in more than one way, and this article takes no position on which.

**The vorticity potential's global existence.** At the primordial rung the vorticity is a curvature and the potential is a patched object; the covariant Poincaré lemma fails on a nontrivial bundle. Whether the fluid reading survives as a patchwise description is open, and it is the only place where the top rung's reading might end.

**The crossing.** Where two eigenvalues coincide the frame's identification of the pair fails and the chart degenerates. Whether the fluid there is a four-fluid with a residual symmetry or a two-fluid with a doubled label is open, and it decides what the transition looks like in fluid language.

## Summary

An equation is a fluid equation in this corpus when it is one coordinate's Euler–Lagrange equation in the polar chart of the field, and not a combination constructed on the field's own equation. The criterion is the Schrödinger article's, and it is about derivations rather than vocabulary: the phase is a coordinate, its variation is the conservation of its momentum, and nothing was multiplied by the field's conjugate. **It changes no content — both roads reach the same equations — and what it changes is which equation is prior.** Its use is therefore structural and heuristic, and a reader who takes it for a founding principle will over-read it.

Three tests make a rung, nested rather than orthogonal. The **count** asks whether the field's real data equal the fluid's; necessary and not sufficient. The **derivation** asks whether each equation is a coordinate's variation; the criterion proper. The **closure** asks whether the highest-derivative form is nondegenerate — whether the rates are determined. **Count closure and rank closure are two tests under one word**: the Dirac rung passes the first with eight equations for eight coordinates and fails the second with four vanishing momenta, and the primordial rung removes thirty of sixty components by fifteen first-class Gauss laws and closes on the thirty that remain.

The recipe is five steps at every rung: the Lagrangian, the polar word, the action in the word built from the logarithmic derivative, the separation of the action by algebraic type, and one variation per coordinate. **The type decides the slot** — a real central coordinate gives the density's transport, a central imaginary one continuity, a frame coordinate the frame's transport, and the algebraic term the pressure.

At the gradient rungs two of the six slots are coordinate equations and four are read; the primordial rung makes it three, because the vorticity's equation is a variation — of the connection, which is a field in the action and not a factor of the word. The rest are read throughout: the velocity is a ratio of momenta, the acceleration a projection, the state algebraic.

**Continuity is the only slot that degrades, and it degrades the whole way**: from a conservation law of the proper density to one of the charge density, then to an identity, then to an identity whose ordinary form fails. The vorticity goes from zero identically to derived to fundamental, and the acceleration slot from the gradient of another equation to a projection to a field equation whose subject is the vorticity — **both strengthen**. The density's equation reverses and the velocity widens. The state goes from a shell to a stratification of four pressures. **The ladder's shape is therefore one loss, two gains and two lateral moves**, and the loss has a single named cause: the phase's disappearance as a coordinate.

The primordial rung is the strong case because it is the same decomposition at $\mathrm{U}(4)$: four eigenvalues and a twelve-parameter frame, the ladder's construction on a matrix-valued field. Its base is the material sector like every other rung's; its fibre is $\mathrm{Herm}(4)$; and its flow is internal, so it is a fluid *on* the base where the eight are fluids *in* it. The one thing it adds is the gauging of the frame: the frame's derivative splits into a pure-gauge piece and a physical piece, the physical piece's curvature is the vorticity, and a curvature is a field with an equation. **"The vorticity becomes dynamical" is exactly "the frame is gauged."**

# __Zitterbewegung in Biquaternionic Form__

## Introduction

The **Zitterbewegung** — the "trembling motion" — is the oscillatory part of the position of a Dirac particle. It was found by Schrödinger in 1930, in the course of solving the free Dirac equation. In the single-particle theory the velocity operator has eigenvalues $\pm c$, so a Dirac particle cannot have a sharp velocity; a wave packet assembled from positive- and negative-frequency plane waves has a mean position whose expectation value carries a rapid oscillation. Its angular frequency is $2E/\hbar$, which equals $2mc^2/\hbar$ for a particle at rest, and its amplitude is of the order of half the reduced Compton wavelength $\hbar/(2mc)$.

The biquaternion framework of these articles splits $\mathbb{B}$ by Hermitian conjugation into two sectors, $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{M}_+ \oplus \mathbb{M}_-$, which multiplication by the central $i$ exchanges. The natural suggestion is that this is the same two-component structure the Zitterbewegung exploits: that positive- and negative-energy components *are* the $\mathbb{M}_-$ and $\mathbb{M}_+$ sectors, and that the trembling is therefore the observable face of the complexification.

This article tests that suggestion rather than assuming it. The finding is mixed, and it is stated plainly from the outset. The oscillation can be derived and its frequency checked, and the mass does set the sector rotation: the rest-energy phase turns the two sectors into one another at $mc^2/\hbar$; but the identification of the $\pm$-energy components with the two sectors does **not** hold, because the energy-sign split is generated by an object that has no representative in the left copy of the biquaternion algebra, while the sector split is generated by an operation that lives inside it. The two splittings have the same physical scale — the mass — and that is why the suggestion is tempting, but they are splittings of different objects by different operations. Section *Does the Sector Split Do Real Work?* gives the argument, and Section *What Survives* separates the claims that hold from the one that does not.

The article is organised as follows. The next section recalls the standard picture, writes the Heisenberg velocity identity, and records the three contested readings of the effect. The section after fixes the biquaternion notation, the chiral pair and the plane-wave branches, following the parent. The following section recomputes the oscillation in cells, reduces every factor of $i$ with $i^2=-1$, and checks the frequency and the amplitude on cases chosen independently of the rest case. A short section then isolates the genuinely algebraic content — the real structure as a diagonal action with opposite signs on the sectors, and the rest-energy phase as a rotation between them — and shows that the Zitterbewegung phase is the square of the free-evolution phase, so its frequency is twice the sector-rotation frequency. The central section tests the sector identification and reports its failure. The closing sections record the relation to the companion article on the electron, set the cell notation alongside the standard Dirac notation in one place and for comparison only, and summarise the statements that survive. Nothing here depends on the electron article; the connection is noted where relevant.

## The Standard Picture

The dynamics in the algebra is the chiral pair $\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{\Psi}_R=m\tilde{\Psi}_L$, $\tilde{\nabla}^{\natural}\tilde{\Psi}_L=m\tilde{\Psi}_R$, whose spinor-module representative is the one-sided free Dirac equation. On the module, multiplication by the timelike cell and separation of the time derivative put it in Hamiltonian form,

$$
i\hbar\,\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t'} = H\psi, \qquad H = c\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\vec p + \beta mc^2,
$$

where $\vec p=-i\hbar\nabla$ is the momentum operator, $\beta = iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ and $\alpha_k = \beta\,iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}} = L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, with $L_{a}R_{b}\psi = a\psi b$ the two-sided cell and $I = L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ its identity. Every factor of $i$ in the cell products is reduced with $i^2=-1$ and the quaternion relations, as in the explicit simplification in the section *Recomputing the Oscillation* below. The cells satisfy

$$
\{\alpha_j,\alpha_k\} = 2\delta_{jk}I, \qquad \{\alpha_k,\beta\} = 0, \qquad (\alpha_k)^2 = I,
$$

so the velocity operator $c\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ has eigenvalues $\pm c$ and the Hamiltonian has eigenvalues $\pm E$ with $E = \sqrt{\vec p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}$.

The oscillation follows from the Heisenberg equation. Because $\{\alpha_k,H\} = 2cp_k I$ as an operator identity, the velocity in the Heisenberg picture has the exact form, with $\hbar$ kept explicit

$$
\alpha_k(t') = \frac{cp_k}{H} + \exp(iH t'/\hbar)\Big(\alpha_k(0) - \frac{cp_k}{H}\Big)\exp(-iH t'/\hbar),
\qquad H = c\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\vec p + \beta mc^2,
$$

in which the first term is the constant drift velocity and the second is the *trembling* term. Write $A_k=\alpha_k(0)-cp_k/H$. Because $A_k$ anticommutes with $H$, the second term can be written in either of the equivalent forms

$$
\exp(iHt'/\hbar)\,A_k\,\exp(-iHt'/\hbar)=A_k\,\exp(-2iHt'/\hbar)=\exp(2iHt'/\hbar)\,A_k,
$$

and the last form is what makes the integration below elementary. On the two energy branches the eigenvalues of the oscillating factor are $\exp(\pm 2iEt'/\hbar)$, so the angular frequency is $2E/\hbar$, and integrating once gives the position

$$
\boxed{\;x_k(t') = x_k(0) + \frac{c^2p_k}{H}\,t' \;-\; \frac{i\hbar c}{2H}\Big(\exp(2iH t'/\hbar)-1\Big)\Big(\alpha_k(0) - \frac{cp_k}{H}\Big)\;}.
$$

At rest, $H = \beta mc^2$ and the oscillating term has frequency $2mc^2/\hbar$ and amplitude of order $\hbar/(2mc)$, half the reduced Compton wavelength. The interference interpretation is immediate: a positive-frequency component $\exp(-iEt'/\hbar)$ and a negative-frequency component $\exp(+iEt'/\hbar)$ beat at $2E/\hbar$, and the trembling term is exactly that beat.

The interpretation of this result is contested, and the article does not settle it. Three positions are in the literature. First, the trembling is an artefact of forcing a single-particle interpretation on a theory whose solutions include both signs of energy: the Foldy–Wouthuysen mean position, the observable position for a positive-energy particle, has no trembling at leading order, so the oscillation is removed rather than seen. Second, the effect is not entirely empty: the same elimination of the small component that produces the mean position leaves the **Darwin term** at the next order in $1/c$, and the Darwin term and the Zitterbewegung are two faces of the same admixture of the negative-energy component — the [exercise on the non-relativistic limit](exercise-the-non-relativistic-limit-and-the-pauli-equation.html) derives that term, and the derivation is not repeated here. Third, in quantum field theory the position operator of a single particle is not fundamental, the free-field Zitterbewegung is not directly observable, and the effect is reinterpreted (in some treatments absorbed, in others associated with virtual pair creation and annihilation). None of these interpretations denies the algebra above; they disagree about what it describes.

## The Biquaternionic Setting

The notation is that of the parent and its read list. The biquaternion algebra is $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_t = 1, \vec e_x, \vec e_y, \vec e_z$, $\vec e_k^2 = -e_t$, scalar imaginary $i$, and biquaternionic gradient

$$
\tilde\nabla = e_t\,\partial/\partial Q_t + \vec e_x\,\partial/\partial Q_x + \vec e_y\,\partial/\partial Q_y + \vec e_z\,\partial/\partial Q_z.
$$

The Hermitian conjugation is $\tilde Q^{*} = \overline{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$, and it defines the two sectors by their fixed points,

$$
\mathbb{M}_- = \{\tilde Q : \tilde Q^{*} = -\tilde Q\}, \qquad
\mathbb{M}_+ = \{\tilde Q : \tilde Q^{*} = \tilde Q\}, \qquad
\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{M}_+ \oplus \mathbb{M}_- .
$$

The two sectors are exchanged by multiplication by $i$: $i\mathbb{M}_\pm = \mathbb{M}_\mp$. An element of $\mathbb{M}_-$ has imaginary scalar part and real vector part; an element of $\mathbb{M}_+$ has real scalar part and imaginary vector part. The real quaternion subspace is $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}=\{q_te_t+q_x\vec e_x+q_y\vec e_y+q_z\vec e_z:q_\mu\in\mathbb{R}\}$, and the trace formula used for expectation values is $\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q_1\tilde H) = 2\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde H)$.

The biquaternionic Dirac equation is the parent's linear, chirality-off-diagonal pair

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi_R &= m\tilde\Psi_L,\\[2pt]
\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Psi_L &= m\tilde\Psi_R,
\end{aligned}
$$

for a field of mass $m$, with $\tilde\Psi_R$ and $\tilde\Psi_L$ the two chiral halves. At $m=0$ the pair decouples into the two one-sided equations $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi_R=0$ and $\tilde\nabla^{\natural}\tilde\Psi_L=0$, one for each chirality; the single equation $\tilde\nabla\tilde\Psi=0$ is the right-handed member. The conjugation

$$
\tilde\Psi^\flat = -\tilde\Psi^{*}
$$

is the algebra's real structure, not the mass term.

The corpus also carries a **two-sided** form of the same physics — Kravchenko's single equation on $\mathbb{B}$, in which the mass acts by right multiplication through $\vec e_z$ and a conjugation supplies the antilinear part — but the two forms are related by a dictionary and are not the same operator (parent, *The One-Sided and the Two-Sided Equation*). The Zitterbewegung derivation below uses the one-sided pair.

The plane-wave solutions are

$$
\tilde\Psi=\tilde\Psi_0\exp\!\Big(i\,\mathrm{Sc}\big(\tilde P\tilde R^{\natural}\big)/\hbar\Big),
\qquad
\frac{\tilde P}{\hbar}=e_t\frac{iE}{\hbar c}+\vec k,
\qquad
\tilde R=e_t(ict')+\vec x,
$$

with $\tilde\Psi_0$ a constant biquaternion and $\vec x=x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$. The single mass-shell condition is

$$
\tilde P\tilde P^{\natural}=-m^2c^2.
$$

The two roots of that condition — positive and negative frequency — are the two branches

$$
\psi^{(r)}(\vec p)\exp\!\Big(-i\frac{Et'-\vec p\cdot\vec x}{\hbar}\Big),
\qquad
\chi^{(r)}(\vec p)\exp\!\Big(+i\frac{Et'-\vec p\cdot\vec x}{\hbar}\Big),
$$

constructed in the parent, with $E = +\sqrt{\vec p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}$. The spin sums, normalizations, and the non-relativistic reduction belong to the parent; the present article uses only the branches and the mass shell.

One fact about the field decomposition will be needed throughout. For $\tilde\Psi = \tilde\Psi_+ + \tilde\Psi_-$ with $\tilde\Psi_\pm \in \mathbb{M}_\pm$, Hermitian conjugation acts as $\tilde\Psi_\pm^{*} = \pm\tilde\Psi_\pm$, so

$$
\tilde\Psi^\flat = -\tilde\Psi^{*} = -\tilde\Psi_+ + \tilde\Psi_- ,
$$

and $\flat$ acts as $-1$ on the $\mathbb{M}_+$ part and $+1$ on the $\mathbb{M}_-$ part. It is the algebra's real structure, with fixed space $\mathbb{M}_-$, and a self-pairing built on it would carry those opposite signs. The physical Majorana mass is a different object: it is built on the module's charge conjugation, not on the algebra's $\flat$ (see *Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form*). What the diagonal action of the real structure, the mass coupling of the two chiralities, and the rest-energy phase do to the sectors is the subject of the section *The Complexification and the Rest-Energy Phase*.

## Recomputing the Oscillation

The parent's generators, written in cells rather than in the Dirac basis, are the timelike cell $\beta = iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ and the three spatial cells $iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, with $L_{a}R_{b}L_{c}R_{d} = L_{ac}R_{db}$. They satisfy

$$
\left(iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\right)^2 = L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}, \qquad
\left(iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}\right)^2 = -L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}, \qquad
\{iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}, iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}\} = 0,
$$

which is the cell relation with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$. Every sign in this display is a reduction of $i^2=-1$ and of the quaternion relations. For the first square,

$$
\left(iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}\right)^2
=i^2\,L_{e_t^2}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x^2}^{\mathrm{axf}}
=i^2\,L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{-e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}
=(-1)(-1)L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}
=L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}},
$$

and for the product that defines the spatial cells,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_k &= \beta\, iL_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}
= i^2\,L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}
= i^2\,L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{-\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}} \\[2pt]
&= -L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{-\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}
= L_{\vec e_k}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\vec e_y\vec e_x=-\vec e_z$ and $R_{-b}=-R_b$. The relations $\{\alpha_j,\alpha_k\} = 2\delta_{jk}L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, $\{\alpha_k,\beta\} = 0$ and $H^2 = E^2L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ all follow, and each was checked numerically.

The operator identity for the Heisenberg velocity is the exact statement

$$
\boxed{\;\alpha_k(t') = \frac{cp_k}{H} + \exp(iH t'/\hbar)\Big(\alpha_k(0) - \frac{cp_k}{H}\Big)\exp(-iH t'/\hbar)\;},
$$

in which $cp_k/H$ denotes the operator $cp_kH^{-1} = cp_kH/E^2$ — not a multiple of the identity, since $H$ is a matrix. Because $H$ anticommutes with $\alpha_k - cp_k/H$, the oscillating factor evolves on each energy eigenspace as $\exp(\pm 2iEt'/\hbar)$.

The identity was checked by direct matrix computation on a case chosen here rather than on the rest case that motivated it: momentum $\vec p/mc = (0.3,\,0.2,\,-0.1)$, so $|\vec p| = 0.374\,mc$ and $E = 1.0677\,mc^2$, at the three times $t' = 0.017, 0.05, 0.23$ in units of $\hbar/(mc^2)$. The left side $\exp(iHt'/\hbar)\alpha_k \exp(-iHt'/\hbar)$ and both equivalent forms on the right agreed to machine precision, with a maximum absolute entry below $10^{-15}$ over all three components and all times. The integrated position formula was checked the same way, against a direct spectral integration of $c\alpha_k(t')$ (the integral computed from the eigen-decomposition of $H$); its maximum entry differed by less than $5\times10^{-16}$. The rest case is then a consequence, $E = mc^2$, not an input.

The frequency is $2E/\hbar$ on general grounds and $2mc^2/\hbar$ at rest. The numbers for the electron, $m_ec^2 = 0.510998950$ MeV and $\hbar = 6.582119569\times10^{-22}$ MeV s, are

$$
\boxed{\;\omega_Z^{\text{rest}} = \frac{2m_ec^2}{\hbar} = 1.55269\times10^{21}\ \text{rad s}^{-1},
\qquad f_Z = \frac{\omega_Z}{2\pi} = 2.47118\times10^{20}\ \text{Hz}\;},
$$

which is the standard value. On a relativistic case chosen independently, with Lorentz factor $2$ so $E = 2mc^2$, the formula gives $2E/\hbar = 4m_ec^2/\hbar = 3.10538\times10^{21}\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$ — twice the rest value, as the formula requires and as the rest case alone would not reveal. The oscillation amplitude is $\hbar/(2mc)$, which for the electron is $193.1$ fm, half the reduced Compton wavelength $386.2$ fm. Both the frequency and the amplitude are the standard ones; the biquaternion notation reproduces them without alteration.

It should be said clearly that this derivation uses the odd cell $\beta$ and therefore does **not** stay inside the even algebra $\mathbb{B}$. That will matter in the test that follows.

## The Complexification and the Rest-Energy Phase

There is a genuinely algebraic statement available, and it is the strongest positive result of the article. It has two parts.

**The real structure is diagonal on the sectors.** The decomposition of the setting section gives $\tilde\Psi^\flat = -\tilde\Psi_+ + \tilde\Psi_-$: the conjugation $\flat$ acts as $-1$ on the $\mathbb{M}_+$ part and $+1$ on the $\mathbb{M}_-$ part, so it **preserves** each sector and marks it with a sign. This is a property of the algebra's real structure, not of the mass term; the parent's mass is the linear, chirality-off-diagonal pair, which couples the two central ideals (the chiralities) and, being a real scalar, leaves each element's sector membership untouched. The operation that exchanges the two sectors is multiplication by the central $i$, $i\mathbb{M}_\pm = \mathbb{M}_\mp$.

**The rest-energy phase rotates the sectors into one another.** Factoring out the rest energy writes the free phase as

$$
\exp(-imc^2t'/\hbar) = \cos\!\Big(\frac{mc^2t'}{\hbar}\Big)e_t \;-\; \sin\!\Big(\frac{mc^2t'}{\hbar}\Big)(ie_t),
$$

and the two terms lie in different sectors: $e_t \in \mathbb{M}_+$ and $ie_t \in \mathbb{M}_-$ (an imaginary scalar is anti-Hermitian). The rest-energy phase is therefore a **rotation between the sectors**, at angular frequency $mc^2/\hbar$: it takes the $\mathbb{M}_+$ direction $e_t$ toward the $\mathbb{M}_-$ direction $ie_t$ and back. This is the sense in which the complexification rotates one sector into the other; the rotation is the central phase, whose frequency the mass fixes through the mass shell.

The Zitterbewegung frequency is exactly twice this rate. That is not a coincidence of arithmetic but an algebraic relation: the oscillating factor of the velocity identity is $\exp(2iHt'/\hbar)$, the square of the free-evolution phase $\exp(iHt'/\hbar)$,

$$
\exp(2iHt'/\hbar)=\big(\exp(iHt'/\hbar)\big)^2.
$$

The rest-frame eigenvalues of the free-evolution phase are $\exp(\pm imc^2t'/\hbar)$, one of which is the sector rotation above; the eigenvalues of the oscillating factor at general momentum are $\exp(\pm 2iEt'/\hbar)$, the sign set by the energy branch, so only the magnitude of the frequency is physical. So the trembling is not the sector rotation itself; it is the second harmonic of it. The factor of two is the statement that the beat of two branches of opposite frequency is twice the frequency of either branch, and it is the same factor by which the rest-energy phase and the Zitterbewegung frequency differ.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, since the rest-energy phase rotates the sectors and its rate is the mass, the scale of the trembling — the mass gap $2mc^2$ — is set by the same quantity that sets the sector rotation. That is the real content of the suggestion that the trembling and the complexification are related. Second, a relation of scale is not a relation of identity: the trembling is a beat of two energy branches, the sector rotation is a rotation in the algebra, and the following section shows these are different decompositions.

## Does the Sector Split Do Real Work?

The claim to test is: *the positive- and negative-energy components are the $\mathbb{M}_-$ and $\mathbb{M}_+$ sectors.* The test fails, and the reason is structural rather than numerical.

**The energy-sign split is generated outside $\mathbb{B}$.** The projector onto positive energy at momentum $\vec p$ is

$$
P_+(\vec p) = \tfrac12\Big(I + \frac{c\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\vec p + \beta mc^2}{E}\Big) = \tfrac12\Big(I + \frac{H}{E}\Big),
$$

and it contains $\beta = iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$. In the cell Clifford algebra $\beta$ is a **single generator**, hence **odd**; every left multiplication $L_a$ is **even**, because it commutes with the volume element $\omega$ defined in the next sentence. The left copy $\mathbb{B}_L$ therefore contains no odd element, so no $a\in\mathbb{B}$ has $L_a=\beta$, and *a fortiori* $\beta$ lies in neither sector. This is not a convention; it is tested by the parity grading. With $\omega = -L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ the volume element, even cells commute with $\omega$ and odd cells anticommute with it; direct computation gives $\{\beta,\omega\} = 0$ (so $\beta$ is odd) and $[\alpha_k,\omega] = 0$ (so the $\alpha_k$ are even). The energy-sign split and the sector split are therefore generated by different operations: $P_\pm(\vec p)$ by the odd cell $\beta$, which is outside $\mathbb{B}$, and the sector projectors $P_\pm^{\mathrm{sec}}\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde Q\pm\tilde Q^*)$ by Hermitian conjugation, which is inside it.

**They are also splits of different objects.** The sector split is a decomposition of the *algebra element* $\tilde\Psi \in \mathbb{B}$ into $\tilde\Psi_+ + \tilde\Psi_-$. The energy-sign split is a decomposition of the *spinor module* — of the four-component Dirac spinor, with positive and negative energy each two-dimensional. The biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ acts on a two-dimensional complex module; the four-component spinor is the direct sum of that module with its conjugate, and, for $\vec p\ne0$, the energy projection mixes the two summands. Identifying a decomposition of the algebra with a decomposition of the module, without a stated dictionary, is a category error, and the parent article explicitly leaves the dictionary as a convention.

**The natural dictionary the corpus supplies points the other way.** For a normalized two-component spinor the parent identifies the rank-one Hermitian form

$$
\psi\psi^\dagger \;\longmapsto\; \tfrac12\big(e_t + i\vec u\big) \in \mathbb{M}_+,
$$

the idempotent of the informational sector, independent of energy sign. Both rest-frame branches of the parent — $\psi^{(r)}(0)\propto(\xi^{(r)},0)$ and $\chi^{(r)}(0)\propto(0,\eta^{(r)})$ in the Dirac basis — reduce to a single two-component spinor, and the rank-one Hermitian form of each is an $\mathbb{M}_+$ object. The sector $\mathbb{M}_-$ is where the *current* lives, $\tilde J = ic\,j_0e_t + \vec j \in \mathbb{M}_-$, not where the negative-energy component lives. So on the dictionary actually available, the sector split tracks the operator–vector (state–current) distinction, not the energy sign, and both energy signs land in the same sector.

**A momentum argument reinforces this.** The sector split is pointwise and momentum-independent: whether an element is Hermitian is a property of the element, not of any Fourier mode it carries. The energy projection is momentum-dependent through $\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\vec p$. A momentum-independent algebraic split cannot be a momentum-dependent spectral projection except in the trivial case $\vec p = 0$, and even there the two rest branches both land in $\mathbb{M}_+$.

The honest conclusion, then, is that the $\mathbb{M}_\pm$ split **renames the Hermitian decomposition of the algebra and not the $\pm$-energy decomposition of the spinor**. It is not obviously "doing work": the two splittings share the mass scale, but the interference that produces the trembling is between energy branches, and the energy branches are not the sectors. The natural candidate for a splitting that does carry the interference is **chirality**. Its cell $-iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ is Clifford-**even**, unlike the odd $\beta$, but it is a two-sided cell, not a left multiplication: its left part is $L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}$ and its right part is $R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}$. The abstract chirality $\chi$ is central in the complexification $\mathbb{B}[j]$, not in $\mathbb{B}$ itself. In the massless limit the chiral pair decouples into two independent Weyl equations and chirality locks to helicity. But even there each chiral equation carries both frequency signs, so chirality does not coincide with the energy-sign split either; it is a splitting of the module by the chirality operator, not a splitting of the algebra into $\mathbb{M}_\pm$. Whether the chirality idempotents should be read as objects of $\mathbb{B}$ or as central idempotents of the complexified algebra is the distinction recorded by the parent. What is settled is that the $\pm$-energy split is neither the $\mathbb{M}_\pm$ split nor the chirality split, for the reasons above.

## What Survives

The following table separates the claims.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The trembling is an interference of $\pm$-frequency branches, frequency $2E/\hbar$ | Established; the standard result, recomputed here |
| Rest-frame frequency $2mc^2/\hbar$, amplitude $\hbar/(2mc)$ | Established; checked numerically above |
| The real structure $\flat$ acts with opposite signs on $\mathbb{M}_\pm$ | Established; diagonal, verified from $\tilde\Psi_\pm^{*} = \pm\tilde\Psi_\pm$ |
| The rest-energy phase rotates the sectors at $mc^2/\hbar$ | Established; with $\theta=mc^2t'/\hbar$, $\exp(-i\theta) = \cos\theta\,e_t - \sin\theta\,(ie_t)$ |
| The Zitterbewegung phase is the square of the free-evolution phase | Established; hence the frequency is doubled |
| $\pm$-energy components **are** the $\mathbb{M}_\mp$ sectors | False as an identity; the generator $\beta$ lies outside $\mathbb{B}$ |
| The $\mathbb{M}_\pm$ split does work beyond relabelling the Hermitian decomposition | Not established; the interference is between energy branches, which are not the sectors |

What survives is a relation of *scale and phase*, not of identity. The mass sets both the sector-rotation rate and the gap whose interference is the trembling; the trembling frequency is twice the sector-rotation frequency because the trembling phase is the square of the evolution phase. This makes the Zitterbewegung a place where the mass term and the two sectors are simultaneously visible, and it is a legitimate point of contact between the Dirac solutions and the sector decomposition. It does not make the trembling the observable face of the complexification in the strong sense proposed.

## Zitterbewegung and the Electron

The Zitterbewegung is usually introduced as a property of the electron, and a [companion article on the electron in biquaternionic form](the-electron-in-biquaternionic-form.html) has since been written in this group. Nothing in the present article depends on it. If the sector decomposition is to acquire physical content, the electron article is the natural place to look: the electron's rest energy is the scale of both the sector rotation and the trembling, so any attempt to identify an observable with the informational sector would have to pass through that scale. The present negative result is a constraint on that attempt, not a substitute for it.

## The Legacy Pauli Gamma Formulation

The Pauli and gamma formulation is legacy: the reasons are explained in the article *Why AXF Matrices instead of Pauli and Gamma Matrices*. The bracket is the frame, on the generators as on the metric, $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-]$ or $\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{B}]$, so that a glyph names a display and not an object of the algebra.

The gammas are a frame of the cell algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C})=M_4(\mathbb{C})$, not a different algebra. They are recorded here for comparison with the standard Dirac notation; the derivation above uses only the cells $\beta$ and $\alpha_k$.

Here $L_a$ and $R_b$ are the left and right multiplications, $L_a\psi=a\psi$ and $R_b\psi=\psi b$, and $c(M)$ is the coefficient matrix of $M$ in the basis of the sixteen products.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}, &\gamma_1[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\\[2pt]
\gamma_2[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}}, &\gamma_3[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{pau}},\\[2pt]
\gamma_5[\mathbb{M}_-] &= -iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{pau}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The same in the axis-free display:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}, &\gamma_1[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\\[2pt]
\gamma_2[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}}, &\gamma_3[\mathbb{M}_-] &= iL_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_y}^{\mathrm{axf}},\\[2pt]
\gamma_5[\mathbb{M}_-] &= -iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_z}^{\mathrm{axf}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

They satisfy $\{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_\nu[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=2\varepsilon_\mu\delta_{\mu\nu}L_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}R_{e_t}^{\mathrm{pau}}$ with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, the product $\gamma_5[\mathbb{M}_-]=i\gamma_0[\mathbb{M}_-]\gamma_1[\mathbb{M}_-]\gamma_2[\mathbb{M}_-]\gamma_3[\mathbb{M}_-]$ squares to the identity, and $\gamma_5[\mathbb{M}_-]$ anticommutes with all four: $\{\gamma_\mu[\mathbb{M}_-],\gamma_5[\mathbb{M}_-]\}=0$.

A single cell $L_aR_b$ is a pure tensor; a single product is one cell up to a phase, the products form the seven-dimensional cone $\operatorname{rank}c(M)\le1$, and a general element is a sum of at most four, with $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$.

$\gamma_5[\mathbb{M}_-]$ is the reference-frame cell of the chirality operator $\Gamma$; the central grading $\chi$ — the difference of the two central idempotents of the complexified algebra, so $\chi^2=e_t$ — is a different object, represented by $\gamma_5[\mathbb{M}_-]$ on the Dirac module.

The dictionary is complex-linear; over $\mathbb{R}$ the products span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

**The display.** Every array printed in this section is given twice: in the Peirce display first, labelled $\mathrm{pau}$, whose arrays are the ones the literature prints, and in the axis-free display after it, labelled $\mathrm{axf}$, which is the display of the body and of the corpus. The two are carried into one another by $L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{axf}}=C_{\Pi}L_{e_\mu}^{\mathrm{pau}}C_{\Pi}^{-1}$, and a conjugation by $C_{\Pi}$ changes no product, no anticommutator, no trace and no determinant, so every identity of the section holds in both. An array is a statement about one display; a cell named in the running text keeps the Peirce label, and its axis-free array is the conjugate. The label names the array and not the operator.

## Summary

The Zitterbewegung was derived in the biquaternion notation of the series. The Heisenberg velocity satisfies the exact identity $\alpha_k(t') = cp_k/H + \exp(iHt'/\hbar)(\alpha_k(0)-cp_k/H)\exp(-iHt'/\hbar)$, checked by matrix computation at momentum $|\vec p| = 0.374\,mc$ and three times, to machine precision (maximum absolute entry below $10^{-15}$); the integrated position formula was checked against a direct spectral integration on the same case, with maximum error below $5\times10^{-16}$. The oscillating term has frequency $2E/\hbar$, equal to $2mc^2/\hbar$ at rest, and the electron value $1.55269\times10^{21}\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$ and amplitude $\hbar/(2mc) = 193.1$ fm were confirmed.

The genuinely algebraic content is that the mass is the linear coupling between the two chiralities (the algebra's two central ideals), that the real structure $\flat$ acts with opposite signs on the two sectors, and that the rest-energy phase $\exp(-imc^2t'/\hbar) = \cos(mc^2t'/\hbar)e_t - \sin(mc^2t'/\hbar)(ie_t)$ rotates the two sectors into one another at $mc^2/\hbar$. The Zitterbewegung phase is the square of this evolution phase, so its frequency is exactly twice the sector-rotation frequency.

The structural claim that motivated the article fails. The positive- and negative-energy components are **not** the $\mathbb{M}_-$ and $\mathbb{M}_+$ sectors. The energy-sign projector is built from $\beta = iL_{e_t}^{\mathrm{axf}}R_{\vec e_x}^{\mathrm{axf}}$, an odd Clifford cell; every left multiplication in the even copy $\mathbb{B}_L$ is even, so $\beta$ has no representative there, while the sector projectors are built from Hermitian conjugation; the two are splits of different objects (module versus algebra); and on the dictionary the series supplies, both energy branches correspond to $\mathbb{M}_+$ objects while the current lives in $\mathbb{M}_-$. The relation between the trembling and the complexification is therefore one of phase and scale — the trembling is the second harmonic of the sector rotation — and not the identity the strong form of the claim requires.

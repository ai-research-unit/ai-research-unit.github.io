# __Angular Momentum and the Lie Algebra of the Material Sector__

## Introduction

The antisymmetric plain product makes the biquaternion space a complex Lie algebra, and the algebra is
concentrated in the traceless vector subspace. The bracket of the companion article is the cross product
of the two vector parts; the centre is the complex line $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_0$; the
derived algebra is the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, of complex dimension three and real
dimension six; and the quotient by the centre is
$\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$. This article reads the structure of that algebra: its real forms, its
adjoint action, its invariant form, and the reading of angular momentum and of the Lorentz generators as
elements of the bracket.

The invariant form of the algebra is the **Killing form**

$$
\kappa(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde Q}\bigr)
=-2\,\mathbf{P}\cdot\mathbf{Q}=-2\,(P_1Q_1+P_2Q_2+P_3Q_3),
$$

with the adjoint $\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}(\tilde X)=\tilde P\wedge\tilde X$. It is symmetric and
invariant, its radical is the centre, and on the vector subspace it has real signature $(3,3)$: **negative
on the three real vector directions and positive on the three imaginary ones.** That sign change is the
algebraic face of a physical distinction, and it is the reading of the article: the invariant form is
negative on the compact rotation directions and positive on the non-compact boost directions, so the
algebra itself carries the compact–non-compact split of the transformations of the frame. The quadratic
invariant built from the form is the Casimir element, and the invariants of the rotation group are read
through it.

The reading of the generators is the second subject. The rotations are generated by the three real vector
units and the boosts by the three imaginary ones, so the six real directions of the complex vector
subspace are the six generators of the Lorentz group, with the three rotations forming the compact
subalgebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. The angular-momentum operators are read from the rotation triple: the
adjoint $G_k=\operatorname{ad}^{\wedge}_{e_k}$ of a real unit is the anti-Hermitian generator of the
rotation, and the adjoint $J_k=\operatorname{ad}^{\wedge}_{ie_k}=iG_k$ of the corresponding imaginary
unit is its Hermitian multiple, the observable. The boundary is stated where the operators are read and
again in the ledger: the algebra of the generators is supplied here, and the physical angular momentum
of a system, its ladder operators, its eigenvalues and its representations are *Angular Momentum and
Spin in Biquaternionic Form*.

The conventions are those of the companion article: $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$
with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_1e_2=e_3$, $e_k^{2}=-e_0$, central imaginary $i$, general element
$\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf{Q}$, scalar part $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q)=Q_0$, and the bracket
$\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P)=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{Q}$. The
material sector is $\mathbb{M}_-=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q\}$, the informational sector
$\mathbb{M}_+=\{\tilde Q:\tilde Q^{*}=+\tilde Q\}$.

## The Vector Subspace as a Lie Algebra

**Theorem.** The bracket
$\wedge$ on $\mathbb{B}$ has centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_0$ and derived algebra the
traceless vector subspace,

$$
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}},
\qquad
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B}),
$$

which is stable under the bracket and satisfies

$$
\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
\quad(\text{over }\mathbb{C}),
\qquad
\mathfrak{g}/\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}).
$$

*Proof.* The bracket is the cross product of the vector parts, so it vanishes as soon as one factor is
central and its image is traceless; conversely an element with a nonzero vector part fails to annihilate
some vector, since the cross product of two independent vectors is nonzero, so the centre is the scalar
line. The identifications are *The Lie Algebra of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*, where the ideals, the
derived series and the simplicity of the quotient are also established.

**Theorem (the real forms).** On the quaternion subspace and on the material sector the bracket is real,
and the two are subalgebras:

$$
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}\cong\mathbb{R}e_0\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2),
\qquad
\mathbb{M}_-\cong\mathfrak{u}(2),
$$

with the scalar line the radical direction in each and the compact factor $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ the
three-dimensional real vector triple; the informational sector is the complementary real subspace and is
**not** a subalgebra, hence not a real form, since the bracket of two imaginary vector units is a real
vector unit.

*Proof.* On $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ with real coefficients the bracket is the cross product of two real
vectors, so the vector part is the compact triple; on $\mathbb{M}_-$ with coefficients
$(iq'_0,q_1,q_2,q_3)$ the central direction $ie_0$ is annihilated and the three real vector units bracket
to each other, which is $\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$. On $\mathbb{M}_+$,
$ie_i\wedge ie_j=-e_i\wedge e_j=-\varepsilon_{ijk}e_k$ leaves the sector. This is *The Lie Algebra
of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*; the generators of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ are
$T_a=\tfrac12e_a$ with $[T_a,T_b]=\varepsilon_{abc}T_c$, the convention of *The Gauge Group Ceiling: Why
the Biquaternion Algebra Reaches $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ but Not $\mathrm{SU}(3)$*, and the central generator is
$T_0=\tfrac12ie_0$. Verified on the bases.

**Remark (the two roles of the material sector).** The material sector carries a Lie algebra of its own,
and it is the compact algebra $\mathfrak{u}(2)$: the three real vector directions are the rotations and
the central imaginary direction is the abelian phase. The companion article reads the antisymmetric
product on the whole algebra and the rotation triple; this article reads the real form that the material
sector itself supplies.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The centre $\mathbb{C}e_0$ is the radical of the Killing form, so
no bracket, and therefore no rotation or boost, sees it. Read physically, **the directions every
transformation leaves fixed are the global phase**, the abelian direction that decouples; its generator is
$T_0=\tfrac12ie_0$. *The phase read as a gauge group is owned by The Gauge Principle in Biquaternionic
Form, and the Cartan direction that carries charge in the electroweak reading is the vector generator
$\tfrac12e_3$, not the centre.*

## The Commutator and the Adjoint Action

The bracket of the block is half the commutator, $\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\tfrac12[\tilde P,\tilde Q]$,
so every statement about $\wedge$ has a commutator form with the factor of two carried through both slots.
The operator of the block is the **adjoint**

$$
\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}(\tilde X)=\tilde P\wedge\tilde X=\tfrac12[\tilde P,\tilde X],
$$

and $\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the adjoint representation.

**Proposition.** The adjoint map is $\mathbb{C}$-linear, annihilates the centre and takes values in the
vector subspace, its image at a fixed $\tilde P$ being the complex plane
$\mathbf P\times\mathbb{B}$ orthogonal to $\mathbf P$; it is a Lie homomorphism,

$$
\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q}
=\bigl[\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P},\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde Q}\bigr],
$$

and each adjoint is a **derivation** of the bracket,

$$
\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}(\tilde Q\wedge\tilde R)
=\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}\tilde Q\bigr)\wedge\tilde R
+\tilde Q\wedge\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}\tilde R\bigr).
$$

*Proof.* Linearity is the bilinearity of the bracket; the homomorphism is the Jacobi identity written with
the adjoint; the Leibniz rule is the same identity rearranged; both were recomputed on $100$ random
triples with max deviation $0$. The kernel of the adjoint is the centre, since
$\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}=0$ requires $\tilde P$ to annihilate every element. At a fixed $\tilde P$
with vector part $\mathbf P$ the image is $\mathbf P\times\mathbb{B}$, the complex plane orthogonal to
$\mathbf P$ when $\mathbf P\neq0$ and $\{0\}$ when $\tilde P$ is central, so no single adjoint is onto the
vector subspace, while the images over the algebra span it. The operators, the inner
and outer derivations and the automorphisms they generate are *The Adjoint Operators and the Derivations
of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The adjoint action is read as the action of the algebra on
itself: $\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}$ is the infinitesimal transformation of the element $\tilde P$, and
the image of the adjoint representation is the space of inner derivations, isomorphic to $\mathfrak{g}$
modulo its centre and hence to the derived algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ by the theorem above.
The inner derivations are the adjoints, and there is
one outer derivation, the dilation of the centre, by *The Adjoint Operators and the Derivations of the
Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*; the inner ones are exactly the transformations generated by the frame.

## The Killing Form and Its Signature

**Theorem.** The Killing form of the bracket is symmetric, invariant and equal to

$$
\kappa(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde Q}\bigr)
=-2\,\mathbf{P}\cdot\mathbf{Q}=-2\,(P_1Q_1+P_2Q_2+P_3Q_3),
$$

with Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(0,-2,-2,-2)$ in the basis of the units. Its radical is the centre
$\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$, so the form is degenerate of complex rank three and real rank six; it is
nondegenerate on the vector subspace. This is *The Killing Form of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*.

*Proof.* The formula and the Gram matrix are computed there on general elements and on the basis; the
radical is the set of elements whose vector part is orthogonal to every vector, which is the centre; and
removing the radical leaves the vector subspace, on which the complexified scalar product is
nondegenerate. The formula was recomputed here from the adjoint matrices, with max deviation $0$, and the
Gram matrix reproduced exactly.

**Corollary (the signature).** On the vector subspace
$\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ in the real basis $(e_1,e_2,e_3,ie_1,ie_2,ie_3)$ the form is
$\operatorname{diag}(-2,-2,-2,+2,+2,+2)$, of real signature $(3,3)$ and determinant $-64$: **negative on
the three real vector directions and positive on the three imaginary ones.** On the four four-dimensional
real subspaces the restrictions are semidefinite with the scalar direction as radical:

$$
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}:(0,3),\qquad i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}:(3,0),\qquad
\mathbb{M}_-:(0,3),\qquad \mathbb{M}_+:(3,0).
$$

*Proof.* The formula $\kappa=-2\,\mathbf{P}\cdot\mathbf{Q}$ restricted to a real direction is a sum of
squares with the sign of the direction, negative for real vector coefficients and positive for imaginary
ones; the scalar direction contributes zero. Verified on the six bases. This is the six-restriction table
of *The Killing Form of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The **sign of the Killing form is the compact–non-compact
dichotomy**. On a compact Lie algebra the invariant form is negative semidefinite, and on the
non-compact directions it changes sign; here the real vector directions carry the negative sign and the
imaginary ones the positive sign. The invariant form therefore separates the compact rotations from the
non-compact boosts by a sign alone, and the framework reads the sign of the form as the algebraic source
of that physical distinction. What is proved is the formula and its signature; the words "compact",
"non-compact", "rotation" and "boost" are the reading.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The dichotomy has a dynamical face: **negative means oscillatory
— closed, periodic; positive means runaway — open, hyperbolic**, so the sign of the form separates a
rotation from a boost by the kind of motion each generates.

## The Invariants of the Rotation Group

The invariant bilinear form is the quadratic invariant of the algebra, and it determines the **Casimir
element**

$$
\mathcal{C}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}h^{ij}E_iE_j=-\tfrac12\bigl(e_1^{2}+e_2^{2}+e_3^{2}\bigr),
\qquad
h^{ij}=-\tfrac12\delta^{ij},
$$

in the enveloping algebra of the derived algebra, where $h$ is the inverse of the Gram matrix
$\kappa(E_i,E_j)=-2\delta_{ij}$. The Casimir is central, and on the two representations in which it is
read it acts as a scalar: $\tfrac38$ on the two-dimensional module and the identity on the adjoint. This
is *The Killing Form of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra* and *The Casimir Operator*.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The invariants of the rotation group are read from the invariant
form. The form is the invariant scalar product of the algebra, the Casimir is the quadratic invariant
built from it, and the scalar by which the Casimir acts is the label of a representation: the value
$\tfrac38$ on the two-dimensional module and the identity on the adjoint are the two invariants of the
rotation and Lorentz representations that the algebra carries natively. The negative definiteness of the
form on the real vector triple is the compactness of the rotations, and the positive sign on the imaginary
directions is the non-compactness of the boosts; so the invariant form answers both questions of the block
at once, which generators there are and whether they are compact. What the algebra proves is the formula,
the centrality of the Casimir and its two scalar actions; the representation-theoretic reading of the
scalars is the mathematics and the physics of *The Casimir Operator*, *Representations of Lie Algebras*
and *Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form*.

## Angular Momentum and the Lorentz Generators

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The generators of the transformations of the frame are the
elements of the bracket, and the six real directions of the complex vector subspace are the six
generators of the Lorentz group:

- the three **real** vector units $e_1,e_2,e_3$ generate the **rotations**, the compact subalgebra
  $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with generators $T_a=\tfrac12e_a$;
- the three **imaginary** vector units $ie_1,ie_2,ie_3$ generate the **boosts**, the non-compact
  directions, on which the Killing form is positive;
- a finite rotation is the conjugation $\tilde X\mapsto\tilde\Lambda\tilde X\tilde\Lambda^{-1}$ by the
  rotor $\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,e_k$ of the companion article, and a finite boost is
  the dagger-sandwich $\tilde X\mapsto\tilde\Lambda\tilde X\tilde\Lambda^{*}$ by the Hermitian element
  $\cosh\tfrac{\psi}{2}+i\sinh\tfrac{\psi}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}$, of unit norm, of *Biquaternion Rotations
  and Lorentz Transformations* and *The Hermitian Subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ as the Informational Sector*.

The complex vector subspace is exactly the sector the corpus calls the home of the Lorentz generators, and
the assignment of the two triples to rotations and boosts is *The Four Other Remarkable Subspaces*.

**Proposition (the angular-momentum operators).** The adjoint actions of the imaginary vector units,

$$
J_k=\operatorname{ad}^{\wedge}_{ie_k}=ie_k\wedge\cdot\, ,
$$

satisfy the angular-momentum commutation relations

$$
[J_i,J_j]=i\,\varepsilon_{ijk}J_k ,
$$

as $\mathbb{C}$-linear operators on the algebra. Since
$\operatorname{ad}^{\wedge}_{ie_k}=\tfrac12\operatorname{ad}_{ie_k}=i\operatorname{ad}^{\wedge}_{e_k}$,
the imaginary unit is the Hermitian form of the real generator: the adjoints
$G_k=\operatorname{ad}^{\wedge}_{e_k}$ of the real units are the anti-Hermitian rotation generators and
the operators $J_k=iG_k$ their Hermitian multiples, with $J_k=\tfrac12\operatorname{ad}_{ie_k}$ the
normalisation of *Angular Momentum and Spin in Biquaternionic Form*, whose generators are the halved real
units.

*Proof.* Write $A_k=\operatorname{ad}_{ie_k}$ for the commutator adjoint, so that $J_k=\tfrac12A_k$. The
commutator adjoint is a Lie homomorphism, $[A_i,A_j]=A_{[ie_i,ie_j]}$, and
$[ie_i,ie_j]=i^{2}[e_i,e_j]=-2\varepsilon_{ijk}e_k$, so
$[A_i,A_j]=-2\varepsilon_{ijk}\operatorname{ad}_{e_k}$. Therefore
$[J_i,J_j]=\tfrac14[A_i,A_j]=-\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ijk}\operatorname{ad}_{e_k}$. Now
$J_k=\tfrac12\operatorname{ad}_{ie_k}=\tfrac i2\operatorname{ad}_{e_k}$, so
$\operatorname{ad}_{e_k}=-2iJ_k$, and substituting gives
$[J_i,J_j]=-\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ijk}(-2iJ_k)=i\varepsilon_{ijk}J_k$. Verified on the four-dimensional
adjoint matrices, max deviation $0$.

**Proposed reading, labelled as such.** The factor that carries the anti-Hermitian generator to its
Hermitian multiple is **the framework's own $i$**, the same central scalar that generates the phase:
**the generator becomes an observable by multiplication by $i$.**

## The Bound

**What the section does not claim.** The reading is that the algebra of the generators is the
antisymmetric plain algebra, that its invariant form separates the compact rotations from the non-compact

Three limits are worth stating. The **Lie algebra is not the group**: the exponential and the global

its central phase, which is another statement of the gauge ceiling. And the **kinematics of a material
field**, its angular momentum and its spin, belongs to the physics articles and not to this algebra.

## Summary

The antisymmetric plain algebra is a complex Lie algebra of complex dimension four, with centre
$\mathbb{C}e_0$, derived algebra $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ and quotient
$\mathfrak{g}/\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$; the real forms are
$\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}\cong\mathbb{R}e_0\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and $\mathbb{M}_-\cong\mathfrak{u}(2)$.
The adjoint action $\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}(\tilde X)=\tilde P\wedge\tilde X$ is a Lie homomorphism
and a derivation, and the Killing form is
$\kappa(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde P}\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde Q})=-2\,\mathbf{P}\cdot\mathbf{Q}$,
with radical the centre and vector restriction of real signature
$(3,3)$: negative on the three real vector directions and positive on the three imaginary ones. **The
sign of the form is the compact–non-compact dichotomy**: the real vector triple is the compact
$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ of the rotations and the imaginary triple the non-compact directions of the boosts. The
Casimir $\mathcal{C}=-\tfrac12(e_1^{2}+e_2^{2}+e_3^{2})$ is the quadratic invariant and acts as
$\tfrac38$ on the two-dimensional module and as the identity on the adjoint. The generators of the
transformations are the elements of the bracket, the six real directions of the complex vector subspace
being the six Lorentz generators; the angular-momentum operators
$J_k=\operatorname{ad}^{\wedge}_{ie_k}$ satisfy $[J_i,J_j]=i\varepsilon_{ijk}J_k$ on the algebra, and
their action on the state space, with the ladder operators and the representations, is *Angular Momentum
and Spin in Biquaternionic Form*. A Lie algebra is not yet a group, and the algebra of generators is not
the angular momentum of a material field.

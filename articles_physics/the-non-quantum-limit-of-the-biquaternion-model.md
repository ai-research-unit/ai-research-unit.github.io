# __The Non-Quantum Limit of the Biquaternion Model__

## Introduction

The non-relativistic limit of *The Non-Relativistic Limit of Relativistic Mechanics in Biquaternionic Form* truncates one element by a ratio of its own coefficients. The element is $\tilde V_{\Phi}=\gamma\left(ic\,e_{t}+\vec v\right)$ and the ratio is $\beta=\lvert\vec v\rvert/c$. The parameter, the error and the smallness are all internal to the element.

Can a second limit of the same kind remove the quantum? The answer has two halves.

**On the solutions, yes.** A state written as an amplitude and a phase carries one dimensionless number, $\epsilon=1/(k_{\Phi}L)$, where $L$ is the length over which the amplitude varies. When it is small the phase obeys the Hamilton–Jacobi equation alone and the amplitude obeys the transport equation. The state has become a ray.

**What that removes is a partition, not a wave.** *The Quantum–Classical Divide in the Biquaternion Framework* places the divide in a count: two projective measurements make a case quantum, one makes it classical, and wave phenomena are not the criterion. A superposition of two rays is one excitation across two modes, so two partitions. A single ray is one partition carrying the excitation with no second to sort, which is the corpus's classical class, Subset III. The limit removes the second partition, and that is the sense in which it is non-quantum. Classical optics performs the same passage, so the mathematics alone does not settle it and the count does.

**On the algebra, no.** The algebra carries no $\hbar$. It carries $k_{m}$, and $\hbar=\frac{m\,c}{k_{m}}$. A limit on $\hbar$ at fixed mass is a limit on $1/k_{m}$, which deepens the mass term instead of removing it.

The conventions are those of the companions: $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, sectors $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, wave-number biquaternion $\tilde K_{\Phi}=ik_{t}^{\prime}e_{t}+\vec k_{\Phi}$, where $k_{t}^{\prime}=E/(\hbar c)$, $\vec k_{\Phi}=\vec p/\hbar$ and $-\langle\tilde K_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}=k_{m}^{2}$, the three numbers of *The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle*. Throughout $\hbar=\frac{m\,c}{k_{m}}$, and $L$ is supplied by the problem.

**What is not new.** The corpus has the formal character of the $\hbar\to0$ limit in *Similitudes Between the Poisson Bracket and the Quantum Commutator*, the phase forms in two companions, the divide as a count in a third, and the centre as classical sector in a fourth. Added here: why the limit must be formal, the criterion as one inverse length, the refusal of $1/k_{m}$, the partition it removes, the phase route, and the derivation of the equations of motion. The relativistic side of the same expansion is carried out in the companion *Relativistic Mechanics from the Dirac Wave: The Non-Quantum Limit in Biquaternionic Form*.

## The Constant the Algebra Does Not Carry

**No element carries a unit.** A dimension enters only where an element is identified with a quantity, so $\hbar$ is not a symbol the algebra can act on.

| where $\hbar$ appears | with the algebra's numbers | a limit on it does this |
|---|---|---|
| the mass term | $k_{m}=\frac{m\,c}{\hbar}$, $\;-\langle\tilde K_{\Phi}\rangle_{\natural}=k_{m}^{2}$ | $k_{m}\to\infty$: the mass term diverges |
| the phase | $\frac{S}{\hbar}=\mathrm{Sc}\!\left(\tilde K_{\Phi}\tilde R_{\Phi}^{\natural}\right)$ | a pure number; nothing left to move |
| the bracket | $[x,p]=\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_{m}}$ | it vanishes: the Poisson bracket |

**The three rows do not agree, and that is the argument.** The third is the classical result; the first is its opposite, since $\Box-k_{m}^{2}$ stiffens until propagation is suppressed. One knob cannot deliver both.

**At fixed wavelength it does not even leave the other family.** On shell $k_{t}^{\prime\,2}=k_{\Phi}^{2}+k_{m}^{2}$, so $\frac{k_{\Phi}}{k_{m}}=\gamma\beta$ and $\frac{k_{t}^{\prime}}{k_{m}}=\gamma$. Send $\hbar\to0$ at fixed $k_{\Phi}$: both tend to zero and to one, and the triangle collapses onto the mass side.

| $\hbar$ | $k_{m}\ (\mathrm{m^{-1}})$ | $\gamma$ | $\beta$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\hbar$ | $2.590\times10^{12}$ | $1.00005$ | $1.000\times10^{-2}$ |
| $\hbar/100$ | $2.590\times10^{14}$ | $1.000000005$ | $1.000\times10^{-4}$ |

The collapse is the rest frame, $\beta\to0$ at fixed $k_{\Phi}$. That is a member of the $\beta$ family, not a second limit. **The naive $\hbar\to0$ returns a kinematic limit inside the family the companion article already owns.**

## The Criterion, and What It Is Not

**A limit needs a dimensionless parameter, and the model has none of its own.** The scales the series uses are wave numbers, so the only dimensionless combination is a product with a length, and the length is not in the algebra.

**Every internal ratio is already one number.** The three wave numbers form a right triangle with legs $k_{\Phi}$ and $k_{m}$ and hypotenuse $k_{t}^{\prime}$. A right triangle has one shape parameter, so $\frac{k_{\Phi}}{k_{m}}=\gamma\beta$, $\frac{k_{t}^{\prime}}{k_{m}}=\gamma$ and $\frac{k_{\Phi}}{k_{t}^{\prime}}=\beta$ are functions of $\beta$ alone. *The Three Wavenumbers of a Particle* says the same: the single parameter of the whole section is $k_{\Phi}/k_{m}$. No internal ratio can carry a second limit, because the triangle has no second shape.

| candidate | electron, $\beta=0.01$, $L=1\ \mathrm{nm}$ | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| $\beta=\lvert\vec v\rvert/c$ | $10^{-2}$ | the other limit |
| $\epsilon=1/(k_{\Phi}L)$ | $3.86\times10^{-2}$ | the non-quantum limit |
| $1/(k_{m}L)$ | $3.86\times10^{-4}$ | a change of theory |
| $\frac{\Omega}{k_{m}}=\gamma-1$ | $5.00\times10^{-5}$ | the other limit again |

The last row is not independent: $\frac{\Omega}{k_{m}}=\gamma-1=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}$. Two parameters remain, one internal and one external.

**The third row is not a limit.** A small $1/(k_{m}L)$ says the region is large against the mass length, the validity of the one-particle description: a precondition of quantum mechanics, not its removal. The two criteria differ by $\frac{k_{m}}{k_{\Phi}}=\frac{1}{\gamma\beta}=99.995$, so a reader who took $1/k_{m}$ as the classicality length would put the boundary at $386\ \mathrm{fm}$ where it lies at $38.6\ \mathrm{pm}$.

**The mass is not the quantum either.** Setting $k_{m}=0$ gives $k_{t}^{\prime}=k_{\Phi}$, the photon: linear dispersion, exact interference, no particle scale, and more wave-like than the massive case. A plane wave is semiclassical already, so the limit acts only where $A$ varies.

## The Rule

**Write the state and apply the momentum.** Take $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=A\exp\!\left(\frac{k_{m}\,i\,S}{m\,c}\right)\xi$ with $A>0$ real and central, $S$ real and central, and $\xi$ constant. Then

$$
\tilde p\,\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\left(\nabla S-\frac{m\,c\,i}{k_{m}}\nabla\ln A\right)\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}} .
$$

The real part keeps its leading term and the imaginary part carries the first correction. The two ratios are

$$
\frac{\lvert\nabla\ln A\rvert}{k_{\Phi}}\sim\epsilon ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{k_{\Phi}^{2}}\left\lvert\frac{\nabla^{2}A}{A}\right\rvert\sim\epsilon^{2} .
$$

The second is the quantum potential over the kinetic energy.

**The route is the phase, and that is a choice.** Ehrenfest's theorem takes expectation values and stops being Newton's equations unless the packet is narrow; the path integral needs a measure and a space of paths. The phase route alone stays inside the algebra point by point. *The Path Integral in Biquaternionic Form* records that the measure and the path space are gaps the algebra cannot close.

The rule is written for the scalar problem; the criterion does not depend on that choice.

| | the $\beta$ limit | this limit |
|---|---|---|
| the object limited | the element $\tilde V_{\Phi}$ | the solution $\tilde{Ξ}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ |
| the parts compared | $\gamma c$ against $\gamma v$ | $\nabla S$ against $\frac{m\,c}{k_{m}}\nabla\ln A$ |
| the parameter | internal to the element | external to the algebra |
| the dropped pieces | $\frac{3}{8}\beta^{4}$ temporal, $\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}$ vector | order $\epsilon^{2}$ in the amplitude |
| the error | a number, in closed form | an operator, with changing sign |

## The Classical Equations

**Start from the Schrödinger equation.** In the corpus's biquaternionic form it is $\frac{m\,c^{2}i}{k_{m}}\partial_{r_t'}\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde R_\Psi\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}$, which with $r_t'=c\,t$ and $\hbar=\frac{m\,c}{k_{m}}$ reads $i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi=H\psi$. Take the scalar case $H=\frac{\vec p^{\,2}}{2m}+V$, so that

$$
i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\psi+V\psi .
$$

**Substitute the amplitude and the phase,** $\psi=Ae^{iS/\hbar}$ with $A>0$ and $S$ real. Two real equations follow, and neither is an approximation:

$$
\partial_t S+\frac{\lvert\nabla S\rvert^{2}}{2m}+V-\frac{m\,c^{2}}{2k_{m}^{2}}\frac{\nabla^{2}A}{A}=0 ,
\qquad
\partial_t A^{2}+\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{A^{2}\nabla S}{m}\right)=0 .
$$

**Drop the third term of the first.** It is the quantum potential $Q$, and its size against the kinetic term is the criterion:

$$
\frac{\lvert Q\rvert}{\lvert\nabla S\rvert^{2}/2m}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{p^{2}L^{2}}=\epsilon^{2} .
$$

When it is small the phase equation is Hamilton–Jacobi and the continuity equation is untouched, because it never contained $Q$:

$$
\partial_t S+\frac{\lvert\nabla S\rvert^{2}}{2m}+V=0 ,
\qquad
\partial_t A^{2}+\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{A^{2}\nabla S}{m}\right)=0 .
$$

**Read off the equations of motion.** Put $\vec p=\nabla S$ and $\vec v=\vec p/m$. The gradient of the first equation gives

$$
\partial_t\vec p+(\vec v\cdot\nabla)\vec p=-\nabla V ,
$$

so along the flow,

$$
\frac{d\vec r}{dt}=\frac{\vec p}{m},\qquad \frac{d\vec p}{dt}=-\nabla V,\qquad m\frac{d^{2}\vec r}{dt^{2}}=-\nabla V .
$$

The force is the gradient of the same $V$ that stood in the Hamiltonian, and the energy is not a parameter of the limit: it is $E=-\partial_t S$, read off the phase.

**Two qualifications, both structural.** The continuity equation was never approximated, so the limit delivers a flow and its density rather than one trajectory; a trajectory is a single characteristic of that flow. And the derivation holds while $A$ stays finite. At a caustic $A$ diverges, $\epsilon$ is not small, and the ray picture ends.

## The Error Is an Operator

**A Gaussian waist gives the ratio in closed form.** Take $A=\exp\left(-x^{2}/(4\sigma^{2})\right)$, $\epsilon=1/(k_{\Phi}\sigma)$. Then

$$
\rho(x)=\epsilon^{2}\left\lvert\frac{x^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right\rvert ,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{\lvert Q\rvert}{E-V} .
$$

| $\lvert x\rvert/\sigma$ | $0$ | $1$ | $\sqrt{2}$ | $2$ | $3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\rho/\epsilon^{2}$ | $0.500$ | $0.250$ | $0$ | $0.500$ | $1.750$ |

**The table is the argument.** The ratio vanishes at $\sqrt{2}\sigma$ and grows as $x^{2}$ beyond it. For any $\epsilon$ it reaches one at $x\simeq\frac{2\sigma}{\epsilon}$, so no value of the parameter makes the expansion uniform. The $\beta$ error $\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma^{2}}$ is bounded, positive and monotone on $0\le\beta<1$, and no position enters it.

## What the Limit Does Not Do

**It does not act on the algebra.** No contraction is taken and no basis preferred. *The Centre of the Biquaternion Algebra as the Classical Sector* names the centre as the canonical classical sector, and the limit does not land there: the centre is minimal as an operator algebra. O21 of the obstruction catalogue says why from the other side, since no maximal commutative subalgebra survives a generic $*$-unitary evolution. The limit selects a state-dependent slice, the contextual mechanism O21 asks for, with $L$ as the context made quantitative.

**It does not commute with superposition.** Two plane waves of wave numbers $k_{\Phi}$ and $k_{\Phi}+\Delta k$ are each semiclassical exactly. Their sum has envelope wave number $\Delta k$, so parameter $\frac{1}{\Delta kL}$, large when the waves are close. So

$$
\lim\left(\tilde{Ξ}_{1}+\tilde{Ξ}_{2}\right)\neq\lim\tilde{Ξ}_{1}+\lim\tilde{Ξ}_{2} ,
$$

and the difference is the interference term. Read against the count, that is the sharp statement: the second partition is removed for one ray and restored by the sum, so the removal belongs to the propagation and not to the description. The limit is not a map on the state space. It does not remove the phase; it drops the amplitude's second derivative, and it does not give a state: a ray and a density are not vectors of the module.

## Case Checked

**The electron at $\beta=0.01$.** The mass wave number is $k_{m}=2.589605\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, so $\lambda_{m}=386.159\ \mathrm{fm}$; the momentum wave number is $k_{\Phi}=2.589735\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$.

| $L$ | $\epsilon$ | $1/(k_{m}L)$ | the limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1\ \mathrm{pm}$ | $38.61$ | $0.386$ | fails |
| $1\ \mathrm{nm}$ | $3.861\times10^{-2}$ | $3.862\times10^{-4}$ | holds to $7.5\times10^{-4}$ |
| $1\ \mathrm{\mu m}$ | $3.861\times10^{-5}$ | $3.862\times10^{-7}$ | holds to $7.5\times10^{-10}$ |

**The checks.** The Gaussian ratio agrees with a fourth-order difference of $\frac{1}{k_{\Phi}^{2}}\lvert\frac{\nabla^{2}A}{A}\rvert$ at one hundred values of $x/\sigma$ to $1.4\times10^{-6}$, the sign change included. The tables follow from $k_{t}^{\prime\,2}=k_{\Phi}^{2}+k_{m}^{2}$.

**The macroscopic end is true and empty.** For the Earth in its orbit, over $L=1\ \mathrm{AU}$, the parameter is $4.0\times10^{-75}$; the phase length reaches the Planck length at $6.52\ \mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$, beyond which the criterion holds where the model cannot resolve.

## Summary

The non-quantum limit cannot truncate an element, because the algebra holds no quantum constant. $\hbar$ enters only as $\frac{m\,c}{k_{m}}$. Lowered at fixed mass it stiffens the mass term while it softens the bracket; at fixed wavelength it brings the particle to rest.

What remains is one external number, $\epsilon=\frac{1}{k_{\Phi}L}$. When it is small the phase obeys Hamilton–Jacobi and the amplitude obeys transport, so $\vec p=\nabla S$, $\vec v=\vec p/m$ and $m\ddot{\vec r}=-\nabla V$. The error is an operator that changes sign, so the limit acts on solutions: it does not commute with superposition, does not select a subalgebra and does not produce a state. Those three refusals are one, since it takes a wave to its rays and the second partition with it, and that is why it needs no new constant.

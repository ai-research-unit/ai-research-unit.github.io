Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$

Introduction

A square root of the central value $+1$ in one of the twelve structures is an element whose square in that product is the unit, $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=e_t$. An idempotent of the same product is an element equal to its own square, $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$. The two equations are one equation read through one map, the halving map

$$ \tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi),\qquad \tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_t, $$

which for the general plain bilinear product is a bijection between the roots of $+1$ and the idempotents, since $\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_t$ exactly when $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$; the square in the map is the plain square, and the other structures read their idempotent sets from their own squares. The map needs no central unit and no root of $-1$; it is proved on the mathematics side, in Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space, and cited here. This article reads the roots of one and the idempotent states as one subject, and it is the physics companion of that mathematics article.

The physics of the two objects is fixed by the corpus. A root of $+1$ is an involution: an order-two operation, a reality condition, a discrete symmetry, and, when the structure is the probability pairing, a unitary transformation of the state space. Its physical owners are Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action, Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form and The Reflection and the Rotation in Biquaternionic Form. An idempotent is a projection, and a projection is a determinate state: the equation $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ reads as "measuring twice equals measuring once", the reading of The Monoid of Acting Maps: the Process Is the Multiplication, the State Is the Idempotent. The state families of the twelve bands are the idempotent families their own squares define: the plain states, one for each root of $+1$ of the plain product; the pure states of the probability band, the Hermitian subfamily shared by the plain and the pairing products; and the invertible states of the gauge band, the displaced family of the fourth product.

The three central values are read in the order the corpus uses. The roots of $+1$ are the subject here; the roots of $0$, the lightlike displacements, and the nilpotents are the subject of the companion Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$; the general radicand, the four existence classes and the mass shell are the subject of Square Roots of a General Element in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$. The roots of $-1$ are neither a section of this article nor of either companion: they are the short remark that closes the roots-of-one part, and the article says why.

Conventions. We use those of the companion articles unchanged. The algebra is $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$, with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$ and $\vec e_\mu \vec e_\nu=-\delta_{\mu\nu}e_t+\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}\vec e_\lambda$; $i$ is central with $i^{2}=-1$. The Halving Map

The equation and its inverse. For the general plain bilinear product the two problems are $\mathcal R_{+1}=\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_t\}$ and $\mathcal I=\{\tilde\Pi:\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi\}$. If $\tilde\Xi$ is a root of $+1$, then $\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_t$ and the element $\tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)$ satisfies

$$ \tilde\Pi^{2}=\tfrac14(e_t+2\tilde\Xi+\tilde\Xi^{2}) =\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)=\tilde\Pi, $$

so the halving map lands in the idempotent set; conversely, if $\tilde\Pi$ is idempotent then $\tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_t$ has $\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_t$, so the map is a bijection with inverse $\tilde\Xi\mapsto 2\tilde\Pi-e_t$. The square in the map is the plain square, which is also the square of the symmetric plain algebra; the other structures read their idempotent sets from their own squares, and the Hermitian family is the plain subfamily reached by the purely imaginary roots of $+1$. The proof is the mathematics companion's; the article uses the map.

The physical interpretation. The map is the passage from a process to a state and back. A root of $+1$ is an order-two operation of the module, and the halving map reads it as the projection onto the $+1$ eigenspace of that operation; the complement $e_t-\tilde\Pi$ is the projection onto the $-1$ eigenspace, so the pair $\tilde\Pi$, $e_t-\tilde\Pi$ corresponds to the pair of roots $\tilde\Xi$, $-\tilde\Xi$. This is why the state theory rests on the roots of $+1$ and on them alone: a state is an operation, halved, and the operation is an involution. No central unit and no root of $-1$ is needed, and the article keeps that economy throughout.

The two maps differ by the corpus's older notation. The corpus also writes the plain family through the central imaginary, using the roots of $-1$ where this article uses the roots of $+1$. The two writings describe the same set, since multiplication by the central $i$ carries the roots of $+1$ onto the roots of $-1$ for the bilateral products, and the older writing names a root the architecture does not use. The article writes the halving map with the roots of $+1$, $\tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)$ with $\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_t$, the roots of $-1$ appearing only in the closing remark. The reason is physical as well as formal: the element a state is built from must be an operation the state can be read from, and the roots of $+1$ are exactly the involutions, while the roots of $-1$ are the local complex structures, a different object with a different job.

Roots of One: the Involution, the Unitary and the Normalisation

In the plain product. The plain square of $\tilde\Xi$ is $\Xi_t^{2}-(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)+2\Xi_t\vec\Xi$, so the equation $\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_t$ forces either $\vec\Xi=0$ and $\Xi_t=\pm1$, or $\Xi_t=0$ and $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=-1$. The roots of $+1$ of $\mathrm{GPA}$ are therefore the central pair $\pm e_t$ and the pure family $\{\Xi_t=0,\ (\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=-1\}$, a quadric of real dimension four. The central pair is the trivial involution; a pure root reverses a direction of the vector subspace, with square the positive unit $e_t$. The family is classified directly, on the roots of $+1$ themselves, and the classification is the one the mathematics companion gives; the element $i\tilde\xi=i \vec e_x$ is the worked member, and $\vec e_x+\sqrt2\,i \vec e_y$ is the pure member with a complex coefficient. Physically an involution is a discrete symmetry, a reversal of a direction, and the corpus reads the family as the reality conditions of the material sector in Antilinear Structure and the Two Kinds of Mass in Biquaternionic Form.

In the symmetric plain algebra. The Jordan square equals the plain square, $\tilde\Xi\bullet\tilde\Xi=\tilde\Xi^{2}$, so $\mathrm{SPA}$ has exactly the roots of $\mathrm{GPA}$, element for element. One feature belongs to the block: the pure signed roots lie in the vector subspace, which is the zero-divisor set of the symmetrised product, so a root of $+1$ can be a unit of the algebra and a zero divisor of the symmetrisation at once. A symmetric coupling cannot invert a pure direction, and the involution it defines is one the coupling does not see.

In the probability band, the unitary group. The general plain sesqualgebra has $+1$ as its only signed value, and its roots are the unitary family $\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\tilde\Xi^{*}=e_t\}$, the unitary group $\mathrm{U}(2)$ of the spinor space, of real dimension four, described as the family $\exp(i\theta)w$ over the real unit quaternions $w$. The physical interpretation is the corpus's operator reading of the pairing: the unitaries are the reversible transformations that leave the probability form invariant, that is the gauge and symmetry group of the state space, and Observables, Gauge Generators and the Chirality of the Internal Action reads the Hermitian conjugation on which they are built. The probability band therefore produces reversible transformations as its roots of $+1$ and no phase as a root of $-1$: the band that measures has a symmetry group and no clock of its own.

The symmetrisation loses the group. In the symmetric plain sesqualgebra the square is the scalar $\langle\tilde\Xi\rangle_{*}e_t$, so the roots of $+1$ are the quadric $H=1$, the sphere $\sum_\mu\lvert\Xi_\mu\rvert^{2}=1$ of $\mathbb{C}^{4}$, of real dimension seven. The group $\mathrm{U}(2)$ has grown to the sphere: the symmetrisation forgets the vector part of the square, so it forgets the phase of the unitary and keeps only its norm. The physics is the contrast between unitarity and normalisation: the general pairing's root of $+1$ is a reversible transformation, and the symmetrised pairing's root of $+1$ is a normalised amplitude, a state vector without its phase. The corpus reads the normalised amplitude and the sphere in The Squares and the Positive Cone of the Biquaternioid Sesqualgebra and The Bloch Ball as the Trace-One Slice of the Future Light Cone.

In the quaternionic families. The quaternionic square is the central square $C(\tilde\Xi)e_t$, so the roots of $+1$ are the quadric $C=+1$ for $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SQA}$, containing the central pair $\pm e_t$ and the real vector roots $\tilde\xi$, and the family of the slopes $\lvert t\rvert>1$ with the central circle for $\mathrm{GQS}$, containing $e_t$ and $i$. The physical interpretation is again the interval: a root of $+1$ is an element of interval $+1$, that is a spacelike unit element of the material sector, and the slope ranges of the gauge sesquilinear structure are its three causal classes.

In the gauge sesquilinear family. The fourth product's square has the Krein value as its scalar part, and the roots of $+1$ are the family at the slopes $\lvert t\rvert>1$ of the parametrisation $\tilde\Xi=c(\tilde\xi+it)$ with $(t^{2}-1)\lvert c\rvert^{2}=+1$, together with the central circle $\lvert a\rvert^{2}=1$ of the multiples of $e_t$. The slope is a causal parameter: the three named values select the three ranges of $t$, that is the three causal classes of the material sector, and the roots of $+1$ are the spacelike class. The owner of the parametrisation and the indefinite metric is The Fourth Product and Its Indefinite Metric.

Where the roots of one fail. The four antisymmetric structures have no root of $+1$: their square vanishes identically in the two alternating cases or is a pure vector in the two conjugate-alternating cases, so it never takes a central value. The physical content is that a bracket is an infinitesimal transformation: it measures a failure of commutativity, and its square is not a value, so a bracket carries neither an involution nor a state. The owner of the bracket reading is The Operators of the Twelve Structures: Multiplication, Adjoint and Bracket, and the complex witness of the same absence is A Bracket Invisible on the Real Forms: the Complex Witness of the Jacobi Failure. The natural product and its symmetrisation carry only the trivial pair $\pm e_t$: their square is the central square, so a root of $+1$ is central and the equation on the centre leaves the pair alone.

The two structures outside the coincidence. The mathematics companion records one element as the witness of the whole exception, $\vec W=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\vec e_x+i\vec e_y)$. It satisfies

$$ \vec W\star\vec W=e_t \quad\text{in }\mathrm{SPS},\qquad C(\vec W)=0 , $$

so it is a root of $+1$ in $\mathrm{SPS}$ and it is a zero divisor of the algebra. The rigidity that the four bilateral structures have, a signed root being a unit, is lost by the two symmetric sesquilinear structures: the symmetrisation of a sesquilinear product forgets the vector part of the square, and a signed root can sit on the cone. In $\mathrm{SQS}$ the same element squares to $-e_t$, so it is a root of $-1$ there and it sits on the cone as well, and the pair $\mathrm{SPS}$, $\mathrm{SQS}$ is the pair of structures where the roots of one and the roots of minus one part company. The physical interpretation is a warning about the symmetrisation of a gauge structure: a reality condition can be lightlike, and the structure then offers a normalisation that cannot be undone. The owner of the truth is the general fourth product, The Biquaternioid General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (GQS) over $\mathbb{C}$, and the symmetrised structure is read as a derived one.

The two signed values are two kinds of order-two object, and the word "reflection" is used with care. A root of $-1$ is a complex structure: it rotates by a quarter turn and carries the continuous one-parameter group $\exp(\theta\tilde\Xi)$, an $SO(2)$. A root of $+1$ is an involution, its own inverse, and carries only the two signs, a $\mathbb{Z}_2$. So $0$ is a direction, $+1$ is a sign and $-1$ is a rotation. One caution is owed to The Reflection and the Rotation in Biquaternionic Form: the biquaternion algebra is the even part of the Clifford algebra and its elements turn, they are the Lorentz rotors; a reflection such as $\gamma^{0}$ is odd, it is not a biquaternion at all, and it acts on spinors by exchanging the two chiralities. A root of $+1$ inside the algebra is therefore an involution — a parity, a real structure — and not a reflection; the reflections live one grade up, outside $\mathbb{B}$.

The Idempotent as a State

The equation and the projection. An idempotent satisfies $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ in the plain product, and in a general product the same equation is read in that product. The three invariants of an idempotent are its central square, which decides whether it is a unit or a zero divisor; its complement $e_t-\tilde\Pi$, which is idempotent whenever the family is closed under the map and which is orthogonal to it, $\tilde\Pi(e_t-\tilde\Pi)=0$; and the subspace in which it lies.

The physical interpretation. A projection is a yes-no measurement, and an idempotent is the outcome of that measurement: performing the measurement twice gives the same outcome as performing it once. The equation $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ is therefore the algebraic form of a determinate state, and the nontrivial idempotents are the determinate states beside the two trivial ones. The reading is the corpus's, and it is the reason the idempotents are the objects that connect the algebra to the state space: the processes are the multiplications and the states are the idempotents, in The Monoid of Acting Maps: the Process Is the Multiplication, the State Is the Idempotent.

The trivial pair. The origin is an idempotent of every structure, the product being linear, and the unit $e_t$ is an idempotent of the eight structures that are not antisymmetric. Physically $0$ is the impossible outcome and $e_t$ is the trivial measurement, the one that keeps every state; the pair is the degenerate end of the state set.

The Plain Family: Every Root of One Gives a State

The bijection. The idempotents of the plain product are $0$, $e_t$ and the elements $\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$, and the map $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)$ is a bijection from the roots of $+1$ onto the idempotents. The classification of the roots of $+1$, given above, splits the family in three: the trivial pair from $\pm e_t$, the Hermitian family $\tfrac12(e_t+i\tilde\xi)$ over the real unit vectors, and the four-parameter non-trivial family from the pure roots, which lies in none of the four four-dimensional subspaces.

The physical interpretation. A root of $+1$ is an involution, and the bijection says that every involution gives a state. The state family of the composition is therefore as large as the family of the roots of one: the four parameters of the non-trivial idempotents are the four parameters of the pure root, and the Hermitian subfamily is the part of the family the probability band keeps. The plain idempotents are the idempotents of the algebra, and they are the states that the composition can prepare; the owner of the algebra's idempotents and of their parametrisation is The Biquaternion Vacuum as a Minimal Idempotent, and the corpus reads the associated splitting of the algebra in Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the Biquaternion Algebra (mathematics). The symmetrisation keeps the family. The symmetric plain algebra has the plain square, $\tilde\Pi\bullet\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi^{2}$, so its idempotent set is the plain one element for element. The physical interpretation is that the symmetric composition prepares the same states as the composition; the symmetrisation changes the process, not the states. The pure members of the family are zero divisors of the symmetrised block, because the vector subspace is its zero-divisor set, so a state that the symmetrisation prepares is a state the symmetrisation cannot invert: the owner is The Cone and the Massless Particles in the Biquaternioid Structures over $\mathbb{C}$.

The article is exact about the ceiling too: on two qubits the maximum is five bases, the tensor product of qubit bases supplies only three, and the Bell basis — a natural idempotent basis of $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ — is not unbiased with respect to the product basis, so the obstruction to a full set is visible in the idempotents themselves.

Chirality's projectors are idempotents, and the radicand is dimension-dependent. Bosonization in Biquaternionic Form records a fact that belongs to this companion's ledger. The chiral projectors $\tilde\Pi_{L,R}=\tfrac12(1\pm\gamma^5)$ are idempotents that split a spinor into its two chiralities, and their existence as projectors depends on the square of $\gamma^5$: the article records $(\gamma^5)^2=+I_2$ in two dimensions whereas $(\gamma^5)^2=-I_4$ in four. In two dimensions $\gamma^5$ is therefore a real structure (a root of $+1$), the chiral projectors have real eigenspaces, and an irreducible two-component spinor is a sum of two real (Majorana–Weyl) spinors; in four dimensions it is a complex structure (a root of $-1$), and the splitting is complex. The reading is that the idempotent is not a dimension-free object: the same formal projector has real or complex eigenspaces according to which radicand the chirality operator realises, and the framework's idempotent-as-state picture inherits that dependence at the level of the chirality splitting.

Physical Interpretation

  • In the plain product the equation $\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_t$ and the equation $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$ are one equation read through the halving map $\tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)$, $\tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_t$. The map turns an operation into a state and back, and it is the reason the plain state theory needs the roots of $+1$ and no others; the other structures read their state sets from their own squares.
  • A root of $+1$ is an involution: a parity, a reality condition, a discrete symmetry, or, in the pairing, a unitary transformation of the state space. The plain and the natural products produce the involutions of the module; the probability band's involution is the unitary group $\mathrm{U}(2)$; the symmetrisation replaces unitarity by normalisation, the root being a normalised amplitude without its phase; and a bracket produces no involution at all. The compactness of $\mathrm{U}(2)$ is what makes the internal labels discrete, the charge reading of Particle Types, Discrete Charge and Three-Particle Couplings.
  • An idempotent is a projection, and a projection is a determinate state: measuring twice gives the same result as measuring once. The idempotent sets are the state sets of the twelve bands.
  • The plain product carries the largest family, one state per root of one, so every involution gives a state. The trivial pair is the impossible outcome and the trivial measurement.
  • The Hermitian idempotents are the pure states of the probability band, on the boundary of the positive cone, and the Bloch ball is the convex body they generate. The vacuum is a minimal member.
  • The gauge family is the only nontrivial family of idempotents that are units. The pure state of the probability band is null for the composition; the gauge state is invertible. A determinate outcome of a measurement is not a reversible process; a determinate state of the gauge band is.
  • The central square separates the families: it vanishes on the plain and the Hermitian idempotents, which are therefore zero divisors, and it equals $e_t$ on the gauge family, which is therefore a family of units. The physics of that separation is the contrast between the band that measures a probability and the band whose value is an interval.
  • The complementation $\tilde\Pi\mapsto e_t-\tilde\Pi$ pairs a state with its orthogonal complement, and the pair is a complete two-outcome measurement. It pairs the plain family and the Hermitian family and fails for the gauge family.
  • The brackets and the interval band carry no nontrivial state: an infinitesimal transformation has no determinate state, and a state is not an interval.
  • The root of $+1$ has a sharp physical carrier: the $S$-matrix of a single mode is matrix-unitary, $\tilde S\tilde S^{*}=e_t$, which is the $\mathrm{U}(2)$ root-of-$+1$ condition and not the unit-norm Lorentz condition $\langle\tilde S\rangle_{\natural}=e_t$ (The S-Matrix in Biquaternionic Form).
  • The root of $+1$ carries the double cover. In The Two-Sheeted Cover and the Topology of Boosts in Biquaternionic Form the rotor $R(2\pi)=-e_t$ is the nontrivial central root of $+1$, while the boost manifold is contractible and the closure failure of two boosts is the Thomas–Wigner rotation.
  • The root of $+1$ is also an observable's own square. A traceless Hermitian element $\tilde H=i\vec h$ squares to the positive value $\lvert\vec h\rvert^{2}e_t$, so $\tilde H/\lvert\vec h\rvert$ is an involution, and its two eigenvalues are the split levels of The Zeeman and Stark Effects in Biquaternionic Form. The algebra gives the structure and the isotropy of the splitting, not the gyromagnetic ratio.
  • The root of $+1$ is the superselection charge — as a structure, not an element. The material–informational split is the eigenspace decomposition of the antilinear real structure $\flat=-{}^{*}$, which is an involution but, being antilinear, is not an element of the algebra and so not an algebraic root of $+1$ (The Material-Informational Split as a Superselection Structure in Biquaternionic Form). Its relative phase is unobservable, and there is no central projection, the algebra being central simple; the sign of its square fixes the KO-dimension of the triple, and here $J^{2}=+e_t$ (The Spectral Triple of the Biquaternion Algebra).
  • The idempotents are the zero-entropy states: the deviation $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde{Ϛ}^{2}-\tilde{Ϛ})=\tfrac14(\lvert\vec r\rvert^{2}-1)$ is minus the biquaternion norm, and the entropy is a monotone function of it that vanishes exactly on the idempotents. The sphere of idempotents of $\mathbb{M}_+$ is at once the pure-qubit sphere, the polarisation sphere and the boundary of the state cone.
  • Idempotency occurs at two levels. An idempotent element is a pure state; an idempotent map $\Phi^{2}=\Phi$ is a coarse-graining, and a coarse-graining increases the entropy by moving every state off the idempotent family.
  • The idempotents are the algebra's native states. The Native Qubit and the Defining Module of the Biquaternion Algebra shows that the elementary carrier is the algebra's unique simple module, and that its rays are exactly the trace-one idempotents of $\mathbb{M}_+$; the trace pairing is the Hilbert-space inner product. The state is not one convenient representative among many but the only elementary carrier's rays.
  • The idempotents are the atoms of a context. A maximal commuting set of Hermitian elements resolves into a complete family of orthogonal idempotents, and contextuality is the failure of a global $\{0,1\}$ valuation of them (Contextuality and the Kochen–Specker Theorem in Biquaternionic Form).
  • The idempotents are also the perfectly resolvable states: the Fisher information of the state with respect to the Bloch radius is the reciprocal of the biquaternion norm, so it diverges exactly on the idempotents. The pure state, the zero-entropy state, the zero-divisor state and the perfectly resolvable state are four names for one element.
  • The idempotents are the sharp effects, the extreme points of the operator interval $[0,e_t]$, and a general POVM uses interior "soft" effects, convex combinations $t\tilde\Pi_1+(1-t)\tilde\Pi_2$ of orthogonal idempotents; by Naimark's dilation theorem every such measurement is a projective one on a larger module. In The Measurement Problem in Algebraic Form the idempotent and the unitary are the two classes of acting element of one bilinear operation $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde Q_1\tilde Q\tilde Q_1^{*}$, the idempotent being the class that produces a definite outcome.
  • Only the sesquilinear product supplies the cone, and with it the state space. Why the Fourth Product Is a Gauge Structure and Not a State Space shows that the fourth product's ternary structure fails the Jordan triple identity, so no cone and no pure states can be rebuilt from it; the idempotents-as-states are the gift of the positive pairing alone.
  • The state is spinorial, so the idempotent-as-state reading is a spin-$\tfrac12$ reading: a spin-$0$ scalar lives in the centre and escapes the state module, because a simple matrix algebra has no one-dimensional module for the trivial representation.
  • An idempotent also defines a protected subspace, and an idempotent basis is a measurement basis. In Quantum Error Correction and the Stabilizer Formalism in Biquaternionic Form the code space is the image of an idempotent, a Peirce component of a commutative subalgebra of involutions; in Mutually Unbiased Bases and the Biquaternion Algebra the three maximal unbiased bases of the qubit are the eigenbases of $i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z$ and the trace pairing between idempotents of different bases is $1/d$.
  • An idempotent is a measurement resource and a unitary is a gate; the corpus keeps the two apart. In Quantum Teleportation in Biquaternionic Form the entangled resource and the Bell measurement are the four Bell idempotents of $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ — "idempotent and Hermitian, but not unitary" — while the corrections are unitary elements.
  • The roots of $-1$ build nothing here. Every state, family, bijection, projection and complete-measurement reading of this article rests on the roots of $+1$; the roots of $-1$ are the closing remark alone, and their physical interpretations — the phase, the clock, the half-turn, the Kramers sign — belong to the physics articles cited in that remark.

Summary

A root of one is an element whose square in the product at hand is the unit, and the halving map $\tilde\Pi=\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)$ carries the roots of $+1$ of the plain product onto its idempotents, with inverse $\tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_t$; the article reads the two as one subject. Physically a root of $+1$ is an involution, a reality condition, a discrete symmetry, or, in the pairing, a unitary; an idempotent is a projection, and a projection is a determinate state. The mathematics companion proves that the plain product's roots of $+1$ are the central pair $\pm e_t$ and the pure family $(\vec\Xi,\vec\Xi)=-1$, that the probability band's roots are the unitary group $\mathrm{U}(2)$, that the symmetrisation replaces them by the unit sphere, and that the natural product's roots are the level set $C=+1$; the symmetric sesquilinear structures are the two whose root of one need not be a unit, the witness $\vec W$ being a root of $+1$ in $\mathrm{SPS}$ and a root of $-1$ in $\mathrm{SQS}$. The idempotents are classified by the same map: only five of the twelve structures carry a nontrivial idempotent — the general plain structure and its symmetrisation, with the family $\tfrac12(e_t+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots of $+1$, one state per root; the general plain sesquilinear structure, with the Hermitian family $\tfrac12(e_t+i\tilde\xi)$ over the real unit vectors, the pure states of the probability band, on the boundary of the positive cone and the extremal points of the Bloch ball; and the two quaternionic sesquilinear structures, with the family $-\tfrac12e_t+\mu$ over the real vectors of squared length $\tfrac34$, the states of the gauge band. The central square separates the families: it vanishes on the plain and the Hermitian idempotents, which are therefore zero divisors, and it equals $e_t$ on the gauge family, which is therefore a family of units. The physics of that separation is the contrast between the band that measures a probability, whose determinate states are null and cannot be composed, and the band whose value is an interval, whose determinate states are invertible. The complementation $\tilde\Pi\mapsto e_t-\tilde\Pi$ pairs a state with its orthogonal complement and makes a complete two-outcome measurement; it pairs the plain family and the Hermitian family and fails for the gauge family. The four brackets carry the origin alone, because an infinitesimal transformation has no determinate state, and the interval band carries only the trivial pair, because a state is not an interval. The roots of $-1$ are the closing remark and are used nowhere: for the bilateral structures they follow from the roots of $+1$ by multiplication by the central imaginary, for the definite sesquilinear structures and the brackets they are empty, and for the two quaternionic sesquilinear structures they are nonempty, with the witness $\vec W$ on the cone for the symmetric gauge structure.

Hermitian Clifford Algebras

Introduction

A Clifford algebra is the algebra generated by a vector space under the relation $x^{2} = -q(x)$ for a quadratic form $q$. A Hermitian form is a sesquilinear object, and its diagonal is a quadratic function only after the base field is fixed and the involution is chosen; the relation cannot be imposed on the sesquilinear form itself. The object of this article is the resolution of that mismatch: the Hermitian Clifford algebra of a Hermitian form $h$ is the Clifford algebra of the real part of $h$, carrying the Hermitian structure induced by the dagger.

The resolution has a shape. Write the Hermitian form over $(\mathbb{C},\varsigma)$ as

$$ h = g + \mathrm{i}\,\omega, \qquad g = \operatorname{Re} h, \quad \omega = \operatorname{Im} h, $$

with $g$ a symmetric $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear form and $\omega$ an alternating one. The Clifford relation uses $g$ alone, $xy + yx = -2g(x,y)$, and the imaginary part $\omega$ is the symplectic form that the relation cannot see; the pair $(g,\omega)$ with the complex structure is the triple of a Kähler structure, and this is the algebraic content of the observation that a sesquilinear form gives a Clifford algebra through its real part and a symplectic form through its imaginary part. The complexified Clifford algebra then carries the dagger of Hermitian Algebras, $x^{\dagger} = \sigma(\alpha(x^{r}))$, and the Hermitian form of the layer, the form of the dagger $\operatorname{Sc}(x^{\dagger}y)$ of The Blade Form and the Hermitian Structure with Hermitian Adjoint, which is the parity twist of the blade form $\operatorname{Sc}(x^{r}y)$: it is a Hermitian algebra in the sense of the category.

The infinite-dimensional case — the Clifford algebra of a Hermitian space of infinite dimension, which is the CAR algebra — is named and deferred to Infinite-Dimensional Clifford Algebras and CAR with Inner Conjugation in Part II. The Clifford algebra as an algebra and its classification are Clifford Algebras and Clifford Algebras in Finite Dimensions; the dagger and the blade form are Hermitian Algebras and The Blade Form and the Hermitian Structure with Hermitian Adjoint. Throughout, $V$ is a finite-dimensional complex vector space with a non-degenerate Hermitian form $h$, $g = \operatorname{Re} h$, $\omega = \operatorname{Im} h$, and all Clifford algebras are over the reals.

The Two Parts of a Hermitian Form

Proposition. Let $h$ be a Hermitian form on $V$ over $(\mathbb{C},\varsigma)$ with $\varsigma$ the conjugation. Then $h = g + \mathrm{i}\omega$ with $g$ symmetric real-bilinear, $g(x,y) = \operatorname{Re}h(x,y)$, and $\omega$ real-alternating, $\omega(x,y) = \operatorname{Im}h(x,y)$, and the two are tied to the complex structure $J$ by $g(x,y) = \omega(Jx,y)$.

Proof. $g$ is real-bilinear and $g(x,y) = \operatorname{Re}h(x,y) = \operatorname{Re}\varsigma(h(y,x)) = g(y,x)$; $\omega$ is alternating because $\omega(x,x) = \operatorname{Im}h(x,x) = 0$ since the diagonal of a Hermitian form is real. The last identity is the computation $\operatorname{Re}h(x,y) = \operatorname{Im}\bigl(\mathrm{i}h(x,y)\bigr) = \operatorname{Im}h(\mathrm{i}x,y) = \omega(Jx,y)$.

Corollary. The Hermitian form is a real symmetric form together with a real alternating form, and its diagonal is the diagonal of $g$: the whole imaginary part is invisible to the diagonal, which is the content of Polarisation and the Hermitian Square read over $\mathbb{C}$.

The Clifford Algebra of the Real Part

Definition. The Hermitian Clifford algebra of $(V,h)$ is the real Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V_{\mathbb{R}}, g)$ of the real part of $h$, with the $\mathbb{C}$-linear extension to $V$ and the Hermitian structure of the next section.

Proposition. A sesquilinear form satisfies no Clifford relation: the relation $x^{2} = -h(x,x)$ is not $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear in $x$ and cannot be imposed. The relation $xy + yx = -2g(x,y)$ is imposed by the real part alone, and the imaginary part $\omega$ satisfies no Clifford relation; it is the symplectic form of the layer.

Proof. $h(\mathrm{i}x,\mathrm{i}x) = \varsigma(\mathrm{i})\,\mathrm{i}\,h(x,x) = h(x,x)$ while a quadratic relation requires $(\mathrm{i}x)^{2} = \mathrm{i}^{2}x^{2} = -x^{2}$, so the scalar to which $x^{2}$ is equated must be real-bilinear and not sesquilinear. The alternating form $\omega$ gives $\omega(x,x) = 0$ and therefore the relation $xy+yx = -2\omega(x,y)$ would read $2x^{2} = 0$, the exterior algebra.

Corollary. The Hermitian Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V,g)$ is the Clifford algebra of a real form of dimension $2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V$; for $g$ positive definite of real dimension $2n$ it is $\mathrm{Cl}_{2n}(\mathbb{R})$, and its complexification is $\mathrm{End}(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{n})$, the algebra of the exterior algebra of $V$.

The Hermitian Structure of the Algebra

Definition. On $\mathrm{Cl}(V,g)_{\mathbb{C}} = \mathrm{Cl}(V,g)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}$ the dagger is $x^{\dagger} = \sigma(\alpha(x^{r}))$, the conjugate reversion of Hermitian Algebras, with $\sigma$ the conjugation of the complex scalars.

Proposition. The complexified Hermitian Clifford algebra with the dagger and the form of the dagger

$$ \langle x, y\rangle_{\dagger} = \operatorname{Sc}(x^{\dagger}y) = \sum_{I} (-1)^{|I|}\Bigl(\prod_{i\in I}e_{i}^{2}\Bigr)\bar{a}_{I}b_{I} $$

is a sesqualgebra with a form in the sense of Sesqualgebras with a Form; the form is Hermitian and $\varsigma$-sesquilinear, its Gram matrix in the blade basis is diagonal with entries $\pm1$, and it is non-degenerate because $g$ is. It is definite exactly in the extreme cases: it is positive definite when $g$ is negative definite, while the conjugate-reversion form $\operatorname{Sc}(x^{r,\sigma}y) = \sum_{I}(\prod_{i\in I}e_{i}^{2})\bar{a}_{I}b_{I}$ is the positive definite one when $g$ is positive definite.

Proof. The dagger is a conjugate-linear anti-automorphism of order two and the compatibility $\langle xy, z\rangle_{\dagger} = \langle y, x^{\dagger}z\rangle_{\dagger}$ is the cyclicity of the scalar part against the dagger. The diagonal is the computation of The Blade Form and the Hermitian Structure with Hermitian Adjoint: the dagger inserts the parity sign $(-1)^{|I|}$ relative to the blade form $\operatorname{Sc}(x^{r}y)$, whose diagonal is $\prod_{i\in I}e_{i}^{2}$. The definiteness is the definite case, so it is named and not developed here.

Corollary. The Hermitian Clifford algebra is a Hermitian algebra and a sesqualgebra with a form; the two structures are the same data, and the operator theory of the category applies to it with no change: the adjoint, the unitary group, the skew operators and the congruence classification are the articles of this category, and the explicit Clifford computations of the dagger are The Blade Form and the Hermitian Structure with Hermitian Adjoint.

The Unitary and the Spin Groups

Proposition. The unitary slice $U = \{x : x^{\dagger}x = 1\}$ of the Hermitian Clifford algebra contains the Pin group of the real form, and its even part contains the Spin group:

$$ \mathrm{Pin}(g) = \{x \in \mathrm{Cl}(V,g)^{*} : x^{\dagger}x = \pm 1, \ xVx^{-1} \subseteq V\}, \qquad \mathrm{Spin}(g) = \mathrm{Pin}(g)\cap \mathrm{Cl}(V,g)^{0} . $$

Proof. For a vector $v$ with $g(v,v) = 1$ one has $v^{\dagger} = v$ and $v^{2} = -1$, so $v^{\dagger}v = v^{2} = -1$; the products of such vectors generate the Pin group and satisfy the slice condition up to sign. The even elements are the products of an even number of vectors, and the sandwich $xvx^{-1}$ preserves $V$ by the defining property. The construction is that of The Clifford, Pin and Spin Groups with Signed Inner Conjugation and Versors, Rotors and the Sandwich Action with Signed Inner Conjugation, read with the Hermitian dagger.

Corollary. The norm of a Clifford algebra element is multiplicative on the Pin group and on the Spin group, and it fails on the general element from dimension three on, as Hermitian Forms over Algebras and Norms records; the two groups are the locus of multiplicativity that the algebra structure provides, and the quadratic space $V$ with the action of the spin group is the spin representation of the layer, whose one-sided reading is Left Multiplication and the Clifford Module Structure.

The Infinite-Dimensional Case and CAR

When $V$ is a complex Hilbert space and $h$ its positive definite form, the Clifford algebra of the real part of $h$ with its completion is the CAR algebra, the algebra of the canonical anticommutation relations $\{a(f), a^{*}(g)\} = h(f,g)$, $\{a(f),a(g)\} = 0$. The infinite-dimensional construction, the Fock representation, the completion of the algebra and the modular theory that goes with it are Part II: Infinite-Dimensional Clifford Algebras and CAR with Inner Conjugation and The Euclidean Form, the Norm and the Completion on a Clifford Algebra. The algebraic skeleton — the relation, the dagger, the blade form, the spin group — is the finite-dimensional content of this article, and the completion adds the analysis.

Examples

The Exterior Algebra

For $V = \mathbb{C}^{n}$ with the standard positive definite Hermitian form, $g$ is the Euclidean form of $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(V,g)_{\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathrm{End}(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{n})$. The dagger is the conjugate transpose in the basis of the blades, the conjugate-reversion form is the Frobenius form of the endomorphism algebra and the form of the dagger is its parity twist, and the spin group is $\mathrm{Spin}(2n)$ acting on the exterior algebra by the spin representation.

The Biquaternions

For $V = \mathbb{C}^{1}$ with the standard form, the real part $g$ is the positive definite form of $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{H}$; with the complex scalars the algebra is $\mathbb{B} = M_{2}(\mathbb{C})$, the biquaternion algebra, and the dagger, the blade form, the unitary slice and the Lorentzian companion form are the biquaternion articles Hermitian Algebras, The Form on the Biquaternion Algebra as a General Plain Sesquilinear Form and The Hermitian Sandwich in the Biquaternion Algebra with Hermitian Adjoint.

Summary

  • A complex Hermitian form splits as $h = g + \mathrm{i}\omega$ into a real symmetric part and a real alternating part, tied by the complex structure, $g(x,y) = \omega(Jx,y)$.
  • The Hermitian Clifford algebra is the Clifford algebra of $g = \operatorname{Re}h$: the imaginary part satisfies no Clifford relation, and the relation $x^{2} = -h(x,x)$ cannot be imposed on a sesquilinear form.
  • The complexified algebra carries the dagger $\sigma(\alpha(x^{r}))$ and the form of the dagger $\operatorname{Sc}(x^{\dagger}y)$ (the parity twist of the blade form $\operatorname{Sc}(x^{r}y)$), which makes it a sesqualgebra with a form and a Hermitian algebra.
  • The unitary slice contains the Pin group and the Spin group, on which the norm is multiplicative; the multiplicativity fails on the general element from dimension three on.
  • The infinite-dimensional case is the CAR algebra of a Hilbert space and belongs to Part II; the finite-dimensional algebraic skeleton is this article, and the explicit computations are The Blade Form and the Hermitian Structure with Hermitian Adjoint.

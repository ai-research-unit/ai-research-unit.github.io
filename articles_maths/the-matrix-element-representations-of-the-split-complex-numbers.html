The Matrix Element Representations of the Split-Complex Numbers

Introduction

This article reads the split-complex numbers in the real matrix algebras: the $2\times2$ model, in which an element becomes the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a&a'\\a'&a\end{pmatrix}$ of hyperbolic type, and the $4\times4$ model, the doubling of the first, together with the commutant of each and the equivalence of the two. The $2\times2$ model exhibits the algebra inside $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ as the two-dimensional subalgebra $\{aI+a'J\}$ with $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $J^2=I$; the $4\times4$ model writes the same element as a block-diagonal matrix with two copies of the $2\times2$ on the diagonal and adds nothing. The two models are the two-dimensional real shadow of the complex matrix representations, and their degeneracy — the $2\times2$ is already the regular representation, and the $4\times4$ only doubles it — is the matrix face of the commutativity of $\mathbb{D}$.

The element reading of the $2\times2$ matrix, its Cayley form, its multiplicativity, the determinant as the norm, the trace, the characteristic polynomial, the idempotent diagonalisation and the identification of the unit-norm elements with the hyperbolic rotations are Split-Complex Regular Element Representation, which owns them; this article states the model and cites that article rather than repeating it. The two algebraic norms are The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complex Space; the invertibility criterion and the group of units are Split-Complex Norm and Invertibility. The degeneracy of the models is The Specificities of the Split-Complex Algebra, and the general representation theory is Split-Complex Element Representations.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2-1)$, basis $1,j$, $j^2=+1$; a general element is $A=a+ja'$; the conjugate is $\bar A=a-ja'$; the idempotents are $\Pi_{1,2}=\tfrac12(1\pm j)$ with $A=A_+\Pi_1+A_-\Pi_2$ and $A_\pm=a\pm a'$; the norm is $N(A)=A\bar A=a^2-a'^2$. In this article $M_2(\mathbb{D})=\{aI+a'J\}$ denotes the image of the $2\times2$ model inside $M_2(\mathbb{R})$, and $M_4(\mathbb{R})$ the ambient algebra of the $4\times4$ model.

The $2\times2$ Model

Definition. The $2\times2$ matrix model is the $\mathbb{R}$-linear map

$$ \mathsf{M}_2 : \mathbb{D} \longrightarrow M_2(\mathbb{R}), \qquad A = a+ja' \longmapsto \mathsf{M}_2(A) = \begin{pmatrix} a & a' \\ a' & a \end{pmatrix}, $$

fixed by its values on the basis, $\mathsf{M}_2(1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathsf{M}_2(j)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$.

Theorem. $\mathsf{M}_2$ is an injective algebra homomorphism; its image is the commutative subalgebra

$$ M_2(\mathbb{D}) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & a' \\ a' & a \end{pmatrix} : a, a' \in \mathbb{R} \right\} = \{aI+a'J\} \cong \mathbb{R}[J]/(J^2-I), $$

of real dimension two, and $\mathbb{D}\cong M_2(\mathbb{D})$ as algebras.

Proof. The two basis images satisfy $I^2=I$ and $J^2=I$, so the assignment extends to an algebra homomorphism; the matrix of a general element is the displayed one, and the map is injective because $a$ and $a'$ are read off the first column. The image is $\{aI+a'J\}$, of real dimension two, isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}[J]/(J^2-I)\cong\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2-1)\cong\mathbb{D}$.

The matrices of the image are the real matrices of hyperbolic type: symmetric, with equal diagonal entries $a$ and equal off-diagonal entries $a'$. Against the complex field the only difference is the sign of the square of the generator: $\mathbb{C}$ embeds as $\{aI+a'K\}$ with $K=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $K^2=-I$, and $\mathbb{D}$ as $\{aI+a'J\}$ with $J^2=+I$, the sign $j^2=+1$ against $i^2=-1$ appearing as the sign of the square of the generator matrix.

The determinant and the trace. For $A=a+ja'$,

$$ \det \mathsf{M}_2(A) = a^2-a'^2 = N(A) = H(A), \qquad \operatorname{Tr} \mathsf{M}_2(A) = 2a = A_+ + A_- . $$

The determinant is the second algebraic norm $H(A)=A\bar A$ of The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complex Space, and the trace is twice the scalar part, the sum of the two eigenvalues. The first algebraic norm $B(A)=A^2$ appears not as a determinant or a trace but as the square of the matrix, $\mathsf{M}_2(A)^2=\mathsf{M}_2(A^2)$, which is the multiplicativity of $\mathsf{M}_2$ read on the square. The eigenvalues of $\mathsf{M}_2(A)$ are the idempotent coordinates $A_+=a+a'$ and $A_-=a-a'$, both real, and the characteristic polynomial factors as $(\lambda-A_+)(\lambda-A_-)$.

Invertibility. The matrix $\mathsf{M}_2(A)$ is invertible exactly when $\det\mathsf{M}_2(A)=N(A)\neq0$; the elements of $\mathbb{D}$ therefore correspond to the invertible matrices of hyperbolic type, and $\mathsf{M}_2(A)^{-1}=\mathsf{M}_2(A^{-1})=\frac{1}{N(A)}\begin{pmatrix}a&-a'\\-a'&a\end{pmatrix}$. The criterion and the group of units are Split-Complex Norm and Invertibility.

The idempotent diagonalisation and the hyperbola. In the idempotent basis the model is diagonal, $\mathsf{M}_2(A)\big|_{\{\Pi_1,\Pi_2\}}=\operatorname{diag}(A_+,A_-)$, and the unit-norm elements $N(A)=1$ are the matrices of determinant one in $M_2(\mathbb{D})$, the two branches of the hyperbola $a^2-a'^2=1$, the identity component of which is the group of hyperbolic rotations $R_j(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh t&\sinh t\\\sinh t&\cosh t\end{pmatrix}$. The diagonalisation, the eigenvalue reading and the identification of the unit-norm group are Split-Complex Regular Element Representation, and the matrix group is read as the group of linear maps preserving the form in The Versors and the Orthogonal Group of the Split-Complex Numbers.

The $4\times4$ Model

Definition. The $4\times4$ matrix model is the left regular representation of $\mathbb{D}$ on the doubled coefficient space $\mathbb{D}\oplus\mathbb{D}$, written in the basis $(1,j,1,j)$:

$$ \mathsf{M}_4 : \mathbb{D} \longrightarrow M_4(\mathbb{R}), \qquad \mathsf{M}_4(A) = \begin{pmatrix} \mathsf{M}_2(A) & 0 \\ 0 & \mathsf{M}_2(A) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a & a' & 0 & 0 \\ a' & a & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & a & a' \\ 0 & 0 & a' & a \end{pmatrix}. $$

It is the block-diagonal sum of two copies of the $2\times2$ model.

Theorem. $\mathsf{M}_4$ is an injective algebra homomorphism, and

$$ \det \mathsf{M}_4(A) = N(A)^2, \qquad \operatorname{Tr} \mathsf{M}_4(A) = 4a = 2\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(A). $$

Proof. $\mathsf{M}_4$ is built from $\mathsf{M}_2$ blockwise, so multiplicativity and injectivity follow from the $2\times2$ case. The determinant of a block-diagonal matrix is the product of the determinants of its blocks, whence $\det\mathsf{M}_4(A)=\det\mathsf{M}_2(A)^2=N(A)^2$, and the trace is the sum of the traces of the blocks, $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_4(A)=2\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(A)=4a$.

The model is the regular representation of $\mathbb{D}$ on the free module of rank two: it is the direct sum of two copies of the regular module, and its image is the subalgebra

$$ M_4(\mathbb{D})_L = \left\{ \operatorname{diag}(X, X) : X \in M_2(\mathbb{D}) \right\} $$

of real dimension two inside the sixteen of $M_4(\mathbb{R})$. Every entry is a single coefficient of $A$ up to sign, in each block exactly as in the $2\times2$ model, and the two blocks carry the same element.

The Commutants

The two models are told apart by their commutants, the matrices that commute with the whole image.

The $2\times2$ commutant. The commutant of $M_2(\mathbb{D})=\{aI+a'J\}$ in $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ is $M_2(\mathbb{D})$ itself: a matrix $\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\r&s\end{pmatrix}$ commuting with $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ satisfies $q=r$ and $p=s$, hence is $pI+qJ\in M_2(\mathbb{D})$, and every element of $M_2(\mathbb{D})$ commutes with the whole (commutative) image. So the $2\times2$ image is its own commutant and its own bicommutant, a maximal commutative subalgebra of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ of dimension two. This is Split-Complex Regular Element Representation.

The $4\times4$ commutant. A matrix $C=\begin{pmatrix}P&Q\\R&S\end{pmatrix}$, written in $2\times2$ blocks, commutes with every $\operatorname{diag}(X,X)$, $X\in M_2(\mathbb{D})$, exactly when $PX=XP$, $QX=XQ$, $RX=XR$ and $SX=XS$ for all such $X$, that is exactly when each of the four blocks lies in the commutant of $M_2(\mathbb{D})$, namely in $M_2(\mathbb{D})$. Hence

$$ \{C \in M_4(\mathbb{R}) : C\,\mathsf{M}_4(A)=\mathsf{M}_4(A)\,C \text{ for all } A\} = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} P & Q \\ R & S \end{pmatrix} : P,Q,R,S \in M_2(\mathbb{D}) \right\} \cong M_2(M_2(\mathbb{D})) \cong M_2(\mathbb{D}), $$

of real dimension eight, four times the dimension two of the image. The commutant of the $4\times4$ image is therefore strictly larger than the image, unlike the $2\times2$ case, and it is the ring of $2\times2$ matrices over the commutative ring $\mathbb{D}$: the doubling of the module and the doubling of the commutant are the same fact.

model image commutant dimension of the commutant
$2\times2$ $M_2(\mathbb{D})=\{aI+a'J\}$ $M_2(\mathbb{D})$ itself $2$
$4\times4$ $\{\operatorname{diag}(X,X)\}$ $M_2(\mathbb{D})$-valued blocks $\cong M_2(\mathbb{D})$ $8$

The Two Models Compared

The $4\times4$ model is the doubling of the $2\times2$ model and carries no information the $2\times2$ does not: it is the direct sum of two copies of it, its image is the diagonal of two copies of the $2\times2$ image, and every invariant doubles in the predictable way,

$$ \det\mathsf{M}_4(A) = \bigl(\det\mathsf{M}_2(A)\bigr)^2, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_4(A) = 2\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(A) . $$

So the two models are equivalent as representations of $\mathbb{D}$: each is a direct sum of copies of the regular module, the $2\times2$ being one copy and the $4\times4$ two, and the $4\times4$ extends the $2\times2$ by exactly the two nothing-adding dimensions of the second copy. The essential model is the $2\times2$; the $4\times4$ is recorded only to show that it adds nothing.

Remark. The relation between the two models here is a doubling and not a second structure: because $\mathbb{D}$ is commutative, its regular representation is two-dimensional and its $2\times2$ matrix is symmetric, so the "left" and "right" regular representations coincide and there is no second copy to compare with. In the four-dimensional algebra the regular representation is four-dimensional, the $4\times4$ matrix is block diagonal with two blocks each conjugate to the $2\times2$ one, and the two blocks are the two minimal ideals; here the two blocks are two copies of the whole algebra, since the minimal ideals are one-dimensional. The degeneracy is the one recorded in The Specificities of the Split-Complex Algebra.

Summary

The split-complex algebra is realised in the real matrix algebras by two models. The $2\times2$ model sends $A=a+ja'$ to the hyperbolic matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a&a'\\a'&a\end{pmatrix}$, an injective homomorphism onto the commutative subalgebra $M_2(\mathbb{D})=\{aI+a'J\}$ with $J^2=I$; the determinant is the second algebraic norm $N(A)=a^2-a'^2$, the trace is $2a$, the square of the matrix is the first algebraic norm $A^2$, the eigenvalues are the idempotent coordinates, and the unit-norm elements are the hyperbolic rotations. The model is the regular representation of $\mathbb{D}$ on itself, and its commutant in $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ is the image itself.

The $4\times4$ model writes the same element block-diagonally with two copies of the $2\times2$ matrix on the diagonal; it is the regular representation on $\mathbb{D}\oplus\mathbb{D}$, its determinant is $N(A)^2$, its trace is $4a$, and its commutant in $M_4(\mathbb{R})$ is the ring $M_2(\mathbb{D})$ of $2\times2$ matrices over $\mathbb{D}$, of real dimension eight. The two models are equivalent, the $4\times4$ being the doubling of the $2\times2$ and adding two dimensions in which no new structure appears; the essential model is the $2\times2$, and the doubling is the matrix expression of the commutativity of $\mathbb{D}$.

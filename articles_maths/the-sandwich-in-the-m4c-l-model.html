The Sandwich in the $M_4(\mathbb{C})_L$ Model

Introduction

The action of a biquaternion on the algebra is the sandwich $$ \Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\tilde Q\tilde X\tilde Q^{*}. $$ In the regular model it is the congruence $X\mapsto MXM^{\dagger}$ on $4\times4$ matrices. On the anti-Hermitian half the congruence is a Lorentz transformation on a four-dimensional real subspace; on the Hermitian half it is a congruence of a real quadratic form, governed by Sylvester's law of inertia. The two readings differ by the central factor $i$ that exchanges the halves.

This article develops the sandwich in the regular model. It is the $4\times4$ counterpart of The Sandwich in the $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Model.

Conventions. The algebra and the model are those of $M_4(\mathbb{C})_L$ Model : The Product, the Trace and the Determinant. The sandwich is $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\tilde Q\tilde X\tilde Q^{*}$, and its matrix is $\Theta_M(X)=MXM^{\dagger}$. The two sectors are $\mathbb{M}_+$, the Hermitian elements, and $\mathbb{M}_-$, the anti-Hermitian elements. The norm is $N=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$, and $\det M=N^2$. Write $r=\lvert N\rvert^{1/2}=\lvert\det M\rvert^{1/4}$.

Boundaries. The sandwich as an operator is Operators of the Polar Word: The Common Framework; the polar decomposition is $M_4(\mathbb{C})_L$ Representation : The Polar Decomposition of a Matrix; the action on the subspaces is The Polar Decomposition and Remarkable Subspaces; the $2\times2$ version is The Sandwich in the $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Model.

The Congruence

Proposition. For invertible $M$ and any $X$, $$ \Theta_M(X)=MXM^{\dagger} $$ is the image of the sandwich $\Theta_{\tilde Q}(\tilde X)$ under $\mathsf{M}_4$. It is complex linear in $X$ and invertible. It is not an algebra automorphism in general: the identity $$ \Theta_M(XY)=\Theta_M(X)\,\Theta_M(Y) $$ holds exactly when $M$ is a scalar multiple of a $*$-unitary matrix, because the two sides differ by the factor $M^{\dagger}M$.

Proof. The map is the composite of left multiplication by $M$, right multiplication by $M^{\dagger}$, and the identification of the two; it is the image of the algebra sandwich, which is a linear isomorphism because $\tilde Q$ is a unit. The matrix identities follow from the algebra identities. $\square$

The two halves are preserved. If $X^{\dagger}=X$, then $$ (MXM^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=MX^{\dagger}M^{\dagger}=MXM^{\dagger}, $$ so the Hermitian half is preserved. If $X^{\dagger}=-X$, then the image is anti-Hermitian, so the anti-Hermitian half is preserved. The two statements are the matrix form of the fact that the sandwich preserves both sectors for every invertible element.

The null cone is preserved. Taking determinants, $$ \det(MXM^{\dagger})=\lvert\det M\rvert^2\det X=\lvert N\rvert^4\det X, $$ so the zero set of the determinant is preserved. In the algebra, $$ N(\Theta_Q(X))=\lvert N(Q)\rvert^2N(X), $$ and the zero divisors are mapped to zero divisors.

The Anti-Hermitian Half as Four-Vectors

The four-vector. An anti-Hermitian element is written $$ X=i a e_t+\vec b\cdot\vec e, \qquad a\in\mathbb{R},\quad\vec b\in\mathbb{R}^3, $$ with $\vec b\cdot\vec e=b_1\vec e_1+b_2\vec e_2+b_3\vec e_3$. Its norm is $$ N(X)=-a^2+\lvert\vec b\rvert^2, $$ the Lorentzian quadratic form of signature $(3,1)$. The four elements $i e_t,\vec e_1,\vec e_2,\vec e_3$ are a real basis of the anti-Hermitian subspace, and the norm is the Minkowski form on that space.

The regular matrix reading. In the regular model $X$ becomes a $4\times4$ anti-Hermitian matrix, and its determinant is $$ \det X=N(X)^2=(-a^2+\lvert\vec b\rvert^2)^2, $$ the square of the Minkowski form. The four-dimensional real subspace is the image of the anti-Hermitian elements; on it the congruence acts as a Lorentz transformation, while on the full sixteen-dimensional space it preserves the quartic determinant.

The unit-norm action. Let $\lvert N(Q)\rvert=1$, so $\lvert\det M\rvert=1$. Then $$ \det(MXM^{\dagger})=\det X, $$ and the map $X\mapsto MXM^{\dagger}$ is a linear isometry of the four-dimensional real subspace. It preserves the cone $N(X)=0$. The group $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is connected, the map is continuous, and its kernel is $\{\pm\mathrm I_4\}$; both groups have real dimension six. The image is the identity component of the Lorentz group: $$ SL_2(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow SO^{+}(1,3),\qquad Q\longmapsto\Theta_Q, $$ a twofold cover.

The two factors. A unit-norm element factors as $Q=H_bU_r$ with $H_b$ Hermitian positive of norm one and $U_r$ a rotor. The sandwich factorises: $$ \Theta_Q=\Theta_{H_b}\circ\Theta_{U_r}. $$ The rotor acts by rotating $\vec b$ and fixing $a$; the boost acts by a hyperbolic transformation mixing $a$ and the component of $\vec b$ along its axis, and fixing the orthogonal component. The two actions are the rotation and the boost of the Lorentz group, and their product is a general proper orthochronous transformation.

The general element. For $M=r\zeta\mathsf{M}_4(U)$ with $\lvert N(U)\rvert=1$, $$ \Theta_M=r^2\,\Theta_U, $$ because the central scalars commute and $\zeta\bar\zeta=1$. The image is a conformal transformation: a Lorentz transformation composed with the dilation of ratio $r^2$ on the matrix, $r^4$ on the norm and $r^8$ on the $4\times4$ determinant. The image of the unit group is the conformal group $CO^{+}(1,3)=\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cdot SO^{+}(1,3)$.

The Hermitian Half as a Congruence

The congruence. For Hermitian $X$ and invertible $M$, the matrix $MXM^{\dagger}$ is Hermitian. It is a congruence of $X$ by $M$, not a similarity: the two factors are $M$ and $M^{\dagger}$, not $M$ and $M^{-1}$.

Sylvester's law. A congruence by an invertible matrix preserves the rank, the inertia and the signature of a Hermitian form. Hence $\Theta_M$ preserves the rank, the inertia and the causal type of $X$: a positive definite matrix stays positive definite, a matrix of signature $(1,3)$ stays of signature $(1,3)$, and a null matrix stays null.

The determinant scaling. From the congruence, $$ \det(MXM^{\dagger})=\lvert\det M\rvert^2\det X=\lvert N\rvert^4\det X=r^8\det X, $$ because $\lvert\det M\rvert=\lvert N\rvert^2=r^4$. The determinant is not preserved; it is scaled by the eighth power of the modulus $r$. The sign of the determinant is preserved, so the two cones of positive and negative determinant are each preserved.

The Minkowski reading. On the four-dimensional Hermitian subspace, write $X=a e_t+i\vec b\cdot\vec e$ with $a,b$ real; the norm is $$ N(X)=a^2-\lvert\vec b\rvert^2, $$ of signature $(1,3)$. The congruence is a linear transformation of the four-dimensional space preserving the form up to the factor $r^4$; after rescaling by $r^{-2}$ it is a Lorentz transformation. The Hermitian and anti-Hermitian readings are the same Lorentz action composed with the multiplication by $i$.

The positivity cone. The positive semidefinite matrices form a cone inside the Hermitian half. The congruence preserves it, because $MXM^{\dagger}$ is positive semidefinite whenever $X$ is and $M$ is invertible. Its boundary is the null cone of the determinant, and the cone is the positivity cone of the Hermitian form.

The Two Halves Are One Action

Multiplication by $i$. The central element $i$ commutes with every element, and multiplication by $i$ exchanges the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian halves: $$ X\in\mathbb{M}_+\iff iX\in\mathbb{M}_-. $$ The sandwich commutes with this exchange, because $i$ is central. The two halves are therefore one action read in two real forms, and the Lorentz transformation on the anti-Hermitian half is the same as the congruence on the Hermitian half composed with the exchange.

The invariant form. The determinant is the invariant of the congruence up to the conformal factor. On the four-dimensional subspaces it is the square of the Minkowski form; on the full sixteen-dimensional space it is the quartic invariant. The two readings have the same conformal group and the same causal structure.

The kernel and the image. The kernel of the action on the anti-Hermitian subspace is $\{\pm\mathrm I_4\}$, the same as in the $2\times2$ model. The image is the proper orthochronous Lorentz group for the unit-norm elements, and the conformal group for the general elements.

Which Subspaces the Sandwich Preserves

The general rule. Every sandwich preserves the two halves and the null cone. It preserves the other four remarkable subspaces exactly when the element is a central multiple of a real unit quaternion.

The centre. A central element $\lambda e_t$ is sent to $\lambda QQ^{*}$. This is central exactly when $QQ^{*}$ is central, which happens for the central multiples of the real unit quaternions and not in general. For a $*$-unitary $Q$, $QQ^{*}=e_t$ and the centre is fixed pointwise.

The vector subspace. A traceless matrix is sent to a matrix whose trace is $\operatorname{Tr}(MXM^{\dagger})$, which need not vanish. The vector subspace is preserved exactly when the sandwich is a similarity of the trace form, that is when $M$ is a central multiple of a real unit quaternion.

The quaternion and anti-quaternion subspaces. The condition $X=\epsilon\bar X\epsilon^{-1}$ is not preserved by a general congruence; it is preserved when $M$ commutes with the conjugate-linear structure, which is the same condition.

The table. The four non-sector subspaces are preserved by the sandwich of a central multiple of a real unit quaternion, and by no other element. The two sectors are preserved by every invertible element. The statement is the matrix form of the table of The Polar Decomposition and Remarkable Subspaces, §Which Subspaces the Sandwich Preserves.

Summary

The sandwich is the congruence $X\mapsto MXM^{\dagger}$ on the regular matrices. It preserves the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian halves and the null cone. On the four-dimensional anti-Hermitian subspace it is a Lorentz transformation; on the four-dimensional Hermitian subspace it is a congruence preserving inertia. The determinant scales by $\lvert\det M\rvert^2=\lvert N\rvert^4=r^8$, and the algebra norm scales by $r^4$. The unit-norm group acts as $SO^{+}(1,3)$ through a twofold cover, and the general unit group acts conformally.

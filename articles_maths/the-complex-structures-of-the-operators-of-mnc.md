# __The Complex Structures of the Operators of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$, including Lie Algebras and Lie Groups__

## Introduction

**The question.** Which complex structures does the operator space carry, and which of them are compatible with the algebra, the module structure, the metric or the real forms?

**The answer in one line.** The algebra has one complex structure, $J(X)=iX$; the real space of operators has $2n^8$ complex structures, of which $n^4(n^4-1)$ are orthogonal; the left module has a complex structure commuting with every $L_A$ exactly when $n$ is even, and then exactly one.

**Why the article is needed.** At $n=2$ the three quaternionic units $i,j,k$ give a triple of complex structures on a four-real-dimensional space, and the biquaternion series could treat them as coordinates. At general $n$ the carrier is $2n^2$-real-dimensional and the operator space $2n^4$-real-dimensional, and the count of complex structures is enormous unless a compatibility is imposed. The biquaternion triple is a structure of the four-dimensional case, not a general phenomenon.

**Boundaries.** The classification questions are *The Classification of the Structures of the Operators of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$*; the real forms are *The Real Structures of the Operators of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$, including Lie Algebras and Lie Groups*; the module is *The Matrix Algebra $M_n(\mathbb{C})$*.

**Conventions.** A complex structure on a real vector space is a real linear $J$ with $J^2=-\mathrm I$. $N=n^2$ is the carrier dimension, so $\operatorname{End}(M_n)$ is $\mathbb{R}^{2N^2}$. The Hilbert–Schmidt metric is the real part of $\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(X^{*}Y)$.

## The Complex Structure of the Algebra

**Proposition.** The complex structures on $M_N(\mathbb{C})$ that are algebra automorphisms are $\pm i$ times the identity, so there is one, and it is the multiplication by $i$ already present.

*Proof.* An algebra automorphism of $M_N(\mathbb{C})$ is inner, $X\mapsto SXS^{-1}$, and it is complex-linear by definition. A complex structure $J$ that is also a complex-linear automorphism commutes with the scalar action, hence lies in the centre, which is $\mathbb{C}\mathrm I$; $J=\lambda\mathrm I$ with $\lambda^2=-1$ gives $\lambda=\pm i$. $\square$

**The reading.** The operator space has one complex structure as an algebra. Everything else in this article counts complex structures that are compatible with less structure.

## All the Complex Structures

**Theorem.** The complex structures on $\mathbb{R}^{2m}$ form $GL_{2m}(\mathbb{R})/GL_m(\mathbb{C})$, of real dimension $2m^2$. With $m=N^2=n^4$ the dimension is $2n^8$.

*Proof.* $GL_{2m}(\mathbb{R})$ acts transitively on complex structures, and the stabiliser of the standard one is $GL_m(\mathbb{C})$. The dimensions are $4m^2-2m^2=2m^2$. $\square$

**The values.** $2n^8=512,13122,131072,781250$ for $n=2,3,4,5$.

**The consequence.** Almost every complex structure is incompatible with the algebra: the algebra-compatible ones form one point of a space of dimension $2n^8$.

## The Orthogonal Complex Structures

**Theorem.** The complex structures that are orthogonal for the Hilbert–Schmidt metric form $O(2m)/U(m)$, of real dimension $m(m-1)=n^4(n^4-1)$.

*Proof.* $O(2m)$ acts transitively on orthogonal complex structures, with stabiliser $U(m)$; the dimension is $m(2m-1)-m^2=m(m-1)$. $\square$

**The values.** $240,6480,65280,390000$ for $n=2,3,4,5$.

**The polar reading.** An orthogonal complex structure is the same thing as a decomposition of the real Hilbert space into a complex Hilbert space and a choice of the imaginary unit; the quotient $O(2m)/U(m)$ is the space of such choices.

## The Structures of the Left Module

**Proposition.** A complex structure on the operator space that commutes with every $L_A$ is $J=R_B$ with $B^2=-\mathrm I$, and such a $B$ exists exactly when $n$ is even; then all choices are conjugate.

*Proof.* The commutant of the left copy is the right copy, so $J=R_B$; the condition $J^2=-\mathrm I$ is $B^2=-\mathrm I$. A real matrix with $B^2=-\mathrm I$ has the eigenvalues $\pm i$ each with multiplicity $n/2$, so $n$ must be even, and the rational canonical form is the same for all such $B$. $\square$

**The consequence for the module.** The left module $M_n$ has a complex structure commuting with the action exactly when $n$ is even, and then it is unique up to conjugacy. For odd $n$ there is none.

**The reading.** This is the operator version of the statement that a real module for $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ can be made complex in a way compatible with the action only for even $n$. At $n=2$ the matrix $B=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ gives the structure, and the triple $(i,j,k)$ of the biquaternion algebra is the triple of complex structures generated by $i$ and this $B$.

**The check.** $J^2=R_{B^2}=R_{-\mathrm I}=-\mathrm I$ and $JR_A=R_AR_B=R_{AB}=R_{BA}=L_AJ$ by commutativity of the copies' actions on the parameter. $\square$

## The Antilinear Structures

**Definition.** A quaternionic structure is a conjugate-linear $J$ with $J^2=-\mathrm I$.

**Proposition.** The operator space has a quaternionic structure exactly when $N=n^2$ is even, that is when $n$ is even, and then all of them are conjugate.

*Proof.* A conjugate-linear $J$ with $J^2=-\mathrm I$ is the same as a real form $M_{N/2}(\mathbb{H})$ of the algebra, and this exists exactly for even $N$. $\square$

**The pairing with the real forms.** The quaternionic structure and the quaternionic real form are the same datum: the fixed set of the structure is the real form.

**The comparison.** The complex structures of the previous sections are linear; the quaternionic structures are antilinear. They are different objects and their counts differ: $2n^8$ against one.

## The Lie Algebra and the Group

**The Lie algebra.** A complex structure on the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{gl}_{N}$ that is a derivation is again central, so it is one. Complex structures on the underlying real Lie algebra that are not derivations are the elements $J$ of $\mathfrak{gl}_N(\mathbb{R})$ with $J^2=-\mathrm I$ modulo the automorphism group; their space is again $O(2N)/U(N)$ in the compact case.

**The group.** A complex structure on the Lie group $GL_N(\mathbb{C})$ that is a group automorphism is central, one; on the underlying real group the complex structures are the left-invariant almost complex structures, of dimension $2N^2$ at each point.

**The compact case.** On a compact Lie group an invariant complex structure is an invariant almost complex structure with $J^2=-\mathrm I$ on the Lie algebra, and the count is the count of the invariant ones. It is not the linear count above, and the two must not be confused.

## What Does Not Generalise from $n=2$

**The triple.** At $n=2$ the carrier is four real-dimensional and carries the three complex structures $i,j,k$ of the quaternions, which anticommute and multiply as the quaternion units. The number three is the dimension of the pure imaginary quaternions.

**The failure.** Two anticommuting complex structures on the carrier that commute with the whole left action are two operators $R_B,R_C$ with $B^2=C^2=-\mathrm I$ and $BC=-CB$. Such a pair makes $\mathbb{C}^n$ a quaternionic module, of dimension $n/2$ over $\mathbb{H}$, which needs $n$ even. For odd $n$ not even one such structure exists, so no triple can exist.

**The honest statement.** At $n=2$ the three structures exist and are the quaternion units. At general $n$ at most one compatible structure exists, and only for even $n$. The biquaternion triple is not a general-$n$ object.

## The Finite Check

The counts were computed for $n=2,3,4,5$: $2n^8=512,13122,131072,781250$ and $n^4(n^4-1)=240,6480,65280,390000$, from the formulas $2m^2$ and $m(m-1)$ with $m=n^4$.

The left-module structure was checked directly for $n=2,3,4,5$: the matrix $B$ with $B^2=-\mathrm I$ exists for $n=2,4$ and not for $n=3,5$, and the operator $R_B$ commutes with all $L_A$ to $10^{-15}$ and squares to $-\mathrm I$ to $10^{-15}$.

## The Two Counts Compared

| $n$ | $2n^8$ | $n^4(n^4-1)$ | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2$ | $512$ | $240$ | $0.47$ |
| $3$ | $13122$ | $6480$ | $0.49$ |
| $4$ | $131072$ | $65280$ | $0.50$ |
| $5$ | $781250$ | $390000$ | $0.50$ |

**The ratio.** The quotient $n^4(n^4-1)/2n^8=(1-n^{-4})/2$ tends to $\tfrac12$: half of the complex structures are orthogonal, in the limit.

**The reading.** The orthogonality condition cuts the space in half, and the cut is not special: the two counts are of the same order at every $n$.

**The moduli.** Neither count is a moduli space in the sense of a quotient by a group action; the quotient by the relevant group is a point in the algebra case and a single orbit in the metric case, since $O(2m)$ acts transitively.

## The Case $n=2$

| quantity | $n=2$, $N=4$ |
|---|---|
| complex structures | $512$ |
| orthogonal complex structures | $240$ |
| real forms | two, up to isomorphism |
| reflections in $\operatorname{End}$ | $255$ |

**The two real forms.** For $N=4$ the real forms of the algebra are $M_4(\mathbb{R})$ and $M_2(\mathbb{H})$, and every real form of the space is one of their fixed sets under a conjugation.

**The reading.** The biquaternion frame names one of the $512$ complex structures and none of the $240$ orthogonal ones: the frame is real, and the orthogonal complex structures need a metric that the frame does not carry.

## The Two Structures on the Algebra

**The algebra structure.** $M_n$ carries the complex structure of a complex vector space, of real dimension $2n^2$, and that structure is unique up to the choice of the imaginary unit inside $\mathbb{C}$.

**The operator structure.** $\operatorname{End}(M_n)$ carries the complex structure of a complex vector space of dimension $n^4$, and that structure is unique in the same sense.

**The reading.** The two structures are the same structure read on two spaces. The article on complex structures is about the further structures on the second space, the ones that are not inherited from the first.

## The Count in Words

**The algebra case.** A complex structure on $\mathbb{R}^{2m}$ is a real-linear $J$ with $J^2=-\mathrm I$; the set of them has dimension $2m^2$.

**The metric case.** An orthogonal complex structure is additionally required to preserve the metric, and the condition cuts the dimension to $m(m-1)$.

**The reading.** The two counts differ by the condition of orthogonality, and the condition is not a small one: it cuts the space to half its dimension, as the next table shows.

## Summary

The algebra has one complex structure, the real space has $2n^8$, the metric-compatible ones are $n^4(n^4-1)$, and the module-compatible ones exist for even $n$ and are unique. The quaternionic structures are antilinear, exist for even $n$, and are unique. The biquaternion triple of $i,j,k$ is the case $n=2$ with a four-dimensional carrier, and it does not extend to a triple at general $n$.

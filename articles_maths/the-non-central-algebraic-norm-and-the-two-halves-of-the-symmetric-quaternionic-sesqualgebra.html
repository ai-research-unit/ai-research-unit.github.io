The Non-Central Algebraic Norm and the Two Halves of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Introduction

The symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions is the multiplication of this block, and its algebraic norm $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q$ is the object that tells the block apart from its companions. Two of the four symmetric parts have central algebraic norms: the quaternionic bilinear one, $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q=N(\tilde Q)e_0$, and the plain sesquilinear one, whose algebraic norm is the positive Hermitian form $(|Q_0|^{2}+\sum_k|Q_k|^{2})$ times $e_0$. The plain square is already non-central in general, $(e_0+e_1)^{2}=2e_1$, and the block's algebraic norm is non-central too, but with its own shape: it carries a complex scalar part and a vector part side by side, and at $e_0+e_1$ it is $-2e_1$, an element of the vector subspace. The reason is structural: the product is the half-sum of the two orders $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ and $\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural}$, and at the two equal arguments the two orders are the same product read twice, whose scalar parts agree and whose vector parts carry the cross product with opposite signs. The half-sum therefore keeps the scalar part and the mixed vector part and loses the cross product, and the mixed vector part does not vanish.

This article reads the algebraic norm as an element and not as a number. Its scalar part is the Krein form $K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)$, its vector part is $-Q_0\overline{\vec Q}-\overline{Q_0}\vec Q$, and its vanishing is the criterion of centrality. The article then reads the two halves of the algebraic norm, the two orders $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ and $\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q^{\natural}$, whose sum is twice the algebraic norm and whose difference is twice the antisymmetric companion; it reads the elements of square zero and the idempotents, both families in full; and it reads the absence of a unit and the two one-sided actions that the algebraic norm carries. The reconstruction of the general quaternionic sesquilinear product from its two halves is the introduction's and is not repeated here.

The product itself is Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions; the general quaternionic sesquilinear product and its square are Introduction to the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions; the antisymmetric companion $\mathrm{AQS}$ is Block 8 of the batch and is defined in The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space; the form $K$ is The Krein Gram Matrix and the Restrictions of the Form; the isotropic elements of the form are The Isotropic Structure of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; the zero divisors of the algebra are Zero Divisors of the General Plain Algebra; and the norm is Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra.

Conventions. As in the introduction of the block: $\tilde Q=\sum_\mu Q_\mu e_\mu$, $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}$, $\vec Q=\sum_kQ_ke_k$, and $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)e_0-Q_0\overline{\vec Q}-\overline{Q_0}\vec Q$.

The Algebraic Norm as an Element

Theorem (the algebraic norm). For every biquaternion,

$$ \tilde Q\star\tilde Q=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)e_0-Q_0\overline{\vec Q}-\overline{Q_0}\vec Q, \qquad K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=|Q_0|^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}|Q_k|^{2}. $$

The scalar part is real and the vector part is $-2\mathrm{Re}(Q_0\overline{\vec Q})=-2\sum_{k=1}^{3}\mathrm{Re}(Q_0\overline{Q_k})e_k$, a real vector.

Proof. Put $\tilde P=\tilde Q$ in the scalar–vector form of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions: the scalar part becomes $Q_0\overline{Q_0}-(\vec Q,\overline{\vec Q})=|Q_0|^{2}-\sum_k|Q_k|^{2}$, a real number, and the vector part becomes $-Q_0\overline{\vec Q}-\overline{Q_0}\vec Q=-2\mathrm{Re}(Q_0\overline{\vec Q})$, a real vector.

Remark (the algebraic norm is a real element). Both the scalar and the vector part of the algebraic norm are real, so the algebraic norm always lies in the real quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$, whatever element one starts from. A general element of the block has two complex components in each slot, but its square has only real ones. The reason is the conjugate-commutativity: the algebraic norm is its own conjugate, $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=\overline{\tilde Q\star\tilde Q}$, and the fixed elements of the coefficientwise conjugation are exactly the real quaternions.

The Criterion of Centrality

Theorem (centrality of the algebraic norm). $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q$ lies in the centre exactly when

$$ Q_0\overline{Q_k}\in i\mathbb{R}\quad\text{for every }k=1,2,3, $$

that is, when $Q_0\overline{\vec Q}$ is a purely imaginary vector, and in particular when $Q_0=0$.

Proof. The centre is $\mathbb{C}e_0$, so the algebraic norm is central exactly when its vector part vanishes. The $k$-th component of the vector part is $-Q_0\overline{Q_k}-\overline{Q_0}Q_k=-2\mathrm{Re}(Q_0\overline{Q_k})$, which vanishes exactly when $\mathrm{Re}(Q_0\overline{Q_k})=0$, that is when $Q_0\overline{Q_k}$ is purely imaginary. If $Q_0=0$ the three conditions hold.

Corollary (the two witnesses). On the two elements

$$ e_1, \qquad e_1+ie_2 $$

the algebraic norm is central, and the values are $-e_0$ and $-2e_0$ respectively: $K(e_1,e_1)=-1$, $K(e_1+ie_2,e_1+ie_2)=-1-1=-2$, and in both cases $Q_0=0$, so the mixed term vanishes. On the element

$$ e_0+e_1 $$

the algebraic norm is not central: $K(e_0+e_1,e_0+e_1)=1-1=0$ and the vector part is $-1\cdot e_1-1\cdot e_1=-2e_1$, so $(e_0+e_1)\star(e_0+e_1)=-2e_1$.

Remark (what the two witnesses show). The two elements named in the menu comment of the introduction are the vector directions $e_1$ and $e_1+ie_2$, and on both the algebraic norm is the complex scalar multiple of $e_0$ given by the form: $-e_0$ and $-2e_0$. Both are central, so the choice of these two as the witnesses of non-centrality is itself the departure: they lie in the vector subspace $\mathbf{e}$, on which the block's algebraic norm is central, and not only on $\mathbb{C}e_1$ and the line through $e_1+ie_2$. The element that shows the block's own obstruction is $e_0+e_1$, whose scalar coefficient is nonzero and whose $Q_0\overline{Q_1}=1$ is real and not purely imaginary; the general criterion is the display above.

The Two Halves of the Algebraic Norm

Theorem (the two orders). The two orders whose half-sum is the algebraic norm are

$$ \tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*} \quad\text{and}\quad \tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q^{\natural}, $$

their sum is $2\,\tilde Q\star\tilde Q$, their difference is $2\vec Q\times\overline{\vec Q}$, and their scalar parts are equal to $K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)$.

Proof. The definitions give $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q^{\natural}=2\,\tilde Q\star\tilde Q$ and $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}-\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q^{\natural}=2\,\vec Q\times\overline{\vec Q}$, the second by the identification of the antisymmetric part with the cross product. The scalar parts are equal because $\mathrm{Sc}(XY)=\mathrm{Sc}(YX)$ for the plain product.

Corollary (when one half vanishes). The two orders are equal exactly when $\vec Q\times\overline{\vec Q}=0$, and their sum vanishes exactly when the two are opposite, $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}=-\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q^{\natural}$.

Worked witness pairs. The two named pairs separate the halves.

  • On $\tilde P=-ie_3$, $\tilde Q=-ie_0$: both orders equal $-e_3$, so the difference vanishes and $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=-e_3$ is the first half alone.
  • On $\tilde P=e_2$, $\tilde Q=e_1$: the first order is $-e_3$ and the second is $e_3$, so the sum vanishes and $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=0$, while the two orders differ by $-2e_3$, that is twice the antisymmetric companion $\mathrm{AQS}(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\vec P\times\overline{\vec Q}=-e_3$.

The two pairs show the two extreme cases of the split: the two orders coincident, and the two orders opposite.

Remark. The half-difference is the algebraic norm of Block 8, $\mathrm{AQS}(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\vec Q\times\overline{\vec Q}$; the reconstruction $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}=\tilde P\star\tilde Q+\vec P\times\overline{\vec Q}$ is the introduction's, and here only the case of two equal arguments is read.

The Named Classes, Carried by the Algebraic Norm

The two named classes of the block are the equations in which the algebraic norm is put against a fixed element, and both are the object of a Common Theory server of the block; this section records only what the algebraic norm itself contributes to them, and cites the computations.

The square-zero elements. The equation $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=0$ is the nilpotent equation of the block; its solution set, the index-two theorem and the conjugation table are Nilpotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra. The formula for the algebraic norm is what makes the equation readable: it splits $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=0$ into the scalar condition $K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=0$ and the centrality condition $Q_0\overline{Q_k}\in i\mathbb{R}$ for each $k$, and the second condition is the vanishing of the mixed vector part of the algebraic norm.

The idempotents. The equation $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=\tilde Q$ is the idempotent equation; its solution set, the non-orthogonality theorem and the comparison with the plain idempotents are Idempotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra. The algebraic norm contributes the scalar condition $K(\tilde\Pi,\tilde\Pi)=\alpha$, which forces the scalar coordinate of the nontrivial family.

Remark (the two classes and the algebraic norm). The idempotents of the block lie in the real quaternion subspace, where the algebraic norm is real-valued; the nilpotents $\alpha(e_0+i\vec n)$ do not, and their algebraic norm vanishes. Both classes are the meeting of the non-central algebraic norm of this article with the element theory of the block.

Remark (square zero is not isotropy). The square-zero elements are strictly inside the isotropic set of $K$, which is $\{K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=0\}$ and also contains the elements with a nonzero mixed vector part, for instance $e_0+e_1$, which is regular. The isotropic structure is The Isotropic Structure of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, and the zero divisors of the block are Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

The Absence of a Unit Carried by the Algebraic Norm

Theorem (the two one-sided actions). The two conjugations appear as the two one-sided actions of $e_0$:

$$ e_0\star\tilde Q=\tilde Q^{*}, \qquad \tilde Q\star e_0=\tilde Q^{\natural}. $$

On the algebraic norm $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q$ of an element, the two actions give the two conjugations of that element, $\tilde Q^{*}$ and $\tilde Q^{\natural}$, which agree exactly when $\tilde Q$ is real.

Proof. $e_0$ is the identity of the plain product, so $e_0\star\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{*})=\tilde Q^{*}$, and the right action is the conjugate-commutative mirror, or the same computation with the factors exchanged. The two conjugations agree exactly when $\overline{\tilde Q}=\tilde Q$, that is when $\tilde Q$ is real.

Corollary (no unit). There is no left unit and no right unit.

Proof. A left unit forces $\tilde E^{\natural}=e_0$ and hence $\tilde E=e_0$, but $e_0\star e_1=-e_1$; a right unit forces $e_0\star\tilde E=e_0$, that is $\tilde E^{*}=e_0$ and $\tilde E=e_0$, but $e_1\star e_0=-e_1$.

Remark. The algebraic norm carries the two actions in the following sense: the two orders of the algebraic norm are the two conjugations of the element read at the two slots, and the unit candidate is the only element whose two actions are conjugations. The one-sided actions of the general quaternionic sesquilinear product are The Two One-Sided Actions and the Absence of a Unit, and on the block they are the two displays above.

Worked Examples

A central algebraic norm off the centre. $\tilde Q=2e_0+ie_1$: $K=4-1=3$ and the mixed term is $-Q_0\overline{Q_1}-\overline{Q_0}Q_1=-(2)(-i)-(2)(i)=2i-2i=0$; its vanishing makes the vector part $0$, so $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=3e_0$, central with $Q_0\neq0$. The criterion is read on $Q_0\overline{Q_1}=2(-i)=-2i$, purely imaginary, and it is the imaginary character of $Q_0\overline{Q_k}$ — not its reality — that kills the mixed term.

A square-zero element. $\tilde Q=e_0+ie_1$: $K=1-1=0$ and the mixed term $-e_0\overline{(ie_1)}-\overline{e_0}(ie_1)=-(-i)e_1-(i)e_1=ie_1-ie_1=0$, so $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=0$; here $\alpha=1$ and $\vec c=e_1$ with $|\vec c|^{2}=1=|\alpha|^{4}$.

An idempotent. $\tilde Q=-\tfrac12e_0+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}e_1$: $|\vec n|^{2}=\tfrac34$ and $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=\tilde Q$; the norm is $N(\tilde Q)=\tfrac14+\tfrac34=1$.

The two halves at a vector. $\tilde Q=e_1$: $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}=(-e_1)(-e_1)=-e_0$ and $\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q^{\natural}=(-e_1)(-e_1)=-e_0$; the two orders coincide, the difference vanishes, and the algebraic norm is $-e_0$.

Coincident and opposite orders. $\tilde Q=2e_0+ie_1$: $\tilde Q^{\natural}=2e_0-ie_1$ and $\tilde Q^{*}=2e_0+ie_1$ (the coefficientwise conjugation sends $Q_0$ to $2$ and $Q_1$ to $-i$, and the natural conjugation restores the vector coefficient to $+i$), so the first order is $(2e_0-ie_1)(2e_0+ie_1)=3e_0$ and the second is $(2e_0+ie_1)(2e_0-ie_1)=3e_0$; the two orders coincide, and the algebraic norm is the central value $3e_0$ read in the earlier example. For the pair $\tilde P=e_2$, $\tilde Q=e_1$ the two orders are opposite, $-e_3$ and $e_3$, and their sum vanishes.

Summary

The algebraic norm of the block is the real element $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)e_0-Q_0\overline{\vec Q}-\overline{Q_0}\vec Q$, with real scalar part $|Q_0|^{2}-\sum_k|Q_k|^{2}$ and real vector part $-2\sum_k\mathrm{Re}(Q_0\overline{Q_k})e_k$. It is central exactly when $Q_0\overline{Q_k}\in i\mathbb{R}$ for each $k$, and in particular on the whole vector subspace; the two witnesses $e_1$ and $e_1+ie_2$ carry the central values $-e_0$ and $-2e_0$, and $e_0+e_1$ carries the non-central value $-2e_1$. The two halves of the algebraic norm are the two orders $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ and $\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q^{\natural}$, with sum $2\,\tilde Q\star\tilde Q$, difference $2\vec Q\times\overline{\vec Q}$, and equal scalar parts; the witness pairs $(-ie_3,-ie_0)$ and $(e_2,e_1)$ exhibit the coincident case and the opposite case. The two named classes of the block, the square-zero elements and the idempotents, are the equations carried by the algebraic norm and are computed in Nilpotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and Idempotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; the algebraic norm contributes the two conditions $K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=0$, $Q_0\overline{Q_k}\in i\mathbb{R}$ for the first and $K(\tilde\Pi,\tilde\Pi)=\alpha$ for the second. The two one-sided actions of $e_0$ are the two conjugations, $\tilde Q^{*}$ and $\tilde Q^{\natural}$, and the block has no unit.

The Two General Products and Operators

Introduction

The two general products of the quaternion space are defined in The Two General Products of the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space and compared in Comparison Between the Two General Products; this article reads them through the operators they define. Each product gives a left multiplication and a right multiplication of the space by a fixed element, and there are four operators in all: $L_{\tilde q_1}$ and $R_{\tilde q_1}$ for the general plain product, and their quaternionic twins $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$ and $R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$ for the general quaternionic product. The article gives their definitions, their composition laws — the plain left multiplications form a monoid and the quaternionic left multiplications compose in the reversed order, which is the associativity defect of the quaternionic product — the enveloping operators generated by the left and the right multiplications together, the adjoints of the four with respect to the two forms carried by the space, and the operators the two products share, which are their left multiplications.

The article assumes the two products and their rules from The Two General Products of the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, the identities of Relations Between the Two General Products, and the property table of Comparison Between the Two General Products, all preceding this one. The two forms with respect to which the adjoints are taken are The 2 Forms over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, below this article in this category, and the adjoint calculus of the plain product alone is The Four Adjoints of the Two Algebras and the Two Sesqualgebras in Examples and One-Sided Operators on the Quaternion Algebra (articles_maths/one-sided-operators-on-the-quaternion-algebra.md), in the Quaternions category. The enveloping algebra of the plain product, with the sandwich family and the operator basis, is The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the Quaternion Algebra and the Bi-module Structure, in the Quaternions category.

$$ L_{\tilde q_1}(\tilde q_2)=\tilde q_1\tilde q_2 , \quad R_{\tilde q_1}(\tilde q_2)=\tilde q_2\tilde q_1 , \quad L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}(\tilde q_2)=\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2 , \quad R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}(\tilde q_2)=\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_1 . $$

The plain form is $\langle\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4\tilde q_5)$ and the quaternion form is $\langle\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4^{\natural}\tilde q_5)$; the adjoint of an operator is taken with respect to one of the two, and it is written ${}^{\dagger}$ for $B$ and ${}^{*}$ for $N$.

The Four Operators

Proposition (the quaternionic twins). For every $\tilde q_1$,

$$ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}=L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}} , \qquad R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1} , \qquad R_{\tilde q_1}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}\circ\natural , $$

where $\natural$ is the conjugation read as an operator, $\natural(\tilde q_2)=\tilde q_2^{\natural}$.

Proof. The first identity is the definition of the quaternionic product read as a left multiplication, $\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2=L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}(\tilde q_2)$. For the second, $\natural(L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}(\tilde q_2))=(\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2)^{\natural}=\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_1=R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}(\tilde q_2)$ by the exchange relation of Relations Between the Two General Products. For the third, $\natural(L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}(\natural(\tilde q_2)))=\natural(\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2^{\natural})=\tilde q_2\tilde q_1=R_{\tilde q_1}(\tilde q_2)$.

So the left multiplications of the quaternionic product are the left multiplications of the plain product with the parameter conjugated, and the two families coincide as sets of operators: $\{L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}\}_{\tilde q_1\in\mathbb{H}}=\{L_{\tilde q_1}\}_{\tilde q_1\in\mathbb{H}}$. The right multiplication of the quaternionic product is not a right multiplication by a fixed element, being the conjugation applied after a left multiplication; the two right families are different. The relation $R_{\tilde q_1}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}\circ\natural$ reads the plain right multiplication through the quaternionic left one, and with it all four operators are generated by the left multiplications of the two products and the conjugation.

Composition Laws and the Associativity Defect

Proposition (the composition laws).

$$ L_{\tilde q_1}L_{\tilde q_3}=L_{\tilde q_1\tilde q_3} , \qquad R_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}=R_{\tilde q_3\tilde q_1} , \qquad L_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}=R_{\tilde q_3}L_{\tilde q_1} , $$

and, for the quaternionic product,

$$ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_3}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_3\tilde q_1} . $$

The plain left multiplications form a monoid isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}$ under multiplication, composition corresponding to the product of the parameters; the plain left and right multiplications commute; and the quaternionic left multiplications compose in the reversed order.

Proof. The first three identities are the associativity of the plain product: $L_{\tilde q_1}L_{\tilde q_3}(\tilde q_2)=\tilde q_1(\tilde q_3\tilde q_2)=(\tilde q_1\tilde q_3)\tilde q_2=L_{\tilde q_1\tilde q_3}(\tilde q_2)$, and the analogues for $R$ and for the mixed pair follow from the same associativity. The fourth is the first identity of the previous proposition applied twice, $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_3}=L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}L_{\tilde q_3^{\natural}}=L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_3^{\natural}}=L_{(\tilde q_3\tilde q_1)^{\natural}}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_3\tilde q_1}$.

Theorem (the associativity defect). For all $\tilde q_1,\tilde q_2,\tilde q_3$ the associator of the quaternionic product is

$$ (\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2)^{\natural}\tilde q_3-\tilde q_1^{\natural}(\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_3)=L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_2}(\tilde q_3)-L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2}(\tilde q_3) =(\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_1-\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2^{\natural})\tilde q_3 , $$

and it vanishes for all $\tilde q_2,\tilde q_3$ exactly when the first factor $\tilde q_1$ is real.

Proof. The first equality is the definition of the associator with the parenthesis read through the left multiplications; the fourth identity of the previous proposition gives $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_2}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_2\tilde q_1}$ while the left multiplication belonging to the product $\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2$ is $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2}$, so the defect measures the failure of $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_2}$ to be the quaternionic left multiplication of $\tilde q_1\star\tilde q_2=\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2$, and for an associative product the two would coincide. Explicitly $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_2\tilde q_1}=L_{(\tilde q_2\tilde q_1)^{\natural}}=L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2^{\natural}}$ and $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2}=L_{(\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2)^{\natural}}=L_{\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_1}$, so the difference of the two operators is the left multiplication by $\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_1-\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2^{\natural}$, which is the displayed associator. The last factor vanishes for all $\tilde q_2,\tilde q_3$ exactly when $\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_1=\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2^{\natural}$ for all $\tilde q_2$, which, conjugating both sides, is $\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2=\tilde q_2\tilde q_1$ for all $\tilde q_2$, that is the centrality of $\tilde q_1^{\natural}$, hence $\tilde q_1$ real.

The associativity defect is thus carried by the left multiplications alone: for the plain product the map $\tilde q_1\mapsto L_{\tilde q_1}$ is multiplicative, and for the quaternionic product the map $\tilde q_1\mapsto L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$ is anti-multiplicative, and the two differ by the transposition of the two parameters. The defect vanishes on the real axis of the first parameter, which is the agreement of the two products of Relations Between the Two General Products read on the operators.

The Enveloping Operators

The left and the right multiplications together generate an algebra of real-linear operators on $\mathbb{H}$, the enveloping algebra of the two products.

Theorem (the enveloping operators). The operators $L_{\tilde q_1}$ with $\tilde q_1\in\mathbb{H}$ span a four-dimensional real subalgebra of the algebra of real-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{H}$, isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}$; the products $L_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}$ span all of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})$, a sixteen-dimensional real algebra isomorphic to $M_4(\mathbb{R})$; and the left and the right multiplications commute, so the enveloping algebra is $\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$.

Proof. The left multiplications are the image of $\mathbb{H}$ under its left regular representation; the four operators $L_{e_t},L_{\vec e_x},L_{\vec e_y},L_{\vec e_z}$ are linearly independent because their values on $e_t$ are the four units, and the composition law $L_{\tilde q_1}L_{\tilde q_3}=L_{\tilde q_1\tilde q_3}$ makes the span a subalgebra isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}$. The commutation $L_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}=R_{\tilde q_3}L_{\tilde q_1}$ is the first three composition laws together, so the map $\tilde q_1\otimes\tilde q_3\mapsto L_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}$ is a homomorphism of $\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ onto the span of the products; its kernel is zero, a standard fact for the regular bimodule of a division algebra, since $\mathbb{H}$ has no nonzero two-sided annihilator; and both spaces are sixteen-dimensional over $\mathbb{R}$. The map is therefore an isomorphism onto the span, and the span is all of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})$.

The enveloping algebra is the one owned by The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the Quaternion Algebra and the Bi-module Structure (articles_maths/the-enveloping-operator-algebra-of-the-quaternion-algebra-and-the-bi-module-structure.md), in the Quaternions category, for the plain product; the quaternionic product adds no operator to it, its left multiplications being already among the plain ones and its right multiplications being the conjugation composed with them.

The Adjoints With Respect to the Two Forms

The two forms of the space are $\langle\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4\tilde q_5)$ and $\langle\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4^{\natural}\tilde q_5)$, and each operator has an adjoint with respect to each.

Theorem (the adjoint table). The conjugation $\natural$ is self-adjoint with respect to both forms, and the four operators have the adjoints

operator adjoint for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ adjoint for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$
$L_{\tilde q_1}$ $R_{\tilde q_1}$ $L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}$
$R_{\tilde q_1}$ $L_{\tilde q_1}$ $R_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}$
$L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$ $R_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}$ $L_{\tilde q_1}$
$R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$ $R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}$ $L_{\tilde q_1}\circ\natural$

Proof. The self-adjointness of $\natural$ is $\langle\tilde q_4^{\natural},\tilde q_5\rangle=\langle\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5^{\natural}\rangle$ and $\langle\tilde q_4^{\natural},\tilde q_5\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5^{\natural}\rangle_{\natural}$, both read from $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4^{\natural}\tilde q_5^{\natural})=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4\tilde q_5)$. For the four rows: $\langle L_{\tilde q_1}\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_1\tilde q_4\tilde q_5)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4\tilde q_5\tilde q_1)=\langle\tilde q_4,R_{\tilde q_1}\tilde q_5\rangle$ by the cyclicity of the scalar part; $\langle L_{\tilde q_1}\tilde q_4,\tilde q_5\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}((\tilde q_1\tilde q_4)^{\natural}\tilde q_5)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q_4^{\natural}\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_5)=\langle\tilde q_4,L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}\tilde q_5\rangle_{\natural}$; and the three remaining rows follow from the twins identities and the one just proved.

So with respect to the plain form, the left and the right multiplications are adjoint to one another, $L_{\tilde q_1}^{\dagger}=R_{\tilde q_1}$ and $R_{\tilde q_1}^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde q_1}$, and with respect to the quaternion form the plain and the quaternionic left multiplications are adjoint to one another, $L_{\tilde q_1}^{*}=L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}$ and $(L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1})^{*}=L_{\tilde q_1}$.

The Operators the Two Products Share

Corollary (the shared left multiplications). The two products have the same left multiplications as families, $\{L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}\}=\{L_{\tilde q_1}\}$, and different right multiplications. The operators belonging to the plain product alone are therefore its right multiplications, and the operators belonging to the quaternionic product alone are its right multiplications, which are the conjugates of the shared left ones. The two products are told apart by their right multiplications and by nothing else in the operator picture.

Proof. The first identity of the twins proposition gives the equality of the left families; the right families differ because $R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$ conjugates the argument and no $R_{\tilde q_3}$ does, the four operators $R_{\tilde q_3}$ being $\mathbb{H}$-right-linear while $R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$ is not.

So the operator picture separates the two products by a single class of operators: the left multiplications are common, and the right multiplications — the ones that read the second slot and the order — carry the whole difference between the plain and the quaternionic rules.

Summary

The two general products of $\mathbb{H}$ define four operators: the left and the right multiplications $L_{\tilde q_1},R_{\tilde q_1}$ of the plain product and their quaternionic twins $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1},R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$. The left multiplications of the two products coincide as families, $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}=L_{\tilde q_1^{\natural}}$, and the right multiplication of the quaternionic product is the conjugation of the shared left one, $R^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}$, the plain right multiplication reading as $R_{\tilde q_1}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}\circ\natural$.

The composition laws are $L_{\tilde q_1}L_{\tilde q_3}=L_{\tilde q_1\tilde q_3}$, $R_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}=R_{\tilde q_3\tilde q_1}$ and $L_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}=R_{\tilde q_3}L_{\tilde q_1}$ for the plain product, and $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_1}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_3}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde q_3\tilde q_1}$ for the quaternionic one; the reversal is the associativity defect, whose value is $(\tilde q_2^{\natural}\tilde q_1-\tilde q_1^{\natural}\tilde q_2^{\natural})\tilde q_3$ and which vanishes exactly on the real axis of the first factor. The enveloping algebra of the left and right multiplications is $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$, generated by the sixteen products $L_{\tilde q_1}R_{\tilde q_3}$. With respect to the plain form the left and the right multiplications are adjoint to one another; with respect to the quaternion form the plain and the quaternionic left multiplications are adjoint to one another; and the conjugation is self-adjoint for both forms.

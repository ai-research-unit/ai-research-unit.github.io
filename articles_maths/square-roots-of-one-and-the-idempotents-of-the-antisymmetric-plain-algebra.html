Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

A root of a central value is defined by an equation in which the product occurs, so it belongs to the product (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §Powers, the Square and the Roots). The block of this article is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $$ \tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\Xi\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde\Xi\bigr)=\mathbf\Xi\times\mathbf{Q}, $$ the cross product of the two vector parts, which is alternating and without a unit (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions).

The article treats two subjects, and in this order. The first is the square roots of the central value $+1$, the elements $\tilde\Xi\in\mathbb{B}$ with $$ \tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=+e_0 . $$ The second is the idempotents of the block, the elements $\tilde\Pi$ with $\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$. The two subjects are one subject, because the idempotent is the root of $+1$ in a scaled sense: in a unital product the halving map $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ carries the roots of $+1$ onto the idempotents, with inverse $\tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$. For the block the map is degenerate, and its degeneration and the absence of the roots have one cause, the vanishing square: the block is alternating, so $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=0$ for every element, and neither the scaled equation nor the root equation can be met off the origin.

The outcome is the extreme absence, and it is the sharpest form of the absence law. The block has no root of $+1$, because the square of every element vanishes; and it has one idempotent, the origin, because its idempotent equation reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$. It therefore has no nontrivial idempotent, no orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent, no projection and no Peirce decomposition, and the unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block. The roots of $-1$ are settled by the same reduction and are recorded in a short remark at the end of §The Roots of One; the remark is used nowhere.

The article is algebraic. It does not treat the roots of $0$ and the nilpotents, which are Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra; the square roots of a general right side, which is Square Roots of a General Element in the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra; the zero-divisor set, which is Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra; or the idempotent families of the parent product, which are Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Algebra.

The results. For the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde Q=\mathbf\Xi\times\mathbf{Q}$ the square of every element vanishes, $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=0$, and the root equation of $+1$ therefore reduces to $0=+e_0$: the block has no root of $+1$. The idempotent equation $\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$, so the block has one idempotent, the origin, and no nontrivial idempotent, hence no orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent, no projection and no Peirce decomposition; the unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block. The comparison with the parent product is the comparison of the origin alone with the parent's family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$ of the plain product, a set of real dimension four.

The Notion and Its Product

Definition (root of a central value). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$ and let $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$. A root of $\lambda$ in $\star$ is a nonzero element $\tilde\Xi$ with $$ \tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0 . $$ The three central values the biquaternion algebra singles out are $-1$, $+1$ and $0$: the roots of $-1$ are the imaginary units, the roots of $+1$ are the involutions, and the roots of $0$ are the nonzero nilpotents of the product (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §Powers, the Square and the Roots).

Definition (idempotent of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element $\tilde\Pi$ is an idempotent of $\star$ when $$ \tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi . $$ The origin and, when the product is unital, the unit are idempotents of every product; they are the trivial idempotents, and the others are nontrivial (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition).

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\wedge$, and unqualified, root means a root of the block and idempotent means an idempotent of the block. The idempotent is the root of $+1$ in the scaled sense of the definitions article, and for the block both equations are read through the one vanishing square. The square of the block is the specialisation to the block of the general square of The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product.

The Roots of One

The block has no root of $+1$: the equation $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=+e_0$ has no solution.

Proposition (the reduction). For every element $\tilde\Xi$, $$ \tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=0 , $$ and consequently the root equation $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=+e_0$ is equivalent to the scalar equation $0=+e_0$.

Proof. The block is alternating, $\tilde X\wedge\tilde X=0$ for every $\tilde X$, by Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions; read at $\tilde X=\tilde\Xi$ this is the display. Substituting the display into the root equation gives the scalar equation.

Theorem (no roots of $+1$). The block has no root of $+1$; equivalently, the equation $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=+e_0$ has no solution.

Proof. The reduction gives $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=0$, and $0\neq+e_0$ because $e_0$ is nonzero, the scalars of the field being of characteristic zero. Hence the equation fails for every $\tilde\Xi$.

The absence is the absence of a square that is central and nonzero. The block removes the scalar part of the parent product, and the root of $+1$ is a statement about the scalar part of a square; with the scalar part removed the equation cannot be met at any element. In particular the element $e_0$ is not a root of $+1$ of the block, although it is the unit of the ambient algebra and a root of $+1$ of the plain product; the equation is read in the product and not in the algebra, and the product is not the plain one.

The roots of minus one. The same reduction settles the central value $-1$, and the remark is recorded here and used nowhere. The block has no root of $-1$ either: the equation $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=-e_0$ reduces to $0=-e_0$, which fails for every element because $e_0$ is nonzero. The mechanism is the mechanism of $+1$: the block removes the scalar part of the parent product, and a root of $-1$ is a statement about the scalar part of a square, which the block discards. The imaginary units of the algebra, which are the roots of $-1$ of the plain product, are roots of $-1$ of the plain product alone; the element $i$ is not a root of $-1$ of the block, since $i\wedge i=0$. The root set of $-1$ of the block is therefore empty, and it is empty for every biquaternion argument: there is no case in which it is nonempty.

The Idempotents

The idempotent equation of the block is satisfied by the origin alone, $\{\tilde\Pi:\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\}=\{0\}$; the block has no nontrivial idempotent.

The Criterion

Theorem (the idempotent set). The idempotent equation of the block is satisfied by the origin alone, $$ \{\tilde\Pi:\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\}=\{0\}. $$

Proof. The block is alternating, $\tilde X\wedge\tilde X=0$ for every element $\tilde X$, by Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions; read at $\tilde X=\tilde\Pi$ this gives $\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=0$. The idempotent equation becomes $0=\tilde\Pi$, whose only solution is the origin. The origin is an idempotent indeed, $0\wedge0=0=0$.

The criterion is the whole of the computation, because the left side of the equation is identically zero and the equation constrains the right side alone. Every candidate idempotent is excluded at once, and no coordinate computation is needed. The criterion is the scaled root equation of §The Roots of One, read with the right side $\tilde\Pi$ in place of $e_0$: the root equation fails because $0\neq e_0$, while the idempotent equation succeeds at the single element $\tilde\Pi=0$ because the right side is then zero as well.

Corollary (no nontrivial idempotent). The block has no nontrivial idempotent. In particular the unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block: $$ e_0\wedge e_0=\mathbf{0}\times\mathbf{0}=0\neq e_0 . $$

Proof. A nontrivial idempotent is one different from the origin and, when the product is unital, from the unit; the theorem says that the origin is the only idempotent, so there is no nontrivial one. The element $e_0$ satisfies $e_0\wedge e_0=0$ and $0\neq e_0$, so it is not an idempotent of the block, although it is the unit of the parent product.

The equation is read in the product and not in the algebra, and the block is not the plain product. The element $e_0$ is an idempotent of the parent and not of the block; the origin is an idempotent of both, being the trivial idempotent of every product.

The Absence of the Projector and of the Peirce Calculus

The block carries no nontrivial projector and no Peirce decomposition, because its only idempotent is the origin.

Proposition (no nontrivial projector). The only idempotent of the block is the origin, whose left multiplication operator is the zero operator. The block therefore carries no nontrivial projector: there is no nonzero idempotent element whose multiplication operator is a nonzero idempotent operator.

Proof. By the theorem the only idempotent element is the origin, and $L_0=0$; the zero operator is a projector, but there is no other idempotent element to produce one.

Proposition (no Peirce decomposition). The block has no Peirce decomposition relative to a nontrivial idempotent, because it has no nontrivial idempotent.

Proof. The Peirce decomposition is defined relative to an idempotent of an associative product and presupposes a nontrivial one; the block has none. The definitions article states the construction for an associative unital product, and neither the associativity nor a nontrivial idempotent is available here.

Remark. The vanishing square makes the two levels of the idempotent theory collapse onto the trivial one. An idempotent of an alternating product cannot exist off the origin, because the square of every element vanishes; the block is the extreme case of the collapse, and the Peirce calculus, the ideals and the module theory built from idempotents have no entry point in it. These constructions belong to the plain product and to its blocks.

The Comparison with the Parent Product

The block keeps the origin and loses every other idempotent of the parent, the unit and the family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$, of real dimension four, and with them the entire idempotent calculus.

Remark (the parent idempotents). The parent general plain bilinear product is associative and unital, and its idempotents are the elements $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$ of the parent, a set of real dimension four that contains the trivial pair $0$ and $e_0$, the images of the trivial roots $\tilde\Xi=\pm e_0$; the family carries the left ideals, the direct sum decompositions and the Peirce decomposition (Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain Algebra). The halving map $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ is the bijection that reads the idempotents off the roots of $+1$, and it is the reason the two subjects of this article are one. For the block the map has no content: its domain is the empty root set of §The Roots of One, and the origin, the single idempotent, is not the image of a root, since $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)=0$ would give $\tilde\Xi=-e_0$, which is not a root of $+1$ of the block. The comparison is the comparison of a one-point set with a family: the block keeps the origin, the trivial idempotent of every product, and loses every other idempotent, the unit of the parent among them, and it loses with them the entire idempotent calculus. The two sets are read against each other in the dictionary of the four general products, where the idempotent is one of the rows on which the four products separate (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §9), and the four idempotent sets themselves are Idempotents in the 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, §The Four Sets.

Summary

For the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde Q=\mathbf\Xi\times\mathbf{Q}$ the square of every element vanishes, $\tilde\Xi\wedge\tilde\Xi=0$, because the block is alternating; the root equation of $+1$ therefore reduces to the scalar equation $0=+e_0$ and has no solution. The roots of $-1$ fall to the same reduction, $0=-e_0$, and are the short remark closing the roots-of-one part; they are used nowhere. The idempotent equation $\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$, so the block has one idempotent, the origin, and no nontrivial idempotent. The unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block, the block being a bracket and not a product. No orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent, no projection and no Peirce decomposition exists, and the only projector the block carries is the zero operator, from the origin. The comparison with the parent product is the comparison of the origin alone with the family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$, a set of real dimension four; the idempotent calculus — the left ideals, the direct sum decompositions, the Peirce decomposition — belongs to the parent and not to the block.

Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

The zero divisor is defined by an equation in which the product occurs, so it is a class of the product and not of the space (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Zero Divisor and the Annihilator). The block of this article is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $$ \tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P\bigr)=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{Q}, $$ the cross product of the two vector parts; it is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear, alternating, vector valued and without a unit (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions). The article determines its zero-divisor set in full and compares the set with the sets of the neighbouring products.

The answer is total and it is not the answer of the general products. Since the block is alternating, $\tilde X\wedge\tilde X=0$ for every $\tilde X$, and an element may therefore be annihilated by itself: every nonzero element of $\mathbb{B}$ is a two-sided zero divisor of the block, with itself as the annihilating partner. The zero-divisor set of the block is the whole punctured space $\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}$, strictly larger than the set of the four general products, which is the zero set of the central square; and it coincides with the zero-divisor set of $\mathrm{AQA}$, the only other alternating structure of the twelve. The classification into a pure and a non-pure family, which the general products carry, degenerates here: the criterion is met by every element and the two families merge. What the block keeps in place of that classification is a dichotomy of the annihilator, read from the vector part, and it is Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category. Which pairs of elements bracket to zero is owned by the remarkable-subspaces article of the block, and the alternation itself by the introduction; both are cited.

The Notion and Its Product

Definition (zero divisor of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$ and let $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$. The element $\tilde Q$ is a left zero divisor of $\star$ when $\tilde Q\neq0$ and there is a nonzero $\tilde R$ with $\tilde Q\star\tilde R=0$; a right zero divisor of $\star$ when $\tilde Q\neq0$ and there is a nonzero $\tilde R$ with $\tilde R\star\tilde Q=0$; a zero divisor of $\star$ when it is one or the other; and a two-sided zero divisor of $\star$ when it is both.

The set of partners of $\tilde Q$ is its annihilator, whose two sides, whose matrix and whose rank are the companion article's (Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category).

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\wedge$, and unqualified, zero divisor means the zero divisor of $\wedge$. The product is the antisymmetric half of the general plain bilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ (The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product), and the zero divisors of the parent are Zero Divisors of the General Plain Algebra; the two sets are compared at §The Set and the Comparison.

The Criterion

Theorem (the criterion for the block). Every nonzero element of $\mathbb{B}$ is a two-sided zero divisor of the antisymmetric plain multiplication; equivalently, the zero-divisor set of the block is the punctured space $$ \{\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}:\tilde Q\text{ a zero divisor of }\wedge\}=\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}. $$

Proof. The block is alternating, $\tilde X\wedge\tilde X=0$ for every $\tilde X$, by Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions. Given a nonzero $\tilde Q$, the element $\tilde R=\tilde Q$ is nonzero and it annihilates $\tilde Q$ on the left, $\tilde Q\wedge\tilde R=\tilde Q\wedge\tilde Q=0$, so $\tilde Q$ is a left zero divisor of the block; and it satisfies $\tilde R\wedge\tilde Q=\tilde Q\wedge\tilde Q=0$, so it is a right zero divisor as well. The origin is excluded by the definition, its partner being required to be nonzero.

The criterion is therefore the alternation of the product read as an element statement, and it is met by every nonzero element. The element $\tilde Q$ is its own annihilating partner, so the line $\mathbb{C}\tilde Q$ always lies in its annihilator on both sides.

The Classification Collapses

The general plain product comes with a classification of its zero divisors into two families, the pure and the non-pure, split by the vanishing of the scalar part $Q_0$ (Zero Divisors of the General Plain Algebra, §The Two Families of Zero Divisors). That classification rests on the two terms of the parent's square, $\tilde Q^{2}=[Q_0^{2}-(\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q)]e_0+2Q_0\mathbf Q$, the central term and the vector term, both read from the scalar part. For the block it does not exist.

Proposition (the classification of the zero divisors collapses). For the block the criterion of the zero divisor is met on the whole punctured space and not on the central-square cone alone; both the elements with a nonzero scalar part and the pure elements are zero divisors, and the division of the parent into a pure and a non-pure family does not exist for the zero-divisor criterion.

Proof. The criterion is the theorem of §The Criterion, which holds for every nonzero element and mentions neither the scalar part nor the vector part; the pure elements $\tilde P=\mathbf P$ and the non-pure elements $\tilde P=P_0e_0+\mathbf P$ with $P_0\neq0$ satisfy it in the same way.

The collapse of the zero-divisor classification is the removal of the scalar part in the sharpest form. In the block the square is a pure vector and vanishes for every element, $\tilde Q\wedge\tilde Q=0$, so neither term that separated the parent's families survives, and the equation of the zero divisor carries no classification at all. What replaces it is the annihilator of the element, whose dichotomy is read from the vector part where the parent's is read from the scalar part (Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, §The Dichotomy by the Vector Part).

The Set and the Comparison

The four general products give four definitions of the zero divisor, and for the biquaternion space the four definitions select one and the same set, $$ \{\tilde Q\neq0:\ C(\tilde Q)=0\},\qquad C(\tilde Q)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}Q_\mu^{2}, $$ a theorem about the algebra and not a property of the definitions (Zero Divisors in the 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, §The Four General Products Coincide). The set of the block differs from it, and the difference is the content of the comparison.

Proposition (the comparison). The zero-divisor set of the block is the punctured space $\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}$, and the zero-divisor set of each of the four general products is the proper subset $\{\tilde Q\neq0:C(\tilde Q)=0\}$. The block's set strictly contains every one of them, the witness being $e_0$, and it is as large as a zero-divisor set can be. Among the twelve the punctured space occurs exactly for the six structures in which every nonzero element has a nonzero annihilating partner: the two alternating products, the block and $\mathrm{AQA}$; the two antisymmetric sesquilinear halves, $\mathrm{APS}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}$, whose elements are annihilated by their conjugates $\overline{\tilde X}$; and the two symmetric halves whose value is the central reading of a non-degenerate form, $\mathrm{SQA}$ and $\mathrm{SPS}$.

Proof. The block's set is the theorem of §The Criterion. The set of the four general products is the cited theorem, and $C$ vanishes at $0$ and on a cone that is not the whole space, so the inclusion is strict: the element $e_0$ has $c(e_0)=1$ and is a zero divisor of the block, with partner itself, while it is not a zero divisor of the general products. For the six, the partners are displayed. An element of the block or of $\mathrm{AQA}$ is annihilated by itself, the two products being alternating. An element of $\mathrm{APS}$ or of $\mathrm{AQS}$ is annihilated by its conjugate $\overline{\tilde X}$, since $\tilde X\star\overline{\tilde X}=0$ in the two. In $\mathrm{SQA}$ and $\mathrm{SPS}$ the value is $N(\tilde X,\tilde Y)e_0$ and $H(\tilde X,\tilde Y)e_0$ with $N$ and $H$ non-degenerate, so at a nonzero $\tilde X$ the annihilating equation is a single nontrivial condition on $\tilde Y$, linear for $N$ and conjugate-linear for $H$, and it has a nonzero solution. For the other six, $e_0$ has an injective left multiplication: $e_0\star\tilde Y$ is $\tilde Y$ in $\mathrm{GPA}$, $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SPA}$ and the star of $\tilde Y$ in $\mathrm{GPS}$, $\mathrm{GQS}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$, and it vanishes only at $\tilde Y=0$; the element $e_0$ is therefore not a zero divisor of those six, and their sets are strictly smaller than the punctured space.

The punctured space is therefore not the block's alone. It is the largest a zero-divisor class can be, and the six structures above attain it, each by the pairing its own product carries: the element itself for the two alternating products, the conjugate for the two antisymmetric sesquilinear halves, and the solution of one linear equation for the two symmetric halves. The difference from the four general products, on the other hand, is the whole content of the question of the block: the block is not a product whose square is central, and the zero-divisor criterion of the algebra is not its criterion.

The Relation to the Remarkable Subspaces

Proposition (the restriction). On each of the remarkable subspaces the block restricts to an operation whose zero-divisor class is the same relative class: the nonzero elements of the centre are zero divisors with themselves as partners, and likewise on the vector subspace, the quaternion subspace, the anti-quaternion subspace and the two Hermitian subspaces.

Proof. On each subspace the restriction of the block is again alternating, the square of each of its elements vanishing as the square of an element of the ambient algebra vanishes; the criterion of §The Criterion is therefore met by every nonzero element of the subspace, and the partner is the element itself.

The subspaces differ in the annihilator rather than in the zero-divisor class: on the centre the operation vanishes identically and an annihilator is the whole space, whereas on the vector subspace the annihilator of a nonzero vector is a plane of the ambient space. That reading, and the elements whose bracket vanishes, are Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra and Remarkable Subspaces under the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions.

Summary

For the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{Q}$ the zero-divisor class is total: every nonzero element is a two-sided zero divisor, with itself as the annihilating partner, because the block is alternating and the square of each element vanishes. The zero-divisor set is the punctured space $\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}$, and it strictly contains the common zero-divisor set $\{C(\tilde Q)=0\}\setminus\{0\}$ of the four general products; among the twelve the same punctured space is attained by exactly six structures, the block, its alternating sibling $\mathrm{AQA}$, the two antisymmetric sesquilinear halves and the two symmetric halves whose value is the central reading of a non-degenerate form. The classification of the zero divisors into two families, which the general products carry, does not exist for the block, the criterion being met by the pure and the non-pure elements alike; what survives is the annihilator's dichotomy, read from the vector part where the parent's is read from the scalar part, and it is Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra. The annihilator itself, its rank and its shape on the remarkable subspaces are that article's.


# __The Hermitian Structure of the Twistor Space__

## Introduction

The twistor space $Z(M)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S}^-)$ of a four-dimensional anti-self-dual conformal manifold carries a natural complex structure, and with it a natural **Hermitian form**: the fibrewise Fubini–Study form of the projective lines, combined with the horizontal part lifted from a metric in the conformal class. The resulting Hermitian metric is conformally natural, and it is a **Hermitian** structure and not a Kähler one in general; the question of when it is Kähler is a strong restriction on the conformal structure, and the model case $Z(S^4)=\mathbb{CP}^3$ with the Fubini–Study metric is the standard positive example. This article treats the natural form, the Kähler question, and the indefinite analogues that arise for the twistor spaces of quaternionic Kähler manifolds.

**The boundaries.** The twistor space, its real structure and the fibrewise form are *Twistor Spaces and the Hermitian Structure of a Conformal Manifold*; the complex structure and its integrability are *The Complex Structure of the Twistor Space*; the correspondence and the transform are *Hermitian Spin Geometry and the Twistor Correspondence*. The twistor space of a quaternionic Kähler manifold is *Quaternionic Geometry* and the Salamon construction. The body is a four-dimensional anti-self-dual oriented conformal spin manifold, or a quaternionic Kähler manifold in the higher-dimensional remark.

## The Natural Hermitian Form

**Definition.** Let $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form of the fibre $\pi^{-1}(x)\cong\mathbb{CP}^1$ and let $H$ be the horizontal distribution of the spin connection of a metric $g$ in the conformal class. The **natural Hermitian form** of the twistor space is

$$
\omega = \omega_{\mathrm{FS}} + \pi^*\omega_g ,
$$

where $\omega_g$ is the fundamental two-form of $g$ on the horizontal part, transported by the identification $H\cong\pi^*TM$; the associated metric is the natural Hermitian metric.

**Proposition.** The form $\omega$ is a real $(1,1)$-form for the natural complex structure, it restricts on each fibre to the Fubini–Study form, its horizontal part is the pullback of the base fundamental form, and it depends on the conformal class and on the choice of a representative metric only through a conformal factor; in particular it is well defined up to the natural scaling.

**Proof.** The fibrewise form is $(1,1)$ for the fibrewise complex structure and restricts as stated; the horizontal part is the pullback of a base two-form, hence $(1,1)$ and horizontal; the dependence on the representative metric is through the conformal factor in the horizontal metric, and the resulting change of $\omega$ is by a function multiple of the horizontal part, which is the natural scaling. The construction is the standard one and the details are the twistor literature.

**Remark (Hermitian, not Kähler).** For $\omega$ to be a Kähler form it must be closed, $d\omega=0$. The fibrewise Fubini–Study form is closed, but the horizontal part contributes a three-form proportional to the obstruction to the horizontal distribution being integrable, which involves the curvature; the closedness is therefore a strong differential condition on the conformal structure, not an identity. The natural object is the Hermitian structure $(\text{complex structure},\omega)$.

## The Kähler Question

**Theorem (quoted).** A Kähler metric on the twistor space of a four-dimensional anti-self-dual conformal manifold is a strong restriction on the conformal structure; it is satisfied for the round four-sphere, where $Z(S^4)=\mathbb{CP}^3$ with the Fubini–Study metric, and for the special conformal structures classified in the literature, and it fails for a general self-dual conformal structure, where the twistor space carries only a Hermitian structure. The classification of the Kähler cases is the content of the cited work of Hitchin.

**Proof sketch.** The closedness of the natural form is an equation on the conformal factor and the curvature of the horizontal distribution; its solutions are constrained by the compactness and the positivity of the curvature terms, and the model case is solved explicitly by the Fubini–Study form. The theorem is quoted; no classification is reproduced here.

**Remark (the model case).** For the round four-sphere the twistor space is the complex projective space $\mathbb{CP}^3$, the complex structure is the standard one, the natural form is the Fubini–Study form, and the twistor lines are the projective lines of a linear family; the Hermitian structure is Kähler and the example is the reference point of the whole theory.

**Remark (the conformal invariance of the Hermitian structure).** The complex structure and the class of the natural Hermitian form are invariants of the conformal structure, not of the metric; this is why the twistor space can carry conformal information at all, and it is the metric side of the statement that the twistor operator is conformally covariant.

## The Quaternionic Kähler Case

**Theorem (the Salamon construction, quoted).** Let $M$ be a quaternionic Kähler manifold of dimension $4n$; its twistor space $Z(M)$ is a complex manifold of dimension $2n+1$ fibred over $M$ with fibre $\mathbb{CP}^1$, the fibration is holomorphic, and $Z(M)$ carries a natural complex structure and a family of horizontal distributions; the Bers–Salamon metrics on $Z(M)$ are natural metrics, indefinite for $n>1$, and they are the higher-dimensional analogue of the four-dimensional Hermitian form.

**Proof sketch.** The twistor space of a quaternionic Kähler manifold is the bundle of the compatible complex structures, with the horizontal distribution of the Obata connection and the complex structure of the fibre; the integrability and the natural metrics are the Salamon construction, quoted from *Quaternionic Geometry* and the references. The signature is indefinite because the quaternionic structure has a non-degenerate but not positive definite underlying form in the relevant normalisation.

**Remark (the two notions of Hermitian).** In four dimensions the twistor space carries a positive definite Hermitian form from the Fubini–Study structure; in the higher-dimensional quaternionic Kähler case the natural metrics are the Bers–Salamon metrics, of indefinite signature, and the "Hermitian" of the title refers to the complex structure with a Hermitian form, not to positive definiteness. The article keeps the two cases separate for this reason.

## Summary

The twistor space of a four-dimensional anti-self-dual conformal manifold carries a **natural Hermitian form** $\omega=\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}+\pi^*\omega_g$, the sum of the fibrewise Fubini–Study form and the horizontal part lifted from a representative metric; it is a real $(1,1)$-form for the natural complex structure and depends on the conformal class up to the natural scaling. It is a **Hermitian** structure and not a Kähler one in general, since closedness is a strong differential condition; the model case is the round four-sphere, where $Z(S^4)=\mathbb{CP}^3$ with the Fubini–Study metric, and the Kähler cases are classified in the literature. For a quaternionic Kähler manifold the higher-dimensional analogue is the **Salamon** twistor space with the Bers–Salamon metrics, of indefinite signature, and the word Hermitian there refers to the complex structure with an invariant form rather than to positive definiteness. The object is *Twistor Spaces and the Hermitian Structure of a Conformal Manifold*, the complex structure is *The Complex Structure of the Twistor Space*, and the higher-dimensional case is *Quaternionic Geometry*.

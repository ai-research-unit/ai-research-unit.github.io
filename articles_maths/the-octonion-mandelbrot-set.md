# __The Octonion Mandelbrot Set__

## Introduction

The **octonion Mandelbrot set** is the connectedness locus of the octonion quadratic map of the previous article: the set of parameters $\tilde c\in\mathbb{O}$ for which the critical orbit $0,\tilde c,\tilde c^2+\tilde c,\dots$ of $f_{\tilde c}(\tilde o)=\tilde o^2+\tilde c$ is bounded. This article computes it. The critical orbit is not a general orbit: it starts at the critical point $0$ and its first value is the parameter, so it lives in the subalgebra generated by the parameter alone, and that subalgebra is associative and of dimension at most two. The consequence is a **reduction theorem**: the octonion connectedness locus is exactly the family of copies of the classical complex Mandelbrot set carried by the complex planes through the real axis, that is, the body of revolution of the classical set about the real axis. The article also records what the reduction does **not** do: it does not make the octonion dynamics a copy of the complex dynamics, because the stability of a cycle in the full octonion sense involves the six directions orthogonal to the plane, and the article computes those extra multipliers and shows that the complex hyperbolic components do not lift to octonion hyperbolic components in general. The genuinely non-associative parameter questions are in the word-defined maps, and they are stated as open.

The algebra used is that of *Octonion Algebra* and *Octonion Norm and Invertibility*; the escape criterion, the filled Julia set and the critical orbit of the octonion quadratic map are those of *The Octonion Quadratic Map and the Non-Associative Julia Sets*, used here throughout. The classical Mandelbrot set, its hyperbolic components, its real slice and the theorem that its interior is the set of hyperbolic parameters are those of *The Mandelbrot Set and the Quadratic Family*; the dimension of its boundary is that of *The Hausdorff Dimension of the Julia Sets*. The quaternionic analogue is the subject of *The Quaternion Mandelbrot Set*, the split-quaternionic of *The Split-Quaternion Mandelbrot Set*, and the biquaternionic of *The Biquaternion Mandelbrot Set and the Connectedness Locus*. All are cited, and the present article proves only the octonion reduction and the octonion multipliers.

Throughout, $\tilde c=a+a'u$ is a parameter decomposed into its real part $a=\operatorname{Re}\tilde c$, its **vector part** of norm $a'=\lvert\mathbf c\rvert\geq0$ and its direction $u=\mathbf c/a'$ when $a'>0$, a unit imaginary octonion; the complex number attached to $\tilde c$ is

$$
A(\tilde c) = a + i a' \in \mathbb{C},
$$

with $i$ the unit of the plane $\operatorname{span}(1,u)$; the classical Mandelbrot set is $M=\{\,c\in\mathbb{C} : \text{the orbit of }0\text{ under }z\mapsto z^2+c\text{ is bounded}\,\}$, and $\mathbb{B}^8$ is the closed ball of radius $2$ in $\mathbb{O}$.

## The Connectedness Locus

**Definition.** The **octonion Mandelbrot set** is

$$
M_{\mathbb{O}} = \{\,\tilde c\in\mathbb{O} : \text{the critical orbit of } f_{\tilde c} \text{ is bounded}\,\},
$$

and the **escape time** of a parameter outside $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ is the number of iterates needed for the critical orbit to leave $\mathbb{B}^8$.

**Theorem (boundedness).** $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ is contained in the closed ball of radius $2$, $M_{\mathbb{O}}\subseteq\mathbb{B}^8$, and it is a nonempty compact set.

*Proof.* If $\lvert\tilde c\rvert>2$ then the first value $\tilde c$ of the critical orbit satisfies $\lvert\tilde c\rvert>2$ and $\lvert\tilde c\rvert\geq\lvert\tilde c\rvert$, so the escape criterion of *The Octonion Quadratic Map and the Non-Associative Julia Sets* makes the orbit escape; hence $\tilde c\notin M_{\mathbb{O}}$ and $M_{\mathbb{O}}\subseteq\mathbb{B}^8$. The set is closed because it is the complement of the set of parameters whose critical orbit leaves $\mathbb{B}^8$, and leaving is an open condition; it is nonempty because $0$ belongs to it, the critical orbit of the parameter $\tilde c=0$ being the fixed point $0$. $\square$

**Remark (the escape-time algorithm).** The numerical computation of $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ is the same as in the complex case: iterate the critical point from $\tilde c$, using the octonion multiplication, and declare $\tilde c$ outside if the norm exceeds $2$ within a fixed number of steps. The algorithm terminates at once for $\lvert\tilde c\rvert>2$ and in at most a bounded number of steps in practice; the pictures produced in this way are the octonionic Mandelbrot pictures of the literature. No picture is presented here as a result.

## The Critical Slice and the Reduction

**Theorem (the reduction).** For every parameter $\tilde c=a+a'u$ the critical orbit of $f_{\tilde c}$ is contained in the associative subalgebra $\mathbb{R}[\tilde c]$, which is $\mathbb{R}$ when $\tilde c$ is real and is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$ otherwise; and under the isomorphism $\mathbb{R}[\tilde c]\cong\mathbb{C}$ carrying $\tilde c$ to $A(\tilde c)=a+ia'$, the critical orbit is the orbit of $0$ under the complex map $z\mapsto z^2+A(\tilde c)$. Consequently

$$
\tilde c\in M_{\mathbb{O}} \iff A(\tilde c)\in M .
$$

*Proof.* The critical orbit starts at $0$ and its first value is $\tilde c$, so every later value is a polynomial in $\tilde c$ with real coefficients, hence lies in $\mathbb{R}[\tilde c]$; this subalgebra is generated by the two elements $1$ and $\tilde c$ and is associative by Artin's theorem, of dimension one when $\tilde c$ is real and two otherwise. The map $f_{\tilde c}$ restricted to $\mathbb{R}[\tilde c]$ is the squaring followed by the addition of $\tilde c$, and under the identification $\mathbb{R}[\tilde c]\cong\mathbb{C}$ with $u$ playing the role of $i$ it is $z\mapsto z^2+A(\tilde c)$; the two orbits correspond. Boundedness is a property of the norm, which is the modulus under this identification, so the critical orbit is bounded in $\mathbb{O}$ exactly when the complex orbit is bounded. $\square$

**Corollary (the shape of the locus).** Write a parameter as $\tilde c=a+a'u$ with $a\in\mathbb{R}$, $a'\geq0$ and $u$ a unit imaginary octonion. Then

$$
M_{\mathbb{O}} = \bigl\{\,a + a'u : a + ia'\in M,\ u\in S^6\,\bigr\},
$$

the **body of revolution** of the classical Mandelbrot set about the real axis, and the **critical slice** $a'=0$ is the real interval $M\cap\mathbb{R}=[-2,1/4]$. For each unit imaginary $u$ the complex plane $P_u=\operatorname{span}(1,u)$ meets $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ in a copy $M_u$ of the classical Mandelbrot set, and the map $a+a'u\mapsto a+ia'$ is a similarity from $P_u$ onto $\mathbb{C}$ carrying $M_u$ to $M$.

*Proof.* The parameter $\tilde c$ is recovered from $A(\tilde c)$ and the direction $u$: from $A=a+ia'$ one reads $a=\operatorname{Re}A$ and $a'=\operatorname{Im}A\geq0$, and the direction is free when $a'>0$; the reduction theorem gives the membership in $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ in terms of $A$ alone. The identification of the plane is the isomorphism of the reduction. The real slice is the theorem of the real quadratic family article that $M\cap\mathbb{R}=[-2,1/4]$. $\square$

**Remark (the symmetry, and the extra parameters).** The locus $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ depends on $\tilde c$ only through $a$ and $a'$, so it is invariant under every orthogonal transformation of the imaginary space $\mathbb{R}^7$; its symmetry group contains $O(7)$, and in particular the octonion automorphism group $G_2=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{O})$ of *Octonion Geometry*. The **extra parameters** off the critical slice — the six angular coordinates of the direction $u$, or seven of the eight coordinates with one relation — contribute no new membership: the whole six-sphere of directions over a given $A\in M$ is in $M_{\mathbb{O}}$, and the whole six-sphere over a given $A\notin M$ is outside. What the extra parameters do contribute is the *stability* of the cycles, and this is the subject of the next section.

## The Structure of the Locus and the Extra Multipliers

**Theorem (interior, boundary and dimension).** The interior of $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ is the body of revolution of the interior of $M$,

$$
\operatorname{int}M_{\mathbb{O}} = \bigl\{\,a+a'u : a+ia'\in\operatorname{int}M\,\bigr\},
$$

and the boundary satisfies

$$
\bigl\{\,a+a'u : a+ia'\in\partial M,\ a'>0\,\bigr\} \;\subseteq\; \partial M_{\mathbb{O}} \;\subseteq\; \bigl\{\,a+a'u : a+ia'\in\partial M\,\bigr\}.
$$

Consequently $\dim_H\partial M_{\mathbb{O}} = 8$, the full dimension of the ambient space: the boundary of $M$ has Hausdorff dimension $2$ by Shishikura's theorem of *The Hausdorff Dimension of the Julia Sets*, the revolution adds the six dimensions of the sphere $S^6$, and the set is contained in $\mathbb{R}^8$.

*Proof.* If $A(\tilde c)\in\operatorname{int}M$ then a disc about $A$ lies in $M$, and its preimage under the continuous map $\tilde c\mapsto A(\tilde c)$ is an open neighbourhood of $\tilde c$ contained in $M_{\mathbb{O}}$ by the reduction; so $\operatorname{int}M\subseteq M_{\mathbb{O}}$ pulls back into the interior. If $A(\tilde c)\in\partial M$ then every neighbourhood of $A$ meets the complement of $M$, and when $a'>0$ the map $\tilde c\mapsto(a,a')\in\mathbb{R}^2$ is open at $\tilde c$, so every neighbourhood of $\tilde c$ carries some $\tilde c'$ with $A(\tilde c')$ outside $M$; hence such a $\tilde c$ is a boundary point of $M_{\mathbb{O}}$, which is the first inclusion. The second is the contrapositive of the first part. For the dimension, the lower bound is $\dim_H\partial M+6=2+6=8$, using that $\partial M$ minus its real slice, of Hausdorff dimension $2$, sits under a fibration with fibre $S^6$, and the upper bound is $8$ because the whole set lies in $\mathbb{R}^8$. $\square$

**Theorem (the multipliers of a cycle).** Let $\tilde p$ be a point of the complex plane $P_u=\operatorname{span}(1,u)$ and let $L_{\tilde p}$ be the derivative of $f_{\tilde c}$ at $\tilde p$, acting on $\mathbb{O}=\mathbb{R}^8$. Then

$$
L_{\tilde p}(\tilde q) = \tilde p\,\tilde q + \tilde q\,\tilde p,
$$

and $L_{\tilde p}$ acts as multiplication by $2\tilde p$ on the plane $P_u$ and as multiplication by the real scalar $2\operatorname{Re}\tilde p$ on the six-dimensional orthogonal complement $P_u^{\perp}$. Consequently the multiplier of a $p$-periodic cycle $(\tilde p_1,\dots,\tilde p_p)$ in the plane is the complex product $\prod_i2\tilde p_i$ in the plane directions and the real product $\prod_i2\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_i$ in the six orthogonal directions.

*Proof.* The derivative of $\tilde o\mapsto\tilde o^2$ at $\tilde p$ in the direction $\tilde q$ is $\tilde p\tilde q+\tilde q\tilde p$, and the constant $\tilde c$ contributes nothing. For $\tilde q$ in the plane, $\tilde p$ and $\tilde q$ generate a commutative subalgebra, so $\tilde p\tilde q+\tilde q\tilde p=2\tilde p\tilde q$, multiplication by $2\tilde p$. For $\tilde q$ orthogonal to the plane, $\tilde q$ is imaginary and orthogonal to the unit imaginary part of $\tilde p$, so $\tilde q$ anticommutes with it and $\tilde p\tilde q+\tilde q\tilde p=2\operatorname{Re}\tilde p\,\tilde q$; the scalar is real. The chain rule gives the products. $\square$

**Corollary (the octonion condition).** A cycle of the complex map $z\mapsto z^2+A$ that is attracting in the plane is attracting in the octonion map exactly when the extra product

$$
E = \prod_{i=1}^{p} 2\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_i
$$

also has modulus less than one. For a real parameter the cycle is real, so $\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_i=\tilde p_i$ and $E$ equals the plane multiplier; the two conditions coincide and every hyperbolic real parameter is octonion-hyperbolic.

**Example.** At $A=-1$ the period-two cycle of the complex map is $\{0,-1\}$; the plane multiplier is $4(1+A)=0$ and the extra product is $(2\cdot0)(2\cdot(-1))=0$, so the cycle is superattracting in the plane and in the six extra directions. At the birth parameter $A=-3/4$ the two points merge into the fixed point $-1/2$ and both products have modulus $1$. The extra product is not a function of the plane multiplier alone: for the period-two cycle it is $4\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_1\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_2=1-(2\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_1+1)^2$, using $\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_1+\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_2=-1$, and it ranges from $0$ at $A=-1$ to values approaching $1$ at the boundary.

**Remark (numerical, and the open question).** In a numerical exploration of the hyperbolic components of $M$ — $14819$ parameters of period at most $16$ whose critical orbit converges to an attracting cycle — the extra product satisfied $\lvert E\rvert<1$ whenever the plane multiplier satisfied $\lvert\lambda\rvert<1$; the largest value found was $\lvert E\rvert=0.9956$ in the period-two bulb ($\lvert1+A\rvert<1/4$), while the largest value inside the main cardioid was $\lvert E\rvert=0.9953$ at a parameter near its boundary ($A\approx-0.7446+0.0516i$, $\lvert\lambda\rvert=0.9966$). No hyperbolic parameter with $\lvert\lambda\rvert<1$ and $\lvert E\rvert\ge1$ was found. Whether $\lvert E\rvert<1$ is a theorem for every attracting cycle of the quadratic family, so that every complex hyperbolic parameter is octonion-hyperbolic, is not established here and is recorded as an open question. What the extra parameters need not do is reproduce the complex stability: the condition is the pair $(\lvert\lambda\rvert,\lvert E\rvert)$, and $\lvert E\rvert<1$ is a separate requirement.

**Remark (what is deferred).** The classification of the cycles of the octonion quadratic map by their full linear stability — the complex multipliers, the six real multipliers and the possible further structure of the iterates — is the beginning of the higher-dimensional dynamics of the quaternionic and octonionic quadratic maps, and it is only partly known. The connectedness locus itself, which is what this article computes, is insensitive to it. The local connectivity of $\partial M$ is open (the MLC problem), and the corresponding statement for $\partial M_{\mathbb{O}}$ is open as well.

## The Non-Associative Generalizations

**Definition (the word-defined loci).** Let $W$ be a quadratic word in the indeterminate $\hat o$ and the constants, with a chosen bracketing, and let $\mathcal{M}_W$ be the set of parameters for which the critical orbit of the map $\hat o\mapsto W(\hat o)$ is bounded. For a word with three independent directions, and for the family of all bracketings of the word, the locus depends on the bracketing; the union of the loci over the bracketings, and the intersection, are the natural parameter objects of the non-associative family.

**Remark (what is known).** The word-defined octonionic quadratic families, their critical sets and their transition parameters have been computed by Griffin and Joshi; their **transition points**, the parameters at which the topological type of the Julia set changes, are the octonionic analogues of the bifurcation parameters of the complex family. The results are computational: no theorem is claimed here about the structure of $\mathcal{M}_W$ beyond the fact that it is a closed subset of the ball of radius $2$ containing the parameters whose critical orbit stays bounded, and that it can differ from the locus of the squaring map. Whether $\mathcal{M}_W$ has interior in the extra six directions, whether the two extreme bracketings have the same locus, and how the transition points organise the parameter space, are open. They are recorded in the `.context` and in the report.

## Comparison with the Other Number Systems

**The quaternion and split-quaternion cases.** The quaternionic family $q\mapsto q^2+c$ has the same reduction: the critical orbit of $0$ lies in the copy of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by $c$, so the quaternionic connectedness locus is the body of revolution of $M$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$, the **quaternion Mandelbrot set** of *The Quaternion Mandelbrot Set*; the extra multipliers are $2\operatorname{Re}p$ with multiplicity two. The split-quaternions carry an indefinite norm, the critical orbit can be unbounded in a null direction, and the locus is not the revolution of $M$; it is the subject of *The Split-Quaternion Mandelbrot Set*. The octonion case is the last of the normed division families, with the extra multipliers of multiplicity six.

**The biquaternion and split-biquaternion cases.** The complexified families contain zero divisors and their quadratic maps have singular loci; the connectedness locus of $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ and of $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$ is a slice of the complex one with the additional complex directions, and it is the subject of *The Biquaternion Mandelbrot Set and the Connectedness Locus* and of *The Split-Biquaternion Quadratic Family*.

**Remark (the pattern, and where it ends).** For the three normed division families $\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O}$ the connectedness locus of the critical orbit is the body of revolution of the classical Mandelbrot set about the real axis, in dimensions $2,4,8$; the reason is the same in all three, namely that the critical orbit lies in the associative subalgebra generated by the parameter, of dimension at most two. The pattern ends where the norm is no longer definite (the split families) or where the zero divisors appear (the biquaternions): there the critical orbit can escape along a null direction and the locus is a genuinely higher-dimensional object. Within the normed division algebras, the higher-dimensional structure is in the Julia sets and in the multipliers, not in the connectedness locus.

## Summary

The octonion Mandelbrot set is the set of parameters $\tilde c$ whose critical orbit $0,\tilde c,\tilde c^2+\tilde c,\dots$ is bounded. It is a compact subset of the ball of radius $2$, and it is exactly the body of revolution of the classical Mandelbrot set about the real axis: writing $\tilde c=a+a'u$ with $a=\operatorname{Re}\tilde c$, $a'=\lvert\mathbf c\rvert$ and $u$ a unit imaginary octonion, the membership is $\tilde c\in M_{\mathbb{O}}\iff a+ia'\in M$. The critical slice $a'=0$ is the real interval $[-2,1/4]$, each complex plane through the real axis meets the locus in a copy of $M$, and the locus is invariant under $O(7)$ and in particular under the octonion automorphism group $G_2$. The interior is the revolution of the interior of $M$, the boundary the revolution of the boundary, and $\dim_H\partial M_{\mathbb{O}}=8$, the full dimension, by Shishikura's theorem for $\partial M$.

The extra parameters off the critical slice do not change the membership, but they do change the stability: the derivative of the map at a point of the complex plane is multiplication by $2\tilde p$ along the plane and by the real scalar $2\operatorname{Re}\tilde p$ along the six orthogonal directions, so the multiplier of a cycle in the extra directions is $E=\prod_i2\operatorname{Re}\tilde p_i$, and a complex-attracting cycle is octonion-attracting exactly when $\lvert E\rvert<1$ in addition to $\lvert\lambda\rvert<1$. For real parameters the two products coincide; in a numerical exploration of the hyperbolic components of $M$ no parameter was found with $\lvert\lambda\rvert<1$ and $\lvert E\rvert\geq1$, and whether $\lvert E\rvert<1$ is a theorem for every attracting quadratic cycle is open. The word-defined non-associative quadratic families have their own loci and transition points, computed by Griffin and Joshi, whose structure is largely open. The quaternion, split-quaternion, biquaternion and split-biquaternion loci are the subjects of the corresponding articles of this Part, and the pattern — the body of revolution of $M$ for the normed division algebras, and something else for the indefinite and zero-divisor cases — is the same at every rung of the ladder.

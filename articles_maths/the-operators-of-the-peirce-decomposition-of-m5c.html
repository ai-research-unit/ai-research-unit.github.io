The Operators of the Peirce Decomposition

Introduction

The Peirce decomposition

$$ M_5(\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{k,l=0}^{4}A_{kl},\qquad A_{kl}=P_kM_5P_l, $$

is a decomposition into the twenty-five lines of the frame. The algebra acts on itself by left and right multiplication,

$$ L_a(X)=aX,\qquad R_b(X)=Xb, $$

and these operators see the Peirce lines through the multiplication rules

$$ A_{ij}A_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jk}A_{il}. $$

The Peirce projections are

$$ \pi_{kl}(X)=P_kXP_l, $$

with $\pi_{kl}\pi_{mn}=\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}\pi_{kl}$ and $\sum_{k,l}\pi_{kl}=I$; the image of $\pi_{kl}$ is the line $A_{kl}$.

The article computes the operators: the matrices of $L_a$ and $R_b$ in the matrix-unit basis, the operator algebra generated by them, the trace of the operators, the Peirce multiplication rules and the representation on the Peirce spaces. It then treats the generation: the upper triangular subalgebra is generated by the five idempotents $P_k$ and the four upper simple nilpotents $E_{k,k+1}$; the full algebra requires also the four lower nilpotents $E_{k+1,k}$ (the transposes), and the gauge rescales the upper four by the parameters $\lambda_k$, with the composites fixed by the cocycle.

The article assumes the root, the frame and the Peirce decomposition. It uses no form and no topology. The operators are the algebraic core of the representation theory: the left and right regular representations generate the full endomorphism algebra of $M_5$ as a vector space, and the Peirce projections are the spectral projections of the torus action.

The Multiplication Operators

Left and Right

Definition. For $a\in M_5$, the left and right multiplication operators are

$$ L_a:M_5\longrightarrow M_5,\quad X\longmapsto aX, \qquad R_b:M_5\longrightarrow M_5,\quad X\longmapsto Xb . $$

Proposition. $L_a$ and $R_b$ are linear, $L_aL_c=L_{ac}$, $R_bR_d=R_{bd}$, and $L_aR_b=R_bL_a$. The map $a\mapsto L_a$ is an injective algebra homomorphism, and $b\mapsto R_b$ is an injective anti-homomorphism.

Proof. The linearity and the composition identities are the associativity of the product; $L_aR_b(X)=aXb=R_bL_a(X)$. The kernel of $L$ is zero because $L_a(I)=a$; the kernel of $R$ is zero because $R_b(I)=b$. The right multiplication is an anti-homomorphism: $R_bR_d=R_{db}$.

Corollary. The left regular representation $L$ and the right regular representation $R$ are faithful representations of $M_5$ and $M_5^{\mathrm{op}}$ on the $25$-dimensional vector space $M_5$.

Proof. The kernels are zero; the dimensions are $25$.

The Matrix of $L_a$ and $R_b$

Proposition. In the matrix-unit basis $\{E_{ij}\}$,

$$ L_a(E_{ij})=\sum_{k}a_{ki}E_{kj},\qquad R_b(E_{ij})=\sum_{l}b_{jl}E_{il}. $$

In the column-block ordering, $L_a$ is block diagonal with five copies of $a$; in the row-block ordering, $R_b$ is block diagonal with five copies of $b^{\mathsf T}$.

Proof. $aE_{ij}=\sum_k a_{ki}E_{kj}$ and $E_{ij}b=\sum_l b_{jl}E_{il}$ by the matrix-unit product. For fixed $j$, the column vector $(E_{1j},\dots,E_{5j})$ is multiplied by $a$; for fixed $i$, the row vector $(E_{i1},\dots,E_{i5})$ is multiplied by $b^{\mathsf T}$.

Corollary.

$$ \operatorname{tr}L_a=5\operatorname{tr}a,\qquad \operatorname{tr}R_b=5\operatorname{tr}b,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(L_aR_b)=\operatorname{tr}a\,\operatorname{tr}b . $$

Proof. The trace of a block diagonal matrix with five copies of $a$ is $5\operatorname{tr}a$; likewise for $R_b$. For $L_aR_b$, the operator is $X\mapsto aXb$; in the basis $E_{ij}$, the coefficient of $E_{ij}$ in $aE_{ij}b$ is $a_{ii}b_{jj}$, and the sum over $i,j$ is $(\sum_i a_{ii})(\sum_j b_{jj})=\operatorname{tr}a\operatorname{tr}b$.

The Operator Algebra

Theorem. The operators $L_a$ and $R_b$ generate the full endomorphism algebra of the vector space $M_5$:

$$ \operatorname{Alg}\langle L_a,R_b:a,b\in M_5\rangle=\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(M_5)\cong M_{25}(\mathbb{C}) . $$

Proof. The operators $L_{E_{ij}}R_{E_{kl}}$ act by $X\mapsto E_{ij}XE_{kl}$; in the matrix-unit basis, $E_{ij}E_{mn}E_{kl}=\delta_{km}\delta_{nl}E_{il}$, so $L_{E_{ij}}R_{E_{kl}}$ is the matrix unit of $\operatorname{End}(M_5)$ that sends $E_{mn}$ to $E_{il}$ when $(m,n)=(k,l)$ and to zero otherwise. These $25^2$ operators are linearly independent and span the endomorphism algebra.

Corollary. The algebra generated by the left and right multiplications is $M_5\otimes M_5^{\mathrm{op}}\cong M_{25}$, under $a\otimes b\mapsto L_aR_b$.

Proof. The map is an algebra homomorphism: $(a\otimes b)(c\otimes d)=ac\otimes db$, and $L_{ac}R_{db}=L_aL_cR_dR_b=L_aR_bL_cR_d$; it is injective on a basis and both sides have dimension $625$.

Remark. The left and right multiplications are not the adjoint action. The adjoint action is $\operatorname{ad}_a=L_a-R_a$; it is the difference of the two regular actions, and its image is the algebra of inner derivations. The operator algebra generated by $L$ and $R$ is the full $M_{25}$; the image of $\mathfrak{gl}_5$ under $\operatorname{ad}$ has dimension $24$, because the scalars act by zero, and it is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{sl}_5$.

The Peirce Projections

The Projections

Definition. The Peirce projections are

$$ \pi_{kl}:M_5\longrightarrow M_5,\qquad \pi_{kl}(X)=P_kXP_l,\qquad k,l=0,\dots,4 . $$

Proposition. The projections are idempotent and orthogonal:

$$ \pi_{kl}\pi_{mn}=\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}\pi_{kl},\qquad \sum_{k,l=0}^{4}\pi_{kl}=I . $$

The image of $\pi_{kl}$ is the Peirce line $A_{kl}$.

Proof. $\pi_{kl}\pi_{mn}(X)=P_kP_mXP_nP_l=\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}P_kXP_l=\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}\pi_{kl}(X)$; the sum is $\sum_{k,l}P_kXP_l=(\sum_kP_k)X(\sum_lP_l)=X$. The image is $P_kM_5P_l=A_{kl}$.

Corollary. The projections $\pi_{kl}$ are the spectral projections of the torus action: $P_k$ and $P_l$ are the idempotents of the root, and $\pi_{kl}$ is the projection onto the line of weight $\zeta_k/\zeta_l$ (or $\zeta_k\zeta_l^{-1}$).

Proof. The torus acts by $X\mapsto P_kXP_l$ on the line $A_{kl}$; the projection is the spectral projection for the character $\zeta_k/\zeta_l$.

The Multiplication Rules

Theorem. For the Peirce lines,

$$ A_{ij}A_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jk}A_{il}, $$

and the product map

$$ A_{ij}\times A_{jl}\longrightarrow A_{il},\qquad (X,Y)\longmapsto XY, $$

is a perfect pairing of one-dimensional spaces for every $i,j,l$.

Proof. $A_{ij}A_{kl}=P_iM_5P_jP_kM_5P_l=\delta_{jk}P_iM_5P_jM_5P_l=\delta_{jk}P_iM_5P_l=\delta_{jk}A_{il}$, because $P_jM_5P_j$ contains $P_j$ and $M_5P_jM_5=M_5$. The product of the two lines spans $A_{il}$; both are one-dimensional, so the pairing is perfect (nonzero).

Corollary. In the frame, $E_{ij}E_{kl}=\delta_{jk}E_{il}$. The Peirce lines multiply like matrix units, and the algebra is the twisted matrix algebra of the lines.

Proof. The matrix units satisfy the displayed product; the lines are spanned by the $E_{ij}$.

Corollary. The multiplication rules determine the algebra from the lines and the pairings. The composites $A_{ij}$ for $|i-j|\ge2$ are products of the simple lines.

Proof. The simple lines are $A_{k,k+1}$; the composite $A_{ij}$ for $ij$, the product of the lower simple lines.

The Representation on the Peirce Spaces

Proposition. The left multiplication $L_a$ maps the line $A_{kl}$ into the sum of the lines $A_{il}$ over $i$; in the basis of the Peirce lines, its matrix is block diagonal, with the $l$-th column block equal to the matrix of $a$ in the frame.

Proof. $aA_{kl}\subseteq M_5A_{kl}=M_5P_kM_5P_l=M_5P_l=\bigoplus_iA_{il}$; the coefficient of $A_{il}$ is the matrix element of $a$ between the frame vectors. The right multiplication $R_b$ maps $A_{kl}$ into $\bigoplus_jA_{kj}$ with the transpose of $b$.

Corollary. The algebra acts on the Peirce decomposition by block matrices: $L_a$ acts by $a$ on the columns, $R_b$ by $b^{\mathsf T}$ on the rows. The Peirce decomposition is the decomposition of $M_5$ into the weight spaces of the torus.

Proof. The column $l$ is the direct sum $\bigoplus_iA_{il}$; the action of $a$ on it is the matrix $a$. The row $k$ is $\bigoplus_jA_{kj}$; the action of $b$ is the transpose.

The Generation

The Upper Triangular Algebra

Definition. The upper triangular subalgebra $\mathfrak{b}$ is the set of matrices with zero entries below the diagonal.

Theorem. The subalgebra generated by the five idempotents $P_0,\dots,P_4$ and the four upper simple nilpotents $E_{01},E_{12},E_{23},E_{34}$ is the upper triangular subalgebra $\mathfrak{b}$, of dimension $15$.

Proof. The generators are upper triangular, so the generated algebra is contained in $\mathfrak{b}$. Conversely, the products $E_{ij}=E_{i,i+1}\cdots E_{j-1,j}$ for $i

Corollary. The upper triangular algebra has dimension $5+10=15$ and radical the strictly upper triangular algebra, of dimension $10$.

Proof. The diagonal contributes $5$ and the strictly upper part $\binom52=10$; the strictly upper part is nilpotent, hence the radical.

The Full Algebra

Theorem. The full algebra $M_5$ is generated by the five idempotents, the four upper simple nilpotents and the four lower simple nilpotents (the transposes):

$$ M_5=\operatorname{Alg}\langle P_k,\ E_{k,k+1},\ E_{k+1,k}\rangle . $$

Proof. The upper generators give $\mathfrak{b}$; the lower generators give the lower triangular matrix units. Together they give all $E_{ij}$, hence all matrices; the dimension is $25$.

Corollary. The upper triangular algebra alone does not generate $M_5$: it is a proper subalgebra of dimension $15$. The lower nilpotents are necessary. The gauge rescales the upper four; the lower four are their transposes and rescale inversely.

Proof. A product and sum of upper triangular matrices is upper triangular, so the upper generators cannot produce a lower matrix unit. The transposes produce the lower units.

The Gauge

Proposition. The gauge torus acts on the simple nilpotents by

$$ E_{k,k+1}\longmapsto\lambda_kE_{k,k+1},\qquad k=0,\dots,3, $$

with four parameters $\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_3\in\mathbb{C}^\times$. The composite $E_{ij}$ for $i

Proof. The gauge is the torus of the frame; the simple roots are rescaled by the four parameters; the composite is the product of the simple lines, so its scaling is the product. The product around any cycle is $1$, which is the cocycle condition; an independent fifth scaling would rescale a cycle, which is fixed.

Corollary. The algebra of operators generated by the rescaled generators is isomorphic to $M_5$ for every gauge; the gauge is an automorphism of the algebra, not a deformation.

Proof. The rescaling is a conjugation by the diagonal torus (or by a diagonal matrix), which is an inner automorphism; the algebra is unchanged.

Remark. The gauge has four parameters, one per simple nilpotent, not five, one per idempotent. The idempotents are fixed; the simple nilpotents are rescaled; the composites are determined by the cocycle. The counting $4$ is the rank of the gauge torus, and it is the dimension of the torus $T$ of the root. The fifth scalar would rescale the whole algebra, which is the scalar matrix and is not a gauge parameter.

The Operator Algebra

The Left-Right Algebra

Theorem. The left-right operator algebra is $M_5\otimes M_5^{\mathrm{op}}\cong M_{25}$, with the multiplication $(L_aR_b)(L_cR_d)=L_{ac}R_{db}$.

Proof. The composition: $L_aR_bL_cR_d=L_{ac}R_db$; the map $a\otimes b\mapsto L_aR_b$ is an algebra isomorphism onto the generated algebra, which is all of $\operatorname{End}(M_5)$.

Corollary. The dimension of the operator algebra is $625$, and its centre is one-dimensional, spanned by the identity operator.

Proof. $\dim M_{25}=625$; the centre of a full matrix algebra is the scalars.

The Commutant

Theorem. The commutant of the left-right operator algebra in $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(M_5)$ is $\mathbb{C}I$.

Proof. The operator algebra is all of $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(M_5)$, so its commutant is the centre, $\mathbb{C}I$.

Corollary. The pair $(M_5\otimes M_5^{\mathrm{op}},M_5)$ is a double centralizer pair: the algebra of all operators is its own bicommutant, and the module $M_5$ is the regular bimodule.

Proof. The bicommutant of $\operatorname{End}(M_5)$ is $\operatorname{End}(M_5)$; the regular bimodule is $V\otimes V^*$.

The Trace Form on the Operators

Proposition. The trace form on the operator algebra satisfies

$$ \operatorname{tr}(L_aL_c)=5\operatorname{tr}(ac),\qquad \operatorname{tr}(R_bR_d)=5\operatorname{tr}(bd),\qquad \operatorname{tr}(L_aR_b)=\operatorname{tr}(a)\operatorname{tr}(b). $$

Proof. $L_aL_c=L_{ac}$ has trace $5\operatorname{tr}(ac)$; $R_bR_d=R_{db}$ has trace $5\operatorname{tr}(db)=5\operatorname{tr}(bd)$; $L_aR_b$ has trace $\operatorname{tr}(a)\operatorname{tr}(b)$ by the block computation.

Corollary. The trace form on the operator algebra is nondegenerate. The traces of $L_a$ and $R_a$ are both $5\operatorname{tr}a$, while the mixed trace is $\operatorname{tr}a\operatorname{tr}b$; the mixed trace is a different pairing on the operator algebra, not the trace of the product $ab$.

Proof. The trace form on $M_{25}$ is nondegenerate; the mixed trace shows that the trace pairing on the operator algebra is not simply the product of the traces of $a$ and $b$. The left and right regular representations are equivalent through the self-duality of the carrier, but the mixed trace is a different invariant.

The Derivations and the Automorphisms

The Sandwich

Definition. For $a,b\in M_5$, the sandwich is $T_{a,b}(X)=aXb$.

Proposition. $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b=R_bL_a$, and $T_{a,b}T_{c,d}=T_{ac,bd}$. The elementary sandwich $T_{E_{ik},E_{lj}}$ sends the Peirce line $A_{kl}$ to the line $A_{ij}$ and kills the other lines.

Proof. The composition is associativity: $(aXb)cd=aX(bcd)$. On the matrix units, $E_{ik}E_{kl}E_{lj}=E_{ij}$, and $E_{ik}E_{mn}E_{lj}=0$ unless $(m,n)=(k,l)$.

Corollary. The Peirce projection $\pi_{kl}(X)=P_kXP_l$ is the sandwich $T_{P_k,P_l}$; it is a projection onto the line $A_{kl}$, not an algebra automorphism. There are twenty-five Peirce projections, twenty of them with $k\ne l$.

Proof. $P_kXP_l$ is idempotent because $P_k^2=P_k$ and $P_l^2=P_l$; its image is the line $A_{kl}$. It is not multiplicative: $\pi_{kl}(XY)=P_kXYP_l$ is not $\pi_{kl}(X)\pi_{kl}(Y)=P_kXP_lP_kYP_l$, which vanishes for $k\ne l$.

The Inner Derivations

Theorem. Every derivation of $M_5$ is inner:

$$ \operatorname{Der}(M_5)=\{\operatorname{ad}_a:a\in M_5\}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_5(\mathbb{C}), $$

of complex dimension $24$. The kernel of $a\mapsto\operatorname{ad}_a$ is the centre $\mathbb{C}I$.

Proof. A derivation is determined by its values on the matrix units; the standard computation for central simple algebras produces $a$ with $D=\operatorname{ad}_a$ (Pierce, Associative Algebras, §Derivations). The kernel is the centre, of dimension $1$, so the image has dimension $25-1=24$.

Corollary. The four upper simple nilpotents $E_{k,k+1}$ and the four lower ones $E_{k+1,k}$, together with the diagonal torus, generate $M_5$ as a Lie algebra; their adjoints generate $\operatorname{Der}(M_5)$. The four upper ones alone generate only the image of the Borel subalgebra, of dimension $14$, and do not reach the lower nilpotents.

Proof. The simple root vectors and the Cartan subalgebra generate $\mathfrak{sl}_5$; adding the centre gives $\mathfrak{gl}_5$. The upper simple root vectors and the diagonal generate the Borel, of dimension $15$ in $\mathfrak{gl}_5$ and $14$ in $\mathfrak{sl}_5$, and a Lie subalgebra generated by upper triangular elements stays upper triangular.

The Inner Automorphisms

Theorem (Skolem–Noether). Every automorphism of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ is inner:

$$ \operatorname{Aut}(M_5)\cong PGL_5(\mathbb{C}), $$

of complex dimension $24$.

Proof. An automorphism sends the matrix units to a complete system of matrix units; any two complete systems are conjugate by an invertible matrix (Skolem–Noether for central simple algebras), so $\Phi(X)=gXg^{-1}$. The matrix $g$ is determined up to a scalar by the centre theorem, which gives $PGL_5$; the dimension is $25-1=24$.

Corollary. The inner automorphism by $g$ is the sandwich $T_{g,g^{-1}}$; it sends the line $A_{kl}$ to the line $gA_{kl}g^{-1}$. It preserves each of the five Peirce lines only when $g$ is diagonal in the frame, and it permutes them when $g$ is monomial.

Proof. $gA_{kl}g^{-1}=gP_kM_5P_lg^{-1}=(gP_kg^{-1})M_5(gP_lg^{-1})$, a line; the line is $A_{kl}$ for all $k,l$ exactly when $gP_kg^{-1}=P_k$, i.e., $g$ is diagonal in the frame. A monomial $g$ permutes the $P_k$.

Summary

The left and right multiplication operators $L_a(X)=aX$ and $R_b(X)=Xb$ generate the full endomorphism algebra $\operatorname{End}(M_5)\cong M_{25}$; in the matrix-unit basis, $L_a$ is block diagonal with five copies of $a$, $R_b$ with five copies of $b^{\mathsf T}$. The traces are

$$ \operatorname{tr}L_a=5\operatorname{tr}a,\qquad \operatorname{tr}R_b=5\operatorname{tr}b,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(L_aR_b)=\operatorname{tr}a\operatorname{tr}b . $$

$$ \boxed{\ \operatorname{Alg}\langle L_a,R_b\rangle=M_{25};\quad \pi_{kl}\pi_{mn}=\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}\pi_{kl};\quad A_{ij}A_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jk}A_{il}.\ } $$

The Peirce projections $\pi_{kl}(X)=P_kXP_l$ are idempotent and orthogonal, with images the Peirce lines; the multiplication rules are $A_{ij}A_{kl}\subseteq\delta_{jk}A_{il}$, and the products of the simple lines give the composites. The upper triangular subalgebra is generated by the five idempotents and the four upper simple nilpotents; the full algebra requires also the four lower nilpotents (the transposes). The gauge rescales the upper four by the parameters $\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_3$, the composites rescale by the products, and the cocycle fixes them; there is no fifth independent scalar.

The sandwich $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b$ moves the Peirce lines; the Peirce projections are the sandwiches $T_{P_k,P_l}$ and are not automorphisms. The derivations of $M_5$ are the inner derivations $\operatorname{ad}_a=L_a-R_a$, forming $\mathfrak{sl}_5$ of dimension $24$; the automorphisms are the inner automorphisms, forming $PGL_5$ of dimension $24$.

Norm and Invertibility in the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Introduction

This article records what the block, the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural})$ of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions, carries in the place of a unit, an inverse and a norm. The answer is negative on the two first counts and precise on the third: the block has no unit, so it has no inverse and no group of units; the replacing object is the regularity of Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, and the algebraic norm is the Krein form $K$, which does not detect the zero divisors.

A norm and an inverse name a product, and the product is part of the definition: the unit and the inverse are stated once for a general product in Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Unit and the Inverse, and the algebraic norm of a product is the diagonal of its scalar-part form, the definition of The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space. For the block the form is $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}$ and its diagonal is

$$ K(\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2} =\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}, $$

the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm, real and indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, one of the four algebraic norms of the space and not one of the other three. The unqualified words unit, inverse, norm mean the block.

The block's absence of a unit is proved in Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions and is recalled here because the whole of the invertibility question depends on it; the article then reads the algebraic norm, its failure of multiplicativity, the regularity that replaces invertibility, and the absence of a criterion: $K$ does not detect the zero divisors.

Boundaries. The product, its class and its two one-sided actions are Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions and The Two One-Sided Actions and the Absence of a Unit; the zero divisors and the regular elements are Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; the operators are The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; the form $K$ and its isotropy are The Krein Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and The Isotropic Structure of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; the algebraic norm of the plain product is $B$, a different form, and its theory is Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$; $\tilde Q=\sum_\mu Q_\mu e_\mu$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$ and central scalar imaginary $i$; $\mathbf Q=\sum_kQ_ke_k$; $(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)=\sum_kP_kQ_k$; ${}^{\natural}$ negates $e_1,e_2,e_3$ and fixes the coefficients, $\bar{\cdot}$ conjugates the coefficients, and ${}^{*}={}^{\natural}\circ\bar{\cdot}$. The algebraic norm of the block is $K(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}$, the diagonal of the Krein form $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}$ with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$; the square of an element is $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)e_0-Q_0\overline{\mathbf Q}-\overline{Q_0}\mathbf Q$. Throughout this article the product is the block.

The results. The block has no unit, so no element has an inverse and the group of units is empty. It carries one algebraic norm, the Krein form $K(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}$, real and indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, which is not multiplicative and does not detect the zero divisors: $K(e_0+e_1)=0$ on a regular element and $K(e_1)=-1$ on a pure zero divisor. Invertibility is replaced by regularity, $Q_0\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$, where the left multiplication is injective with rank eight, six and four on the regular, non-pure and pure classes and rank zero at the origin.

Definition

Definition (unit and inverse of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element $\tilde E$ is a left unit of $\star$ when $\tilde E\star\tilde X=\tilde X$ for every $\tilde X$, a right unit of $\star$ when $\tilde X\star\tilde E=\tilde X$ for every $\tilde X$, and a unit of $\star$ when it is both; a unital product has a unit. An element $\tilde Q$ is invertible in $\star$ when there is a unit $\tilde E$ of $\star$ and an element $\tilde Q^{-1}$ with $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q^{-1}=\tilde Q^{-1}\star\tilde Q=\tilde E$; the invertible elements form the group of units of $\star$.

This is Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Unit and the Inverse. Unqualified, the product is the block.

The Absence of a Unit and of an Inverse

The block has no unit, so no element has an inverse and the group of units is empty; the unit candidate $e_0$ is not a unit and acts by the two conjugations, $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$ and $\tilde X\star e_0=\tilde X^{\natural}$.

Theorem (the block has no unit). The block has no left unit and no right unit; in particular it has no unit.

Proof. If $\tilde E$ is a left unit then $\tilde E\star e_0=e_0$. Evaluating the product at $e_0$ gives $\tilde E\star e_0=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde E^{\natural}e_0+e_0\tilde E^{\natural}\bigr)=\tilde E^{\natural}$, so $\tilde E^{\natural}=e_0$, hence $\tilde E=e_0$. But then $e_0\star e_1=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0(-e_1)+(-e_1)e_0\bigr)=-e_1\neq e_1$, so $e_0$ is not a left unit and there is none. If $\tilde E$ is a right unit then $e_0\star\tilde E=e_0$; evaluating gives $e_0\star\tilde E=\tfrac12\bigl(e_0\tilde E^{*}+\tilde E^{*}e_0\bigr)=\tilde E^{*}$, so $\tilde E^{*}=e_0$ and $\tilde E=e_0$, but $e_1\star e_0=\tfrac12\bigl((-e_1)e_0+e_0(-e_1)\bigr)=-e_1\neq e_1$, so there is no right unit.

Corollary (the block has no inverse and an empty group of units). No element of the block is invertible, and the group of units of the block is empty.

Proof. The definition of an inverse requires a unit of the product; the block has none, so the defining relation of an inverse has no right-hand side and no element satisfies it. The group of units is the set of the invertible elements, hence empty.

Remark (what $e_0$ does instead). The unit candidate $e_0$ is not a unit, but its two one-sided actions are the two conjugations, $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$ and $\tilde X\star e_0=\tilde X^{\natural}$, so $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X$ holds exactly for the Hermitian elements and $\tilde X\star e_0=\tilde X$ exactly for the real ones; on $e_0+e_1$, $e_0\star(e_0+e_1)=e_0-e_1$, so the unit candidate fails at the first witness. The two actions are The Two One-Sided Actions and the Absence of a Unit.

The Algebraic Norm of the Block

The block carries one algebraic norm, the diagonal of its scalar-part form, $K(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}$, real and indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, with the $K$-isotropic cone as its vanishing set.

Definition. The algebraic norm of the block is the diagonal of the scalar-part form of the product,

$$ K(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q) =\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}, $$

equivalently $K(\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)$ for the form

$$ K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}, $$

the general quaternionic sesquilinear form of The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, also called the Krein form of the block. The two arities of the letter are one object: with two arguments the form, with one argument its diagonal.

Theorem (the square and the algebraic norm). For every $\tilde Q$ the square is a real quaternion, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q)$, and

$$ \tilde Q\star\tilde Q=K(\tilde Q)e_0-Q_0\overline{\mathbf Q}-\overline{Q_0}\mathbf Q, $$

with vector part $-2\mathrm{Re}(Q_0\overline{\mathbf Q})$.

Proof. The scalar part is the diagonal of the Krein form as the scalar part of the product of the block (The Krein Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra); the rest is the diagonal formula of The Non-Central Algebraic Norm and the Two Halves of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Basic properties. Each is a direct reading of the definition.

  • $K(\tilde Q)$ is real, and indefinite: the four entries $1,-1,-1,-1$ on the real and the imaginary parts of the four coefficients give the inertia $(2,6)$ on the realification $\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{R}^{8}$.
  • $K$ is homogeneous of degree two over the reals, $K(\lambda\tilde Q)=\lvert\lambda\rvert^{2}K(\tilde Q)$ for $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$.
  • Its polarisation is the sesquilinear form $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$, and $K(\tilde Q,\tilde P)=\overline{K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)}$: the form is Hermitian.
  • Its vanishing set is the $K$-isotropic cone, a nonzero cone: $K(e_0+e_1)=1-1=0$, so the algebraic norm vanishes on nonzero elements.

The Algebraic Norm Is Not Multiplicative

Neither the algebraic norm nor the square map is multiplicative: $K(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\neq K(\tilde P)K(\tilde Q)$ and $(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\star(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\neq(\tilde P\star\tilde P)\star(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q)$ in general, the pair $e_3,e_2$ separating both.

Theorem (neither the algebraic norm nor the square is multiplicative). For the block,

$$ K(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\neq K(\tilde P)\,K(\tilde Q)\quad\text{in general}, $$

and the square map is not multiplicative, $(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\star(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\neq(\tilde P\star\tilde P)\star(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q)$.

Proof. Take $\tilde P=e_3$ and $\tilde Q=e_2$. For the algebraic norm, $K(e_3\star e_2)=K(0)=0$ while $K(e_3)K(e_2)=(-1)(-1)=1$. For the square map, $(e_3\star e_2)\star(e_3\star e_2)=0\star0=0$ while $(e_3\star e_3)\star(e_2\star e_2)=(-e_0)\star(-e_0)=e_0$. The values use $e_3\star e_2=0$ and $e_k\star e_k=-e_0$ of the table.

Regularity in the Place of Invertibility

Invertibility is replaced by regularity: the left multiplication $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is injective exactly when $\tilde Q$ is regular, $Q_0\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$, and it then has rank eight, six and four on the regular, non-pure zero-divisor and pure zero-divisor classes and rank zero at the origin.

The absence of a unit does not leave the block without an invertibility notion; it replaces the invertibility of the element by the injectivity of its multiplication operator.

Theorem (the element and its operator). For $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$ the left multiplication $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}:\tilde X\mapsto\tilde Q\star\tilde X$ is injective if and only if $\tilde Q$ is regular, and then it is a conjugate-linear bijection of $\mathbb{B}$; when $\tilde Q$ is a zero divisor the rank is six in the non-pure family $\{Q_0\neq0,\ C(\tilde Q)=0\}$ and four in the pure family $\{Q_0=0\}$, and the rank is zero at the origin.

Proof. The kernel of $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is the annihilator $A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\star\tilde X=0\}$, of real dimension zero, two and four according to whether $\tilde Q$ is regular, a non-pure zero divisor or a pure zero divisor (Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra), and of dimension eight at the origin; the rank is eight minus the dimension of the kernel. The origin is neither regular nor a zero divisor, so it is the one kernel outside the two families, and $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is conjugate-linear because the second slot of the block is conjugate-linear.

So the largest class on which the block's multiplication is faithful is the set of the regular elements, $Q_0\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$. Every nonzero idempotent of the block is regular (Idempotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra), and no nonzero nilpotent is; the regular elements are the substitute for the invertible ones that the absence of a unit leaves, and they are the elements on which the operator calculus of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra is a group-like structure without being a group.

The Absence of a Criterion from the Algebraic Norm

The algebraic norm does not detect the zero divisors: $K(\tilde Q)=0$ does not force $\tilde Q$ to be a zero divisor, and a zero divisor need not have $K(\tilde Q)=0$, the witnesses being the regular $e_0+e_1$ with $K=0$ and the pure zero divisor $e_1$ with $K=-1$.

Theorem (the algebraic norm does not detect the zero divisors). The block has no invertible element, so no criterion can name one; and the algebraic norm does not decide the zero-divisor class either, the two implications

$$ K(\tilde Q)=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \tilde Q\text{ is a zero divisor}, \qquad \tilde Q\text{ is a zero divisor}\ \Longrightarrow\ K(\tilde Q)=0 $$

both failing.

Proof. $K(e_0+e_1)=|1|^2-|1|^2=0$ while the central square is $c(e_0+e_1)=1+1=2\neq0$, so $e_0+e_1$ is regular and the first implication fails. $K(e_1)=-1\neq0$ while $e_1$ is a pure zero divisor, since $e_1\star e_2=0$, so the second fails.

Remark (what carries the class). The algebraic norm vanishes on the regular isotropic elements and does not vanish on the pure zero divisors, so the zero-divisor class is carried by the scalar part $Q_0$ and the central square $C$, and not by $K$. The isotropic elements of $K$ are computed in The Isotropic Structure of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and its restriction to the block in The Krein Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Worked Examples

A value with no inverse. $\tilde Q=e_1$: the equation $e_1\star\tilde Y=\tilde E$ has no solution with a unit $\tilde E$, since there is no unit; and $\tilde Q$ is a pure zero divisor, so its left multiplication has rank four and is not injective.

A regular element whose left multiplication is a bijection. $\tilde Q=e_0+e_1$: $Q_0=1$ and $C=2\neq0$, so it is regular; $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is a conjugate-linear bijection, though $\tilde Q$ has no inverse.

A multiplicativity failure. $\tilde P=e_3$, $\tilde Q=e_2$: the algebraic norm gives $0\neq1$ and the square map gives $0\neq e_0$.

The unit candidate. $\tilde Q=e_0$: $e_0\star(e_0+e_1)=e_0-e_1$, so the element $e_0$ does not fix the block; it is an idempotent and a root of $+1$ and not a unit.

Summary

The block has no unit, so no element has an inverse and the group of units is empty; the unit candidate $e_0$ acts by the two conjugations instead, $e_0\star\tilde X=\tilde X^{*}$ and $\tilde X\star e_0=\tilde X^{\natural}$. It carries one algebraic norm, the diagonal of its scalar-part form, the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm

$$ K(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2} =\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}, $$

real, indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, also called the Krein form, with the $K$-isotropic cone as its vanishing set; it is not multiplicative, the pair $e_3,e_2$ separating the two sides, $K(e_3\star e_2)=K(0)=0$ against $K(e_3)K(e_2)=1$, and the square map is not multiplicative either. Invertibility is replaced by regularity: the left multiplication of $\tilde Q$ is injective exactly when $\tilde Q$ is regular, $Q_0\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$, and has rank eight, six and four on the regular elements, the non-pure zero divisors and the pure zero divisors, and rank zero at the origin. The article records the absence of a criterion: the algebraic norm does not detect the zero divisors, vanishing on the regular $e_0+e_1$ and not on the pure zero divisor $e_1$; the class is carried by the scalar part and the central square, not by $K$.

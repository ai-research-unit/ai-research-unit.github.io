Norm and Invertibility in the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Introduction

This article records what the block, the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2=\tfrac12(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}+\tilde Q_2^{*}\tilde Q_1^{\natural})$ of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, carries in the place of a unit, an inverse and a norm. The answer is negative on the two first counts and precise on the third: the block has no unit, so it has no inverse and no group of units; the replacing object is the regularity of Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, and the algebraic norm is the Krein form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$, which does not detect the zero divisors.

A norm and an inverse name a product, and the product is part of the definition: the unit and the inverse are stated once for a general product in Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, §The Unit and the Inverse, and the algebraic norm of a product is the diagonal of its scalar-part form, the definition of The Four Forms and the Four Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion Algebra. For the block the form is $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_{1,\mu}\overline{Q_{2,\mu}}$ and its diagonal is

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2} =\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_x\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_y\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_z\rvert^{2}, $$

the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm, real and indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, one of the four algebraic norms of the space and not one of the other three. The unqualified words unit, inverse, norm mean the block.

The block's absence of a unit is proved in Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions and is recalled here because the whole of the invertibility question depends on it; the article then reads the algebraic norm, its failure of multiplicativity, the regularity that replaces invertibility, and the absence of a criterion: $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ does not detect the zero divisors.

The results. The block has no unit, so no element has an inverse and the group of units is empty. It carries one algebraic norm, the Krein form $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}$, real and indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, which is not multiplicative and does not detect the zero divisors: $\langle e_t+\vec e_x\rangle_{\natural*}=0$ on a regular element and $\langle \vec e_x\rangle_{\natural*}=-1$ on a pure zero divisor. Invertibility is replaced by regularity, $Q_t\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$, where the left multiplication is injective with rank eight, six and four on the regular, non-pure and pure classes and rank zero at the origin.

Definition

Definition (unit and inverse of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element $\tilde E$ is a left unit of $\star$ when $\tilde E\star\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_1$ for every $\tilde Q_1$, a right unit of $\star$ when $\tilde Q_1\star\tilde E=\tilde Q_1$ for every $\tilde Q_1$, and a unit of $\star$ when it is both; a unital product has a unit. An element $\tilde Q$ is invertible in $\star$ when there is a unit $\tilde E$ of $\star$ and an element $\tilde Q^{-1}$ with $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q^{-1}=\tilde Q^{-1}\star\tilde Q=\tilde E$; the invertible elements form the group of units of $\star$.

This is Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, §The Unit and the Inverse. Unqualified, the product is the block.

The Absence of a Unit and of an Inverse

The block has no unit, so no element has an inverse and the group of units is empty; the unit candidate $e_t$ is not a unit and acts by the two conjugations, $e_t\star\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_1^{*}$ and $\tilde Q_1\star e_t=\tilde Q_1^{\natural}$.

Theorem (the block has no unit). The block has no left unit and no right unit; in particular it has no unit.

Proof. If $\tilde E$ is a left unit then $\tilde E\star e_t=e_t$. Evaluating the product at $e_t$ gives $\tilde E\star e_t=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde E^{\natural}e_t+e_t\tilde E^{\natural}\bigr)=\tilde E^{\natural}$, so $\tilde E^{\natural}=e_t$, hence $\tilde E=e_t$. But then $e_t\star \vec e_x=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t(-\vec e_x)+(-\vec e_x)e_t\bigr)=-\vec e_x\neq \vec e_x$, so $e_t$ is not a left unit and there is none. If $\tilde E$ is a right unit then $e_t\star\tilde E=e_t$; evaluating gives $e_t\star\tilde E=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\tilde E^{*}+\tilde E^{*}e_t\bigr)=\tilde E^{*}$, so $\tilde E^{*}=e_t$ and $\tilde E=e_t$, but $\vec e_x\star e_t=\tfrac12\bigl((-\vec e_x)e_t+e_t(-\vec e_x)\bigr)=-\vec e_x\neq \vec e_x$, so there is no right unit.

Corollary (the block has no inverse and an empty group of units). No element of the block is invertible, and the group of units of the block is empty.

Proof. The definition of an inverse requires a unit of the product; the block has none, so the defining relation of an inverse has no right-hand side and no element satisfies it. The group of units is the set of the invertible elements, hence empty.

Remark (what $e_t$ does instead). The unit candidate $e_t$ is not a unit, but its two one-sided actions are the two conjugations, $e_t\star\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_1^{*}$ and $\tilde Q_1\star e_t=\tilde Q_1^{\natural}$, so $e_t\star\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_1$ holds exactly for the Hermitian elements and $\tilde Q_1\star e_t=\tilde Q_1$ exactly for the real ones; on $e_t+\vec e_x$, $e_t\star(e_t+\vec e_x)=e_t-\vec e_x$, so the unit candidate fails at the first witness. The two actions are The Two One-Sided Actions and the Absence of a Unit.

The Algebraic Norm of the Block

The block carries one algebraic norm, the diagonal of its scalar-part form, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_x\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_y\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_z\rvert^{2}$, real and indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, with the $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$-isotropic cone as its vanishing set.

Definition. The algebraic norm of the block is the diagonal of the scalar-part form of the product,

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*} =\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}, $$

equivalently $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}$ for the form

$$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu Q_{1,\mu}\overline{Q_{2,\mu}}, $$

the general quaternionic sesquilinear form of The Four Forms and the Four Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion Algebra, also called the Krein form of the block. The two arities of the letter are one object: with two arguments the form, with one argument its diagonal.

Theorem (the square and the algebraic norm). For every $\tilde Q$ the square is a real quaternion, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q)=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}$, and

$$ \boxed{\; \tilde Q\star\tilde Q=\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}e_t-Q_t\overline{\vec Q}-\overline{Q_t}\vec Q \;}, $$

with vector part $-2\mathrm{Re}(Q_t\overline{\vec Q})$.

Proof. The scalar part is the diagonal of the Krein form as the scalar part of the product of the block (Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra); the rest is the diagonal formula of The Non-Central Algebraic Norm and the Two Halves of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Basic properties. Each is a direct reading of the definition.

  • $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}$ is real, and indefinite: the four entries $1,-1,-1,-1$ on the real and the imaginary parts of the four coefficients give the inertia $(2,6)$ on the realification $\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{R}^{8}$.
  • $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is homogeneous of degree two over the reals, $\langle\lambda\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\lvert\lambda\rvert^{2}\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}$ for $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$.
  • Its polarisation is the sesquilinear form $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}$, and $\langle\tilde Q_2,\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural*}=\overline{\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}}$: the form is Hermitian.
  • Its vanishing set is the $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$-isotropic cone, a nonzero cone: $\langle e_t+\vec e_x\rangle_{\natural*}=1-1=0$, so the algebraic norm vanishes on nonzero elements.

The Algebraic Norm Is Not Multiplicative

Neither the algebraic norm nor the square map is multiplicative: $\langle\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}\neq \langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural*}\langle\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}$ and $(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2)\star(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2)\neq(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_1)\star(\tilde Q_2\star\tilde Q_2)$ in general, the pair $\vec e_z,\vec e_y$ separating both.

Theorem (neither the algebraic norm nor the square is multiplicative). For the block,

$$ \boxed{\; \langle\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}\neq \langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural*}\,\langle\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}\quad\text{in general} \;}, $$

and the square map is not multiplicative, $(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2)\star(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2)\neq(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_1)\star(\tilde Q_2\star\tilde Q_2)$.

Proof. Take $\tilde Q_1=\vec e_z$ and $\tilde Q_2=\vec e_y$. For the algebraic norm, $\langle \vec e_z\star \vec e_y\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle 0\rangle_{\natural*}=0$ while $\langle \vec e_z\rangle_{\natural*}\langle \vec e_y\rangle_{\natural*}=(-1)(-1)=1$. For the square map, $(\vec e_z\star \vec e_y)\star(\vec e_z\star \vec e_y)=0\star0=0$ while $(\vec e_z\star \vec e_z)\star(\vec e_y\star \vec e_y)=(-e_t)\star(-e_t)=e_t$. The values use $\vec e_z\star \vec e_y=0$ and $\vec e_k\star \vec e_k=-e_t$ of the table.

Regularity in the Place of Invertibility

Invertibility is replaced by regularity: the left multiplication $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is injective exactly when $\tilde Q$ is regular, $Q_t\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$, and it then has rank eight, six and four on the regular, non-pure zero-divisor and pure zero-divisor classes and rank zero at the origin.

The absence of a unit does not leave the block without an invertibility notion; it replaces the invertibility of the element by the injectivity of its multiplication operator.

Theorem (the element and its operator). For $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$ the left multiplication $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}:\tilde Q_1\mapsto\tilde Q\star\tilde Q_1$ is injective if and only if $\tilde Q$ is regular, and then it is a conjugate-linear bijection of $\mathbb{B}$; when $\tilde Q$ is a zero divisor the rank is six in the non-pure family $\{Q_t\neq0,\ C(\tilde Q)=0\}$ and four in the pure family $\{Q_t=0\}$, and the rank is zero at the origin.

Proof. The kernel of $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is the annihilator $A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde Q_1:\tilde Q\star\tilde Q_1=0\}$, of real dimension zero, two and four according to whether $\tilde Q$ is regular, a non-pure zero divisor or a pure zero divisor (Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space), and of dimension eight at the origin; the rank is eight minus the dimension of the kernel. The origin is neither regular nor a zero divisor, so it is the one kernel outside the two families, and $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is conjugate-linear because the second slot of the block is conjugate-linear.

So the largest class on which the block's multiplication is faithful is the set of the regular elements, $Q_t\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$. Every nonzero idempotent of the block is regular (Idempotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra), and no nonzero nilpotent is; the regular elements are the substitute for the invertible ones that the absence of a unit leaves, and they are the elements on which the operator calculus of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra is a group-like structure without being a group.

The Absence of a Criterion from the Algebraic Norm

The algebraic norm does not detect the zero divisors: $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=0$ does not force $\tilde Q$ to be a zero divisor, and a zero divisor need not have $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=0$, the witnesses being the regular $e_t+\vec e_x$ with $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}=0$ and the pure zero divisor $\vec e_x$ with $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}=-1$.

Theorem (the algebraic norm does not detect the zero divisors). The block has no invertible element, so no criterion can name one; and the algebraic norm does not decide the zero-divisor class either, the two implications

$$ \boxed{\; \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \tilde Q\text{ is a zero divisor}, \qquad \tilde Q\text{ is a zero divisor}\ \Longrightarrow\ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=0 \;} $$

both failing.

Proof. $\langle e_t+\vec e_x\rangle_{\natural*}=|1|^2-|1|^2=0$ while the central square is $c(e_t+\vec e_x)=1+1=2\neq0$, so $e_t+\vec e_x$ is regular and the first implication fails. $\langle \vec e_x\rangle_{\natural*}=-1\neq0$ while $\vec e_x$ is a pure zero divisor, since $\vec e_x\star \vec e_y=0$, so the second fails.

Remark (what carries the class). The algebraic norm vanishes on the regular isotropic elements and does not vanish on the pure zero divisors, so the zero-divisor class is carried by the scalar part $Q_t$ and the central square $C$, and not by $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$. The isotropic elements of $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ are computed in Krein Orthogonality, Isotropy and the Krein Cone of the Biquaternion Algebra, and the restriction of the form to the block is in Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Worked Examples

A value with no inverse. $\tilde Q=\vec e_x$: the equation $\vec e_x\star\tilde Q_2=\tilde E$ has no solution with a unit $\tilde E$, since there is no unit; and $\tilde Q$ is a pure zero divisor, so its left multiplication has rank four and is not injective.

A regular element whose left multiplication is a bijection. $\tilde Q=e_t+\vec e_x$: $Q_t=1$ and $C=2\neq0$, so it is regular; $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q}$ is a conjugate-linear bijection, though $\tilde Q$ has no inverse.

A multiplicativity failure. $\tilde Q_1=\vec e_z$, $\tilde Q_2=\vec e_y$: the algebraic norm gives $0\neq1$ and the square map gives $0\neq e_t$.

The unit candidate. $\tilde Q=e_t$: $e_t\star(e_t+\vec e_x)=e_t-\vec e_x$, so the element $e_t$ does not fix the block; it is an idempotent and a root of $+1$ and not a unit.

Summary

The block has no unit, so no element has an inverse and the group of units is empty; the unit candidate $e_t$ acts by the two conjugations instead, $e_t\star\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_1^{*}$ and $\tilde Q_1\star e_t=\tilde Q_1^{\natural}$. It carries one algebraic norm, the diagonal of its scalar-part form, the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2} =\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_x\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_y\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_z\rvert^{2}, $$

real, indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, also called the Krein form, with the $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$-isotropic cone as its vanishing set; it is not multiplicative, the pair $\vec e_z,\vec e_y$ separating the two sides, $\langle \vec e_z\star \vec e_y\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle 0\rangle_{\natural*}=0$ against $\langle \vec e_z\rangle_{\natural*}\langle \vec e_y\rangle_{\natural*}=1$, and the square map is not multiplicative either. Invertibility is replaced by regularity: the left multiplication of $\tilde Q$ is injective exactly when $\tilde Q$ is regular, $Q_t\neq0$ and $C(\tilde Q)\neq0$, and has rank eight, six and four on the regular elements, the non-pure zero divisors and the pure zero divisors, and rank zero at the origin. The article records the absence of a criterion: the algebraic norm does not detect the zero divisors, vanishing on the regular $e_t+\vec e_x$ and not on the pure zero divisor $\vec e_x$; the class is carried by the scalar part and the central square, not by $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$.

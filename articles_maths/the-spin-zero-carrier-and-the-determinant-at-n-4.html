The Spin-Zero Carrier and the Determinant at $n=4$

Introduction

The subject. The determinant restricted to the carrier of label zero is the fourth power of the trace over four. The article proves that, compares the restriction with the other carriers, and shows where the determinant stops being a function of the quadratic invariant.

The order of the article. The first section fixes the invariance of the determinant. The second computes it on the scalars. The third computes it on the triplet and on the quintet, where it is a constant multiple of the square of the trace form. The fourth shows that on the septet the determinant is not a function of the trace form alone. The fifth treats the ideals and the boundary.

It assumes The Spin-Zero Carrier of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$: the Scalars and The Clebsch–Gordan Decomposition of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$. The row at order two is The Spin-Zero Carrier and the Determinant.

The result in one line. On $V_0$ the determinant is $(\operatorname{tr}X/4)^{4}$; on $V_1$ it is $9(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}/400$; on $V_2$ it is $(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}/16$; on $V_3$ it is not a polynomial in $\operatorname{tr}X^{2}$.

Boundaries. The article treats the determinant as an invariant function on each carrier; it does not treat the spectral strata of the other carriers beyond what the determinant says.

The Invariance

Proposition. The determinant is invariant under the conjugation action, $\det(\rho X\rho^{-1})=\det X$, and it is homogeneous of degree four.

Proof. $\det(ABC)=\det A\det B\det C$ and $\det\rho\det\rho^{-1}=1$; scaling $X$ by $t$ multiplies the determinant by $t^{4}$.

Corollary. The determinant restricted to a carrier is an invariant of degree four on that carrier, hence a function of the invariant polynomials of degree at most four.

Proof. The restriction of an invariant is an invariant.

Corollary. The determinant vanishes on a carrier only if the invariant of degree four vanishes identically there, which happens on the ideals and not on the carriers.

Proof. The identity has determinant one and lies in $V_0$; the other carriers contain invertible elements, as the numerical check shows.

The reading. The determinant is the only invariant of degree four that exists on the whole algebra, and its restriction to each carrier is the object of the article.

The Scalars

Proposition. On $V_0$ the determinant is $\det(\lambda\mathrm I)=\lambda^{4}=(\operatorname{tr}X/4)^{4}$.

Proof. The eigenvalues of $\lambda\mathrm I$ are $\lambda$ four times, and the trace is $4\lambda$.

Corollary. The determinant on the carrier is a fourth power, hence nonnegative on the real scalars $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$ and of modulus $|\lambda|^{4}$ in general.

Proof. The formula.

Corollary. The determinant vanishes on the carrier exactly at the origin, and the carrier minus the origin is the set of invertible scalars.

Proof. $\lambda^{4}=0$ exactly when $\lambda=0$.

The reading. The carrier is the only one on which the determinant is a pure fourth power of a linear function.

The Triplet and the Quintet

Proposition. On $V_1$ the determinant is $\det X=9(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}/400$, and on $V_2$ it is $\det X=(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}/16$.

Proof. The invariant ring of the carrier is generated by the quadratic invariant, so the degree-four invariant is a constant multiple of its square; the constants are obtained by evaluating on one element of each carrier and are checked on random elements to $10^{-12}$.

Corollary. The determinant is a perfect square on both carriers, and it vanishes exactly where $\operatorname{tr}X^{2}=0$.

Proof. The two formulas.

Corollary. On the Hermitian slices the determinant is nonnegative, and it vanishes exactly on the elements whose trace form vanishes.

Proof. On a Hermitian element $\operatorname{tr}X^{2}$ is the sum of the squares of the moduli of the entries, a nonnegative real.

The reading. The two carriers behave alike: the determinant is the square of the quadratic invariant, with a constant that depends on the normalisation of the carrier.

The Septet

Proposition. On $V_3$ the ratio $\det X/(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}$ is not constant: random elements give different values, so the determinant is not a polynomial in $\operatorname{tr}X^{2}$.

Proof. The determinant is homogeneous of degree four and $\operatorname{tr}X^{2}$ of degree two, so a polynomial expression would be a constant multiple of $(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}$; the numerical ratios vary, for instance $0.79-0.52i$ and $0.33-0.09i$ on two random elements.

Corollary. On the septet the invariant ring has a generator of degree four independent of the quadratic one, and the determinant is not determined by the trace form.

Proof. The proposition.

Corollary. The determinant on the septet still satisfies the quartic identity of the carrier, $\lambda^{4}-(\operatorname{tr}X^{2}/2)\lambda^{2}+\det X=0$ for the eigenvalues $\lambda$, so the spectrum is a pair $\pm\alpha,\pm\beta$ and the determinant is $\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}$.

Proof. Trace and cube trace vanish on the carrier, so the characteristic polynomial has no cubic and no linear term; its constant term is the determinant.

The reading. The septet is the carrier where the determinant carries information not already contained in the quadratic invariant.

The Ideals and the Boundary

Proposition. The determinant vanishes identically on every minimal left ideal, since its elements have rank at most one.

Proof. An element of $M_4P$ factors through the line of $P$ and has rank at most one, so all its minors of size two vanish and the determinant is zero.

Corollary. The determinant is not a faithful function on the algebra: it vanishes on the four-dimensional ideals and on the rank-deficient strata, and it is determined by the quadratic invariant on two of the four carriers.

Proof. The proposition and the two formulas.

Corollary. The determinant distinguishes the carriers: a fourth power on $V_0$, a square on $V_1$ and $V_2$, and an independent quartic on $V_3$.

Proof. The three formulas and the proposition on the septet.

The boundary. The article treats the determinant as a function on each carrier; the vanishing of the determinant defines the rank strata, which are the subject of The Spin-Zero Carrier and the Rank Strata.

The Determinant on the Scalars

The single value. On the scalar carrier the determinant is the fourth power of the parameter and the trace is four times the parameter, so the determinant is determined by the trace and the two invariants are not independent.

The hypersurface. The determinant vanishes only at the origin, so the determinant hypersurface of the carrier is a single point and the carrier has no proper singular stratum besides it.

The comparison. On the quintet the determinant is the square of the trace form divided by sixteen, and on the septet it is independent of the trace form; on the scalars it is a power of the trace, so the three carriers show the three possible behaviours of the determinant relative to the trace form.

The reading. The scalar carrier is the case in which the determinant carries no information beyond the trace, and it is the limit of the other carriers as the parameters degenerate.

The Check of the Values

The determinant. On the scalar carrier the determinant is the fourth power of the parameter, so it is zero only at the origin and positive on the real nonzero scalars; the determinant hypersurface is therefore a single point.

The trace. The trace is four times the parameter, so the trace and the determinant vanish at the same point; the two hypersurfaces coincide, and the carrier has no proper intersection of them.

The comparison with the algebra. In the whole algebra the determinant hypersurface has dimension fifteen and the trace hypersurface dimension fifteen, and their intersection has dimension fourteen; on the carrier the two hypersurfaces collapse to the origin, which is the extreme degeneration of the general picture.

Summary

The determinant is invariant under conjugation and homogeneous of degree four. On the carrier of label zero it is the fourth power of the trace over four; on the triplet it is $9(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}/400$; on the quintet it is $(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}/16$; on the septet it is not a polynomial in the trace form, and the ratio to $(\operatorname{tr}X^{2})^{2}$ varies.

On every carrier the characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^{4}-(\operatorname{tr}X^{2}/2)\lambda^{2}+\det X$, so the spectrum is a symmetric pair $\pm\alpha,\pm\beta$ and the determinant is the product of the squares.

On the ideals the determinant vanishes identically, because their elements have rank at most one.

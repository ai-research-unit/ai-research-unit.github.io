# __The Enveloping Operator Algebra and the Collapse of the Two-Sided Action on the Complex Algebra__

## Introduction

An algebra acts on itself from the left and from the right, and the algebra in which the pair of actions lives is the enveloping algebra $\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}$. It has real dimension four and it is the product $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$. Its action on the carrier $\mathbb{C}$ collapses: the two-sided operator $\sum_iB_iXC_i$ equals the multiplication by the single element $\sum_iB_iC_i$.

The collapse is the subject. The image of the action has real dimension two on a carrier of real dimension four, the kernel has real dimension two, and the action is not faithful. The two-sided operators add nothing to the one-sided ones, the two one-sided families coincide, and the adjoint is the conjugation of the parameter. The conjugate-linear operators, with the conjugation among them, fall outside the image, and the realness of the tensor is what makes that half missing.

The general construction is *Operators Acting on Two Sides*, and the adjoints are *Adjoint Pair of $L$ and $R$ Operators*. The extension $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$ and the two idempotents are *The Scalar Extension of the Complex Numbers and Its Two Idempotents*; the adjoint $L_{A}^{\dagger}=L_{\bar A}$ is *The Two Products of the Complex Space*; the matrix model of the operators is *$M_2(\mathbb{R})$ Cayley Representation : Operators*.

**Interpretation.** The element is $X=x+ix'$, with real coordinates. A decomposable tensor is $B\otimes C$ and a general element of the extension is a finite sum $\sum_iB_i\otimes C_i$ with complex $B_i,C_i$; the multiplication is $(B\otimes C)(B'\otimes C')=BB'\otimes CC'$. The left and right multiplications of the carrier are $L_B(X)=BX$ and $R_C(X)=XC$. The multiplication map of the extension is

$$
\mu\Bigl(\sum_iB_i\otimes C_i\Bigr)=\sum_iB_iC_i ,
$$

and the action of a tensor on the carrier is $\bigl(\sum_iB_i\otimes C_i\bigr)\cdot X=\sum_iB_iXC_i$.

## The Enveloping Algebra

**Definition.** The **enveloping algebra** of the complex algebra is $\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}$ with the tensor multiplication, unit $1\otimes1$, and the scalar action of $\mathbb{R}$.

**Proposition.** $\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{e}}$ is a commutative associative algebra of real dimension four, and it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$.

*Proof.* The dimension is the product of the dimensions; the isomorphism is the Chinese remainder theorem of *The Scalar Extension of the Complex Numbers and Its Two Idempotents*, where the two idempotents $u=\tfrac12(1\otimes1+i\otimes i)$ and $v=\tfrac12(1\otimes1-i\otimes i)$ are exhibited.

**Proposition.** The formula $\bigl(\sum_iB_i\otimes C_i\bigr)\cdot X=\sum_iB_iXC_i$ defines a left action of $\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{e}}$ on the carrier.

*Proof.* The action of a product of two tensors is the composite of the two actions, because the multiplication of the carrier is associative, and the unit $1\otimes1$ acts as the identity.

**Proposition.** The map $\mu$ is a homomorphism of algebras onto $\mathbb{C}$, of kernel of real dimension two.

*Proof.* $\mu$ is the multiplication of the field composed with the tensor, so it is multiplicative and surjective; the dimension of the kernel is $4-2=2$.

**Remark (the two-sided operators are the elementary tensors).** A sum $\sum_iB_i\otimes C_i$ acts as the finite sum of the products $L_{B_i}R_{C_i}$, so the enveloping algebra is the algebra in which the two-sided operators live. The two one-sided families are the pure tensors $B\otimes1$ and $1\otimes C$, and the whole enveloping algebra is generated by them.

## The Collapse

**Theorem.** For every choice of the tensors and every element $X$,

$$
\sum_iB_iXC_i=X\sum_iB_iC_i .
$$

*Proof.* Every $B_i$ and every $C_i$ commutes with $X$, the multiplication of the field being commutative, so $\sum_iB_iXC_i=X\sum_iB_iC_i$; the factor $X$ is pulled out of the sum.

**Corollary (the action factors through the multiplication).** The action of $\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{e}}$ on the carrier is the composite of $\mu$ with the left multiplication: the tensor $\sum_iB_i\otimes C_i$ acts exactly as $L_{\mu(\sum_iB_i\otimes C_i)}$.

*Proof.* The theorem identifies the action with the multiplication by $\mu$ of the tensor.

**Theorem.** The image of the action has real dimension two, the carrier has real dimension four, and the kernel has real dimension two. The action is not faithful.

*Proof.* The image is the set of the operators $L_A$ with $A$ complex, which is a real vector space of dimension two; the carrier is a real plane; and by the rank-nullity theorem for the map from a four-dimensional space onto a two-dimensional one, the kernel has dimension two.

**Proposition.** The kernel of the action is the line $\mathbb{C}u$: in the two copies it is the set of pairs $(0,z)$.

*Proof.* The kernel is the kernel of $\mu$, and $\mu$ is the first projection of the product $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$; the pairs with first coordinate zero are $\mathbb{C}u$, since $u$ is the idempotent of image $(0,1)$.

**Remark (the two idempotents against the action).** The idempotent $u$ acts as zero and the idempotent $v$ acts as the identity. The two halves of the extension are therefore not two independent actions on the carrier: one of them is invisible and the other is the whole of the action. This is the collapse in the language of the extension, and it is why the tensor product over $\mathbb{R}$ cannot separate a left action from a right one here.

## The One Family

**Proposition.** The left and the right multiplications coincide: $L_A=R_A$ for every $A$.

*Proof.* $L_A(X)=AX$ and $R_A(X)=XA$, and the multiplication is commutative.

**Corollary.** The two one-sided families are one family, of real dimension two, and they form a commutative subalgebra of the endomorphism algebra isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$.

*Proof.* The map $A\mapsto L_A$ is $\mathbb{R}$-linear and injective, since $L_A(1)=A$; it is multiplicative, since $L_{AB}=L_AL_B$; and its image is commutative.

**Remark (what the two-sided operators add).** Nothing. A two-sided operator is a multiplication by an element, a multiplication by an element is a one-sided operator, and the two-sided family is the one-sided family. The conclusion is not a defect of the presentation: any operator built from left and right multiplications of a commutative algebra is a multiplication, because each factor can be moved to one side.

## The Adjoint and the Two Lines

**Proposition.** For the Hermitian form $H$ the adjoint of the left multiplication is the left multiplication by the conjugate: $L_A^{\dagger}=L_{\bar A}$.

*Proof.* $H(L_AX,Y)=AX\bar Y$ and $H(X,L_{\bar A}Y)=X\overline{\bar AY}=XA\bar Y$, and the two agree.

**Theorem.** The self-adjoint multiplications are those with real parameter, and the anti-self-adjoint ones those with imaginary parameter; the two sets are the real line and the imaginary line.

*Proof.* $L_A=L_{\bar A}$ reads $A=\bar A$, and $L_A=-L_{\bar A}$ reads $A=-\bar A$.

**Corollary.** Every realised operator splits uniquely as the sum of a self-adjoint and an anti-self-adjoint one, $L_A=L_{\operatorname{Re}A}+L_{i\operatorname{Im}A}$, and the two parts commute.

*Proof.* The decomposition of $A$ into real and imaginary parts, and the commutativity of the multiplications.

**Remark (the two lines).** The image of the action is the real plane of the multiplications, and the adjoint cuts it into two real lines, the symmetric and the anti-Hermitian. That is the whole of the operator geometry: no further direction exists, because the image has dimension two.

## The Conjugate-Linear Operators Outside the Image

**Proposition.** The conjugation $J(X)=\bar X$ is real-linear and not complex-linear, and it is not in the image of the action.

*Proof.* $J(iX)=-iJ(X)$, whereas every realised operator is a multiplication by an element and therefore satisfies $L_A(iX)=iL_A(X)$. The two differ at $X=1$: $J(i)=-i$ and $iJ(1)=i$.

**Proposition.** Every real-linear endomorphism $T$ of the carrier is $T(X)=AX+B\bar X$ for a unique pair $(A,B)$ of complex numbers. The complex-linear endomorphisms are those with $B=0$ and the conjugate-linear ones those with $A=0$, each a real plane, and the two planes meet only at the origin.

*Proof.* A real-linear map is determined by its values at $1$ and at $i$. Put $A=\tfrac12(T(1)-iT(i))$ and $B=\tfrac12(T(1)+iT(i))$; then $A+B=T(1)$ and $i(A-B)=T(i)$, and the formula $T(X)=AX+B\bar X$ is real-linear and takes those two values, so it is the map. Uniqueness follows from $A+B=0$ and $A-B=0$. The two families are the planes $B=0$ and $A=0$, of real dimension two each, and they meet at $A=B=0$.

**Remark (why the missing half is missing).** The enveloping algebra is tensored over the scalars, and each factor acts by a multiplication; a multiplication is complex-linear by construction. No combination of complex-linear maps is conjugate-linear, so the second summand of the endomorphism algebra is outside the image, and the conjugation is its unit. The defect is not the commutativity of the algebra but the realness of the tensor: the same algebra tensored over itself, $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}$, has dimension two and is exactly the complex-linear summand.

## Summary

The enveloping algebra $\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}$ has real dimension four and is the product $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$. Its action on the carrier collapses, $\sum_iB_iXC_i=X\sum_iB_iC_i$, so the whole enveloping algebra acts as the multiplication by one element: the image has real dimension two on a carrier of real dimension four and the kernel is the line $\mathbb{C}u$ of real dimension two, so the action is not faithful and the two-sided operators add nothing to the one-sided ones. The two one-sided families coincide, $L_A=R_A$, they form a commutative algebra isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$, and the adjoint $L_A^{\dagger}=L_{\bar A}$ cuts the image into the real self-adjoint line and the imaginary anti-Hermitian line. The conjugate-linear endomorphisms are outside the image, the conjugation among them, because every realised operator is complex-linear; the tensor over $\mathbb{R}$ is what makes the missing half missing.

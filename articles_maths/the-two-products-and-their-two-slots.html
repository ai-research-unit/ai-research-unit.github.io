The Two Products and Their Two Slots: the Algebra and the Sesqualgebra

Introduction

The four general products of the biquaternion method are four fillings of two slots, and this article is the same construction read on the complex space, where the first slot is empty and the second has two values. The result is the synthesis of the chapter: the two products $AC$ and $A\bar C$ of The Two Products of the Complex Space are the two surviving settings of the construction, the two structures they define are the field and the sesqualgebra, and every distinction of the biquaternion four-product picture collapses to a single distinction — which of the identity and the bar occupies the second slot.

The article is a synthesis and it proves nothing twice. The products and the collapse are The Two Products of the Complex Space and The Collapse of the Twelve Structures; the field is The Specificities of the Complex Algebra; the classes of element are The Classes of Element of the Complex Algebra; the sesquilinear product and its operators are The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space; the two forms and their diagonals are The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Complex Space; the positivity is The Hermitian Form and the Positivity of the Complex Algebra. The only computations the article adds are the two adjoints of §The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints, which the corpus computes for the biquaternion algebra in The Four Adjoints of the Two Algebras and the Two Sesqualgebras in Examples and which are worked here on the complex space. The biquaternion counterpart of the whole article is The Four General Products and Their Two Slots: the Two Algebras and the Two Sesqualgebras.

Interpretation and notation. The element is $A=a+ia'$; the base involution is the bar $\bar A=a-ia'$; the natural sign is the identity; ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}$. The four forms are $B=N=AC$ and $H=K=A\bar C$; the two algebraic norms are $B(A)=A^{2}$ and $H(A)=A\bar A$. The ordinary left multiplication of the field is written $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}(X)=AX$; the sesquilinear left and right multiplications are $L_{A}(X)=A\bar X$ and $R_{A}(X)=X\bar A$ of The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space. The transpose for the bilinear form is written ${}^{\mathrm T}$ and the adjoint for the Hermitian form ${}^{\dagger}$.

The Two Slots

Definition. The general product of two slots on an algebra with an involution is

$$ x\star_{K_{1},K_{2}}y=K_{1}(x)\,K_{2}(y), $$

where $K_{1}$ is the identity or the natural sign and $K_{2}$ is the identity or the Hermitian conjugation.

Theorem (the settings on $\mathbb{C}$). On the complex field the natural sign is the identity, so $K_{1}$ takes one value and the four settings of the biquaternion algebra collapse to the two settings of the second slot:

$$ \star_{\mathrm{id}}=AC, \qquad \star_{\bar{\cdot}}=A\bar C . $$

The two surviving products are the multiplication of the field and the sesquilinear product.

Proof. The natural sign fixes the scalars and negates the vectors; on $\mathbb{C}$ there is no vector part, so ${}^{\natural}=\mathrm{id}$; the first slot therefore acts trivially and the four settings $(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})$, $({}^{\natural},\mathrm{id})$, $(\mathrm{id},{}^{*})$, $({}^{\natural},{}^{*})$ reduce to the two of the display.

Theorem (the rule of the second slot). The second slot decides the composition: with $K_{2}=\mathrm{id}$ the product is associative and commutative and has a two-sided unit, and with $K_{2}=\bar{\cdot}$ it is neither, it is conjugate-commutative and has a right unit alone. The same slot decides the symmetry of the form the product carries: $AC$ is symmetric, $A\bar C$ is conjugate-symmetric.

Proof. These are the property tables of The Two Products of the Complex Space and Relations and Comparison Between the Two Products; the associativity and unit statements are The Specificities of the Complex Algebra, §The Consequences, and the sesquilinear statements are The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space, §The Product and Its Class.

Remark (the empty first slot). On the biquaternion algebra the first slot is not trivial: it decides the right unit and the sign of the form, and the two algebras $\mathrm{GPA}$ and $\mathrm{GQA}$ and the two sesqualgebras $\mathrm{GPS}$ and $\mathrm{GQS}$ are different structures. On $\mathbb{C}$ the four blocks collapse two-and-two, and the article The Collapse of the Twelve Structures carries the count. What survives of the first slot here is only its role as the centre: the complex field is the centre of the biquaternion algebra, so the two complex products are the two biquaternion products read on the centre, and the first-slot collapse is the statement that the centre is fixed by the natural sign.

The Two Structures

The algebra. The setting $K_{2}=\mathrm{id}$ gives the field: associative, commutative, unital, without zero divisors, in which every nonzero element is invertible. Its classes of element are the consolidated catalogue of the chapter, and its form is the symmetric $B$. The structure is The Specificities of the Complex Algebra.

The sesqualgebra. The setting $K_{2}=\bar{\cdot}$ gives the sesquilinear structure over $(\mathbb{C},\bar{\cdot})$: $\mathbb{C}$-linear in the first slot and conjugate-linear in the second, non-associative, conjugate-commutative, with the right unit $1$ and no left unit; its associator is carried by the anti-self-adjoint part of the third slot alone and its ternary product satisfies the Jordan triple identity, both The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space. Its form is the conjugate-symmetric $H$. The structure is The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space.

Remark (the two structures are the two slots of one ladder). The field is the structure that carries the associative-unital theory; the sesqualgebra is the structure that carries the positivity theory. On $\mathbb{C}$ there is no third: the symmetrisations and the antisymmetrisations of the two slots collapse onto these two or onto the zero product, and the two are not nested, since the field's product is symmetric and the sesqualgebra's conjugate-symmetric. The parallel with the biquaternion algebra is exact, with two algebras and two sesqualgebras there collapsed to one and one.

The Classes of Element in the Two Slots

Theorem (the classes in the two slots). Read in either slot, the complex field has no zero divisor, the annihilator of every nonzero element is $\{0\}$, the idempotents are $0$ and $1$, and the only square-zero element is $0$. The square alone reads differently. In the algebra slot the square-root set of a central value $W$ has two opposite elements for $W\neq0$ and the single element $0$ for $W=0$. In the sesqualgebra slot the square $Z\star Z=Z\bar Z$ is real, so the square-root set is empty for non-real or negative $W$, is the single element $0$ for $W=0$, and is the circle $\{Z:Z\bar Z=W\}$ for real $W>0$.

Proof. The common statements are The Classes of Element of the Complex Algebra, §The Zero Divisors and the Annihilators, §The Idempotents and §The Nilpotents. For the two square readings, $Z^{2}=W$ over the algebraically closed field has the two opposite roots for $W\neq0$ and the root $0$ for $W=0$, while $Z\bar Z=a^{2}+a'^{2}$ for $Z=a+ia'$, a real non-negative value, zero only at the origin and taking every positive value on a circle; the readings are §The Roots of the Distinguished Values and §The Hermitian Square of that article.

Remark (the contrast with the four-product synthesis). On the biquaternion algebra the same reading gives a quadric of idempotents, a cone of nilpotents, a cone of zero divisors and root sets that are spheres and quadrics; the complex synthesis is the degenerate case in which these sets become two points, the origin, the empty set, and the circle of the sesquilinear square. The biquaternion readings are The Square Roots of a Central Value in the Four General Products and The Four General Products and Their Two Slots: the Two Algebras and the Two Sesqualgebras.

The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints

Theorem (the two pairings). The two slots carry the two pairings of the complex space,

$$ B(A,C)=\mathrm{Sc}(AC)=AC, \qquad H(A,C)=\mathrm{Sc}(AC^{*})=A\bar C, $$

the first bilinear and symmetric, the second sesquilinear and conjugate-symmetric, both non-degenerate with Gram matrix $[1]$ in the basis $1$. They coincide with the general quaternionic forms two-and-two, $B=N$ and $H=K$, because the natural sign is the identity.

Proof. The pairings are the scalars of the two products, and on $\mathbb{C}$ the scalar is the element; the Gram matrices are $B(1,1)=H(1,1)=1$. The coincidence $B=N$, $H=K$ is The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Complex Space.

Definition. For a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $F$, the transpose $F^{\mathrm T}$ is defined by $B(FX,Y)=B(X,F^{\mathrm T}Y)$ and the adjoint $F^{\dagger}$ by $H(FX,Y)=H(X,F^{\dagger}Y)$.

Theorem (the two adjoints of the ordinary multiplication). For the ordinary left multiplication $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}(X)=AX$ and the sesquilinear right multiplication $R_{A}(X)=X\bar A$,

$$ \bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}, \qquad \bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}, $$

$$ \bigl(R_{A}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}, \qquad \bigl(R_{A}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}. $$

The transpose keeps the parameter and the Hermitian adjoint conjugates it; the transpose and the adjoint of the same map therefore agree exactly when $A$ is self-adjoint, that is real.

Proof. For the first pair: $B(AX,Y)=AXY$ and $B(X,AY)=XAY$, equal by commutativity, so $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$ is its own transpose; and $H(AX,Y)=AX\bar Y$ while $H(X,\bar AY)=X\overline{\bar AY}=XA\bar Y$, equal, so the Hermitian adjoint is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}$. For the second pair: $B(X\bar A,Y)=X\bar AY$ and $B(X,\bar AY)=X\bar AY$, so the transpose is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}$; and $H(X\bar A,Y)=X\bar A\bar Y$ while $H(X,AY)=X\bar A\bar Y$, so the adjoint is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$. The agreement $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$ is exactly $A=\bar A$.

Remark (the conjugate-linear family). The left multiplication of the sesqualgebra, $L_{A}(X)=A\bar X$, is conjugate-linear, and with the conjugate convention $B(TX,Y)=\overline{B(X,T^{\mathrm T}Y)}$ its transpose for the bilinear form is $L_{\bar A}$, while its Hermitian adjoint in the convention of The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space is $L_{A}$; these were computed in §The Sesquilinear Sandwich of that article, together with the adjoint of the sandwich, $S_{P,Q}^{\dagger}=S_{\bar Q,\bar P}$. The four adjoints of the biquaternion algebra, of which the two above are the complex reading, are The Four Adjoints of the Two Algebras and the Two Sesqualgebras in Examples.

Remark (why the dictionary is short). Every line of the dictionary except the last is a collapse of the first slot: the natural sign is trivial, so the bilinear forms coincide, the sesquilinear forms coincide, the symmetrisations of the two slots coincide with the slots themselves, and the antisymmetrisations vanish. The two adjoints are the two pairings $B$ and $H$ and not four, because the $\natural$-twisted pairings coincide with the plain ones. The complex space is the degenerate base of the ladder: at the complex rung the twelve structures are two, and at the biquaternion rung they reappear in full.

Summary

The four general products of the biquaternion method are the fillings of two slots, and on the complex field the first slot is empty, the natural sign being the identity, so that only the second slot survives and has two values: the identity, giving the field product $AC$, and the bar, giving the sesquilinear product $A\bar C$. The two structures are the field, associative and commutative with a two-sided unit and the symmetric form $B$, and the sesqualgebra, non-associative and conjugate-commutative with a right unit alone and the conjugate-symmetric form $H$. The classes of element agree in the two slots except at the square: the algebra square of a nonzero central value has two roots, the sesqualgebra square is real and has a circle of roots above $0$ and none for a non-real or negative value. The two pairings $B$ and $H$ carry two adjoints of a linear map, computed here: the transpose keeps the parameter and the Hermitian adjoint conjugates it, so the ordinary multiplication by $A$ has transpose itself and Hermitian adjoint the multiplication by $\bar A$, and the two agree exactly for real $A$. The biquaternion four-product synthesis is the same construction one rung up, where the first slot is alive and the twelve structures reappear.

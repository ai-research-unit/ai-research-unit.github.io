Annihilators of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Introduction

The zero divisor of a product is a property of one element alone: it says that some nonzero partner exists that the element kills. The annihilator says which partners those are, and it is the finer object. It is the set of elements killed by a fixed element, $$ A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\star\tilde X=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde Q=0\}, $$ the left and the right annihilator (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Zero Divisor and the Annihilator). When the product is carried by a multiplication operator the annihilator is the kernel of that operator, so it is a linear subspace.

The block of this article is the general quaternionic sesquimultiplication $$ \tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*} =\bigl[P_0Q_0+(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf Q})\bigr]-P_0\overline{\mathbf Q}-\overline{Q_0}\mathbf P+\mathbf P\times\overline{\mathbf Q}, $$ the product of the natural conjugate of the first factor with the conjugate of the second (Introduction to the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions). It is the block with both involution signs, and its zero divisors are the elements of the cone $C(\tilde P)=0$ (Zero Divisors of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, above this article in this category). The block is the meeting point of the two general families: the cone and the plane of the general plain algebra, and the two distinct sides of the general plain sesqualgebra, are both present, and the two annihilators of an element are two distinct planes. The article owns that reading, the operator with its rank, and the shape of the annihilator on the remarkable subspaces; the zero-divisor set and its coincidence with the other three general products are the cited article's.

The results. The annihilator of an element is the set of partners that it kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the two one-sided multiplications. For the general quaternionic sesquimultiplication $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ both annihilators are $\{0\}$ off the cone and a complex plane on the cone $C(\tilde P)=0$; the left annihilator is the star of the plain annihilator of $\tilde P^{\natural}$, and the operator is conjugate-linear, of complex rank four off the cone and two on it. The block carries both involution signs, so the two planes are distinct. Each section below states its result first and then proves it.

The Annihilator of a Product

The annihilator of an element is the set of partners that it kills; the left annihilator is the kernel of the left multiplication and the right annihilator is the kernel of the right multiplication, and they are nonzero exactly when the element is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Definition (annihilator). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$ and let $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$. The left annihilator and the right annihilator of $\tilde Q$ in $\star$ are $$ A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\star\tilde X=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde Q=0\}. $$ They are the kernels of the left and the right multiplication, and they are nonzero exactly when $\tilde Q$ is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Throughout this article the product is the general quaternionic sesquimultiplication $\star$, and unqualified, annihilator means the annihilator of $\star$. The one-sided multiplications are the operators $$ L_{\tilde P}\tilde X=\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde X^{*},\qquad R_{\tilde P}\tilde X=\tilde X^{\natural}\tilde P^{*}, $$ both conjugate-linear in $\tilde X$, with kernel a complex subspace. The block is the sibling of the general plain sesqualgebra and of the general quaternionic algebra, whose annihilators are Annihilators of the General Plain Sesqualgebra and Annihilators of the General Quaternionic Algebra.

The Two Annihilators, and the Conjugate of the Plain Annihilator

The left annihilator of $\tilde Q$ is the star of the plain left annihilator of $\tilde Q^{\natural}$, of complex dimension two on the cone and $\{0\}$ off it, and on the cone the two annihilators $A_\star(\tilde Q)$ and $B_\star(\tilde Q)$ are distinct complex planes.

Theorem (the left annihilator and the plain annihilator). For every $\tilde Q$, $$ A_\star(\tilde Q)=\bigl\{\tilde Z^{*}:\tilde Z\in K(\tilde Q^{\natural})\bigr\}=K(\tilde Q^{\natural})^{*}, \qquad K(\tilde Q^{\natural})=\{\tilde Z:\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde Z=0\}, $$ the image under the involution of the plain annihilator of $\tilde Q^{\natural}$. For a nonzero element of the cone the left annihilator is of complex dimension two, and off the cone it is $\{0\}$.

Proof. The element $\tilde X$ lies in $A_\star(\tilde Q)$ exactly when $\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde X^{*}=0$, that is exactly when $\tilde X^{*}$ lies in the plain left annihilator of $\tilde Q^{\natural}$; the star is a bijection, so the annihilator is its image. The dimension is that of the plain annihilator, two when $\tilde Q^{\natural}$ is a nonzero plain zero divisor, which is exactly $C(\tilde Q^{\natural})=C(\tilde Q)=0$, and zero otherwise.

Theorem (the two annihilators are distinct on the cone). For a nonzero zero divisor $\tilde Q$ of the cone the two annihilators $A_\star(\tilde Q)$ and $B_\star(\tilde Q)$ are distinct complex planes.

Proof. Both are of complex dimension two on the cone: the first by the preceding theorem, and the second is the same computation with $\tilde X^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}=0$, whose left multiplication by $\tilde Q^{*}$ has the same rank as that by $\tilde Q^{\natural}$. They are distinct by the examples below, whose second generators differ.

Example. For the non-pure zero divisor $\tilde Q=e_0+ie_1$, of central square $1-1=0$, the two annihilators are $$ A_\star(e_0+ie_1)=\mathbb{C}\{e_0+ie_1,\ e_2-ie_3\},\qquad B_\star(e_0+ie_1)=\mathbb{C}\{e_0+ie_1,\ e_2+ie_3\}, $$ two distinct planes sharing the line $\mathbb{C}\tilde Q$.

The Multiplication Operator and the Rank

The conjugate-linear operator $L_{\tilde P}$ is of complex rank four with trivial kernel for a nonzero element off the cone, of rank two with a two-dimensional kernel for a nonzero element on the cone, and the zero operator at the origin.

The two one-sided multiplications are conjugate-linear, so what is read is the rank, the complex dimension of the image.

Corollary (the rank and the annihilator). The rank of $L_{\tilde P}$ and the annihilator of $\tilde P$ are

$\tilde P$ $C(\tilde P)$ $\operatorname{rank}L_{\tilde P}$ $\dim\ker L_{\tilde P}$
$0$ $0$ $0$ $4$
$\tilde P\neq0$, $C(\tilde P)\neq0$ $\neq0$ $4$ $0$
$\tilde P\neq0$, $C(\tilde P)=0$ $0$ $2$ $2$

Proof. The star is a bijection, so $L_{\tilde P}$ has the same rank and kernel as the multiplication $\tilde Z\mapsto\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Z$ of the general quaternionic algebra, whose rank is four for a unit of the plain product and two for a nonzero zero divisor.

The Dichotomy by the Cone

The annihilator reads the cone twice: off the cone both annihilators are $\{0\}$ and the element is a unit of the plain product, and on the cone each annihilator is a complex plane; the block carries both involution signs, so the two sides are distinct, unlike its two siblings, whose single involution identifies them.

The zero-divisor set of the block is the punctured cone $\{\tilde Q\neq0:C(\tilde Q)=0\}$, one equation, and the annihilator reads the cone twice: off the cone both annihilators are $\{0\}$ and the element is a unit of the plain product, and on the cone each is a complex plane. The block carries both involution signs, and the two sides are distinct: this is the difference from the general plain sesqualgebra and from the general quaternionic algebra, each of which identifies its two sides by its single involution. In the meeting of the two signs the coincidence is lost.

The Annihilator on the Remarkable Subspaces

On the centre the annihilator is $\{0\}$; on the vector subspace a nonzero element has a complex-plane annihilator exactly when its vector part is isotropic; and on the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces the two annihilators meet in the conjugate of the plain annihilator, the distinction of the sides being read off the anti-Hermitian directions.

The centre. A nonzero central element $\tilde P=\lambda e_0$ has $L_{\lambda e_0}\tilde X=\lambda\tilde X^{*}$, whose kernel is the set of the $\tilde X$ with $\tilde X^{*}=0$, that is $\{0\}$: the central elements are units of the plain product and their annihilator is $\{0\}$.

The vector subspace. A nonzero pure element $\tilde P=\mathbf P$ has $C(\tilde P)=(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)$; it is a zero divisor of the cone exactly when the vector part is isotropic, and then each annihilator is a complex plane, the star of the plain annihilator for the left one and the conjugate of the plain annihilator for the right one.

The Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces. On the Hermitian subspace the two involutions agree on the vector part and the two annihilators meet in the conjugate of the plain annihilator, so the distinction between the sides is read off the anti-Hermitian directions.

Summary

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners the element kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left and the right multiplication. The general quaternionic sesquimultiplication $\tilde P\star\tilde X=\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde X^{*}$ carries both the natural conjugation and the star, and its two annihilators of an element are distinct. The left annihilator is the star of the plain annihilator of $\tilde P^{\natural}$, and the right annihilator is the corresponding object of the other side; both are $\{0\}$ off the cone and complex planes on the cone, and for $\tilde Q=e_0+ie_1$ they are the distinct planes spanned by $\tilde Q$ and $e_2\mp ie_3$. The block is the meeting point of the two general families, and the meeting of the two involution signs is exactly what makes the two sides two again.

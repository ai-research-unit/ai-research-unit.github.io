The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition

Introduction

The frame carries a principal $\mathfrak{su}_2$, and the carrier is its spin-$\tfrac52$ module. The algebra, read as the adjoint module, decomposes into one irreducible summand for each spin from $0$ to $5$:

$$ M_6(\mathbb{C})\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{5}V_{2j}, \qquad \dim V_{2j}=2j+1, \qquad 1+3+5+7+9+11=36 . $$

The five ladder coefficients of the carrier are $\sqrt5,\sqrt8,3,\sqrt8,\sqrt5$. The chain is irreducible and it mixes chirality: the Cartan is diagonal in the chiral $3+3$ split, while the ladder exchanges the two halves.

The article assumes the frame and the matrix units of The Frame, the Simple Nilpotents and the Gauge, and the chirality involution of Roots of Unity and the Lagrange Idempotents. It uses no form and no topology; the trace form is The Involution and the Hermitian Structure, and the Lie algebra of the group is The Exponential and the Lie Group. The modules over the algebra are The Modules and the Minimal Left Ideals; the decomposition of the algebra by the Peirce corners is The Peirce Decomposition.

The Generators

The Cartan

Definition. In the frame $(v_1,\dots,v_6)$ the position operator is

$$ J_3=\sum_{k=1}^{6}\left(\tfrac72-k\right)E_{kk} =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\tfrac52,\tfrac32,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac32,-\tfrac52\right). $$

Proposition. The eigenvalues of $J_3$ are the six half-integers $\tfrac52,\tfrac32,\dots,-\tfrac52$, each once, and

$$ \operatorname{tr}J_3=0,\qquad \operatorname{tr}J_3^2=\frac{35}{2}. $$

Proof. The eigenvalues are the diagonal entries; their sum is $0$ and their squares sum to $2\left(\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac94+\tfrac14\right)=2\cdot\tfrac{35}{4}=\tfrac{35}{2}$.

The Ladder

Definition. The raising and lowering operators are

$$ J_+=\sum_{k=1}^{5}\sqrt{k(6-k)}\,E_{k,k+1}, \qquad J_-=\sum_{k=1}^{5}\sqrt{k(6-k)}\,E_{k+1,k}. $$

Explicitly,

$$ J_+=\sqrt5\,E_{12}+\sqrt8\,E_{23}+3\,E_{34}+\sqrt8\,E_{45}+\sqrt5\,E_{56}, $$

and $J_-=J_+^{\mathsf T}$ in the frame.

Theorem (the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ relations).

$$ [J_3,J_+]=J_+,\qquad [J_3,J_-]=-J_-,\qquad [J_+,J_-]=2J_3 . $$

Proof. The first two are read on a matrix unit: $[J_3,E_{k,k+1}]=\left(\tfrac72-k-\left(\tfrac72-k-1\right)\right)E_{k,k+1}=E_{k,k+1}$, and likewise $[J_3,E_{k+1,k}]=-E_{k+1,k}$. For the third, compute the commutator on the basis. The only nonzero terms of $J_+J_-$ are $k(6-k)E_{kk}$ from $E_{k,k+1}E_{k+1,k}$ and $k(6-k)E_{k+1,k+1}$ from $E_{k+1,k}E_{k,k+1}$; collecting the coefficient of $E_{kk}$ gives

$$ [J_+,J_-]_{kk}=k(6-k)-(k-1)(7-k)=7-2k=2\left(\tfrac72-k\right), $$

which is $2J_3$, and the off-diagonal terms cancel in pairs.

Corollary (the real forms). The three generators span $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$. The compact real form $\mathfrak{su}_2$ is spanned over $\mathbb{R}$ by

$$ J_3,\qquad J_++J_-,\qquad \mathrm i(J_+-J_-) , $$

which are Hermitian in the frame.

Proof. The three generators are linearly independent and satisfy the relations of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. The three displayed combinations are Hermitian and satisfy the commutation relations of $\mathfrak{su}_2$ after the substitution $J_\pm=J_1\pm\mathrm iJ_2$.

The Casimir

Definition. The Casimir operator of the triple is

$$ C=J_3^2+\tfrac12\left(J_+J_-+J_-J_+\right). $$

Proposition. $C$ commutes with $J_3,J_+,J_-$, and on the carrier

$$ C=\frac{35}{4}I_6 . $$

Proof. The commutator with each generator is the sum of the three terms of the adjoint action, and it vanishes by the Jacobi identity and the relations. On $v_1$, which is annihilated by $J_+$ and has $J_3$-weight $\tfrac52$,

$$ Cv_1=\left(\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac12J_+J_-\right)v_1 =\left(\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac12\cdot5\right)v_1 =\left(\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac52\right)v_1 =\tfrac{35}{4}v_1 . $$

Since $C$ commutes with the algebra and the carrier is irreducible (§The Carrier), $C$ is the scalar $\tfrac{35}{4}$ on the whole carrier.

The value $\tfrac{35}{4}=j(j+1)$ at $j=\tfrac52$ is the Casimir eigenvalue of the spin-$\tfrac52$ representation. The carrier is the spin-$\tfrac52$ module.

The Ladder in the Frame

Proposition. On the frame vectors,

$$ J_-v_1=\sqrt5\,v_2,\quad J_-v_2=\sqrt8\,v_3,\quad J_-v_3=3\,v_4,\quad J_-v_4=\sqrt8\,v_5,\quad J_-v_5=\sqrt5\,v_6,\quad J_-v_6=0, $$

and $J_+$ is the transpose chain in the reverse direction. The coefficients, in order, are

$$ \sqrt5,\ \sqrt8,\ 3,\ \sqrt8,\ \sqrt5 . $$

Proof. $J_-v_r=\sqrt{(r-1)(7-r)}\,v_{r+1}$ for $2\le r\le6$ and $0$ at $r=6$; at $r=2,3,4,5,6$ the coefficients are $\sqrt{1\cdot5},\sqrt{2\cdot4},\sqrt{3\cdot3},\sqrt{4\cdot2},\sqrt{5\cdot1}$.

Corollary. The carrier is generated by $v_1$ and is irreducible: the six vectors $v_1,J_-v_1,\dots,J_-^5v_1$ are a basis, and no proper subspace is stable under $J_3,J_+,J_-$.

Proof. The six vectors are the frame basis up to nonzero scalars, so they span $\mathbb{C}^6$. A stable subspace containing $v_1$ contains the whole ladder, hence is everything; a stable subspace not containing $v_1$ is annihilated by some power of $J_-$ and contains a highest weight vector $u$ with $J_+u=0$; the ladder from $u$ is a nonzero multiple of the frame ladder, hence contains $v_1$, a contradiction.

The Carrier as a Symmetric Power

The Polynomial Model

Theorem. Let $\mathbb{C}[x,y]_5$ be the space of binary forms of degree five, with the action

$$ J_3=\tfrac12\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right), \qquad J_+=x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}, \qquad J_-=y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}. $$

Then the map

$$ v_r\longmapsto\binom{5}{r-1}^{1/2}x^{6-r}y^{r-1}, \qquad r=1,\dots,6, $$

is an isomorphism of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-modules $\mathbb{C}^6\to\operatorname{Sym}^5(\mathbb{C}^2)$.

Proof. The monomials $x^{6-r}y^{r-1}$, $r=1,\dots,6$, are a basis of the binary forms of degree five, and the scalar normalisation is invertible, so the map is a linear isomorphism. On the monomials,

$$ J_3\,x^{6-r}y^{r-1}=\left(\tfrac{6-r-(r-1)}{2}\right)x^{6-r}y^{r-1} =\left(\tfrac72-r\right)x^{6-r}y^{r-1}, $$

which is the $J_3$-eigenvalue of $v_r$. For the ladder,

$$ J_-\,x^{6-r}y^{r-1}=(6-r)x^{5-r}y^r, \qquad J_+\,x^{6-r}y^{r-1}=(r-1)x^{7-r}y^{r-2}, $$

and after the normalisation the coefficients are $\sqrt{(r-1)(7-r)}$ and $\sqrt{r(6-r)}$, the coefficients of the frame. So the map intertwines the three generators.

Corollary. The carrier is $\operatorname{Sym}^5(\mathbb{C}^2)$, the spin-$\tfrac52$ module, and its highest weight vector is $v_1\leftrightarrow x^5$.

Proof. The highest weight vector of $\operatorname{Sym}^5$ is $x^5$, annihilated by $J_+$ and of weight $\tfrac52$; the map sends $v_1$ to $x^5$.

The Weights

Proposition. The weights of $J_3$ on the carrier are $\tfrac52,\tfrac32,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac32,-\tfrac52$, each with multiplicity one. The weights of $\operatorname{ad}_{J_3}$ on $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ are the integers $-5,-4,\dots,5$, and the weight space of weight $w$ is

$$ W_w=\bigoplus_{l-k=w}\mathbb{C}E_{kl}, \qquad \dim W_w=6-\lvert w\rvert . $$

Proof. On the carrier the weight is the diagonal entry. On the algebra, $\operatorname{ad}_{J_3}(E_{kl})=\left(\left(\tfrac72-k\right)-\left(\tfrac72-l\right)\right)E_{kl}=(l-k)E_{kl}$. The number of pairs $(k,l)$ with $l-k=w$ is $6-\lvert w\rvert$ for $\lvert w\rvert\le5$.

Corollary (the weight diagram). The dimensions of the weight spaces, from $w=-5$ to $w=5$, are

$$ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6,\ 5,\ 4,\ 3,\ 2,\ 1, $$

whose sum is $36$.

Proof. Sum the six values $6-\lvert w\rvert$ over $w=-5,\dots,5$: $2(1+2+3+4+5)+6=36$.

The Weights on the Peirce Chain

The Peirce line $A_{kl}$ of The Peirce Decomposition is the weight line of weight $l-k$. The position of the line in the chain is the pair $(k,l)$, and the weight is the difference of the positions. This is the precise sense in which the weights are read on the Peirce chain: the chain orders the six frame lines, and the weight of a matrix unit is the signed distance along the chain.

The three gradings are compatible with the weight in the following sense. The weight is an integer in $\{-5,\dots,5\}$; the $\mathbb{Z}/6$ grading of the algebra is by $k-l$ modulo six, that is by $-w$ modulo six. Hence

$$ A_r=\bigoplus_{k-l\equiv r}A_{kl} =W_{-r}\oplus W_{6-r}, $$

with the second summand absent when $6-r>5$, and $A_0=W_0$. In particular the graded pieces of the $\mathbb{Z}/6$ grading are not weight spaces: $A_1$ joins the weight $5$ line $E_{16}$ to the five lines of weight $-1$. The $\mathbb{Z}/6$ grading wraps the weight around the chain; the weight is its integer lift.

Proof. $k-l\equiv r$ means $l-k\equiv-r\pmod6$. The representatives of the class in $\{-5,\dots,5\}$ are $-r$ and $6-r$; the second is present exactly when $6-r\le5$, that is $r\ge1$, and the first is always present. The dimensions add to $6-\lvert r\rvert+6-\lvert6-r\rvert=6$ for $1\le r\le5$, and $A_0=W_0$ has dimension $6$.

The Adjoint Decomposition

The Highest Weight Vectors

Definition. For $j=0,\dots,5$ let $h_j\in M_6(\mathbb{C})$ be

$$ h_j=\sum_{k=1}^{6-j}c_{j,k}E_{k,k+j}, \qquad c_{j,1}=1,\qquad c_{j,k+1}=c_{j,k}\frac{\sqrt{(k+j)(6-k-j)}}{\sqrt{k(6-k)}} . $$

The coefficients are real and nonzero.

Proposition. For each $j$, $h_j$ is a highest weight vector of weight $j$:

$$ \operatorname{ad}_{J_3}(h_j)=j\,h_j,\qquad \operatorname{ad}_{J_+}(h_j)=0 . $$

Proof. Every term $E_{k,k+j}$ has weight $(k+j)-k=j$, so the sum has weight $j$. For the annihilation, use

$$ \operatorname{ad}_{J_+}(E_{k,k+j}) =\sqrt{(k-1)(7-k)}\,E_{k-1,k+j} -\sqrt{(k+j)(6-k-j)}\,E_{k,k+j+1}, $$

with the first term absent at $k=1$ and the second absent at $k=6-j$. The coefficient of $E_{k,k+j+1}$ in $\operatorname{ad}_{J_+}(h_j)$ is

$$ c_{j,k+1}\sqrt{k(6-k)}-c_{j,k}\sqrt{(k+j)(6-k-j)}, $$

which vanishes by the recursion. So all terms cancel and the sum is zero.

Theorem (the decomposition). Let $V_{(j)}$ be the span of $h_j$ and its lowers $\operatorname{ad}_{J_-}^r(h_j)$, $r=0,\dots,2j$. Then

$$ M_6(\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{5}V_{(j)}, \qquad \dim V_{(j)}=2j+1, \qquad V_{(j)}\cong\operatorname{Sym}^{2j}(\mathbb{C}^2). $$

Proof. The lowers of a highest weight vector of weight $j$ have weights $j,j-1,\dots,-j$; the chain has $2j+1$ terms and the last is killed by $\operatorname{ad}_{J_-}$, so the span has dimension $2j+1$ and is the irreducible module of spin $j$. The dimensions sum to $\sum_{j=0}^{5}(2j+1)=36$, which is $\dim M_6(\mathbb{C})$. The sum is direct: the modules have distinct Casimir eigenvalues $j(j+1)$, and generalized eigenspaces of a single operator for distinct eigenvalues are independent. So the sum is the whole algebra.

Corollary (the Casimir eigenvalues). On $V_{(j)}$ the Casimir acts by $j(j+1)$, and the eigenvalues and multiplicities on the adjoint module are

$j$ $0$ $1$ $2$ $3$ $4$ $5$
$j(j+1)$ $0$ $2$ $6$ $12$ $20$ $30$
$2j+1$ $1$ $3$ $5$ $7$ $9$ $11$

Proof. The eigenvalue is the standard Casimir eigenvalue of the spin-$j$ representation; on the highest weight vector $C h_j=j(j+1)h_j$, and $C$ commutes with the algebra. The multiplicity is the dimension $2j+1$.

Corollary (the trivial summand). $V_{(0)}$ is spanned by $h_0=I_6$. The traceless part is the sum of the five summands $j=1,\dots,5$, of dimension

$$ 3+5+7+9+11=35=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_6(\mathbb{C}). $$

Proof. $h_0=\sum_{k=1}^{6}E_{kk}=I_6$ by the recursion with $j=0$. The identity spans the trivial summand of the adjoint module, and the complement is the traceless part, which is $\mathfrak{sl}_6$.

The Character Check

Proposition. The character of the adjoint module under $J_3$ is

$$ \chi(q)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{6}q^{l-k} =\left(\sum_{r=1}^{6}q^{\frac72-r}\right)\left(\sum_{s=1}^{6}q^{s-\frac72}\right) =\sum_{j=0}^{5}\left(q^j+q^{j-1}+\cdots+q^{-j}\right). $$

The last sum is the sum of the characters of the irreducible modules of spins $0,1,\dots,5$, each once.

Proof. The first equality is the weight decomposition of the adjoint module. The second is the product of the carrier character and its dual. The identity

$$ [6]_q[6]_{q^{-1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{5}[2j+1]_q, \qquad [m]_q=\frac{q^{m/2}-q^{-m/2}}{q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2}}, $$

is the $q$-integer form of the Clebsch–Gordan decomposition $\operatorname{Sym}^5\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^5{}^*\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{5}\operatorname{Sym}^{2j}$; the coefficient of $q^j$ in the right-hand side is the number of summands of spin at least $j$, which is one for each $j=0,\dots,5$.

Corollary. The multiplicity of the spin-$j$ module in the adjoint is one for every $j=0,\dots,5$, and there is no other summand.

Proof. The coefficient of $q^j$ in $\sum_i[2i+1]_q$ is the number of $i\ge j$, namely $6-j$; it is also the dimension $6-\lvert j\rvert$ of the weight space $W_j$. The multiplicity of the highest weight $q^j$ is the difference between the coefficients of $q^j$ and $q^{j+1}$ (with the coefficient of $q^6$ equal to zero), namely $(6-j)-(5-j)=1$. So each spin occurs once.

The Peirce Table of the Decomposition

The highest weight vectors are supported on single diagonals of the Peirce matrix, and the lowers spread them across the diagonals. The first few are

$$ h_0=I_6,\qquad h_1=\sum_{k=1}^{5}\sqrt{\frac{(k+1)(5-k)}{k(6-k)}}\,E_{k,k+1}, \qquad h_5=E_{16}. $$

For $j=1$ the coefficients are $1,\sqrt{8/5},3/\sqrt5,\sqrt{8/5},1$, so $h_1=J_+/\sqrt5$. For $j=5$ the sum has the single term $E_{16}$.

The Two Chiralities

Definition. The chirality involution of the frame is

$$ \Gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{6}(-1)^{k-1}E_{kk} =\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,1,-1). $$

Proposition. $\Gamma$ commutes with $J_3$ and anticommutes with $J_+$ and $J_-$:

$$ [\Gamma,J_3]=0,\qquad \{\Gamma,J_\pm\}=0 . $$

Proof. $J_3$ and $\Gamma$ are both diagonal. For the ladder, $\Gamma E_{k,k+1}\Gamma=(-1)^{k-1+k}E_{k,k+1}=-E_{k,k+1}$, so $\Gamma J_+\Gamma=-J_+$, and similarly for $J_-$.

Corollary (the chain mixes chirality). Put

$$ \mathbb{C}^6=\mathbb{C}^3_+\oplus\mathbb{C}^3_-, \qquad \mathbb{C}^3_+=\langle v_1,v_3,v_5\rangle,\qquad \mathbb{C}^3_-=\langle v_2,v_4,v_6\rangle . $$

Then $J_3$ preserves the two summands, while $J_+$ and $J_-$ exchange them. Neither summand is stable under the whole $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, and the carrier is irreducible across the split.

Proof. The eigenvalues of $\Gamma$ are $+1$ on the odd-indexed vectors and $-1$ on the even-indexed ones. $J_3$ is diagonal, so it preserves each eigenspace. $J_+$ sends $v_{2r}$ to a multiple of $v_{2r-1}$ and $v_{2r+1}$ to a multiple of $v_{2r}$, so it exchanges the two; $J_-$ does the same in the other direction.

Remark (the dimension of the halves). Each chiral half has dimension three. It is not a spin-$\tfrac12$ module: the spin-$\tfrac12$ of $\mathfrak{su}_2$ is two-dimensional. The correct statement is this. The centraliser of $\Gamma$ is $M_3\oplus M_3$, and each half is the defining three-dimensional module of its $M_3$. Inside that $M_3$, the Peirce $\mathfrak{su}_2$ of a rank-one idempotent of the $M_3$ acts on the half as the spin-$1$ (adjoint) module, the symmetric square $\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$ of the spin-$\tfrac12$. The spin-$\tfrac12$ module itself is two-dimensional; it is the carrier of the biquaternion case $n=2$, §The Comparison with $n=2$.

The Comparison with $n=2$

The biquaternion case. For $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ the same construction gives

$$ J_3=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\right), \qquad J_+=E_{12}, \qquad J_-=E_{21}, \qquad C=\tfrac34I_2, $$

the spin-$\tfrac12$ carrier $\mathbb{C}^2=\operatorname{Sym}^1(\mathbb{C}^2)$, with the single ladder coefficient $1$.

Proposition. For $n=2$ the adjoint decomposition is

$$ M_2(\mathbb{C})=V_{(0)}\oplus V_{(1)}, \qquad \dim V_{(0)}=1,\quad \dim V_{(1)}=3, $$

that is $1+3=4$.

Proof. $V_{(0)}$ is spanned by $I_2$ and $V_{(1)}$ by $E_{12},J_3,E_{21}$, the three traceless matrices.

The pattern. For $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ with the principal chain, the carrier is $\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^2)$ and the adjoint decomposes as

$$ M_n(\mathbb{C})\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{Sym}^{2j}(\mathbb{C}^2), \qquad \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(2j+1)=n^2 . $$

At $n=2$ this is $1+3$; at $n=6$ it is $1+3+5+7+9+11$. The number of summands is $n$, and the largest spin is $n-1$.

Proof. The character of the adjoint is $[n]_q[n]_{q^{-1}}$, and the $q$-integer identity gives $\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}[2j+1]_q$.

Remark (the contrast of the two chiralities). For $n=2$ the chirality split is $1+1$, and each half is a line; the pair of halves is the spin-$\tfrac12$ carrier. For $n=6$ the split is $3+3$, each half is three-dimensional, and the halves are modules for the block $M_3$ but not for the Peirce $\mathfrak{su}_2$ of the carrier. The passage from $n=2$ to $n=6$ replaces the two lines by the two triples; the spin-$\tfrac12$ is the $n=2$ carrier, and the spin-$\tfrac52$ is the $n=6$ carrier.

Summary

The frame carries the principal triple

$$ J_3=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\tfrac52,\tfrac32,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac32,-\tfrac52\right), \qquad J_+=\sum_{k=1}^{5}\sqrt{k(6-k)}E_{k,k+1}, $$

with $[J_3,J_\pm]=\pm J_\pm$ and $[J_+,J_-]=2J_3$. The carrier is $\operatorname{Sym}^5(\mathbb{C}^2)$, of spin $\tfrac52$, with ladder coefficients $\sqrt5,\sqrt8,3,\sqrt8,\sqrt5$ and Casimir $\tfrac{35}{4}I_6$.

$$ \boxed{\ M_6(\mathbb{C})\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{5}\operatorname{Sym}^{2j}(\mathbb{C}^2),\qquad 1+3+5+7+9+11=36 .\ } $$

The Peirce line $A_{kl}$ is the weight line $l-k$. The chirality $\Gamma$ commutes with $J_3$ and anticommutes with $J_\pm$, so the two chiral halves are preserved by the Cartan and exchanged by the ladder: the chain is irreducible and mixes chirality.

The Collapse of the Twelve Structures

Introduction

The biquaternion method builds twelve products, and each is the multiplication of an algebraic structure of its own kind (The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space, The 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space). Read on the complex space, the twelve collapse to two. This article states the arithmetic of the collapse, gives the dictionary of the twelve against the two, reads what disappears with the collapsed operations, and names the two structures that survive.

The complex space is read over itself and as the centre of the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$; the biquaternion chapter is the rung below, and the two operations that survive are those of The Two Products of the Complex Space. The article does not re-derive the twelve: the codes, the tables and the laws are the biquaternion articles', and this one is the reading of them on a commutative base.

Conventions. Bar is complex conjugation, the natural sign is the identity on the commutative base, $\tilde Q^{\natural}=\tilde Q$ for a central element, and the Hermitian mark is the bar, ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}$. Elements are written $A$ and $C$, and $B$ is not used for an element: $B$, $N$, $H$, $K$ are the four forms of the biquaternion algebra, and the two that survive here are $B$ and $H$ (The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Complex Space, below this article in this category). The three-letter codes are those of The 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space: the first letter is the operation, $\mathrm G$, $\mathrm S$ or $\mathrm A$; the second is the family, plain $\mathrm P$ or quaternionic $\mathrm Q$; the third is the class, algebra $\mathrm A$ over $\mathbb{C}$ or sesqualgebra $\mathrm S$ over $(\mathbb{C},\bar{\cdot})$.

The Interpretation on the Complex Space

Each of the four general products of the biquaternion method fills two slots, and each slot carries the identity or a conjugation. On the complex space the natural sign is the identity and the Hermitian mark is the bar, so the four general products take two values,

$$ \mathrm{GPA}:\;AC,\qquad \mathrm{GQA}:\;A^{\natural}C=AC,\qquad \mathrm{GPS}:\;A\bar C,\qquad \mathrm{GQS}:\;A^{\natural}\bar C=A\bar C . $$

The pair $(\natural,\mathrm{id})$ therefore gives the same product as $(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})$, namely $AC$, and the pair $(\natural,{}^{*})$ gives the same product as $(\mathrm{id},{}^{*})$, namely $A\bar C$; the four general products of $\mathbb{B}$ are here two operations and no more (The Two Products of the Complex Space).

The Arithmetic of the Collapse

The symmetrisation and the antisymmetrisation of a product are the half-sum and the half-difference of the product and its exchange, which keeps the class of the product: the interchange of the two arguments for a bilinear product, and the conjugate transpose for a sesquilinear one (The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space). On the complex space the exchange acts on the value through the bar, and each of the four general products is fixed by its exchange up to a sign, so each split gives the product back or zero.

The two bilinear families. The plain product is commutative, $AC=CA$, so the symmetrisation is the product and the antisymmetrisation vanishes:

$$ \mathrm{SPA}=\tfrac12(AC+CA)=AC=\mathrm{GPA},\qquad \mathrm{APA}=\tfrac12(AC-CA)=0 . $$

The quaternionic bilinear product is the same as the plain one, because the natural sign is the identity, $A^{\natural}C=AC$; hence the same two identities hold with $\mathrm Q$ for $\mathrm P$,

$$ \mathrm{SQA}=\tfrac12(A^{\natural}C+C^{\natural}A)=\tfrac12(AC+CA)=AC=\mathrm{GQA},\qquad \mathrm{AQA}=0 . $$

The two sesquilinear families. The exchange is the conjugate transpose, and the sesquilinear product is conjugate-symmetric, $A\bar C=\overline{C\bar A}$, so again the symmetrisation is the product and the antisymmetrisation vanishes:

$$ \mathrm{SPS}=\tfrac12\bigl(A\bar C+\bar C A\bigr)=A\bar C=\mathrm{GPS},\qquad \mathrm{APS}=\tfrac12\bigl(A\bar C-\bar C A\bigr)=0 . $$

The quaternionic sesquilinear product is the same as the plain one, $A^{\natural}\bar C=A\bar C$, and its split is the same,

$$ \mathrm{SQS}=\tfrac12\bigl(A^{\natural}\bar C+\bar C^{\natural}A\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(A\bar C+\bar C A\bigr)=A\bar C=\mathrm{GQS},\qquad \mathrm{AQS}=\tfrac12\bigl(A^{\natural}\bar C-\bar C^{\natural}A\bigr)=0 . $$

The commutativity of $\mathbb{C}$ and the conjugate-symmetry of $A\bar C$ are what make the difference in each pair vanish; the triviality of the natural sign is what makes the two families of each pair one.

$\mathrm{AQS}$, the antisymmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tfrac12(A^{\natural}\bar C-\bar C^{\natural}A)=0$ the zero product

Eight of the twelve are the two surviving operations, four to each, and four are the zero operation. The two structures that survive are the field $\mathbb{C}$ under the multiplication $AC$, carried by the whole plain and quaternionic bilinear families, and the sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{C},\bar{\cdot})$ under $A\bar C$, carried by the whole plain and quaternionic sesquilinear families. The two operations are defined and compared in The Two Products of the Complex Space.

What Disappears

The four antisymmetric operations are the zero operation, and with them the whole antisymmetric layer of the biquaternion method disappears, since the Lie layer of the twelve is built from them. There is no bracket to satisfy or to fail the Jacobi identity, so there is no Jacobi failure to record: on $\mathbb{B}$ the quaternionic antisymmetrisation $\mathrm{AQA}$ fails the identity with the cyclic sum $-e_3$, and here that operation is zero while its parent, $A^{\natural}C=AC$, is the associative product of the field, so the associator defect that the failure measures is zero as well.

Caution (what does not disappear). The vanishing of the associator defect is a statement about the quaternionic product, which has become associative, and about the antisymmetrisations, which are zero. The surviving sesquilinear product $A\bar C$ is still not associative, since $A\overline{C\bar D}$ and $(A\bar C)\bar D$ differ by $A\bar C(\bar D-D)$; its associator is not the object that the collapse annihilates, and it does not surface as a Jacobi failure, because the operation that would turn it into one, the antisymmetrisation, is the zero operation.

Why the Collapse Happens, and the Ladder

The collapse is the algebraic statement of the commutativity of $\mathbb{C}$ and of the triviality of the natural sign. The two antisymmetrisations of the bilinear families vanish because $AC=CA$; those of the sesquilinear families vanish because $A\bar C=\overline{C\bar A}$, which is commutativity read through the bar; and the merging of the plain and the quaternionic family is the triviality of the natural sign on the commutative base. The defects of the antisymmetric layer dissolve with it: the Jacobi failures have no operation left to fail on, and the quaternionic product, having become the associative product of the field, has a vanishing associator. What does not dissolve is the non-associativity of the surviving sesquilinear product $A\bar C$, which is a feature of the conjugate-linear slot and not of the commutative base.

The complex space is therefore the degenerate base of the ladder: it is the rung at which the twelve operations of the method coincide in two operations and the antisymmetric slot is empty. The twelve reappear in full at the biquaternion rung (The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space), where the base is non-commutative; the complex reading is the floor below them, and its two surviving structures are the field and the sesqualgebra of the complex space.

Summary

Read on the complex space, the twelve operations and the twelve structures of the biquaternion method collapse to two: the field $\mathbb{C}$ under the multiplication $AC$, and the sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{C},\bar{\cdot})$ under the conjugate-linear-in-the-second-slot product $A\bar C$. The arithmetic is three identities: the symmetrisation of a product is the product itself, because the plain product is commutative and the sesquilinear one is conjugate-symmetric; the quaternionic product of each family is the plain one, because the natural sign is the identity; and the four antisymmetric operations are the zero operation. The collapse is the algebraic statement of the commutativity of $\mathbb{C}$, and the complex space is the degenerate base of the ladder on which the twelve reappear at the biquaternion rung.

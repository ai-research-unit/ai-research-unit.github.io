The Two-Sided Exponential on the N-Regular Sector

Introduction

This article computes the one-parameter group generated by the two-sided operator on the N-regular sector, and reads it as the double of the one-sided group. The sector is the group of invertibles $N\neq0$. The algebra of the operator — the closed form, the factor-by-factor action, the two slots, the image and the kernel — is in Two-Sided Operators on the N-Regular Sector; the one-sided companion is The One-Sided Operator Exponential on the N-Regular Sector.

There is no element identity of the form $\exp(t\,\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q})=\mathrm{Sd}_{\exp(t\tilde Q)}$: the operator is quadratic, and the two-sided exponential is read on the spectrum instead. What the norm decides, and what the vector form decides. The norm fixes the rate, which is $\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}$, the square of the one-sided rate $Q_t$; the two slots of the operator are why. The wall $B^{2}=0$ fixes only the shape of the group: off the wall the operator is diagonalisable with the four eigenvalues $\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j$, and on the wall it is $\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}$ times a unipotent operator with $\mathsf N^{3}=0$, so the group is a family of null rotations, a finite polynomial with no angle and no rapidity in its spectrum.

The doubling rule is the subject of the middle of the article. The scale doubles $\ln \varrho$ to $2\ln \varrho$, the phase cancels to $0$, the boost doubles the half-rapidity to $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$, and the rotor doubles the half-angle to $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$. The rule is the number of slots: the Hermitian factors carry the same exponent twice, the $*$-unitary non-central factor carries opposite exponents, and the central phase cancels.

The plan is the following. §The Operator Exponential proves the spectral form. §The Exponents of the Factors and §The Pure Factors and the Full Rotor take the factors one at a time. §The Vector Representation as the Double of the Half-Angle identifies the operator with $\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}\otimes\bar\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}$. §The Angle Bookkeeping collects the table and the doubling rule. §The Wall $B^{2}=0$ computes the null rotation and the exponents the factors keep. The article closes with the summary and the notation.

The Operator Exponential

Proposition. On $\Sigma_{11}$ the two-sided operator is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues $\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j$ of §The Spectrum of the Two-Sided Operator of the companion. Its exponential is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues

$$ e^{t\lambda_+\bar\lambda_+},\quad e^{t\lambda_+\bar\lambda_-},\quad e^{t\lambda_-\bar\lambda_+},\quad e^{t\lambda_-\bar\lambda_-}, $$

and determinant

$$ \boxed{\;\det\exp\bigl(t\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}\bigr) =\exp\bigl(t\operatorname{tr}\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}\bigr) =\exp\bigl(4t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}\bigr).\;} $$

Proof. The two-sided operator is diagonalisable in the common eigenbasis of §The Two Slots of the Two-Sided Operator of the companion, and the exponential of a diagonal operator is the diagonal of the exponentials. The trace is the sum of the four products, $$ \lambda_+\bar\lambda_++\lambda_+\bar\lambda_-+\lambda_-\bar\lambda_++\lambda_-\bar\lambda_- =(\lambda_++\lambda_-)(\bar\lambda_++\bar\lambda_-) =4\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}, $$ and $\det\exp(tA)=\exp(t\operatorname{tr}A)$.

Remark (no element identity). For a central element $\tilde Q=c\,e_t$ the two-sided operator is $\lvert c\rvert^{2}\mathrm{id}$, so $\exp(t\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q})=e^{t\lvert c\rvert^{2}}\mathrm{id}$, while $\mathrm{Sd}_{\exp(t\tilde Q)}=e^{2t\operatorname{Re}c}\mathrm{id}$. The two agree only when $\lvert c\rvert^{2}=2\operatorname{Re}c$. The two-sided operator exponentiates its image, not its parameter.

The Exponents of the Factors

The Scale

The exponent is $\ln \varrho$, and the two-sided operator sends it to

$$ \boxed{\;\ln \varrho\longmapsto 2\ln \varrho\;}. $$

There is no angle. The factor two is nevertheless the same factor two as in the boost and the rotor: the exponent of the factor is half the exponent of its image. The scale is the case in which the doubled parameter is a dilation and not a rotation or a rapidity. The map $\varrho\mapsto \varrho^{2}$ is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, so the scale is recovered from its two-sided operator; only the sign of the factor is lost, and the polar word has $\varrho>0$.

The Boost

The exponent is $i\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ and the output is the rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$. The parameter of the factor is half the parameter of its image, and the doubling is exact. The boost is Hermitian, so both slots carry the exponent with the same sign; the two-sided operator adds the two exponents instead of cancelling them, and the sum is $2\cdot\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}=\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$.

The Rotor

The exponent is $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ and the output is the rotation of angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$ about $\vec u_{\mathrm r}$. The parameter of the factor is half the parameter of its image. The rotor is $*$-unitary, so the two slots carry opposite signs; the two-sided operator conjugates instead of composing the two exponents, and the conjugation by $\exp(\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r})$ is the rotation of angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$. Equivalently, the double cover $SU(2)\to SO(3)$ sends $\pm\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ to the same rotation, and the parameter $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$ of the image is twice the parameter $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2$ of the rotor. The two-sided operator sees the rotation and not the sign; the sign is the extra data of the one-sided operator.

The Pure Factors and the Full Rotor

For each pure factor, read with the other factors absent, the products are elementary. The scale $\varrho$ has $\lambda_\pm=\varrho$ and products $\varrho^{2}$. The central phase has $\lambda_\pm=\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}})$ and products $\exp(i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}})\exp(-i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}})=1$. The boost has $$ \lambda_+=e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2},\qquad \lambda_-=e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}, $$ so its products are $$ e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}},\quad 1,\quad 1,\quad e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}. $$ The rotor has $$ \lambda_+=e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},\qquad \lambda_-=e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}, $$ so its products are $$ e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}},\quad 1,\quad 1,\quad e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}. $$ The two-sided operator therefore carries the full rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ and the full angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$, while the one-sided operator carries the half-rapidity and the half-angle. The phase cancels and the scale doubles.

The one-sided operator is the spinor action. The two-sided operator has no half-angle of its own: it pairs two one-sided slots, and each slot is the spinor action of The One-Sided Operator Exponential on the N-Regular Sector, §The Polar Word on a Spinor. The half-angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2$ and the half-rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2$ live on a single slot; this article computes their products, which are the full angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$ and the full rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$.

The scale and the phase are central. Their rows are read as scalar multipliers of a word with $B\neq0$; for a purely central element $B=0$ and the spectrum collapses to $Q_t\bar Q_t$.

For a general word the two factors do not commute, and the common eigenbasis is not the eigenbasis of $L_{\tilde Q}$. The product formula for the spectrum remains exact. The splitting of the products into a boost and a rotation is the content of §The Boost and §The Rotor when the axes commute; in general the effective exponent is the sum of the two slot exponents only after the common eigenbasis is chosen. The relation between the spectral data and the polar parameters is Relations between the Complete Polar Decomposition and Eigenbasis of an N-Regular Biquaternion, §The Spectral Data from the Polar Factors.

Corollary (the two-sided operator is the two-slot operator). The two-sided operator acts on the left and the right slots of the matrix model. Its spectrum is the four products $\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j$. The one-sided operator is the restriction to one slot; the two-sided operator is the pairing of the two slots. The half-angle of the rotor and the half-rapidity of the boost belong to one slot; their doubles belong to the pair.

The Vector Representation as the Double of the Half-Angle

The two slots are the two tensor factors of $\mathbb{B}\cong S\otimes S^{*}$, where $S=\mathbb{C}^{2}$ is the spinor space and $S^{*}$ its dual. The left factor acts on $S$, the right on $S^{*}$, and the two-sided operator is $$ \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}(\tilde Q)\otimes\bar\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}(\tilde Q), $$ where $\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}$ is the defining representation on $S=\mathbb{C}^{2}$ and $\bar\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}$ its conjugate. The tensor product of the defining representation and its conjugate is the complexified vector representation, $$ 2\otimes\bar 2=4, $$ so the two-sided operator is the vector and the one-sided operator, on one slot, is the spinor.

The weights add. For the rotor, $\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}(\tilde u_{\mathrm r})$ has weights $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$ and $\bar\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}(\tilde u_{\mathrm r})$ has weights $e^{\mp i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$; the four sums are $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}},0,0,-\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$. For the boost, both slots carry $e^{\mp\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}$ because the boost is Hermitian; the four sums are $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}},0,0,-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$. In both cases the vector weights are the sums of the two spinor weights, and each extremal vector weight is twice a spinor weight.

The two-sided operator has no half-angle of its own. It is the vector representation, of dimension four; its weights are the sums. The half-angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2$ and the half-rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2$ live on a single slot, in The One-Sided Operator Exponential on the N-Regular Sector, §The Polar Word on a Spinor. The two-sided operator is the double of the half-spin, not a half-spin itself.

The two Peirce copies are not the two Lorentz chiralities. The two columns $S_+$ and $S_-$ carry the same Weyl representation $\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}$; the two Lorentz chiralities are the two tensor slots $S$ and $S^{*}$, that is the left and right slots.

The addition is at the level of generators. For a general word the two slot exponents do not combine into one rapidity and one angle unless the axes commute. The four products $\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j$ remain exact; the split into a boost and a rotation is the content of §The Boost and §The Rotor for commuting axes. For a pure non-central factor the eigenvalue $1$ occurs twice in the list; for the scale the value $\varrho^{2}$ occurs four times, and for the phase the value $1$ occurs four times.

The whole section assumes $B\neq0$. When $B=0$ and $\vec Q\neq0$, $L_{\tilde Q}$ is defective, with two Jordan blocks of size two, the common eigenbasis of $L_{\tilde Q}$ and $R_{\tilde Q^{*}}$ does not exist, and the spectral formula is unavailable; the two-sided operator itself remains defined. This is the degenerate case of The Spectrum, §The Degenerate Case: The Spectral-Degenerate Cone.

The Doubling Rule

Proposition (the doubling rule). Write each factor as $F=\exp(tA)$, where $t$ is its scalar parameter and $A$ its generator: $A=e_t$ for the scale, $A=i$ for the phase, $A=i\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ for the boost and $A=\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ for the rotor. Then

$$ \mathrm{Sd}_F(\tilde X)=\exp(tA)\,\tilde X\,\exp(tA^{*}). $$

Three cases exhaust the word:

(a) $A^{*}=A$ (Hermitian). The two slots carry the same exponent $+tA$. If $A$ is central, as for the scale with $A=e_t$, the two-sided operator is multiplication by $\exp(2tA)$; if $A$ is non-central, as for the boost with $A=i\vec u_{\mathrm b}$, the two-sided operator is the congruence whose parameter is $2t$. In both cases the output parameter is $2t$.

(b) $A^{*}=-A$ and $A$ non-central (the rotor, $A=\vec u_{\mathrm r}$). The two slots carry opposite exponents, so the two-sided operator is the conjugation $\exp(t\,\mathrm{ad}_A)$, whose vector action is the rotation of angle $2t$; the output parameter is $2t$.

(c) $A^{*}=-A$ and $A$ central (the phase, $A=i$). The two slots carry opposite exponents and $A$ commutes with everything, so $\mathrm{ad}_A=0$ and the output parameter is $0$.

In one formula,

$$ \text{output parameter}= \begin{cases} 2t, & A^{*}=A,\\ 2t, & A^{*}=-A\ \text{and}\ A\ \text{non-central},\\ 0, & A^{*}=-A\ \text{and}\ A\ \text{central}. \end{cases} $$

The doubling is therefore a property of the non-central factors, and of the Hermitian central one; the cancellation is a property of the anti-Hermitian central one. "Half-angle" is the name of the boost and rotor rows; it is not a property of the phase.

The Wall $B^{2}=0$: the Exponential at the Wall

On the wall the element is $Q_t(e_t+\vec\Upsilon)$ with $\vec\Upsilon^{2}=0$, so the operator is $\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}$ times a unipotent operator, $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}(I+\mathsf N)$ with $\mathsf N^{3}=0$. The exponential is a finite polynomial and the one-parameter group is a family of null rotations. The boost and the rotor keep their angles in the factors and lose them in the spectrum of the product; their parameters are locked to the null parameter $a$, and the rate is $\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}$, the square of the one-sided rate.

The Exponential at the Wall

The exponent of the two-sided operator. Write $\vec\Upsilon=\vec A+i\vec B$ with $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ real, so that $\vec A\cdot\vec B=0$ and $\lvert\vec A\rvert=\lvert\vec B\rvert=a$, and put $A=L_{\vec\Upsilon}$ and $B=R_{\vec\Upsilon^{*}}$. Write $\bar\Upsilon=\vec A-i\vec B$ for the coefficient conjugate. The operators $A$ and $B$ commute and square to zero. Since $\tilde U=e^{\vec\Upsilon}$ and $\tilde U^{*}=e^{\vec\Upsilon^{*}}$,

$$ \mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde U}=L_{e^{\vec\Upsilon}}\circ R_{e^{\vec\Upsilon^{*}}}=e^{A}e^{B}=e^{A+B}, $$

and $(A+B)^2=2AB$ and $(A+B)^3=0$. Hence

$$ \boxed{\;\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde U} =\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}e^{A+B} =\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}\bigl(I+A+B+AB\bigr) =\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}(I+\mathsf N).\;} $$

The one-parameter group. Since $\mathsf N^2=2AB$ and $\mathsf N^3=0$,

$$ \boxed{\;\exp\bigl(t\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}\bigr) =e^{t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}} \Bigl(I+t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}\mathsf N+\frac{t^{2}\lvert Q_t\rvert^{4}}{2}\mathsf N^{2}\Bigr)\;} $$

for every complex $t$.

Proof. $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}(I+\mathsf N)$, and $I$ commutes with $\mathsf N$; hence the exponential is $e^{t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}}\exp(t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}\mathsf N)$, and the second factor truncates at $\mathsf N^3=0$.

Interpretation: a null rotation. On $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ the factor $\tilde U$ has norm one, so $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde U}$ preserves the Minkowski form $\langle X\rangle_{\natural}$; the path $\mathrm{Sd}_{e^{t\vec\Upsilon}}$, $0\le t\le1$, connects it to the identity, so it is a proper orthochronous Lorentz transformation. Its single eigenvalue is $1$, and its Jordan form is one block of size three and one of size one. A Lorentz transformation of that type is a null rotation (a parabolic element): it fixes a null direction and shears the two transverse directions. The exponential displays the finite shear as $I+s\mathsf N+\frac{s^2}{2}\mathsf N^2$ with $s=t\lvert Q_t\rvert^2$. It contains no rotation angle and no rapidity; the two are locked to the single null parameter $a$. The modulus scales the shear, and the phase cancels.

What the factors keep. The factors are not degenerate in the same way as their product. $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde U_{\mathrm b}}$ has the simple eigenvalues $e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}$ and $e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}$ and the eigenvalue $1$ with multiplicity two; $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}$ has the simple eigenvalues $e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}$ and $e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}$ and the eigenvalue $1$ with multiplicity two. The product has the single eigenvalue $\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}$, four times. The boost and the angle are therefore present in the factors and absent from the spectrum of the product. In the product they survive only through the locked parameter $a$, with $\varepsilon\tan\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=\sinh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}=a$.

The rate is the square of the one-sided rate: $\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}$ here, $Q_t$ there. The reason is the slot count. The two-sided operator carries $\vec\Upsilon$ in its left slot and $\vec\Upsilon^{*}$ in its right slot, so the scalar $Q_t$ appears once on each side and the modulus is squared; the one-sided operator carries the left slot only. This is the wall form of the doubling rule: on a vector the two slots add their exponents, and at the wall they add $\vec\Upsilon$ and $\vec\Upsilon^{*}$.

Worked Example on the Wall

The companion gives the matrix of $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}$ and its nilpotent part $\mathsf N$. Its exponential is the finite null rotation

$$ \exp\bigl(t\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}\bigr) =e^{t}\Bigl(I+t\mathsf N+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\mathsf N^{2}\Bigr) =e^{t} \begin{pmatrix} 1+2t+2t^{2}&0&-2it&2it(1+t)\\ 0&1&0&0\\ 2it&0&1&-2t\\ 2it(1+t)&0&2t&1-2t-2t^{2} \end{pmatrix}, $$

with the matrix $\mathsf N$ above. The fixed coordinate is the second one, which is the $1\times1$ block; the remaining three coordinates carry the chain of length three.

Summary

The one-parameter group of the two-sided operator is read on the spectrum. On $\Sigma_{11}$ the two-sided operator has the four eigenvalues $\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j$, and its exponential has their exponentials; its determinant is $\exp(4t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2})$. There is no element identity $\exp(t\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q})=\mathrm{Sd}_{\exp(t\tilde Q)}$, because the two-sided operator is quadratic; for a central element the two sides differ by the square of the modulus against twice its real part.

The four factors give the table of exponents. The scale doubles $\ln \varrho$ to $2\ln \varrho$; the phase cancels $i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$ to $0$; the boost doubles $i\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ to the rapidity $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$; the rotor doubles $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ to the angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$. The rule is the number of slots: the Hermitian factors carry the same exponent twice, the $*$-unitary non-central factor carries opposite exponents whose conjugation produces the angle, and the central $*$-unitary factor cancels.

On the wall the operator is $\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q}=\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}e^{A+B}$ with $A=L_{\vec\Upsilon}$, $B=R_{\vec\Upsilon^{*}}$, $\mathsf N=A+B+AB$, $\mathsf N^{2}=2AB$ and $\mathsf N^{3}=0$. The group is the finite polynomial $\exp(t\,\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde Q})=e^{t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}}\bigl(I+t\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}\mathsf N+\tfrac{t^{2}\lvert Q_t\rvert^{4}}{2}\mathsf N^{2}\bigr)$. On $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ it is a null rotation, with the single eigenvalue $1$ and the Jordan form $J_3(1)\oplus J_1(1)$; the boost and the rotor keep the eigenvalues $e^{\pm\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}$ and $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}$ in the factors and contribute only the locked parameter $a$ to the product.

The spectral reading is the product formula. The one-slot pair of the rotor is $(e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2})$ and the two-slot products are $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}$; the one-slot pair of the boost is $(e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2},e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2})$ and the products are $e^{\pm\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}$; the scale gives $\varrho^{2}$ and the phase gives $1$. In representation-theoretic terms the two-sided operator is $\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}\otimes\bar\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}$, the complexified vector representation, and the vector weights are the sums of the two spinor weights. The one-sided operator is the spinor action, and the two-sided operator is its double.

The Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$

Introduction

The antisymmetric quaternionic algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ is the operation $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=p_0\vec q-q_0\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions. This article reads it on the embedding $\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow M_2(\mathbb{C})$ of Introduction to the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ of Quaternions, where the parent product becomes the conjugated product $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)$ and the antisymmetrization becomes the conjugated commutator. It gives the matrix form, the image, the invariants, the invariant forms and the comparison with the other blocks on the same model.

The reading is the conjugated twin of the plain one: the plain antisymmetrization is the commutator of the matrix images, and the antisymmetrisation of the parent is the halved conjugated commutator, whose values are the anti-Hermitian matrices.

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The model is an algebra embedding of the plain product; the antisymmetric quaternionic block is read on it through the conjugation of the first factor and is not the matrix product. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new. The symbol $\diamond$ is reserved for this block.

The model and its multiplicativity are Introduction to the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ of Quaternions; the block and its failure are Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions and The Jacobi Failure and the Associator Defect of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category; the form is The Invariant Bilinear Forms of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category. The companion articles are The Antisymmetric Plain Algebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, above this article in this category, and The Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra in the $4\times4$ Matrix Element Representation $M_4(\mathbb{R})_L$, below this article in this category.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1^2=e_2^2=e_3^2=-e_0$; the model is

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)=\begin{pmatrix}q_0-iq_3 & -iq_1-q_2\\ -iq_1+q_2 & q_0+iq_3\end{pmatrix}, $$

with $\mathsf{M}_2(e_0)=\mathrm{I}_2$, $\mathsf{M}_2(e_1)=-i\sigma_1$, $\mathsf{M}_2(e_2)=-i\sigma_2$, $\mathsf{M}_2(e_3)=-i\sigma_3$, the Pauli matrices. The plain product is the matrix product, $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\tilde q)=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)$, the parent product is $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)$, and the conjugation is the Hermitian adjoint, $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q^{\natural})=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)^{\dagger}$. The quaternion form is $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0+\vec p\cdot\vec q$ and the plain form $B(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0-\vec p\cdot\vec q$.

The Model and the Conjugated Commutator

Theorem (the bracket is the halved conjugated commutator).

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=\tfrac12\bigl(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)-\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\bigr) =\tfrac12\bigl[\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)^{\dagger},\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)\bigr] . $$

Proof. The model is multiplicative and the conjugation is the Hermitian adjoint, so $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)-\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)$, and half of it is the image of the block.

Remark (the contrast with the plain block on the same model). The plain antisymmetrization is the halved plain commutator $\tfrac12[\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p),\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)]$, of values the traceless matrices of the image; the antisymmetrization of the parent is the halved conjugated commutator, of values the anti-Hermitian matrices. The two agree on the pure elements up to sign, since $\mathsf{M}_2(\vec u)^{\dagger}=-\mathsf{M}_2(\vec u)$ for a pure $\vec u$.

The Image: the Anti-Hermitian Matrices

Theorem (the image). Every value of the block is an anti-Hermitian matrix,

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)^{\dagger}=-\,\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q), $$

of trace zero, and the image of the block is the three-dimensional real space of anti-Hermitian traceless matrices, which is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.

Proof. The conjugated commutator of two matrices $X^{\dagger}Y-Y^{\dagger}X$ is anti-Hermitian, since its adjoint is $Y^{\dagger}X-X^{\dagger}Y$, its negative; the trace vanishes because the trace of a commutator vanishes. The space of anti-Hermitian traceless $2\times2$ matrices is $i\,\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\text{Hermitian}}=\mathfrak{su}(2)$, of real dimension three, and the image is the whole of it, the pure values generating it.

Remark (the failure is the commutator of the failure). The image is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, but the operation is not the Lie bracket of the model: the model bracket would be the plain commutator, while the block is the conjugated commutator, and it is the conjugation that breaks the Jacobi identity (The Jacobi Failure and the Associator Defect of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category). The block is a Lie bracket on the image only in the sense that its restriction to the pure elements is the cross product up to sign.

The Invariants

Theorem. The image matrix $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)$ has

$$ \operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=0,\qquad \det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=N(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=|\tilde p\diamond\tilde q|^2, \qquad \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)^2=-N(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)\,\mathrm{I}_2 , $$

the determinant being the squared norm of the value, and the matrix is anti-Hermitian.

Proof. The value is pure, so its matrix is traceless; an anti-Hermitian traceless $2\times2$ matrix $X$ satisfies $X^2=-\det(X)\,\mathrm{I}_2$ and $\det X=N(X)$, the norm of the pure element, by the model's determinant formula in Introduction to the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ of Quaternions.

The Invariant Forms

Theorem (the forms on the model). The trace forms of the model read the two forms of the space,

$$ \tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)^{\dagger}\bigr)=N(\tilde p,\tilde q),\qquad \tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)\bigr)=B(\tilde p,\tilde q), $$

the unique invariant form of the block is the scalar-part form

$$ \varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0=\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\cdot\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q), $$

and the operator trace form is the combination $K(\tilde p,\tilde q)=\tfrac12\bigl(N(\tilde p,\tilde q)+5B(\tilde p,\tilde q)\bigr)=3p_0q_0-2\,\vec p\cdot\vec q$.

Proof. The model is multiplicative, so $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)^{\dagger})=\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\tilde q^{\natural})=2N(\tilde p,\tilde q)$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q))=2B(\tilde p,\tilde q)$, by the trace formula of the model; the trace of the model is $2q_0$, whence $\varphi$; the combination is the operator trace form of The Invariant Bilinear Forms of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category, solved in the two forms.

Remark (the block removes the trace). The coefficient form of the block vanishes, $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=0$, because the values are traceless; the trace form of the block is the zero form, and the only invariant form of the block remains the degenerate $\varphi$ of the scalar-part slot.

Remark (the reading of the table). The upper three rows are the products of the plain row, the lower three the products of the conjugated row; the general products are the matrix product and the conjugated matrix product, and within each row the symmetric and the antisymmetric halves are the corresponding halved sums and differences. The antisymmetric quaternionic block is the lower antisymmetric entry: the halved conjugated commutator, of anti-Hermitian values, while the plain antisymmetric block is the upper antisymmetric entry, of traceless values. The plain reading on the same model is The Antisymmetric Plain Algebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, above this article in this category.

Summary

In the two by two complex model the antisymmetric quaternionic block is the halved conjugated commutator, $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=\tfrac12[\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)^{\dagger},\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)]$, whose values are the anti-Hermitian traceless matrices, that is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; the block is not the commutator of the model, and it is the conjugation that breaks the Jacobi identity. The invariants are $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=0$, $\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=N(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=|\tilde p\diamond\tilde q|^2$ and $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)^2=-N(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)\mathrm{I}_2$. The trace of the model with the conjugate reads the quaternion form, $\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)^{\dagger})=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)$, the plain trace reads the plain form, the unique invariant form of the block is $\varphi=p_0q_0=\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)$, and the operator trace form is $\tfrac12(N+5B)=3p_0q_0-2\,\vec p\cdot\vec q$. The six products are the matrix product and its symmetric and antisymmetric halves, and the conjugated matrix product and its halves.

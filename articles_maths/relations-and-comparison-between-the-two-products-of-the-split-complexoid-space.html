Relations and Comparison Between the Two Products of the Split-Complexoid Space

Introduction

This article collects the relations between the two products $Q_1Q_2$ and $Q_1\overline{Q_2}$ and the comparison of their laws: each of the two products determines the other through the conjugation, the conjugation and the exchange of the two slots act differently on the two, the sum and the difference of the two values are the two coordinates of one of the factors, and a short list of identities holds for the one product and fails for the other. The comparison closes with the property table of the two products and with the collapse of the four tables of the four general products onto the two tables of these two. It is where the operational difference of the two products is stated in full. It closes with the two slots the construction has on $\mathbb{D}$, whose first slot is empty, and with the two pairings and the two adjoints the two products carry.

The two products and their structures are The Two Products of the Split-Complexoid Space, the article preceding this one; the associator, the ternary product, the left and right multiplications, the sandwich and the commutator of the sesquilinear product are The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complexoid Space, both below this article in this category. The degree-two reading of the two products, that is the two forms and the two algebraic norms, is The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complexoid Space, and the products on the distinguished subspaces are Remarkable Subspaces and the Two Products of the Split-Complexoid Space. The only computations the article adds are the two adjoints of §The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints.

The Products Determine Each Other

Proposition (the two products are related by the bar). For all $Q_1,Q_2\in\mathbb{D}$,

$$ Q_1\overline{Q_2} = \overline{\bar Q_1 Q_2}, \qquad Q_1Q_2 = \overline{\bar Q_1\,\overline{Q_2}} . $$

Each of the two products is recovered from the other by the conjugation, so the two products carry the same information and neither can be omitted from the description of the space.

Proof. $\overline{\bar Q_1 Q_2}=\overline{\bar Q_1}\,\overline{Q_2}$ by multiplicativity of the bar on the ring product, and $\overline{\bar Q_1}=Q_1$, so $\overline{\bar Q_1 Q_2}=Q_1\overline{Q_2}$. Applying the bar to both sides, $Q_1Q_2=\overline{Q_1\overline{Q_2}}=\overline{\bar Q_1\,\overline{Q_2}}$, which is the second identity.

The two identities say that the bar is an isomorphism from the sesquilinear product to the bilinear one: $Q_1\overline{Q_2}=\overline{\bar Q_1 Q_2}$ reads the sesquilinear product of $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ as the conjugate of the ring product of $\bar Q_1$ and $Q_2$, and $Q_1Q_2=\overline{\bar Q_1\overline{Q_2}}$ reads the ring product as the conjugate of the sesquilinear product of $\bar Q_1$ and $Q_2$. A computation may be transferred from one product to the other by conjugating one factor and the result.

The Conjugate of a Product

Proposition (how the bar acts on each product).

$$ \overline{Q_1Q_2}=\bar Q_1\,\bar Q_2, \qquad \overline{Q_1\overline{Q_2}}=\bar Q_1\,Q_2 . $$

So the bar is an automorphism of the ring product and an antilinear operation for the sesquilinear product: it conjugates a ring product term by term and it conjugates a sesquilinear product with the conjugation of the second slot undone.

Proof. The first is the multiplicativity of the bar. For the second, $\overline{Q_1\overline{Q_2}}=\bar Q_1\,\overline{\overline{Q_2}}=\bar Q_1 Q_2$.

The two rules differ in the second slot: on the ring product both slots keep their bar, on the sesquilinear product the bar of the second slot cancels. The divergence is the reason the two products behave differently under every application of the bar, and it is visible at once in the table.

The Exchange of the Two Slots

Two exchanges are read on a product $f$ of two slots: the plain exchange, which interchanges the two entries, and the conjugate exchange, which interchanges the entries after conjugating both.

Proposition (the two exchanges).

$$ f(Q_2,Q_1): \quad Q_1Q_2\mapsto Q_1Q_2, \quad Q_1\overline{Q_2}\mapsto \overline{Q_1\overline{Q_2}}; \qquad \overline{f(\bar Q_1,\bar Q_2)}: \quad Q_1Q_2\mapsto Q_1Q_2, \quad Q_1\overline{Q_2}\mapsto Q_1\overline{Q_2}. $$

So the ring product is symmetric under both exchanges, and the sesquilinear product is conjugate-symmetric under the plain exchange and invariant under the conjugate exchange.

Proof. For the ring product, $f(Q_2,Q_1)=Q_2Q_1=Q_1Q_2$ and $\overline{f(\bar Q_1,\bar Q_2)}=\overline{\bar Q_1\bar Q_2}=\overline{\overline{Q_1Q_2}}=Q_1Q_2$. For the sesquilinear product, $f(Q_2,Q_1)=Q_2\overline{Q_1}=\overline{\overline{Q_2\bar Q_1}}=\overline{Q_1\bar Q_2}$, the conjugate of the value, so the plain exchange conjugates it; and $\overline{f(\bar Q_1,\bar Q_2)}=\overline{\bar Q_1\,\overline{\bar Q_2}}=\overline{\bar Q_1 Q_2}=Q_1\bar Q_2$, so the conjugate exchange returns it unchanged.

The invariance of $Q_1\bar Q_2$ under the conjugate exchange is the conjugate-symmetry of the sesquilinear product, $Q_1\bar Q_2=\overline{Q_2\bar Q_1}$, and it is what the sesqualgebra has in place of the symmetry of the ring. Its failure under the plain exchange is the statement that the sesquilinear product is not commutative.

The Sum and the Difference

The sum and the difference of the two values on the same pair separate the two coordinates of one factor.

Proposition (the sum and the difference). With $q_2=\operatorname{Re}Q_2$ and $q'_2=\operatorname{Im}Q_2$,

$$ Q_1Q_2 + Q_1\overline{Q_2} = 2q_2\,Q_1, \qquad Q_1Q_2 - Q_1\overline{Q_2} = 2q'_2\,jQ_1 . $$

Proof. From the coordinates, $Q_1Q_2+Q_1\overline{Q_2}=(2q_1q_2)+(2q'_1q_2)j=2q_2(q_1+jq'_1)=2q_2Q_1$ and $Q_1Q_2-Q_1\overline{Q_2}=(2q'_1q'_2)+(2q_1q'_2)j=2q'_2(q'_1+q_1j)=2q'_2j(q_1+jq'_1)=2q'_2jQ_1$.

So the two products on a fixed pair $(Q_1,Q_2)$ differ by the term $2q'_2jQ_1$, which vanishes exactly when $Q_2$ is real or $Q_1$ is zero: the two products agree on a pair exactly when the second factor has no split imaginary part. On a pair with $Q_2=j$ they differ by $2jQ_1$, and on the pair $(j,j)$ they take the two opposite real values $1$ and $-1$.

Corollary (the two products separate the coordinates of the first factor). The sum recovers the first factor whenever the second is not purely imaginary, $Q_1=(Q_1Q_2+Q_1\bar Q_2)/(2q_2)$ for $q_2\neq0$, and the difference recovers it whenever the second has a non-zero split imaginary part, $Q_1=(Q_1Q_2-Q_1\bar Q_2)/(2jq'_2)$ for $q'_2\neq0$.

Proof. The ring column is the associativity, commutativity and unit of $\mathbb{D}$ under $Q_1Q_2$, together with the multiplicativity of the bar on the ring product; the two products are both $\mathbb R$-bilinear maps into $\mathbb D$, and the matrix column is the multiplicativity of $\mathsf M_2$ read on each. The sesquilinear column is the list of failures: the bar of the product is $\bar Q_1Q_2$ and not $\bar Q_1\bar Q_2$; the plain exchange conjugates the value; the element $1$ satisfies $1\overline{Q_2}=\bar Q_2$; and the associator with $Q_1=Q_2=1$, $Q_3=j$ does not vanish, so the associativity, power-associativity, alternativity and flexibility all fail on the displayed witnesses.

The single row that holds for both is the matrix row, and it holds for the trivial reason that both products are built from the algebra operations and $\mathsf M_2$ is multiplicative: each product is the image of the corresponding matrix product, $\mathsf M_2(Q_1Q_2)=\mathsf M_2(Q_1)\mathsf M_2(Q_2)$ and $\mathsf M_2(Q_1\bar Q_2)=\mathsf M_2(Q_1)\mathsf M_2(\bar Q_2)$. The failures of the sesquilinear column are all confined to the sesquilinear shape and are owned by The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complexoid Space, below this article in this category.

The Two Slots of the Construction

Definition (the general product of two slots). On an algebra with an involution, the general product of two slots is

$$ x\star_{K}y=x\,\langle y\rangle_{\natural*}, $$

where $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is the identity or the conjugation and the first slot carries the conjugation of the coefficient ring.

Theorem (the two settings on $\mathbb{D}$). The coefficients of $\mathbb{D}$ are real, so the first slot is the identity and only the second switches, giving the two products of this article:

$$ \star_{\mathrm{id}}=Q_1Q_2, \qquad \star_{\bar{\cdot}}=Q_1\bar Q_2 . $$

The construction with both slots alive has four settings; on $\mathbb{D}$ the first slot is empty and the four collapse two-and-two onto the ring product and the sesquilinear product.

Theorem (the second slot rules the composition and the form). With $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{id}$ the product is associative, commutative and two-sidedly unital, and the form it carries is symmetric; with $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}=\bar{\cdot}$ it is non-associative, conjugate-commutative and right-unital alone, and the form it carries is conjugate-symmetric.

Proof. The two settings are $Q_1Q_2$ and $Q_1\bar Q_2$, and their laws are the two columns of the property table above: the first column is the ring, whose laws are The Specificities of the Split-Complex Algebra, and the second is the sesquilinear product of The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complexoid Space.

Remark (the empty first slot and the centre). The first slot carries the conjugation of the coefficient ring; the coefficients of $\mathbb{D}$ are real, so that conjugation is the identity and the first slot contributes nothing. What survives of it is the centre: the real scalars are the centre of the algebra, the two products are the construction read on the centre, and the collapse of the first slot is the statement that the centre is fixed by the conjugation.

The Collapse of the Four Tables

The four general products each carry a property table, and on the split-complex space the collapse happens at the level of the tables: the settings with the conjugated first slot coincide with the plain ones, so the four tables become two.

family of the four general products the table it carries on $\mathbb{D}$
the bilinear family the table of $Q_1Q_2$
the sesquilinear family the table of $Q_1\overline{Q_2}$

The collapse of the tables is the collapse of the products themselves: the table with the conjugated first slot coincides with the plain one because the first slot carries the identity, and the symmetrised and antisymmetrised products add no row because the algebra is commutative, the antisymmetrised ones vanishing and the symmetrised ones coinciding with the general. The count of the properties that hold is therefore two tables and not four, and the two that remain are the two columns of the table above.

The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints

Theorem (the two pairings). The two slots carry the two pairings of the space,

$$ \langle Q_1,Q_2\rangle=\operatorname{Sc}(Q_1Q_2), \qquad \langle Q_1,Q_2\rangle_{*}=\operatorname{Sc}(Q_1\bar Q_2)=g(Q_1,Q_2), $$

the first bilinear and symmetric, $\mathbb{D}$-valued with Gram matrix $[1]$ in the basis $1$, the second sesquilinear and conjugate-symmetric. Their real scalar parts are

$$ \operatorname{Sc}\langle Q_1,Q_2\rangle=q_1q_2+q'_1q'_2, \qquad \operatorname{Sc}\langle Q_1,Q_2\rangle_{*}=q_1q_2-q'_1q'_2=g(Q_1,Q_2), $$

and the second, the form of the Hermitian side, is of signature $(1,1)$ in the basis $\{1,j\}$. They are the forms of The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complexoid Space.

Proof. The pairings are the two products followed by the scalar part, $\operatorname{Sc}(Q_1Q_2)=q_1q_2+q'_1q'_2$ and $\operatorname{Sc}(Q_1\bar Q_2)=q_1q_2-q'_1q'_2$, the first symmetric and the second conjugate-symmetric, since the $j$-part of $Q_1\bar Q_2$ changes sign under the exchange of the two arguments. The Gram values are $\langle 1,1\rangle=\langle j,j\rangle=1$ and $\langle 1,j\rangle=0$, giving $\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$ for the scalar part of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, and $\langle 1,1\rangle_{*}=1$, $\langle j,j\rangle_{*}=-1$ and $\langle 1,j\rangle_{*}=0$, giving $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$, of signature $(1,1)$. The form theory, including the realification of the two forms, is The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complexoid Space and The Hermitian Form and the Cone of the Split-Complexoid Algebra.

Definition. For a real-linear map $F$ the transpose $F^{\mathrm T}$ is defined by $\langle FX,Y\rangle=B(X,F^{\mathrm T}Y)$, and the adjoint $F^{\dagger}$ by $\langle FX,Y\rangle_{*}=\langle X,F^{\dagger}Y\rangle_{*}$ for a $\mathbb{D}$-linear $F$ and by $\langle FX,Y\rangle_{*}=\overline{\langle X,F^{\dagger}Y\rangle_{*}}$ for a $\bar\cdot$-semilinear $F$.

Theorem (the two adjoints of the ordinary and of the sesquilinear right multiplication). For the ordinary left multiplication $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}(X)=QX$ and the sesquilinear right multiplication $R_{Q}(X)=X\bar Q$,

$$ \bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}, \qquad \bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar Q}, $$

$$ \bigl(R_{Q}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar Q}, \qquad \bigl(R_{Q}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}. $$

The transpose keeps the parameter and the adjoint conjugates it, so the transpose and the adjoint of one and the same map agree exactly when $Q$ is real.

Proof. For the first pair, $\langle QX,Y\rangle=QXY$ and $\langle X,QY\rangle=XQY$ are equal because the ring is commutative, so $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}$ is its own transpose; and $\langle QX,Y\rangle_{*}=QX\bar Y$ while $\langle X,\bar QY\rangle_{*}=X\overline{\bar QY}=XQ\bar Y$, so the adjoint of $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}$ is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar Q}$. For the second pair, $\langle X\bar Q,Y\rangle=X\bar QY$ and $\langle X,\bar QY\rangle=X\bar QY$, so the transpose of $R_{Q}$ is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar Q}$; and $\langle X\bar Q,Y\rangle_{*}=X\bar Q\bar Y$ while $\langle X,QY\rangle_{*}=X\bar Q\bar Y$, so the adjoint of $R_{Q}$ is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{Q}$. The two agree exactly at $Q=\bar Q$, that is at the real $Q$.

Remark (the conjugate-linear family). The sesqualgebra left multiplication $L_{Q}(X)=Q\bar X$ is $\bar\cdot$-semilinear and self-adjoint for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, and the two projections onto the idempotent lines are mutually adjoint, $S_{\Pi_1,\Pi_2}^{\dagger}=S_{\Pi_2,\Pi_1}$; the adjoints of the one-sided multiplications and of the sandwich are computed in The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complexoid Space.

Summary

The two products of the split-complex space determine each other through the bar: $Q_1\overline{Q_2}=\overline{\bar Q_1Q_2}$ and $Q_1Q_2=\overline{\bar Q_1\overline{Q_2}}$. The bar conjugates the ring product term by term, $\overline{Q_1Q_2}=\bar Q_1\bar Q_2$, and conjugates the sesquilinear product with the second slot undone, $\overline{Q_1\bar Q_2}=\bar Q_1Q_2$. The plain exchange of the two slots leaves the ring product unchanged and conjugates the sesquilinear one; the conjugate exchange leaves both unchanged, which is the conjugate-symmetry of the sesquilinear product. The sum and the difference of the two values are $Q_1Q_2+Q_1\bar Q_2=2(\operatorname{Re}Q_2)Q_1$ and $Q_1Q_2-Q_1\bar Q_2=2(\operatorname{Im}Q_2)\,jQ_1$, so the two products agree exactly when the second factor is real or the first is zero.

The comparison table lists the two columns: the ring product is bilinear, symmetric, associative, with a two-sided unit and a definite position in the forms; the sesquilinear product is conjugate-linear in the second slot, conjugate-symmetric, non-associative, with a right unit alone, and it is the second algebraic norm $\langle Q\rangle_{*}=Q\bar Q$. The identities that hold for the one and fail for the other are read on the witnesses $Q_1=Q_2=1$, $Q_3=j$ and $Q_1=Q_2=j$, and the only identity common to the two is the multiplicativity of the matrix model. The four tables of the four general products collapse to the two tables of these two. The two products are the two settings of the two-slot construction on a real base: the first slot is empty, the second decides the composition and the symmetry of the form, and what remains of the first slot is the centre. The two pairings carry two adjoints of a linear map, computed here: the transpose keeps the parameter and the adjoint conjugates it, so the ordinary multiplication by $Q$ has transpose itself and adjoint the multiplication by $\bar Q$, and the two agree exactly for real $Q$.

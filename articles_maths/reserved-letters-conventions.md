# __Reserved Letters Conventions__

## Introduction

This article is the tenth and last of the conventions articles of the mathematical corpus. It gathers the eight glyphs that the corpus spends once, together with the glyph that every other scalar takes, so that a reader who meets a scalar anywhere in the corpus finds the rule in one place.

The letters are fixed with the base. *Algebra and Element Conventions* states the groups, rings and operations, the base and its units, the element and its coefficients, the remarkable subspaces and the conjugations, and leaves the scalars that are not elements to this article. Nothing here is new: the article is a summary, restated where a reader can find it without searching a long article for it, and it adds no glyph.

A convention is not a description. Nothing in the algebra forces $\theta$ rather than another letter for an angle; what is not free is that one letter has one sense in one article.

## The Reserved Letters

**Eight glyphs carry eight roles, and each is spent once.** An angle is $\theta$, a scale is $\varrho$, a wave number is $\nu$, a wavelength is $\lambda$, and an eigenvalue is $\eta$. The scalar imaginary is $i$, and the material and informational sectors are the uppercase $\Phi$ and $\Psi$. None of the eight carries a second sense, and no other scalar borrows one of them. A scalar that is none of the eight takes $\digamma$ with a subscript that names its sense.

**The eight are the letters that stand alone, and $h$ is not one of them.** A letter that is never written bare, but always with a subscript that names its sense, is outside the eight and conflicts with none of them. The deformation parameter is $h_{\mathrm{def}}$ and the topological entropy is $h_{\mathrm{tpl}}$, two senses told apart by the subscript that each already carries; no third quantity takes $h$, and neither takes it bare.

**Three letters of the physical corpus are not reserved here.** This corpus excludes physics, so $c$, $\tau$ and $m$ keep the senses they have in mathematics: $c$ is a generic constant, $\tau$ a generic parameter, and $m$ a natural number and the second inertia index of $\mathrm{Cl}_{n,m}$ and $O(n,m)$.

### Angles: $\theta$

**An angle is real, and it is written $\theta$.** A rotation enters the algebra as $\exp(i\theta)$, so the $i$ belongs to the exponential and not to the angle. The glyph $\theta$ therefore carries a real number, and a subscript names which angle it is: $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ the rapidity, $\theta_{\mathrm{rat}}$ the rotation angle of an evolution. One glyph, one case, and no second letter spent on the imaginary reading.

**An imaginary angle is written $i\theta$ where it is used.** A boost is the rotation at an imaginary argument: $\exp(i\theta)$ at $\theta=i\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ is $\exp(-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}})$. The $i$ stands at the point of substitution and $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ stays real and subscripted. A purely imaginary angle is never a glyph of its own; it is $i$ times a real one, and the rotation and the boost are one formula at two values of the slot.

**The letter carries no second role.** The uses that are not angles have glyphs of their own: the involution, the automorphism and the orbit map are $\mathrm{J}$; the curvature of a connection is $\Omega$; the two-sided operator is $\mathrm{Sd}$; the congruence relation is $\vartheta_{\mathrm{cng}}$; the symplectic potential is $\varpi$; the theta function is $\vartheta_{\mathrm{thf}}$. A subscript on $\theta$ names the angle's own coordinate and does not make the letter an index of another kind: $p_\theta$ and $A_\theta$ are the momentum and the potential along the angular coordinate.

### Scales: $\varrho$

**The glyph $\varrho$ carries a scale and nothing else.** A polar decomposition writes an element as a positive real scale times a unit element, $\tilde\Xi=\varrho\,\tilde u$ with $\varrho=\lVert\tilde\Xi\rVert\ge0$ and $\lVert\tilde u\rVert=1$, and the amplitude of a wave is the same letter read as a magnitude. A subscript names which scale: $\varrho_{\mathrm{num}}$ a number density, $\varrho_{\mathrm{scl}}$ a scale factor, $\varrho_{\mathrm{ifm}}$ the coefficient of an indefinite form. The letter never carries an angle, a wave number, a wavelength or an eigenvalue.

### Wavenumbers: $\nu$

**The glyph $\nu$ carries a wave number and nothing else.** The wave number is the number of turns per unit length, $\nu=k/2\pi=1/\lambda$, and an element has three of them, $\nu_m$, $\nu_{\Phi}$ and $\nu_t^{\prime}$. The letter is never an index, because the space index is $k$; never a frequency in hertz, which is $f$ or $\omega$; and never a mass.

### Wavelengths: $\lambda$

**The glyph $\lambda$ carries a wavelength and nothing else.** It is the reciprocal of the wave number, $\lambda=1/\nu$, written $\lambda_m$, $\lambda_{\Phi}$ and $\lambda_t$ for the three of an element. The letter never carries an eigenvalue, a coupling, a scale factor or a parameter of a family; those are $\eta$, $\digamma_{\mathrm{qtc}}$, $\digamma_{\mathrm{scl}}$ and $\digamma_{\mathrm{prm}}$.

### Eigenvalues: $\eta$

**The glyph $\eta$ carries an eigenvalue and nothing else.** The spectrum is $\eta$, $\eta_\pm$, $\eta_{1,2}$, $\eta_1\dots\eta_N$, $\eta_i$ or $\eta_n$ according to how many there are, the characteristic equation is $\eta^{2}-2Q_t\eta+N=0$, and the conjugate eigenvalue is $\bar\eta$. A spectral parameter is an eigenvalue and keeps the letter. The letter is never an index, never a scale and never an indefinite form.

### Scalar Imaginary: $i$

**The glyph $i$ is the scalar imaginary and nothing else.** It is the complex unit of the complexification, $i^{2}=-1$, and it is central: it commutes with every element because it belongs to the $\mathbb{C}$ factor of the tensor product. Its one further use is a display of that centrality, $i(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=(i\tilde Q_1)\tilde Q_2$, which spends no second sense.

**The letter is never an index, because the index is the dotless $\imath$.** The space index is $\imath$ and the second one is $\jmath$, written with the dotless glyphs precisely so that a reader never has to ask whether $i$ in a subscript is the imaginary unit. A summation reads $\sum_{\imath}\varepsilon_{\imath}Q_{\imath}$. The corpus still writes $i$ as an index in some five hundred sites, and those are the sites this rule does not describe; the rule is the convention the corpus is brought to.

**The imaginary unit of a rotation sits in the exponential.** A rotation enters the algebra as $\exp(i\theta)$, so the $i$ is a factor of the exponential and the angle stays real. The complex structure is the map $J=L_{ie_t}$, written at capital size because it is a map and not a number. The split and reduced layers keep their own unit $j^{2}=+1$, and that glyph is never the scalar $i$.

### Sectors: $\Phi$ and $\Psi$

**The two uppercase Greek letters are the two sectors and nothing else.** The material sector is $\Phi$ and the informational one is $\Psi$: the algebra carries them in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, an element in $\tilde Q_{\Phi}$ and $\tilde Q_{\Psi}$, and the same two letters label the derivative and any carrier read on one sector.

**The case is the whole difference, and the lowercase is not allowed.** There is no lowercase form of a sector: $\phi$, $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are forbidden, and a display that writes one of them has spent a glyph the sectors own. The reason is the confusion, not the taste. The shape is the same and the case is the only signal, so a lowercase $\phi$ one character from $\Phi$ is read as a sector by a reader who is scanning a formula, and the same holds for $\psi$ and $\Psi$. The objects that have carried the lowercase — a potential, an angle, a map, a wavefunction, the totient — take a glyph of their own instead: the fallback $\digamma$ with a role label, or a named glyph fixed where it is introduced. A display that writes $\phi$ where $\Phi$ is meant has changed the object and lost the label; one that writes $\phi$ for a potential has crowded the sector vocabulary.

## Reserved Element Glyphs

**Three families carry the elements, and each is spent once.** The generic element is the $Q$ family, a root is $Ζ$, and the idempotent and the nilpotent are the $\Pi$ family. No letter outside the three stands for an element of the algebra: a letter that is not in them names a specific object, or an element bound where it is introduced. The three are fixed in *Algebra and Element Conventions* and in *Special Element and Peirce Conventions*, and they are gathered here so that the whole reservation is read in one place.

### Generic Elements: $Q$

**The glyph $Q$ is the generic element and nothing else.** There is one alphabet for the elements, and it is the Q family. The case separates a real element from a complex one and the accent names the layer: $q$ is real, $Q$ is complex, split-complex or dual, $\breve Q$ is reduced biquaternion, with the breve and no tilde, $\tilde q$ is quaternion and $\tilde Q$ is biquaternion. A statement that holds for every element is written $\tilde Q$; two elements of one statement are $\tilde Q_1$ and $\tilde Q_2$, and a third takes the next number.

**The coefficients carry the letter of the element.** An element is $\tilde Q=\sum_\mu Q_\mu e_\mu$, so a coefficient is $Q_\mu$ and its imaginary part $Q'_\mu$, and no second alphabet is spent on the components. The pure part carries the arrow, $\vec Q$, by the priority of the arrow over the tilde.

**The letter is never a scalar.** The scalars are the ones of §*The Reserved Letters*, and $Q$ stands among none of them. A letter outside the Q family that appears in a display is a specific object, a parameter bound at the point of use, or one of those scalars.

### Roots: $Ζ$

**The glyph $Ζ$ is a root of the algebra and nothing else.** A root of $1$ is an element $\tilde{Ζ}$ with $\tilde{Ζ}^2=1$, and a root of a central value $Q_t$ is an element with $\tilde{Ζ}^2=Q_te_t$; the square is read in a named product, the algebra product unless the statement names another, as for the nilpotents.

**The case rule splits the glyph, as it does for the elements.** The plain capital $Ζ$ is a root of the complex, split-complex or dual layer; $Ζ_\pm$ is a root of the reduced biquaternion layer; $\tilde\zeta$ is a root of a quaternion or split-quaternion layer; and $\tilde{Ζ}$ is a root of a biquaternion or split-biquaternion layer. A pure root carries the arrow and no tilde, $\vec{Ζ}$ or $\vec\zeta$.

**The root carries no tag and stands outside the potent family.** Its subscript is numeric when it counts and a role label when it names, so three roots are $\tilde{Ζ}_1, \tilde{Ζ}_2, \tilde{Ζ}_3$. The glyph never doubles as an eigenvalue: the spectrum is $\eta$.

### Potents: $\Pi$

**The glyph $\Pi$ is an idempotent or a nilpotent and nothing else.** An idempotent is $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ with $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}^2=\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$, and a nilpotent is $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ with $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}^2=0$. The kind is the first word of the subscript, so the tag is what makes the glyph a potent, and the subscript reads sector, kind, sign, index.

**The case names the layer.** The biquaternion and split-biquaternion layers carry the tilde, $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ and $\tilde\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$; the quaternion and split-quaternion layers carry the lower-case tilde, $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ and $\tilde\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$; the complex, split-complex and dual layers carry the plain capital, $\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$; and the reduced biquaternion layer writes the plain capital with the sign pair, $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}$.

**The bare glyph is forbidden, with one exception.** The glyph of the $\Pi$ shape carries nothing on its own. A potent always carries $\mathrm{id}$ or $\mathrm{nil}$ as the first word of its subscript, a projection carries its sign, $\Pi_\pm$, and the reduced idempotent pair is $\Pi_{\mathrm{id},\pm}$; a bare $\Pi$ is allowed for none of them. The one exception is the number, and the lowercase is spent on it and on nothing else: $\pi=3.14159\ldots$, the ratio of a circumference to its diameter.

**The pure nilpotent carries the arrow.** A pure nilpotent is $\vec\Pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$ or $\vec\pi_{\mathrm{nil}}$, with the arrow and no tilde. An idempotent has no pure form, and no arrowed idempotent exists.
## Summary

Eight glyphs carry eight roles, and each is spent once: $\theta$ an angle, $\varrho$ a scale, $\nu$ a wave number, $\lambda$ a wavelength, $\eta$ an eigenvalue, $i$ the scalar imaginary, $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ the two sectors. The angle is the one letter whose case needs a rule of its own, and an imaginary angle is written $i\theta$ where it is used. Every other scalar takes $\digamma$ with a subscript that names its sense.

Three families carry the elements, and each is spent once: the Q family, $q$, $Q$, $\breve Q$, $\tilde q$ and $\tilde Q$, with the case and the accent naming the layer and the coefficients carrying the same letter; the roots $Ζ$, split by the case rule; and the $\Pi$ family, with $\mathrm{id}$ or $\mathrm{nil}$ as the first word of the subscript and the case naming the layer.

# __The Two General Products and Operators__

## Introduction

The two general products of the quaternion space are defined in *The Two General Products of the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space* and compared in *Comparison Between the Two General Products*; this article reads them through the operators they define. Each product gives a **left multiplication** and a **right multiplication** of the space by a fixed element, and there are four operators in all: $L_{\tilde p}$ and $R_{\tilde p}$ for the general plain product, and their **quaternionic twins** $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$ and $R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$ for the general quaternionic product. The article gives their definitions, their **composition laws** — the plain left multiplications form a monoid and the quaternionic left multiplications compose in the reversed order, which is the **associativity defect** of the quaternionic product — the **enveloping operators** generated by the left and the right multiplications together, the **adjoints** of the four with respect to the two forms carried by the space, and the **operators the two products share**, which are their left multiplications.

The article assumes the two products and their rules from *The Two General Products of the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space*, the identities of *Relations Between the Two General Products*, and the property table of *Comparison Between the Two General Products*, all preceding this one. The two forms with respect to which the adjoints are taken are *The 2 Forms over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space*, below this article in this category, and the adjoint calculus of the plain product alone is *The Four Adjoints of the Two Algebras and the Two Sesqualgebras in Examples* and *One-Sided Operators on the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions*, in the GPA block below. The enveloping algebra of the plain product, with the sandwich family and the operator basis, is *The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the General Plain Algebra and the Bi-module Structure*, in the GPA block below.

**Conventions.** Throughout, $\tilde p=p_0e_0+\vec p$ and $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\vec q$ name elements and $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_0e_0-\vec q$ is the quaternion conjugation. The two products are written $\tilde p\tilde q$ and $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, and the operators are

$$
L_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde p\tilde q , \quad R_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde q\tilde p , \quad L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q , \quad R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p .
$$

The **plain form** is $B(\tilde x,\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x\tilde y)$ and the **quaternion form** is $N(\tilde x,\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x^{\natural}\tilde y)$; the adjoint of an operator is taken with respect to one of the two, and it is written ${}^{\dagger}$ for $B$ and ${}^{*}$ for $N$.

## The Four Operators

**Proposition (the quaternionic twins).** For every $\tilde p$,

$$
L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}=L_{\tilde p^{\natural}} , \qquad R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde p} , \qquad R_{\tilde p}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}\circ\natural ,
$$

where $\natural$ is the conjugation read as an operator, $\natural(\tilde q)=\tilde q^{\natural}$.

*Proof.* The first identity is the definition of the quaternionic product read as a left multiplication, $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=L_{\tilde p^{\natural}}(\tilde q)$. For the second, $\natural(L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}(\tilde q))=(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}=\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p=R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)$ by the exchange relation of *Relations Between the Two General Products*. For the third, $\natural(L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}(\natural(\tilde q)))=\natural(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural})=\tilde q\tilde p=R_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)$.

So the left multiplications of the quaternionic product are the left multiplications of the plain product with the parameter conjugated, and the two **families coincide as sets of operators**: $\{L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}\}_{\tilde p\in\mathbb{H}}=\{L_{\tilde p}\}_{\tilde p\in\mathbb{H}}$. The right multiplication of the quaternionic product is not a right multiplication by a fixed element, being the conjugation applied after a left multiplication; the two right families are different. The relation $R_{\tilde p}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}\circ\natural$ reads the plain right multiplication through the quaternionic left one, and with it all four operators are generated by the left multiplications of the two products and the conjugation.

## Composition Laws and the Associativity Defect

**Proposition (the composition laws).**

$$
L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde r}=L_{\tilde p\tilde r} , \qquad R_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}=R_{\tilde r\tilde p} , \qquad L_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}=R_{\tilde r}L_{\tilde p} ,
$$

and, for the quaternionic product,

$$
L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}L^{\natural}_{\tilde r}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde r\tilde p} .
$$

The plain left multiplications form a monoid isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}$ under multiplication, composition corresponding to the product of the parameters; the plain left and right multiplications commute; and the quaternionic left multiplications compose in the **reversed** order.

*Proof.* The first three identities are the associativity of the plain product: $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde r}(\tilde q)=\tilde p(\tilde r\tilde q)=(\tilde p\tilde r)\tilde q=L_{\tilde p\tilde r}(\tilde q)$, and the analogues for $R$ and for the mixed pair follow from the same associativity. The fourth is the first identity of the previous proposition applied twice, $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}L^{\natural}_{\tilde r}=L_{\tilde p^{\natural}}L_{\tilde r^{\natural}}=L_{\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde r^{\natural}}=L_{(\tilde r\tilde p)^{\natural}}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde r\tilde p}$.

**Theorem (the associativity defect).** For all $\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r$ the associator of the quaternionic product is

$$
(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}\tilde r-\tilde p^{\natural}(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde r)=L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q}(\tilde r)-L^{\natural}_{\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q}(\tilde r) =(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural})\tilde r ,
$$

and it vanishes for all $\tilde q,\tilde r$ exactly when the first factor $\tilde p$ is real.

*Proof.* The first equality is the definition of the associator with the parenthesis read through the left multiplications; the fourth identity of the previous proposition gives $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde q\tilde p}$ while the left multiplication belonging to the product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ is $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q}$, so the defect measures the failure of $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}L^{\natural}_{\tilde q}$ to be the quaternionic left multiplication of $\tilde p\star\tilde q=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, and for an associative product the two would coincide. Explicitly $L^{\natural}_{\tilde q\tilde p}=L_{(\tilde q\tilde p)^{\natural}}=L_{\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural}}$ and $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q}=L_{(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}}=L_{\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p}$, so the difference of the two operators is the left multiplication by $\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural}$, which is the displayed associator. The last factor vanishes for all $\tilde q,\tilde r$ exactly when $\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural}$ for all $\tilde q$, which, conjugating both sides, is $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=\tilde q\tilde p$ for all $\tilde q$, that is the centrality of $\tilde p^{\natural}$, hence $\tilde p$ real.

The associativity defect is thus carried by the left multiplications alone: for the plain product the map $\tilde p\mapsto L_{\tilde p}$ is multiplicative, and for the quaternionic product the map $\tilde p\mapsto L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$ is anti-multiplicative, and the two differ by the transposition of the two parameters. The defect vanishes on the real axis of the first parameter, which is the agreement of the two products of *Relations Between the Two General Products* read on the operators.

## The Enveloping Operators

The left and the right multiplications together generate an algebra of real-linear operators on $\mathbb{H}$, the **enveloping algebra** of the two products.

**Theorem (the enveloping operators).** The operators $L_{\tilde p}$ with $\tilde p\in\mathbb{H}$ span a four-dimensional real subalgebra of the algebra of real-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{H}$, isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}$; the products $L_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}$ span all of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})$, a sixteen-dimensional real algebra isomorphic to $M_4(\mathbb{R})$; and the left and the right multiplications commute, so the enveloping algebra is $\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$.

*Proof.* The left multiplications are the image of $\mathbb{H}$ under its left regular representation; the four operators $L_{e_0},L_{e_1},L_{e_2},L_{e_3}$ are linearly independent because their values on $e_0$ are the four units, and the composition law $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde r}=L_{\tilde p\tilde r}$ makes the span a subalgebra isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}$. The commutation $L_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}=R_{\tilde r}L_{\tilde p}$ is the first three composition laws together, so the map $\tilde p\otimes\tilde r\mapsto L_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}$ is a homomorphism of $\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ onto the span of the products; its kernel is zero, a standard fact for the regular bimodule of a division algebra, since $\mathbb{H}$ has no nonzero two-sided annihilator; and both spaces are sixteen-dimensional over $\mathbb{R}$. The map is therefore an isomorphism onto the span, and the span is all of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})$.

The enveloping algebra is the one owned by *The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the General Plain Algebra and the Bi-module Structure*, in the GPA block below, for the plain product; the quaternionic product adds no operator to it, its left multiplications being already among the plain ones and its right multiplications being the conjugation composed with them.

## The Adjoints With Respect to the Two Forms

The two forms of the space are $B(\tilde x,\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x\tilde y)$ and $N(\tilde x,\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x^{\natural}\tilde y)$, and each operator has an adjoint with respect to each.

**Theorem (the adjoint table).** The conjugation $\natural$ is self-adjoint with respect to both forms, and the four operators have the adjoints

| operator | adjoint for $B$ | adjoint for $N$ |
|---|---|---|
| $L_{\tilde p}$ | $R_{\tilde p}$ | $L_{\tilde p^{\natural}}$ |
| $R_{\tilde p}$ | $L_{\tilde p}$ | $R_{\tilde p^{\natural}}$ |
| $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$ | $R_{\tilde p^{\natural}}$ | $L_{\tilde p}$ |
| $R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$ | $R^{\natural}_{\tilde p^{\natural}}$ | $L_{\tilde p}\circ\natural$ |

*Proof.* The self-adjointness of $\natural$ is $B(\tilde x^{\natural},\tilde y)=B(\tilde x,\tilde y^{\natural})$ and $N(\tilde x^{\natural},\tilde y)=N(\tilde x,\tilde y^{\natural})$, both read from $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x^{\natural}\tilde y^{\natural})=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x\tilde y)$. For the four rows: $B(L_{\tilde p}\tilde x,\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde p\tilde x\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x\tilde y\tilde p)=B(\tilde x,R_{\tilde p}\tilde y)$ by the cyclicity of the scalar part; $N(L_{\tilde p}\tilde x,\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}((\tilde p\tilde x)^{\natural}\tilde y)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde x^{\natural}\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde y)=N(\tilde x,L_{\tilde p^{\natural}}\tilde y)$; and the three remaining rows follow from the twins identities and the one just proved.

So with respect to the plain form, the left and the right multiplications are adjoint to one another, $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}=R_{\tilde p}$ and $R_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde p}$, and with respect to the quaternion form the plain and the quaternionic left multiplications are adjoint to one another, $L_{\tilde p}^{*}=L_{\tilde p^{\natural}}$ and $(L^{\natural}_{\tilde p})^{*}=L_{\tilde p}$.

## The Operators the Two Products Share

**Corollary (the shared left multiplications).** The two products have the same left multiplications as families, $\{L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}\}=\{L_{\tilde p}\}$, and different right multiplications. The operators belonging to the plain product alone are therefore its right multiplications, and the operators belonging to the quaternionic product alone are its right multiplications, which are the conjugates of the shared left ones. The two products are told apart by their right multiplications and by nothing else in the operator picture.

*Proof.* The first identity of the twins proposition gives the equality of the left families; the right families differ because $R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$ conjugates the argument and no $R_{\tilde r}$ does, the four operators $R_{\tilde r}$ being $\mathbb{H}$-right-linear while $R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$ is not.

So the operator picture separates the two products by a single class of operators: the left multiplications are common, and the right multiplications — the ones that read the second slot and the order — carry the whole difference between the plain and the quaternionic rules.

## Summary

The two general products of $\mathbb{H}$ define four operators: the left and the right multiplications $L_{\tilde p},R_{\tilde p}$ of the plain product and their quaternionic twins $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p},R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$. The left multiplications of the two products coincide as families, $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}=L_{\tilde p^{\natural}}$, and the right multiplication of the quaternionic product is the conjugation of the shared left one, $R^{\natural}_{\tilde p}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}$, the plain right multiplication reading as $R_{\tilde p}=\natural\circ L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}\circ\natural$.

The composition laws are $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde r}=L_{\tilde p\tilde r}$, $R_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}=R_{\tilde r\tilde p}$ and $L_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}=R_{\tilde r}L_{\tilde p}$ for the plain product, and $L^{\natural}_{\tilde p}L^{\natural}_{\tilde r}=L^{\natural}_{\tilde r\tilde p}$ for the quaternionic one; the reversal is the associativity defect, whose value is $(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural})\tilde r$ and which vanishes exactly on the real axis of the first factor. The enveloping algebra of the left and right multiplications is $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$, generated by the sixteen products $L_{\tilde p}R_{\tilde r}$. With respect to the plain form the left and the right multiplications are adjoint to one another; with respect to the quaternion form the plain and the quaternionic left multiplications are adjoint to one another; and the conjugation is self-adjoint for both forms.

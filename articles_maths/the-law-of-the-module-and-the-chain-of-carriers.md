# __The Law of the Module and the Chain of Carriers at Every $n$__

## Introduction

**The subject.** The article states the pattern that the row of order four exemplifies: for every $n$ the algebra of $n$-by-$n$ matrices is the tensor square of the module of dimension $n$ with its dual, and it decomposes into the carriers of integer labels from $0$ to $n-1$. The article proves the dimension count, the weight count and the Casimir count, and reads the four carrier lists of order four as instances.

**The order of the article.** The first section states the module and the algebra for every $n$. The second proves the decomposition and the dimension count. The third counts the weights. The fourth states the law of the one-sided operators. The fifth reads the special case $n=4$ and draws the boundary.

**It assumes** *The Clebsch–Gordan Decomposition of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$* and *The Law of the Two-Sided Operators*. The row at order two is the corresponding law for the biquaternions.

**The result in one line.** For every $n$ the algebra $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ decomposes as $\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}V_k$ with $\dim V_k=2k+1$ and $\sum(2k+1)=n^{2}$; the module is $V_{(n-1)/2}$, of dimension $n$, and it is quaternionic when $n$ is even.

**Boundaries.** The article states the general pattern and proves it for the case at hand; the detailed articles remain the reference for $n=4$.

## The Module and the Algebra

**Proposition.** For every $n$ the group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ through the symmetric power $\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}$ of its defining representation, and the conjugation action on $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ is the tensor square of that module with its dual.

*Proof.* The symmetric power of the defining representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ has dimension $n$ and label $(n-1)/2$; the conjugation action on endomorphisms is the action on the tensor product of the module with its dual.

**Corollary.** The kernel of the action on the module is $\{\pm\mathrm I\}$ for $n\geq2$, since the label is half-integral for even $n$ and integral for odd $n$ but the symmetric power is faithful up to sign in both cases.

*Proof.* The centre of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts on the label $j$ by $(-1)^{2j}$, which is $-1$ for every half-integer and $+1$ for every integer; only the sign change survives.

**Corollary.** At $n=2$ the module is $V_{1/2}$ and at $n=4$ it is $V_{3/2}$.

*Proof.* The formula $(n-1)/2$.

**The reading.** The whole construction is generated by one integer $n$: the module, the copy, the algebra.

## The Decomposition

**Proposition.** The tensor square of the module with its dual decomposes as $\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}V_k$ with multiplicity one, and $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)=n^{2}$.

*Proof.* The Clebsch–Gordan rule for the tensor product of the label $(n-1)/2$ with itself gives the labels $0,1,\dots,n-1$, each once; the sum of the odd numbers is the square $n^{2}$.

**Corollary.** The dimension of the algebra is the sum of the dimensions of the carriers, and each carrier is irreducible and unique for its label.

*Proof.* The proposition.

**Corollary.** At $n=4$ the decomposition is $1+3+5+7=16$; at $n=2$ it is $1+3=4$; at $n=6$ it is $1+3+5+7+9+11=36$.

*Proof.* The sum.

**The reading.** The chain of carriers has $n$ terms, of dimensions the odd numbers from one to $2n-1$, and their sum is the square of the dimension of the module.

## The Count of the Weights

**Proposition.** Under the Cartan element of the copy the module has the weights $(n-1)/2,(n-3)/2,\dots,-(n-1)/2$ once each, and the algebra has the weight $m$ with multiplicity $n-|m|$ for $|m|\leq n-1$.

*Proof.* The weights of the module are the differences in steps of one; a weight of the algebra is the difference of two weights of the module, and the number of pairs with a given difference is $n-|m|$.

**Corollary.** At $n=4$ the weight multiplicities are $4$ for zero, $3$ for $\pm1$, $2$ for $\pm2$ and $1$ for $\pm3$, summing to $4+6+4+2=16$.

*Proof.* The formula.

**Corollary.** The Casimir acts on $V_k$ by $k(k+1)$, so its spectrum is $0,2,6,\dots,n(n-1)$ with multiplicities $1,3,5,\dots,2n-1$.

*Proof.* The eigenvalue on the label $k$ and the dimension of the carrier.

**The reading.** The weights and the Casimir eigenvalues both count the same dimensions, and both are determined by $n$.

## The One-Sided Operators

**Proposition.** For $A\in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ the left multiplication $L_A$ has rank $n\operatorname{rank}A$ on the $n^{2}$-dimensional algebra, and its image is the left ideal generated by $A$.

*Proof.* The map $X\mapsto AX$ has image the set of matrices whose columns lie in the image of $A$; the dimension of that set is $n$ times the dimension of the image of $A$.

**Corollary.** At $n=4$ the rank of a left multiplication is four times the rank of the matrix, and at $n=2$ it is twice the rank.

*Proof.* The formula.

**Corollary.** The two-sided operator $T_{A,B}$ has rank the product of the ranks, so its ranks at $n=4$ are the ten values from the product of two integers between zero and four.

*Proof.* The Kronecker form of the operator and the rank law.

**The reading.** The one-sided law is linear in $n$ and the two-sided law is multiplicative, and both follow from the tensor structure.

## The Case $n=4$ and the Boundary

**Proposition.** At $n=4$ the four carrier lists $7+5+3+1$, $1+15$, $1+3+3+9$ and $8+3+3+2$ are the same decomposition read through the principal copy, the block copy, the symmetric-alternating split and the real partition.

*Proof.* The first list is the labels, the second separates the scalars from the traceless part, the third groups the labels by the block structure, and the fourth by the real dimension of the Hermitian and imaginary parts.

**Corollary.** Every list sums to sixteen, and every list is a reading of the same algebraic fact.

*Proof.* The sums and the identification of the summands.

**Corollary.** The general law reduces to the biquaternion law at $n=2$, where the lists are $3+1$ and $1+3$.

*Proof.* The formula with $n=2$.

**The boundary.** The article states the pattern; the detailed proofs for each carrier at $n=4$ are in the corresponding articles, and the operator laws are in the articles on the one-sided and two-sided operators.

## The Same Count at Other Orders

**The weight count.** At order six the module has the weights $\pm\tfrac52,\pm\tfrac32,\pm\tfrac12$ and the algebra has the weight $m$ with multiplicity $6-|m|$, which gives $6+10+8+6+4+2=36$; the count is the general formula and the case of order four is its smallest even instance with a nontrivial carrier of label two.

**The Casimir count.** The Casimir eigenvalues are $k(k+1)$ for the labels zero to five, and the multiplicities are the odd numbers; the sum of the products is the square of the dimension of the module, by the same identity.

**The operator count.** The left multiplication by a matrix of rank $r$ has rank $nr$ at every order, and the two-sided operator the product of the ranks, so the two laws of the operators are independent of the order.

**The reading.** The law of the module is the single sentence that all the numbers of the row are determined by the dimension of the module, and the four readings at order four are instances of it.

## The Check of the Identity

**The sum of the odd numbers.** The identity $1+3+\cdots+(2n-1)=n^{2}$ is the dimension count of the decomposition, and it is proved by induction in one line: adding the next odd number to the square of the previous integer gives the next square.

## Summary

For every $n$ the algebra of $n$-by-$n$ matrices is the tensor square of the module of dimension $n$ with its dual, and it decomposes into the carriers of labels zero to $n-1$, of dimensions the odd numbers, summing to $n^{2}$. The weight $m$ occurs $n-|m|$ times and the Casimir eigenvalue $k(k+1)$ occurs $2k+1$ times.

The left multiplication by a matrix of rank $r$ has rank $nr$, the two-sided operator the product of the ranks, and at $n=4$ the four carrier lists are four readings of the same sixteen dimensions.

The Peirce Basis of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$

Introduction

The Peirce cut of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ gives $25$ lines and no element inside the mixed ones. A Peirce basis selects one nonzero element in each line, with the matrix-unit product rule. The diagonal elements are forced; the mixed elements are not, and their freedom is the gauge.

This article proves that the basis exists, computes the gauge, gives the coordinates, and reads the count $24=20+4$ as an orbit–stabiliser count. It assumes The Peirce Decomposition of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$.

The Definition

Definition. A Peirce basis relative to the family $P_1,\dots,P_5$ is a family $E_{kl}\in A_{kl}$ with

$$ E_{kl}E_{pq}=\delta_{lp}E_{kq},\qquad E_{kk}=P_k . $$

The diagonal is forced. Since $A_{kk}=\mathbb{C}P_k$, a nonzero diagonal element is $cP_k$; idempotence gives $c^{2}=c$, hence $c=1$. So $E_{kk}=P_k$ is a consequence of the rule, not an extra normalisation.

The mixed elements are a choice. A nonzero $E_{kl}\in A_{kl}$ can be rescaled, and the rescalings are constrained by the rule.

Existence

Theorem. Every complete family of 5 nonzero idempotents of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ admits a Peirce basis.

Proof. Choose $v_k$ spanning $V_k=\operatorname{im}P_k$ and $\ell_l$ with $\ell_l(v_k)=\delta_{kl}$, and set $E_{kl}=v_k\ell_l$. Then $E_{kl}\in A_{kl}$, $E_{kk}=v_k\ell_k=P_k$, and

$$ E_{kl}E_{pq}=v_k\ell_l(v_p)\ell_q=\delta_{lp}E_{kq}. $$

So the $25$ elements are a basis.

The basis is a pair of dual bases. The vectors $v_1,\dots,v_5$ are a basis of $\mathbb{C}^{5}$ and the forms $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_5$ are the dual basis. The Peirce basis is that pair written as matrices.

Coordinates

Every element has a unique expansion

$$ X=\sum_{k,l=1}^{5}X_{kl}E_{kl}, $$

and the numbers $X_{kl}$ are its Peirce coordinates. If $Y=\sum Y_{kl}E_{kl}$, then

$$ XY=\sum_{k,q}\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{5}X_{kl}Y_{lq}\Bigr)E_{kq}, $$

so in a Peirce basis the algebra reads as the array of coordinates with the matrix product. The array is the element, not a new object.

The Gauge

Theorem. Two Peirce bases of the same family differ by

$$ E'_{kl}=t_kt_l^{-1}E_{kl},\qquad t_1,\dots,t_5\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}. $$

Conversely, every such choice is a Peirce basis of the same family.

Proof. Each primed element is a nonzero multiple of the unprimed one, $E'_{kl}=c_{kl}E_{kl}$. The product rule gives $c_{kl}c_{lq}=c_{kq}$, so $c_{kl}=c_{k1}c_{1l}$. From $c_{1l}c_{l1}=c_{11}=1$ and $c_{11}=1$, set $t_k=c_{k1}$; then $c_{kl}=t_kt_l^{-1}$. The converse is a direct check.

The effective gauge. A common scalar acts trivially, since $(\lambda t_k)(\lambda t_l)^{-1}=t_kt_l^{-1}$. The effective group is

$$ (\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{5}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{5-1}, $$

of complex dimension $4$. The coordinates transform by

$$ X'_{kl}=t_k^{-1}t_lX_{kl}. $$

In the Hermitian case the gauge is restricted to $|t_k|=1$, of real dimension $4$.

The second root is not extra data. In the biquaternion case $n=2$ a second root is sometimes used to name the mixed units. It is one way to fix the gauge, not a parameter beyond the basis: a different second root changes $E_{kl}$ by the allowed rescaling.

The Count

The spaces. The family space is the set of ordered $5$-tuples of lines summing to $\mathbb{C}^{5}$; the gauge is the fibre of the map from bases to families; the frame space is the set of Peirce bases modulo the common scale.

The dimensions.

$$ \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\text{families})=20,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\text{gauge})=4,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\text{frames})=24 . $$

The orbit–stabiliser reading. $PGL_5(\mathbb{C})$ acts transitively on the family space, and the stabiliser of a family is the diagonal torus modulo the scalars, of dimension $4$. Hence

$$ \dim PGL_5=24=20+4 . $$

In real dimensions the count is $48=40+8$.

The projective reading. A family is an ordered $5$-tuple of points of $\mathbb{P}^{5-1}$ in general position. A Peirce basis is a lift of that tuple to a basis of $\mathbb{C}^{5}$, and the gauge is the freedom of the lift, one scale per point modulo the common scale. The family is the orbit; the gauge is the stabiliser; the basis is a point of the total space.

What the Basis Is Not

The basis is not canonical: two bases of the same family differ by the gauge. It is not a second decomposition: the lines are the cut, and the $E_{kl}$ are one element per line. It is not a list of spinors: the units are elements of the algebra, while a spinor is a column of coordinates.

Summary

A Peirce basis of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ is a choice $E_{kl}\in A_{kl}$ with $E_{kl}E_{pq}=\delta_{lp}E_{kq}$ and $E_{kk}=P_k$. It exists, and it is unique up to $E_{kl}\mapsto t_kt_l^{-1}E_{kl}$, a torus of dimension $4$. The coordinates multiply by the matrix product and transform by $X_{kl}\mapsto t_k^{-1}t_lX_{kl}$. The family has dimension $20$, the gauge $4$, the frame $24$, and the count is the orbit–stabiliser count of $PGL_5$.

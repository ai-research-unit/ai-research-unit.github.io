The One-Sided Operator Exponential of a N-Regular, Spectrally Regular Biquaternion

Introduction

This article is the analytic companion of The One-Sided Operators of a N-Regular, Spectrally Regular Biquaternion. It computes the one-parameter group generated by a one-sided operator and the exponent that one spinor slot carries. The algebra of the operator — the composition laws, the factorisation through the polar word, the eigenbasis, the block form, the image and the kernel — is in the companion; this article uses it and does not repeat it.

A one-sided operator is a representation of the algebra, so its exponential is the exponential of the element:

$$ \exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)},\qquad \exp\bigl(tR_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=R_{\exp(t\tilde Q)} . $$

That identity is the reason the one-sided reading is the natural home of the spinor exponent. A spinor is acted on by one slot; the exponent of the slot is half of the exponent of the vector action; and the group generated by $L_{\tilde Q}$ displays the half in its spectrum.

The plan is the following. §The Operator Exponential proves the identity and reads the spectrum and the rate. §The Exponential Polar Word on a Spinor proves the half-spin theorem. §The Weight Relation and the Two Copies reads the weights of $S\otimes S^{*}$. §The Exponents of the Four Factors collects the exponents in one table. The article closes with the summary, the notation and the readings.

The Operator Exponential

Theorem. For every $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$ and every $t\in\mathbb{C}$,

$$ \boxed{\;\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)},\qquad \exp\bigl(tR_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=R_{\exp(t\tilde Q)}\;} $$

Proof. The left multiplication is multiplicative, $L_{\tilde Q}^{n}=L_{\tilde Q^{n}}$, so the exponential series is

$$ \exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr) =\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}L_{\tilde Q}^{n} =\sum_{n\ge0}L_{t^{n}\tilde Q^{n}/n!} =L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)} . $$

The same computation holds on the right, because $R_{\tilde Q}^{n}=R_{\tilde Q^{n}}$; the right multiplication is an anti-representation, but a single element commutes with its own powers.

On $\Sigma_{11}$ the operator is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues $\lambda_+,\lambda_+,\lambda_-,\lambda_-$ of §The Eigenbasis and the Two Spinor Spaces of the companion. Hence $\exp(tL_{\tilde Q})$ is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues

$$ e^{t\lambda_+},\ e^{t\lambda_+},\ e^{t\lambda_-},\ e^{t\lambda_-}, \qquad \lambda_\pm=Q_t\pm iB . $$

Its determinant and trace are

$$ \det\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=\exp\bigl(4tQ_t\bigr), \qquad \operatorname{tr}\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr) =2\bigl(e^{t\lambda_+}+e^{t\lambda_-}\bigr) =4e^{tQ_t}\cos(tB). $$

The rate is the eigenvalue itself, not its square. For a central element $\tilde Q=c\,e_t$ the operator is $c\,\mathrm{id}$ and the group is $e^{tc}\,\mathrm{id}$; the two-sided companion has $e^{t\lvert c\rvert^{2}}\,\mathrm{id}$. The one-sided rate is $c$, the two-sided rate is $\lvert c\rvert^{2}$.

The Exponential Polar Word on a Spinor

Write the polar word as $$ \tilde Q=\Rho\,\tilde U_{\mathrm b}\,\tilde u_{\mathrm r}, \qquad \Rho=r\exp(i\alpha), $$ and put $$ \lambda_\pm=\Rho\,\mu_\pm,\qquad \mu_+\mu_-=1 . $$ The numbers $\mu_\pm$ are the eigenvalues of the unit factor $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$. On either spinor space the operator multiplies the first coordinate by $\lambda_+$ and the second by $\lambda_-$, so the spinor coordinates carry the pair $(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$.

For each pure factor, read with the other factors absent, the pair is elementary. The scale $r$ gives $\mu_+=\mu_-=1$. The central phase $\exp(i\alpha)$ gives $\mu_+=\mu_-=1$ and multiplies both coordinates by $\exp(i\alpha)$. The boost gives $$ \tilde U_{\mathrm b}=\exp\Bigl(i\tfrac{\psi}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}\Bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu_+=e^{-\psi/2},\qquad \mu_-=e^{\psi/2}, $$ and the rotor gives $$ \tilde u_{\mathrm r}=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}\Bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu_+=e^{i\theta/2},\qquad \mu_-=e^{-i\theta/2}. $$ Theorem (the half-spin is the one-sided operator). The one-sided operator produces the half-angle and the half-rapidity; the two-sided sandwich produces the full angle and the full rapidity. The rotor case is $$ \boxed{\; L_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,L_{\vec u_{\mathrm r}}\Bigr) \ \Longrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto(e^{i\theta/2}\alpha,\ e^{-i\theta/2}\beta), \qquad \Theta_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u_{\mathrm r}}\Bigr) \ \Longrightarrow\ \vec v\longmapsto R_{\vec u_{\mathrm r}}(\theta)\vec v \; }. $$ The exponent is $\tfrac{\theta}{2}$ in both readings. The exponent $\tfrac{\theta}{2}$ of $L_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}$ multiplies the two spinor coordinates by $e^{i\theta/2}$ and $e^{-i\theta/2}$, hence the output is the half-angle; the same $\tfrac{\theta}{2}$ in $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u}(X)=[\vec u,X]$ gives the rotation of angle $\theta$, because $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u}=2\,[\vec u\times]$ and the factor $2$ doubles it. The boost is the same: $$ \boxed{\; L_{\tilde U_{\mathrm b}}=\exp\Bigl(i\tfrac{\psi}{2}\,L_{\vec u_{\mathrm b}}\Bigr) \ \Longrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto(e^{-\psi/2}\alpha,\ e^{\psi/2}\beta), \qquad \Theta_{\tilde U_{\mathrm b}}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{rapidity }\psi \; }. $$ A spinor transforms by half of what a vector transforms by, and the operator that produces the half is the one-sided one. This is the precise sense in which the spin representation of the polar word is the one-sided operator, and the vector representation is the two-sided one. The spectral form of the same statement is in §The Spectrum of the Sandwich of The Two-Sided Operator of a N-Regular, Spectrally Regular Biquaternion and §The Pure Factors and the Full Spin of The Two-Sided Operator Exponential of a N-Regular, Spectrally Regular Biquaternion.

The following table collects the pure factors. The last column is the nontrivial factor in the spectrum of the two-sided operator, that is, in the products $\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j$.

factor $\mu_+,\mu_-$ spinor matrix $\Delta$ exponent per coordinate nontrivial two-sided factor
scale $r$ $1,1$ $rI_2$ $\ln r$ $r^{2}$
phase $\exp(i\alpha)$ $1,1$ $\exp(i\alpha)I_2$ $i\alpha$ $1$
boost $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}$ $e^{-\psi/2},e^{\psi/2}$ $\operatorname{diag}(e^{-\psi/2},e^{\psi/2})$ $\mp\psi/2$ $e^{\pm\psi}$
rotor $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ $e^{i\theta/2},e^{-i\theta/2}$ $\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta/2},e^{-i\theta/2})$ $\pm i\theta/2$ $e^{\pm i\theta}$

The scale and the phase are central. Their rows are read as scalar multipliers of a word with $B\neq0$; for a purely central element $B=0$, every basis is an eigenbasis, and the single eigenvalue is $Q_t$.

For a general word the two factors do not commute, and $\mu_\pm$ are not separately exponentials of half the boost and half the rotor angle. The spinor matrix is still $\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$; the half-angle formulas are the statement for the pure factors, and for commuting axes. The general relation between the spectral data and the polar angles is Relations between Exponential Polar Decomposition and Eigenbasis of a N-Regular, Spectrally Regular Biquaternion, §The Spectral Data from the Polar Factors.

The Weight Relation and the Two Copies

The algebra is the tensor product of a spinor space and its dual, $$ \mathbb{B}\cong S\otimes S^{*}, $$ where $S=\mathbb{C}^{2}$ is the spinor space and $S^{*}$ its dual, and $S_+\cong S_-\cong S$ as left modules. Left multiplication acts on the first factor, right multiplication on the second: $$ L_{\tilde Q}=\rho(\tilde Q)\otimes 1, \qquad R_{\tilde Q}=1\otimes\rho(\tilde Q)^{T}, \qquad R_{\tilde Q^{*}}=1\otimes\bar\rho(\tilde Q), $$ where $\rho$ is the defining representation on $S$ and $\bar\rho$ its conjugate. The two factors commute, so the exponents add: $$ \exp\bigl(tL_A\bigr)\exp\bigl(sR_B\bigr)=\exp\bigl(tL_A+sR_B\bigr). $$

For the rotor, $\rho(\tilde u_{\mathrm r})$ has weights $e^{\pm i\theta/2}$ and $\bar\rho(\tilde u_{\mathrm r})$ has weights $e^{\mp i\theta/2}$. The tensor weights, in additive notation, are the four sums $$ \tfrac{\theta}{2}-\tfrac{\theta}{2}=0,\qquad \tfrac{\theta}{2}+\tfrac{\theta}{2}=\theta,\qquad -\tfrac{\theta}{2}-\tfrac{\theta}{2}=-\theta,\qquad -\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\tfrac{\theta}{2}=0, $$ that is $(\theta,0,0,-\theta)$. For the boost, both slots carry $e^{\mp\psi/2}$ because the boost is Hermitian, and the sums are $(\psi,0,0,-\psi)$. These are the weights of the vector. The invariant statement is that the weights of $S\otimes S^{*}$ are the pairwise sums of the weights of $S$ and $S^{*}$; the factor two sits in the pairing, not in a rescaling of the angle.

"Half" is relative. The angle $\theta$ is defined by the vector action, as the rotation of $\mathbb{R}^{3}$. The spinor weight is $\theta/2$ because the rotor is $\exp(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r})$. A different normalisation of the angle would change the number and not the relation.

The whole operator is two copies. On $\mathbb{B}=S_+\oplus S_-$ the two summands are isomorphic as left modules, so $$ \operatorname{spec}L_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}=\{e^{i\theta/2},e^{i\theta/2},e^{-i\theta/2},e^{-i\theta/2}\}, $$ each weight twice. The one-sided operator is $2\rho$ as a left module: two copies of the Weyl spinor, not the Dirac spinor $\rho\oplus\bar\rho$. One Peirce column is one Weyl spinor; the whole of $L$ is two of them. The precise phrase is therefore: the one-sided operator, restricted to one Peirce component, is the half-spin.

The two Peirce chiralities are not the two Lorentz chiralities. Both $S_+$ and $S_-$ carry the same representation $\rho$; they differ by the Peirce idempotent, not by the Lorentz chirality. The two Lorentz chiralities are the two tensor factors $S$ and $S^{*}$, that is the left and right slots.

Factor by factor. For a general word the factors do not commute and $\lambda_\pm$ are not $e^{\pm i\theta/2}$ or $e^{\mp\psi/2}$. There is no single half-angle of a general polar word; the additive relation is the statement for the pure factors and for commuting axes.

The whole of $L$ is twisted. $L_{\tilde Q}$ also carries the scale $r$ and the phase $e^{i\alpha}$. The half-spin statement concerns the norm-one, phase-one part $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$; the full operator is the twisted spinor representation of the module.

The double cover. Replacing $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ by $-\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ leaves the sandwich unchanged and changes $L$ by $-1$. On the norm-one slice the sandwich determines the element only up to sign; $L$ distinguishes the sign. The half-angle is therefore defined only after the lift to the double cover is chosen, and $L$ is the operator that carries the choice.

Corollary (the one-sided operator is the spinor action, hence the half-spin). The operator $L_{\tilde Q}$ is the action of $\tilde Q$ on a single spinor. It preserves chirality, acts by the same $\Delta$ on $S_+$ and $S_-$, and its rotor and boost exponents are half those of the two-sided sandwich.

The Exponents of the Four Factors

The following table collects the exponents. The exponent is the element $A$ such that the factor is $\exp(A)$; the scalar parameter is the real or imaginary coefficient inside that exponential, and the two-sided output is the parameter of the corresponding transformation in the sandwich.

factor exponent $A$ scalar parameter two-sided output
scale $r$ $\ln r$ $\ln r$ $2\ln r$
phase $\exp(i\alpha)$ $i\alpha$ $\alpha$ $0$
boost $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}$ $i\tfrac{\psi}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ $\tfrac{\psi}{2}$ $\psi$
rotor $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ $\tfrac{\theta}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ $\tfrac{\theta}{2}$ $\theta$

The rule is the number of slots. A one-sided operator is one factor of the word, so its exponent is the exponent of that factor: the scale keeps $\ln r$, the phase keeps $i\alpha$, the boost keeps the half-rapidity in $i\psi/2\,\vec u_{\mathrm b}$, the rotor keeps the half-angle in $\theta/2\,\vec u_{\mathrm r}$. A two-sided operator uses two slots, the parameter and its Hermitian conjugate. For the Hermitian factors the two exponents are equal and add, giving $2\ln r$ for the scale and $\psi$ for the boost; for the unitary rotor they are opposite and the conjugation gives the angle $\theta$; for the central imaginary phase they cancel to $0$. The doubling of the companion article is therefore a two-slot phenomenon for the scale, the boost and the rotor, and the phase is its exceptional case.

Summary

The one-sided exponential is the element exponential in the regular representation:

$$ \exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)}, \qquad \exp\bigl(tR_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=R_{\exp(t\tilde Q)} . $$

On $\Sigma_{11}$ it is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues $e^{t\lambda_\pm}$, each twice, where $\lambda_\pm=Q_t\pm iB$; its determinant is $\exp(4tQ_t)$ and its trace is $4e^{tQ_t}\cos(tB)$. The one-sided rate is the eigenvalue $\lambda_\pm$, not its square.

The half-spin is the one-sided operator. On one Peirce column the operator is the Weyl spinor $\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$; the rotor carries $e^{\pm i\theta/2}$ and the boost $e^{\mp\psi/2}$, so the slot carries the half-angle and the half-rapidity. The whole of $L$ is two copies of that spinor, one per Peirce column, and the two Lorentz chiralities are the two tensor slots of $\mathbb{B}\cong S\otimes S^{*}$, not the two Peirce columns.

The weights add on the tensor product. For the rotor they are $(\theta,0,0,-\theta)$ and for the boost $(\psi,0,0,-\psi)$; the factor two sits in the pairing of the two slots, not in a rescaling of the angle. The one-sided operator sees one slot and does not double.

The four factors give the table of exponents. The scale keeps $\ln r$, the phase keeps $i\alpha$, the boost keeps $i\tfrac{\psi}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ and the rotor keeps $\tfrac{\theta}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$. The two-sided output is $2\ln r$, $0$, $\psi$ and $\theta$; the doubling and the cancellation are the subject of The Two-Sided Operator Exponential of a N-Regular, Spectrally Regular Biquaternion.

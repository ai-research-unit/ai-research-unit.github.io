# __Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra__

## Introduction

A root of a central value is defined by an equation in which the product occurs, so it belongs to
the product (*Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures*, §*Powers, the Square and
the Roots*). The block of this article is the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication
$$
\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde Q-\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde\Xi\bigr)
=\Xi_0\vec Q-Q_0\vec\Xi-\vec\Xi\times\vec Q ,
$$
which is alternating and without a unit (*Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of
Biquaternions*).

The article treats two subjects, and in this order. The first is the square roots of the central
value $+1$, the elements $\tilde\Xi\in\mathbb{B}$ with
$$
\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=+e_0 .
$$
The second is the idempotents of the block, the elements $\tilde\Pi$ with
$\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$. The two subjects are one subject, because the idempotent is
the root of $+1$ in a scaled sense: in a unital product the **halving map**
$\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ carries the roots of $+1$ onto the idempotents, with
inverse $\tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$. For the block the map is degenerate, and its degeneration and
the absence of the roots have one cause, the vanishing square: the block is alternating, so
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$ for every element, and neither the scaled equation nor the root
equation can be met off the origin.

The outcome is the extreme absence, and it is the sharpest form of the absence law. The block has
**no root of $+1$**, because its square is zero for every element; and it has **one idempotent,
the origin**, because its idempotent equation reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$. It therefore has no
nontrivial idempotent, no orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent, no projection and no Peirce
decomposition, and the unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block. The
roots of $-1$ are settled by the same reduction and are recorded in a short remark at the end of
§*The Roots of One*; the remark is used nowhere.

The article is algebraic. It does not treat the roots of $0$ and the nilpotents, which are *Square
Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*; the square roots of a
general right side, which is *Square Roots of a General Element in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic
Algebra*; the zero-divisor set and the annihilators, which are *Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric
Quaternionic Algebra* and *Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*; the element
$e_0$ as an operator, which is *The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*; or
the idempotent families of the plain product, which are *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of
the General Plain Algebra*.

**The results.** For the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde Q=\Xi_0\vec Q-Q_0\vec\Xi-\vec\Xi\times\vec Q$ the square of
every element vanishes, $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$, and the root equation of $+1$ therefore
reduces to $0=+e_0$: the block has **no root of $+1$**. The idempotent equation
$\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$, so the block has **one idempotent,
the origin**, and **no nontrivial idempotent**, hence no orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent,
no projection and no Peirce decomposition; the unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an
idempotent of the block, although its left multiplication is the projector onto the vector subspace,
so the element and its operator are distinct. The comparison is the comparison of the origin alone
with the plain product's family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$.

## The Notion and Its Product

**Definition (root of a central value).** Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$ and let
$\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$. A **root of $\lambda$ in $\star$** is a nonzero element $\tilde\Xi$ with
$$
\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0 .
$$
The three central values the biquaternion algebra singles out are $-1$, $+1$ and $0$: the roots of
$-1$ are the imaginary units, the roots of $+1$ are the involutions, and the roots of $0$ are the
nonzero nilpotents of the product (*Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures*,
§*Powers, the Square and the Roots*).

**Definition (idempotent of a product).** Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element
$\tilde\Pi$ is an **idempotent of $\star$** when
$$
\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi .
$$
The origin and, when the product is unital, the unit are idempotents of every product; they are the
**trivial idempotents**, and the others are **nontrivial** (*Definitions for the Study of the 12
Algebraic Structures*, §*The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition*).

Throughout this article the product is the **antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication** $\diamond$,
and unqualified, *root* means a root of the block and *idempotent* means an idempotent of the block.
The idempotent is the root of $+1$ in the scaled sense of the definitions article, and for the block
both equations are read through the one vanishing square. The square of the block is the
specialisation to the block of the general square of *The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an
Algebra Product*.

## The Roots of One

**The block has no root of $+1$: the equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=+e_0$ has no solution.**

**Proposition (the reduction).** For every element $\tilde\Xi$,
$$
\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0 ,
$$
and consequently the root equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=+e_0$ is equivalent to the scalar
equation $0=+e_0$.

*Proof.* The block is alternating, $\tilde X\diamond\tilde X=0$ for every $\tilde X$, by
*Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Biquaternions*; read at
$\tilde X=\tilde\Xi$ this is the display. Substituting the display into the root equation gives the
scalar equation.

**Theorem (no roots of $+1$).** The block has no root of $+1$; equivalently, the equation
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=+e_0$ has no solution.

*Proof.* The reduction gives $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$, and $0\neq+e_0$ because $e_0$ is
nonzero, the scalars of the field being of characteristic zero. Hence the equation fails for every
$\tilde\Xi$.

The roots of $+1$ are the involutions of the product, and the block has none. In particular the
element $e_0$ is not a root of $+1$ of the block, although it is the unit of the ambient algebra and
a root of $+1$ of the plain product; the equation is read in the product and not in the algebra, and
the product is not the plain one.

The absence is the absence of a square that is central and nonzero. The block removes the scalar
part of the parent quaternionic product, whose square is the central value
$\tilde\Xi^{\natural}\tilde\Xi=N(\tilde\Xi)e_0$ with $N(\tilde\Xi)=\sum_\mu\Xi_\mu^{2}$ the
algebraic norm; the parent's root condition is the level set $N(\tilde\Xi)=+1$, and with the scalar
part removed the level set is replaced by the empty set.

**The roots of minus one.** The same reduction settles the central value $-1$, and the remark is
recorded here and used nowhere. For a product that is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear in both slots the root
set of $-1$ is read off the root set of $+1$ by one map: if $\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=e_0$ then
$(i\tilde\Xi)\star(i\tilde\Xi)=i^{2}(\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi)=-\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=-e_0$, the
scalar $i$ passing through both slots. The block is bilinear in both slots, the mechanism applies,
and the two signed sets vanish together: the block has **no root of $-1$** either, and the equation
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=-e_0$ reduces to $0=-e_0$, which fails for every element. The mechanism
is not universal — a product sesquilinear in a slot carries the scalar through the conjugation there
and does not reverse the sign — and the root set of $-1$ of the block is empty in every case.

## The Idempotents

**The idempotent equation of the block is satisfied by the origin alone, $\{\tilde\Pi:\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\}=\{0\}$; the block has no nontrivial idempotent.**

The idempotent rests on the roots of $+1$ and on nothing else, through the **halving map**. In a
unital product the map $\tilde\Xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ carries the roots of $+1$ onto the
idempotents, because $\tilde\Xi^{2}=e_0$ exactly when $\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tilde\Pi$; its inverse is
$\tilde\Xi=2\tilde\Pi-e_0$, and the complementary pair $\tilde\Pi$, $e_0-\tilde\Pi$ corresponds to
the pair $\tilde\Xi$, $-\tilde\Xi$. The equivalence is the expansion
$\tilde\Pi^{2}=\tfrac14(e_0+2\tilde\Xi+\tilde\Xi^{2})$, and it needs a two-sided unit of the
product.

For the block the map has no content: its domain is the empty root set of §*The Roots of One*, and
the origin, the single idempotent, is not the image of a root, since $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)=0$
would give $\tilde\Xi=-e_0$, which is not a root of $+1$ of the block. The two subjects of this
article are nevertheless one, because the root equation and the idempotent equation are the same
vanishing square read twice, $0=+e_0$ against $0=\tilde\Pi$. It is the idempotent equation that is
read directly.

### The Criterion

**Theorem (the idempotent set).** The idempotent equation of the block is satisfied by the origin
alone,
$$
\{\tilde\Pi:\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\}=\{0\}.
$$

*Proof.* The block is alternating, $\tilde X\diamond\tilde X=0$ for every element $\tilde X$, by
*Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Biquaternions*; at $\tilde X=\tilde\Pi$
this gives $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=0$. The idempotent equation becomes $0=\tilde\Pi$, whose only
solution is the origin. The origin is an idempotent indeed, $0\diamond0=0$.

The criterion is the whole of the computation, because the left side of the equation is identically
zero and the equation constrains the right side alone. The criterion is the scaled root equation of
§*The Roots of One*, read with the right side $\tilde\Pi$ in place of $e_0$: the root equation fails
because $0\neq e_0$, while the idempotent equation succeeds at the single element $\tilde\Pi=0$
because the right side is then zero as well.

**Corollary (no nontrivial idempotent and no projection).** The block has no nontrivial idempotent
and no projection. In particular the unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the
block:
$$
e_0\diamond e_0=P_0\vec Q-Q_0\vec P-\vec P\times\vec Q=0\neq e_0
\qquad(\tilde P=\tilde Q=e_0,\ P_0=Q_0=1,\ \vec P=\vec Q=0).
$$

*Proof.* A nontrivial idempotent is one different from the origin and, when the product is unital,
from the unit; the theorem says that the origin is the only idempotent, so there is no nontrivial
one. The element $e_0$ satisfies $e_0\diamond e_0=0$ and $0\neq e_0$, so it is not an idempotent of
the block, although it is the left unit of the parent quaternionic product and the two-sided unit of
the plain product. A
projection is an idempotent with the additional Hermitian condition $\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi$; the
origin meets it, and no other element is an idempotent.

### The Absence of the Peirce Decomposition

**The block has no Peirce decomposition, its three ingredients — a unit, a nontrivial idempotent and associativity — being all absent.**

**Proposition (no Peirce decomposition).** The block has no Peirce decomposition relative to a
nontrivial idempotent, because it has no unit, no nontrivial idempotent and no associativity.

*Proof.* The Peirce decomposition is defined relative to a nontrivial idempotent of an associative
unital product (*Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures*, §*The Idempotent, the
Projection and the Peirce Decomposition*); the theorem gives the idempotent set $\{0\}$, so no
nontrivial idempotent is available, and the block has neither a unit nor an associativity to add.
The three ingredients are all absent.

The absence is not peculiar to the block. Of the twelve structures the Peirce decomposition belongs
to the products that carry it, the general plain product among them, where it is the decomposition
of *Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Algebra*; the block has no nontrivial
idempotent to index it and no associativity or unit to build it from. The block's own projector
theory is short and exact — the zero operator, the projector $L_{e_0}$ onto the vector subspace,
one splitting and one left ideal, and no Peirce calculus — and it is *The Specificities of the
Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra* that states it.

### The Element $e_0$ and Its Operator

**The element $e_0$ is not an idempotent, but its left multiplication is a projector, the projection onto the vector subspace; the element and its operator are two different objects.**

**Remark (an element that is not idempotent but has a projector).** The element $e_0$ is not an
idempotent of the block, since $e_0\diamond e_0=0$; it is nevertheless the element whose left
multiplication is a projector, because
$$
L_{e_0}\tilde X=e_0\diamond\tilde X=\vec X , \qquad L_{e_0}^{2}=L_{e_0} ,
$$
the projection onto the vector subspace along the centre. The element and its operator are two
different objects, and the block separates them: the element $e_0$ is not idempotent, while the
operator $L_{e_0}$ is idempotent. The exclusion of $e_0$ from the idempotent set is therefore not
the exclusion of its operator from the projectors. The exact projector class of the block, the
identity $L_{e_0}=P_{\mathrm{Vect}}$ and the comparison with the other eleven structures are *The
Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*; what is recorded here is that the
idempotent equation of the block and the projector equation of its operators are not the same
equation.

### The Comparison with the Neighbouring Products

**The block keeps the origin and loses the idempotent family of the plain product, $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$; the parent quaternionic product, whose square is central, carries the trivial pair alone.**

**Remark (the neighbouring idempotent sets).** The general plain product is associative and unital,
and its idempotents are the elements $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $+1$ of
that product, a family containing the trivial pair $0$ and $e_0$, the images of the trivial roots
$\tilde\Xi=\pm e_0$; it is the family that carries the left ideals and the direct sum
decompositions of that product (*Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Plain
Algebra*), with the Peirce decomposition written out in *Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the
General Plain Algebra*. The parent quaternionic product is not unital, its unit being a left unit
alone, and its square is the central value $\tilde\Pi^{\natural}\tilde\Pi=N(\tilde\Pi)e_0$; its
idempotent equation therefore forces $\tilde\Pi$ to be central with $N(\tilde\Pi)=\tilde\Pi$, so its
idempotent set is the trivial pair $\{0,e_0\}$ and carries no family (*Square Roots of One and the
Idempotents of the General Quaternionic Algebra*). The block, with the scalar part of the parent
removed, keeps the origin alone: it loses the parent's left unit and the plain product's family, and
with them the entire idempotent calculus, the left ideals, the direct sum decompositions and the
Peirce decomposition belonging to the other products and not to it. The idempotent sets of the four
general products are read side by side in *Idempotents in the 12 Algebraic Structures over the
Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*.

## Summary

For the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde Q=\Xi_0\vec Q-Q_0\vec\Xi-\vec\Xi\times\vec Q$ the square of every
element vanishes, $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$, because the block is alternating; the root equation
of $+1$ therefore reduces to the scalar equation $0=+e_0$ and has no solution. The roots of $-1$
fall to the same reduction, $0=-e_0$, and are the short remark closing the roots-of-one part; the
block is bilinear in both slots, so the root set of $-1$ is read off the empty root set of $+1$ by
one multiplication by the central imaginary, and it is empty. The idempotent equation
$\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$, so the block has one idempotent, the
origin, and no nontrivial idempotent. The unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of
the block, the block being a bracket and not a product; its left multiplication is nevertheless the
projector onto the vector subspace, so the element and its operator are distinct objects. No
orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent, no projection and no Peirce decomposition exists. The
idempotent architecture rests on the roots of $+1$ through the halving map, whose source is empty
here; the comparison with the neighbouring products is the comparison of the origin alone with the
plain product's family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi)$ over the roots of $+1$ and with the parent
quaternionic product's trivial pair.

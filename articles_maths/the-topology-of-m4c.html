The Topology of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$

Introduction

The algebra $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{32}$ and contractible; its group of units $GL_4(\mathbb{C})$ is homotopy equivalent to $U(4)$, with $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}$ and $\pi_2=0$. The idempotents of rank $p$ are homotopy equivalent to the Grassmannian $\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$; the full flag variety $GL_4/B$ is simply connected; the determinant bundle has first Chern class the generator of $H^2$, and the determinant bundles over the Grassmannians have first Chern class the hyperplane class.

The article computes the homotopy type of the algebra and its group of units, the homotopy groups, the topology of the idempotents and the Grassmannians, the cohomology of the flag variety, and the determinant bundles and their first Chern classes.

The geometry of the idempotents is The Geometry of the Idempotents; the polar decomposition is The Polar Decomposition; the exponential and the Lie group are The Exponential and the Lie Group; the adjoint action and the flag variety are The Adjoint Action, the Orbits and the Flag Variety.

The Contractible Algebra

Theorem. The algebra $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{32}$ and is contractible. Its cohomology is

$$ H^0(M_4;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z},\qquad H^k(M_4;\mathbb{Z})=0\quad(k>0), $$

and all homotopy groups vanish.

Proof. The sixteen complex entries give a linear isomorphism $M_4\cong\mathbb{C}^{16}\cong\mathbb{R}^{32}$. A vector space is contractible by the straight-line homotopy $H_t(A)=(1-t)A$ to $0$. A contractible space has the cohomology and homotopy groups of a point.

The singular set. The singular set

$$ \Sigma=\{A:\det A=0\} $$

is a real cone with apex $0$: if $A\in\Sigma$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$, then $\lambda A\in\Sigma$. A cone is contractible by the homotopy $H_t(A)=(1-t)A$ to the apex, so

$$ \Sigma\simeq\text{point}. $$

Its link

$$ L=\Sigma\cap S^{31} $$

is a real algebraic hypersurface of dimension $29$ in the unit sphere $S^{31}\subset\mathbb{R}^{32}$, singular along the rank-at-most-two locus; it is the interesting part of the singular set.

The complement. The complement of the singular set is the general linear group:

$$ M_4\smallsetminus\Sigma=GL_4(\mathbb{C}), $$

an open subset of real dimension $32$. It is not contractible; its homotopy type is that of $U(4)$.

The Homotopy Type of $GL_4$

Theorem. The inclusion $U(4)\hookrightarrow GL_4(\mathbb{C})$ is a homotopy equivalence:

$$ GL_4(\mathbb{C})\simeq U(4). $$

Proof. The polar decomposition $A=UP$ gives a deformation retraction

$$ H_t(A)=U P^{1-t},\qquad t\in[0,1], $$

from $GL_4$ to $U(4)$: at $t=0$, $H_0(A)=UP=A$; at $t=1$, $H_1(A)=U$. The path stays in $GL_4$ because $P^{1-t}$ is positive definite. The map $r(A)=U$ is continuous and satisfies $r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_{U(4)}$ and $i\circ r\simeq\operatorname{id}_{GL_4}$.

The quotient. The homogeneous space

$$ GL_4(\mathbb{C})/U(4)\cong P_+ $$

is the contractible cone of positive definite Hermitian matrices. The fibration

$$ U(4)\longrightarrow GL_4\longrightarrow P_+ $$

has contractible base, which again gives the homotopy equivalence.

The maximal compact. The $*$-unitary group $U(4)$ is a maximal compact subgroup of $GL_4(\mathbb{C})$. Every compact subgroup is conjugate into $U(4)$; the polar decomposition shows that $U(4)$ meets every component of $GL_4$ and is a deformation retract.

The dimensions. The real dimensions are

$$ \dim_\mathbb{R}GL_4=32,\qquad \dim_\mathbb{R}U(4)=16,\qquad \dim_\mathbb{R}P_+=16. $$

The homotopy equivalence forgets the sixteen contractible directions of the positive factor.

The Homotopy Groups

The fundamental group. Because $GL_4\simeq U(4)$,

$$ \pi_1(GL_4(\mathbb{C}))\cong\pi_1(U(4))\cong\mathbb{Z}. $$

The generator is the loop

$$ \gamma(t)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{2\pi i t},1,1,1),\qquad t\in[0,1]. $$

The scalar loop $e^{2\pi i t}I$ is four times the generator; its determinant is $e^{8\pi i t}$, of winding four.

The determinant map. The determinant

$$ \det:U(4)\longrightarrow U(1) $$

induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$. The section $z\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(z,1,1,1)$ shows that $\det_*$ is surjective; the kernel $SU(4)$ is simply connected, so $\det_*$ is injective. Hence

$$ \pi_1(U(4))\cong\pi_1(U(1))=\mathbb{Z}, $$

and the determinant bundle is the pullback of the generator.

The second homotopy group. Every Lie group has

$$ \pi_2(G)=0 $$

(Cartan's theorem; see Helgason, Differential Geometry, Lie Groups, and Symmetric Spaces, Ch. II). Hence

$$ \pi_2(GL_4)=0,\qquad \pi_2(U(4))=0. $$

The higher groups. The higher homotopy groups of $GL_4$ are those of $U(4)$. In low degrees,

$$ \pi_3(U(4))=\mathbb{Z}, $$

generated by the inclusion $SU(2)\hookrightarrow U(4)$ in a $2\times2$ block. The low-degree values $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_2=0$, $\pi_3=\mathbb{Z}$ are the stable values of $U$; the unstable groups do not enter this article.

The universal cover. The universal cover of $U(4)$ is

$$ \widetilde{U(4)}\cong SU(4)\times\mathbb{R}, $$

because $U(4)\cong(SU(4)\times U(1))/\mathbb{Z}_4$ and $SU(4)$ is simply connected. The universal cover of $GL_4$ is a complex Lie group with the same homotopy type; the extra directions are the contractible positive factor.

The Idempotents and the Grassmannians

The homotopy equivalence. For $0\le p\le4$, the idempotents of rank $p$ form the affine bundle

$$ \mathcal{I}_p\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gr}(p,4) $$

with contractible fibre the affine space of complements. Hence

$$ \mathcal{I}_p\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(p,4). $$

The orthogonal projection onto the image $p$-plane is a section, the zero section of the affine bundle, so the inclusion $\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{I}_p$ is a homotopy equivalence.

The Grassmannian topology. The Grassmannian $\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$ is a compact complex manifold of dimension $p(4-p)$, simply connected, with a Schubert cell decomposition indexed by the partitions in a $p\times(4-p)$ box. Its Poincaré polynomial is the Gaussian binomial

$$ P_{\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)}(q)=\binom{4}{p}_q, $$

and its Euler characteristic is

$$ \chi(\operatorname{Gr}(p,4))=\binom{4}{p}. $$

The idempotent topology. Because $\mathcal{I}_p$ is homotopy equivalent to $\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$,

$$ \pi_1(\mathcal{I}_p)=0,\qquad H^*(\mathcal{I}_p;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^*(\operatorname{Gr}(p,4);\mathbb{Z}), \qquad \chi(\mathcal{I}_p)=\binom{4}{p}. $$

The rank-one idempotents are homotopy equivalent to $\mathbb{P}^3$; the rank-two idempotents are homotopy equivalent to $\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)$; the rank-three idempotents are homotopy equivalent to $\mathbb{P}^3$. For $1\le p\le3$ the idempotent stratum itself is a noncompact affine bundle, but its homotopy type is compact.

The rank stratification. The rank is locally constant on the idempotent variety, since $\operatorname{rank}P=\operatorname{tr}P$ is continuous and integer-valued. Hence each stratum is open and closed, with

$$ \overline{\mathcal{I}_p}=\mathcal{I}_p, $$

and the space of all idempotents is the disjoint union of the five clopen strata, with the Grassmannian homotopy types on the strata. The zero and identity strata are points; the rank-one and rank-three strata are $\mathbb{P}^3$ up to homotopy; the rank-two stratum is $\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)$ up to homotopy.

The Flag Variety

The full flag variety. The full flag variety

$$ \mathcal{F}=GL_4/B $$

is a compact complex manifold of dimension $6$, simply connected, with the Bruhat cell decomposition

$$ \mathcal{F}=\bigsqcup_{w\in S_4}BwB/B. $$

The cells are indexed by the permutations of four elements, and the Poincaré polynomial is

$$ P_{\mathcal{F}}(q)=[4]_q! =(1+q)(1+q+q^2)(1+q+q^2+q^3) =1+3q+5q^2+6q^3+5q^4+3q^5+q^6. $$

The Euler characteristic is

$$ \chi(\mathcal{F})=4!=24. $$

The Betti numbers. They are

$$ 1,\ 3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 5,\ 3,\ 1 $$

in degrees $0,\dots,6$. The middle Betti number is $6$, the number of positive roots of $\mathfrak{sl}_4$.

Simple connectivity. The Bruhat decomposition has a unique open cell of dimension $6$ and a unique closed cell of dimension $0$; the $2$-skeleton is connected and simply connected, and the higher cells do not change $\pi_1$. Hence

$$ \pi_1(\mathcal{F})=0. $$

The same conclusion follows from the quotient $\mathcal{F}\cong SU(4)/T'$: $SU(4)$ is simply connected, and the torus $T'$ is connected, so the quotient is simply connected.

The line bundles. The line bundles on $\mathcal{F}$ are the homogeneous bundles $L_\lambda$ associated to the characters $\lambda$ of the Borel $B$. The first Chern class is

$$ c_1(L_\lambda)=\lambda\in H^2(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{Z}). $$

The canonical bundle is

$$ K_{\mathcal{F}}=L_{-2\rho}, \qquad c_1(K_{\mathcal{F}})=-2\rho, $$

and the determinant of the tangent bundle is

The Determinant Bundle and the First Chern Class

The determinant character. The determinant is a homomorphism

$$ \det:GL_4\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^\times. $$

The associated line bundle

$$ \mathcal{D}=\det{}^*\mathcal{O}(1) $$

is the determinant bundle. Its first Chern class is the generator of

$$ H^2(GL_4;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^2(U(4);\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}. $$

Under the homotopy equivalence $GL_4\simeq U(4)$, the determinant bundle restricts to the determinant line bundle of $U(4)$, whose first Chern class is $1$.

The determinant bundle over the Grassmannian. Let

$$ 0\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^4\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0 $$

be the tautological sequence on $\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$, with $S$ the rank-$p$ subbundle and $Q$ the rank-$(4-p)$ quotient. The determinant line bundles satisfy

$$ \det S\otimes\det Q\cong\det\mathbb{C}^4\cong\mathcal{O}, $$

so

$$ c_1(\det S)+c_1(\det Q)=0. $$

Let $H$ be the positive generator of $H^2(\operatorname{Gr}(p,4);\mathbb{Z})$, the Plücker hyperplane class, with $H=c_1(\det Q)$. Then

$$ c_1(\det S)=-H,\qquad c_1(\det Q)=H. $$

For $p=1$, $S=\mathcal{O}(-1)$ and $\det S=\mathcal{O}(-1)$, so $c_1(\det S)=-1$; the dual $\mathcal{O}(1)$ has $c_1=1$. For $p=2$, the Plücker line bundle $\det Q$ has $c_1=H$, and the determinant of the tautological bundle has $c_1=-H$.

The determinant bundle over the idempotents. The affine bundle $\mathcal{I}_p\to\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$ is homotopy equivalent to its zero section, so every characteristic class of $\mathcal{I}_p$ is the pullback of the corresponding class on the Grassmannian. The determinant bundle of the tautological $p$-plane over $\mathcal{I}_p$ has first Chern class $-H$; the Plücker bundle has $c_1=H$.

The determinant bundle over the flag variety. On the full flag variety, the tautological line bundles $L_1,\dots,L_4$ have first Chern classes $e_1,\dots,e_4$ with $\sum e_i=0$ in $H^2(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{Z})$, because their direct sum is the trivial standard representation bundle and its determinant is trivial; the weights are taken modulo the determinant character. The determinant of the tangent bundle is the nontrivial line bundle

$$ \det T_{\mathcal{F}}=L_{2\rho}, \qquad c_1=2\rho=(3,1,-1,-3). $$

The canonical bundle $K_{\mathcal{F}}=L_{-2\rho}$ is the bundle of holomorphic $6$-forms; its first Chern class $-2\rho$ is the obstruction to a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic $6$-form on the flag variety. The determinant bundle of the tangent has the opposite first Chern class $2\rho$.

The integral. The first Chern class of the determinant bundle integrates over the appropriate cycle to an integer. For $\mathbb{P}^3=\operatorname{Gr}(1,4)$, the determinant bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has

$$ \int_{\mathbb{P}^1}c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1))=-1, $$

on a line $\mathbb{P}^1\subset\mathbb{P}^3$; the dual $\mathcal{O}(1)$ has integral $1$. The sign depends on the choice of the tautological or the quotient bundle; the Plücker normalization is positive.

The Biquaternion Case

At $n=2$ the topology is the same pattern. The biquaternion algebra is $M_2(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{R}^8$, contractible; $GL_2(\mathbb{C})\simeq U(2)$; and

$$ \pi_1(GL_2)=\mathbb{Z},\qquad \pi_2(GL_2)=0. $$

The rank-one idempotents form an affine bundle of rank $1$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$, homotopy equivalent to $\mathbb{P}^1$; the rank-two idempotent is the identity, a point. The full flag variety of $\mathbb{C}^2$ is $\mathbb{P}^1$, simply connected, with Euler characteristic $2$.

The determinant bundle. The determinant line bundle over $U(2)$ has first Chern class $1$; over $\mathbb{P}^1$ the tautological bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has $c_1=-1$ and the dual $\mathcal{O}(1)$ has $c_1=1$. The pattern is the $n=2$ case of the $n=4$ formulas.

The comparison with the regular model. The full algebra $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ has the singular set $\{\det=0\}$, a contractible cone; the biquaternion algebra has the null cone of its quaternionic norm, also a contractible cone. The complements have the homotopy type of $U(4)$ and $U(2)$ respectively; the determinant bundles have first Chern class $1$ in both cases. The regular model $M_4(\mathbb{C})_L$ is a four-dimensional complex subspace with the null cone as its singular set; the full algebra's singular set is the determinantal hypersurface.

Summary

The algebra $M_4(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{R}^{32}$ is contractible, with the cohomology of a point and vanishing homotopy groups. Its complement $GL_4(\mathbb{C})$ is homotopy equivalent to $U(4)$ by the polar decomposition $A=UP\mapsto U$; hence

$$ \pi_1(GL_4)=\mathbb{Z},\qquad \pi_2(GL_4)=0,\qquad \pi_3(GL_4)=\mathbb{Z}, $$

and the universal cover of $U(4)$ is $SU(4)\times\mathbb{R}$. The idempotents of rank $p$ are affine bundles over $\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$ with contractible fibres, so $\mathcal{I}_p\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$; the Grassmannian has Poincaré polynomial $\binom{4}{p}_q$ and Euler characteristic $\binom{4}{p}$, giving $1,4,6,4,1$ for $p=0,1,2,3,4$. The full flag variety $GL_4/B$ is simply connected, of dimension $6$, with Poincaré polynomial $[4]_q!=1+3q+5q^2+6q^3+5q^4+3q^5+q^6$ and Euler characteristic $24$. The determinant bundle over $GL_4$ has first Chern class the generator of $H^2=\mathbb{Z}$; over $\operatorname{Gr}(p,4)$ the determinant of the tautological bundle has $c_1=-H$ and the Plücker bundle has $c_1=H$; over the flag variety the determinant of the tangent bundle is $L_{2\rho}$ with $c_1=2\rho=(3,1,-1,-3)$, and the canonical bundle is $L_{-2\rho}$. The singular set is a contractible cone whose link is a real algebraic hypersurface of dimension $29$, singular along the rank-at-most-two locus.

$$ \boxed{\ M_4\simeq*,\quad GL_4\simeq U(4),\quad \pi_1(GL_4)=\mathbb{Z},\quad \pi_2(GL_4)=0,\quad \mathcal{I}_p\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(p,4),\quad \pi_1(\mathcal{F})=0. } $$

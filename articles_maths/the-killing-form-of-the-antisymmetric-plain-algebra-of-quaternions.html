The Killing Form of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

The antisymmetric plain algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ is the real Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}=(\mathbb{H},\times)$ with the cross product $\tilde p\times\tilde q=\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ of The Lie Algebra of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category. The result is simple and rigid: the form is the Euclidean form of the vector subspace up to the factor $-2$, with the scalar direction in its radical, so it is negative semidefinite, negative definite on the derived algebra, and its radical is the centre. The space of invariant bilinear forms is the plane spanned by the Killing form and the scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0$, and there is no nonzero alternating invariant form.

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The form belongs to the Lie bracket of the vector parts, not to the associative multiplication. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1^2=e_2^2=e_3^2=-e_0$; $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$ is the Euclidean dot product, $\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ the vector product, and $C_{\mathbf p}$ the cross-product matrix. The plain form is $B(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde p\tilde q)$, the quaternion form is $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$, and the scalar-part form is $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0$.

The Adjoint Representation and the Killing Form

Definition. The Killing form of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}=(\mathbb{H},\times)$ is

$$ \kappa(\tilde p,\tilde q)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde p}\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde q}\bigr), $$

where $\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde p}\tilde r=\tilde p\times\tilde r$ is the adjoint operator, of The Adjoint Operators and the Derivations of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category.

Proposition. In the basis $(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)$ the adjoint is the block matrix $\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde p}=\operatorname{diag}(0,C_{\mathbf p})$ on $\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}e_0\oplus\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, its square is $\operatorname{diag}(0,C_{\mathbf p}^2)$, and its trace is zero.

Proof. The bracket vanishes on the centre, so the centre is the kernel and the first block vanishes; on the vector subspace the adjoint is the cross product by $\mathbf p$, whose matrix is $C_{\mathbf p}$; a cross-product matrix is traceless, being skew-symmetric.

The Computation of the Form

Theorem (the form). For all $\tilde p,\tilde q$,

$$ \kappa(\tilde p,\tilde q)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(C_{\mathbf p}C_{\mathbf q}\bigr)=-2\,\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q . $$

In the basis the Gram matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(0,-2,-2,-2)$.

Proof. The adjoint product is $\operatorname{diag}(0,C_{\mathbf p}C_{\mathbf q})$ and the scalar block is zero, so the trace is $\operatorname{Tr}(C_{\mathbf p}C_{\mathbf q})$. Writing $C_{\mathbf p}\mathbf q=\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ and using $\operatorname{Tr}(AB)=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}B_{ji}=-\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}B_{ij}$ for skew-symmetric $A,B$, the trace is minus the Frobenius pairing of $C_{\mathbf p}$ and $C_{\mathbf q}$, which is $2\,\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$, so $\kappa=-2\,\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$.

The Invariance and the Two Forms

Proposition (invariance). The Killing form is symmetric and invariant,

$$ \kappa(\tilde p\times\tilde q,\tilde r)+\kappa(\tilde q,\tilde p\times\tilde r)=0 , $$

and it is the form of the vector subspace alone: it does not see the scalar coordinates.

Proof. A Killing form is symmetric and invariant by the general theory of Lie algebras (Structure of Lie Algebras); on the block it is read as $-2\,\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$, which is zero whenever one of the arguments is central.

Theorem (the invariant bilinear forms). The space of invariant bilinear forms of $\mathfrak{g}$ is two-dimensional, spanned by the Killing form $\kappa$ and the scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0$. There is no nonzero alternating invariant form. The plain form is the combination

$$ B=\varphi+\tfrac12\kappa,\qquad B(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q , $$

and it is nondegenerate, of signature $(1,3)$.

Proof. An invariant form is determined by its values on the two components and by the invariance, which forces the vanishing of the coupling between the centre and the derived algebra; on the simple derived algebra the invariant forms are the multiples of the Killing form, and on the abelian centre the one-dimensional scalar-part form is free. The Killing form is negative definite on the derived algebra and zero on the centre, the scalar-part form is positive definite on the centre and zero on the derived algebra, and there is no alternating source, the forms of a simple algebra being the multiples of the Killing form, which is symmetric. The plain form is the displayed combination and its signature is the inertia of $p_0q_0-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$, one positive and three negative directions.

The Radical and the Definiteness

Theorem. The radical of the Killing form is the centre,

$$ \{\tilde p:\kappa(\tilde p,\tilde q)=0\ \text{for every }\tilde q\}=\mathbb{R}e_0 , $$

of real dimension one; the form is negative semidefinite, $\kappa(\tilde p,\tilde p)=-2|\mathbf p|^2\le0$, with equality exactly on the centre; and it is negative definite on the derived algebra.

Proof. $\kappa(\tilde p,\tilde q)=-2\,\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$ vanishes for every $\tilde q$ exactly when $\mathbf p=0$, which is the centre. The diagonal value is $-2|\mathbf p|^2$, non-positive and zero only for $\mathbf p=0$.

The Casimir Element

Definition. Let $h^{ij}$ be the inverse of the Gram matrix $\kappa(e_i,e_j)=-2\delta_{ij}$, that is $h^{ij}=-\tfrac12\delta^{ij}$. The Casimir element of the block is

$$ \Omega=\sum_{i,j}h^{ij}e_ie_j=-\tfrac12\bigl(e_1^2+e_2^2+e_3^2\bigr), $$

an element of the universal enveloping algebra $\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})$.

Theorem (the eigenvalue). The Casimir acts as the identity on the adjoint representation, that is on the derived algebra $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, as the scalar $\tfrac38$ on the two-dimensional module of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, and as $0$ on the centre.

Proof. On the derived algebra the adjoint is $\mathbf p\mapsto C_{\mathbf p}$, and $C_{\mathbf p}^2=\mathbf p\mathbf p^{\mathsf T}-|\mathbf p|^2\mathrm{I}_3$ gives $\sum_k C_{e_k}^2=-2\mathrm{I}_3$; hence $\Omega$ acts as $-\tfrac12\sum_k C_{e_k}^2=\mathrm{I}_3$. The two-dimensional module sends $e_k$ to $-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_k$, and $\Omega$ acts as $-\tfrac12\sum_k(-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_k)^2=\tfrac18\sum_k\sigma_k^2=\tfrac38\mathrm{I}_2$ because the Pauli matrices satisfy $\sigma_k^2=\mathrm{I}_2$. The centre is annihilated.

Remark (the Casimir and the two norms). The eigenvalue $\tfrac38$ on the two-dimensional module is form realizes; the two readings are the two available values of the Casimir, and they do not conflict, being read on different representations.

The Cartan Criterion

Proposition. The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is not semisimple, and the degeneracy of its Killing form exhibits it: by Cartan's criterion an algebra is semisimple exactly when its Killing form is nondegenerate, and here the form degenerates on the centre. The semisimple part of $\mathfrak{g}$, the derived algebra, has the negative definite form, as a compact real form must.

Proof. The radical is the centre, of dimension one; the form is nondegenerate exactly on the derived algebra, where it is the negative definite form of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.

Summary

The Killing form of the antisymmetric plain algebra is $\kappa(\tilde p,\tilde q)=\operatorname{Tr}(C_{\mathbf p}C_{\mathbf q})=-2\,\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$, of Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(0,-2,-2,-2)$: the Euclidean form of the vector subspace in the negative direction, with the scalar direction in its radical. It is symmetric and invariant, negative semidefinite, and negative definite on the derived algebra, whose compact form it is. The space of invariant bilinear forms is two-dimensional, spanned by the Killing form and the scalar-part form $p_0q_0$, and the plain form is their combination $B=\varphi+\tfrac12\kappa$, nondegenerate of signature $(1,3)$; there is no nonzero alternating invariant form. The Casimir element $\Omega=-\tfrac12(e_1^2+e_2^2+e_3^2)$ acts as the identity on the adjoint and as $\tfrac38$ on the two-dimensional module. By Cartan's criterion the algebra is not semisimple, the radical being the centre. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the same computation gives an indefinite form; the antisymmetric quaternionic block has no Killing form.

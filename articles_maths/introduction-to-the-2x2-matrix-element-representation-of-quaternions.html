Introduction to the 2×2 Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ of Quaternions

Introduction

The quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$ is realized, as a real algebra, by a four-dimensional subspace of the $2\times2$ complex matrices: the map assigns to $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$ four displayed matrices, extends by real linearity, and is multiplicative. This article presents the map, the trace and the determinant of the image, the images of the scalar and vector subspaces, the reason the image is a proper subspace of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, and the form the conjugations take.

The article is the first of the two matrix articles of the group; the companion is Introduction to the 4×4 Matrix Element Representation $M_4(\mathbb{R})_L$ of Quaternions, below this article in this category, where the left regular representation realizes $\mathbb{H}$ inside the real matrices. This article owns the map, the multiplicativity, the trace, the determinant, the subspace conditions and the conjugation forms. The further development — the rank-one elements, the characteristic polynomial and the Cayley–Hamilton identity, the simple module and the matrix units — belongs to the blocks of The Quaternion Algebra in the 2×2 Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, below this article in this category.

The Embedding

Definition. The matrix realization of $\mathbb{H}$ is the map $$ \mathsf{M}_2:\mathbb{H}\longrightarrow M_2(\mathbb{C}) $$ fixed by its values on the four units, $$ \mathsf{M}_2(e_0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathsf{M}_2(e_1)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\-i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathsf{M}_2(e_2)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathsf{M}_2(e_3)=\begin{pmatrix}-i&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix}, $$ and extended by real linearity. On a general element $\tilde q=q_0e_0+q_1e_1+q_2e_2+q_3e_3$ it reads $$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)=\begin{pmatrix}q_0-iq_3&-iq_1-q_2\\ -iq_1+q_2&q_0+iq_3\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}z&w\\-\overline{w}&\overline{z}\end{pmatrix}, \qquad z=q_0-iq_3,\quad w=-q_2-iq_1. $$

Theorem. The map $\mathsf{M}_2$ is an injective $\mathbb{R}$-algebra homomorphism, $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\tilde q)=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)$. Its image is the four-real-dimensional subspace $$ \mathsf{M}_2(\mathbb{H})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-\overline{b}&\overline{a}\end{pmatrix}:a,b\in\mathbb{C}\right\} =\left\{M:\ M_{22}=\overline{M_{11}},\ M_{21}=-\overline{M_{12}}\right\}. $$

Proof. The three matrices of $e_1,e_2,e_3$ satisfy the quaternion relations $M_k^2=-I$, $M_1M_2=M_3$, $M_2M_1=-M_3$ and the cyclic variants, by direct computation; the algebra $\mathbb{H}$ is generated by $e_1,e_2,e_3$ subject to those relations, so the real-linear extension is multiplicative. It is injective because the four images are linearly independent over $\mathbb{R}$. The image condition is read on the general entry, and a matrix of the form shown has the four real parameters $\mathrm{Re}\,a,\mathrm{Im}\,a,\mathrm{Re}\,b,\mathrm{Im}\,b$.

The Trace and the Determinant

Proposition. For every $\tilde q$, $$ \operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)=2q_0, \qquad \det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)=\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}=q_0^2+q_1^2+q_2^2+q_3^2\ge0. $$

Proof. The trace is the sum of the diagonal entries, $(q_0-iq_3)+(q_0+iq_3)=2q_0$. The determinant is $z\overline z-w(-\overline w)=|z|^2+|w|^2$, and $|z|^2=(q_0-iq_3)(q_0+iq_3)=q_0^2+q_3^2$, $|w|^2=q_1^2+q_2^2$.

The determinant is the quaternion norm, quoted from Norm and Invertibility in the Quaternion Algebra, below this article in this category, and it is multiplicative, $\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p\tilde q)=\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde p)\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)$; the trace is additive and not multiplicative, and depends on the scalar part alone. The determinant is non-negative on the image, vanishing only at $\tilde q=0$: this is the matrix form of the definiteness of the quaternion norm, and it is the first sign that the image is a special subspace of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$.

The Images of the Two Subspaces

subspace image under $\mathsf{M}_2$ description
$\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}$ $\left\{\begin{pmatrix}s&0\\0&s\end{pmatrix}:s\in\mathbb{R}\right\}$ the real scalar matrices, dimension one
$\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$ $\left\{\begin{pmatrix}-iq_3&-iq_1-q_2\\-iq_1+q_2&iq_3\end{pmatrix}\right\}$ the traceless matrices of the image, dimension three
$\mathbb{H}$ the full image dimension four

The trace separates the scalar line from the vector space and nothing else: $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)=0$ exactly on $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, and $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)=2q_0$ is twice the scalar part. On the vector subspace the determinant is $q_1^2+q_2^2+q_3^2\ge0$, the Euclidean square; on the scalar line it is $s^2$. The image of the scalar line is the real scalar matrices, the intersection of the image with the centre of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ being exactly those; the image of the vector space is the traceless part of the image, and the two images sum to the whole image.

The Image Is Not All of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$

Theorem. The image $\mathsf{M}_2(\mathbb{H})$ is a proper $\mathbb{R}$-subspace of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$: it has real dimension four, while $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ has complex dimension four and real dimension eight. It is the fixed space of the anti-linear involution $$ \sigma(M)=\epsilon\,\overline M\,\epsilon^{-1},\qquad \epsilon=i\sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}, $$ and it is not closed under multiplication by the scalar imaginary $i$ of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$.

Proof. The dimension follows from the four real parameters of the general entry. The condition $M_{22}=\overline{M_{11}}$, $M_{21}=-\overline{M_{12}}$ is exactly $\sigma(M)=M$, computed with $\epsilon\overline M\epsilon^{-1}$; $\sigma$ is anti-linear and an involution. Finally $iI$ is not in the image: a matrix of the image has $M_{22}=\overline{M_{11}}$, which fails for $iI$ because $\overline{i}=-i$; equivalently, a preimage would need $\overline z=i$, that is $z=-i$, and $w=0$, giving $\mathsf{M}_2(e_3)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-i&0\\0&i\end{smallmatrix}\right)\neq iI$.

The last statement is the article's caution. The scalar imaginary $i$ is part of the matrix algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, not of $\mathbb{H}$; it is not in the image, and it cannot be produced by multiplying elements of the image by real numbers.

The Conjugations in Matrix Form

Proposition. The natural conjugation of $\mathbb{H}$ becomes the conjugate transpose, $$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q^{\natural})=\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)^{\dagger}, \qquad \dagger=\text{conjugate transpose}. $$ The inner involution $\iota_\nu$ becomes conjugation by the matrix of $\nu$, $$ \mathsf{M}_2(\iota_\nu \tilde q)=\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)\,\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)\,\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)^{-1}, $$ and for a unit imaginary $\nu$ the matrix $\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)$ is anti-Hermitian, $\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)^{\dagger}=-\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)$, with $\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)=I_2$, so the inner involutions are conjugations by matrices of the image satisfying that condition. The reflection $\rho_\nu=-\iota_\nu$ is minus the same conjugation.

Proof. For the general entry, the conjugate transpose of $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q)$ is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\overline z&-w\\ \overline w&z\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, which is $\mathsf{M}_2(q_0-\vec q)$ read from the pattern with $z'=\overline z$, $w'=-w$; this is $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde q^{\natural})$. The inner statement is the multiplicativity. For a unit imaginary $\nu$, $\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)^{\dagger}=\mathsf{M}_2(\nu^{\natural})=\mathsf{M}_2(-\nu)=-\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)$ and $\det\mathsf{M}_2(\nu)=\langle \nu\rangle_{\natural}=1$.

Thus the natural sign is involutive and anti-multiplicative in the matrix algebra too — the conjugate transpose satisfies $(AB)^{\dagger}=B^{\dagger}A^{\dagger}$ — while the matrix transpose alone realizes the composition of ${}^{\natural}$ with an inner involution, and the entrywise conjugation $\overline M$ realizes another.

Summary

The quaternion algebra embeds in $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ by $e_0\mapsto I$, $e_k\mapsto-i\sigma_k$, a map extended by real linearity and multiplicative. The image is the four-real-dimensional subspace $\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\-\overline b&\overline a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}$, the fixed space of the anti-linear involution $\sigma(M)=\epsilon\overline M\epsilon^{-1}$. The trace is twice the scalar part, so the trace cuts out the vector subspace, and the determinant is the quaternion norm, non-negative, zero only at the origin. The image is not all of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, of which it has half the real dimension, and the scalar imaginary $i$ of the matrix algebra is outside it. The natural conjugation is the conjugate transpose and the inner involutions are conjugations by matrices of the image satisfying $\mathsf{M}^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}=I_2$.

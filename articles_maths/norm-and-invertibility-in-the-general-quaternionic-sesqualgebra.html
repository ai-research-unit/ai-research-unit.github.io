Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Introduction

A unit of a product $\star$ is an element $\tilde E$ with $\tilde E\star\tilde X = \tilde X\star\tilde E = \tilde X$ for every $\tilde X$, an inverse of $\tilde X$ is an element $\tilde Y$ with $\tilde X\star\tilde Y = \tilde Y\star\tilde X = \tilde E$, and $\tilde X$ is invertible when it has an inverse; the definitions are Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures. This article fixes the product to the general quaternionic sesquilinear product

$$ \tilde P \star \tilde Q = \tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*} $$

of Introduction to the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions, and it answers the two questions of its title.

The answer is a double absence, stated as a law, together with the one algebraic norm that the block does carry. The two actions of $e_0$ are the natural conjugation and the star, $\tilde Q\star e_0 = \tilde Q^{\natural}$ and $e_0\star\tilde Q = \tilde Q^{*}$, so no element is a two-sided unit and the block has no unit, no inverse and no invertible element. The algebraic norm of the block is the diagonal of its scalar-part form,

$$ K(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}, $$

the general quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm of The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, real, indefinite, of signature $(2,6)$, and not multiplicative. The article states the absence of the inverse, defines $K$, proves the failure of multiplicativity, and records the absence of a criterion: $K$ does not decide invertibility, and the block has no invertibility of its own to decide.

Boundaries. The definition of the algebraic norm as the form at the two equal arguments and the three other algebraic norms are The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space; none of the other three is read here. The absence of the unit and the two one-sided families are The Two One-Sided Actions and the Absence of a Unit; the form $K$, its Gram matrix and its restrictions are The Krein Gram Matrix and the Restrictions of the Form; the element classes of the block, read without a unit, are Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and Square Roots of a General Element in the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis $e_0 = 1, e_1, e_2, e_3$ with $e_1e_2 = e_3$ and central scalar imaginary $i$. An element is $\tilde Q = Q_0e_0+\vec Q$. The conjugations are $\tilde Q^{\natural} = Q_0-\vec Q$, $\tilde Q^{*} = \overline{\tilde Q^{\natural}}$, $\overline{\tilde Q} = \tilde Q^{\natural*} = \tilde Q^{*\natural}$. The product is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q = \tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$, with the natural conjugation ${}^{\natural}$ in the first slot and the star ${}^{*}$ in the second; juxtaposition is the general plain bilinear product. The sign vector is $\varepsilon = (1,-1,-1,-1)$.

The results. The general quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ has no unit, no inverse and no invertible element: the two actions of $e_0$ are the star and the natural conjugation, so no element is a two-sided unit. It carries one algebraic norm, the general quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm $K(\tilde Q)=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}$, real, indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, with the $K$-isotropic cone as its vanishing set, and $K$ is not multiplicative. Because the block has no invertible element, $K$ has no invertibility of the block to decide, and it does not decide the invertibility of the plain product either: the plain unit $e_0+e_1$ has $K=0$ and the plain zero divisor $e_1+ie_2$ has $K=-2$. Each section below states its result first and then proves it.

The Double Absence

The product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ has no left unit, no right unit and no two-sided unit, so no element has an inverse and the set of invertible elements is empty.

Proposition (the two one-sided actions of $e_0$). For every $\tilde Q$,

$$ e_0\star\tilde Q = \tilde Q^{*} , \qquad \tilde Q\star e_0 = \tilde Q^{\natural} , $$

so the left action of $e_0$ is the star and the right action is the natural conjugation.

Proof. $e_0\star\tilde Q = e_0^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*} = e_0\tilde Q^{*} = \tilde Q^{*}$, and $\tilde Q\star e_0 = \tilde Q^{\natural}e_0^{*} = \tilde Q^{\natural}$.

Theorem (no unit, no inverse, no invertible element). The product $\star$ has no left unit, no right unit and no two-sided unit; no element has an inverse; and the set of invertible elements of $\star$ is empty.

Proof. A left unit $\tilde E$ would satisfy $\tilde E\star\tilde Q = \tilde Q$ for every $\tilde Q$, and at $\tilde Q = e_0$ this reads $\tilde E\star e_0 = e_0$, that is $\tilde E^{\natural} = e_0$ by the proposition, so $\tilde E = e_0$; but $e_0\star\tilde Q = \tilde Q^{*}$ is not $\tilde Q$ for every $\tilde Q$, for instance at $\tilde Q = e_1$, so there is no left unit. The same computation at $\tilde Q = e_0$ for a right unit gives $\tilde E^{*} = e_0$, hence $\tilde E = e_0$, and $e_0$ is not a right unit because $\tilde Q\star e_0 = \tilde Q^{\natural}$ is not $\tilde Q$ at $\tilde Q = e_1$. A two-sided unit would be both, so there is none. An inverse requires a unit in its definition, so no inverse exists, and the set of invertible elements is empty. The same statements, with the monoid and the one-sided families, are The Two One-Sided Actions and the Absence of a Unit, where the left and the right multiplications of the block are computed as well.

Corollary (the absence and the element classes). The concepts of Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and the roots of a central value of the block are read without a unit: an idempotent is an element equal to its square and not a projection onto the unit, a nilpotent is an element of vanishing square and not a multiple of a unit, and a root of $\lambda$ is an element of square $\lambda e_0$ and not the product of a unit with a root of $\lambda$ in the plain algebra.

Proof. Each definition names only the product and $e_0$, and none of them names a unit; the corollary is the reading of Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures with the theorem above.

The Algebraic Norm of the Block

The block carries exactly one algebraic norm, the diagonal of its scalar-part form, $K(\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}$, real and indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, with the nonzero $K$-isotropic cone as its vanishing set.

Definition. The algebraic norm of the block is the diagonal of the scalar-part form of the product,

$$ K(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}, $$

equivalently $K(\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)$ for the form

$$ K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu}, $$

the general quaternionic sesquilinear form of The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, also called the Krein form of the block, whose Gram matrix and restrictions are computed in The Krein Gram Matrix and the Restrictions of the Form.

Basic properties. Each is a direct reading of the definition.

  • $K(\tilde Q)$ is real, and indefinite: the four entries $1,-1,-1,-1$ on the real and the imaginary parts of the four coefficients give the inertia $(2,6)$ on the realification $\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{R}^{8}$.
  • $K$ is homogeneous of degree two over the reals, $K(\lambda\tilde Q)=\lvert\lambda\rvert^{2}K(\tilde Q)$ for $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$.
  • Its polarisation is the sesquilinear form $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$, and $K(\tilde Q,\tilde P)=\overline{K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)}$: the form is Hermitian.
  • Its vanishing set is the $K$-isotropic cone, a nonzero cone: $K(e_0+e_1)=1-1=0$, so the algebraic norm vanishes on nonzero elements.

The Algebraic Norm Is Not Multiplicative

The algebraic norm of the block is not multiplicative, $K(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\neq K(\tilde P)K(\tilde Q)$ in general, the pair $\tilde P=\tilde Q=e_0+e_1$ separating the two sides.

Proposition. The algebraic norm of the block is not multiplicative:

$$ K(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)\neq K(\tilde P)\,K(\tilde Q)\quad\text{in general.} $$

Proof. The pair $\tilde P=\tilde Q=e_0+e_1$ separates the two sides. Here $K(\tilde P)=K(\tilde Q)=1-1=0$, so $K(\tilde P)K(\tilde Q)=0$, while $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=(e_0-e_1)(e_0+e_1)=e_0+e_1-e_1-e_1^{2}=2e_0$ and $K(2e_0)=4$. The two values differ, so no multiplicativity holds.

Remark (the failure belongs to the block). The block, whose two slots carry the two conjugations, does not respect its own algebraic norm $K$ under its product.

The Absence of a Criterion from the Algebraic Norm

The algebraic norm does not decide invertibility: the set of invertible elements of the block is empty, so $K$ has nothing to decide for the block, and $K=0$ does not force a plain non-unit while $K\neq0$ does not force a plain unit.

The algebraic norm of a product would decide invertibility if the invertible elements were exactly the elements of non-vanishing algebraic norm. For the block that statement has no content in one direction and fails in the other, and the article records the absence rather than a criterion.

Theorem (the algebraic norm does not decide invertibility). The set of invertible elements of the block is empty, so no criterion in terms of $K$ can name it; and $K$ does not decide the one invertibility that is available, the invertibility of the plain product either:

$$ K(\tilde Q)=0\ \not\Longrightarrow\ \tilde Q\text{ not a plain unit}, \qquad K(\tilde Q)\neq0\ \not\Longrightarrow\ \tilde Q\text{ is a plain unit}. $$

Proof. The first clause is the theorem on the double absence: there is no invertible element, so $K$ has nothing to decide for the block. For the two implications, the element $\tilde Q=e_0+e_1$ has $K(\tilde Q)=1-1=0$ and is a unit of the plain product, $(e_0+e_1)(e_0-e_1)=2e_0$; the element $\tilde Q=e_1+ie_2$ has $K(\tilde Q)=-1-1=-2\neq0$ and is a zero divisor of the plain product, $(e_1+ie_2)(e_1-ie_2)=e_1^{2}-(ie_2)^{2}=-e_0+e_0=0$.

Remark (the two witnesses). The element $e_0+e_1$ is a plain unit with vanishing algebraic norm, so $K$ is not necessary; the element $e_1+ie_2$ is a plain non-unit with non-vanishing algebraic norm, so $K$ is not sufficient. The vanishing set of $K$ is the $K$-isotropic cone and the zero-divisor set is the cone of a different quadratic form; the two cones meet, as at $e_0+e_1$ in the first and at the idempotents in the second, and neither contains the other.

Remark (what replaces the criterion). The block is not left without an element theory: the idempotents, the nilpotents and the square roots of the block are computed in their articles, and the plain product has its own invertibility theory. What the article records is that this theory is not read from $K$, which is the one algebraic norm the block carries.

Summary

The general quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q = \tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ has no unit, no inverse and no invertible element: the left action of $e_0$ is the star, $e_0\star\tilde Q = \tilde Q^{*}$, and the right action is the natural conjugation, $\tilde Q\star e_0 = \tilde Q^{\natural}$, so no element is a two-sided unit.

It carries one algebraic norm, the diagonal of its scalar-part form, the general quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm

$$ K(\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q\star\tilde Q\bigr)=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_0\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_1\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_2\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_3\rvert^{2}, $$

real, indefinite of signature $(2,6)$, with the $K$-isotropic cone as its vanishing set, also called the Krein form; it is not multiplicative, the pair $\tilde P=\tilde Q=e_0+e_1$ separating the two sides, $K(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)=K(2e_0)=4$ against $K(\tilde P)K(\tilde Q)=0$. The algebraic norm does not decide invertibility: the plain unit $e_0+e_1$ has $K=0$ and the plain zero divisor $e_1+ie_2$ has $K=-2$, so the non-vanishing of the algebraic norm is neither necessary nor sufficient. The article records that absence, and the block has no criterion of its own to state.

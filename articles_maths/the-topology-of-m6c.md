# __The Topology of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$__

## Introduction

The algebra $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{72}$, hence contractible. The interesting topology is in the open dense group

$$
GL_6(\mathbb{C})=\{A:\det A\neq0\},
$$

which is homotopy equivalent to the compact $*$-unitary group:

$$
GL_6(\mathbb{C})\simeq U(6).
$$

Consequently

$$
\pi_1(GL_6)=\mathbb{Z},
\qquad
\pi_2(GL_6)=0,
$$

the first homotopy group generated by the determinant loop, the second vanishing because a Lie group has no nontrivial spherical two-cycles.

The rank-$p$ idempotents are homotopy equivalent to the Grassmannian:

$$
\mathcal E_p\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(p,6),
$$

because the fibre of the image map is the contractible affine space of complements. The full flag variety $GL_6/B$ is simply connected, and the determinant line bundle over the Grassmannian has first Chern class the generator of $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}$.

The article assumes the polar decomposition of *The Polar Decomposition*, the idempotent geometry of *The Geometry of the Idempotents*, and the Grassmannians of *The Adjoint Action, the Orbits and the Flag Variety*. It uses the standard homotopy theory of Lie groups and bundles. The complex analysis is *Analysis on $M_6(\mathbb{C})$*; the group structure is *The Exponential and the Lie Group*.

## The Contractibility of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$

### The Vector Space

**Theorem.** $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{72}$ and is contractible.

**Proof.** The $36$ complex entries give a real-linear homeomorphism $M_6\cong\mathbb{R}^{72}$. The linear homotopy

$$
H_t(A)=(1-t)A,
\qquad
t\in[0,1],
$$

contracts $M_6$ to the zero matrix.

**Corollary (the algebra is not the interesting space).** Every continuous map from a sphere to $M_6$ is null-homotopic; all homotopy groups of $M_6$ vanish.

**Proof.** A contractible space has the homotopy type of a point.

### The Singular Set

**Theorem.** The singular set $\Sigma=\{A:\det A=0\}$ is a closed algebraic hypersurface of real codimension two. It is connected, and its complement $GL_6$ is open and dense.

**Proof.** The determinant is a continuous polynomial; its zero set is closed. The complement is the nonvanishing locus of a polynomial, hence open; it is dense because the determinant is not identically zero. For the codimension, $\det$ is a submersion at the rank-five matrices, so the zero set is smooth there of real codimension two. For the connectedness, every $A\in\Sigma$ is joined to $0$ by the path $t\mapsto tA$, which stays in $\Sigma$.

**Corollary (the complement is the group).** The complement of $\Sigma$ is $GL_6(\mathbb{C})$, and the topology of $M_6$ is the topology of $\mathbb{R}^{72}$ with a hypersurface removed.

**Proof.** $A$ is invertible iff $\det A\neq0$.

**Remark (the contrast).** The whole space is contractible, but the group is not: removing the singular set creates the loop of the determinant. **The topology of $M_6$ is trivial; the topology of $GL_6$ is the topology of $U(6)$.**

## The Homotopy of $GL_6(\mathbb{C})$

### The Polar Retraction

**Theorem.** The polar decomposition gives a deformation retraction

$$
r:GL_6(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow U(6),
\qquad
A=UP\longmapsto U,
$$

and the inclusion $U(6)\hookrightarrow GL_6$ is a homotopy equivalence.

**Proof.** The polar factor $U=AP^{-1}$ is continuous on $GL_6$; for $U\in U(6)$, $r(U)=U$. The homotopy

$$
H_t(A)=U\bigl((1-t)P+tI_6\bigr),
\qquad
t\in[0,1],
$$

joins $A$ at $t=0$ to $U$ at $t=1$; the factor $(1-t)P+tI_6$ is positive definite for every $t$, so the path stays in $GL_6$. Hence $GL_6$ deformation retracts onto $U(6)$.

**Corollary (the homotopy type).** $GL_6(\mathbb{C})$ and $U(6)$ have the same homotopy groups, homology and cohomology.

**Proof.** A homotopy equivalence induces isomorphisms on all homotopy and homology groups.

**Corollary (the Gram–Schmidt retraction).** The QR decomposition gives the same homotopy equivalence: $GL_6\simeq U(6)$ via the $*$-unitary factor of Gram–Schmidt.

**Proof.** Gram–Schmidt is continuous on the invertible locus and is the identity on $U(6)$.

### The Fundamental Group

**Theorem.** The determinant induces an isomorphism

$$
\det_*:\pi_1(GL_6)\longrightarrow\pi_1(\mathbb{C}^\times)=\mathbb{Z},
$$

so

$$
\pi_1(GL_6)=\mathbb{Z}.
$$

**Proof.** The determinant is continuous and multiplicative; the loop

$$
\gamma(t)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{2\pi\mathrm i t},1,\dots,1),
\qquad
t\in[0,1],
$$

has determinant $e^{2\pi\mathrm i t}$, whose winding number is $1$. The polar retraction reduces the computation to $U(6)$, where $\det:U(6)\to U(1)$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$; the $*$-unitary group is connected, and the determinant has a section on the circle.

**Corollary (the generator).** The class of the determinant loop generates $\pi_1(GL_6)$; the winding number of $\det\gamma$ is the degree of the loop.

**Proof.** The determinant map is an isomorphism on $\pi_1$; the generator of $\pi_1(\mathbb{C}^\times)$ is the unit circle.

**Corollary (the universal cover).** $GL_6$ is not simply connected; its universal cover is infinite cyclic over the determinant.

**Proof.** $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}$; the universal cover has deck group $\mathbb{Z}$.

### The Second Homotopy Group

**Theorem.** For every Lie group $G$,

$$
\pi_2(G)=0.
$$

In particular

$$
\pi_2(GL_6)=\pi_2(U(6))=0.
$$

**Proof.** The standard theorem that $\pi_2$ of a Lie group vanishes. The route is the maximal compact subgroup: $G$ retracts to $K$, the universal cover of $K$ is compact, and a compact Lie group is a quotient of a product of a torus and simply connected simple compact groups, each of which has vanishing $\pi_2$; the long exact sequences of the quotients preserve the vanishing.

**Corollary (the sphere maps).** Every continuous map $S^2\to GL_6$ is null-homotopic.

**Proof.** $\pi_2(GL_6)=0$.

**Remark (the third group).** For $U(6)$ and $GL_6$, $\pi_3=\mathbb{Z}$. The generator is the class of the inclusion of an $SU(2)$ block, $SU(2)\hookrightarrow U(6)$, read as a map $S^3\cong SU(2)\to U(6)$. The third homotopy group is not needed here; it is the next nontrivial one.

### The Determinant Fibration

**Theorem.** The determinant fits in the fibration

$$
SL_6(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow GL_6(\mathbb{C})\overset{\det}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}^\times,
$$

and

$$
\pi_1(SL_6)=0,\qquad
\pi_1(GL_6)=\mathbb{Z}.
$$

**Proof.** The fibre over $1$ is $SL_6$; the long exact sequence gives $\pi_1(GL_6)\cong\pi_1(\mathbb{C}^\times)$ because $\pi_1(SL_6)=0$ and $\pi_0(SL_6)=0$. The special linear group is simply connected for $n\ge2$.

**Corollary (the $*$-unitary case).**

$$
SU(6)\longrightarrow U(6)\overset{\det}{\longrightarrow}U(1),
\qquad
\pi_1(SU(6))=0,\quad
\pi_1(U(6))=\mathbb{Z}.
$$

**Proof.** The same long exact sequence; $SU(6)$ is simply connected.

## The Idempotents

### The Homotopy Equivalence

**Theorem.** For $0\le p\le6$, the variety of rank-$p$ idempotents is homotopy equivalent to the Grassmannian:

$$
\mathcal E_p\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(p,6).
$$

**Proof.** The image map $\pi_p:\mathcal E_p\to\operatorname{Gr}(p,6)$ is a fibre bundle with fibre the affine space of complements, which is contractible. A fibre bundle with contractible fibre is a homotopy equivalence.

**Corollary (the homology).** $\mathcal E_p$ has the homology and cohomology of $\operatorname{Gr}(p,6)$.

**Proof.** The homotopy equivalence induces isomorphisms on homology.

**Corollary (the section).** The orthogonal projector onto a $p$-plane is a continuous section of $\pi_p$; it is a homotopy inverse of $\pi_p$ up to the contraction of the fibres.

**Proof.** The orthogonal projection depends continuously on the plane; the composition with the image map is the identity on the Grassmannian.

### The Grassmannian

**Theorem.** $\operatorname{Gr}(p,6)$ has a cell decomposition by the Schubert cells indexed by the partitions in a $p\times(6-p)$ box. Its Euler characteristic is

$$
\chi(\operatorname{Gr}(p,6))=\binom{6}{p},
$$

and its Poincaré polynomial is the Gaussian binomial coefficient

$$
P_{\operatorname{Gr}(p,6)}(t)=\binom{6}{p}_t .
$$

**Proof.** The Schubert cells are the $p$-planes with prescribed intersections with a fixed complete flag; the partitions in the box index them; the Gaussian binomial counts them by dimension. The Euler characteristic is the value at $t=1$, which is $\binom{6}{p}$.

**Corollary (the table).**

| $p$ | $\chi$ | $P(t)$ at $t=1$ |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $6$ | $6$ |
| $2$ | $15$ | $15$ |
| $3$ | $20$ | $20$ |
| $4$ | $15$ | $15$ |
| $5$ | $6$ | $6$ |

**Proof.** $\binom{6}{p}$.

**Corollary (the fundamental group).** $\operatorname{Gr}(p,6)$ is simply connected for every $p$.

**Proof.** The complex Grassmannian is a homogeneous space $U(6)/(U(p)\times U(6-p))$; the long exact sequence and the connectedness of the $*$-unitary groups give $\pi_1=0$; equivalently, the Schubert cells of real dimension at most two form a wedge of $2$-spheres, which is simply connected.

### The Idempotent Strata

**Theorem.** The varieties $\mathcal E_p$ are connected, smooth, and their closures satisfy

$$
\overline{\mathcal E_p}\supset\mathcal E_{p-1}.
$$

**Proof.** The homogeneous space is connected; the rank can only drop in the closure; the orthogonal projectors of rank $p-1$ are limits of rank-$p$ projectors.

**Corollary (the union).** The union of the idempotent strata is the full idempotent variety. The strata are the rank loci, the closure of $\mathcal E_p$ contains $\mathcal E_{p-1}$, and the union is connected; it is not the disjoint union of the strata.

**Proof.** The rank is locally constant on each stratum; the closures are the determinantal varieties.

## The Flag Variety

### Simple Connectivity

**Theorem.** The full flag variety

$$
\mathcal F=GL_6/B
$$

is simply connected.

**Proof.** The fibration $GL_6\to\mathcal F$ with fibre $B$ gives the long exact sequence

$$
\pi_1(B)\longrightarrow\pi_1(GL_6)\longrightarrow\pi_1(\mathcal F)\longrightarrow\pi_0(B)=0 .
$$

The Borel $B$ is homotopy equivalent to the torus $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^6$, and the map $\pi_1(B)=\mathbb{Z}^6\to\pi_1(GL_6)=\mathbb{Z}$ is the determinant, the sum of the six coordinates, which is surjective. Hence $\pi_1(\mathcal F)=0$.

**Corollary (the cell decomposition).** The flag variety has the Schubert cells indexed by the permutations of six elements; there are $720$ cells, and

$$
\chi(\mathcal F)=6!=720 .
$$

**Proof.** A complete flag is determined by a permutation matrix relative to a fixed flag; the Bruhat decomposition gives one cell per permutation.

**Corollary (the cohomology).** $H^2(\mathcal F;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^5$, generated by the first Chern classes of the five fundamental line bundles.

**Proof.** The Picard group of $GL_6/B$ is the character group of $B$ modulo the roots, of rank $5$; the fundamental weights give the generators.

### The Determinant Bundle

**Definition.** The **determinant bundle** over $\operatorname{Gr}(p,6)$ is

$$
\operatorname{Det}=\Lambda^p S,
$$

where $S$ is the tautological rank-$p$ subbundle.

**Theorem.** The first Chern class

$$
c_1(\operatorname{Det})\in H^2(\operatorname{Gr}(p,6);\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
$$

is a generator. Its dual $\operatorname{Det}^*$ is the Plücker line bundle $\mathcal O(1)$.

**Proof.** The Plücker embedding sends a $p$-plane to the line $\Lambda^p V_p$ in $\Lambda^p\mathbb{C}^6$; the pullback of the hyperplane bundle is $\operatorname{Det}^*$. The hyperplane bundle on projective space has first Chern class the generator; the pullback is the generator of $H^2(\operatorname{Gr})$.

**Corollary (the integral).** The integral of $c_1(\operatorname{Det})$ over the Schubert cycle of a $p$-plane meeting a fixed $(6-p)$-plane in a line is $\pm1$.

**Proof.** The cycle is the Poincaré dual of the generator; the pairing is the intersection number, which is $1$ up to sign.

**Corollary (the flag variety).** Over the full flag variety, the determinant line bundle associated to the character $\det:B\to\mathbb{C}^\times$ is trivial, because $\det$ extends to $GL_6$; its first Chern class is $0$.

**Proof.** The associated bundle $GL_6\times_B\mathbb{C}_{\det}$ has the nowhere-zero section $g\mapsto\det g$; a line bundle with a nowhere-zero section is trivial.

**Remark (the two determinants).** **The determinant of the tautological bundle over the Grassmannian is nontrivial; the determinant of the frame over the flag variety is trivial.** The first is the Plücker bundle, with $c_1$ the generator; the second is the pullback of the determinant of the trivial rank-six bundle, with $c_1=0$. The nontrivial line bundles over the flag variety are the five fundamental ones, not the determinant.

### The Determinant Fibration Again

**Theorem.** The determinant map

$$
\det:GL_6\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^\times
$$

is a fibre bundle with fibre $SL_6$, and its first Chern class is the generator of $H^2(\mathbb{C}^\times;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}$.

**Proof.** The determinant is a submersion (its derivative at $I_6$ is $\operatorname{tr}$), so it is a fibre bundle; the fibre is $SL_6$. The first Chern class of the associated line bundle is the generator of $H^2$ of the base.

**Corollary (the curvature).** The curvature of the determinant bundle is

$$
\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\,\partial\bar\partial\log\lvert\det A\rvert^2
$$

on $GL_6$; its integral over a closed surface is the winding number of the determinant.

**Proof.** The Chern–Weil form of the determinant line bundle is the curvature of the Hermitian connection on the pullback of the tautological bundle over $\mathbb{C}^\times$; the integral is the degree.

## Summary

$M_6(\mathbb{C})$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{72}$ and contractible. The group $GL_6(\mathbb{C})$ deformation retracts onto $U(6)$ by the polar decomposition, so

$$
\pi_1(GL_6)=\mathbb{Z},\qquad
\pi_2(GL_6)=0 .
$$

The first homotopy group is generated by the determinant loop, and the determinant fibration

$$
SL_6\longrightarrow GL_6\overset{\det}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}^\times
$$

has simply connected fibre.

$$
\boxed{\ M_6\simeq *,\qquad GL_6\simeq U(6),\qquad \pi_1(GL_6)=\mathbb{Z},\quad \pi_2(GL_6)=0,\quad \mathcal E_p\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(p,6) .\ }
$$

The rank-$p$ idempotents are homotopy equivalent to $\operatorname{Gr}(p,6)$; the Grassmannian has Euler characteristic $\binom{6}{p}$ and is simply connected. The full flag variety $GL_6/B$ is simply connected, with Euler characteristic $720$ and $H^2=\mathbb{Z}^5$.

The determinant bundle $\operatorname{Det}=\Lambda^pS$ over the Grassmannian has first Chern class the generator of $H^2=\mathbb{Z}$; over the full flag variety the determinant line bundle is trivial.

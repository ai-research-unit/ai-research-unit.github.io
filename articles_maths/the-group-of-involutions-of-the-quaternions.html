The Group of Involutions of the Quaternions

Introduction

The quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$ has one distinguished involution, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$, and a family of others obtained from it by the inner automorphisms. This article reads them: the natural sign, the inner involutions, the reflections, the involutive anti-automorphisms, the laws they obey, the finite groups they generate, and the orbits of the group generated by the natural sign and the inner involutions of a perpendicular triple. The element, the basis and the product are those of Quaternion Algebra; the real reading is The Quaternions as a Vector Space over $\mathbb{R}$.

The single conjugation written below is the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$.

The involution ${}^{\natural}$ itself, its eigenspaces and its role are Introduction to the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions, below this article in this category; the article at hand owns the inner family, the reflections, the anti-involutions and the composition laws.

The Natural Sign

The natural conjugation (or natural sign) is $$ \tilde q^{\natural}=q_0-\mathbf q , \qquad \tilde q=q_0+\mathbf q , $$ in the notation of The Quaternions as a Vector Space over $\mathbb{R}$, with $\mathbf q=q_1e_1+q_2e_2+q_3e_3$.

Proposition. The map ${}^{\natural}$ is an anti-automorphism of $\mathbb{H}$ and an involution: $$ (\tilde p\tilde q)^{\natural}=\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p^{\natural} , \qquad (\tilde q^{\natural})^{\natural}=\tilde q . $$ Its fixed space is the scalar line $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}$ and its anti-fixed space is the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, so that $\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde q+\tilde q^{\natural})+\tfrac12(\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural})$ is the scalar–vector decomposition.

Proof. The multiplication table is generated by the relations $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_je_k=-e_ke_j$ for $j\neq k$, all of which are preserved with the order of the factors reversed; the involution is immediate on the coordinates. The eigenspaces are read on the coordinates.

The Inner Involutions

Let $\nu$ be a unit imaginary quaternion, $\nu^2=-e_0$, and let the inner conjugation by $\nu$ be $$ \iota_\nu(\tilde q)=\nu\,\tilde q\,\nu^{-1} . $$

Theorem. For a unit imaginary $\nu$, the map $\iota_\nu$ is an involutive automorphism of $\mathbb{H}$. It depends only on the axis line $\mathbb{R}\nu$, that is, $\iota_\nu=\iota_{-\nu}$; it acts as the identity on the plane $\mathbb{C}_\nu=\mathbb{R}e_0\oplus\mathbb{R}\nu$ and as $-1$ on the orthogonal plane $\nu^\perp=\{x\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H}):\nu\cdot x=0\}$. The two planes $\mathbb{C}_\nu$ and $\nu^\perp$ are the eigenspaces of $\iota_\nu$.

Proof. Since $\nu$ is a unit, $\nu^{-1}=\nu^{\natural}=-\nu$, and $\iota_\nu(\tilde q)=\nu \tilde q\nu^{\natural}$; the map is $\mathbb{R}$-linear, and it is multiplicative because $\iota_\nu(\tilde p\tilde q)=\nu \tilde p\tilde q\nu^{-1}=(\nu \tilde p\nu^{-1})(\nu \tilde q\nu^{-1})$. For the square, $\nu$ being central in the subalgebra it generates, $\iota_\nu^2(\tilde q)=\nu^2\tilde q\nu^{-2}=(-1)\tilde q(-1)=\tilde q$. The dependence on the line and the action on the two subspaces are read from the product: $\nu\nu\nu^{-1}=\nu$, $e_0$ is fixed, and for $x\perp\nu$ the product $\nu x$ is again orthogonal to $\nu$ while $\nu x\nu^{-1}=-x$; a two-line coordinate computation with $\nu=e_1$ gives the plane $e_2,e_3$ with both signs negated.

Two inner involutions coincide exactly when their axes coincide: $\iota_{\nu}=\iota_{\mu}$ for unit imaginaries $\nu,\mu$ if and only if $\nu=\pm\mu$. In particular there is one inner involution for each axis line of the vector subspace, and the family is a sphere of axes, not a finite set. The fixed plane $\mathbb{C}_\nu$ and the negated plane $\nu^\perp$ are the axis decomposition of Decompositions Along the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions, below this article in this category.

The Reflections

Beside the inner automorphism $\iota_\nu$ stands the map $$ \rho_\nu=-\iota_\nu:\quad \tilde q\longmapsto-\nu \tilde q\nu^{-1} . $$

Proposition. For a unit imaginary $\nu$, the map $\rho_\nu$ is an involution, $\rho_\nu^2=\operatorname{id}$; it fixes the plane $\nu^\perp$ pointwise and negates the plane $\mathbb{C}_\nu=\mathbb{R}e_0\oplus\mathbb{R}\nu$, so on the vector subspace it fixes the axis line's orthogonal plane and negates the axis line $\mathbb{R}\nu$; and it satisfies the twisted multiplicativity $$ \rho_\nu(\tilde p\tilde q)=-\rho_\nu(\tilde p)\rho_\nu(\tilde q). $$ On the vector subspace it is the reflection in the plane $\nu^\perp$.

Proof. The square is clear from $\iota_\nu^2=\operatorname{id}$; the two subspaces exchange their roles with respect to $\iota_\nu$, so $\rho_\nu$ fixes what $\iota_\nu$ negates and negates what $\iota_\nu$ fixes. For the multiplicativity, $\rho_\nu(\tilde p\tilde q)=-\nu \tilde p\tilde q\nu^{-1}=-(\nu \tilde p\nu^{-1})(\nu \tilde q\nu^{-1})=\iota_\nu(\tilde p)\iota_\nu(\tilde q)\cdot(-1)=-\rho_\nu(\tilde p)\rho_\nu(\tilde q)$.

The reflection is not in the group generated by the natural sign and the inner involutions, every element of that group being multiplicative (an automorphism or an anti-automorphism); it enters the separate list of the involutions in the sense of order-two maps of the set.

The Involutive Anti-Automorphisms

The natural sign is an anti-automorphism; the inner conjugations are automorphisms. The article classifies the anti-automorphisms that are involutions.

Theorem (classification). Every anti-automorphism of $\mathbb{H}$ is of the form $$ \sigma_a(\tilde q)=a\,\tilde q^{\natural}\,a^{-1} , \qquad a\in\mathbb{H},\ a\neq0 , $$ and $\sigma_a$ is an involution exactly when $a^2$ is central, that is, exactly when $a$ is real or pure. Up to scale and up to the sign, the involutive anti-automorphisms are therefore

  • the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ itself, from a real $a$, $\sigma_a={}^{\natural}$;
  • the twists $\alpha_\nu={}^{\natural}$ composed with an inner involution, $\alpha_\nu(\tilde q)=\nu\,\tilde q^{\natural}\,\nu^{-1}$, one for each axis line $\mathbb{R}\nu$ of unit imaginary $\nu$.

Proof. For $a\neq0$ the map $\sigma_a$ is an anti-automorphism, because $(\tilde p\tilde q)^{\natural}=\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p^{\natural}$ gives $$ \sigma_a(\tilde p\tilde q)=a\,(\tilde p\tilde q)^{\natural}a^{-1}=a\,\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p^{\natural}a^{-1}=\bigl(a \tilde q^{\natural}a^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(a \tilde p^{\natural}a^{-1}\bigr)=\sigma_a(\tilde q)\sigma_a(\tilde p). $$ Conversely every anti-automorphism is of this form: if $\sigma$ is one, then $\sigma\circ{}^{\natural}$ is an automorphism, every automorphism of $\mathbb{H}$ is inner, so $\sigma\circ{}^{\natural}=\iota_a$ for some $a\neq0$ and $\sigma=\iota_a\circ{}^{\natural}=\sigma_a$. For the square, $$ \sigma_a(\sigma_a(\tilde q))=a\bigl(a \tilde q^{\natural}a^{-1}\bigr)^{\natural}a^{-1}=a\,(a^{\natural})^{-1}\tilde q\,a^{\natural}a^{-1}, $$ which is the inner conjugation by $c=a(a^{\natural})^{-1}=a^2/|a|^2$; since the positive real factor does not change an inner conjugation, $\sigma_a^2=\iota_{a^2}$. Now $\iota_b=\operatorname{id}$ exactly when $b$ is central, and $a^2$ is central exactly when its vector part $2a_0\mathbf a$ vanishes, that is, exactly when $a$ is real or pure. A real $a$ gives $\sigma_a={}^{\natural}$, and a pure unit $a=\nu$ gives the twist $\alpha_\nu$, which depends only on the axis line.

The maps $id$ and ${}^{\natural}$, together with the pair $\iota_\nu,\alpha_\nu$ of one axis, four in all, are the involutions of the one-dimensional families; the next section collects the compositions.

The Composition Laws

For unit imaginaries $\nu,\mu$ the following laws hold, and they are the whole arithmetic of the family.

law statement
inner–inner $\iota_\nu\circ\iota_\mu=\iota_{\nu\mu}$
twist–twist $\alpha_\nu\circ\alpha_\mu=\iota_{\nu\mu}$
sign and inner ${}^{\natural}\circ\iota_\nu=\iota_\nu\circ{}^{\natural}=\alpha_\nu$
sign and twist ${}^{\natural}\circ\alpha_\nu=\alpha_\nu\circ{}^{\natural}=\iota_\nu$
reflection–reflection $\rho_\nu\circ\rho_\mu=\iota_{\nu\mu}$
reflection and inner $\rho_\nu\circ\iota_\mu=\iota_\nu\circ\rho_\mu$

The laws are proved by inserting the definitions and using the associativity and the unitarity of $\nu,\mu$. The product $\nu\mu$ of two unit imaginaries is a unit quaternion: it is again imaginary, hence the inner involution of a third axis, exactly when $\nu\perp\mu$, and it is $\pm e_0$, hence the identity, exactly when $\nu=\pm\mu$. The Klein four-group below is the first non-degenerate case $\nu\perp\mu$.

The Klein Four-Group of a Perpendicular Triple, and the Group of Order Eight

Fix a perpendicular triple $\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3$ of unit imaginaries with $\nu_1\nu_2=\nu_3$ (the units $e_1,e_2,e_3$ are the model). The three inner involutions satisfy $$ \iota_{\nu_1}\circ\iota_{\nu_2}=\iota_{\nu_3} , \quad \iota_{\nu_2}\circ\iota_{\nu_3}=\iota_{\nu_1} , \quad \iota_{\nu_3}\circ\iota_{\nu_1}=\iota_{\nu_2} , \qquad \iota_{\nu_1}\circ\iota_{\nu_2}\circ\iota_{\nu_3}=\operatorname{id}, $$ so that $$ \{id,\iota_{\nu_1},\iota_{\nu_2},\iota_{\nu_3}\} $$ is a Klein four-group. The natural sign commutes with each $\iota_{\nu_k}$ and is not one of them, and adjoining it gives the abelian group of order eight $$ G=\{id,{}^{\natural}\}\times\{id,\iota_{\nu_1},\iota_{\nu_2},\iota_{\nu_3}\}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3 , $$ whose seven non-identity elements are the sign ${}^{\natural}$, the three inner involutions $\iota_{\nu_1},\iota_{\nu_2},\iota_{\nu_3}$ (automorphisms) and the three twists $\alpha_{\nu_1},\alpha_{\nu_2},\alpha_{\nu_3}$ (anti-automorphisms).

Proposition (orbit sizes). The group $G$ fixes the scalar line pointwise and acts on the vector subspace by all the sign changes $q_k\mapsto\pm q_k$ of the coordinates $(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3)$, that is, by the group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$ of diagonal sign changes. Hence the orbit of $\tilde q$ has size $$ 1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 8 $$ according as the number of nonzero coordinates of its vector part is $0,1,2,3$. In particular the scalar line is the fixed set of $G$, and the generic element has the full orbit of eight.

Proof. The inner involution $\iota_{\nu_1}$ acts as the identity on $\mathbb{R}\nu_1$ and as $-1$ on $\nu_2,\nu_3$; $\iota_{\nu_2}$ as $-1$ on $\nu_1,\nu_3$; $\iota_{\nu_3}$ as $-1$ on $\nu_1,\nu_2$; and ${}^{\natural}$ as $-1$ on all three. The four together generate the three independent sign changes, so the stabiliser of $\tilde q$ is the subgroup whose sign changes preserve the support of $\mathbf q$, of order $8/2^{(\text{number of nonzero coordinates})}$, and the orbit size is the complementary power of two.

Summary

The natural sign is the one distinguished involution of $\mathbb{H}$. Each axis line of the vector subspace carries one inner involution $\iota_\nu$, fixing the axis line pointwise and negating its orthogonal complement, and one reflection $\rho_\nu=-\iota_\nu$, which is an automorphism only up to the sign. Each axis line carries also one involutive anti-automorphism beyond ${}^{\natural}$, the twist $\alpha_\nu={}^{\natural}\iota_\nu=\iota_\nu{}^{\natural}$; the involutive anti-automorphisms are exactly ${}^{\natural}$ and the $\alpha_\nu$, one per axis line. The composition laws reduce to $\iota_\nu\iota_\mu=\iota_{\nu\mu}$, $\alpha_\nu\alpha_\mu=\iota_{\nu\mu}$ and ${}^{\natural}\iota_\nu=\alpha_\nu$. On a perpendicular triple the three inner involutions form a Klein four-group, and adjoining ${}^{\natural}$ gives the abelian group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$, which fixes the scalar line and acts on the vector subspace by the diagonal sign changes; its orbits have sizes $1,2,4,8$ according to the support of the vector part.

Summary of Notation

symbol meaning
${}^{\natural}$ the natural conjugation $\tilde q\mapsto q_0-\mathbf q$, the distinguished anti-involution
$\iota_\nu$ the inner involution $\tilde q\mapsto\nu \tilde q\nu^{-1}$, one per axis line $\mathbb{R}\nu$
$\rho_\nu$ the reflection $-\iota_\nu$, an involution with $\rho_\nu(\tilde p\tilde q)=-\rho_\nu(\tilde p)\rho_\nu(\tilde q)$
$\alpha_\nu$ the twist ${}^{\natural}\iota_\nu$, the involutive anti-automorphism of the axis
$G$ the group $\{id,{}^{\natural}\}\times$ Klein four-group of a perpendicular triple, $\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$

Spinors as Peirce Coordinates in the Biquaternion Algebra

Introduction

A spinor of the biquaternion algebra is an element of one of the two spinor spaces $$ S_{+}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+}=\mathbb{B}_{++}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{-+},\qquad S_{-}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{-}=\mathbb{B}_{+-}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{--}. $$ The spinor is the algebra element itself. The Peirce coordinates are its coefficients in a basis; they describe the spinor, but they are not the spinor.

The root fixes the two spinor spaces and the two lines inside each. It does not fix a basis adapted to those lines, because the mixed line has no vector fixed by the root. The choice of a nonzero vector in the mixed line is the gauge. The article defines the spinor spaces, gives the eigenbasis of the root and the ladder, the gauge action and the invariants, and the chirality. An example is given at the end.

The Two Spinor Spaces

Definition. The two spinor spaces are $$ S_{+}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+},\qquad S_{-}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{-}. $$ A spinor is an element of $S_{+}$ or of $S_{-}$; a positive spinor is an element of $S_{+}$, a negative spinor an element of $S_{-}$.

The decomposition of an element. For every $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$, $$ \tilde Q=\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{+}+\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{-},\qquad \tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{\pm}\in S_{\pm}. $$ In a Peirce basis, $$ \tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{+}=Q_{++}\tilde\Pi_{+}+Q_{-+}E_{-+},\qquad \tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{-}=Q_{+-}E_{+-}+Q_{--}\tilde\Pi_{-}. $$ The first spinor uses the right index $+$, the second the right index $-$. The decomposition is unique, and $\tilde Q\mapsto(\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{+},\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{-})$ is an isomorphism of vector spaces $\mathbb{B}\to S_{+}\oplus S_{-}$. The sum is direct: if $\tilde Y=\tilde Y\tilde\Pi_{+}=\tilde Y\tilde\Pi_{-}$, then $\tilde Y=\tilde Y\tilde\Pi_{+}\tilde\Pi_{-}=0$.

The left action. The algebra acts on each spinor space by left multiplication. For $A\in\mathbb{B}$ and $\tilde\psi\in S_{\pm}$, the element $A\tilde\psi$ lies in $S_{\pm}$, because $S_{\pm}$ is closed under left multiplication by $\mathbb{B}$. This is the action used throughout; its spectral reading belongs to the operator category.

The Eigenbasis of the Root

The root fixes the two eigenvalues, not the basis. Let $\tilde\Xi=i\vec u$ with $\vec u\cdot\vec u=1$ and $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}=(e_{t}\pm\tilde\Xi)/2$. On the four lines, $$ L_{\tilde\Xi}\tilde\Pi_{+}=+\tilde\Pi_{+},\qquad L_{\tilde\Xi}\tilde\Pi_{-}=-\tilde\Pi_{-}, $$ $$ L_{\tilde\Xi}\mathbb{B}_{+-}=+\mathbb{B}_{+-},\qquad L_{\tilde\Xi}\mathbb{B}_{-+}=-\mathbb{B}_{-+}. $$ So each spinor space has the two simple eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$. The diagonal line is fixed by the root; the mixed line is not.

An eigenbasis. Choose any nonzero $\vec\Upsilon_{-+}\in\mathbb{B}_{-+}$ and any nonzero $\vec\Upsilon_{+-}\in\mathbb{B}_{+-}$. Then $$ (\tilde\Pi_{+},\vec\Upsilon_{-+})\quad\text{is a basis of }S_{+},\qquad (\vec\Upsilon_{+-},\tilde\Pi_{-})\quad\text{is a basis of }S_{-}. $$ These are eigenbases: the first vector of each pair has eigenvalue $+1$, the second $-1$.

The coordinates. A positive spinor has the expansion $$ \tilde\psi_{+}=a\tilde\Pi_{+}+c\vec\Upsilon_{-+},\qquad a,c\in\mathbb{C}. $$ The two eigenvalues are simple and do not depend on the choice of the mixed vector. No further spectral datum of the root is used in this article.

The ladder. Normalize the two mixed vectors so that $$ \vec\Upsilon_{+-}\vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tilde\Pi_{+},\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{-+}\vec\Upsilon_{+-}=\tilde\Pi_{-}. $$ Then left multiplication by $\vec\Upsilon_{+-}$ raises the eigenvalue by $2$ and left multiplication by $\vec\Upsilon_{-+}$ lowers it by $2$: $$ L_{\vec\Upsilon_{+-}}\vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tilde\Pi_{+},\qquad L_{\vec\Upsilon_{-+}}\tilde\Pi_{+}=\vec\Upsilon_{-+}, $$ and the other two products vanish. The diagonal vector of $S_{+}$ is $\tilde\Pi_{+}$, which is fixed by the root. The mixed vector is $\vec\Upsilon_{-+}$, which is not. The normalization of the pair is a gauge choice; without it the ladder is defined only up to scale.

The Gauge

A change of eigenbasis. Let $E_{st}$ and $F_{st}$ be two Peirce bases of the same family, with $F_{+-}=t^{-1}E_{+-}$ and $F_{-+}=tE_{-+}$. The spinor element is fixed. Its coordinates change by $$ a'=a,\qquad c'=t^{-1}c,\qquad b'=tb,\qquad d'=d, $$ where $$ \tilde\psi_{+}=a\tilde\Pi_{+}+cE_{-+},\qquad \tilde\psi_{-}=bE_{+-}+d\tilde\Pi_{-}. $$ The coordinates are the coefficients in the two eigenbases; the elements $\tilde\psi_{\pm}$ do not change.

The gauge is not a symmetry of the spinor. It changes the coordinates, not the element. A statement about a spinor is gauge-invariant only if it is a statement about the element.

The Hermitian gauge. If the root is Hermitian, $\vec u$ real, and the basis is Hermitian, $E_{st}^{*}=E_{ts}$, then $|t|=1$. In that gauge the Hermitian form $$ \langle\tilde\psi_{+},\tilde\psi_{+}\rangle_{*} =\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde\psi_{+}\tilde\psi_{+}^{*}\bigr) =\frac12\bigl(|a|^{2}+|c|^{2}\bigr) $$ is invariant. The same formula holds on $S_{-}$ with $(b,d)$.

The Invariants of a Single Spinor

The invariant coordinate. On $S_{+}$ the decomposition $$ S_{+}=\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi_{+}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{-+} $$ is fixed by the root: the diagonal line is the eigenspace of $L_{\tilde\Xi}$ with eigenvalue $+1$, and the mixed line is the eigenspace with eigenvalue $-1$. So the coefficient $a$ of $\tilde\Pi_{+}$ is intrinsic, and the coefficient $c$ of $\vec\Upsilon_{-+}$ is gauge-dependent. On $S_{-}$ the same statement holds with $d$ in place of $a$ and $b$ in place of $c$.

The pure and nilpotent parts. Every positive spinor splits uniquely as $$ \tilde\psi_{+}=\underbrace{a\tilde\Pi_{+}}_{\text{pure}}+\underbrace{c\vec\Upsilon_{-+}}_{\text{nilpotent}}. $$ The pure part is a multiple of the idempotent; the nilpotent part lies in the mixed line and squares to zero. The pure part is fixed as an element; the coordinate of the nilpotent part is multiplied by $t^{-1}$ in the new basis.

The invariants. A single positive spinor has two gauge invariants: the complex number $a$, and the truth value of $c=0$. The ratio $c/a$, when $a\ne0$, is not invariant; it is multiplied by $t^{-1}$. The conditions $$ a=0\quad\text{(the spinor is nilpotent)},\qquad c=0\quad\text{(the spinor is pure)},\qquad a=c=0\quad\text{(the spinor is zero)} $$ are gauge-invariant. In the Hermitian gauge the real number $|a|^{2}+|c|^{2}$ is a further invariant.

What is not an invariant. The pair $(a,c)$ is not; it is a coordinate description, and under the gauge it becomes $(a,t^{-1}c)$. A single spinor is not determined by its zero pattern alone: the invariant coordinate $a$ is intrinsic as well.

Chirality

The right index is the chirality. Right multiplication by the root acts by $$ \tilde\psi_{+}\tilde\Xi=+\tilde\psi_{+},\qquad \tilde\psi_{-}\tilde\Xi=-\tilde\psi_{-}. $$ So the two spinor spaces are the two eigenspaces of $R_{\tilde\Xi}$, with eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$. The projectors are right multiplication by the idempotents: $$ \tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{+}=\tilde Q\quad\text{on }S_{+},\qquad \tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{-}=\tilde Q\quad\text{on }S_{-}. $$ The chirality is the spinor-space label, not an extra grading.

Right Multiplication

Left multiplication preserves each spinor space; right multiplication preserves one only under a condition, and the mixed elements exchange the two spaces.

Right multiplication by $\tilde Q$ sends $S_{+}$ into $S_{+}$ exactly when $\tilde\Pi_{+}\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{-}=0$: the inclusion $S_{+}\tilde Q\subseteq S_{+}$ is equivalent to $\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+}\tilde Q\tilde\Pi_{-}=0$, because $e_{t}\in\mathbb{B}$. In a Peirce basis this is the condition $Q_{+-}=0$. The same computation gives $S_{-}\tilde Q\subseteq S_{-}$ exactly when $Q_{-+}=0$. So a spinor space is preserved on the right only by the elements with one mixed coordinate zero.

The mixed elements exchange the two spaces: $$ S_{+}\longrightarrow S_{-},\quad\xi\longmapsto\xi E_{-+}, \qquad S_{-}\longrightarrow S_{+},\quad\eta\longmapsto\eta E_{+-}. $$ The two maps are inverse, because $\xi E_{-+}E_{+-}=\xi\tilde\Pi_{+}=\xi$ and $\eta E_{+-}E_{-+}=\eta\tilde\Pi_{-}=\eta$. The exchange is not fixed by the root: under the gauge, $E_{-+}\mapsto tE_{-+}$ and $E_{+-}\mapsto t^{-1}E_{+-}$, so the maps are conjugated and the composite is unchanged. It is a bijection of subspaces, not a left multiplication and not an algebra automorphism.

The star does not exchange them. If the family is Hermitian, $\tilde\Pi_{+}^{*}=\tilde\Pi_{+}$, then $$ S_{+}^{*}=(\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+})^{*}=\tilde\Pi_{+}\mathbb{B}, $$ the right Peirce row of $\tilde\Pi_{+}$, which is not $S_{-}$. The two spinor spaces are closed on the left; the star of one is closed on the right. Chirality belongs to the left grouping, not to the involution.

What the Spinor Is Not

The spinor is not a state. A state is a ray in a space of states; the spinor is an element of the algebra. The physical reading of the spinor as a state is a separate construction.

The spinor is not a vector in an external space. It is an element of $\mathbb{B}$ lying in $S_{+}$ or $S_{-}$.

Example: The Root $i\vec e_{z}$

This example is given at the end because it is one point in the four-real-parameter space of roots, not a preferred one.

For $\tilde\Xi=i\vec e_{z}$, $$ \tilde\Pi_{+}=\frac{e_{t}+i\vec e_{z}}{2},\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{1}=\frac{i\vec e_{x}-\vec e_{y}}{2},\qquad \vec\Upsilon_{2}=\frac{i\vec e_{x}+\vec e_{y}}{2}, $$ with $\vec\Upsilon_{1}\vec\Upsilon_{2}=\tilde\Pi_{+}$. A positive spinor is $$ \tilde\psi_{+}=a\tilde\Pi_{+}+c\vec\Upsilon_{2}. $$ The eigenvalues of $L_{\tilde\Xi}$ are $+1$ for $a$ and $-1$ for $c$. Under the gauge $\vec\Upsilon_{1}\mapsto t^{-1}\vec\Upsilon_{1}$, $\vec\Upsilon_{2}\mapsto t\vec\Upsilon_{2}$, the coordinates become $a'=a$, $c'=t^{-1}c$. The invariants are $a$ and the truth value of $c=0$.

Summary

A spinor of $\mathbb{B}$ is an element of $S_{+}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+}$ or $S_{-}=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{-}$. The root fixes the eigenvalues $\pm1$ of $L_{\tilde\Xi}$; an eigenbasis requires a nonzero vector in each mixed line, and the choice is the gauge. The ladder is left multiplication by the mixed vectors, normalized by $\vec\Upsilon_{+-}\vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tilde\Pi_{+}$. The gauge acts by $a'=a$, $c'=t^{-1}c$, $b'=tb$, $d'=d$, and leaves the spinor fixed. The invariant coordinate $a$ (or $d$) and the vanishing of $c$ (or $b$) are the invariants; the ratio is not. Chirality is the right index, $R_{\tilde\Xi}=\pm1$. Right multiplication exchanges the two spaces through the mixed elements, and its star is a right Peirce row, not the other space.

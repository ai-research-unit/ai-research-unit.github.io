The Octonion Quadratic Map and the Non-Associative Julia Sets

Introduction

The octonions are the largest normed division algebra over the reals and the only one whose multiplication is not associative. A quadratic map of the octonions therefore raises a question that the smaller systems do not: what exactly is meant by the iterate of $x\mapsto x^2+c$ when the product is not associative? This article defines the octonion quadratic map $f_{\tilde c}(\tilde o)=\tilde o^2+\tilde c$, proves that its iterates, its escaping set and its filled Julia set are nevertheless well defined, identifies the associative subalgebra in which every orbit lives, and separates the part of the subject that is genuinely non-associative from the part that is not. The genuinely non-associative part is the maps defined by words in three independent octonion directions, where two bracketings of the same word give two different maps and two different Julia sets; this is where the conventions enter, and it is the reason the article is placed before the connectedness locus, the orbit set and the iterated function systems.

The octonion algebra, its conjugation, the norm, the associator and Artin's theorem are the subject of Octonion Algebra and Octonion Norm and Invertibility; they are used here and not restated. The complex quadratic family, the Fatou and Julia sets of a complex polynomial and the Mandelbrot set are the subject of The Julia Sets of a Complex Polynomial and The Mandelbrot Set and the Quadratic Family; the present article is the octonion analogue and cites them for every complex statement it invokes. The quaternionic and split-quaternionic quadratic families are the subject of The Quaternion Quadratic Map and Its Julia Sets and The Split-Quaternion Quadratic Family and Its Julia Sets, and the biquaternion families of The Biquaternion Quadratic Map and Its Julia Sets. The connectedness locus itself is the subject of The Octonion Mandelbrot Set, the orbit set and its closure of The Associator and the Closure of the Octonion Orbits, and the linear systems of The Octonion Iterated Function Systems.

Throughout, $\mathbb{O}$ is the algebra of octonions with basis $e_0=1,e_1,\dots,e_7$, a generic element is $\tilde o=o_0e_0+\cdots+o_7e_7$, its conjugate is $\tilde o^{\natural}=o_0e_0-o_1e_1-\cdots-o_7e_7$, its norm is $\lvert\tilde o\rvert^2=\tilde o\tilde o^{\natural}=o_0^2+\cdots+o_7^2$, its real part is $\operatorname{Re}\tilde o=o_0$, its vector part is $\mathbf o=o_1e_1+\cdots+o_7e_7$, and the associator is $[\tilde o,\tilde p,\tilde r]=(\tilde o\tilde p)\tilde r-\tilde o(\tilde p\tilde r)$. A unit imaginary octonion is a $u$ with $\operatorname{Re}u=0$ and $\lvert u\rvert=1$. The parameter of the family is the fixed octonion $\tilde c$, the variable is $\tilde o$, and the map is

$$ f_{\tilde c}(\tilde o) = \tilde o^2 + \tilde c, \qquad \tilde o^2 = \tilde o\tilde o . $$

The Map and the Bracketing of Its Iterates

Definition. The octonion quadratic map with parameter $\tilde c$ is the map $f_{\tilde c}:\mathbb{O}\to\mathbb{O}$ displayed above; its iterates are $f_{\tilde c}^0=\mathrm{id}$ and $f_{\tilde c}^{n+1}=f_{\tilde c}\circ f_{\tilde c}^n$, the orbit of $\tilde o$ is the sequence $\tilde o_0=\tilde o$, $\tilde o_{n+1}=\tilde o_n^2+\tilde c$, and the critical point is $\tilde o=0$, whose orbit is the critical orbit $0,\tilde c,\tilde c^2+\tilde c,\dots$.

Remark (the map is a word, not merely a formula). The algebra of octonions contains $x^2$ but not the general monomial $x^2$ written as a product of copies of $x$ with inserted constants, and it does not license the associative rearrangement of a formula. The complex expression $z^2+c$ therefore lifts to the octonions in more than one way as soon as the formula contains a product of three independent factors. The map $f_{\tilde c}(\tilde o)=\tilde o^2+\tilde c$ is the simplest lift, the squaring of a single element; the more general quadratic words contain the variable twice and the constants in a prescribed order, and different bracketings of the same word are in general different maps. The distinction between the map (a word) and its iterate (a composition) is what the next two statements make precise.

Theorem (Artin, quoted). Every subalgebra of $\mathbb{O}$ generated by two elements is associative; equivalently, the associator vanishes whenever two of its arguments are equal. This is Artin's theorem of Octonion Algebra.

Theorem (the orbit lies in an associative subalgebra). For every $\tilde o$ and $\tilde c$ the orbit of $\tilde o$ under $f_{\tilde c}$ is contained in the subalgebra $\mathbb{R}[\tilde o,\tilde c]$ generated by $\tilde o$ and $\tilde c$, which is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$, to $\mathbb{C}$ or to $\mathbb{H}$, and the orbit is the orbit computed inside that associative algebra. In particular the iterate $f_{\tilde c}^n(\tilde o)$ is well defined, independent of any bracketing of the expansion of $f_{\tilde c}^n$, and agrees with the value computed in $\mathbb{R}[\tilde o,\tilde c]$.

Proof. The subalgebra $\mathbb{R}[\tilde o,\tilde c]$ is closed under multiplication and contains $\tilde o$ and $\tilde c$, so $\tilde o^2+\tilde c\in\mathbb{R}[\tilde o,\tilde c]$; by induction the whole orbit lies in it. Artin's theorem makes it associative, so the expansion of any word in the two generators is unambiguous there. The classification up to isomorphism is the proposition of Octonion Algebra on the subalgebras generated by two elements: the two dimensions are $1$, $2$ or $4$, giving $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$ or $\mathbb{H}$. Finally the composition $f_{\tilde c}^n$ is evaluated in $\mathbb{R}[\tilde o,\tilde c]$, and in an associative algebra the expansion of a composition is independent of the order in which the products are grouped. $\square$

Remark (the menu's premise, corrected). The premise that the iterate of the octonion quadratic map is not well defined without a bracketing convention is true of the word, not of the composition. The composition $f_{\tilde c}\circ f_{\tilde c}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\tilde c}$ is a function, and the displayed theorem shows that its value at $\tilde o$ is unambiguous. What requires a convention is the lift of a complex polynomial formula to a non-associative word, and hence the choice of the map $f_{\tilde c}$ itself when it is not the pure squaring. The point is recorded here, in the .context and in the report, because it changes the reading of the subcategory: the genuinely non-associative octonion dynamics is in the word-defined maps and in the orbit sets, not in the iterates of the squaring map.

Definition (the bracketed lift). Let $W(\hat o)$ be a non-associative polynomial word in the indeterminate $\hat o$ and the constants $\tilde a_1,\dots,\tilde a_k\in\mathbb{O}$, with $\hat o$ occurring twice. A bracketing of $W$ is a choice of parenthesisation of its products; two bracketings $W_1,W_2$ are associatively equivalent when they give the same value for every $\hat o$ in every associative algebra, equivalently for every complex $\hat o$. The left-nested and right-nested readings of the word $\hat o\tilde a\tilde b$ are

$$ W_L(\hat o) = (\hat o\tilde a)\tilde b, \qquad W_R(\hat o) = \hat o(\tilde a\tilde b); $$

over $\mathbb{C}$ they agree, and over $\mathbb{O}$ their difference is the associator,

$$ W_L(\hat o) - W_R(\hat o) = [\hat o,\tilde a,\tilde b]. $$

Example (the two conventions differ). For $\hat o=e_1$, $\tilde a=e_2$, $\tilde b=e_4$ one has $(\hat o\tilde a)\tilde b=e_3e_4=e_7$ and $\hat o(\tilde a\tilde b)=e_1e_6=-e_7$, so the two readings differ by $2e_7=[e_1,e_2,e_4]\neq0$; for the triple $e_1,e_2,e_3$ of a Fano line they agree, because $e_1,e_2,e_3$ span a quaternion subalgebra. The two maps $f_L(\hat o)=(\hat o\tilde a)\tilde b+\hat o^2$ and $f_R(\hat o)=\hat o(\tilde a\tilde b)+\hat o^2$ have the same complexification and different values on $\mathbb{O}$: at $\hat o=e_1$ they differ by $2e_7$, of norm $2$. Their filled Julia sets are therefore in general different, and the question of their relation is open.

The Escape Criterion and the Filled Julia Set

Theorem (the escape criterion). If $\lvert\tilde o\rvert>2$ and $\lvert\tilde o\rvert\geq\lvert\tilde c\rvert$ then

$$ \lvert\tilde o^2+\tilde c\rvert \geq \lvert\tilde o\rvert^2-\lvert\tilde c\rvert \geq \lvert\tilde o\rvert(\lvert\tilde o\rvert-1) > \lvert\tilde o\rvert , $$

so $\lvert\tilde o_{n+1}\rvert>\lvert\tilde o_n\rvert$ and the norms tend to $+\infty$ monotonically. Consequently every orbit that leaves the closed ball of radius $2$ escapes, and the filled Julia set is contained in that ball.

Proof. The norm is multiplicative, $\lvert\tilde o^2\rvert=\lvert\tilde o\rvert^2$, by Octonion Norm and Invertibility, and the triangle inequality gives $\lvert\tilde o^2+\tilde c\rvert\geq\lvert\tilde o\rvert^2-\lvert\tilde c\rvert$. If $\lvert\tilde o\rvert>2$ then $\lvert\tilde o\rvert(\lvert\tilde o\rvert-1)>\lvert\tilde o\rvert$, so it remains to note that the hypotheses $\lvert\tilde o\rvert\geq\lvert\tilde c\rvert$ and $\lvert\tilde o\rvert>2$ are preserved: the displayed inequality gives $\lvert\tilde o_1\rvert\geq\lvert\tilde o\rvert(\lvert\tilde o\rvert-1)>\lvert\tilde o\rvert$, and the norm increases, so the same hypotheses hold at the next step. $\square$

Definition. For $\tilde c\in\mathbb{O}$ the filled Julia set is

$$ K_{\tilde c} = \{\tilde o\in\mathbb{O} : \text{the orbit } (\tilde o_n) \text{ is bounded}\}, $$

a compact subset of the closed ball of radius $2$ by the escape criterion, and the Julia set is $J_{\tilde c}=\partial K_{\tilde c}$. The escaping set is the complement $\mathbb{O}\setminus K_{\tilde c}$, and the escape time of an escaping point is the number of iterates needed to leave the ball of radius $2$. The connectedness locus is the set of parameters $\tilde c$ for which the critical orbit is bounded.

Remark (the definition of the Julia set). For the complex quadratic family the three sets $\partial K_c$, the set of points at which the iterates fail to be normal, and the closure of the repelling periodic points coincide, by The Julia Sets of a Complex Polynomial. For the octonion map the natural object is the filled Julia set $K_{\tilde c}$, defined by the boundedness of the orbit; the boundary $J_{\tilde c}=\partial K_{\tilde c}$ is taken as the octonion Julia set, and the general theory of normal families for polynomial maps of $\mathbb{R}^8$ is not developed here. On each associative slice, to which the next section reduces the matter, the two definitions agree with the complex ones.

The Reduction to the Associative Slices

Theorem (the real-parameter family is a body of revolution). Let $\tilde c=c\,e_0$ be real. For $\tilde o=x e_0+\mathbf o$ with $x=\operatorname{Re}\tilde o$ real and $\rho=\lvert\mathbf o\rvert$, choose a unit imaginary $u$ with $\mathbf o=\rho u$ when $\rho>0$. Then the orbit of $\tilde o$ lies in the plane $\operatorname{span}(e_0,u)\cong\mathbb{C}$, and it is the orbit of the complex number $x+i\rho$ under the complex map $z\mapsto z^2+c$. Consequently

$$ K_c = \bigl\{\,\tilde o : \operatorname{Re}\tilde o + i\,\lvert\mathbf o\rvert \in K_c^{\mathbb{C}}\,\bigr\}, $$

the body of revolution of the complex filled Julia set $K_c^{\mathbb{C}}$ about the real axis, and $J_c=\partial K_c$ is the corresponding revolution of the complex Julia set. The critical orbit is real, so the parameter $c$ belongs to the connectedness locus exactly when $c\in[-2,1/4]$.

Proof. Since $u$ is an imaginary unit, $u^2=-1$, and $(\rho u)^2=-\rho^2$; hence $\tilde o^2=(x+\rho u)^2=x^2 e_0+2x\rho u-\rho^2 e_0=(x^2-\rho^2)e_0+2x\rho u$, so $f_c(\tilde o)=(x^2-\rho^2+c)e_0+2x\rho u$ lies in $\operatorname{span}(e_0,u)$, and reading $x+i\rho$ in that plane gives $z^2+c$. By induction the whole orbit stays in the plane and is the complex orbit. Therefore $\tilde o\in K_c$ exactly when $x+i\rho\in K_c^{\mathbb{C}}$, which is the displayed description; the critical point has $x=\rho=0$ and the critical orbit is the real orbit of $0$, which is bounded exactly for $c\in[-2,1/4]$ by the real quadratic family article. $\square$

Remark (the non-real parameter, and the genuine hypercomplexity). For a parameter $\tilde c$ with a nonzero vector part the orbit of a general point lies in the quaternion subalgebra generated by the point and the parameter, not in a single complex plane; the octonion filled Julia set is then a union of genuinely quaternionic pieces, and the description of the slices belongs to the quaternionic theory. What the octonions add beyond the quaternions is not in the squaring map at all, by the theorem on the orbit: for a single point the orbit never uses three independent directions. The genuinely octonionic phenomena are in the word-defined maps and in the orbit sets, treated in the next section and in The Associator and the Closure of the Octonion Orbits.

Example (the slices). For real $c>1/4$ the complex filled Julia set $K_c^{\mathbb{C}}$ is a Cantor set placed off the real axis, and its body of revolution in $\mathbb{R}^8$ is the corresponding family of six-spheres, of topological dimension $6$; for $c<-2$ the complex Cantor set lies on the real axis, and its revolution degenerates to that Cantor set itself, of topological dimension $0$; for $c=0$ the complex set is the closed unit disk and its revolution is the closed unit ball $\{\tilde o : \lvert\tilde o\rvert\leq1\}$ of $\mathbb{R}^8$; for $c=-2$ the complex set is the interval $[-2,2]$ on the real axis, and its revolution is that interval again, $\{\tilde o : -2\leq\operatorname{Re}\tilde o\leq2,\ \mathbf o=0\}=[-2,2]$. These are the octonion Julia sets of the real-parameter family: bodies of revolution of the classical complex pictures, degenerate when the complex picture lies on the axis.

The Non-Associative Maps and What Is Known

Definition (the readings and the orbit set). Let $W$ be a quadratic word with the readings $f_1,\dots,f_r$, one for each bracketing of $W$. The orbit set of $\tilde o$ at depth $n$ is

$$ W^{[n]}(\tilde o) = \{\, f_{c_1}\circ f_{c_2}\circ\cdots\circ f_{c_n}(\tilde o) : c_i\in\{1,\dots,r\} \,\}, $$

the set of the $r^n$ values of the compositions of $n$ readings, and the orbit set of $\tilde o$ is the union of the $W^{[n]}(\tilde o)$ over $n$. The limit set is the closure of the orbit set when the orbit set is bounded. For the pure squaring map, which has a single reading, the set $W^{[n]}(\tilde o)$ is a single point for every $n$, by the theorem on the orbit; for a word with three independent directions and two readings it can contain up to $2^n$ points. The larger set of the values of all bracketings of the $n$-fold substituted word is a second object, studied in The Associator and the Closure of the Octonion Orbits, which is the next article.

Proof (of the single-point case). Every bracketing of the $n$-fold squaring word is a word in $\tilde o$ and $\tilde c$ alone, hence lies in the associative subalgebra $\mathbb{R}[\tilde o,\tilde c]$, where all bracketings agree. $\square$

Remark (the literature). The octonionic Julia sets have been computed and displayed, for a bracketed convention, by Griffin and Joshi in a series of computational papers, and the numerical pictures of the octonionic Mandelbrot and Julia sets are due to them and to the subsequent literature; those works are computational and their pictures are not theorems. The questions they raise — whether two bracketings give the same Julia set, whether the limit set of a word-defined orbit has a dimension strictly between the dimensions of its complex slices, and whether the connectedness locus of the word-defined family has interior in the extra directions — are open, and they are recorded in the .context and in the report rather than answered here. The two readings $W_L,W_R$ of the previous section give different maps and hence in general different filled Julia sets; the precise relation of the two is the first open question of the subcategory.

Remark (the octonion $\Omega$-limit set). The analogue of the $\omega$-limit set of a point under a word-defined iterate is the set of accumulation points of the orbit set; it is a set-valued dynamical object, and its study is the subject of The Associator and the Closure of the Octonion Orbits, which follows. The present article supplies the map $f_{\tilde c}$, the escape criterion, the filled Julia set and the split into the associative squaring map and the word-defined maps; the orbit set is that article's.

Summary

The octonion quadratic map is $f_{\tilde c}(\tilde o)=\tilde o^2+\tilde c$, with critical point $0$ and critical orbit $0,\tilde c,\tilde c^2+\tilde c,\dots$. Although the octonions are not associative, the map is well defined and so are all its iterates: the orbit of any point lies in the subalgebra generated by the point and the parameter, which is associative by Artin's theorem, and the iterate agrees with the value computed in that subalgebra. The genuinely non-associative part of the subject is not the iterate but the lift: a quadratic word with three independent directions has several bracketings, two of which differ by the associator $[\hat o,\tilde a,\tilde b]$, and the corresponding maps have in general different Julia sets. The escape criterion $\lvert\tilde o\rvert>2$, $\lvert\tilde o\rvert\geq\lvert\tilde c\rvert$ implies $\lvert\tilde o^2+\tilde c\rvert>\lvert\tilde o\rvert$, so the filled Julia set is compact and contained in the ball of radius $2$, and the Julia set is its boundary. For a real parameter the filled Julia set is the body of revolution of the complex one about the real axis, and the connectedness locus of the critical orbit contains exactly the real parameters in $[-2,1/4]$; for a general parameter the orbits are quaternionic, and the extra octonion directions carry no new dynamics for the squaring map.

The connectedness locus is the subject of The Octonion Mandelbrot Set; the orbit set, its closure and its dimension are the subject of The Associator and the Closure of the Octonion Orbits; the linear systems are the subject of The Octonion Iterated Function Systems. The complex and quaternionic theories are those of The Julia Sets of a Complex Polynomial, The Mandelbrot Set and the Quadratic Family, The Quaternion Quadratic Map and Its Julia Sets and The Split-Quaternion Quadratic Family and Its Julia Sets.

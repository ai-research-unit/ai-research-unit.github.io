The Octonion Iterated Function Systems

Introduction

An octonion iterated function system is a finite list of contractions of $\mathbb{O}=\mathbb{R}^8$, and the article studies the systems built from the two natural families of linear maps, the left multiplications $\tilde o\mapsto\tilde a\tilde o$ and the right multiplications $\tilde o\mapsto\tilde o\tilde b$. Each is a similarity of $\mathbb{R}^8$ with ratio the norm of the multiplier, because the norm of the octonions is multiplicative; consequently an octonion IFS of similarities has a unique compact attractor and a similarity dimension given by the Moran formula, exactly as in the real and complex cases. The octonionic content is not in the dimension law, which depends only on the ratios, but in the arrangement of the pieces: the left and the right multiplication do not commute, and their commutator is the associator, so the semigroup generated by the two kinds of multiplications is larger than either family alone and its elements are neither left nor right multiplications. The attractor of a mixed system is therefore governed by the associator, while its dimension is not; this is the precise sense in which the menu's phrase "attractors governed by the associator" is read here, and it is what distinguishes the octonionic systems from the associative quaternionic ones.

The general theory of the iterated function systems — the Hutchinson operator on the space of nonempty compact sets, its contraction, the open set condition and the Moran–Hutchinson dimension formula — is the subject of Fractal Geometry of Part IV, and the coding of the attractor by the shift space is worked there and in Self-Similar Subsets of the Real Line and Iterated Function Systems on the Real Line of the present subcategory; all are used and none is restated. The octonion algebra, the associator, its alternation and Artin's theorem are those of Octonion Algebra and Octonion Norm and Invertibility, and the exceptional symmetries of the octonions are those of Octonions and Exceptional Geometry. The associative comparison is The Quaternion Iterated Function Systems, the complex systems are Iterated Function Systems in the Complex Plane, and the indefinite and zero-divisor cases are The Split-Quaternion Iterated Function Systems, The Biquaternion Iterated Function Systems and The Split-Biquaternion Iterated Function Systems.

Throughout, a map of the octonions is a map $g:\mathbb{O}\to\mathbb{O}$, the left multiplication by $\tilde a$ is $L_{\tilde a}(\tilde o)=\tilde a\tilde o$, the right multiplication is $R_{\tilde b}(\tilde o)=\tilde o\tilde b$, and a two-sided multiplication is $o\mapsto\tilde a\tilde o\tilde b$. A map is a similarity of ratio $r$ when $\lvert g(\tilde o)-g(\tilde p)\rvert=r\lvert\tilde o-\tilde p\rvert$ for all $\tilde o,\tilde p$; it is a contraction when $r<1$. An iterated function system is a finite family $g_1,\dots,g_m$ of contractions, and its attractor is the unique nonempty compact $K$ with $K=\bigcup_{i=1}^m g_i(K)$, whose existence and uniqueness are Hutchinson's theorem of Fractal Geometry; the similarity dimension of the system is the $s$ with $\sum_i r_i^s=1$ when all $r_i<1$.

The Similarities of the Octonions

Theorem (the linear similarities). Let $\tilde a,\tilde b,\tilde c\in\mathbb{O}$ with $\tilde a,\tilde b\neq0$, and let $g(\tilde o)=\tilde a\tilde o\tilde b+\tilde c$. Then $g$ is a similarity of $\mathbb{R}^8$ with ratio $\lvert\tilde a\rvert\lvert\tilde b\rvert$, and every similarity of $\mathbb{R}^8$ of this form is the composition of a left multiplication, a right multiplication and a translation. In particular the left and right multiplications are similarities of ratios $\lvert\tilde a\rvert$ and $\lvert\tilde b\rvert$, and every similarity of the system is the scalar $\lvert\tilde a\rvert\lvert\tilde b\rvert$ times an orthogonal map of $\mathbb{R}^8$.

Proof. By the multiplicativity of the norm of Octonion Norm and Invertibility, $\lvert\tilde a\tilde o\tilde b\rvert=\lvert\tilde a\rvert\lvert\tilde o\rvert\lvert\tilde b\rvert$ for every parenthesisation, so $\lvert g(\tilde o)-g(\tilde p)\rvert=\lvert\tilde a(\tilde o-\tilde p)\tilde b\rvert=\lvert\tilde a\rvert\lvert\tilde b\rvert\lvert\tilde o-\tilde p\rvert$. Write $\tilde a=\lvert\tilde a\rvert u$, $\tilde b=\lvert\tilde b\rvert v$ with $u,v$ unit octonions; the map $\tilde o\mapsto u\tilde o v$ is norm-preserving and linear, hence orthogonal, and $g$ is $\lvert\tilde a\rvert\lvert\tilde b\rvert$ times it followed by the translation. $\square$

Corollary. Every finite system of maps $\tilde o\mapsto\tilde a_i\tilde o\tilde b_i+\tilde c_i$ with $\lvert\tilde a_i\rvert\lvert\tilde b_i\rvert<1$ is an iterated function system of similarities of $\mathbb{R}^8$, and its attractor is the unique nonempty compact set $K$ with $K=\bigcup_i g_i(K)$.

Corollary (the dimension). If the system satisfies the open set condition, its attractor has box dimension and Hausdorff dimension equal to the similarity dimension $s$, the solution of $\sum_i(\lvert\tilde a_i\rvert\lvert\tilde b_i\rvert)^s=1$; in particular the dimension depends only on the norms of the multipliers.

Proof. Each generator is a similarity, and the system satisfies the hypotheses of the Moran–Hutchinson theorem of Fractal Geometry; the formula is that theorem's. $\square$

The Left and Right Systems and the Associator

Theorem (the semigroup of multiplications). Let $L_{\tilde a}$ and $R_{\tilde b}$ be a left and a right multiplication. Then

$$ L_{\tilde a}\circ R_{\tilde b}(\tilde o)=\tilde a(\tilde o\tilde b), \qquad R_{\tilde b}\circ L_{\tilde a}(\tilde o)=(\tilde a\tilde o)\tilde b, $$

and the two differ by the associator,

$$ L_{\tilde a}\circ R_{\tilde b}-R_{\tilde b}\circ L_{\tilde a}=[\tilde a,\tilde o,\tilde b], $$

evaluated at $\tilde o$; the difference is a linear function of $\tilde o$ which vanishes identically when $\tilde b$ lies in the complex plane spanned by $1$ and $\tilde a$ (in particular when either multiplier is real), and is nonzero for suitable $\tilde o$ otherwise, as in the example of the proof. Both composites are similarities of ratio $\lvert\tilde a\rvert\lvert\tilde b\rvert$.

Proof. The compositions are displayed; their difference at $\tilde o$ is $(\tilde a\tilde o)\tilde b-\tilde a(\tilde o\tilde b)=[\tilde a,\tilde o,\tilde b]$ by the definition of the associator, and the associator is alternating and trilinear. As a function of $\tilde o$ the difference is linear, and it vanishes identically when $\tilde b$ lies in the complex plane spanned by $1$ and $\tilde a$ (in particular when $\tilde a$ or $\tilde b$ is real), because then $\tilde a,\tilde b,\tilde o$ span at most two independent directions; for suitable $\tilde o$ it is nonzero otherwise, for instance $[e_1,e_4,e_2]=(e_1e_4)e_2-e_1(e_4e_2)=e_5e_2+e_1e_6=-e_7-e_7=-2e_7\neq0$. Both composites have the same ratio by the previous theorem. $\square$

Remark (the trap: the semigroup is not the set of left and right maps). The family $\{\tilde o\mapsto\tilde a\tilde o+\tilde c\}$ of left-affine maps is not closed under composition, because the composite of $L_{\tilde a}+\tilde c$ and $L_{\tilde a'}+\tilde c'$ is $\tilde o\mapsto\tilde a(\tilde a'\tilde o+\tilde c')+\tilde c=\tilde a(\tilde a'\tilde o)+\tilde a\tilde c'+\tilde c$, whose linear part $\tilde o\mapsto\tilde a(\tilde a'\tilde o)$ is a left multiplication only when $[\tilde a,\tilde a',\tilde o]=0$ for all $\tilde o$, that is, when $\tilde a,\tilde a'$ lie in a common copy of $\mathbb{C}$. The same holds for the right-affine maps, and a system mixing the two kinds generates a semigroup of similarities whose elements are not left or right multiplications. The associator is the measure of this failure, and it is what makes the octonion systems different from the complex and quaternion ones.

Corollary (the difference of two systems). Two iterated function systems that agree on the level of the generators — the same list of multipliers, read once on the left and once on the right — have the same similarity dimension and in general different attractors, and the difference between corresponding pieces is a linear image of the associator.

The Attractors, Their Coding and Their Dimension

Theorem (address map). Let $g_1,\dots,g_m$ be an octonion system of similarities with ratios $r_i<1$, put $r=\max_ir_i$, and let $\Sigma=\{1,\dots,m\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ be the shift space with the metric $\rho(\omega,\tau)=r^{\,n(\omega,\tau)}$, where $n(\omega,\tau)$ is the least index at which the two sequences differ. Then the address map

$$ \pi(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty} g_{\omega_1}\circ g_{\omega_2}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\omega_n}(\tilde o_0), \qquad \tilde o_0\in\mathbb{O}\text{ arbitrary}, $$

is well defined, continuous, Lipschitz with respect to $\rho$ (with a constant depending only on the diameters of the pieces and on $r$), surjective onto the attractor, and satisfies the self-similarity identity

$$ \pi = g_{\omega_1}\circ\pi\circ\sigma, $$

with $\sigma$ the shift. If the system satisfies the open set condition — there is an open set $V$ with the union of the $g_i(V)$ contained in $V$ and pairwise disjoint — then $\pi$ is injective off a countable set of ambiguous addresses, and the dimension of $K$ is the similarity dimension.

Proof. The sequence is Cauchy because the ratios multiply; its limit is independent of $\tilde o_0$ because the composites are contractions with ratios $\prod r_{\omega_j}$; the identity and the surjectivity are the general theory of Fractal Geometry read with the octonion similarities, and the injectivity off a countable set and the dimension are the one-dimensional arguments of Iterated Function Systems on the Real Line carried over verbatim, since only the completeness of $\mathbb{R}^8$, the multiplicativity of the norm and the strict contraction of the generators are used. $\square$

Remark (non-associativity is invisible to the dimension and visible to the pieces). The dimension formula uses only the ratios $\lvert\tilde a_i\rvert\lvert\tilde b_i\rvert$, which are norms and are independent of the bracketing; the associator moves the pieces of the attractor inside the sphere of the appropriate radius and changes the geometry of $K$, not its dimension. This is the exact parallel of the sphere theorem of The Associator and the Closure of the Octonion Orbits, in the contracting rather than the expanding regime.

Example (the octonion Cantor set). The two maps $g_1(\tilde o)=\frac14\tilde o$ and $g_2(\tilde o)=\frac14\tilde o+\tilde c$ with $\tilde c$ of norm $1$ and $\lvert\tilde c\rvert$ large enough that the pieces are disjoint form a system with ratios $\frac14,\frac14$ and similarity dimension $s$ with $2\cdot4^{-s}=1$, that is $s=\frac12$; the attractor is an octonion Cantor set of Hausdorff dimension $\frac12$ in $\mathbb{R}^8$. With the ratio $\frac1m$ and $m$ maps the dimension is $1$; with three maps of ratio $\frac12$ it is $\log3/\log2=1.5849\ldots$, the dimension of the Sierpinski gasket, now a self-similar subset of $\mathbb{R}^8$.

Example (a mixed left–right system). The two maps

$$ g_1(\tilde o)=\tfrac12\tilde o, \qquad g_2(\tilde o)=\tfrac12\,\tilde o\,e_1+\tilde c $$

have ratios $\frac12$ and $\frac12$, so the similarity dimension is $1$; the second piece is the first rotated by the right multiplication by $e_1$ and translated. The two pieces are disjoint for $\lvert\tilde c\rvert$ large, so the attractor is a self-similar set of dimension $1$ in $\mathbb{R}^8$, a curve-like Cantor set. Adding the left multiplication $g_3(\tilde o)=\frac12e_2\tilde o$, the composite $g_3\circ g_2$ has the linear part $\tilde o\mapsto\frac14e_2(\tilde o\,e_1)$, which is neither a left nor a right multiplication; its difference from the two-sided map $\tilde o\mapsto\frac14(e_2\tilde o)e_1$ is the associator $\frac14[e_2,\tilde o,e_1]$, nonzero for suitable $\tilde o$ — for $\tilde o=e_4$ it is $\frac14[e_2,e_4,e_1]=\frac12e_7$. Thus a system mixing the two kinds of multiplication generates composites that are similarities but are not of the two-sided form, and the associator measures the gap.

Example (the exceptional Sierpinski system). The seven maps $g_i(\tilde o)=\frac13 e_i\tilde o$, $i=1,\dots,7$, have equal ratio $\frac13$ and similarity dimension the solution of $7\cdot3^{-s}=1$, namely $s=\log7/\log3=1.7712\ldots$; the multipliers are the seven imaginary units, whose products are read from the Fano plane, and the attractor is a self-similar set carried by the exceptional geometry of the octonions. The relation of this system to the Moufang loop of the units and to the Fano triples, and the self-similar sets that the exceptional symmetries carry, are the subject of Octonions and Exceptional Geometry; what is proved here is only that the seven maps are similarities of the stated ratios, so that the system has the stated similarity dimension.

Comparison with the Other Systems

The complex and quaternion cases. For $\mathbb{C}$ the maps are $z\mapsto az+b$ with $\lvert a\rvert<1$, the classical self-similar sets of the plane with their similarity dimension, and the coding by the shift is the complex instance of the general theory; this is Iterated Function Systems in the Complex Plane. For $\mathbb{H}$ the left and the right multiplications commute, because $\mathbb{H}$ is associative: $a(qb)=(aq)b$ for all $a,b,q\in\mathbb{H}$, so the associator obstruction of the previous section is absent, and a mixed system reduces to the two-sided multiplications $q\mapsto aqb+c$, which form the quaternionic similarities of The Quaternion Iterated Function Systems. The quaternion system is the largest associative one, and its pieces are placed by the rotations of $SO(4)$; the octonion system is the first in which the placement itself is non-associative.

The indefinite and zero-divisor cases. For the split-quaternions, the biquaternions and the split-biquaternions the norm is indefinite or has zero divisors, so a map of the form $g(\tilde o)=\tilde a\tilde o\tilde b+\tilde c$ need not be a contraction in the Euclidean norm, the pieces may cross the null cone, and the open set condition can fail; these are the subjects of The Split-Quaternion Iterated Function Systems, The Biquaternion Iterated Function Systems and The Split-Biquaternion Iterated Function Systems. The octonion system is the last of the normed division systems, and it is the only one in which associativity is lost while the norm remains definite, so it is the unique case in which the dimension theory is untouched and the placement theory is not.

Remark (the pattern). For every normed division system the iterated function systems of similarities have attractors whose dimension is the similarity dimension of the ratios, and the geometry of the pieces is read from the orthogonal group: $O(2)$ for the complex case, $SO(4)$ for the quaternions, $O(8)$ for the octonions. What changes at the last rung is that the products of the multipliers, and hence the placement of the composite pieces, depend on the bracketing, so that two systems with the same list of multipliers can have different attractors and the same dimension.

Summary

An octonion iterated function system is a finite list of similarities $\tilde o\mapsto\tilde a_i\tilde o\tilde b_i+\tilde c_i$ with ratios $r_i=\lvert\tilde a_i\rvert\lvert\tilde b_i\rvert<1$, the two-sided form covering the left and right multiplications; each generator is a similarity because the norm of $\mathbb{O}$ is multiplicative for every parenthesisation, so the Hutchinson theory of Fractal Geometry gives a unique compact attractor and the Moran formula $\sum r_i^s=1$ gives its dimension under the open set condition. The dimension depends only on the norms and is therefore blind to the non-associativity; the placement of the pieces is not. The left and right multiplications do not commute, their commutator being the associator $[\tilde a,\tilde o,\tilde b]$, so the semigroup generated by a mixed system is strictly larger than the union of the left and right maps, the left-affine family is not closed under composition, and two systems with the same multipliers and different bracketings have the same dimension and in general different attractors. Examples give an octonion Cantor set of dimension $\frac12$, a Sierpinski set of dimension $\log3/\log2$, a mixed left–right system of dimension $1$, and the seven-unit system of dimension $\log7/\log3$. The address map from the shift space onto the attractor is Lipschitz, surjective and unique off a countable set of ambiguous addresses under the open set condition. The quaternion systems are the associative comparison, in which the obstruction vanishes; the split and zero-divisor systems are the subjects of the neighbouring articles, and the exceptional geometry carried by the seven-unit systems is the subject of Octonions and Exceptional Geometry.

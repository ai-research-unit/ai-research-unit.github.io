# __The One-Sided Operator Exponential on the N-Regular Sector__

## Introduction

This article computes the one-parameter group generated by a one-sided operator on the **N-regular sector** and the exponent that one spinor slot carries. The sector is the unit group $N\neq0$; the algebra of the operator — the composition laws, the factorisation through the polar word, the eigenbasis, the block form, the image and the kernel — is in *One-Sided Operators on the N-Regular Sector*, and this article uses it without repeating it.

A one-sided operator is a representation of the algebra, so its exponential is the exponential of the element:

$$
\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)},\qquad
\exp\bigl(tR_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=R_{\exp(t\tilde Q)} .
$$

That identity holds on the whole sector. It is the reason the one-sided reading is the natural home of the spinor exponent: a spinor is acted on by one slot, the exponent of the slot is half of the exponent of the vector action, and the group generated by $L_{\tilde Q}$ displays the half in its spectrum.

**What the norm decides, and what the vector form decides.** The identity above fixes the exponential from the element on the whole sector, so the norm decides nothing new here: invertibility, the rate and the group law are the same at every point of the sector. The wall $B^{2}=0$ decides only the **shape of the one-parameter group**. Off the wall the operator is diagonalisable and the group is a torus of rotations and boosts, with the eigenvalues $e^{t\lambda_\pm}$. On the wall the operator is $Q_t(I+L_{\vec\Upsilon})$ with $L_{\vec\Upsilon}^{2}=0$, the group is a line of shears, and the exponential truncates at degree one. On the wall the two polar factors survive, but their exponents are locked to the single null parameter $a$: the axes are orthogonal and $\varepsilon\tan\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=\sinh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}=a$.

The plan is the following. §*The Operator Exponential* proves the identity and reads the spectrum and the rate. §*The Exponential Polar Word on a Spinor* proves the half-spin theorem. §*The Weight Relation and the Two Copies* reads the weights of $S\otimes S^{*}$. §*The Exponents of the Four Factors* collects the exponents in one table. §*The Wall $B^{2}=0$* computes the shear and the locked angles. The article closes with the summary, the notation and the readings.

## The Operator Exponential

**Theorem.** For every $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$ and every $t\in\mathbb{C}$,

$$
\boxed{\;\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)},\qquad
\exp\bigl(tR_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=R_{\exp(t\tilde Q)}\;}
$$

*Proof.* The left multiplication is multiplicative, $L_{\tilde Q}^{n}=L_{\tilde Q^{n}}$, so the exponential series is

$$
\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)
=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}L_{\tilde Q}^{n}
=\sum_{n\ge0}L_{t^{n}\tilde Q^{n}/n!}
=L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)} .
$$

The same computation holds on the right, because $R_{\tilde Q}^{n}=R_{\tilde Q^{n}}$; the right multiplication is an anti-representation, but a single element commutes with its own powers.

On $\Sigma_{11}$ the operator is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues $\lambda_+,\lambda_+,\lambda_-,\lambda_-$ of §*The Eigenbasis and the Two Spinor Spaces* of the companion. Hence $\exp(tL_{\tilde Q})$ is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues

$$
e^{t\lambda_+},\ e^{t\lambda_+},\ e^{t\lambda_-},\ e^{t\lambda_-},
\qquad \lambda_\pm=Q_t\pm iB .
$$

Its determinant and trace are

$$
\det\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=\exp\bigl(4tQ_t\bigr),
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)
=2\bigl(e^{t\lambda_+}+e^{t\lambda_-}\bigr)
=4e^{tQ_t}\cos(tB).
$$

The rate is the eigenvalue itself, not its square. For a central element $\tilde Q=c\,e_t$ the operator is $c\,\mathrm{id}$ and the group is $e^{tc}\,\mathrm{id}$; the two-sided companion has $e^{t\lvert c\rvert^{2}}\,\mathrm{id}$. The one-sided rate is $c$, the two-sided rate is $\lvert c\rvert^{2}$.

## The Exponential Polar Word on a Spinor

Write the polar word as
$$
\tilde Q=\Rho\,\tilde U_{\mathrm b}\,\tilde u_{\mathrm r},
\qquad
\Rho=r\exp(i\alpha),
$$
and put
$$
\lambda_\pm=\Rho\,\mu_\pm,\qquad
\mu_+\mu_-=1 .
$$
The numbers $\mu_\pm$ are the eigenvalues of the unit factor $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$. On either spinor space the operator multiplies the first coordinate by $\lambda_+$ and the second by $\lambda_-$, so the spinor coordinates carry the pair $(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$.

For each pure factor, read with the other factors absent, the pair is elementary. The scale $r$ gives $\mu_+=\mu_-=1$. The central phase $\exp(i\alpha)$ gives $\mu_+=\mu_-=1$ and multiplies both coordinates by $\exp(i\alpha)$. The boost gives
$$
\tilde U_{\mathrm b}=\exp\Bigl(i\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}\Bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_+=e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2},\qquad \mu_-=e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2},
$$
and the rotor gives
$$
\tilde u_{\mathrm r}=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}\Bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_+=e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},\qquad \mu_-=e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}.
$$
**Theorem (the one-sided operator is the spinor action).** The one-sided operator produces the half-angle and the half-rapidity; the two-sided operator produces the full angle and the full rapidity. The rotor case is
$$
\boxed{\;
L_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\,L_{\vec u_{\mathrm r}}\Bigr)
\ \Longrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto(e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}\alpha,\ e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}\beta),
\qquad
\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}=\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\,\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u_{\mathrm r}}\Bigr)
\ \Longrightarrow\ \vec v\longmapsto R_{\vec u_{\mathrm r}}(\theta_{\mathrm{axr}})\vec v
\; }.
$$
The exponent is $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}$ in both readings. The exponent $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}$ of $L_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}$ multiplies the two spinor coordinates by $e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$ and $e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$, hence the output is the half-angle; the same $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}$ in $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u}(X)=[\vec u,X]$ gives the rotation of angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$, because $\mathrm{ad}_{\vec u}=2\,[\vec u\times]$ and the factor $2$ doubles it. The boost is the same:
$$
\boxed{\;
L_{\tilde U_{\mathrm b}}=\exp\Bigl(i\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\,L_{\vec u_{\mathrm b}}\Bigr)
\ \Longrightarrow\ (\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto(e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}\alpha,\ e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}\beta),
\qquad
\mathrm{Sd}_{\tilde U_{\mathrm b}}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{rapidity }\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}
\; }.
$$
A spinor transforms by half of what a vector transforms by, and the operator that produces the half is the one-sided one. This is the precise sense in which the spin representation of the polar word is the one-sided operator, and the vector representation is the two-sided one. The spectral form of the same statement is in §*The Spectrum of the Two-Sided Operator* of *The Hermitian Pair on the N-Regular Sector* and §*The Pure Factors and the Full Spin* of *The Two-Sided Exponential on the N-Regular Sector*.

The following table collects the pure factors. The last column is the nontrivial factor in the spectrum of the two-sided operator, that is, in the products $\lambda_i\bar\lambda_j$.

| factor | $\mu_+,\mu_-$ | spinor matrix $\Delta$ | exponent per coordinate | nontrivial two-sided factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scale $r$ | $1,1$ | $rI_2$ | $\ln r$ | $r^{2}$ |
| phase $\exp(i\alpha)$ | $1,1$ | $\exp(i\alpha)I_2$ | $i\alpha$ | $1$ |
| boost $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}$ | $e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2},e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}$ | $\operatorname{diag}(e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2},e^{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2})$ | $\mp\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2$ | $e^{\pm\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}$ |
| rotor $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ | $e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$ | $\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2})$ | $\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2$ | $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}$ |

The scale and the phase are central. Their rows are read as scalar multipliers of a word with $B\neq0$; for a purely central element $B=0$, every basis is an eigenbasis, and the single eigenvalue is $Q_t$.

For a general word the two factors do not commute, and $\mu_\pm$ are not separately exponentials of half the boost and half the rotor angle. The spinor matrix is still $\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$; the half-angle formulas are the statement for the pure factors, and for commuting axes. The general relation between the spectral data and the polar angles is *Relations between Exponential Polar Decomposition and Eigenbasis of an N-Regular, Spectrally Regular Biquaternion*, §*The Spectral Data from the Polar Factors*.

## The Weight Relation and the Two Copies

The algebra is the tensor product of a spinor space and its dual,
$$
\mathbb{B}\cong S\otimes S^{*},
$$
where $S=\mathbb{C}^{2}$ is the spinor space and $S^{*}$ its dual, and $S_+\cong S_-\cong S$ as left modules. Left multiplication acts on the first factor, right multiplication on the second:
$$
L_{\tilde Q}=\rho(\tilde Q)\otimes 1,
\qquad
R_{\tilde Q}=1\otimes\rho(\tilde Q)^{T},
\qquad
R_{\tilde Q^{*}}=1\otimes\bar\rho(\tilde Q),
$$
where $\rho$ is the defining representation on $S$ and $\bar\rho$ its conjugate. The two factors commute, so the exponents add:
$$
\exp\bigl(tL_A\bigr)\exp\bigl(sR_B\bigr)=\exp\bigl(tL_A+sR_B\bigr).
$$

For the rotor, $\rho(\tilde u_{\mathrm r})$ has weights $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$ and $\bar\rho(\tilde u_{\mathrm r})$ has weights $e^{\mp i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$. The tensor weights, in additive notation, are the four sums
$$
\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}-\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=0,\qquad
\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}+\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=\theta_{\mathrm{axr}},\qquad
-\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}-\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=-\theta_{\mathrm{axr}},\qquad
-\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}+\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=0,
$$
that is $(\theta_{\mathrm{axr}},0,0,-\theta_{\mathrm{axr}})$. For the boost, both slots carry $e^{\mp\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}$ because the boost is Hermitian, and the sums are $(\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}},0,0,-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}})$. These are the weights of the vector. The invariant statement is that the weights of $S\otimes S^{*}$ are the pairwise sums of the weights of $S$ and $S^{*}$; the factor two sits in the pairing, not in a rescaling of the angle.

**"Half" is relative.** The angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$ is defined by the vector action, as the rotation of $\mathbb{R}^{3}$. The spinor weight is $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2$ because the rotor is $\exp(\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r})$. A different normalisation of the angle would change the number and not the relation.

**The whole operator is two copies.** On $\mathbb{B}=S_+\oplus S_-$ the two summands are isomorphic as left modules, so
$$
\operatorname{spec}L_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}=\{e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2},e^{-i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}\},
$$
each weight twice. The one-sided operator is $2\rho$ as a left module: two copies of the Weyl module, not the Dirac module $\rho\oplus\bar\rho$. One Peirce column is one elementary spinor, a Weyl spinor in the language of physics; the whole of $L$ is a pair of them, which is a Dirac spinor (*Spinors and Dirac Spinors in the Biquaternion Algebra*, §*The Pair Is a Multiplicity*). The precise phrase is therefore: the one-sided operator, restricted to one Peirce component, is the half-spin.

**The two Peirce copies are not the two Lorentz chiralities.** Both $S_+$ and $S_-$ carry the same representation $\rho$; they differ by the Peirce idempotent, not by the Lorentz chirality. The two Lorentz chiralities are the two tensor factors $S$ and $S^{*}$, that is the left and right slots.

**Factor by factor.** For a general word the factors do not commute and $\lambda_\pm$ are not $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$ or $e^{\mp\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}$. There is no single half-angle of a general polar word; the additive relation is the statement for the pure factors and for commuting axes.

**The whole of $L$ is twisted.** $L_{\tilde Q}$ also carries the scale $r$ and the phase $e^{i\alpha}$. The spinor statement concerns the norm-one, phase-one part $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$; the full operator is the twisted spinor representation of the module.

**The double cover.** Replacing $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ by $-\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ leaves the two-sided operator unchanged and changes $L$ by $-1$. On the norm-one slice the two-sided operator determines the element only up to sign; $L$ distinguishes the sign. The half-angle is therefore defined only after the lift to the double cover is chosen, and $L$ is the operator that carries the choice.

**Corollary (the one-sided operator is the spinor action).** The operator $L_{\tilde Q}$ is the action of $\tilde Q$ on a single spinor. It preserves chirality, acts by the same $\Delta$ on $S_+$ and $S_-$, and its rotor and boost exponents are half those of the two-sided operator.

## The Exponents of the Four Factors

The following table collects the exponents. The **exponent** is the element $A$ such that the factor is $\exp(A)$; the **scalar parameter** is the real or imaginary coefficient inside that exponential, and the **two-sided output** is the parameter of the corresponding transformation in the two-sided operator.

| factor | exponent $A$ | scalar parameter | two-sided output |
|---|---|---|---|
| scale $r$ | $\ln r$ | $\ln r$ | $2\ln r$ |
| phase $\exp(i\alpha)$ | $i\alpha$ | $\alpha$ | $0$ |
| boost $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}$ | $i\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ | $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}$ | $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ |
| rotor $\tilde u_{\mathrm r}$ | $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ | $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}$ | $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$ |

The rule is the number of slots. A one-sided operator is one factor of the word, so its exponent is the exponent of that factor: the scale keeps $\ln r$, the phase keeps $i\alpha$, the boost keeps the half-rapidity in $i\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2\,\vec u_{\mathrm b}$, the rotor keeps the half-angle in $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2\,\vec u_{\mathrm r}$. A two-sided operator uses two slots, the parameter and its Hermitian conjugate. For the Hermitian factors the two exponents are equal and add, giving $2\ln r$ for the scale and $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ for the boost; for the $*$-unitary rotor they are opposite and the conjugation gives the angle $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$; for the central imaginary phase they cancel to $0$. The doubling of the companion article is therefore a two-slot phenomenon for the scale, the boost and the rotor, and the phase is its exceptional case.

## The Wall $B^{2}=0$: the Exponential at the Wall

On the wall the element is $Q_t(e_t+\vec\Upsilon)$ with $\vec\Upsilon^{2}=0$, so the operator is $Q_t(I+L_{\vec\Upsilon})$ and its exponential is a shear. The two polar factors of the element survive, but their two exponents no longer add to the exponent of the element: the log defect is non-zero.

### The Exponential at the Wall

**One null exponent.** On the sector the element is a scalar times a unipotent element, so its exponential is polynomial. Since $\vec\Upsilon^2=0$,

$$
\boxed{\;\tilde Q=Q_t\bigl(e_t+\vec\Upsilon\bigr)=r e^{i\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}}\varepsilon\,e^{\vec\Upsilon},\qquad
e^{\vec\Upsilon}=e_t+\vec\Upsilon,\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac{Q_t}{\Rho}=\pm1.\;}
$$

Write $\vec\Upsilon=\vec A+i\vec B$ with $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ real; then $\vec A\cdot\vec B=0$ and $\lvert\vec A\rvert=\lvert\vec B\rvert=a$. The regular word carries two independent exponents, $i\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ and $\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$, about two independent axes. At the wall both factors remain, but their exponents do not add. The formal exponent $i\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}+\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ is not the logarithm: its difference from the true exponent $\vec\Upsilon$ is the log defect of *Exponential Polar Decomposition of an N-Regular, Spectrally Degenerate Biquaternion*, §*The Formal Exponent and the Defect*, whose cross term $i\varepsilon\tanh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\tan\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\,\vec u_{\mathrm b}\times\vec u_{\mathrm r}$ is non-zero because the axes are orthogonal. The exact exponent is $\vec\Upsilon$ itself. What the wall leaves is a pair of constraints between the two factors:

$$
\boxed{\;\vec u_{\mathrm b}\cdot\vec u_{\mathrm r}=0,\qquad
\varepsilon\tan\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=\sinh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}=a.\;}
$$

*Proof.* From the coordinate boxes of *Exponential Polar Decomposition of an N-Regular, Spectrally Degenerate Biquaternion*, $\cosh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}=\sqrt{1+a^2}$, $\sinh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}=a$, $\lvert\cos\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\rvert=1/\sqrt{1+a^2}$ and $\sin\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=a/\sqrt{1+a^2}$. Hence $\varepsilon\tan\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=a=\sinh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}$. The axes are $\vec u_{\mathrm b}\propto\vec B+\vec A\times\vec B$ and $\vec u_{\mathrm r}=\varepsilon\vec A/a$. The vectors $\vec B$ and $\vec A\times\vec B$ are both orthogonal to $\vec A$, because $\vec A\cdot\vec B=0$, so $\vec u_{\mathrm b}\cdot\vec u_{\mathrm r}=0$.

The wall therefore removes neither factor. It removes two real degrees of freedom: the rapidity and the angle are locked to the single parameter $a$, and the two axes are forced orthogonal. The product of the two factors is the unipotent $\tilde U$, up to the sign $\varepsilon$.

**The operator exponential.** Left multiplication is a representation, $L_{\tilde X}L_{\tilde Y}=L_{\tilde X\tilde Y}$, hence $L_{\exp\tilde X}=\exp L_{\tilde X}$. Since $L_{\vec\Upsilon}^2=L_{\vec\Upsilon^2}=0$,

$$
\boxed{\;\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)
=\exp\bigl(tQ_t(I+L_{\vec\Upsilon})\bigr)
=e^{tQ_t}\bigl(I+tQ_tL_{\vec\Upsilon}\bigr)\;}
$$

for every complex $t$. In particular $\exp L_{\tilde Q}=e^{Q_t}(I+Q_tL_{\vec\Upsilon})$.

*Proof.* $L_{\tilde Q}=Q_tL_{e_t+\vec\Upsilon}=Q_t(I+L_{\vec\Upsilon})$ and $L_{\vec\Upsilon}^2=0$. The two terms of the exponent commute, because both are polynomials in $L_{\vec\Upsilon}$, so the exponential is $e^{tQ_t}\exp(tQ_tL_{\vec\Upsilon})$, and the second factor is $I+tQ_tL_{\vec\Upsilon}$.

**Interpretation.** The one-parameter group is a line of shears, not a circle of rotations. The exponential moves the identity along the nilpotent direction and stops at degree one; it has no period and carries no angle. The polar angles are in the factors: $L_{\tilde U_{\mathrm b}}$ has the eigenvalues $e^{\pm\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}$, each twice, and $L_{\tilde u_{\mathrm r}}$ has $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$, each twice. Their product has the single eigenvalue $Q_t$, with two Jordan blocks of size two, and its spectrum carries no angle at all. In the regular sector the eigenvalues of $L_{\tilde Q}$ are $\Rho\mu_\pm$ with $\mu_\pm=U_t\pm i\sqrt{1-U_t^2}$ and $U_t=\operatorname{Sc}\tilde U$; at the wall $U_t\to\varepsilon$ and both eigenvalues tend to $\Rho\varepsilon=Q_t$. The only trace of the two angles is the single parameter $a$ that measures the nilpotent part.

At the wall the spinor exponent is $\vec\Upsilon$ itself, carried by the left slot. The vector exponent of the two-sided operator is $\vec\Upsilon$ in the left slot plus $\vec\Upsilon^{*}$ in the right. That is the wall form of the doubling: the spinor reading has one slot, the vector reading has two, and the scalar part appears once per slot. This is why the modulus is $Q_t$ in the one-sided reading and $\lvert Q_t\rvert^2$ in the two-sided one, and not a doubled exponent.

### Worked Example on the Wall

The companion gives the operator and its nilpotent part in the basis $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$. Its exponential is the shear

$$
\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=e^{t}\bigl(I+t\,\mathsf M(L_{\vec\Upsilon})\bigr)
=e^{t}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-t&-it&0\\
t&1&0&it\\
it&0&1&-t\\
0&-it&t&1
\end{pmatrix},
$$

which is the identity plus $t$ times the nilpotent matrix above, multiplied by $e^{t}$. At $t=1$ it is $e$ times the unipotent matrix of the operator, as the box requires.

The example displays the general form: $\exp(tL_{\tilde Q})=e^{tQ_t}(I+tQ_tL_{\vec\Upsilon})$ with $Q_t=1$ and $\mathsf M(L_{\vec\Upsilon})$ the nilpotent matrix above.

## Summary

The one-sided exponential is the element exponential in the regular representation:

$$
\exp\bigl(tL_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=L_{\exp(t\tilde Q)},
\qquad
\exp\bigl(tR_{\tilde Q}\bigr)=R_{\exp(t\tilde Q)} .
$$

On $\Sigma_{11}$ it is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues $e^{t\lambda_\pm}$, each twice, where $\lambda_\pm=Q_t\pm iB$; its determinant is $\exp(4tQ_t)$ and its trace is $4e^{tQ_t}\cos(tB)$. The one-sided rate is the eigenvalue $\lambda_\pm$, not its square.

The one-sided operator is the spinor action. On one Peirce column the operator is the Weyl spinor $\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$; the rotor carries $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$ and the boost $e^{\mp\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}$, so the slot carries the half-angle and the half-rapidity. The whole of $L$ is two copies of that spinor, one per Peirce column, and the two Lorentz chiralities are the two tensor slots of $\mathbb{B}\cong S\otimes S^{*}$, not the two Peirce columns.

The weights add on the tensor product. For the rotor they are $(\theta_{\mathrm{axr}},0,0,-\theta_{\mathrm{axr}})$ and for the boost $(\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}},0,0,-\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}})$; the factor two sits in the pairing of the two slots, not in a rescaling of the angle. The one-sided operator sees one slot and does not double.

On the wall the operator is $Q_t(I+L_{\vec\Upsilon})$ with $L_{\vec\Upsilon}^{2}=0$, so the group is the shear $\exp(tL_{\tilde Q})=e^{tQ_t}(I+tQ_tL_{\vec\Upsilon})$, with no period and no angle. Each eigenvalue $e^{tQ_t}$ carries a Jordan block of size two. The polar factors keep their eigenvalues $e^{\pm\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}/2}$ and $e^{\pm i\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}/2}$, each twice, but their parameters are locked: $\vec u_{\mathrm b}\cdot\vec u_{\mathrm r}=0$ and $\varepsilon\tan\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}=\sinh\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}=a$. The two angles leave the single trace $a$ in the spectrum.

The four factors give the table of exponents. The scale keeps $\ln r$, the phase keeps $i\alpha$, the boost keeps $i\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm b}$ and the rotor keeps $\tfrac{\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}}{2}\vec u_{\mathrm r}$. The two-sided output is $2\ln r$, $0$, $\theta_{\mathrm{rpd}}$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{axr}}$; the doubling and the cancellation are the subject of *The Two-Sided Exponential on the N-Regular Sector*.

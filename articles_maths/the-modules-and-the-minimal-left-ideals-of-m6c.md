# __The Modules and the Minimal Left Ideals of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$__

## Introduction

A rank-one idempotent $P$ of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ gives a minimal left ideal

$$
L=M_6P\cong\mathbb{C}^6,
$$

the column of $P$, and a minimal right ideal

$$
R=PM_6\cong(\mathbb{C}^6)^*,
$$

the row of $P$. The two are dual by the trace pairing. The algebra itself is the tensor product

$$
M_6(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}^6\otimes(\mathbb{C}^6)^*,
$$

and the minimal left ideals are the columns $M_6P_\varphi$ for the lines $\mathbb{C}\varphi$ of $\mathbb{C}^6$. They are all isomorphic as left modules, but the isomorphism is not canonical: the exchange of two columns is a choice, defined only up to a nonzero scalar.

The Peirce carrier

$$
C(P)=PMP\oplus PMQ
$$

has dimension $1+5=6$; it is the row $PM$, the dual of the column. The minimal left ideal is the carrier of the spin chain: the Peirce $\mathfrak{su}_2$ of the rank-one idempotent acts irreducibly on it, with spin $5/2$.

The article assumes the Peirce decomposition of *The Peirce Decomposition of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$* and the spin chain of *The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$*. It uses no form and no topology. The modules over the biquaternion algebra are *Modules over the Biquaternion Algebra*; the Morita equivalence is *Morita Equivalence*.

## The Minimal Left Ideal

### Definition

**Definition.** Let $P\in M_6(\mathbb{C})$ be an idempotent of rank one. The **minimal left ideal** of $P$ is

$$
L=M_6P=\{AP:A\in M_6(\mathbb{C})\}.
$$

**Theorem.** $L$ is a left ideal of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$, and it is minimal: its only left submodules are $0$ and $L$.

**Proof.** For $B\in M_6$ and $AP\in L$, $B(AP)=(BA)P\in L$; so $L$ is a left ideal. If $0\neq X=AP\in L$, then $A$ has rank at least one, and the left ideal generated by $X$ contains $M_6X=M_6AP$; since $P$ has rank one, $M_6P$ is the whole $L$ (the rank-one projector is a single matrix unit in some basis). So every nonzero element generates $L$.

**Theorem (the column).** In a basis with $P=E_{11}$,

$$
L=M_6E_{11}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&0&\cdots&0\\ \vdots& & &\\ a_{61}&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}\right\},
$$

the first column, and $L\cong\mathbb{C}^6$ as a left $M_6$-module.

**Proof.** $AE_{11}$ has only the first column nonzero, equal to the first column of $A$; the map $A\mapsto Ae_1$ identifies it with $\mathbb{C}^6$. The left action is $B\cdot(Ae_1)=(BA)e_1$, the defining action.

### The Minimal Right Ideal

**Definition.** The **minimal right ideal** of $P$ is

$$
R=PM_6=\{PA:A\in M_6(\mathbb{C})\}.
$$

**Theorem.** $R$ is a minimal right ideal, and in the basis with $P=E_{11}$ it is the first row:

$$
R=E_{11}M_6=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{16}\\0&0&\cdots&0\\ \vdots& &\vdots&\\0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}\right\},
$$

with $R\cong(\mathbb{C}^6)^*$ as a right $M_6$-module.

**Proof.** The same argument on the other side; the row is the dual of the column.

### The Column and the Row

**Theorem.** The trace pairing

$$
L\times R\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},
\qquad
(X,Y)\longmapsto\operatorname{tr}(XY)
$$

is well defined and nondegenerate, and it identifies $R$ with the dual $L^*$ of $L$.

**Proof.** For $X=BE_{11}$ and $Y=E_{11}C$, $XY=BE_{11}C$ has entries $B_{i1}C_{1j}$ and trace $\sum_iB_{i1}C_{1i}$, the standard pairing of the column vector $(B_{i1})$ with the row vector $(C_{1i})$. It is nondegenerate: if $X\neq0$, some $B_{i1}\neq0$, and choosing $C$ with $C_{1i}\neq0$ gives a nonzero pairing.

**Corollary (the two dualities).** The left module structure on $L$ dualizes to a right module structure on $L^*=R$, and conversely. The column is the left ideal; the row is the right ideal; the transpose exchanges them.

**Proof.** The dual of a left module is a right module by $(\varphi\cdot A)(x)=\varphi(Ax)$; under the trace pairing this is right multiplication by $A$.

**Remark (the column is not a right ideal).** The column $L$ is not stable under right multiplication by a general matrix: $E_{11}E_{12}=E_{12}\notin L$. The row is not a left ideal. **The column and the row are the two one-sided minimal ideals, and they are different subspaces of the algebra.**

## The Endomorphisms

**Theorem.** The endomorphism ring of the left module $L$ is

$$
\operatorname{End}_{M_6}(L)\cong\mathbb{C}.
$$

**Proof.** An endomorphism $T$ of $L$ is $M_6$-linear; since $L$ is simple, $T$ is either zero or injective; a nonzero injective endomorphism of the six-dimensional space is invertible, so the endomorphism ring is a division algebra over $\mathbb{C}$. The only finite-dimensional division algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ is $\mathbb{C}$. Explicitly, $T$ is right multiplication by an element of $PMP\cong\mathbb{C}$.

**Corollary (Schur's lemma).** Every nonzero $M_6$-linear map between minimal left ideals is an isomorphism, and it is unique up to a nonzero scalar.

**Proof.** The minimal left ideals are simple modules; a nonzero map between them is injective, hence an isomorphism; the endomorphism ring of the target is $\mathbb{C}$, so the map is unique up to a scalar.

**Theorem (the module category).** Every left $M_6$-module is a direct sum of copies of $L$:

$$
M\cong L^{\oplus k},
\qquad
k=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M/6 .
$$

In particular the left regular module is

$$
{}_A A\cong L^{\oplus6}.
$$

**Proof.** $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ is a simple Artinian ring; its left modules are semisimple, and the simple module is unique up to isomorphism, namely $L$. The regular module has dimension $36$, hence six copies of the six-dimensional simple module.

**Corollary (the double centralizer).** The algebra is the endomorphism ring of its simple module over the opposite algebra, and

$$
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(L)\cong M_6(\mathbb{C}).
$$

**Proof.** The action of $M_6$ on $L\cong\mathbb{C}^6$ is faithful and spans $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(L)$; the double centralizer theorem gives the identity.

## The Tensor Product

### The Rank-One Matrices

**Theorem.** The map

$$
\mathbb{C}^6\otimes(\mathbb{C}^6)^*\longrightarrow M_6(\mathbb{C}),
\qquad
u\otimes\varphi\longmapsto u\varphi,
\qquad
(u\varphi)(v)=\varphi(v)u,
$$

is an isomorphism of vector spaces, and

$$
(u\otimes\varphi)(v\otimes\psi)=\varphi(v)\,u\otimes\psi .
$$

**Proof.** The matrix units are $E_{ij}=e_i\otimes e_j^*$, where $e_j^*(e_k)=\delta_{jk}$; they form a basis of both sides. The product formula is the composition of rank-one maps.

**Corollary (the identity and the trace).**

$$
I_6=\sum_{i=1}^{6}e_i\otimes e_i^*,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(u\otimes\varphi)=\varphi(u).
$$

**Proof.** The identity is the sum of the coordinate rank-one projectors; the trace of $u\varphi$ is $\varphi(u)$.

### The Left and Right Actions

**Theorem.** Under the isomorphism $M_6\cong\mathbb{C}^6\otimes(\mathbb{C}^6)^*$, the left action of $M_6$ on $\mathbb{C}^6$ is on the first factor, and the right action on $(\mathbb{C}^6)^*$ is on the second:

$$
A(u\otimes\varphi)=(Au)\otimes\varphi,
\qquad
(u\otimes\varphi)A=u\otimes(\varphi A).
$$

**Proof.** $A(u\varphi)=(Au)\varphi$ and $(u\varphi)A=u(\varphi A)$.

**Corollary (the minimal ideals).** The minimal left ideal $L_\varphi$ is

$$
L_\varphi=\mathbb{C}^6\otimes\mathbb{C}\varphi,
$$

and the minimal right ideal $R_u$ is

$$
R_u=\mathbb{C}u\otimes(\mathbb{C}^6)^*.
$$

**Proof.** The left ideal of the projector with kernel $\ker\varphi$ is the set of all $u\otimes\varphi$; the right ideal of the projector with image $\mathbb{C}u$ is the set of all $u\otimes\psi$.

## The Minimal Left Ideals and the Exchange

### The Family

**Theorem.** The minimal left ideals of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ are exactly

$$
L_\varphi=M_6P_\varphi,
\qquad
P_\varphi=v\otimes\varphi,\quad \varphi(v)=1,
$$

for the nonzero linear forms $\varphi\in(\mathbb{C}^6)^*$, and $L_\varphi=L_\psi$ if and only if $\mathbb{C}\varphi=\mathbb{C}\psi$. The family is the projective space

$$
\mathbb{P}^5=\{(\mathbb{C}^6)^*\text{ modulo scalars}\},
$$

of complex dimension $5$.

**Proof.** A minimal left ideal is the set of matrices vanishing on a fixed hyperplane $H$, namely $H=\ker\varphi$; it is $L_\varphi=\mathbb{C}^6\otimes\mathbb{C}\varphi$, and it depends only on the hyperplane, equivalently on the line $\mathbb{C}\varphi$ in the dual. The projector $P_\varphi=v\otimes\varphi$ with $\varphi(v)=1$ has kernel $H$ and image $\mathbb{C}v$; the left ideal $M_6P_\varphi$ is independent of the choice of $v$. The set of lines in the six-dimensional dual is $\mathbb{P}^5$.

**Corollary (the parabolic stabilizer).** The stabilizer in $GL_6$ of a minimal left ideal is the parabolic subgroup of the hyperplane $H=\ker\varphi$, of dimension

$$
25+5+1=31,
$$

with Levi $GL_5\times GL_1$ and unipotent radical of dimension $5$.

**Proof.** Preserving $L_\varphi$ is preserving the hyperplane $H$. In a basis with $H=\{x_6=0\}$ the block form is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}
$$

with $A\in GL_5$ (dimension $25$), $B\in\mathbb{C}^{5\times1}$ (dimension $5$) and $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^\times$ (dimension $1$); the lower-left block is zero. The total is $25+5+1=31$.

### The Exchange

**Theorem.** Any two minimal left ideals $L_\varphi$ and $L_\psi$ are isomorphic as left modules. Every isomorphism is the restriction of a matrix $A\in GL_6$ with $AL_\varphi=L_\psi$, and it is unique up to a nonzero scalar.

**Proof.** Schur's lemma gives the uniqueness up to a scalar. The existence: choose $A\in GL_6$ with $A(\ker\varphi)=\ker\psi$; then $A$ maps $L_\varphi$ to $L_\psi$, and its restriction is an isomorphism.

**Corollary (the non-canonical exchange).** There is no canonical isomorphism $L_\varphi\cong L_\psi$ for $\varphi\neq\psi$. The set of isomorphisms is a torsor under $\mathbb{C}^\times$, and the choice of an exchange is the choice of a matrix $A$ carrying one hyperplane to the other.

**Proof.** A canonical isomorphism would be a natural section of the $GL_6$-equivariant bundle of isomorphisms; the stabilizer of a line acts nontrivially on the fiber (by the scalar endomorphisms), so no such section exists. The set of isomorphisms is a coset of the stabilizer modulo the scalars.

**Remark (the choice of a column).** The algebra has six coordinate columns, but no one of them is distinguished by the algebra alone. The root $H$ distinguishes the eigenlines, and the Lagrange projectors distinguish the six coordinate columns of the eigenbasis. **The minimal left ideals are all isomorphic; the root chooses a basis of them.**

## The Peirce Carrier

### Definition

**Definition.** Let $P$ be a rank-one idempotent and $Q=I_6-P$. The **Peirce carrier** of $P$ is

$$
C(P)=PMP\oplus PMQ .
$$

**Theorem.** $\dim C(P)=1+5=6$, and $C(P)=PM$, the minimal right ideal of $P$.

**Proof.** $PMP\cong\mathbb{C}$ (the scalar multiples of $P$), and $PMQ$ is the space of maps from the image of $Q$ to the image of $P$, of dimension $1\cdot5=5$. Their sum is $PM$.

**Corollary (the two carriers).** The minimal left ideal is

$$
MP=PMP\oplus QMP,
$$

of dimension $1+5=6$, the column; the Peirce carrier is the row $PM=PMP\oplus PMQ$, the dual. The trace pairing pairs them perfectly; the transpose reverses the multiplication, so it is not an intertwiner of the two actions.

**Proof.** The Peirce decomposition of *The Peirce Decomposition of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$* gives $MP=PMP\oplus QMP$ and $PM=PMP\oplus PMQ$. The trace pairing $\operatorname{tr}(XY)$ pairs the column with the row.

### The Spin Chain

**Theorem.** The Peirce $\mathfrak{su}_2$ of the rank-one idempotent acts irreducibly on the six-dimensional left module $MP$, with highest weight $5/2$:

$$
MP\cong V_{5/2},
\qquad
\dim V_{5/2}=6 .
$$

**Proof.** The spin chain of *The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$* has ladder operators $J_\pm$ and $J_3$ acting on the column $MP$ by left multiplication; the highest weight vector is the top Peirce line, and the ladder has six steps, from $-5/2$ to $5/2$. The module is irreducible because the ladder generates the whole space.

**Corollary (the dual row).** The Peirce carrier $C(P)=PM$ is the dual of the carrier $MP$. The contragredient action has the opposite weights, and the two representations are isomorphic because the spin-$5/2$ representation of $\mathfrak{su}_2$ is self-dual; the isomorphism is the duality pairing, not the transpose.

**Proof.** The trace pairing identifies $PM$ with $(MP)^*$; the contragredient action is $X\cdot r=-rX$, whose weights are the negatives. The spin-$5/2$ representation is self-dual, so the dual is isomorphic to $V_{5/2}$.

**Remark (the module as the carrier).** The minimal left ideal is the six-dimensional carrier of the defining representation, not a Peirce space and not a column of spin coefficients. The Peirce chain acts irreducibly on it. **The module is the carrier; the spin chain is the action.**

## Summary

A rank-one idempotent $P$ of $M_6(\mathbb{C})$ gives the minimal left ideal

$$
L=M_6P\cong\mathbb{C}^6,
$$

the column, and the minimal right ideal

$$
R=PM_6\cong(\mathbb{C}^6)^*,
$$

the row. The trace pairing identifies $R$ with $L^*$. The algebra is

$$
M_6(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}^6\otimes(\mathbb{C}^6)^*,
\qquad
E_{ij}=e_i\otimes e_j^*,
\qquad
I_6=\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i^* .
$$

$$
\boxed{\ L=M_6P\cong\mathbb{C}^6,\qquad R=PM_6\cong L^*,\qquad M_6\cong\mathbb{C}^6\otimes(\mathbb{C}^6)^* .\ }
$$

The minimal left ideals are the columns $L_\varphi$ for the lines $\varphi$ of the dual; they form $\mathbb{P}^5$; they are all isomorphic, and the exchange is non-canonical, unique only up to $\mathbb{C}^\times$. The stabilizer of one column is the parabolic of dimension $31$.

The Peirce carrier

$$
C(P)=PMP\oplus PMQ
$$

has dimension $1+5=6$; it is the row, the dual of the column. The minimal left ideal is the carrier of the spin chain, the irreducible spin-$5/2$ module of the Peirce $\mathfrak{su}_2$.

Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The idempotent is defined by an equation in which the product occurs, so it is a class of the product and not of the space (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition). The block of this article is the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $$ \tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q-\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P\bigr) =P_0\mathbf Q-Q_0\mathbf P-\mathbf P\times\mathbf Q , $$ which is alternating and without a unit (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Biquaternions). The article determines its idempotent set.

The answer is the origin alone. The block is alternating, so $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=0$ for every element, and the idempotent equation $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ is therefore the equation $0=\tilde\Pi$: the origin is the only idempotent of the block, and both nontrivial idempotents and projections are absent. The absence is not the absence of a projector in the operator sense: the element $e_0$, although it is not an idempotent of the block, has a left multiplication that is a projector, the projection onto the vector subspace, and that separation of the element from its operator is the subject of The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, to which the last section points. There is no Peirce decomposition, since the product is not associative and there are no nontrivial idempotents to decompose against.

The Notion and Its Product

Definition (idempotent of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element $\tilde\Pi$ is an idempotent of $\star$ when $$ \tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi . $$ The origin and, when the product is unital, the unit are the trivial idempotents; the others are nontrivial.

Definition (projection). When the product is the multiplication of a $*$-algebra, an idempotent $\tilde\Pi$ with $\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi$ is a projection.

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $\diamond$, and unqualified, idempotent means an idempotent of the block. The idempotent set of the general plain product, to which the block is compared, is Idempotents of the General Plain Algebra; the idempotents of the parent quaternionic product are Idempotents of the Quaternionic Product.

The Idempotent Equation

Theorem (the only idempotent). The identity element $e_0$ is not an idempotent of the block, the origin is, and the idempotent set of the block is the origin alone: $$ \{\tilde\Pi:\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\}=\{0\}. $$

Proof. The block is alternating, so $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=0$ for every element $\tilde\Pi$, by Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Biquaternions. The idempotent equation reads $0=\tilde\Pi$, whose only solution is the origin. The element $e_0$ is excluded because $e_0\diamond e_0=0\neq e_0$.

The idempotent equation is therefore the equation of the origin, and the idempotent set is the smallest it can be. The absence is the mirror image of the absence of a unit: the block removes the scalar part of the parent product, the idempotent equation of the parent reads $\tilde\Pi^{\natural}\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ and has the two trivial solutions and a family besides, and the removal of the scalar part leaves only the origin.

Projections and the Peirce Decomposition

Corollary (no nontrivial idempotent and no projection). The block has no nontrivial idempotent and no projection. Equivalently, the idempotent set is the trivial idempotent of origin alone, and the additional Hermitian condition $\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi$ that a projection imposes on an idempotent has no nonzero solution.

Proof. A nontrivial idempotent is an idempotent other than the origin and the unit; the block has no unit, and its only idempotent is the origin, so there is no nontrivial idempotent. A projection is an idempotent, so a projection is the origin, and the origin satisfies the condition $\tilde\Pi^{*}=\tilde\Pi$; hence the projection set is the origin alone.

Corollary (no Peirce decomposition). The Peirce decomposition of the block does not exist: the construction uses a nontrivial idempotent and the associativity of the product, and the block has neither.

Proof. The Peirce decomposition is indexed by a pair $(\tilde\Pi,e-\tilde\Pi)$ of complementary idempotents of an associative unital product (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition); the block has no unit, no nontrivial idempotent and no associativity, so the three ingredients of the construction are all absent.

The absence of the Peirce decomposition is not a defect of the block alone. Of the twelve structures only the general plain product carries the Peirce decomposition as a theorem of its own idempotents, and the symmetric and antisymmetric halves fail it for their own reasons; the general quaternionic product is the border case, where the projectors exist but are indexed by the plain idempotents. The classification of the twelve by the projectors they carry, and the exact sense in which the block retains a projector, are The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

The Relation to the Roots of Plus One

Remark (the scaled root). For the general plain product the idempotents are the roots of $+1$ read in the scaled sense, the plain product carrying the family $\tfrac12(e_0+\xi i)$ over the roots $\xi$ of $-1$ (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition and §Powers, the Square and the Roots). The block has no root of $+1$ (Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra), and its idempotent set is the origin alone; the two collapses have one cause, and neither produces a decomposition.

The common cause is not an accident: the idempotent equation and the root of $+1$ equation are both read from the square, and the square of the block vanishes identically. In the block the two equations collapse together, and they collapse differently: the idempotent equation $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ becomes $0=\tilde\Pi$ and is solved by the origin alone, while the root of $+1$ equation $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=e_0$ becomes $0=e_0$ and has no solution at all.

The Element $e_0$ and Its Operator

Remark (an element that is not an idempotent but has a projector). The element $e_0$ is not an idempotent of the block, since $e_0\diamond e_0=0$; it is nevertheless the element whose left multiplication is a projector, because $$ L_{e_0}\tilde X=e_0\diamond\tilde X=\mathbf X , \qquad L_{e_0}^{2}=L_{e_0} , $$ the projection onto the vector subspace along the centre. The element and its operator are two different objects, and the block separates them: the element $e_0$ is not idempotent, while the operator $L_{e_0}$ is idempotent. The separation, the exact projector class of the block and the comparison with the other eleven are The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra; what is recorded here is only that the idempotent equation of the block and the projector equation of its operators are not the same equation.

Summary

For the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=P_0\mathbf Q-Q_0\mathbf P-\mathbf P\times\mathbf Q$ the square of every element is zero, so the idempotent equation $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ is the equation $0=\tilde\Pi$ and the idempotent set is the origin alone. The element $e_0$ is not an idempotent of the block; the block has no nontrivial idempotent and no projection; and the Peirce decomposition does not exist, its three ingredients — a unit, a nontrivial idempotent and associativity — being all absent. The absence agrees with the absence of roots of $+1$, the two being the same vanishing square read twice. The element $e_0$, although not idempotent, has an idempotent left multiplication, the projection onto the vector subspace; the element and its operator are distinct objects, and their separation is the subject of the specificities of the block.

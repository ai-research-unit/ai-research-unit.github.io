# __The Square, the Idempotents and the Jordan Inverse of the Symmetric Plain Algebra__

## Introduction

The symmetric plain algebra of $\mathbb{B}$ carries the product $\tilde Q_1\bullet\tilde Q_2=\tfrac12(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2+\tilde Q_2\tilde Q_1)$ of *Introduction to the Symmetric Plain Algebra*. This article reads the three structures a commutative unital Jordan algebra is built from: the **square** $\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q_2\bullet\tilde Q_2$ and the quadratic map it defines, the **idempotents** $\tilde Q_2\bullet\tilde Q_2=\tilde Q_2$, and the **inverse** of an element.

The results are collected here because they are one subject. The block is a Jordan algebra of degree two, and its degree is the statement that every element satisfies a quadratic identity with a **generic trace** $T(\tilde Q)=2Q_t$ and a **generic norm** $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=Q_t^2+Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2$; the idempotents are read off the same identity at integral trace; the inverse is the companion root of the quadratic; and the isotropic cone $\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$ is what makes the algebra not a division Jordan algebra.

The square of the block is the plain square, so the idempotents of the block are the idempotents of the plain product, classified in *Idempotents*, and the norm is the biquaternion norm of *N-Regularity and N-Degeneracy in the Biquaternion Algebra*. What this article adds is the reading of those facts as the degree-two structure of the Jordan algebra, and the fuller theory of the Jordan inverse. The Common Theory articles of the block are *Norm and Invertibility in the Symmetric Plain Algebra*, *Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, *Square Roots of a General Element in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space* and *The Specificities of the Symmetric Plain Algebra*; the trace form of the block is *The Trace Form and the Invariance of the Symmetric Plain Algebra*; the idempotents as minimal ideals of the parent are *The Peirce Decomposition*; and the placement of the block among the twelve operations is *The Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*.

## The Square and the Quadratic Map

### The Square

**Proposition.** The square of an element in the block is the plain square:

$$
\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q=\tilde Q^2=\bigl[Q_t^2-(\vec Q,\vec Q)\bigr]e_t+2Q_t\vec Q.
$$

*Proof.* $\tilde Q_2\bullet\tilde Q_2=\tfrac12(\tilde Q_2^2+\tilde Q_2^2)=\tilde Q_2^2$, and the plain square is the displayed form, read from the scalar–vector rule of *The Biquaternion Algebra* at $\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_2$.

**Remark (the block's square and the algebra's square are one).** Because the antisymmetric part of the plain product is alternating, its diagonal vanishes, and the plain square is carried by the symmetric part alone. **The square of the block and the square of the algebra are therefore the same map** $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde Q^2$, and the antisymmetric part carries none of it. The spectrum of the block is the spectrum of the algebra, and the minimal polynomial is read from this square.

### The Polarisation of the Square

**Proposition.** The product is the polarisation of the square:

$$
\tilde Q_1\bullet\tilde Q_2=\tfrac12\bigl((\tilde Q_1+\tilde Q_2)\bullet(\tilde Q_1+\tilde Q_2)-\tilde Q_1\bullet\tilde Q_1-\tilde Q_2\bullet\tilde Q_2\bigr).
$$

*Proof.* The product is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear and commutative, so the square of a sum expands as the sum of the two squares and twice the product.

So the quadratic map $\tilde Q\mapsto\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q$ and the linear structure determine the product, and the two are the data a Jordan algebra is generated by; this is $\sigma_2$ in the notation of *The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product*.

### The Quadratic Identity

**Proposition (the quadratic identity).** Every $\tilde Q$ satisfies

$$
\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q-T(\tilde Q)\tilde Q+\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}e_t=0,\qquad
T(\tilde Q)=2Q_t,\quad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}Q_\mu^2 .
$$

*Proof.* On the square of the previous section, $\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q=Q_t^2-(\vec Q,\vec Q)+2Q_t\vec Q$ and $T(\tilde Q)\tilde Q=2Q_t\cdot(Q_te_t+\vec Q)=2Q_t^2e_t+2Q_t\vec Q$. Subtracting the two leaves $\bigl(Q_t^2-(\vec Q,\vec Q)-2Q_t^2+\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\bigr)e_t$, and the coefficient vanishes because $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=Q_t^2+(\vec Q,\vec Q)$.

**Definition.** The functional $T(\tilde Q)=2Q_t$ is the **generic trace** of the block and $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ its **generic norm**. The quadratic identity is the generic minimal polynomial of the block, of degree two.

**Remark (the block has degree two).** The quadratic identity holds for every element, and for an element of the centre it degenerates: on $\tilde Q=Q_te_t$ the generic minimal polynomial, in the indeterminate $\lambda$, is $\lambda^2-2Q_t\lambda+Q_t^2=(\lambda-Q_t)^2$, a square. **Generically the identity is of degree two, so the symmetric plain algebra is a Jordan algebra of degree two**, and its elements are the roots of their own quadratic. The generic trace and the generic norm are the two symmetric functions of the roots; they are the Jordan-algebra structures with no analogue in the associative algebra, where the element has a degree-four generic polynomial inherited from the four-dimensional regular representation.

**Remark (the discriminant).** The discriminant of the quadratic identity is

$$
T(\tilde Q)^2-4\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=4Q_t^2-4\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2=-4(\vec Q,\vec Q).
$$

**It vanishes exactly on the set $\{(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0\}$**, the isotropic cone of the vector part, which contains the whole centre; there the two generic roots coincide, and off that set the element has two distinct generic roots. The set is strictly larger than the centre: the element $\vec e_x+i\vec e_y$ has a non-zero vector part and $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=1+i^2=0$, so its quadratic is $\lambda^2$ and its two roots coincide. The computed expression was checked on the coordinate rule.

## The Idempotents of the Block

**Definition.** An **idempotent** of the block is an element $\tilde\Pi$ with

$$
\tilde\Pi\bullet\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi .
$$

The idempotents of the block are the idempotents of the plain product, since the two squares agree, and their theory — the classification, the bijection with the roots of $+1$ through the halving map, the complementary idempotent $e_t-\tilde\Pi$, the isotropy, the cubic Peirce identity and the Jordan Peirce decomposition — is the Common Theory article *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*. **It is not repeated here**, and this section keeps only the definition and the two facts that bear on the degree-two structure of the block.

**Remark (the idempotents and the degree-two structure).** An idempotent is read off the quadratic identity at integral trace: the trivial idempotents have $(T,N)=(0,0)$ and $(2,1)$, and the non-trivial ones $(T,N)=(1,0)$. The non-trivial idempotents are therefore **isotropic**, $\langle\tilde\Pi\rangle_{\natural}=0$, and hence are the non-invertible idempotents, the invertibility criterion being $N\neq0$; the full theory is *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, and the isotropic idempotents as the scalar family of the zero divisors are *Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*.

## The Jordan Inverse

### The Inverse of a Jordan Algebra

**Definition.** In a commutative unital Jordan algebra an element $x$ is **invertible** when there exists $y$ with

$$
x\bullet y=e_t,\qquad x\bullet(y\bullet x)=x,
$$

and $y$ is then the **Jordan inverse** $x^{-1}$. In a Jordan algebra of degree two with generic trace $T$ and generic norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, an element with $\langle x\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$ is invertible and

$$
x^{-1}=\frac{T(x)e_t-x}{\langle x\rangle_{\natural}} .
$$

### The Criterion and the Formula in the Block

**Theorem.** An element $\tilde Q$ of the block is invertible exactly when $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, and its Jordan inverse is

$$
\boxed{\; \tilde Q^{-1}=\frac{2Q_te_t-\tilde Q}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}}=\frac{\tilde Q^{\natural}}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}} \;}.
$$

*Proof.* The quadratic identity of the block, divided by $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$, is

$$
\frac{\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}}-\frac{T(\tilde Q)}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}}\tilde Q+e_t=0,
$$

that is, $\tilde Q\bullet\bigl((T(\tilde Q)e_t-\tilde Q)/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\bigr)=e_t$, so the displayed element is a Jordan inverse; the second equation $x\bullet(y\bullet x)=x$ follows, since $\bullet$ is commutative and associative with respect to the unit in the Jordan sense. The identity $T(\tilde Q)e_t-\tilde Q=2Q_te_t-\tilde Q=\tilde Q^{\natural}$ is the definition of the natural conjugate.

### The Comparison with the Algebra Inverse

**Theorem.** The Jordan inverse of the block is the inverse of the algebra: for $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$,

$$
\boxed{\; \tilde Q^{-1}=\frac{\tilde Q^{\natural}}{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}},\qquad
\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q^{-1}=\tilde Q\tilde Q^{-1}=e_t \;}.
$$

*Proof.* The inverse formula of the algebra is $\tilde Q^{-1}=\tilde Q^{\natural}/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ in *N-Regularity and N-Degeneracy in the Biquaternion Algebra*, and $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$. The two formulas are the same element; the Jordan inverse was computed above as the same quotient.

**Remark (the inverse agrees because the block is special).** In a general Jordan algebra the inverse is a new operation; here the block is the symmetrisation of an associative algebra, and **the Jordan inverse coincides with the associative inverse and is therefore computed by the natural conjugate and the norm**, exactly as in the algebra. The invertibility criterion is the same in the two readings: an element is invertible in the block precisely when it is invertible in $\mathbb{B}$, that is, precisely when it is not a zero divisor.

## The Norm, the Isotropic Cone and the Elements of Square Zero

### The Generic Norm and the Isotropic Cone

The generic norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$ is the biquaternion norm $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ of *N-Regularity and N-Degeneracy in the Biquaternion Algebra*, a complex-valued multiplicative algebraic norm vanishing exactly on the isotropic cone $\{N=0\}$, the origin together with the zero divisors. Its polar form is the complex bilinear dot product $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_{1,\mu} Q_{2,\mu}$, symmetric and non-degenerate, with Gram matrix the identity $\mathrm{I}_4$ in the coefficient basis.

**Definition.** The **isotropic cone** of the block is the set

$$
\{\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}:\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}.
$$

**Remark (the isotropic cone is the zero-divisor cone).** $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0$ holds for $\tilde Q=0$ and for the zero divisors of *N-Regularity and N-Degeneracy in the Biquaternion Algebra*, §*The Zero Divisors*, and for no other element. **The isotropic cone of the block is the union of the origin and the zero-divisor cone of the algebra, so the symmetric plain algebra possesses non-zero isotropic elements and is not a division Jordan algebra.** This is the Jordan reading of the failure of $\mathbb{B}$ to be a division algebra: the block is a Jordan algebra whose norm form is **non-degenerate** but isotropic, of realified signature $(4,4)$; **it is the Hermitian form, and not the norm form, that is definite**.

An element is isotropic exactly when it fails to be invertible, so the invertible elements of the block are its non-isotropic elements, the group of units of *N-Regularity and N-Degeneracy in the Biquaternion Algebra*.

### The Elements of Square Zero

**Theorem.** The elements of square zero, $\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q=0$, are exactly the **pure isotropic vectors**:

$$
\boxed{\; \tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q=0\iff Q_t=0\ \text{and}\ (\vec Q,\vec Q)=0 \;},
$$

that is, exactly the elements of the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ on which the norm vanishes. They are a proper subset of the isotropic cone.

*Proof.* By the square of the block, $\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q=\bigl(Q_t^2-(\vec Q,\vec Q)\bigr)e_t+2Q_t\vec Q$. Its vanishing gives the two scalar conditions $Q_t^2=(\vec Q,\vec Q)$ and $Q_t\vec Q=0$. If $Q_t\neq0$ the second gives $\vec Q=0$, and the first gives $Q_t=0$, a contradiction; so $Q_t=0$, and the first condition becomes $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$. Conversely a pure element with $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$ has zero square.

**Remark (square zero is stronger than isotropic).** The isotropic cone is $\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, which for a pure element is the same condition $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=0$; but for $Q_t\neq0$ the equation $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0$ has the solutions $(\vec Q,\vec Q)=-Q_t^2$, whose square has the non-zero vector part $2Q_t\vec Q$ since $\vec Q\neq0$. The element $e_t+i\vec e_x$ is the witness: $\langle e_t+i\vec e_x\rangle_{\natural}=1+i^2=0$, while $(e_t+i\vec e_x)\bullet(e_t+i\vec e_x)=2e_t+2i\vec e_x\neq0$. **The elements of square zero are the pure part of the isotropic cone, and the isotropic cone is strictly larger**, so the two notions must not be identified.

### The Isotopy with the Two by Two Matrices

**Remark (degree two and the matrix model).** The block is a special Jordan algebra of degree two, and it is **isotopic** to the symmetrised matrix algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})^{+}$: the isomorphism $\mathsf{M}_2$ of *The General Plain Algebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation $M_2(\mathbb{C})$* carries the symmetric plain product to the symmetrised matrix product, $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q\bullet\tilde{Q}_1)=\tfrac12(\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde{Q}_1)+\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde{Q}_1)\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q))$, and carries the generic trace to the matrix trace and the generic norm to the determinant,

$$
T(\tilde Q)=2Q_t=\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q),\qquad
\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2=\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q).
$$

Two Jordan algebras of degree two whose norm forms are carried to one another by an invertible linear map are isotopic, so the block is isotopic to the degree-two matrix algebra, and its quadratic identity is the Cayley–Hamilton identity of the $2\times2$ matrices. **The degree-two structure of the block is the degree-two structure of the matrices**, and the correspondence is read in full in *Introduction to the Symmetric Plain Algebra*.

## Summary

The symmetric plain algebra is a commutative unital Jordan algebra of degree two. Its square is the plain square, $\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q=Q_t^2-(\vec Q,\vec Q)+2Q_t\vec Q$, and it satisfies the quadratic identity $\tilde Q\bullet\tilde Q-T(\tilde Q)\tilde Q+\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}e_t=0$ with generic trace $T(\tilde Q)=2Q_t$ and generic norm $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$, of discriminant $-4(\vec Q,\vec Q)$. Its idempotents are the idempotents of the plain product — the trivial elements $0$ and $e_t$ and the isotropic elements $\tfrac12(e_t+\vec q)$ with $\vec q^2=e_t$ pure — and their theory is the Common Theory article *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*. Its inverse is $\tilde Q^{-1}=(2Q_te_t-\tilde Q)/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde Q^{\natural}/\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$, which is the inverse of the algebra, and the invertibility criterion is $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$. The isotropic cone is $\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, the union of the origin and the zero-divisor cone, so the block is not a division Jordan algebra; the elements of square zero are the pure isotropic vectors, a proper subset of that cone, and $e_t+i\vec e_x$ separates the two. The block is a degree-two Jordan algebra isotopic to the symmetrised $2\times2$ matrices, with the generic trace as the matrix trace and the generic norm as the determinant.

The Adjoint of the Two-Sided Multiplication Operator

Introduction

The two-sided multiplication operators are the finite sums of sandwiches,

$$ T = \sum_i L_{a_i}R_{b_i} , \qquad T(x) = \sum_i a_i x b_i , $$

and they form the algebra generated by the one-sided multiplications, which for a finite-dimensional Clifford algebra is the whole endomorphism algebra of the regular bimodule. The adjoint of such a sum is the sum of the adjoints with the parameters conjugated,

$$ T^{*} = \sum_i R_{\hat b_i}L_{\hat a_i} = \sum_i L_{\hat a_i}R_{\hat b_i} , $$

so the adjoint operation is a conjugate-linear anti-automorphism of the generated algebra and turns it into a $*$-algebra of operators on the regular module. This is the point of view from which the adjoint theory of the group is global: instead of one operator at a time, one has a $*$-algebra generated by the two-sided multiplications, and its self-adjoint, unitary and normal elements are the objects of the geometry of the next articles.

The boundaries. The single sandwich is The Sandwich on a Clifford Algebra and its adjoint is The Adjoint of the Sandwich; the one-sided adjoints are The Adjoint of the Left Multiplication on a Clifford Algebra and The Adjoint of the Right Multiplication; the form and the conjugation are The Twisted Adjoint on a Clifford Algebra; the generation of the endomorphism algebra is The Left and Right Multiplication Operators on a Clifford Algebra. The Hilbert-algebra structure of the two-sided operators, with the trace form and the involution, is Two-Sided Operators on a Hilbert Algebra with Signed Inner Conjugation and Adjoints of the Graded Operators of a Hilbert Algebra, quoted. The base is a field $F$ of characteristic not $2$ with a non-degenerate $q$, $q(u)=B(u,u)$, $uv+vu=2B(u,v)$; the module is the regular bimodule.

The $*$-Algebra of Two-Sided Operators

Theorem. Let $\mathcal{T}$ be the set of finite sums $\sum_iL_{a_i}R_{b_i}$. Then $\mathcal{T}$ is a subalgebra of $\mathrm{End}_F(\mathrm{Cl})$, equal to the algebra generated by the one-sided multiplications and hence, in finite dimension, to an algebra isomorphic to $\mathrm{Cl}(V,q)\otimes\mathrm{Cl}(V,q)^{\mathrm{op}}$; the adjoint operation $T\mapsto T^*$ is a conjugate-linear anti-automorphism of $\mathcal{T}$, and $T^{**}=T$.

Proof. The set is closed under sums and products because $L_aR_bL_cR_d=L_{ac}R_{db}$; it contains the generators, so it is the generated algebra, and the tensor-product isomorphism is the generation theorem of The Left and Right Multiplication Operators on a Clifford Algebra. The adjoint of a sum is the sum of the adjoints and the adjoint is conjugate-linear; the adjoint of a product reverses the order; the double adjoint is the identity by The Adjoint of the Sandwich.

Definition. The Hilbert–Schmidt form on $\mathcal{T}$ is $\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\operatorname{Tr}(B^*A)$, where the trace is that of the regular representation; it is a non-degenerate positive definite form in the real definite case, and the adjoint is its adjoint operation.

Proposition. The Hilbert–Schmidt form is invariant under the adjoint, $\langle A^*,B^*\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\overline{\langle A,B\rangle}_{\mathrm{HS}}$, and the map $A\mapsto A^*$ is its adjoint involution; with respect to it the two-sided operators form a finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra (a Hilbert algebra) whose self-adjoint elements are the fixed points of the involution.

Proof. The trace is invariant under cyclic permutation, so $\operatorname{Tr}((B^*)^*A^*)=\operatorname{Tr}(BA^*)=\operatorname{Tr}(A^*B)=\overline{\operatorname{Tr}(B^*A)}$ up to the conjugation of scalars; the involution property is the double adjoint; the algebra is finite-dimensional and closed in every topology, hence a von Neumann algebra, and Two-Sided Operators on a Hilbert Algebra with Signed Inner Conjugation is the reference for the Hilbert-algebra formalism.

Remark (self-adjoint, unitary, normal). An element $T\in\mathcal{T}$ is self-adjoint when $T^*=T$, unitary when $T^*T=TT^*=\operatorname{id}$, and normal when $T^*T=TT^*$; the decomposition $T=\frac12(T+T^*)+\frac12(T-T^*)$ exhibits every element as the sum of a self-adjoint and a skew-adjoint part, and the unitary elements form a group acting on the algebra. The spectral theory of these elements belongs to the operator-theoretic half of the corpus, and the algebraic classification of the three classes is the content of the geometry of the two-sided operators.

The Generation and the Commutant

Proposition. The commutant of $\mathcal{T}$ in $\mathrm{End}_F(\mathrm{Cl})$ is the centre of the Clifford algebra acting by scalars in the central-simple case, and it is $*$-invariant; the double commutant of the one-sided families is the whole $\mathcal{T}$.

Proof. $\mathcal{T}$ is the generated algebra and in the central-simple case it is the whole endomorphism algebra, so its commutant is the scalars; a scalar is self-adjoint, so the commutant is $*$-invariant. The double-commutant statement is the generation theorem read twice, together with the stability of the commutant under the adjoint proved for the one-sided families in The Adjoint of the Right Multiplication.

Remark (the graded and twisted versions). The same computation with the signed sandwich in place of the ordinary one gives the $*$-algebra generated by the signed two-sided operators, with the adjoint formula of The Adjoint of the Sandwich with the Signed Product; the graded version with the Koszul sign is The Graded Twisted Operators on a Hilbert Algebra and Adjoints of the Graded Operators of a Hilbert Algebra, where the involution is the graded adjoint. The three $*$-algebras differ only in the involution, not in the underlying algebra of operators.

Worked Cases

The Quaternions

For $\mathrm{Cl}\cong\mathbb H$ the two-sided operators form $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb R}(\mathbb H)\cong M_4(\mathbb R)$, the adjoint is the transpose in the standard basis with the conjugation in the parameters, and the unitary group of the Hilbert–Schmidt form is the group $O(4)$; the self-adjoint elements are the symmetric matrices in that basis.

The Matrix Algebra of the Split Plane

For $\mathrm{Cl}\cong M_2(\mathbb R)$ the two-sided operators form $M_2(\mathbb R)\otimes M_2(\mathbb R)^{\mathrm{op}}\cong M_4(\mathbb R)$, and the adjoint is read on the factor pairs; the $*$-algebra is the full matrix algebra with the transpose-involution, the standard example of a finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra.

A Scalar Operator

For $T=\lambda\operatorname{id}$ the adjoint is $\bar\lambda\operatorname{id}$, the operator is self-adjoint for $\lambda$ real (in the real case, always), unitary for $|\lambda|=1$, and normal always; the scalars form the centre and the simplest $*$-subalgebra.

Summary

The two-sided multiplication operators are the finite sums $T=\sum_iL_{a_i}R_{b_i}$, equivalently the operators $x\mapsto\sum_ia_ixb_i$; they form the algebra generated by the one-sided multiplications, isomorphic in finite dimension to $\mathrm{Cl}\otimes\mathrm{Cl}^{\mathrm{op}}$ and hence to a full matrix algebra over the division algebra of the Clifford algebra. Their adjoint is $T^*=\sum_iL_{\hat a_i}R_{\hat b_i}$, so the adjoint is a conjugate-linear anti-automorphism of the algebra, an involution, and a -structure; with the Hilbert–Schmidt form $\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\operatorname{Tr}(B^*A)$ the algebra is a finite-dimensional von Neumann (Hilbert) algebra, whose self-adjoint, unitary and normal elements are the objects of the geometry. The commutant is the centre and is $*$-invariant, and the graded and signed versions differ only in the involution. The single sandwich is The Adjoint of the Sandwich, the signed version is The Adjoint of the Sandwich with the Signed Product, and the Hilbert-algebra formalism is Adjoints of the Graded Operators of a Hilbert Algebra*.

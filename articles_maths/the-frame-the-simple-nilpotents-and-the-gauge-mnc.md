# __The Frame, the Simple Nilpotents and the Gauge__

## Introduction

**A root fixes the flag, and a frame fixes one vector in each line of the flag; the freedom in that choice is a torus, one parameter per simple nilpotent.** The root therefore gives the idempotents and the lines, and the gauge gives the vectors and the units. The two together are the frame.

The article constructs the frame from a basis adapted to the spectral flag, states the matrix-unit rule, isolates the simple nilpotents $E_{k,k+1}$, computes the gauge action and its cocycle, and counts the parameters: $n(n-1)$ complex from the root and $n-1$ complex from the gauge. At $n=2$ the count is four real and two real, the six real dimensions of the biquaternion frame. The article also identifies the older "second root" with a point of the gauge, which is the precise correction of the biquaternion presentation.

## The Adapted Frame

**A frame adapted to the flag is a basis with one vector in each spectral line.** Let $H^{n}=I$ have simple spectrum, with projectors $P_k$ and lines $V_k=\operatorname{im}P_k$. Choose $v_k\in V_k$, $v_k\ne0$, for $k=0,\dots,n-1$. Then $(v_0,\dots,v_{n-1})$ is a basis of $\mathbb{C}^{n}$, and

$$
P_kv_m=\delta_{km}v_k,\qquad Hv_k=\zeta_kv_k.
$$

Let $\ell_k$ be the dual basis of the dual space, $\ell_k(v_m)=\delta_{km}$, and put

$$
E_{kl}=v_k\ell_l.
$$

**Proposition.** The $n^{2}$ elements $E_{kl}$ are a basis of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$, they satisfy

$$
E_{kl}E_{mn}=\delta_{lm}E_{kn},\qquad E_{kk}=P_k,\qquad H=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta_kE_{kk},
$$

and $\operatorname{tr}E_{kl}=\delta_{kl}$.

*Proof.* $E_{kl}E_{mn}=v_k\ell_l(v_m)\ell_n=\delta_{lm}v_k\ell_n=\delta_{lm}E_{kn}$. On $v_m$, $E_{kk}$ acts as $\delta_{km}v_k$, so $E_{kk}=P_k$. The sum $\sum_k\zeta_kE_{kk}$ acts on $v_m$ as $\zeta_mv_m=Hv_m$. The trace is $\ell_l(v_k)=\delta_{kl}$. $\square$

**The frame is the choice of the $v_k$.** The root fixes the lines; the frame fixes the vectors. Different frames of the same flag give different matrices $E_{kl}$ and the same idempotents $E_{kk}=P_k$.

## The Simple Nilpotents

**The superdiagonal units are nilpotent of index two, and every unit is a product of consecutive ones.** Put

$$
N_k=E_{k,k+1},\qquad k=0,\dots,n-2.
$$

Then

$$
N_k^{2}=E_{k,k+1}E_{k,k+1}=0,
$$

and for $k<l$,

$$
E_{kl}=N_kN_{k+1}\cdots N_{l-1}.
$$

The nilpotents $N_k$ are the **simple nilpotents** of the frame. There are $n-1$ of them, one for each gap between consecutive lines of the flag.

**The simple nilpotents generate the frame.** The algebra generated by $N_0,\dots,N_{n-2}$ and their transposes $N_k^{\mathsf T}$ is the whole of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$: the products of consecutive $N$'s give all $E_{kl}$ with $k<l$, the transposes give $k>l$, and the diagonal is recovered as $E_{kk}=N_kN_k^{\mathsf T}$ for $k<n-1$ and $E_{n-1,n-1}=N_{n-2}^{\mathsf T}N_{n-2}$. The frame is therefore determined by the chain of simple nilpotents, and the chain is determined by the flag.

**The chain is the Peirce chain.** The nilpotent $N_k$ maps the line $V_{k+1}$ to the line $V_k$ and kills the other lines. In the Peirce notation of *The Peirce Decomposition and the Corner Recursion*, $N_k$ is a nonzero element of $A_{k,k+1}$. The chain $N_0,\dots,N_{n-2}$ is the sequence of maps that steps down the flag.

## The Gauge Action

**A rescaling of the frame vectors rescales the matrix units by the ratio of the two scales.** Replace $v_k$ by $t_kv_k$ with $t_k\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}$. The dual basis is replaced by $t_k^{-1}\ell_k$, so

$$
E_{kl}=v_k\ell_l\longmapsto t_kt_l^{-1}E_{kl}.
$$

The idempotents are fixed,

$$
E_{kk}\longmapsto E_{kk},
$$

and so is the root $H=\sum_k\zeta_kE_{kk}$. The group acting is the torus

$$
T=\{t=(t_0,\dots,t_{n-1})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\},
$$

and the diagonal $t_0=\cdots=t_{n-1}$ acts trivially, so the effective group is

$$
T/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-1}.
$$

This is the **gauge group** of the frame.

**One parameter per simple nilpotent.** Put

$$
\lambda_k=t_kt_{k+1}^{-1},\qquad k=0,\dots,n-2.
$$

Then

$$
N_k\longmapsto\lambda_kN_k,
$$

and every other unit is fixed by the $\lambda$'s through the cocycle

$$
E_{kl}\longmapsto\lambda_k\lambda_{k+1}\cdots\lambda_{l-1}\,E_{kl},\qquad k<l,
$$

and the inverse product for $k>l$. The composite $E_{kl}$ has no independent scale: its scale is the product of the consecutive ones. This is the **cocycle** of the gauge, and it is the reason the gauge has $n-1$ parameters rather than one per corner.

**The cocycle forbids an extra parameter.** Suppose that in addition to the $t_k$ one tries to rescale a single composite $E_{kl}$, $k<l$, by an independent $\mu$. The product rule forces

$$
E_{kl}=E_{k,k+1}\cdots E_{l-1,l},
$$

so the scale of $E_{kl}$ is the product of the scales of the factors, and $\mu$ is determined by them. Equivalently, the gauge action on the units is the coboundary of the $0$-cochain $t$, and a coboundary has no freedom beyond the cochain modulo constants.

**The idempotents do not depend on the gauge.** The parameters $\lambda_k$ act on the nilpotents and on the off-diagonal units, never on the diagonal. In particular the projectors $P_k$ and the spectral decomposition $H=\sum_k\zeta_kP_k$ are gauge invariant. A statement that depends on $\lambda$ is a statement about a frame, not about the algebra or the idempotents.

## The Count of the Frame

**A frame modulo overall scale has $n^{2}-1$ complex dimensions.** The set of bases of $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ is $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$, of complex dimension $n^{2}$; two bases that differ by an overall scalar give the same matrix units, so the frame is a point of $PGL_n(\mathbb{C})$, of complex dimension $n^{2}-1$.

**The root and the gauge split this count.** The map from frames to roots, $(v_k)\mapsto H=\sum_k\zeta_kE_{kk}$, has fibre the torus $T/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-1}$: the frames that give the same root are exactly the rescalings of the $v_k$. Hence

$$
\underbrace{n^{2}-1}_{\text{frame}/\,\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
=
\underbrace{n(n-1)}_{\text{root}}
+
\underbrace{n-1}_{\text{gauge}} .
$$

**The case $n=2$ gives the six real dimensions.** For $n=2$,

$$
n^{2}-1=3,\qquad n(n-1)=2,\qquad n-1=1,
$$

all complex. In real dimensions the frame modulo scale has $6$, the root has $4$ and the gauge has $2$: the four real parameters of the biquaternion root and the two real parameters of the complex number $\lambda$. This is the six-dimensional basis of the biquaternion frame.

**The general case.** For $n=3,4,5,6$ the complex counts are

| $n$ | frame $/\,\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ | root | gauge |
|---|---|---|---|
| $3$ | $8$ | $6$ | $2$ |
| $4$ | $15$ | $12$ | $3$ |
| $5$ | $24$ | $20$ | $4$ |
| $6$ | $35$ | $30$ | $5$ |

The root column is the dimension of $\mathcal{R}_n$ in *Roots of Unity and the Lagrange Idempotents*; the gauge column is $n-1$, one per simple nilpotent.

## The "Second Root" Is a Gauge Choice

**At $n=2$ the element that the older biquaternion frame called a second root is exactly a point of the gauge.** In the frame $(v_0,v_1)$ with $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, let $R$ satisfy

$$
R^{2}=I,\qquad RH=-HR.
$$

The anticommutation forces $R$ to be off-diagonal, $R=aE_{01}+bE_{10}$, and $R^{2}=abI=I$ forces $b=a^{-1}$. Hence

$$
R=aE_{01}+a^{-1}E_{10},\qquad a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.
$$

Under the gauge $E_{01}\mapsto\lambda E_{01}$, $E_{10}\mapsto\lambda^{-1}E_{10}$, the element $R$ is carried to $a\lambda E_{01}+a^{-1}\lambda^{-1}E_{10}$, which is the same family with $a$ replaced by $a\lambda$. The gauge orbit of $R$ is the whole family, and the parameter $a$ is gauge, not root.

**What the second root did and did not do.** It supplied a vector in each mixed Peirce line: $R$ has nonzero entries exactly in $A_{01}$ and $A_{10}$, and it normalises them relative to each other. It did not supply the idempotents: those are $P_\pm=(I\pm H)/2$, produced by $H$ alone. The presentation of the frame as a root pair therefore overstated the role of $R$. The correct primitive data is the root; the correct additional data is the gauge point, and at $n=2$ the gauge point is a complex number, equivalently a second root of $+1$ anticommuting with the first, defined only up to the gauge action.

**For $n>2$ there is no second root.** The gauge has $n-1$ complex parameters, and the analogue of $R$ is the full torus of rescalings of the $v_k$. No single element plays the role that $R$ plays at $n=2$, because the flag has more than two lines.

## An Explicit Frame for $n=3$

**The Fourier frame.** Take $v_k=(1,\omega^{k},\omega^{2k})^{\mathsf T}$ with $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, the eigenvectors of the cyclic shift of *Roots of Unity and the Lagrange Idempotents*. Then

$$
E_{kl}=\frac{1}{3}v_kv_l^{*}
$$

because the $v_k$ are orthogonal with $\|v_k\|^{2}=3$, and the dual functional is $\ell_l=\tfrac13v_l^{*}$. The three simple nilpotents are

$$
N_0=\frac13v_0v_1^{*},\qquad N_1=\frac13v_1v_2^{*},
$$

and

$$
N_0N_1=\frac19v_0\,(v_1^{*}v_1)\,v_2^{*}=\frac13v_0v_2^{*}=E_{02},
$$

which is the cocycle at work: the scale of $E_{02}$ is the product of the scales of $N_0$ and $N_1$.

**A gauge transformation.** Rescale $v_0\mapsto t_0v_0$ and $v_1\mapsto t_1v_1$, $v_2\mapsto t_2v_2$. Then

$$
N_0\longmapsto\frac{t_0}{t_1}N_0=\lambda_0N_0,\qquad
N_1\longmapsto\frac{t_1}{t_2}N_1=\lambda_1N_1,
$$

and

$$
E_{02}\longmapsto\frac{t_0}{t_2}E_{02}=\lambda_0\lambda_1E_{02}.
$$

The two parameters $\lambda_0,\lambda_1$ are independent; the third scale $\lambda_0\lambda_1$ is not.

## Summary

An adapted frame $(v_0,\dots,v_{n-1})$ gives the matrix units $E_{kl}=v_k\ell_l$, with $E_{kl}E_{mn}=\delta_{lm}E_{kn}$, $E_{kk}=P_k$ and $H=\sum_k\zeta_kE_{kk}$. The simple nilpotents $N_k=E_{k,k+1}$, $n-1$ of them, square to zero and generate the frame; every composite is their product. The gauge $v_k\mapsto t_kv_k$ acts by $E_{kl}\mapsto(t_k/t_l)E_{kl}$, an effective torus $(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-1}$ with one parameter per simple nilpotent, subject to the cocycle that fixes the composites. The frame modulo scale has $n^{2}-1$ complex dimensions, split as $n(n-1)$ for the root and $n-1$ for the gauge; at $n=2$ this is $6=4+2$ real, the biquaternion count. The older "second root" of the biquaternion frame is a point of this gauge, not a second primitive.

## Summary of Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $v_k$ | a nonzero vector in the spectral line $V_k$ |
| $\ell_k$ | the dual functional, $\ell_k(v_m)=\delta_{km}$ |
| $E_{kl}=v_k\ell_l$ | the matrix unit of the frame |
| $N_k=E_{k,k+1}$ | the simple nilpotent, $N_k^{2}=0$ |
| $t_k$ | the gauge scale of $v_k$ |
| $\lambda_k=t_kt_{k+1}^{-1}$ | the gauge parameter on $N_k$ |
| $T$ | the torus of rescalings, effective $(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-1}$ |

The Multiplication Operators of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions

Introduction

This article reads the multiplication of the general plain algebra through its multiplication operators: for an element $T$ the left multiplication is the map $L_{T}:X\mapsto TX$, the right multiplication is $R_{T}:X\mapsto XT$, and the multiplication is studied by studying the operators it produces. Because the algebra is commutative the two operators coincide, $L_{T}=R_{T}$, and this is the first of the article's specificities: the block has one operator per element and not two. The map $T\mapsto L_{T}$ is the regular representation of the algebra, and it is injective, so the algebra is realised as a commutative algebra of operators on itself; the image is the two-dimensional space of the $2\times2$ matrices of the form $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, not the whole endomorphism algebra.

The results. In the basis $1,j$ the operator $L_{T}$ has the matrix $$ \mathsf L(T)=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{pmatrix}, $$ symmetric with the two equal diagonal entries $A$ and the two equal off-diagonal entries $B$; in the idempotent basis $\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}$ it is the diagonal matrix $$ \operatorname{diag}\bigl(A+B,\ A-B\bigr), $$ with the two eigenvalues $q_1=A+B$ and $q_2=A-B$ and the two idempotent lines as eigenspaces. Its trace is $2A$, its determinant is $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}$, and its characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^{2}-2A\lambda+\langle T\rangle_{\natural}$; the determinant is the norm, so $L_{T}$ is invertible exactly when $T$ is a unit, and $L_{T}$ is never nilpotent for $T\neq0$. The map $L$ is an injective algebra homomorphism, $L_{T}L_{W}=L_{TW}$, and its image is the commutative two-dimensional subalgebra of $M_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ of the matrices of the displayed form. The enveloping operator algebra acts through the left multiplications alone, $L_{T}R_{W}=L_{TW}$, so its image is that same two-dimensional space; the derivations of the algebra into itself are all zero, and its $\mathbb{C}$-algebra automorphisms are the two elements $\mathrm{id}$ and ${}^{\natural}$.

Boundaries. The multiplication and its axioms are Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; the criterion that the operator calculus rests on is The Specificities of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; the norm and the units are Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions and The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; the idempotent lines and the ideals are Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; and the forms the operators adjoint are The Forms of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions. The operator calculus with its adjoints and its spectrum in the non-commutative biquaternion category is The Four General Products and Operators; the matrix model that realises the operators as matrices is The General Plain Algebra in the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation of the Reduced Biquaternions.

The Multiplication Operators

The Definition and the Coincidence of the Two Operators

The left and the right multiplication of $T$ coincide, because the algebra is commutative.

Definition. For $T\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}$ the left multiplication by $T$ is the endomorphism $$ L_{T}:\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}\to\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}},\qquad L_{T}(X)=TX, $$ and the right multiplication is $R_{T}(X)=XT$. Both are $\mathbb{C}$-linear, because the product is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear.

Proposition. $L_{T}=R_{T}$ for every $T$.

Proof. The multiplication is commutative, $TX=XT$ for all $X$, so the two maps agree at every element.

Corollary (the operator and the element). An ideal generated by $T$ is the image of $L_{T}$, and the annihilator of $T$ is its kernel, so the left and the right readings give one ideal and one annihilator; the ideal theory of Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions is the image–kernel theory of a single operator.

The Matrix of the Operator

In the basis $1,j$ the operator is the symmetric matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; in the idempotent basis it is diagonal.

Theorem (the matrix). In the complex basis $\{1,j\}$ the matrix of $L_{T}$ is $$ \mathsf L(T)=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{pmatrix}, $$ and in the idempotent basis $\{\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\}$ it is $$ \mathsf L_{\Pi}(T)=\begin{pmatrix}A+B&0\\ 0&A-B\end{pmatrix}=\operatorname{diag}(q_1,q_2). $$

Proof. For the first, $L_{T}(1)=T=A+Bj$ has coordinates $(A,B)$ and $L_{T}(j)=Tj=Aj+Bj^{2}=B+Aj$ has coordinates $(B,A)$, so the two columns are $(A,B)$ and $(B,A)$. For the second, $L_{T}(\Pi_{1})=T\Pi_{1}=q_1\Pi_{1}$ and $L_{T}(\Pi_{2})=T\Pi_{2}=q_2\Pi_{2}$, so the operator is diagonal with entries $q_1$ and $q_2$ in the idempotent basis.

Corollary (the eigenspaces). The idempotent lines $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{1}$ and $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{2}$ are the eigenspaces of $L_{T}$, with eigenvalues $q_1=A+B$ and $q_2=A-B$; in particular the operator is diagonalisable for every $T$, and its two eigenvalues are the idempotent coordinates.

The Trace, the Determinant and the Spectrum

The trace of the operator is $2A$, its determinant is the norm $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}$, and its characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^{2}-2A\lambda+\langle T\rangle_{\natural}$.

Theorem. For $T=A+Bj$, $$ \operatorname{Tr}L_{T}=2A,\qquad \det L_{T}=A^{2}-B^{2}=\langle T\rangle_{\natural},\qquad \chi_{L_{T}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}-2A\lambda+\langle T\rangle_{\natural}. $$

Proof. Read the matrix $\operatorname{diag}(q_1,q_2)$ in the idempotent basis: the trace is $q_1+q_2=2A$, the determinant is $q_1q_2=(A+B)(A-B)=A^{2}-B^{2}$, and the characteristic polynomial is $(\lambda-q_1)(\lambda-q_2)=\lambda^{2}-(q_1+q_2)\lambda+q_1q_2$.

Corollary (no nilpotent operator). $L_{T}$ is nilpotent if and only if $T=0$. It is invertible if and only if $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, that is if and only if $T$ is a unit; and it is a scalar operator, $L_{T}=A\,\mathrm{id}$, if and only if $B=0$, that is if and only if $T$ is a scalar.

Proof. A nilpotent operator has both eigenvalues zero, $q_1=q_2=0$, hence $A=B=0$. The determinant is the product of the eigenvalues, so the operator is invertible exactly when $q_1q_2\neq0$, which is the criterion of The Group of Units of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions. The operator is scalar exactly when its two eigenvalues are equal, $q_1=q_2$, that is $B=0$.

Composition and the Regular Representation

The map $T\mapsto L_{T}$ is an injective algebra homomorphism whose image is the two-dimensional commutative algebra of the matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.

Theorem (the regular representation). For all $T,W$, $$ L_{T}L_{W}=L_{TW}=L_{WT}=L_{W}L_{T},\qquad L_{1}=\mathrm{id}, $$ and the map $L:T\mapsto L_{T}$ is an injective algebra homomorphism from $(\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}},\cdot)$ into $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}})$.

Proof. $L_{T}L_{W}(X)=T(WX)=(TW)X=L_{TW}(X)$ by associativity, and $TW=WT$ by commutativity, which is the second equality; $L_{1}(X)=X$. The kernel is $\{T:L_{T}=0\}$, and $L_{T}=0$ evaluated at $1$ gives $T=0$, so $L$ is injective.

Corollary (the image). The image of $L$ is the set of the $2\times2$ matrices of the form $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $A,B\in\mathbb{C}$, a commutative two-dimensional subalgebra of $M_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ with the unit matrix, in which the two matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\ 0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\ 1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ play the roles of $1$ and $j$. It is not the whole of $M_{2}(\mathbb{C})$, which has complex dimension four.

The Kernel and the Invertibility

The kernel of $L_{T}$ is the annihilator of $T$; it is nonzero exactly when $T$ is a zero divisor, and then it is the other idempotent line.

Theorem (the kernel). For $T=A+Bj$:

  • if $T$ is a unit, $L_{T}$ is an automorphism and $\ker L_{T}=0$;
  • if $T$ lies on the idempotent line $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{1}$ and $T\neq0$, then $\ker L_{T}=\mathbb{C}\Pi_{2}$;
  • if $T$ lies on $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{2}$ and $T\neq0$, then $\ker L_{T}=\mathbb{C}\Pi_{1}$;
  • if $T=0$, $\ker L_{T}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}$.

Proof. The kernel is the set of $X$ with $TX=0$, the annihilator of $T$, computed in Annihilators of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions. For $T=q_1\Pi_{1}$ with $q_1\neq0$ the annihilator is $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{2}$, and symmetrically; for a unit the annihilator is $0$; for $T=0$ it is everything.

The Bi-module Structure and the Enveloping Algebra

The algebra is a bi-module over itself through the two multiplications, but they coincide, so every sandwich operator is a left multiplication and the enveloping algebra is two-dimensional.

Definition. The enveloping operator algebra is generated by the left and the right multiplications, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm e}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, acting on $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}$ by the sandwich $(T\otimes W^{\mathrm{opp}})\cdot X=T X W$.

Theorem (the sandwich). Because the algebra is commutative, $$ (T\otimes W^{\mathrm{opp}})\cdot X=T W\,X=L_{TW}(X), $$ so the sandwich operator of $T\otimes W^{\mathrm{opp}}$ is the left multiplication by $TW$, and the image of $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm e}$ in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}})$ is the two-dimensional space $\{L_{T}:T\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}\}$ of §Composition and the Regular Representation.

Proof. Commutativity gives $TXW=X(TW)=(TW)X$, which is the displayed identity; the image is the set of all $L_{T}$ because $T$ and $W$ range over the algebra.

Remark (the contrast with the biquaternion block). In the biquaternion category the enveloping algebra acts faithfully and exhausts the endomorphisms, $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\cong M_{4}(\mathbb{C})$; here the commutative product collapses the sandwich to a left multiplication, and the image has complex dimension two against the four of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}$. The collapsing is the operator reading of the central idempotents: a sandwich can move an element on both sides, but a central element acts the same way on both sides, so the two-sided action carries no more information than the one-sided one.

The Derivations and the Automorphisms

The algebra has no nonzero derivation, and its $\mathbb{C}$-algebra automorphisms are the two maps $\mathrm{id}$ and ${}^{\natural}$.

Definition. A derivation of the algebra is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $D$ with the Leibniz rule $D(TW)=D(T)W+TD(W)$; it is inner when $D=\mathrm{ad}_{S}:T\mapsto ST-TS$ for some $S$.

Theorem (no derivations). Every derivation of $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}$ into itself is zero.

Proof. A derivation satisfies $D(1)=D(1\cdot1)=2D(1)$, so $D(1)=0$. Evaluating on the idempotents, $D(\Pi_{i})=D(\Pi_{i}^{2})=2\Pi_{i}D(\Pi_{i})$, so $(2\Pi_{i}-1)D(\Pi_{i})=0$; but $2\Pi_{i}-1=\pm j$, a unit, so $D(\Pi_{i})=0$ for $i=1,2$. A derivation vanishing on the spanning set $1,\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}$ is zero.

Corollary (no inner derivations). Because the algebra is commutative every inner derivation vanishes, $\mathrm{ad}_{S}(T)=ST-TS=0$; the identification of $\mathrm{ad}$ with the zero map is the whole of the derivation algebra, and the theorem shows there is nothing else either.

Theorem (the automorphisms). The $\mathbb{C}$-algebra automorphisms of $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}$ are the two maps $$ \mathrm{id},\qquad {}^{\natural}:q_1\Pi_{1}+q_2\Pi_{2}\mapsto q_2\Pi_{1}+q_1\Pi_{2}, $$ so $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}$.

Proof. An automorphism permutes the primitive idempotents and acts on each factor $\mathbb{C}$ by a field automorphism; a $\mathbb{C}$-linear automorphism acts on each factor by the identity, so it is determined by the permutation of $\{\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\}$. The identity permutation gives $\mathrm{id}$ and the transposition gives the map displayed, which is the natural sign, $q_1\Pi_{1}+q_2\Pi_{2}\mapsto(q_1\Pi_{1}+q_2\Pi_{2})^{\natural}=q_1\Pi_{2}+q_2\Pi_{1}$. The group of permutations is $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$.

The Structure Group

The conformal group of the general plain bilinear form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the union of the two lines of the natural sign, isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$.

Definition. The structure group (conformal group) of a form $g$ is the set of invertible $T$ for which there is a scalar $\lambda(T)$ with $$ g(TX,TY)=\lambda(T)\,g(X,Y)\qquad\text{for all }X,Y . $$

Theorem. For the general plain bilinear form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ the structure group is $$ \{T:B(TX,TY)=\lambda B(X,Y)\}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\!\cdot 1\ \cup\ \mathbb{C}^{\times}\!\cdot j, $$ the nonzero elements of the scalar line and of the $j$-line, with $\lambda=A^{2}$ on the scalar line and $\lambda=B^{2}$ on the $j$-line; as a group it is $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$.

Proof. In the basis $1,j$ the form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ has Gram matrix $\mathrm I_{2}$ and the operator $L_{T}$ has matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so $B(TX,TY)=X^{\mathsf t}\,\mathsf L(T)^{\mathsf t}\mathsf L(T)\,Y$ with $$ \mathsf L(T)^{\mathsf t}\mathsf L(T)=\begin{pmatrix}A^{2}+B^{2}&2AB\\ 2AB&A^{2}+B^{2}\end{pmatrix}. $$ This is a scalar multiple of $\mathrm I_{2}$ exactly when $AB=0$, that is $A=0$ or $B=0$; then $\lambda=B^{2}$ on the $j$-line and $\lambda=A^{2}$ on the scalar line. The set is closed under the product and under inversion, with group law $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$: the product of two elements of the scalar line stays there, the product of one of each line lies on the $j$-line, and the product of two elements of the $j$-line lies on the scalar line.

Remark (two components). The structure group has two connected components, the scalar line and the $j$-line, and they are the fixed and the anti-fixed space of the natural sign. It is not the whole unit group $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}$: conformity with $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is stronger than invertibility, and it forces the element onto one of the two lines of the natural sign.

Worked Examples

The operator of a generic element. For $T=2+3j$ the matrix in the basis $1,j$ is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&3\\ 3&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with trace $4$, determinant $4-9=-5=\langle T\rangle_{\natural}$ and eigenvalues $5$ and $-1$ on the lines $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{1}$ and $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{2}$. The element is a unit because $-5\neq0$, and the operator is invertible.

The operator of an idempotent. For $T=\Pi_{1}$ the matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&1/2\\ 1/2&1/2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, of trace $1$, determinant $0$ and eigenvalues $1$ and $0$; its image is the line $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{1}$ and its kernel the line $\mathbb{C}\Pi_{2}$, the reading of the zero divisor.

The operator of $j$. For $T=j$ the matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\ 1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, an involution of trace $0$ and determinant $-1=\langle j\rangle_{\natural}$; it swaps the two scalar coordinates, and its eigenvectors are $\Pi_{1}$ and $\Pi_{2}$ with eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$. It is the conformal operator of the $j$-line of the structure group.

Summary

For an element $T=A+Bj$ the left and the right multiplications coincide, $L_{T}=R_{T}$, and in the basis $1,j$ the operator is the symmetric matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, diagonal $\operatorname{diag}(A+B,A-B)$ in the idempotent basis. Its trace is $2A$, its determinant is the norm $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}$, and its eigenvalues are the idempotent coordinates on the two idempotent lines; it is invertible exactly when $T$ is a unit, nilpotent only for $T=0$, and scalar only for $B=0$. The regular representation $T\mapsto L_{T}$ is an injective algebra homomorphism onto the two-dimensional commutative subalgebra of $M_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ of those matrices, which is not the whole endomorphism algebra. The kernel of $L_{T}$ is the annihilator of $T$: $0$ for a unit, the other idempotent line for a nonzero element of a line, everything for $0$. The enveloping operator algebra acts through the left multiplications alone, $L_{T}R_{W}=L_{TW}$, so its image is the same two-dimensional space and the sandwich carries no more than one-sided multiplication; the contrast with the biquaternion $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm e}\cong M_{4}(\mathbb{C})$ is the collapse produced by the central idempotents. The algebra has no nonzero derivation, inner or otherwise, and its $\mathbb{C}$-algebra automorphisms are $\mathrm{id}$ and the natural sign, a group $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$. The structure group of the general plain bilinear form is the union of the two lines of the natural sign, $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$.

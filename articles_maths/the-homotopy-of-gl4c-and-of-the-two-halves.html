The Homotopy of $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ and of the Two Halves at $n=4$

Introduction

The polar decomposition contracts $\mathrm{Herm}_4$ onto a point and leaves $U(4)$, so $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})\simeq U(4)$, with $\pi_0$ a point, $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_2=0$ and $\pi_3=\mathbb{Z}$; $U(4)$ is $S^{1}\times SU(4)$ up to homotopy, $SU(4)$ is simply connected of dimension $15$ with $\pi_4=\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $\pi_5=\mathbb{Z}$, and the two halves $\mathrm{Herm}_4$ and $\operatorname{Sym}_4^{++}$ are contractible, while the orthogonal quotient $\mathrm{GL}_4/O(4)$ is not.

The article proves the contraction, computes the homotopy groups of $U(4)$ from the bundle with fibre $SU(4)$ and the determinant circle, records the low groups of $SU(4)$, proves the contractibility of the Hermitian space and of the open cone, and contrasts the two quotients $\mathrm{GL}_4/U(4)$ and $\mathrm{GL}_4/O(4)$, one contractible and one not.

It assumes The Two Halves and Their Topology at $n=4$, The Exponential and the Lie Group Structure at $n=4$ and The Symmetric Space $\mathrm{GL}_4/O(4)$ and Its Complex Twin. The model at order two is The Homotopy of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ and of the Two Halves.

The Contraction

Theorem. $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ is diffeomorphic to $\operatorname{Herm}_4^{++}\times U(4)$, and $\operatorname{Herm}_4^{++}$ is convex, hence contractible.

Proof. The polar decomposition gives the diffeomorphism, and a convex set is contractible along straight lines.

Corollary. $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ is homotopy equivalent to $U(4)$, and the inclusion $U(4)\subset\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof. The projection onto the second factor is a deformation retraction up to homotopy.

Corollary. The homotopy groups of $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ and of $U(4)$ agree in every degree, and $\pi_0$ is a point because $U(4)$ is connected.

Proof. Homotopy equivalence preserves all homotopy groups, and the unitary group is connected since every unitary is $e^{iH}$.

Corollary. $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ is connected, and it is not simply connected.

Proof. It is homotopy equivalent to $U(4)$, whose fundamental group is computed below.

The Homotopy of the Unitary Group

Theorem. The determinant gives a fibre bundle $SU(4)\to U(4)\to S^{1}$, and $U(4)$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^{1}\times SU(4)$.

Proof. The determinant is a surjective homomorphism onto the circle with kernel $SU(4)$, so $U(4)\cong(S^{1}\times SU(4))/\mathbb{Z}_4$ with the finite group acting diagonally; the finite group acts trivially on all homotopy groups of the product, so the quotient has the same homotopy groups.

Corollary. $\pi_1(U(4))=\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the determinant circle, and $\pi_2(U(4))=0$.

Proof. The long exact sequence of the bundle, with $\pi_2(S^{1})=0$ and $\pi_1(SU(4))=0$; then $\pi_1(U(4))\cong\pi_1(S^{1})=\mathbb{Z}$.

Corollary. $\pi_3(U(4))=\mathbb{Z}$, and the generator is the class of the map $SU(2)\to U(4)$ given by the block inclusion.

Proof. $\pi_3(SU(4))=\mathbb{Z}$, and the determinant circle contributes nothing in degree three.

Corollary. $\pi_2$ vanishes for every Lie group, so no obstruction lives in degree two.

Proof. A Lie group is a homogeneous space with a smooth multiplication, and the second homotopy group of a Lie group is zero.

The Homotopy Groups of $\mathrm{GL}_4$

Theorem. The low homotopy groups are $$ \pi_0=\text{a point},\qquad \pi_1=\mathbb{Z},\qquad \pi_2=0,\qquad \pi_3=\mathbb{Z}. $$

Proof. The values for $U(4)$ transported along the homotopy equivalence, and $\pi_1$ from the determinant.

Corollary. The fundamental group is infinite cyclic and generated by the loop $t\mapsto e^{2\pi it}\mathrm I_4$, the full phase.

Proof. The determinant of that loop winds once around the circle.

Corollary. The universal cover of $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ is $\mathbb{C}\times\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb{C})$, of the same complex dimension $16$.

Proof. The group is $\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\cdot\mathrm{SL}_4$ modulo a finite centre; the determinant circle is covered by the plane and $\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ is simply connected.

The contrast with the real case. $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{R})$ has two components and its identity component is homotopy equivalent to $SO(4)$, whose fundamental group is $\mathbb{Z}_2$; the complex case has one component and an infinite cyclic group.

Proof. The real polar decomposition and the covering $\mathrm{Spin}(4)\to SO(4)$.

$SU(4)$

Theorem. $SU(4)$ is a simply connected compact Lie group of real dimension $15$, and $$ \pi_4(SU(4))=\mathbb{Z}_2,\qquad \pi_5(SU(4))=\mathbb{Z}. $$

Proof. Simple connectedness and the dimension are the standard facts for $SU(n)$; the two higher groups are the stable values for $SU(n)$ with $n\ge3$, computed from the bundle $SU(3)\to SU(4)\to S^{7}$ and the known groups of $SU(3)$.

Corollary. The dimension $15$ is $4^{2}-1$, and it is also $\dim\mathfrak{su}(4)=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_4$.

Proof. The Lie algebra of $SU(4)$ is the skew-Hermitian traceless part, of real dimension $15$.

Corollary. The fibration $SU(3)\to SU(4)\to S^{7}$ gives $\pi_k(SU(4))\cong\pi_k(SU(3))$ for $k\le5$, which is how the values are transported.

Proof. The long exact sequence with $\pi_k(S^{7})=0$ for $k\le6$.

The two low groups. $\pi_3(SU(4))=\mathbb{Z}$ and $\pi_4(SU(4))=\mathbb{Z}_2$, so there is an integer obstruction in degree three and a mod-two obstruction in degree four.

Proof. The values above.

What is omitted. Nothing is asserted about $\pi_6(SU(4))$ or beyond; the article records only the groups it can compute.

Proof. Deliberate omission.

The Contractible Halves

Theorem. $\operatorname{Herm}_4$ is a real vector space of dimension $16$, hence contractible; $\operatorname{Herm}_4^{++}$ is convex, hence contractible; and the real symmetric cone $\operatorname{Sym}_4^{++}$ is convex, hence contractible.

Proof. Convex subsets of vector spaces are contractible.

Corollary. All homotopy groups of $\operatorname{Herm}_4$, of $\operatorname{Herm}_4^{++}$ and of $\operatorname{Sym}_4^{++}$ vanish, and their $\pi_0$ is a point.

Proof. A contractible space has the homotopy type of a point.

Corollary. The two halves contribute nothing to the topology of $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$; the whole topology sits in the unitary group.

Proof. The product with a contractible factor is homotopy equivalent to the other factor.

The non-contractible pieces. $\operatorname{Herm}_4$ minus the origin, and the invertible Hermitian matrices, are not contractible: the first retracts to a sphere of dimension $15$.

Proof. The radial retraction onto the unit sphere, and the complement of the singular set retracts to the invertible part which contains the sphere.

The Two Quotients

Theorem. $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})/U(4)$ is contractible, while $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})/O(4,\mathbb{C})$ is not.

Proof. The first quotient is $\operatorname{Herm}_4^{++}$ under the polar decomposition, which is convex. The second is the space of nondegenerate complex symmetric forms, which contains the circle of scalars $\lambda\mathrm I_4$ with $\lambda\neq0$ up to the action, giving a nontrivial loop; more precisely $\pi_1(\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})/O(4,\mathbb{C}))=\mathbb{Z}$, since $\pi_1(O(4,\mathbb{C}))=\mathbb{Z}_2$ maps trivially into $\pi_1(\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C}))=\mathbb{Z}$.

Corollary. The symmetric space of noncompact type $\mathrm{GL}_4/U(4)$ is a point up to homotopy, and the Riemannian symmetric space $\mathrm{GL}_4/O(4)$ has the homotopy of a circle factor.

Proof. The two computations.

Corollary. The choice of the compact form inside the complex group decides the topology of the quotient: the unitary form gives a contractible quotient, the orthogonal form does not.

Proof. The two quotients.

Summary

The polar decomposition makes $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ homotopy equivalent to $U(4)$, which is $S^{1}\times SU(4)$ up to homotopy. The low homotopy groups are a point in degree zero, $\mathbb{Z}$ in degrees one and three, and zero in degree two.

$SU(4)$ is simply connected of dimension fifteen, with $\pi_4=\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $\pi_5=\mathbb{Z}$. The Hermitian space and both positive cones are contractible, so the whole topology of the general linear group sits in its unitary part.

The quotient by the unitary group is contractible because it is the convex cone of positive definite matrices; the quotient by the orthogonal group is not, its fundamental group being infinite cyclic. The compact form chosen inside the complex group decides the topology of the quotient.

The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The multiplication operators of a product are the maps $\tilde q\mapsto\tilde p\circ\tilde q$ obtained by fixing one slot. For the symmetric quaternionic product the maps are as degenerate as a nonzero multiplication can be: each of them sends the whole algebra onto the scalar line, and each is a scalar multiple of the identity when restricted to that line. This article computes the operators, their matrices, their ranks and their kernels, their composition and the enveloping algebra they generate, their adjoints with respect to the block's form, the bimodule structure they give, and the derivations of the block; the biquaternion block is the comparison.

Boundaries. The product is Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions; the form is The Quaternion Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, above this article in this category; the definitions of the operator, the adjoint and the derivation are Definitions for the Study of the 6 Algebraic Structures and The Two General Products of the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space. The parallel over the plain product is The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Plain Algebra; the biquaternion counterpart is The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$; an element is $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\mathbf q$ with $\mathbf q=\sum_kq_ke_k$. The quaternion conjugation is $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_0e_0-\mathbf q$; the bar is the identity, the star coincides with the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$. The quaternion form is $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$; the symmetric quaternionic product is $\tilde p\star\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_0$. The block is commutative, so the left and right multiplication operators coincide, and there is one family $L_{\tilde p}$, with

$$ L_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde p\star\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_0 . $$

The Operators, Their Matrices and Their Ranks

Theorem (the operator is the form on the line). For every $\tilde p$ the operator $L_{\tilde p}$ is the composite of the linear form $N(\tilde p,\cdot)$ with the inclusion of the scalar line, and its matrix in the basis is

$$ [L_{\tilde p}]_{0\mu}=p_\mu,\qquad [L_{\tilde p}]_{\nu\mu}=0\ \ (\nu\neq0); $$

that is, row $0$ is the coordinate row of $\tilde p$ and every other row vanishes.

Proof. $L_{\tilde p}(e_\mu)=N(\tilde p,e_\mu)e_0=p_\mu e_0$; the $\mu$-th column of the matrix is therefore $p_\mu$ in the zeroth row and $0$ below, for every $\mu$.

Corollary (rank, image and kernel). For $\tilde p\neq0$ the operator $L_{\tilde p}$ has rank $1$, image the scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_0$, kernel the hyperplane $\tilde p^{\perp}=\{\tilde q:N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=0\}$ of real dimension $3$, trace $p_0$ and determinant $0$. For $\tilde p=0$ the operator is the zero operator, of rank $0$.

Proof. The matrix has a single nonzero row, so its rank is $1$ when the row is nonzero, that is when $\tilde p\neq0$, and its image is spanned by $e_0$. The kernel is the set of $\tilde q$ with $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=0$, a hyperplane by definition; the trace is the single diagonal entry $[L_{\tilde p}]_{00}=p_0$, and the determinant vanishes for a matrix with a zero row.

Remark (every operator is a scalar on the scalar line). Restricted to the image, $L_{\tilde p}(ae_0)=a\,p_0\,e_0$, so on the scalar line the operator acts as the scalar $p_0$; this is the phrase "scalar multiple of the identity on the scalar line". The dependence of the operator on $\tilde p$ is through all four coordinates off the line and only through $p_0$ on it.

Composition and the Enveloping Algebra

Theorem (the composition law). For all $\tilde p,\tilde q$,

$$ L_{\tilde p}\circ L_{\tilde q}=L_{\tilde p_0\tilde q}=p_0\,L_{\tilde q}, $$

Proof. $(L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q})(\tilde r)=L_{\tilde p}\bigl(N(\tilde q,\tilde r)e_0\bigr)=N(\tilde q,\tilde r)L_{\tilde p}(e_0)=N(\tilde q,\tilde r)p_0e_0=N(p_0\tilde q,\tilde r)e_0=L_{p_0\tilde q}(\tilde r)$. The second form is the homogeneity of $L$ in its subscript.

Corollary (no multiplicativity, no commutativity). The map $\tilde p\mapsto L_{\tilde p}$ is injective but not multiplicative: $L_{\tilde p\star\tilde q}=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)L_{e_0}$, which differs from $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=p_0L_{\tilde q}$ in general, and the two differ at $\tilde p=e_1,\tilde q=e_1$, where $N(e_1,e_1)L_{e_0}=L_{e_0}$ while $L_{e_1}L_{e_1}=0$. The monoid of operators is not commutative either: $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$ and $L_{\tilde q}L_{\tilde p}=L_{q_0\tilde p}$ differ at $\tilde p=e_0,\tilde q=e_1$, where the first is $L_{e_1}\neq0$ and the second is $L_0=0$.

Proof. The operator of a product is $L_{N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_0}=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)L_{e_0}$ because $L$ is linear in the subscript; the composition is the theorem. The witnesses are evaluated directly.

Theorem (the enveloping structure). The linear span of the operators is

$$ \mathcal E=\{L_{\tilde p}:\tilde p\in\mathbb{H}\}, $$

a subspace of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})$ of real dimension $4$; it is closed under composition, hence an algebra, and the linear isomorphism $\tilde p\mapsto L_{\tilde p}$ carries it to the algebra $(\mathbb{H},\circ)$ with

$$ \tilde p\circ\tilde q=p_0\,\tilde q . $$

That algebra is associative with left unit $e_0$ and no right unit; its idempotents are $0$ and the elements with $p_0=1$.

Proof. Every $L_{\tilde p}=\sum_\mu p_\mu L_{e_\mu}$ is a combination of the four operators $L_{e_\mu}$, and these are linearly independent because $[L_{e_\mu}]_{0\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}$; so the span has dimension $4$ and the subscript map is an isomorphism onto the stated product, by the composition law $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$. The product $\circ$ is associative because $(\tilde p\circ\tilde q)\circ\tilde r=(p_0\tilde q)\circ\tilde r=p_0q_0\tilde r=\tilde p\circ(\tilde q\circ\tilde r)$; $e_0$ is a left unit, and a right unit $\tilde u$ would satisfy $\tilde p\circ\tilde u=p_0\tilde u=\tilde p$ for all $\tilde p$, which fails at $\tilde p=e_1$. Finally $\tilde p\circ\tilde p=p_0\tilde p=\tilde p$ exactly when $\tilde p=0$ or $p_0=1$, which are the idempotents of the algebra.

Remark (the operators are the four coordinate projections on the line). The basis operators are $L_{e_\mu}(\tilde q)=q_\mu e_0$; each projects one coordinate onto the scalar line. The whole operator family is the four-dimensional space of these projections, and the multiplication of operators is the multiplication of a scalar coordinate by the second factor.

The Adjoint with Respect to the Form

The form $N$ is non-degenerate, so every operator has an adjoint $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}$ characterised by $N(L_{\tilde p}\tilde x,\tilde y)=N(\tilde x,L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}\tilde y)$.

Theorem (the adjoint formula). For every $\tilde p$ and $\tilde y$,

$$ L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}(\tilde y)=y_0\,\tilde p=\sum_\mu p_\mu y_0\,e_\mu, $$

an operator depending on $\tilde y$ through $y_0$ alone, with values in the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$.

Proof. $N(L_{\tilde p}\tilde x,\tilde y)=N(N(\tilde p,\tilde x)e_0,\tilde y)=N(\tilde p,\tilde x)y_0$, and $N(\tilde x,y_0\tilde p)=y_0N(\tilde x,\tilde p)=y_0N(\tilde p,\tilde x)$, so the displayed operator satisfies the defining identity; it is unique because $N$ is non-degenerate.

Corollary (self-adjointness is centrality). $L_{\tilde p}$ is self-adjoint, $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde p}$, if and only if $\tilde p$ is central, that is $\mathbf p=0$.

Proof. $L_{\tilde p}(\tilde y)=N(\tilde p,\tilde y)e_0$ is scalar valued, while $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}(\tilde y)=y_0\tilde p$ has vector part $y_0\mathbf p$; the two are equal for all $\tilde y$ exactly when $\mathbf p=0$. For central $\tilde p=p_0e_0$ both are $p_0y_0e_0$.

Remark (the adjoint is not the multiplication). The adjoint reads only the scalar coordinate $y_0$ of its argument and its value lies in the line of $\tilde p$; the multiplication reads the full pairing and its value lies on the scalar line. The two agree exactly at a central multiplier.

The Bimodule Structure

Proposition (the module and the bimodule). With the action $L_{\tilde p}\cdot\tilde x=\tilde p\star\tilde x$, the space $\mathbb{H}$ is a faithful left module over the enveloping algebra $\mathcal E$; the right action $\tilde x\cdot L_{\tilde p}=L_{\tilde p}(\tilde x)$ is the same, so the module is a symmetric bimodule. The annihilator of the whole module is $\{0\}$, and the module is faithful.

Proof. The action is by definition a linear action of the algebra $\mathcal E$, and associativity of the action is the associativity of composition; the left and right actions coincide because $\mathcal E$ acts by linear maps on the space, and the block's own product is commutative so no asymmetry is introduced. The annihilator is the set of $L_{\tilde p}$ with $L_{\tilde p}=0$, which is $\{\tilde p=0\}$ by the injectivity of the subscript map.

Remark (why the bimodule is not trivial). The enveloping algebra $\mathcal E\cong(\mathbb{H},\circ)$ is not commutative, so the left and right regular modules over $\mathcal E$ are different; but both act on $\mathbb{H}$ through the same evaluation, and the bimodule is symmetric. The action is not the regular action of $\mathbb{H}$: the multiplication of the module is the collapse $\tilde p\star\tilde x=N(\tilde p,\tilde x)e_0$, which loses the vector part of the result.

The Derivations of the Block

A derivation of the block is a linear map $D$ of $\mathbb{H}$ with $D(\tilde p\star\tilde q)=D\tilde p\star\tilde q+\tilde p\star D\tilde q$ (Definitions for the Study of the 6 Algebraic Structures); an inner derivation is $\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde p}(\tilde x)=\tilde p\star\tilde x-\tilde x\star\tilde p$.

Proposition (the inner derivations vanish). Every inner derivation of the block is zero, because the block is commutative; the derived law of the block is the zero operation.

Proof. $\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde p}(\tilde x)=\tilde p\star\tilde x-\tilde x\star\tilde p=0$ by commutativity.

Theorem (the derivation algebra). A linear map $D$ is a derivation of the block if and only if

$$ D e_0=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad N(D\tilde x,\tilde y)+N(\tilde x,D\tilde y)=0\text{ for all }\tilde x,\tilde y, $$

that is $D$ vanishes on the scalar line and is skew-adjoint for the form. Writing $D$ in the basis as the block matrix of $0$ on $\mathbb{R}e_0$ and a $3\times3$ block $S$ on the vector subspace, the condition is $S^{\mathsf T}+S=0$; the derivation algebra is therefore $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ on the vector subspace, of real dimension $3$.

Proof. The derivation identity on the block reads $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)De_0=\bigl(N(D\tilde p,\tilde q)+N(\tilde p,D\tilde q)\bigr)e_0$ for all $\tilde p,\tilde q$. At $\tilde p=\tilde q=e_0$ it gives $De_0=2(De_0)_0e_0$, so $De_0$ is central and equal to twice its scalar part, hence $De_0=0$; the identity becomes $N(D\tilde p,\tilde q)+N(\tilde p,D\tilde q)=0$, the skew-adjointness. Since $De_0=0$, the first column of the matrix vanishes; skew-adjointness makes the first row vanish as well and the remaining $3\times3$ block skew-symmetric. The skew-symmetric $3\times3$ matrices are the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$, of dimension $3$.

Corollary (the weight is forced to zero). There is no derivation with a weight $\delta\neq0$ in the identity $N(D\tilde x,\tilde y)+N(\tilde x,D\tilde y)=\delta\,N(\tilde x,\tilde y)$ for all $\tilde x,\tilde y$: at $\tilde x=\tilde y=e_0$ the identity reads $2(De_0)_0=\delta$, and it also forces $De_0$ to be central, $De_0=(De_0)_0e_0$; the same pair in the form $De_0=\delta e_0$ gives $\delta=2\delta$, hence $\delta=0$. The derivation algebra is exactly the weight-zero one of the theorem.

Proof. The two readings of the pair $(e_0,e_0)$ are as displayed; comparing them gives the conclusion.

Proposition (the operator bracket is not a derivation). The bracket $[L_{\tilde p},L_{\tilde q}]=L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}-L_{\tilde q}L_{\tilde p}=p_0L_{\tilde q}-q_0L_{\tilde p}$ does not satisfy the derivation identity in general. At $\tilde p=e_0,\tilde q=e_1$ the bracket is $L_{e_1}$, and at $(\tilde x,\tilde y)=(e_1,e_0)$ the two sides of the derivation identity are $0$ and $e_0$.

Proof. $[L_{e_0},L_{e_1}]=L_{e_1}$ by the composition law. The derivation identity at $(e_1,e_0)$ reads $[L_{e_0},L_{e_1}](e_1\star e_0)=[L_{e_0},L_{e_1}](e_1)\star e_0+e_1\star[L_{e_0},L_{e_1}](e_0)$. The left side is $L_{e_1}(N(e_1,e_0)e_0)=L_{e_1}(0)=0$; the right side is $L_{e_1}(e_1)\star e_0+0=e_0\star e_0=N(e_0,e_0)e_0=e_0$. The two differ, so the bracket is not a derivation of the block. The bracket does vanish on $e_0$, so it passes the first condition of the theorem, and the obstruction is the skew-adjointness condition $N(D\tilde x,\tilde y)+N(\tilde x,D\tilde y)=0$.

Remark (the correction to the brief). The brief states that the operators "commute" and are "self-adjoint for the form". Neither holds: the composition is $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$ and $L_{\tilde q}L_{\tilde p}=L_{q_0\tilde p}$, which differ at $(e_0,e_1)$, and self-adjointness holds exactly at a central multiplier. What is true is that the operators are the coordinate projections of the block onto its scalar line and that they generate the four-dimensional enveloping algebra $(\mathbb{H},\circ)$.

Proposition (the same structure over the two fields). The operator calculus of the quaternion block is the operator calculus of the biquaternion block with the complex coordinates replaced by real ones and the complex dimension replaced by real dimension; the formulas, the rank, the kernel, the adjoint and the derivation algebra have the same shape.

Proof. The governing formula is the same, $L_{\tilde p}\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_0$, with the coefficient defined over the ground field; each displayed identity is proved in the same way in the two blocks. The form-preserving group is The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra (biquaternion) and is quoted for the comparison only.

Remark (the block's operators are the most degenerate nonzero operators of the six). Every operator of every one of the six structures has rank at least one off the zero element, but the block's operators have rank exactly one and a kernel of dimension three; the plain block's operators are invertible off the zero divisors and the general block's are injective. The collapse of the block is the collapse of its operators, and it is the same collapse read twice.

Summary

The multiplication operators of the block are $L_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde p\star\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_0$, with the coordinates of $\tilde p$ in the zeroth row of the matrix and zero elsewhere; off zero they have rank $1$, image the scalar line, kernel the hyperplane $\tilde p^{\perp}$ of dimension $3$, trace $p_0$ and determinant $0$, and on the scalar line they act as the scalar $p_0$. Their composition is $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$, so they do not commute and are not multiplicative; they span the four-dimensional enveloping algebra $\mathcal E\cong(\mathbb{H},\circ)$ with $\tilde p\circ\tilde q=p_0\tilde q$, associative with left unit $e_0$ alone, and $\mathbb{H}$ is a faithful symmetric $\mathcal E$-bimodule. The adjoint is $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}(\tilde y)=y_0\tilde p$, and $L_{\tilde p}$ is self-adjoint exactly at a central multiplier. The inner derivations vanish, the derivation algebra is $\{De_0=0,\ D^{\mathsf T}+D=0\}\cong\mathfrak{so}(3)$ of real dimension $3$, no derivation has nonzero weight, and the operator bracket is not a derivation in general. The whole calculus is the biquaternion calculus with real coordinates.

Idempotents of the Symmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

The symmetric plain algebra $\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}$ is the real space $\mathbb{H}$ under the Jordan product $\tilde p\bullet\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p\tilde q+\tilde q\tilde p)$ of Introduction to the Symmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions. This article determines the idempotents of the block, classifies them, and reads the correspondence each one produces.

The idempotents of the block are the idempotents of the algebra: the two squares agree, $\tilde\pi\bullet\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi\tilde\pi$, so the equation $\tilde\pi\bullet\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi$ is the plain equation $\tilde\pi^2=\tilde\pi$. The plain product of $\mathbb{H}$ is a division rule, with the unit $e_0$ and no zero divisor, so the plain equation has only the two trivial solutions and the idempotent class of the block is the two-point class $\{0,e_0\}$. The article states that reading and draws the two consequences the algebra alone does not draw: the correspondence with the roots of $+e_0$, and the Jordan Peirce decomposition, which is degenerate here.

The block is a Jordan algebra, and over $\mathbb{R}$ the decisive fact is the definiteness of the norm: the square of a pure element is the non-positive scalar $-|\mathbf{u}|^2e_0$, so the pure root of $+e_0$ that a non-trivial idempotent needs by $\tilde\pi=\tfrac12(e_0+\mathbf{u})$ does not exist, and the class collapses. The complex block, whose pure square is indefinite, carries the whole family and the projector calculus that goes with it.

An idempotent is defined relative to a product, and the definition is stated once for a general product in Definitions for the Study of the 6 Algebraic Structures, §The Element Vocabulary; throughout this article the product is $\bullet$ and, unqualified, idempotent means idempotent of $\bullet$. The idempotents of the plain product are read in Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions, where the division property gives the same two solutions, and in Idempotents of the General Plain Algebra, which owns that class; the associated zero divisors are Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Plain Algebra, above this article in this category; the roots that index the class are Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Symmetric Plain Algebra, above this article in this category; the specific-theory reading is The Square, the Idempotents and the Jordan Inverse of the Symmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category; and the criterion that separates the block from the complex one is The Specificities of the Symmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, so that $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_je_k=-e_ke_j$ for $j\neq k$. An element is $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\mathbf q$ with $\mathbf q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}q_ke_k$ and $q_\mu\in\mathbb{R}$; $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q=\sum_kp_kq_k$ is the Euclidean dot product of the vector parts. The product with the mark is $\tilde p\bullet\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p\tilde q+\tilde q\tilde p)$; the plain square is $\tilde q^2=(q_0^2-|\mathbf q|^2)e_0+2q_0\mathbf q$; the norm is $N(\tilde q)=\tilde q\tilde q^{\natural}=q_0^2+|\mathbf q|^2$. The natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign. The scalar line is $\mathbb{R}e_0$, which is the centre, and the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$ (Introduction to the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions). The left multiplication is $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde p}\tilde q=\tilde p\bullet\tilde q$.

The Definition and the Equation

Definition (idempotent of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$; then $\tilde\pi$ is an idempotent of $\star$ when

$$ \tilde\pi\star\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi . $$

The origin is an idempotent of every product, and a product with a unit has the unit as an idempotent.

Throughout this article the product is the symmetric plain product $\bullet$, and the equation is $\tilde\pi\bullet\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi$. Because the square of the block is the plain square, the equation is the equation of the algebra,

$$ \tilde\pi^{2}=\tilde\pi\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \tilde\pi\bullet\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi , $$

and the idempotents of the block are exactly the idempotents of the plain product. The classification of the plain product applies verbatim; it is restated here in the block's coordinates and with the block's reading.

The Classification

Theorem (the idempotents). The idempotents of the block are exactly the origin and the unit:

$$ \tilde\pi\in\{0,\ e_0\}. $$

There is no non-trivial idempotent.

Proof. Write the equation as $\tilde\pi\bullet\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi$, that is $\tilde\pi^2=\tilde\pi$, and factor it as $\tilde\pi(\tilde\pi-e_0)=0$. The plain product of $\mathbb{H}$ is a division rule: it has the two-sided unit $e_0$ and no zero divisor, every non-zero element being invertible with $\tilde\pi^{-1}=\tilde\pi^{\natural}/N(\tilde\pi)$ (Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions; Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra). A vanishing product in a division algebra has a vanishing factor, so either $\tilde\pi=0$ or $\tilde\pi-e_0=0$, and the class is the two elements displayed.

The same conclusion in the block's coordinates is the argument of Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Plain Algebra, above this article in this category. Write $\tilde\pi=\pi_0e_0+\boldsymbol{\pi}$ and read the plain square $\tilde\pi^2=(\pi_0^2-|\boldsymbol{\pi}|^2)e_0+2\pi_0\boldsymbol{\pi}$. The equation $\tilde\pi^2=\tilde\pi$ is the pair

$$ 2\pi_0\boldsymbol{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\pi},\qquad \pi_0^2-|\boldsymbol{\pi}|^2=\pi_0 . $$

The first forces $(2\pi_0-1)\boldsymbol{\pi}=0$. If $\boldsymbol{\pi}=0$ the second gives $\pi_0^2=\pi_0$, that is $\tilde\pi=0$ or $\tilde\pi=e_0$; if $\boldsymbol{\pi}\neq0$ then $\pi_0=\tfrac12$ and the second reads $\tfrac14-|\boldsymbol{\pi}|^2=\tfrac12$, that is $|\boldsymbol{\pi}|^2=-\tfrac14$, which has no real solution. The two cases exhaust the equation and no non-trivial idempotent occurs.

Remark (why the class collapses over $\mathbb{R}$). The standard construction of a non-trivial idempotent of a symmetrised product is the element $\tfrac12(e_0+\mathbf{u})$ with $\mathbf{u}$ a pure root of $+e_0$: the square $\tfrac14(e_0+2\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}^2)$ equals $\tfrac12(e_0+\mathbf{u})$ exactly when $\mathbf{u}^2=e_0$, and it is the pure roots of $+e_0$ that produce the non-trivial family, the scalar roots $\mathbf{u}=\pm e_0$ producing only the two trivial idempotents. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the square of a pure element is $\mathbf{u}\bullet\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}^2=-|\mathbf{u}|^2e_0$, which is a non-positive scalar, so it equals $+e_0$ only for $|\mathbf{u}|^2=-1$, a value no real vector attains. There is no pure root of $+e_0$, the construction never closes, and the class is the two trivial idempotents.

Corollary (the class is two-point). The idempotent class of the block is $\{0,e_0\}$, and the two are orthogonal, $0\bullet e_0=0$.

The Idempotents and the Roots

The idempotents of a degree-two block correspond to the roots of $+e_0$ under the affine map $\tilde\pi\mapsto2\tilde\pi-e_0$. The map is an involution of the space, and it converts the idempotent equation into the root equation and back:

$$ \tilde\pi\bullet\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad (2\tilde\pi-e_0)\bullet(2\tilde\pi-e_0)=e_0 . $$

Proof. Expand in the plain product: $\tilde\pi^2=\tilde\pi$ is equivalent to $4\tilde\pi^2-4\tilde\pi+e_0=e_0$, that is to $(2\tilde\pi-e_0)^2=e_0$. The two squares are equal because the square of the block is the plain square.

Corollary (the correspondence). The map $\tilde\pi\mapsto2\tilde\pi-e_0$ is a bijection from the idempotents onto the roots of $+e_0$, and the roots of $+e_0$ are the two points $\pm e_0$ (Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Symmetric Plain Algebra, §The Roots of $+e_0$, above this article in this category). The correspondence is

$$ 0\longleftrightarrow-e_0,\qquad e_0\longleftrightarrow+e_0, $$

so the two-point idempotent class is the image of the two-point root class, and the count of the idempotents of the block equals the count of the roots of $+e_0$, two on each side.

Remark (the roots of $-e_0$ index no idempotent). The roots of $-e_0$ are the pure elements of norm one, a two-sphere in the vector subspace (Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Symmetric Plain Algebra, §The Roots of $-e_0$, above this article in this category), and they do not index idempotents: the map $\mathbf{u}\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\mathbf{u})$ sends such a root to an element whose square is $\tfrac14(1-|\mathbf{u}|^2)e_0+\tfrac12\mathbf{u}=\tfrac12\mathbf{u}$, a pure element and not an idempotent. Only the roots of $+e_0$ serve, and over $\mathbb{R}$ there are two of them.

The Orthogonality and the Peirce Decomposition

The Complementary Idempotent

Proposition (the complementary idempotent). If $\tilde\pi$ is an idempotent of the block, then so is $e_0-\tilde\pi$, and the two are orthogonal:

$$ (e_0-\tilde\pi)^{\bullet2}=e_0-\tilde\pi,\qquad \tilde\pi\bullet(e_0-\tilde\pi)=0 . $$

Proof. $(e_0-\tilde\pi)\bullet(e_0-\tilde\pi)=e_0-2\tilde\pi+\tilde\pi^{\bullet2}=e_0-\tilde\pi$, using $\tilde\pi^{\bullet2}=\tilde\pi$; and $\tilde\pi\bullet(e_0-\tilde\pi)=\tilde\pi-\tilde\pi^{\bullet2}=0$.

Corollary (the complement swaps the two idempotents). For the two idempotents of the block the map $\tilde\pi\mapsto e_0-\tilde\pi$ is the exchange

$$ 0\longleftrightarrow e_0, $$

and $0\bullet e_0=0$. The complement is available because $e_0$ is the unit, and it is the reason the two-point class is closed under it; the origin and the unit are orthogonal idempotents.

The Jordan Peirce Decomposition

Theorem (the cubic Peirce identity). For every idempotent $\tilde\pi$ of the block the left multiplication satisfies

$$ 2(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde\pi})^3-3(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde\pi})^2+L^{\bullet}_{\tilde\pi}=0, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad L^{\bullet}_{\tilde\pi}\bigl(2L^{\bullet}_{\tilde\pi}-\mathrm{id}\bigr)\bigl(L^{\bullet}_{\tilde\pi}-\mathrm{id}\bigr)=0 . $$

Proof. The identity is the polynomial identity of an idempotent in a Jordan algebra (Jordan Algebras); in the block it is immediate at the only two idempotents. At $\tilde\pi=e_0$ the operator is the identity, $L^{\bullet}_{e_0}=\mathrm{id}$, and $2-3+1=0$; at $\tilde\pi=0$ the operator vanishes, $L^{\bullet}_{0}=0$, and the identity is $0=0$.

Theorem (the decomposition). Let $\tilde\pi$ be an idempotent and let

$$ J_{\lambda}(\tilde\pi)=\{\tilde x\in\mathbb{H}:\ \tilde\pi\bullet\tilde x=\lambda\tilde x\},\qquad \lambda\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}. $$

Then the decomposition is trivial:

$$ J_1(e_0)=\mathbb{H},\quad J_{1/2}(e_0)=J_0(e_0)=0; \qquad J_0(0)=\mathbb{H},\quad J_1(0)=J_{1/2}(0)=0 . $$

Proof. $L^{\bullet}_{e_0}=\mathrm{id}$ has the single eigenvalue $1$ with multiplicity four, so its Peirce space is the whole space and the other two vanish; $L^{\bullet}_{0}=0$ has the single eigenvalue $0$ with multiplicity four, and the roles are exchanged.

Remark (the would-be shape does not open). A non-trivial idempotent of a degree-two Jordan algebra of this kind would split the space into three Peirce spaces of dimensions $1,2,1$, with a two-dimensional middle space. The block has no such idempotent, so the middle space never opens and the Peirce calculus is degenerate. The block carries no projector calculus of a non-trivial idempotent, no corner subalgebra and no ideal lattice, because over $\mathbb{R}$ the idempotent that would carry them does not exist: the objects are absent, and the calculus is empty with them. The element-class reading is The Specificities of the Symmetric Plain Algebra, §The Degenerate Classes, below this article in this category, and the specific-theory companion is The Square, the Idempotents and the Jordan Inverse of the Symmetric Plain Algebra, §The Peirce Decomposition, below this article in this category.

The Reading on the Remarkable Subspaces

Over $\mathbb{R}$ the remarkable subspaces are the scalar line, which is the centre, and the vector subspace (Introduction to the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions).

The centre. The scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_0$ is the centre of the block, and its elements are the central elements $\lambda e_0$. A scalar element is idempotent exactly when $\lambda^2=\lambda$, that is when $\lambda\in\{0,1\}$, so the centre contains the two idempotents $0$ and $e_0$ and no other. Every idempotent of the block is central, the class being $\{0,e_0\}$.

The vector subspace. A non-zero pure element $\mathbf{p}$ has the square

$$ \mathbf{p}\bullet\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^2=-|\mathbf{p}|^2e_0, $$

a non-zero scalar, so it can equal the pure element $\mathbf{p}$ only if both vanish: the vector subspace contains no idempotent but the origin, and the unit $e_0$ is the only idempotent outside it.

Summary

The idempotents of the block are the idempotents of the plain product, the two squares being equal, and the plain product is a division rule: they are exactly the origin and the unit, $\tilde\pi\in\{0,e_0\}$, a two-point class with no non-trivial member. The reason is that a non-trivial idempotent needs a pure root of $+e_0$ and the pure square is the non-positive scalar $-|\mathbf{u}|^2e_0$, which never equals $+e_0$. The complement $e_0-\tilde\pi$ is an idempotent and is orthogonal to $\tilde\pi$, and it exchanges the origin and the unit. The idempotents correspond to the roots of $+e_0$ under $\tilde\pi\mapsto2\tilde\pi-e_0$, and the roots of $+e_0$ being the two points $\pm e_0$, the correspondence is $0\leftrightarrow-e_0$ and $e_0\leftrightarrow+e_0$. The cubic Peirce identity holds at both idempotents, the Peirce decomposition is trivial, $J_1(e_0)=\mathbb{H}$ and $J_0(0)=\mathbb{H}$, and the would-be middle space of dimension two never opens: the block has no projector calculus of a non-trivial idempotent because it has no non-trivial idempotent. On the remarkable subspaces the idempotents are central, and the vector subspace contains no idempotent but the origin.

Square Roots of a General Element in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The square root of an element is defined by an equation in which the product occurs, so it is read in the product (Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space). The block of this article is the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q =\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p\bigr) =p_0\vec q-q_0\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q , $$

which is alternating and without a unit (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions). The article solves, for a general element $a\in\mathbb{H}$, the equation

$$ \tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=a $$

in the element $\tilde\xi$.

The reduction is the same as the reduction of the three central values, and it is the whole of the problem: the block is alternating, so its square is zero at every element, and the equation is the equation $0=a$. The origin alone has square roots, and every element of the space is a square root of it; no nonzero element has a square root. The classification is therefore the two-line classification of the equation $0=a$, and it is the extreme case of the general reduction of Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra, where the quadratic in the scalar part supplies the finite families of the plain theory. The central values are read off as a special case, and their article is cited.

Conventions. Throughout, $\tilde p=p_0e_0+\vec p$, $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\vec q$ and $\tilde\xi=\xi_0e_0+\vec\xi$ name elements of $\mathbb{H}$ with real coefficients, $(\vec p,\vec q)$ is the dot product and $\vec p\times\vec q$ the cross product of the vector parts, and $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_0e_0-\vec q$ is the quaternion conjugation; the bar is the identity and the star coincides with the natural conjugation, so no bare bar is written on an element of this block. The operation is written $\diamond$ and never $\wedge^{\natural}$ outside a citation. The equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=a$ is read in the product $\diamond$; the element $a$ is general, with no central or pure restriction, and the value $a=0$ is the origin of the space. The quaternion form of the space is $N(\tilde p)=p_0^2+\lvert\vec p\rvert^2$.

Boundaries. The operation and its alternation are Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions; the definitions of the square and of the square root of an element are Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space. The general reduction of the equation in the plain product is Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra; the special case of the three central values is Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra; the square-zero elements and the zero divisors are Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra and Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

The Notion and Its Product

Definition (square root of an element). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$ and let $a\in\mathbb{H}$. A square root of $a$ in $\star$ is an element $\tilde\xi$ with

$$ \tilde\xi\star\tilde\xi=a . $$

The roots of a central value are the special case $a=\lambda e_0$, and at the central values the two notions coincide, no nonzero condition being imposed on a root in this chapter.

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $\diamond$, and unqualified, square root means a square root of the block. The general reduction of the equation in the plain product, and the square it uses, are Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra; the special case of the three central values is Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

The Reduction

Proposition (the reduction). For every element $\tilde\xi$ the square vanishes,

$$ \tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0 , $$

so the equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=a$ is equivalent to the equation $0=a$, and it therefore has the whole space of elements as its solutions when $a=0$ and no solution when $a\neq0$.

Proof. The block is alternating, so $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0$ for every $\tilde\xi$; the equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=a$ is then the equation $0=a$, whose truth is independent of the unknown $\tilde\xi$. When $a=0$ every $\tilde\xi$ satisfies it, and when $a\neq0$ none does.

The reduction is decisive because the unknown $\tilde\xi$ has dropped out of the equation: the left side does not depend on $\tilde\xi$ at all. There is no quadratic to solve, no polarisation to compute and no branching of the solution set; the whole of the answer is the value of $a$.

The Square Roots of the Origin

Theorem (the origin has every square root). The square roots of $0$ in the block are all the elements of the space,

$$ \{\tilde\xi:\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0\}=\mathbb{H}. $$

Proof. The reduction gives $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0$ for every $\tilde\xi$, so the equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0$ holds at every element, and the solution set is the whole space.

The contrast with the general plain algebra is exact. There the square product is a division product, so the square roots of the origin are the origin alone; here every element of the four-dimensional real space is a square root of the origin. The origin is the only element with square roots because the square of the block has the same value at every element, and that value is the origin.

The Square Roots of a Nonzero Element

Theorem (nothing else has a square root). No nonzero element of $\mathbb{H}$ has a square root in the block; equivalently, the equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=a$ has no solution for $a\neq0$.

Proof. The reduction gives $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0$, so a solution of the equation would give $0=a$, which fails for $a\neq0$; the equation fails for every $\tilde\xi$.

The absence is total and is not a statement about a particular element: it holds for the central values, for the pure vectors and for every other nonzero element alike. The reason is the same in every case, namely that the block cannot produce a nonzero value from a pair of equal arguments.

The Classification

the element $a$ the equation the square roots of the block
$0$ $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0$ the whole space $\mathbb{H}$
$a\neq0$ $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=a$ empty

The table is the reduction read value by value. The case-by-case classification of the general plain algebra, with its finite families of roots determined by a discriminant, does not occur here, because the quadratic has collapsed. The single element with square roots is the origin, and its square roots are as many as the space allows; every other element has none. This is the reading of the root problem at a general element for the entry $\mathrm{AQA}$ of The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

The Central Values

Remark (the three central values). The central values are the case $a=\lambda e_0$. At $\lambda=0$ the square roots of $\lambda e_0$ in the sense of this article are all the elements; at $\lambda=-1$ and at $\lambda=+1$ the equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=\lambda e_0$ has no solution, as the reduction shows and as Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra records. The central case of the classification therefore adds no new computation and no new distinction over the real field, the two notions of root coinciding at the central values; it is the paper $a=0$ of the table, read at the three named values.

Remark (the relation to the zero divisors). A square root of $0$ that is nonzero is a square-zero element and hence a zero divisor of the block, the square-zero set and the zero-divisor set coinciding off the origin (Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra). The origin is a square root of itself and is not a zero divisor, by the exclusion of the origin from the definition; the square roots of the origin are therefore the zero divisors of the block together with the origin.

Worked Examples

The examples are read from the explicit form $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=p_0\vec q-q_0\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$, and each is the reduction at a displayed value.

  • The square root of $e_0$. The equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=e_0$ has no solution: the left side is a pure vector, the right side is central and nonzero, and the two cannot agree.
  • The square root of $e_1$. The equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=e_1$ has no solution, for the same reason: the left side is zero and the right side is the nonzero pure vector $e_1$.
  • The square root of a product, $e_1\diamond e_2=-e_3$. The equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=-e_3$ has no solution, the left side vanishing and the right side being the nonzero pure vector $-e_3$.
  • The square root of $0$. Every element is a square root: $e_0$, $e_0+e_1$, $e_0+e_1+e_2+e_3$, and $e_1+2e_2-e_3$, each satisfying $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=0$ by the reduction.

The four examples are the two rows of the classification, read four times: the second row of the table is entered by the central value, by the pure generator and by the computed value $e_1\diamond e_2=-e_3$, a product rather than a chosen element, and the first row is entered by the origin, which is the single element named there although the whole space belongs to it.

Summary

The square root of an element $a$ is an element whose square in the block is $a$, and for the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication the square vanishes identically, so the equation $\tilde\xi\diamond\tilde\xi=a$ reduces to the equation $0=a$. The origin alone has square roots, and its square roots are all the elements of $\mathbb{H}$; no nonzero element has a square root, whether central, pure or general. The classification is the two rows of the table $a=0$ and $a\neq0$, with the whole space in the first row and the empty set in the second, and it is the extreme collapse of the case-by-case theory of the general plain algebra. The central values add no new computation: the square roots of $0$ are all the elements, the nonzero ones among them being the nilpotents and the zero divisors of the block, while the roots of $-1$ and of $+1$ are empty.

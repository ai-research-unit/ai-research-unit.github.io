Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra

Introduction

An idempotent is an element equal to its own square, and it is defined by a product. This article determines the idempotents of the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra $\mathrm{APS}$,

$$ \tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=\operatorname{Vect}\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}\bigr)=-P_0\overline{\mathbf{Q}}+\overline{Q_0}\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{P}\times\overline{\mathbf{Q}}, $$

the antisymmetric half of the general plain sesquilinear product of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions.

The answer is a single element, the origin. The block has no nontrivial idempotent. The equation $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ forces $\tilde\Pi=0$, and the proof is a parity followed by a computation: the left member is a pure vector, so an idempotent must be pure; on the pure elements the diagonal is $-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}$, which is purely imaginary, and a purely imaginary $\mathbf p$ makes the cross product vanish; the only solution is the origin.

The absence is stated and not filled. With no nontrivial idempotent the block has no orthogonal pair, no family, no primitive idempotent, no Peirce decomposition, and no projector among its one-sided multiplications. The vocabulary of the idempotents is a vocabulary of a unital associative product, and the block is neither, so the article is short by necessity and not by omission. The definitions of the idempotent and of the derived notions are Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition, stated there for a general product and read here on $\diamond$; throughout this article idempotent means idempotent of $\diamond$. The idempotents of the plain algebra, its standard pair and its Peirce decomposition are Idempotents of the General Plain Algebra; the idempotents of the parent sesquilinear product are the Hermitian idempotents of The Zero Divisors and the Twelve Structures, §The Square, the Idempotents and the Units; and the projectors that the block would need, the one-sided multiplications, are the subject of The Adjoint Operators and the Sandwich of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra. The neighbouring classes are Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra and Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ with $e_1e_2=e_3$, central scalar imaginary $i$ and coefficients in $\mathbb{C}$; an element is $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf{Q}$ with $\mathbf{Q}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q_ke_k$, and $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$ is its coefficientwise conjugate. The projection $\operatorname{Vect}$ removes the scalar part, its kernel is the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$, and the image is the vector subspace $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$. The idempotent of the biquaternion layer is written $\tilde\Pi$ (Conventions in Mathematics, §The idempotent convention), and a projector is an idempotent of the morphism space, not an element.

The Definition

Definition (idempotent, orthogonal, primitive). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element $\tilde\Pi$ is an idempotent of $\star$ when

$$ \tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi . $$

The origin is an idempotent of every product, and, when the product is unital, so is the unit; these are the trivial idempotents, and the others are nontrivial. For an associative product, idempotents $\tilde\Pi,\tilde\Pi'$ are orthogonal when $\tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi'=\tilde\Pi'\star\tilde\Pi=0$, a family is pairwise orthogonal when $\tilde\Pi_i\star\tilde\Pi_j=0$ for $i\neq j$ and summing to the unit when in addition $\sum_i\tilde\Pi_i$ is the unit, and a nontrivial idempotent is primitive when it is not the sum of two nonzero orthogonal idempotents. The Peirce decomposition is the splitting of the regular module by a pair $(\tilde\Pi,1-\tilde\Pi)$ of orthogonal idempotents of an associative unital product.

The derived notions are stated for an associative product because they are constructed from it, and the block is not associative. The article therefore uses only the first display, specialised to the block,

$$ \tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi , $$

and it records the derived notions as absent rather than reading them without their hypotheses.

The Idempotent Equation of the Block

The Parity

Proposition (an idempotent is pure). An idempotent of the block has vanishing scalar part: $\tilde\Pi=\mathbf p$, a pure vector.

Proof. The left member $\tilde\Pi\diamond\tilde\Pi$ is a value of the block, hence a pure vector, whose scalar part is zero; the right member is $\tilde\Pi$, so the scalar part of $\tilde\Pi$ is zero.

The parity is the same one that removes the roots of $\pm1$ in Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra: the block is central-free, so no equation whose right member has a scalar part can be solved. The idempotent equation is of that kind, and the parity reduces it to the pure elements.

The Diagonal of a Pure Element

For a pure element $\mathbf p$ the diagonal is the conjugate cross product,

$$ \mathbf p\diamond\mathbf p=-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}, $$

the value $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbf p\mathbf p^{*})=\operatorname{Vect}(-\mathbf p\overline{\mathbf p})=-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}$.

Proposition (the diagonal of a pure element is purely imaginary). For every pure $\mathbf p$ the value $-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}$ satisfies

$$ -\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}=-\overline{\bigl(-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}\bigr)}, $$

so its three coefficients are purely imaginary.

Proof. Apply the coefficientwise conjugation to the value: $\overline{-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}}=-\overline{\mathbf p}\times\mathbf p=\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}=-(-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p})$, by the anti-commutativity of the cross product over $\mathbb{C}$ and the fact that $\overline{\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}}=\overline{\mathbf p}\times\mathbf p$.

The Theorem

Theorem (the only idempotent is the origin). The block has exactly one idempotent, the element $0$. There is no nontrivial idempotent.

Proof. Let $\tilde\Pi$ be an idempotent. By the first proposition it is pure, $\tilde\Pi=\mathbf p$, and the equation is

$$ -\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}=\mathbf p . $$

The left member is purely imaginary by the second proposition, so the right member is purely imaginary, $\mathbf p=-\overline{\mathbf p}$; writing $\mathbf p=i\mathbf u$ with $\mathbf u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$, the left member is

$$ -\,(i\mathbf u)\times\overline{(i\mathbf u)}=-(i\mathbf u)\times(-i\mathbf u)=i^{2}(\mathbf u\times\mathbf u)=0 , $$

the cross product of a real vector with itself being zero. The equation then reads $\mathbf p=0$, so $\tilde\Pi=0$. The origin is an idempotent of every product, and it is the only one.

The Consequences of the Absence

No Orthogonal Pair, No Primitive Idempotent

A nontrivial idempotent is the first object the vocabulary asks for, and there is none. The statements that follow are therefore all vacuously void, and the article records them because their absence is the content.

  • No nontrivial idempotent. The idempotent set of the block is $\{0\}$, and the second trivial idempotent of a unital product, the unit, does not exist because the block has no unit (Norm and Invertibility in the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra).
  • No orthogonal pair. The only idempotent is the origin, so the only orthogonal pair of idempotents is $(0,0)$.
  • No primitive idempotent. A primitive idempotent is nontrivial by definition, and there is none.
  • No Peirce decomposition. The decomposition is stated for an associative unital product, and the block is neither associative nor unital; the corner algebras and the minimal left ideals it would produce are the objects of Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the General Plain Algebra and belong to the plain product.

No Projector among the Left Multiplications

The operator vocabulary is also empty, and it is empty for a second parity.

Proposition (the only projector is the zero operator). Let $L^{\diamond}_{\tilde P}$ be the left multiplication of the block by $\tilde P$, the map $\tilde X\mapsto\tilde P\diamond\tilde X$. It is conjugate-linear, and it is a projector if and only if $\tilde P=0$, in which case it is the zero operator.

Proof. The left multiplication of the block is $\mathbb{C}$-conjugate-linear in $\tilde X$, since the product is conjugate-linear in the second slot. If it were a projector, $L^{\diamond}_{\tilde P}\circ L^{\diamond}_{\tilde P}=L^{\diamond}_{\tilde P}$, the composite on the left being $\mathbb{C}$-linear while the right is conjugate-linear; a map that is both linear and conjugate-linear is zero, because $\lambda L(\tilde X)=L(\lambda\tilde X)=\overline{\lambda}L(\tilde X)$ for every $\lambda$, forcing $L(\tilde X)=0$. So the projector is the zero operator. It remains to read $L^{\diamond}_{\tilde P}=0$: at $\tilde X=e_0$ the value is $\mathbf P$, so $\tilde P$ is central, $\tilde P=ce_0$; at $\tilde X=e_1$ the value is $\operatorname{Vect}(ce_0e_1^{*})=-ce_1$, so $c=0$ and $\tilde P=0$. Conversely $L^{\diamond}_0=0$ is a projector.

Remark (the two projectors of the algebra are different objects). The plain algebra has the idempotent elements and, separately, the idempotent operators, and the two correspond through the left multiplication; the block has neither, and both absences have the same source, the conjugate-linearity and the projection. In the sibling general quaternionic algebra the correspondence is shifted to the conjugate index and the projectors survive (The Specificities of the General Quaternionic Algebra); in the block the shift is a projection and nothing survives. This is the first half of The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra.

The Idempotents and the Other Products

The idempotent sets of the twelve products are tabulated product by product in The Zero Divisors and the Twelve Structures, §The Square, the Idempotents and the Units, and the neighbouring classifications are in the articles named there. The rows that bear on the block are these.

structure idempotent set
plain product $\tilde P\tilde Q$ $0$, $e_0$, and the family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $-1$
natural product $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q$ $0$ and $e_0$ alone
plain sesquilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ the Hermitian idempotents
quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ the family $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$
block $\mathrm{APS}$ $0$ alone

The block is the row in which the idempotent set collapses to the origin, and it is sharper than the natural product, which keeps the unit. The parent of the block is the plain sesquilinear product, whose idempotents are the Hermitian idempotents (the projections); the block does not inherit them, because the diagonal of a Hermitian idempotent $\tilde\Pi$ is $\operatorname{Vect}(\tilde\Pi\tilde\Pi^{*})=\operatorname{Vect}(\tilde\Pi)$, its own vector part, so such an element is an idempotent of the block only when it equals its vector part: the central Hermitian idempotents $0$ and $e_0$ give the value $0\neq\tilde\Pi$, and a pure Hermitian idempotent would be a purely imaginary vector, whose block square is $0$ by the theorem. The two idempotent sets therefore meet only at the origin, and the two sesquilinear blocks of the plain row, the symmetric half and the block itself, have disjoint idempotent theories.

Summary

The block has exactly one idempotent, the origin, and no nontrivial one. The proof is the parity that removes the scalar part, reducing an idempotent to a pure $\mathbf p$, followed by the model computation $-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}=\mathbf p$: the left member is purely imaginary, so $\mathbf p=i\mathbf u$ with $\mathbf u$ real, and then $-\mathbf p\times\overline{\mathbf p}=-(i\mathbf u)\times(-i\mathbf u)=0$, forcing $\mathbf p=0$.

With no nontrivial idempotent the block has no orthogonal pair, no family summing to a unit, no primitive idempotent and no Peirce decomposition; and with a conjugate-linear left multiplication it has no projector other than the zero operator. The two absences have one source, the central-free projection of a conjugate-linear product. The parent's idempotents, the Hermitian ones, are not inherited, their diagonal being their own vector part; and among the twelve the block is the row in which the idempotent set is the origin alone.

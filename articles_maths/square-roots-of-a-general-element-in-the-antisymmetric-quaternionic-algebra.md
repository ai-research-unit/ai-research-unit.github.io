# __Square Roots of a General Element in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra__

## Introduction

The square root of an element is defined by an equation in which the product occurs, so it is
read in the product (*Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures*, §*Powers, the
Square and the Roots*). The block of this article is the antisymmetric quaternionic
multiplication
$$
\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q
=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q-\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P\bigr)
=P_0\vec Q-Q_0\vec P-\vec P\times\vec Q ,
$$
which is alternating and without a unit (*Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra
of Biquaternions*). The article solves, for a general element $a\in\mathbb{B}$, the equation
$$
\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a
$$
in the element $\tilde\Xi$.

The reduction is the same as the reduction of the three central values, and it is the whole of
the problem: the block is alternating, so its square is zero at every element, and the equation
is the equation $0=a$. **The origin alone has square roots, and every element of the space is a
square root of it; no nonzero element has a square root.** The classification is therefore the
two-line classification of the equation $0=a$, and it is the extreme case of the general
reduction of *Square Roots of a General Element in the General Plain Algebra*, where the
quadratic in the scalar part supplies up to four roots. The central values are read off as a
special case, and their article is cited.

**The results.** The square of every element of the block vanishes identically, so the equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ is the equation $0=a$: the origin is the only element with square roots, every element of the space is a square root of it, and no nonzero element has a square root. The classification is the two rows $a=0$ and $a\neq0$, with the whole space in the first row and the empty set in the second. For the central values there are no roots of $-1$ or of $+1$, and the roots of $0$ in the narrower sense are the square roots of the origin other than the origin itself.

## The Notion and Its Product

**Definition (square root of an element).** Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$ and let
$a\in\mathbb{B}$. A **square root of $a$ in $\star$** is an element $\tilde\Xi$ with
$$
\tilde\Xi\star\tilde\Xi=a .
$$
The roots of a central value are the special case $a=\lambda e_0$.

Throughout this article the product is the **antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication**
$\diamond$, and unqualified, *square root* means a square root of the block. The general
reduction of the equation in the plain product, and the square it uses, are *Square Roots of a
General Element in the General Plain Algebra*; the special cases of the roots of $-1$, of $0$ and
of $+1$ are *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*
and *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*.

## The Reduction

**For every element $\tilde\Xi$ the square vanishes, so the equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ is equivalent to the equation $0=a$: it is solved by the whole space when $a=0$ and by no element when $a\neq0$.**

**Proposition (the reduction).** For every element $\tilde\Xi$ the square vanishes,
$$
\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0 ,
$$
so the equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ is equivalent to the equation $0=a$, and it
therefore has the whole space of elements as its solutions when $a=0$ and no solution when
$a\neq0$.

*Proof.* The block is alternating, so $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$ for every $\tilde\Xi$; the
equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ is then the equation $0=a$, whose truth is independent of
the unknown $\tilde\Xi$. When $a=0$ every $\tilde\Xi$ satisfies it, and when $a\neq0$ none does.

The reduction is decisive because the unknown $\tilde\Xi$ has dropped out of the equation: the
left side does not depend on $\tilde\Xi$ at all. There is no quadratic to solve, no polarisation
to compute and no branching of the solution set; the whole of the answer is the value of $a$.

## The Square Roots of the Origin

**The square roots of $0$ are all the elements of the space, $\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0\}=\mathbb{B}$.**

**Theorem (the origin has every square root).** The square roots of $0$ in the block are all the
elements of the space,
$$
\{\tilde\Xi:\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0\}=\mathbb{B}.
$$

*Proof.* The reduction gives $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$ for every $\tilde\Xi$, so the equation
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$ holds at every element, and the solution set is the whole space.

The contrast with the general plain algebra is exact. There the square roots of the origin are
the pure isotropic cone, a two-dimensional family over the complex field; here every element of
the four-dimensional complex space is a square root of the origin. The origin is the only
element with square roots because the square of the block has the same value at every element,
and that value is the origin.

## The Square Roots of a Nonzero Element

**No nonzero element of $\mathbb{B}$ has a square root in the block: the equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ has no solution for $a\neq0$.**

**Theorem (nothing else has a square root).** No nonzero element of $\mathbb{B}$ has a square
root in the block; equivalently, the equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ has no solution for
$a\neq0$.

*Proof.* The reduction gives $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$, so a solution of the equation would
give $0=a$, which fails for $a\neq0$; the equation fails for every $\tilde\Xi$.

The absence is total and is not a statement about a particular element: it holds for the central
values, for the pure vectors, for the isotropic elements and for every other nonzero element
alike. The reason is the same in every case, namely that the block cannot produce a nonzero
value from a pair of equal arguments.

## The Classification

**The classification has two rows: the origin, whose square roots are the whole space, and every nonzero element, which has none.**

| the element $a$ | the equation | the square roots of the block |
|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$ | the whole space $\mathbb{B}$ |
| $a\neq0$ | $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ | empty |

The table is the reduction read value by value. The classification of the general plain algebra
— four roots, two roots or none, according to the discriminant of a quadratic and the isotropic
character of the vector part — does not occur here, because the quadratic has collapsed. The
single element with square roots is the origin, and its square roots are as many as the space
allows; every other element has none.

## The Central Values

**At the central values $a=\lambda e_0$ the square roots of $0$ include the origin, and there are no roots of $-1$ or of $+1$.**

**Remark (the three central values).** The central values are the case $a=\lambda e_0$. At
$\lambda=0$ the square roots of $\lambda e_0$ in the sense of this article are all the elements,
and the roots of $\lambda=0$ in the sense of the root article, which are required to be nonzero,
are the punctured space; at $\lambda=-1$ and at $\lambda=+1$ the equation
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=\lambda e_0$ has no solution, as the reduction shows and as
*Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra* records; and
the value $\lambda=0$, whose roots are the punctured space in the narrower sense, is the subject
of *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*.
The central case of the classification therefore adds one distinction and no new computation:
the square roots of $0$ include the origin, which is not a root of $0$ in the narrower sense.

**Remark (the relation to the zero divisors).** A square root of $0$ that is nonzero is a root
of $0$ and hence a zero divisor of the block, the square-zero set and the zero-divisor set
coinciding (*Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra*). The origin is a square
root of itself and is not a zero divisor, by the exclusion of the origin from the definition;
the square roots of the origin are therefore the zero divisors of the block together with the
origin.

## Worked Examples

The examples are read from the explicit form $\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=P_0\vec Q-Q_0\vec P-\vec P\times\vec Q$, and each is the reduction at a displayed value.

- **The square root of $e_0$.** The equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=e_0$ has no solution: the
    left side is a pure vector, the right side is central, and the two cannot agree.
- **The square root of $e_1$.** The equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=e_1$ has no solution, for
    the same reason: the left side is zero and the right side is the nonzero pure vector $e_1$.
- **The square root of a product, $e_1\diamond e_2=-e_3$.** The equation
    $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=-e_3$ has no solution, the left side vanishing and the right side
    being the nonzero pure vector $-e_3$.
- **The square root of $0$.** Every element is a square root: $e_0$, $e_0+e_1$,
    $e_0+ie_1+ie_2$, and $e_1+ie_2$, the last being the isotropic pure element of the block,
    each satisfies $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$ by the reduction.

The four examples are the two rows of the classification, read four times: the second row of the
table is entered by the central value, by the pure generator and by the computed value
$e_1\diamond e_2=-e_3$, a product rather than a chosen element, and the first row is entered by
the origin, which is the single element named there although the whole space belongs to it.

## Summary

For the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication the square of every element is zero,
$\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=0$, so the general equation $\tilde\Xi\diamond\tilde\Xi=a$ is the
equation $0=a$; the origin is the only element of the space with square roots, and every element
of the space is a square root of it, while no nonzero element has a square root. The
classification is the two rows of the table $a=0$ and $a\neq0$, with the whole space in the
first row and the empty set in the second; the quadratic of the general plain algebra has
collapsed, and with it the four-fold, two-fold and empty cases of that theory. The central
values are the special case $\lambda e_0$: the roots of $0$ in the narrower sense are the
square roots of the origin other than the origin itself, and there are no roots of $-1$ or of
$+1$. The square roots of the origin are the zero divisors of the block together with the origin.

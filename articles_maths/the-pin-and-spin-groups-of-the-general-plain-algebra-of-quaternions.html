The Pin and Spin Groups of the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions

Introduction

The pin and spin groups belong to a quadratic form, and the quaternion algebra carries two forms: the plain form $Q(\tilde q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q^2)=q_0^2-q_1^2-q_2^2-q_3^2$, indefinite of signature $(1,3)$, and the definite form, the quaternion norm $N(\tilde q)=\tilde q\tilde q^{\natural}=q_0^2+q_1^2+q_2^2+q_3^2$, positive definite. The two forms behave differently, and the difference decides the whole article: $Q$ is not multiplicative, so its shells are not groups and its pin group is not inside the algebra; $N$ is multiplicative, so its shells are groups and the spin group of $N$ is the unit sphere $\mathbb{H}^1$ of the algebra itself. The article states which form each group belongs to, builds the multiplicative case algebraically from the unit shell, and describes the adjoint action, its kernel, its section and the reflections. The form $Q$ and its transpose calculus are Association and the Transpose on the General Plain Algebra, above this article in this category; the norm $N$, its multiplicativity and the unit group are Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra, above this article in this category; the geometric reading of the adjoint action is Quaternion Rotations and Reflections, below this article in this category, and the group structure of $\mathbb{H}^1$ is The Quaternion Unit Group as a Topological Group, below this article in this category.

The Plain Form Has No Pin Group Inside the Algebra

Proposition (the shells of $Q$ are not closed under the product). The set $\{\tilde q:Q(\tilde q)=1\}$ is not closed under multiplication, and $Q$ is not multiplicative.

Proof. $Q$ is isotropic, $Q(e_0+e_1)=1-1=0$ with $e_0+e_1\neq0$, and a multiplicative form on a division algebra is anisotropic: if $Q$ were multiplicative and $Q(\tilde x)=0$ with $\tilde x\neq0$, then $Q(\tilde x\tilde y)=Q(\tilde x)Q(\tilde y)=0$ for every $\tilde y$, and since $\tilde x\tilde y$ runs over the whole algebra as $\tilde y$ does, $Q$ would vanish identically, against $Q(e_0)=1$. The witness is $\tilde a=\tilde b=\sqrt2\,e_0+e_1$, for which $Q(\tilde a)=Q(\tilde b)=1$ while $\tilde a\tilde b=1+2\sqrt2\,e_1$ has $Q(\tilde a\tilde b)=1-8=-7$.

Corollary. The shells $\{Q=c\}$ are not subgroups of $\mathbb{H}^{\times}$, and the pin group of $Q$ is not a subgroup of $\mathbb{H}$: it lives in the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(Q)$, generated by the vectors of $Q$-norm $\pm1$, with the twisted adjoint action on $\mathbb{H}$. There is no pin group inside the algebra for the plain form.

The Definite Form and Its Unit Shell

Theorem. The quaternion norm $N$ is multiplicative, $N(\tilde p\tilde q)=N(\tilde p)N(\tilde q)$, positive definite, and its shell

$$ \mathbb{H}^1=\{\tilde q:N(\tilde q)=1\} $$

is a subgroup of the group of units $\mathbb{H}^{\times}$.

Proof. Multiplicativity and positive definiteness are Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra, above this article in this category; the shell is closed because $N(\tilde p\tilde q)=N(\tilde p)N(\tilde q)=1$ and $N(\tilde q^{-1})=N(\tilde q)^{-1}=1$, and it contains $e_0$; it is a subgroup.

Corollary. The shell $\{N=\pm1\}$ is also a group; because $N\geq0$ it coincides with $\mathbb{H}^1$.

Proof. $N=-1$ has no solution over $\mathbb{R}$.

The Pin and Spin Groups of the Definite Form

Theorem. For the definite form $N$ the pin and spin groups are subgroups of $\mathbb{H}^{\times}$:

$$ \operatorname{Pin}(N)=\{\tilde q:N(\tilde q)=\pm1\}=\mathbb{H}^1,\qquad \operatorname{Spin}(N)=\{\tilde q:N(\tilde q)=1\}=\mathbb{H}^1 . $$

Proof. An element of the pin group of a definite form is a product of vectors of norm $\pm1$; in dimension four with a multiplicative norm the products are exactly the elements of norm $\pm1$, and since the norm is never negative the two conditions coincide. Equivalently, on the three-dimensional vector subspace the algebra is the even part of the Clifford algebra of the form, and the norm-one shell is that even part. The spin group is the even subgroup and equals the shell.

Thus the spin group of the definite form is the unit sphere of the algebra, and it is a group precisely because the norm is multiplicative; this is the algebraic content of the identification $\operatorname{Spin}\cong\mathbb{H}^1$, and it is a statement about the form $N$ and not about the plain form $Q$.

Reading of $\mathbb{H}^1$. The group $\mathbb{H}^1$ acts on the whole algebra $\mathbb{H}$ by the adjoint action and on the vector subspace $V=\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$ by its restriction; on $V$ the form $N$ is the three-dimensional definite form, and the adjoint action preserves it. The three-dimensional reading is the one of Quaternion Rotations and Reflections, below this article in this category.

The Adjoint Action, Its Kernel and Its Section

Definition. The adjoint action of $\mathbb{H}^1$ on $\mathbb{H}$ is $\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde q}(\tilde p)=\tilde q\tilde p\tilde q^{-1}$; on the vector subspace it is the conjugation by a unit quaternion.

Theorem. $\operatorname{Ad}$ is a group homomorphism $\mathbb{H}^1\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{H})$; its kernel is $\{\pm e_0\}$, so it induces an isomorphism $\mathbb{H}^1/\{\pm e_0\}\cong\operatorname{Ad}(\mathbb{H}^1)$, and on the vector subspace the image is the group of linear maps of $V$ that preserve $N$ and have determinant $+1$.

Proof. $\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde p}\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde q}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde p\tilde q}$; the kernel consists of the units with $\tilde q\tilde p\tilde q^{-1}=\tilde p$ for all $\tilde p$, that is the central units, namely $\pm e_0$. The image on $V$ consists of linear maps of $V$ preserving $N$, and each of them has determinant $+1$ because it is a conjugation by a unit; that the image is the whole group of such maps, and the identification $\mathbb{H}^1/\{\pm e_0\}$ with it, is the statement of Quaternion Rotations and Reflections, below this article in this category.

Theorem (the normalised section). The map $\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde q}\mapsto\tilde q$ on the image is not well defined, but the assignment that sends an element of $\operatorname{Ad}(\mathbb{H}^1)$ to the unit of its fibre with $q_0>0$, and to the first unit with a positive coordinate on the equator $q_0=0$, is a set-theoretic section of the projection; the two elements of a fibre are $\pm\tilde q$, and they give the same adjoint action.

Proof. The fibres of $\operatorname{Ad}$ are the cosets $\{\pm\tilde q\}$ by the kernel computation. If $q_0\neq0$ exactly one of $\pm\tilde q$ has positive scalar part; on the equator $q_0=0$ the two elements have the same scalar part, and the tie is broken by the first nonzero coordinate of $\tilde q$, of which one of the two is positive. The assignment is a section of the projection as a map of sets: it picks one element of each pair $\{\pm\tilde q\}$, by the sign of the scalar part with the tie-break on the equator.

Corollary. The group homomorphism $\mathbb{H}^1\to\{\text{linear maps of }V\text{ preserving }N\text{ with determinant }+1\}$ with kernel $\{\pm e_0\}$ is the double cover of that group; it is $2$-to-$1$ on the group, and the two preimages of a form-preserving map are the two versors $\pm\tilde q$.

Proof. The kernel and the surjectivity above; the count of fibres is two.

The Reflections

Theorem. Let $\tilde u\in V$ with $N(\tilde u)=1$, so that $\tilde u\in\mathbb{H}^1$, and let $\tilde x\in V$. Then

$$ \tilde u\tilde x\tilde u=\tilde x-2\,\mathrm B_N(\tilde u,\tilde x)\tilde u,\qquad \operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde u}(\tilde x)=\tilde u\tilde x\tilde u^{-1}=2\,\mathrm B_N(\tilde u,\tilde x)\tilde u-\tilde x, $$

where $\mathrm B_N$ is the polar form of $N$ restricted to $V$; the first map fixes the plane $\tilde u^{\perp}$ and negates the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde u$, and the second is its negative, fixing the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde u$ and negating the plane $\tilde u^{\perp}$.

Proof. For a pure unit quaternion $\tilde u$ one has $\tilde u^{-1}=\tilde u^{\natural}=-\tilde u$ and $\tilde u^2=-e_0$, so $\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde u}(\tilde x)=-\tilde u\tilde x\tilde u$; expanding $\tilde u\tilde x\tilde u$ with the multiplication table for pure quaternions, and using that the polar form of $N$ on $V$ is $-\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde u\tilde x)$ up to the sign convention, gives the displayed formula; it fixes $\tilde u$ and negates every vector orthogonal to it, so the second map fixes the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde u$ and negates the plane $\tilde u^{\perp}$, and the first fixes the plane and negates the line. The restriction to $V$ is not cosmetic: on the scalar line the same operators act as the negation, $\tilde u\,s\,\tilde u=-s$ for $s\in\mathbb{R}e_0$, so the displayed formulas hold on $V$ and not on the whole algebra.

Corollary. The versor of the reflection in the hyperplane perpendicular to the unit vector $\tilde u$ is the unit quaternion $\tilde u$ itself, the reflection being $\tilde x\mapsto\tilde u\tilde x\tilde u$; the adjoint action $\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde u}$ is the negative of that reflection, fixing the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde u$ and negating the plane $\tilde u^{\perp}$, and the two versors $\pm\tilde u$ realise the same adjoint action. Every element of $\operatorname{Ad}(\mathbb{H}^1)$ on $V$ is a product of such maps, and the geometric reading on the sphere and on space is Quaternion Rotations and Reflections, below this article in this category.

Relation to the Versors

A versor is a product of unit vectors; in the quaternion algebra the versors of the definite form are exactly the elements of $\mathbb{H}^1$, because $\mathbb{H}^1$ is closed under the product and every unit is already a single normalised element. The adjoint action by a versor realises the linear map of $V$ preserving $N$ that is determined by the versor, and the two versors $\pm\tilde q$ realise the same map; this is the content of the kernel and the section above. The group of versors is the spin group of the definite form, and it is not the pin group of the plain form $Q$, whose shells are not groups.

Summary

The plain form $Q(\tilde q)=q_0^2-q_1^2-q_2^2-q_3^2$ is not multiplicative, its shells are not groups, and its pin group is not a subgroup of $\mathbb{H}$ but lives in the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(Q)$. The definite form $N(\tilde q)=\tilde q\tilde q^{\natural}$ is multiplicative and positive definite, and for it the pin and spin groups are the shells inside the algebra: $\operatorname{Pin}(N)=\operatorname{Spin}(N)=\mathbb{H}^1$, the unit sphere, which is a group precisely because the norm is multiplicative. The unit group acts on the algebra by the adjoint action $\operatorname{Ad}_{\tilde q}(\tilde p)=\tilde q\tilde p\tilde q^{-1}$, with kernel $\{\pm e_0\}$, so that the induced maps on the vector subspace are the group of linear maps of $V$ preserving $N$ with determinant $+1$; the normalised section picks one of the two representatives, by the sign of $q_0$ with a tie-break on the equator, the two representatives being $\pm\tilde q$. The adjoint action by a pure unit quaternion is the reflection in the hyperplane perpendicular to it, the versors of the definite form are the elements of $\mathbb{H}^1$, and the double cover of that group is the $2$-to-$1$ group homomorphism $\mathbb{H}^1\to\{\text{linear maps of }V\text{ preserving }N\text{ with determinant }+1\}$.

The Peirce Decomposition

Introduction

Five orthogonal rank-one idempotents $P_0,\dots,P_4$ cut $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ into twenty-five one-dimensional lines

$$ A_{kl}=P_kM_5P_l,\qquad k,l=0,\dots,4, $$

and the algebra is their direct sum. The multiplication is a rule on the indices: the product of a line $A_{kl}$ with a line $A_{mn}$ vanishes unless $l=m$, and then it lies in $A_{kn}$. This is the Peirce decomposition of the algebra with respect to the complete family, and it is the finest decomposition the frame supplies.

A single idempotent cuts more coarsely. If $P$ has rank one and $Q=I-P$, the algebra splits into four corners

$$ M_5(\mathbb{C})=PM_5P\oplus PM_5Q\oplus QM_5P\oplus QM_5Q, $$

of dimensions $1+4+4+16=25$. The coarse split is the fine split blocked by the partition $\{1\},\{2,3,4,5\}$: the $16$-dimensional corner $QM_5Q$ is the sum of sixteen of the Peirce lines, the row and the column of four lines each are the off-diagonal blocks, and the scalar corner is one line. The corner isomorphisms are $PM_5P\cong\mathbb{C}$ and $QM_5Q\cong M_4$.

The article assumes the Lagrange projectors of Roots of Unity and the Lagrange Idempotents: the root $H$, its simple spectrum, the idempotents $P_k$ and the identity $H=\sum_k\zeta_kP_k$. It uses no form, no involution and no topology. The choice of a unit inside each Peirce line is The Frame, the Simple Nilpotents and the Gauge; the rank-two split is The Rank-Two Split and The $M_2\oplus M_3$ Corner; the modules generated by the idempotents are The Modules and the Minimal Left Ideals.

The decomposition is a decomposition of the algebra, not a list of vectors. The lines $A_{kl}$ are one-dimensional subspaces of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$, of dimension one each; they are not spinors, not columns and not minimal left ideals. For $n=5$ there are no spinors at all: the carrier is the five-dimensional module $\mathbb{C}^5=\operatorname{Sym}^4(\mathbb{C}^2)$, of spin $2$, and it is constructed in The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition. A minimal left ideal of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ has dimension $5$, not $1$, and is treated in The Modules and the Minimal Left Ideals.

The Peirce Lines

The Definition

Let $H$ be a root and $P_0,\dots,P_4$ its Lagrange projectors, so that

$$ P_k^2=P_k,\qquad P_kP_l=\delta_{kl}P_k,\qquad \sum_{k=0}^{4}P_k=I . $$

Definition. The Peirce line of the ordered pair $(k,l)$ is

$$ A_{kl}=P_kM_5(\mathbb{C})P_l=\bigl\{P_kAP_l : A\in M_5(\mathbb{C})\bigr\}. $$

The Peirce component of $A$ in the line $A_{kl}$ is $A_{kl}=P_kAP_l$.

Proposition. Each $A_{kl}$ is a complex line, and the algebra is the direct sum of the twenty-five lines:

$$ M_5(\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{k,l=0}^{4}A_{kl},\qquad A=\sum_{k,l=0}^{4}A_{kl}\quad\text{for every }A . $$

The sum is direct: the component $A_{kl}$ is recovered from $A$ by $A_{kl}=P_kAP_l$, and if $\sum_{k,l}B_{kl}=0$ with $B_{kl}\in A_{kl}$, then $B_{kl}=P_k\bigl(\sum B_{mn}\bigr)P_l=0$.

Proof. The identity $A=\bigl(\sum_kP_k\bigr)A\bigl(\sum_lP_l\bigr)=\sum_{k,l}P_kAP_l$ gives the spanning. For the dimension, choose an eigenbasis of $H$ with $P_k=E_{kk}$. Then $P_kAP_l=A_{kl}E_{kl}$, so $A_{kl}=\mathbb{C}E_{kl}$, a line. The twenty-five lines are linearly independent because the twenty-five matrix units are.

Remark. The decomposition is canonical in the projectors, not in the lines' generators. The subspaces $A_{kl}$ are determined by $H$ alone. A vector inside $A_{kl}$ is not: if $E_{kl}$ is one nonzero element, every $\lambda E_{kl}$ with $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^\times$ spans the same line, and the choice of $\lambda$ is the gauge of The Frame, the Simple Nilpotents and the Gauge. The decomposition is intrinsic; its coordinates are not.

The Simultaneous Eigenspaces

Proposition. On the line $A_{kl}$, the left multiplication by $P_k$ and the right multiplication by $P_l$ act as the identity, and the other multiplications by projectors act as zero:

$$ P_kA_{kl}=A_{kl}=A_{kl}P_l,\qquad P_mA_{kl}=0\ (m\ne k),\qquad A_{kl}P_n=0\ (n\ne l). $$

Equivalently, $A_{kl}$ is the simultaneous eigenspace of the two commuting operator families $L_{P_k}$ and $R_{P_l}$ with eigenvalues $(1,1)$, the other eigenvalues being zero.

Proof. Every element of $A_{kl}$ is $P_kAP_l$; multiplying on the left by $P_k$ gives $P_k^2AP_l=P_kAP_l$, and multiplying on the left by $P_m$ with $m\ne k$ gives $P_mP_kAP_l=0$. The right action is the same with the order of the factors.

Corollary. The Peirce lines are the joint eigenspaces of the $5\times5$ array of commuting projectors $L_{P_k}R_{P_l}$; the algebra is the direct sum of the $25$ joint eigenspaces, each of dimension one. The diagonal lines $A_{kk}$ are the images of the idempotents themselves, $A_{kk}=\mathbb{C}P_k$.

Proof. On $A_{kk}$, $P_kAP_k=\lambda P_k$ because $P_k$ has rank one; the scalar is $\lambda=\operatorname{tr}(P_kAP_k)/\operatorname{tr}P_k=\operatorname{tr}(P_kA)$. The line is spanned by $P_k$. The joint eigenspace statement is the proposition read for all pairs.

The Multiplication Rule

Theorem. For all indices,

$$ A_{kl}A_{mn}\subseteq\delta_{lm}A_{kn}, $$

that is, the product is contained in $A_{kn}$ when $l=m$ and vanishes otherwise. For nonzero elements $x\in A_{kl}$ and $y\in A_{lm}$ the product $xy$ is nonzero and spans $A_{km}$; the multiplication is an isomorphism $A_{kl}\otimes A_{lm}\to A_{km}$.

Proof. Let $x=P_kAP_l$ and $y=P_mBP_n$. Then

$$ xy=P_kAP_lP_mBP_n=\delta_{lm}P_kAP_lBP_n\in\delta_{lm}A_{kn}. $$

In the eigenbasis $P_k=E_{kk}$, the line $A_{kl}$ is spanned by $E_{kl}$; for nonzero $x=aE_{kl}$ and $y=bE_{lm}$ with $a,b\ne0$, the product is $abE_{km}\ne0$, which spans $A_{km}$. The map is bilinear and nonzero on the pair of generators, hence an isomorphism of one-dimensional spaces.

Corollary (the Peirce rule in words). The multiplication of the lines is governed by the inner indices: the product of $A_{kl}$ and $A_{mn}$ is contained in $A_{kn}$ exactly when $l=m$, and is zero otherwise. Reading the left factor by its second index $l$ and the right factor by its first index $m$, the rule is the identity matrix $\delta_{lm}$ on the five inner indices; the outer indices $k$ and $n$ are carried to the product.

Remark. The rule is the multiplication table of the matrix units, read without a choice of units. The diagonal lines $A_{kk}$ are subalgebras isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$, with unit $P_k$; the off-diagonal lines are bimodules over the diagonal algebra $\bigoplus_kA_{kk}=\mathbb{C}[H]$. The algebra is graded by the Peirce indices in the sense that the product respects the pair of outer indices; it is not graded by a group in this fine form. The coarser $\mathbb{Z}/5$-grading is the sum over $k-l=r$ of Roots of Unity and the Lagrange Idempotents.

The Peirce Coordinates

Choose a nonzero $E_{kl}\in A_{kl}$ for every pair. Then every $A$ has a unique expansion

$$ A=\sum_{k,l=0}^{4}a_{kl}E_{kl},\qquad a_{kl}\in\mathbb{C}, $$

and the product is

$$ \Bigl(\sum a_{kl}E_{kl}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum b_{mn}E_{mn}\Bigr)=\sum_{k,l,n}a_{kl}b_{ln}E_{kn}. $$

The trace is $\operatorname{tr}A=\sum_k a_{kk}$, the identity is $I=\sum_kE_{kk}$, and the root is $H=\sum_k\zeta_kE_{kk}$ in the spectral frame.

Proposition. In the spectral frame $E_{kl}=v_kv_l^{\vee}$, where $v_k$ spans $\operatorname{im}P_k$ and $v_l^{\vee}$ is the dual functional, the coordinates of $A$ are $a_{kl}=v_k^{\vee}Av_l$, and the product rule is the ordinary matrix product of the coordinate array.

Proof. $v_kv_k^{\vee}=P_k$ and $v_k^{\vee}v_l=\delta_{kl}$; hence $E_{kl}=v_kv_l^{\vee}$ and $P_kAP_l=v_k(v_k^{\vee}Av_l)v_l^{\vee}=a_{kl}E_{kl}$. The product rule is the associativity of the matrix product, and the trace is the sum of the diagonal coordinates because $\operatorname{tr}(v_kv_l^{\vee})=\delta_{kl}$.

Remark. The coordinate array $(a_{kl})$ is a $5\times5$ matrix, and the product is the matrix product. This is the tautology that the algebra is a matrix algebra; the Peirce decomposition is the statement that the array is cut into twenty-five independent entries by the two families of projectors. The coordinates depend on the gauge, the array does not.

The Coarse Split by a Rank-One Idempotent

The Four Corners

Let $P$ be an idempotent of rank one, not necessarily one of the $P_k$, and let $Q=I-P$. The coarse Peirce corners are

$$ M_5(\mathbb{C})=PM_5P\oplus PM_5Q\oplus QM_5P\oplus QM_5Q . $$

Theorem. The four corners have complex dimensions

$$ \dim PM_5P=1,\qquad \dim PM_5Q=4,\qquad \dim QM_5P=4,\qquad \dim QM_5Q=16, $$

and the sum is $1+4+4+16=25$. The corner isomorphisms are

$$ PM_5P\cong\mathbb{C},\qquad PM_5Q\cong\mathbb{C}^{1\times4},\qquad QM_5P\cong\mathbb{C}^{4\times1},\qquad QM_5Q\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}). $$

Proof. Choose a basis $v_1,\dots,v_5$ with $v_1$ spanning $\operatorname{im}P$ and $v_2,\dots,v_5$ spanning $\ker P$. In this basis $P=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0,0,0)$ and $Q=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1,1,1)$. A matrix $A$ written in the block form

$$ A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}&a_{13}&a_{14}&a_{15}\\ a_{21}&a_{22}&a_{23}&a_{24}&a_{25}\\ a_{31}&a_{32}&a_{33}&a_{34}&a_{35}\\ a_{41}&a_{42}&a_{43}&a_{44}&a_{45}\\ a_{51}&a_{52}&a_{53}&a_{54}&a_{55}\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}\\ A_{21}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix} $$

has $PAP=A_{11}$, $PAQ=A_{12}$, $QAP=A_{21}$ and $QAQ=A_{22}$. The corner $PMP$ is the set of scalars $A_{11}$, of dimension $1$; $PMQ$ is the row $A_{12}$, of dimension $4$; $QMP$ is the column $A_{21}$, of dimension $4$; $QMQ$ is the $4\times4$ block $A_{22}$, of dimension $16$. The isomorphisms are the identity on the blocks in this basis.

Corollary. The coarse split is the fine split blocked by the partition $\{1\},\{2,3,4,5\}$ when $P=P_1$ is a spectral projector:

$$ PM_5P=A_{11},\qquad PM_5Q=\bigoplus_{l\ne1}A_{1l},\qquad QM_5P=\bigoplus_{k\ne1}A_{k1},\qquad QM_5Q=\bigoplus_{k,l\ne1}A_{kl}. $$

The dimensions are $1$, $4$, $4$ and $16$ because there are one, four, four and sixteen lines.

Proof. This is the theorem of §The Peirce Lines with the indices split into the two sets $\{1\}$ and $\{2,3,4,5\}$; the direct sum over the corresponding pairs is the corner.

Remark. For a general rank-one idempotent $P$ which is not spectral, the coarse split is still a four-corner decomposition of dimensions $1+4+4+16$, but the corners are not unions of spectral Peirce lines. The dimensions depend only on the rank, not on the choice of $P$; the intersection with the spectral decomposition depends on $P$. The spectral case is the one used in the frame.

The Coarse Multiplication Table

In the block form of the proof, the product is the ordinary block product:

$$ \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ c&D\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}a'&b'\\ c'&D'\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}aa'+bc'&ab'+bD'\\ ca'+Dc'&cb'+DD'\end{pmatrix}, \qquad a,a'\in\mathbb{C},\quad b,b'\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times4},\quad c,c'\in\mathbb{C}^{4\times1},\quad D,D'\in M_4 . $$

Proposition (two-sided Peirce table). The products of the four corners are

left $\backslash$ right $PMP$ $PMQ$ $QMP$ $QMQ$
$PMP$ $PMP$ $PMQ$ $0$ $0$
$PMQ$ $0$ $0$ $PMP$ $PMQ$
$QMP$ $QMP$ $QMQ$ $0$ $QMP$
$QMQ$ $0$ $0$ $QMP$ $QMQ$

Proof. Multiply the block matrices and use $PQ=QP=0$, $P^2=P$, $Q^2=Q$. The nonvanishing products are those with no factor $PQ$ or $QP$ in the middle: the $(1,1)$ block of a matrix in $PMP$ with one in $PMP$ is $aa'\in PMP$; the $(1,2)$ block of a matrix in $PMP$ with one in $PMQ$ is $ab'\in PMQ$; the $(2,1)$ block of a matrix in $QMP$ with one in $PMP$ is $ca'\in QMP$; the $(2,2)$ block of a matrix in $QMP$ with one in $PMQ$ is $cb'\in QMQ$; the $(1,1)$ block of a matrix in $PMQ$ with one in $QMP$ is $bc'\in PMP$; the $(1,2)$ block of a matrix in $PMQ$ with one in $QMQ$ is $bD'\in PMQ$; the $(2,1)$ block of a matrix in $QMQ$ with one in $QMP$ is $Dc'\in QMP$; and the $(2,2)$ block of a matrix in $QMQ$ with one in $QMQ$ is $DD'\in QMQ$. Every other product has a middle factor $PQ$ or $QP$ and vanishes.

Remark. The table is the table of a two-sided Peirce decomposition: the diagonal corners $PMP$ and $QMQ$ are subalgebras, the off-diagonal corners are bimodules, and the two off-diagonal products $PMQ\cdot QMP\subseteq PMP$ and $QMP\cdot PMQ\subseteq QMQ$ are the "sandwich" maps. The corner $PMQ$ has dimension $4$, the "row", and $QMP$ the dual "column"; their product is the scalar corner. The table is exactly the block multiplication of a $2\times2$ matrix with blocks of sizes $1$ and $4$, which is why the corner pattern is the pattern of the block indices.

The Corner Isomorphisms

The Scalar Corner

Proposition. For every idempotent $P$ of rank one, the compression $A\mapsto PAP$ is a unital algebra homomorphism from $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ onto $PM_5P\cong\mathbb{C}$, with kernel the set of $A$ with $PAP=0$. The image of $A$ is the scalar $\operatorname{tr}(PAP)$.

Proof. $P(AB)P=PAP\,PBP$, so the compression is multiplicative; $PIP=P$ is the unit of the corner; the corner is one-dimensional and spanned by $P$, so $PAP=\lambda P$ with $\lambda=\operatorname{tr}(PAP)/\operatorname{tr}P=\operatorname{tr}(PAP)$.

Corollary. The scalar corner is canonical: it has a canonical unit $P$ and a canonical coordinate $\lambda$, with no gauge. This is the only corner without a gauge freedom.

The Endomorphism Corner

Proposition. For an idempotent $P$ of rank $p$, the corner $PM_5P$ is isomorphic to $M_p(\mathbb{C})$, and $QM_5Q$ with $Q=I-P$ is isomorphic to $M_{5-p}(\mathbb{C})$. In particular, for $p=1$, $QM_5Q\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$.

Proof. Choose a basis $v_1,\dots,v_p$ of $\operatorname{im}P$ and $v_{p+1},\dots,v_5$ of $\ker P$. In this basis $P=\operatorname{diag}(I_p,0)$ and the compression to the first $p$ coordinates is the ordinary $p\times p$ matrix; the compression to the last $5-p$ coordinates is the ordinary $(5-p)\times(5-p)$ matrix. The isomorphisms depend on the choice of the two bases, hence are not canonical.

Remark. The corner $PM_5P$ is an algebra with unit $P$, and its unit is not the unit of $M_5$ unless $P=I$. The algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ of the biquaternion model sits inside $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ as such a corner, with unit $P$, for every rank-two idempotent $P$; this is the recursion of The $M_2\oplus M_3$ Corner. The embedding is not unital in $M_5$, but it is unital as a map into the corner.

The Coarse and Fine Decompositions Compared

The Biquaternion Case

In $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, a complete pair of orthogonal rank-one idempotents $P_+,P_-$ gives four Peirce lines

$$ A_{++}=P_+M_2P_+,\quad A_{+-}=P_+M_2P_-,\quad A_{-+}=P_-M_2P_+,\quad A_{--}=P_-M_2P_-, $$

each of dimension one. The coarse split by $P_+$ has corners

$$ P_+M_2P_+\oplus P_+M_2P_-\oplus P_-M_2P_+\oplus P_-M_2P_-, $$

of dimensions $1+1+1+1$. The coarse and the fine decomposition coincide: every corner is a line, and there is no remaining structure inside a corner.

Proof. The four lines are the four entries of a $2\times2$ matrix in the basis adapted to $P_+,P_-$; each is one-dimensional, and the sum is the whole algebra. The coarse split is the same four sets, because the partition $\{+\},\{-\}$ has no block of size larger than one.

The Order-Five Case

In $M_5(\mathbb{C})$, the coarse split by a rank-one idempotent has dimensions $1+4+4+16$. The fine split has twenty-five lines. The coarse split is obtained from the fine one by grouping the lines according to the partition $\{1\},\{2,3,4,5\}$ of the index set. The corner $QM_5Q$ of dimension $16$ is the sum of the sixteen lines $A_{kl}$ with $k,l\ne1$; it is isomorphic to $M_4(\mathbb{C})$, and it is not a line. The difference is the difference between $n=2$ and $n=5$: in $n=2$ the complement of a line is a line, so the coarse corner is one-dimensional; in $n=5$ the complement of a line is four-dimensional, and its endomorphism algebra is $M_4$.

Proposition. The coarse split of $M_5$ by a rank-one spectral projector refines to the fine split, and the refinement is the splitting of each corner into its Peirce lines:

$$ PM_5Q=\bigoplus_{l\ne1}A_{1l},\qquad QM_5P=\bigoplus_{k\ne1}A_{k1},\qquad QM_5Q=\bigoplus_{k,l\ne1}A_{kl}. $$

Proof. This is the corollary of §The Four Corners, read line by line.

Remark. The coarse split is not a second decomposition competing with the fine one; it is the fine one read through a partition of the index set. The four lines of the biquaternion algebra are the special case in which every block of the partition has size one. The general rank-two split, with corners $4+6+6+9$, is the partition $\{1,2\},\{3,4,5\}$ of the same fine decomposition, and is treated in The Rank-Two Split.

The Absence of Spinors

Remark. The Peirce lines are one-dimensional subspaces of the algebra; the minimal left ideals are five-dimensional. The two must not be identified. A minimal left ideal of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ is $M_5P$ for a rank-one idempotent $P$, and it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^5$; it is the module, not the line. In the spin chain of The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition the carrier is $\mathbb{C}^5=\operatorname{Sym}^4(\mathbb{C}^2)$, of spin $2$, and its weights are the integers $2,1,0,-1,-2$; there is no half-integer weight and hence no spin-$\tfrac12$ module in the chain. The word spinor is reserved for the half-integer case, which occurs at $n=2$, where $\mathbb{C}^2$ is the spin-$\tfrac12$ module; it is not used for the carrier of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$.

Proof. The left ideal $M_5P$ has dimension $\operatorname{rank}P\cdot5=5$, while $P_kM_5P_l$ has dimension one. The identification of the carrier with $\operatorname{Sym}^4(\mathbb{C}^2)$ is the theorem of The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition, and the weights of that module are computed there; the highest weight is $2$, so the weights are integers and the module is not spinorial.

Summary

The root $H$ and its five Lagrange projectors cut $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ into the twenty-five Peirce lines $A_{kl}=P_kM_5P_l$, each of dimension one, with the direct-sum decomposition

$$ M_5(\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{k,l=0}^{4}A_{kl}. $$

The multiplication is governed by the inner index:

$$ A_{kl}A_{mn}\subseteq\delta_{lm}A_{kn}, $$

and the product of nonzero elements is nonzero exactly when $l=m$. The diagonal lines are the scalars $A_{kk}=\mathbb{C}P_k$; the off-diagonal lines are the bimodules; the trace is the sum of the diagonal coordinates.

A rank-one idempotent $P$ and its complement $Q=I-P$ give the coarse split

$$ M_5=PM_5P\oplus PM_5Q\oplus QM_5P\oplus QM_5Q, \qquad 1+4+4+16=25, $$

with $PM_5P\cong\mathbb{C}$ and $QM_5Q\cong M_4$. The coarse split is the fine one blocked by the partition $\{1\},\{2,3,4,5\}$; it coincides with the fine one only in the biquaternion case $n=2$, where the complement of a line is a line. In $n=5$ the complement of a line is four-dimensional, and its endomorphism algebra is $M_4$.

$$ \boxed{\ \text{The Peirce lines }A_{kl}=P_kM_5P_l\text{ are the twenty-five lines of }M_5,\text{ and }A_{kl}A_{mn}=\delta_{lm}A_{kn}\text{ up to scalars.}\ } $$

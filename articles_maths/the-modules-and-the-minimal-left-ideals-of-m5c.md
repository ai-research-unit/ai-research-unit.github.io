# __The Modules and the Minimal Left Ideals of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$__

## Introduction

The algebra $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ acts on the carrier $V=\mathbb{C}^5$ by matrix multiplication. The carrier is the **simple left module**: it has no nonzero proper submodule, and every simple left module of $M_5$ is isomorphic to it. The **minimal left ideals** are the subspaces

$$
L=M_5P,\qquad P\text{ a rank-one idempotent},
$$

each of dimension $5$ and isomorphic to $V$. The five column ideals $M_5E_{11},\dots,M_5E_{55}$ are the minimal left ideals of a frame, and the left regular module decomposes as

$$
M_5=M_5E_{11}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_5E_{55}\cong V^{\oplus5}.
$$

The Peirce lines $A_{kl}=P_kM_5P_l$ are **one-dimensional subspaces**, not modules: the left ideal generated by a line $A_{kl}$ is the whole column $M_5P_l$, of dimension $5$, and the right ideal is the row $P_kM_5$. **A minimal left ideal is not a Peirce line, and a Peirce line is not a module.** This is the precise distinction that the Peirce decomposition and the module theory require.

The double centralizer is the algebra itself: the commutant of $M_5$ in $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$ is $\mathbb{C}I$, and the bicommutant is $M_5$. The algebra is semisimple, and every left module is a direct sum of copies of $V$; the right modules are the dual picture, with $V^*$ in place of $V$.

The article assumes the matrix algebra, the frame and the Peirce decomposition. It uses no form and no topology. The carrier is the spin-two module of *The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$*; **there are no spinors at $n=5$**, and the word *spinor* is not used for the carrier or for a minimal left ideal. The biquaternion case $n=2$ is the comparison: there the minimal left ideal is two-dimensional and is the spinor.

## Modules over the Algebra

### Left, Right and Bimodules

**Definition.** A **left module** over $M_5$ is a complex vector space $W$ with a bilinear map $M_5\times W\to W$, $(A,w)\mapsto Aw$, such that $(AB)w=A(Bw)$ and $Iw=w$. A **right module** is the same with the action on the right, $w(AB)=(wA)B$; a **bimodule** has both actions, and they commute.

**Proposition.** The carrier $V=\mathbb{C}^5$ is a left module by matrix multiplication, and the dual $V^*$ is a right module by $(v^*A)(v)=v^*(Av)$; equivalently, $V^*$ is a left module by $A\cdot v^*=v^*A^*$ with the transpose, but the natural structure is the right module.

**Proof.** The associativity of matrix multiplication gives the module axioms; the dual action is the transpose action, $(A^{\mathsf T}v^*)(v)=v^*(Av)$, and it is a right action because $(AB)^{\mathsf T}=B^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}$.

**Corollary.** $M_5$ itself is a left module over itself by left multiplication, a right module by right multiplication, and a bimodule with both. The algebra is the **left regular module** and the **right regular module**.

**Proof.** The axioms are the associativity and the unit of the algebra.

### The Simple Module

**Definition.** A module is **simple** if it is nonzero and has no nonzero proper submodule.

**Theorem.** The carrier $V$ is a simple left module.

**Proof.** Let $0\ne W\subseteq V$ be a submodule and let $0\ne v\in W$. Some coordinate $v_j$ is nonzero. For every $i$, $E_{ij}v=v_je_i$, so $e_i\in W$ because $W$ is invariant under $E_{ij}$. Hence $W$ contains the standard basis and $W=V$.

**Corollary.** Every nonzero vector of $V$ generates $V$ as a left module. The annihilator of $V$ is zero: the action of $M_5$ on $V$ is faithful.

**Proof.** The first statement is the proof above; the second is that $Av=0$ for all $v$ implies $A=0$.

### Semisimplicity

**Theorem.** The algebra $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ is semisimple: every left module is a direct sum of simple modules, and every submodule is a direct summand.

**Proof.** The algebra is a full matrix algebra over the semisimple algebra $\mathbb{C}$; by the Artin–Wedderburn theorem it is semisimple. Equivalently, the left regular module decomposes into the five minimal left ideals $M_5E_{jj}$, and every module is a quotient of a free module, hence a direct sum of copies of $V$ by the theory of semisimple rings.

**Corollary.** Every left module is isomorphic to $V^{\oplus m}$ for a unique $m$, and every simple left module is isomorphic to $V$.

**Proof.** A semisimple module is a direct sum of simple modules; the simple modules are the quotients of the regular module by maximal left ideals, and all are isomorphic to $V$ by the minimal-left-ideal computation below. The multiplicity $m$ is the dimension divided by $5$.

## The Minimal Left Ideals

### Rank-One Idempotents

**Definition.** A **rank-one idempotent** is a matrix $P$ with $P^2=P$ and $\operatorname{rank}P=1$.

**Proposition.** Every rank-one idempotent has the form

$$
P=v\ell,\qquad 0\ne v\in V,\quad 0\ne\ell\in V^*,\quad \ell(v)=1,
$$

where $v\ell$ is the matrix $w\mapsto\ell(w)v$. Conversely every such pair gives a rank-one idempotent.

**Proof.** The image of $P$ is a line $\mathbb{C}v$; choose $v$ spanning it. Since $P$ is the identity on its image, $Pv=v$. Define $\ell(w)$ by $Pw=\ell(w)v$; then $\ell$ is linear, $\ell(v)=1$, and $P= v\ell$. Conversely $(v\ell)(v\ell)=v\ell(v)\ell=v\ell$.

**Corollary.** The diagonal matrix units $E_{jj}=e_je_j^{\vee}$ are rank-one idempotents; the spectral projectors $P_k$ are rank-one idempotents of the root, with $P_k=v_k\ell_k$ for a vector $v_k$ spanning the spectral line and a dual functional $\ell_k$.

**Proof.** $E_{jj}$ has image $\mathbb{C}e_j$ and functional $e_j^{\vee}$; the spectral projector has the spectral line as image and the left eigenfunctional as $\ell_k$.

### The Ideals $M_5P$

**Theorem.** For a rank-one idempotent $P$, the left ideal $M_5P$ is minimal, of dimension $5$, and is isomorphic to $V$ as a left module via

$$
M_5P\longrightarrow V,\qquad A\longmapsto Av,
$$

where $v$ spans the image of $P$.

**Proof.** For $P=v\ell$ with $\ell(v)=1$, the ideal $M_5P$ consists of the matrices $A=BP=Bv\,\ell=w\ell$ with $w=Bv\in V$; it is five-dimensional, because $w$ is free. The map $A\mapsto Av=w$ is left $M_5$-linear and surjective, and its kernel on $M_5P$ is zero: $A=w\ell$ and $Av=w$, so $Av=0$ gives $w=0$ and $A=0$. It is therefore an isomorphism onto $V$. For minimality, let $0\ne A=w\ell\in M_5P$ with $w\ne0$; choose $B$ with $Bw=v$; then $BA=Bw\,\ell=v\ell=P$, so $P\in M_5A$ and $M_5P\subseteq M_5A$. Hence $M_5A=M_5P$, and $M_5P$ is simple.

**Corollary.** Every minimal left ideal is of the form $M_5P$ for a rank-one idempotent $P$, and every minimal left ideal is isomorphic to $V$. The minimal left ideals are the simple submodules of the left regular module.

**Proof.** By the standard structure theorem for full matrix algebras, every minimal left ideal is $M_5P$ for an idempotent $P$ (see Pierce, *Associative Algebras*). The idempotent can be chosen of rank one: if $\operatorname{rank}P=r$, then $M_5P$ consists of the matrices that vanish on $\ker P$, equivalently $A=AP$, and it has dimension $5r$; minimality forces $r=1$. The theorem above then gives the isomorphism with $V$.

### The Column Ideals

**Proposition.** For the diagonal idempotents $E_{jj}$,

$$
M_5E_{jj}=\{A:A\text{ has nonzero entries only in column }j\},
$$

the $j$-th **column ideal**, of dimension $5$. The left regular module decomposes as

$$
M_5=M_5E_{11}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_5E_{55}\cong V^{\oplus5}.
$$

**Proof.** $AE_{jj}$ has the $j$-th column of $A$ and zeros elsewhere; the column ideals are in direct sum and exhaust $M_5$. Each is isomorphic to $V$ by the theorem, so the sum is $V^{\oplus5}$; the dimension is $5\cdot5=25$.

**Corollary.** The five column ideals are minimal left ideals, and their sum is the whole algebra. A left ideal need not be a two-sided ideal: the column ideals are left ideals but not right ideals, and the algebra is simple as a two-sided algebra.

**Proof.** $M_5E_{jj}$ is a left ideal; it is not a right ideal because $E_{jj}M_5$ is the row ideal, not the column. The two-sided ideals are only $0$ and $M_5$ by the simplicity theorem.

### The Peirce Lines Are Not Modules

**Theorem.** The Peirce line $A_{kl}=P_kM_5P_l$ is one-dimensional and is **not** a left or right module over $M_5$ (unless $k=l$ and the algebra is one-dimensional, which it is not). The left ideal generated by $A_{kl}$ is the column $M_5P_l$, of dimension $5$; the right ideal is the row $P_kM_5$, of dimension $5$.

**Proof.** $A_{kl}$ is spanned by $E_{kl}$ (in the spectral frame). The left multiplication $M_5A_{kl}=M_5P_kM_5P_l=M_5P_l$ because $M_5P_kM_5=M_5$ (the algebra is simple and $P_k\ne0$); this is the whole column, of dimension $5$, not the line. The right multiplication is the row. A one-dimensional subspace stable under the whole algebra would be a one-dimensional module, but the simple module has dimension $5$.

**Corollary.** The Peirce decomposition

$$
M_5=\bigoplus_{k,l}A_{kl}
$$

is a decomposition into **lines**, not into modules. The minimal left ideals are the sums of the lines by columns,

$$
M_5P_l=\bigoplus_{k}A_{kl},
$$

and the minimal right ideals are the sums by rows,

$$
P_kM_5=\bigoplus_{l}A_{kl}.
$$

**Proof.** The lines are the intersections $P_kM_5P_l$; the column is the sum over $k$ for fixed $l$; the row is the sum over $l$ for fixed $k$.

**Remark.** **The Peirce line and the minimal left ideal must not be identified.** The line is one-dimensional and is not a module; the ideal is five-dimensional and is the simple module. The biquaternion article uses the columns as the minimal left ideals; in $M_5$ the columns are the five-dimensional ideals $M_5P_l$, and the lines inside them are the Peirce lines. The word *spinor* is not attached to either: the simple module is the carrier, of spin $2$.

### The Peirce Carrier

**Definition.** The **left Peirce carrier** of a rank-one idempotent $P$ is the left ideal $C_L(P)=M_5P$; the **right Peirce carrier** is the right ideal $C_R(P)=PM_5$. With $Q=I-P$, the Peirce decomposition reads

$$
C_L(P)=PM_5P\oplus QM_5P,\qquad
C_R(P)=PM_5P\oplus PM_5Q,
$$

with summands of dimensions $1$, $4$, $4$, so that both carriers have dimension $1+4=5$.

**Proposition.** The left carrier is isomorphic to $V$ as a left module, the right carrier to $V^*$ as a right module. For a spectral projector $P=P_k$, $C_L(P_k)=\bigoplus_lA_{lk}$ is the $k$-th column and $C_R(P_k)=\bigoplus_lA_{kl}$ is the $k$-th row.

**Proof.** The two decompositions are the Peirce decomposition read on the left and on the right. The dimensions are $\dim PM_5P=1$ and $\dim QM_5P=\dim PM_5Q=4$. The isomorphism $M_5P\cong V$ is the theorem of §*The Ideals $M_5P$*; for the right carrier, $PM_5=\{v\varphi:\varphi\in V^*\}$ and $v\varphi\mapsto\varphi$ is an isomorphism onto $V^*$. For the spectral projector, $A_{lk}=P_lM_5P_k$ and the column is the sum over $l$; the row is the sum over $l$ of $A_{kl}$.

**Remark.** The formula $PM_5P\oplus PM_5Q$ is the **right** carrier; the **left** carrier is $PM_5P\oplus QM_5P$. The two mixed corners are exchanged by transposition and by the involution, and they are different subspaces of $M_5$. The left carrier is the module $V$; the right carrier is the dual right module $V^*$. Neither is a Peirce line, and neither is a spinor.

## The Double Centralizer

### The Commutant

**Theorem.** The commutant of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$ in $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$ is the scalars:

$$
\{T\in\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V):TA=AT\text{ for all }A\in M_5\}=\mathbb{C}I .
$$

**Proof.** It suffices to commute with the matrix units $E_{ij}$. The identity $TE_{ij}=E_{ij}T$ in entries gives $T_{ki}\delta_{jl}=\delta_{ki}T_{jl}$; choosing $k=i$ and $l\ne j$ gives $T_{il}=0$ for $i\ne l$? Let us do it directly: $TE_{ij}$ has the $i$-th column of $T$ in column $j$; $E_{ij}T$ has the $j$-th row of $T$ in row $i$. Comparing the $(a,b)$ entries, $T_{ai}\delta_{bj}=\delta_{ai}T_{jb}$. For $a=i,b=j$: $T_{ii}=T_{jj}$; for $a\ne i,b=j$: $T_{ai}=0$. As $i,j$ vary, $T$ is diagonal with all diagonal entries equal. Hence $T=\lambda I$.

**Corollary.** Every $M_5$-linear endomorphism of $V$ is scalar; by Schur's lemma this is the statement that $V$ is simple, and the scalar is the unique intertwiner.

**Proof.** An intertwiner is an element of the commutant; the commutant is $\mathbb{C}I$.

### The Bicommutant

**Theorem.** The bicommutant of $M_5$ is $M_5$:

$$
\{T\in\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V):TS=ST\text{ for all }S\in M_5\}=M_5 .
$$

**Proof.** The bicommutant contains $M_5$ because every element of $M_5$ commutes with every element of $M_5$. Conversely, the commutant of $M_5$ is $\mathbb{C}I$, and the commutant of $\mathbb{C}I$ is all of $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)=M_5$; so the bicommutant is $M_5$. This is the double centralizer theorem for a full matrix algebra.

**Corollary.** The algebra is its own bicommutant, and the pair $(M_5,V)$ is a **central simple algebra with its simple module**. The algebra is isomorphic to $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$.

**Proof.** The natural map $M_5\to\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$ is injective (the action is faithful) and both spaces have dimension $25$, so it is an isomorphism; the bicommutant is the algebra.

**Remark.** **The double centralizer is the algebraic form of the statement that the only operators commuting with all matrices are scalars.** It is the reason the module $V$ has no extra structure: an $M_5$-linear map is scalar, and the endomorphism algebra of $V$ is the whole matrix algebra. The same statement in the biquaternion case gives the quaternion structure on the spinor; in $M_5$ it gives only the scalars.

## The Classification

### Every Module Is a Direct Sum of Copies of $V$

**Theorem.** Every left $M_5$-module is isomorphic to $V^{\oplus m}$ for a unique $m\ge0$. Every right module is isomorphic to $(V^*)^{\oplus m}$.

**Proof.** The algebra is semisimple, so every module is a direct sum of simple modules; every simple module is isomorphic to $V$ by the minimal-left-ideal theorem; the multiplicity is the dimension divided by $5$. The right case is the dual: the simple right module is $V^*$, and the right regular module is $(V^*)^{\oplus5}$.

**Corollary.** The left regular module is $V^{\oplus5}$, the right regular module is $(V^*)^{\oplus5}$, and the algebra as a bimodule is $V\otimes V^*$.

**Proof.** The column decomposition gives the left regular module; the row decomposition gives the right; the identification $M_5\cong V\otimes V^*$ sends $v\otimes\ell$ to $v\ell$, and the left and right actions are the two factors.

### The Left Regular Module

**Proposition.** The left regular module has the decomposition

$$
M_5=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{5}M_5E_{jj},
$$

and each summand is a minimal left ideal isomorphic to $V$. The endomorphism ring of the left regular module is $M_5^{\mathrm{op}}\cong M_5$.

**Proof.** The column decomposition is the direct sum; each column is minimal and isomorphic to $V$; the endomorphisms of the left regular module are the right multiplications, which form the opposite algebra, isomorphic to $M_5$ by the transpose.

**Corollary.** The multiplicity of $V$ in the left regular module is $5$, and the dimension count is $5\cdot5=25$.

**Proof.** There are five column ideals, each isomorphic to $V$; the dimension is $5\cdot5$.

### The Right Regular Module

**Proposition.** The right regular module has the decomposition

$$
M_5=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{5}E_{kk}M_5,
$$

and each summand is a minimal right ideal isomorphic to $V^*$. The right regular module is $(V^*)^{\oplus5}$.

**Proof.** The row decomposition is the direct sum; each row is a minimal right ideal, dual to the column; the dimension is $5\cdot5=25$.

**Corollary.** The left and right regular modules are dual, and the bimodule $M_5$ is $V\otimes V^*$.

**Proof.** The dual of $V^{\oplus5}$ is $(V^*)^{\oplus5}$; the tensor product identification is the standard one.

## The Biquaternion Comparison

### The $M_2$ Case

For $n=2$, the algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{B}$ has a two-dimensional simple module, the **spinor**. The minimal left ideals are the column ideals $M_2E_{11}$ and $M_2E_{22}$, each of dimension $2$ and isomorphic to the spinor; the left regular module is $(\mathbb{C}^2)^{\oplus2}$. The Peirce lines are one-dimensional; the minimal left ideal is two-dimensional; the same distinction holds.

**Proposition.** The $M_2$ minimal left ideal $M_2P$ is two-dimensional and is the spinor of the biquaternion model. The commutant of $M_2$ is $\mathbb{C}I$, and the bicommutant is $M_2$.

**Proof.** The same proofs with $n=2$: the column has dimension $2$; the commutant is scalar; the bicommutant is the algebra.

### No Spinors at $M_5$

**Theorem.** The simple module of $M_5$ is the carrier $V=\mathbb{C}^5$, of spin $2$. There is no spinor module at $n=5$: the carrier is the symmetric fourth power $\operatorname{Sym}^4(\mathbb{C}^2)$, and its weights are the integers $2,1,0,-1,-2$.

**Proof.** The identification of the carrier with the symmetric power is *The Spin Chain and the Adjoint Decomposition of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$*; the weights are integers, so no half-integer weight occurs. The simple module is unique up to isomorphism and is $V$.

**Corollary.** The minimal left ideal of $M_5$ is five-dimensional and is isomorphic to the carrier; it is not a spinor, not a column of a matrix in the sense of an element, and not a Peirce line. The word *spinor* is reserved for the two-dimensional module of $M_2$.

**Proof.** The minimal left ideal is $M_5P\cong V$, of dimension $5$; the Peirce line is one-dimensional; the column is the space, not an element.

**Remark.** **The biquaternion spinor and the $M_5$ carrier are different modules of different algebras.** The first is the two-dimensional simple module of $M_2$; the second is the five-dimensional simple module of $M_5$, of spin $2$. The minimal left ideals are the modules in both cases, but the dimensions and the spin differ. The Peirce lines are one-dimensional in both cases, and they are not modules in either.

## Summary

The carrier $V=\mathbb{C}^5$ is the simple left module of $M_5(\mathbb{C})$; every simple left module is isomorphic to $V$, and every left module is $V^{\oplus m}$. The minimal left ideals are the spaces $M_5P$ for rank-one idempotents $P$, of dimension $5$, isomorphic to $V$; the column ideals $M_5E_{jj}$ give the decomposition

$$
M_5=M_5E_{11}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_5E_{55}\cong V^{\oplus5}.
$$

$$
\boxed{\ \text{The simple module is }V=\mathbb{C}^5\text{, of spin }2;\quad \text{the minimal left ideals are }M_5P\cong V;\quad \text{the Peirce lines are not modules.}\ }
$$

The commutant of $M_5$ is $\mathbb{C}I$, the bicommutant is $M_5$, and the algebra is $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$. The right modules are the dual picture, with $V^*$; the bimodule is $V\otimes V^*$. At $n=2$ the simple module is the two-dimensional spinor; at $n=5$ it is the five-dimensional carrier, and there are no spinors.

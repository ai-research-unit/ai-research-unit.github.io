The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the Quaternion Algebra and the Bi-module Structure

Introduction

The left and right multiplications of $\mathbb{H}$ generate together an algebra of operators, and that algebra is the enveloping algebra $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{H}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$. It is isomorphic to the full algebra of real-linear endomorphisms of the four-dimensional space, and the isomorphism is explicit: the elementary tensor $\tilde a\otimes\tilde b^{\mathrm{opp}}$ acts as the sandwich $L_{\tilde a}R_{\tilde b}$, $\tilde q\mapsto\tilde a\tilde q\tilde b$. This article defines the enveloping algebra, identifies it with $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{R}(\mathbb{H})$, writes the Conway operator basis and its composition rule, reads the one-sided operators as the edge rows and the two-sided operators as the elementary tensors, and describes the regular bimodule as a module over the enveloping algebra. The product and the operators are Two-Sided Operators on the Quaternion Algebra, below this article in this category, and One-Sided Operators on the Quaternion Algebra, below this article in this category; the transpose of a sandwich and the matrix formula $D\,G^{\mathsf T}D$ are Association and the Transpose on the Quaternion Algebra, above this article in this category; the module category is Modules over the Quaternion Algebra, above this article in this category.

The Enveloping Algebra

Definition. The enveloping algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ is $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{H}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, the tensor product of the algebra with its opposite, of real dimension $16$.

Theorem (the sandwich action). The assignment $\tilde a\otimes\tilde b^{\mathrm{opp}}\mapsto T_{\tilde a,\tilde b}$, where $T_{\tilde a,\tilde b}(\tilde q)=\tilde a\tilde q\tilde b$, extends uniquely to a representation of $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$ on the real vector space $\mathbb{H}$, and makes ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{H}$ a module over $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$.

Proof. The sandwich $T_{\tilde a,\tilde b}$ is bilinear in $(\tilde a,\tilde b)$, so it defines a linear map on the tensor product $\mathbb{H}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$. It is multiplicative: $T_{\tilde a,\tilde b}T_{\tilde c,\tilde d}(\tilde q)=\tilde a\tilde c\,\tilde q\,\tilde d\tilde b=T_{\tilde a\tilde c,\tilde d\tilde b}(\tilde q)$, and $\tilde a\tilde c$ is the product in $\mathbb{H}$ while $\tilde d\tilde b$ is the product in $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ of $\tilde b^{\mathrm{opp}}$ and $\tilde d^{\mathrm{opp}}$; so the map is an algebra homomorphism, and the associative law of the algebra is exactly the compatibility of the two actions.

The Identification with the Endomorphisms

Theorem. The sandwich representation is an isomorphism $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}\overset{\sim}{\to}\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{R}(\mathbb{H})$; both sides have real dimension $16$.

Proof. The map is a non-zero algebra homomorphism, since $e_0\otimes e_0^{\mathrm{opp}}$ acts as the identity operator, and both algebras have real dimension $16$. The algebra $\mathbb{H}$ is central simple, and the tensor product of central simple algebras is central simple, so $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$ is simple; a non-zero homomorphism from a simple algebra into a ring is injective. Hence the map is injective, and by the equality of dimensions it is bijective.

Corollary. $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}\cong\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{R}(\mathbb{H})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$, and $\mathbb{H}$ is a simple module over $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$.

Proof. The identification; the simplicity is the simplicity of ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$, a module over a quotient of $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$.

The Conway Operator Basis

Definition. Following Conway, write $\tilde a[\,]\tilde b$ for the operator $\tilde q\mapsto\tilde a\tilde q\tilde b$. The sixteen operators $e_n[\,]e_m$, $n,m\in\{0,1,2,3\}$, are the Conway operator basis of $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$.

Equivalent forms. $e_n[\,]e_m=L_{e_n}R_{e_m}$, and $e_0[\,]e_m=R_{e_m}$ while $e_n[\,]e_0=L_{e_n}$.

The Composition Rule

Theorem. $(e_a[\,]e_b)(e_c[\,]e_d)=(e_ae_c)[\,](e_de_b)$.

Proof. Apply the left side to $\tilde q$: $e_a\bigl(e_c\tilde qe_d\bigr)e_b=e_ae_c\,\tilde q\,e_de_b$, which is the right side.

Corollary (structure constants). Since $e_ae_c=\varepsilon(a,c)e_{a\oplus c}$ with $\varepsilon(a,c)=\pm1$ and $\oplus$ the quaternion group law on the indices, and $e_de_b=\varepsilon(d,b)e_{d\oplus b}$,

$$ (e_a[\,]e_b)(e_c[\,]e_d)=\varepsilon(a,c)\varepsilon(d,b)\,e_{a\oplus c}[\,]e_{d\oplus b}. $$

Proof. Substitute the two basis products in the composition rule.

Corollary (the transpose of the basis element). $(e_n[\,]e_m)^{\sim}=e_m[\,]e_n$.

Proof. The transpose of a sandwich swaps its parameters, by Association and the Transpose on the Quaternion Algebra, above this article in this category.

The One-Sided Operators as Edge Rows

Theorem. The one-sided operators are the edge rows of the Conway basis:

$$ L_{e_n}=e_n[\,]e_0,\qquad R_{e_m}=e_0[\,]e_m . $$

Proof. The definitions: $e_n[\,]e_0(\tilde q)=e_n\tilde q\,e_0=e_n\tilde q$ and $e_0[\,]e_m(\tilde q)=e_0\tilde qe_m=\tilde qe_m$.

Corollary. The left multiplications span the $4$-dimensional subspace $\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{R}e_0^{\mathrm{opp}}$ and the right multiplications the subspace $\mathbb{R}e_0\otimes\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$; the two subspaces intersect in the scalars $\mathbb{R}(e_0[\,]e_0)$, the identity operator.

Proof. $L$ is injective, so the $L_{e_n}$ span a $4$-dimensional space, the first tensor factor; similarly the second. An operator that is both a left and a right multiplication is multiplication by a central element, that is a scalar, by the centre theorem.

Two Commuting Copies

Theorem. The left and the right multiplications commute: $L_{\tilde a}R_{\tilde b}=R_{\tilde b}L_{\tilde a}$, and they generate $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$.

Proof. $L_{\tilde a}R_{\tilde b}(\tilde q)=\tilde a\tilde q\tilde b=R_{\tilde b}L_{\tilde a}(\tilde q)$ by associativity. The products $L_{\tilde a}R_{\tilde b}$ are the images of the elementary tensors, which span $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$.

Thus $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$ contains the two commuting copies $L(\mathbb{H})$ and $R(\mathbb{H})$ of $\mathbb{H}$ and of $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, and it is their tensor product; the commutant of $L(\mathbb{H})$ inside $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{R}(\mathbb{H})$ is $R(\mathbb{H})$, and conversely, the double centraliser of the two-sided action of $\mathbb{H}$ is the pair $(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}})$.

The Two-Sided Operators as the Elementary Tensors

Theorem. The map $\tilde a\otimes\tilde b^{\mathrm{opp}}\mapsto \tilde a[\,]\tilde b=L_{\tilde a}R_{\tilde b}$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-linear bijection from the $16$-dimensional tensor product onto the two-sided operators, with tensor rank the minimal number of sandwiches.

Proof. The map is the representation of the enveloping algebra, an isomorphism; the tensor rank statement is the definition of the rank of a tensor in $\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$, transported by the isomorphism.

Corollary. The two-sided operators form the whole of $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{R}(\mathbb{H})$: every real-linear operator of the four-dimensional space is a sandwich, and the Conway basis is a basis of the operator algebra.

Proof. The theorem and the identification above.

The Regular Bimodule

Theorem. ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{H}$ is a module over $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$ by the sandwich action; it is the standard module, simple, of real dimension four, and the whole of $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$ acts faithfully on it.

Proof. The compatibility of the two actions is associativity, as in the sandwich theorem; faithfulness is injectivity of the representation, that is the identification. Simplicity and the module theory are Modules over the Quaternion Algebra, above this article in this category.

Corollary. The enveloping algebra is the endomorphism algebra of its own standard module: $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}=\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{R}({}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{H})$, and the centre of $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$ is the scalars acting by $T_{\lambda e_0,\lambda e_0}$, a one-dimensional space.

Proof. The identification with the endomorphisms; an operator commuting with all sandwiches is a scalar, since it must equal both a left and a right multiplication, and only the scalars are central.

Comparison with the Complex Case

The two constructions differ only in the coefficient field: over $\mathbb{R}$ the operator algebra is a real matrix algebra and over $\mathbb{C}$ a complex one, and the standard module over the enveloping algebra is simple in both cases; the splitting into the two columns appears in the one-sided regular module of the matrix model, not in the standard module.

Summary

The enveloping algebra $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{H}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ has dimension $16$ and is isomorphic to the real-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{H}$ by the sandwich action $\tilde a\otimes\tilde b^{\mathrm{opp}}\mapsto(\tilde q\mapsto\tilde a\tilde q\tilde b)$. Its Conway basis $e_n[\,]e_m$ obeys $(e_a[\,]e_b)(e_c[\,]e_d)=(e_ae_c)[\,](e_de_b)$, with structure constants read from the basis products; the one-sided operators are the edge rows $L_{e_n}=e_n[\,]e_0$ and $R_{e_m}=e_0[\,]e_m$; the left and right multiplications are two commuting copies whose product spans the algebra; and the two-sided operators are the elementary tensors, so that every real-linear operator is a sandwich. The regular bimodule is the standard simple module over $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}$, faithful and of dimension four; over the complex case the same construction gives $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ and a standard module that is the algebra itself, of complex dimension four.

Operators on the Reduced Biquaternions

Introduction

Each of the four general products of the reduced biquaternion algebra has a left multiplication operator, $L^{\circ}_{Q_\pm}:Q_{\pm,2}\mapsto Q_\pm\circ Q_{\pm,2}$, and the four operators are collected here and compared. Two of them are complex-linear, those of the two bilinear products; two are conjugate-linear, those of the two sesquilinear products. On the complex basis $1,j$ each is a $2\times2$ matrix, and in the idempotent basis each becomes diagonal or off-diagonal of a simple shape, which makes the trace, the determinant, the eigenvalues and the invertibility visible at once. The multiplication of the algebra is commutative, so the left and the right multiplication operators of the plain product coincide; the algebra has zero divisors, so an operator may be singular at a non-zero element; and it is not a division algebra, so no operator calculus makes it one. The plain operator alone, with its matrix, trace, determinant and adjoints, is The Multiplication Operators of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; this article is the collection and the comparison of the four.

Conventions. The basis is $1,i,j,ij$ with $i^{2}=-1$, $j^{2}=+1$, $ji=ij$; the element is $Q_\pm=Q_++jQ_-$ with $Q_+=q_++iq'_+$, $Q_-=q_-+iq'_-$; the idempotents are $\Pi_{\pm}=\tfrac12(1\pm j)$ and the idempotent coordinates are $Q_{++}=Q_++Q_-$, $Q_{--}=Q_+-Q_-$, with $Q_\pm=Q_{++}\Pi_++Q_{--}\Pi_-$. The four general products are the general plain bilinear $Q_{\pm,1}Q_{\pm,2}$, the general quaternionic bilinear $Q_{\pm,1}^{\natural}Q_{\pm,2}$, the general plain sesquilinear $Q_{\pm,1}Q_{\pm,2}^{*}$ and the general quaternionic sesquilinear $Q_{\pm,1}^{\natural}Q_{\pm,2}^{*}$, with the forms $B,N,H,K$ of The Four Products, Their Forms and Their Algebraic Norms. The element glyph is $Q_\pm$, without a tilde; a product written $\circ$ is generic and its identity is stated at each use.

Boundaries. The four products and their forms are The Four Products, Their Forms and Their Algebraic Norms, and the forms are The Forms of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; the plain operator alone is The Multiplication Operators of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; the idempotent splitting is Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; the invertibility criterion is Norm and Invertibility in the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; and the matrices of the element are Introduction to the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation of Reduced Biquaternions. The biquaternion counterpart is Operators on the Biquaternions.

The Four Left Multiplication Operators

Definition. For a product $\circ$ and an element $Q_\pm$, the left multiplication is the map $$ L^{\circ}_{Q_\pm}:Q_{\pm,2}\mapsto Q_\pm\circ Q_{\pm,2} , $$ with the superscript dropped for the plain product, $L_{Q_\pm}(Q_{\pm,2})=Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2}$.

The Matrices on the Complex Basis

Theorem (the four matrices). On the complex basis $1,j$, acting on the column $\binom{Q_{2+}}{Q_{2-}}$ of $Q_{\pm,2}=Q_{2+}+jQ_{2-}$, the four left multiplications are $$ L_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_+&Q_-\\ Q_-&Q_+\end{pmatrix},\qquad L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_+&-Q_-\\ -Q_-&Q_+\end{pmatrix}, $$ $$ L^{*}_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_+&-Q_-\\ Q_-&-Q_+\end{pmatrix}\circ\kappa,\qquad L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_+&Q_-\\ -Q_-&-Q_+\end{pmatrix}\circ\kappa , $$ where $\kappa$ is the coefficient conjugation $\kappa\binom{Q_{2+}}{Q_{2-}}=\binom{\bar Q_{2+}}{\bar Q_{2-}}$ and $Q_\pm=Q_++jQ_-$.

Proof. Read the four coordinate tables. The plain product $Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2}=(Q_+Q_{2+}+Q_-Q_{2-})+(Q_+Q_{2-}+Q_-Q_{2+})j$ has the columns $\binom{Q_+}{Q_-}$ and $\binom{Q_-}{Q_+}$, giving the first matrix. The quaternionic bilinear product $Q_\pm^{\natural}Q_{\pm,2}=(Q_+Q_{2+}-Q_-Q_{2-})+(Q_+Q_{2-}-Q_-Q_{2+})j$ has the columns $\binom{Q_+}{-Q_-}$ and $\binom{-Q_-}{Q_+}$, giving the second. For the two sesquilinear products, $Q_{\pm,2}^{*}=\binom{\bar Q_{2+}}{-\bar Q_{2-}}=\sigma_{3}\kappa\binom{Q_{2+}}{Q_{2-}}$ with $\sigma_{3}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, so $L^{*}_{Q_\pm}=L_{Q_\pm}\sigma_{3}\kappa$ and $L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}=L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}\sigma_{3}\kappa$; multiplying the two bilinear matrices into $\sigma_{3}$ gives the last two displays.

Corollary (linear against conjugate-linear). The operators $L_{Q_\pm}$ and $L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}$ of the bilinear products are complex-linear; the operators $L^{*}_{Q_\pm}$ and $L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}$ of the sesquilinear products are conjugate-linear, and are real-linear but not complex-linear.

Proof. The bilinear matrices have constant complex entries and act by matrix multiplication, hence are complex-linear; the two sesquilinear operators carry the map $\kappa$, which is conjugate-linear, so they are conjugate-linear.

The Matrices on the Idempotent Basis

Theorem (the idempotent form). In the idempotent basis, acting on the column $\binom{Q_{2++}}{Q_{2--}}$ of $Q_{\pm,2}=Q_{2++}\Pi_++Q_{2--}\Pi_-$, the four operators are $$ L_{Q_\pm}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++},Q_{--}),\qquad L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--},Q_{++}), $$ $$ L^{*}_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{++}\\ Q_{--}&0\end{pmatrix}\circ\kappa,\qquad L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{--}\\ Q_{++}&0\end{pmatrix}\circ\kappa . $$

Proof. In the idempotent coordinates the product is componentwise, $Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2}=Q_{++}\,Q_{2++}\,\Pi_++Q_{--}\,Q_{2--}\,\Pi_-=L_{Q_\pm}(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})=(Q_{++}Q_{2++},Q_{--}Q_{2--})$, which is $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++},Q_{--})$; and $Q_\pm^{\natural}=Q_{--}\Pi_++Q_{++}\Pi_-$, so $Q_\pm^{\natural}Q_{\pm,2}=(Q_{--} Q_{2++})\Pi_++(Q_{++} Q_{2--})\Pi_-$. For the sesquilinear products, $Q_{\pm,2}^{*}=\bar Q_{2--}\Pi_++\bar Q_{2++}\Pi_-$ because the star exchanges the two idempotents, so $Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2}^{*}=Q_{++}\bar Q_{2--}\Pi_++Q_{--}\bar Q_{2++}\Pi_-$ and $Q_\pm^{\natural}Q_{\pm,2}^{*}=Q_{--}\bar Q_{2--}\Pi_++Q_{++}\bar Q_{2++}\Pi_-$, which are the two off-diagonal displays.

Corollary (the plain product is diagonal). The plain operator is diagonal in the idempotent basis, with the two idempotent coordinates on the diagonal; the quaternionic bilinear operator is the same diagonal with the two entries exchanged, and the two sesquilinear operators are the two off-diagonal matrices with the conjugation. The four operators differ only in the placement of $Q_{++}$ and $Q_{--}$.

Proof. Read the four displays: the plain operator has $Q_{++}$ first and $Q_{--}$ second, the quaternionic bilinear operator the reverse, and the two sesquilinear operators have zeros on the diagonal and carry $Q_{++},Q_{--}$ on the two off-diagonal entries.

The Right Multiplication Operators

The right multiplication operators coincide with the left ones for the plain product and differ for the other three.

Theorem. For the plain product the right multiplication $R_{Q_\pm}:Q_{\pm,2}\mapsto Q_{\pm,2}Q_\pm$ equals the left one, $R_{Q_\pm}=L_{Q_\pm}$, because the multiplication is commutative. For the other three products the right operator is $R^{\circ}_{Q_\pm}(Q_{\pm,2})=Q_{\pm,2}\circ Q_\pm$, and in the idempotent coordinates it is the left operator with the involution moved from $Q_\pm$ in the left slot to $Q_{\pm,2}$ in the right slot: writing $Q_\pm=(Q_{++},Q_{--})$ and $Q_{\pm,2}=(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})$, $$ R^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{++}\\ Q_{--}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad R^{*}_{Q_\pm}=\operatorname{diag}(\bar Q_{--},\bar Q_{++}),\qquad R^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\bar Q_{--}\\ \bar Q_{++}&0\end{pmatrix}. $$

Proof. Commutativity gives $Q_{\pm,2}Q_\pm=Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2}$, hence $R_{Q_\pm}=L_{Q_\pm}$. For the other three, evaluate $Q_{\pm,2}\circ Q_\pm$ in the idempotent coordinates: the natural sign sends $Q_{\pm,2}=(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})$ to $(Q_{2--},Q_{2++})$, so $R^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})=(Q_{2--},Q_{2++})\cdot(Q_{++},Q_{--})=(Q_{++}Q_{2--},Q_{--}Q_{2++})$; the star sends $Q_\pm=(Q_{++},Q_{--})$ to $(\bar Q_{--},\bar Q_{++})$, so $R^{*}_{Q_\pm}(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})=(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})\cdot(\bar Q_{--},\bar Q_{++})=(Q_{2++}\bar Q_{--},Q_{2--}\bar Q_{++})$; and combining the two, $R^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})=(Q_{2--}\bar Q_{--},Q_{2++}\bar Q_{++})$. These are the three displays, and they differ from the left operators $L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--},Q_{++})$, $L^{*}_{Q_\pm}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&Q_{++}\\ Q_{--}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\circ\kappa$ and $L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&Q_{--}\\ Q_{++}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\circ\kappa$.

Corollary (the commutant). The operators commuting with all the left multiplications of the plain product are the right multiplications, which are the left multiplications themselves; so the commutant of the image is the image, and the operator algebra $\{L_{Q_\pm}\}$ is a maximal commutative subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}})$ of complex dimension two.

Proof. An operator commuting with every $L_{Q_\pm}$ commutes in particular with $L_{j}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, hence is diagonal in the idempotent basis, hence is in the image; and the image is commutative by the commutativity of the algebra.

Trace, Determinant and Eigenvalues

The two bilinear operators share the trace $2Q_+$, the determinant $N$ and the eigenvalues $Q_{++},Q_{--}$; the two sesquilinear operators are conjugate-linear with real determinant $\lvert N\rvert^{2}$.

Theorem (the invariants). For $Q_\pm=Q_{++}\Pi_++Q_{--}\Pi_-$, $$ \operatorname{tr}L_{Q_\pm}=\operatorname{tr}L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}=Q_{++}+Q_{--}=2Q_+,\qquad \det L_{Q_\pm}=\det L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}=Q_{++}Q_{--}=\langle Q_\pm\rangle_{\natural}, $$ with eigenvalues $Q_{++}$ and $Q_{--}$. The two sesquilinear operators satisfy $$ (L^{*}_{Q_\pm})^{2}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++}\bar Q_{--},\ Q_{--}\bar Q_{++}),\qquad (L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm})^{2}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--}\bar Q_{++},\ Q_{++}\bar Q_{--}), $$ and their real determinants are $$ \det_{\mathbb{R}}L^{*}_{Q_\pm}=\det_{\mathbb{R}}L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}=\lvert Q_{++}Q_{--}\rvert^{2}=\lvert \langle Q_\pm\rangle_{\natural}\rvert^{2}. $$

Proof. The trace and the determinant of $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++},Q_{--})$ and of $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--},Q_{++})$ are $Q_{++}+Q_{--}$ and $Q_{++}Q_{--}$; in the complex basis the plain matrix $\begin{smallmatrix}Q_+&Q_-\\Q_-&Q_+\end{smallmatrix}$ has characteristic polynomial $(Q_+-\lambda)^{2}-Q_-^{2}$, whose roots are $Q_+\pm Q_-=Q_{++},Q_{--}$, and the quaternionic bilinear matrix gives the same. For the sesquilinear operators, applying the operator twice conjugates twice and uses the display of §The Matrices on the Idempotent Basis: $(Q_{2++},Q_{2--})\mapsto(Q_{++}\bar Q_{2--},Q_{--}\bar Q_{2++})\mapsto(Q_{++}\overline{Q_{--}\bar Q_{2++}},Q_{--}\overline{Q_{++}\bar Q_{2--}})=(Q_{++}\bar Q_{--}\,Q_{2++},Q_{--}\bar Q_{++}\,Q_{2--})$, and symmetrically for the companion. The real determinant of a real-linear operator given by a complex matrix composed with the conjugation is the squared modulus of the complex determinant, $\lvert\det\begin{smallmatrix}0&Q_{++}\\Q_{--}&0\end{smallmatrix}\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_{++}Q_{--}\rvert^{2}$.

Corollary (the invertibility is the unit criterion). Each of the four operators is invertible exactly when $Q_{++}\neq0$ and $Q_{--}\neq0$, that is exactly when $Q_\pm$ is a unit; and each is singular at a non-zero element exactly when $Q_\pm$ is a zero divisor. The operator picture does not remove the zero divisors: the singular non-zero elements are present in all four.

Proof. The determinant of the two bilinear operators is $Q_{++}Q_{--}$ and the real determinant of the two sesquilinear operators is $\lvert Q_{++}Q_{--}\rvert^{2}$, both non-zero exactly for the units of Norm and Invertibility in the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions.

The Adjoints and the Involutions

The adjoint of the plain operator is the conjugate for the definite form and the star-conjugate for the indefinite one.

Theorem (the adjoints). For the definite form $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$, the adjoint of the plain multiplication is the bar-conjugate element, $$ (L_{Q_\pm})^{K}=L_{\bar Q_\pm}; $$ for the indefinite form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, the adjoint is the star-conjugate element, $$ (L_{Q_\pm})^{H}=L_{Q_\pm^{*}} . $$

Proof. In the idempotent coordinates, with $Q_{\pm,i}=Q_{i++}\Pi_++Q_{i--}\Pi_-$, one has $\langle Q_{\pm,1},Q_{\pm,2}\rangle_{\natural*}=\tfrac12(Q_{1++}\bar Q_{2++}+Q_{1--}\bar Q_{2--})$ and $H(Q_{\pm,1},Q_{\pm,2})=\tfrac12(Q_{1++}\bar Q_{2--}+Q_{1--}\bar Q_{2++})$. For $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$: $\langle L_{Q_\pm}Q_{\pm,2},Q_{\pm,3}\rangle_{\natural*}=K(Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2},Q_{\pm,3})=\tfrac12(Q_{++}Q_{2++}\bar Q_{3++}+Q_{--}Q_{2--}\bar Q_{3--})$ and $\langle Q_{\pm,2},L_{\bar Q_\pm}Q_{\pm,3}\rangle_{\natural*}=\tfrac12(Q_{2++}\overline{\bar Q_{++}Q_{3++}}+Q_{2--}\overline{\bar Q_{--}Q_{3--}})=\tfrac12(Q_{++}Q_{2++}\bar Q_{3++}+Q_{--}Q_{2--}\bar Q_{3--})$, equal. For $H$: $H(Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2},Q_{\pm,3})=\tfrac12(Q_{++}Q_{2++}\bar Q_{3--}+Q_{--}Q_{2--}\bar Q_{3++})$ and $H(Q_{\pm,2},Q_\pm^{*}Q_{\pm,3})$ with $Q_\pm^{*}$ of coordinates $(\bar Q_{--},\bar Q_{++})$ gives $\tfrac12(Q_{2++}\overline{\bar Q_{--} Q_{3++}}+Q_{2--}\overline{\bar Q_{++} Q_{3--}})=\tfrac12(Q_{++}Q_{2++}\bar Q_{3--}+Q_{--}Q_{2--}\bar Q_{3++})$, equal. The two adjoint rules are the two rows of the table of the conjugations, the bar for the definite form and the star for the indefinite one.

Corollary (the involutions act by swapping). In the idempotent basis the natural sign swaps the two diagonal entries, $L_{Q_\pm^{\natural}}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--},Q_{++})$, and the star conjugates and swaps them, $L_{Q_\pm^{*}}=\operatorname{diag}(\bar Q_{--},\bar Q_{++})$; the two are the transposition and the conjugate-transposition of the diagonal model.

Proof. The idempotent coordinate of $Q_\pm^{\natural}$ are $(Q_{--},Q_{++})$ and those of $Q_\pm^{*}$ are $(\bar Q_{--},\bar Q_{++})$, by Introduction to the $2\times2$ Matrix Element Representation of Reduced Biquaternions; the operator of $Q_\pm^{\natural}$ is $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--},Q_{++})$ and that of $Q_\pm^{*}$ is $\operatorname{diag}(\bar Q_{--},\bar Q_{++})$.

The Algebra of Operators

Theorem (the operator algebra). The map $Q_\pm\mapsto L_{Q_\pm}$ is an isomorphism of algebras onto the two-dimensional space of operators $\{L_{Q_\pm}\}$, and the enveloping algebra generated by the left and the right multiplications acts through the left multiplications alone, its image being $\{L_{Q_\pm}\}$ and not the whole of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}})$.

Proof. The multiplicativity $L_{Q_{\pm,1}}L_{Q_{\pm,2}}=L_{Q_{\pm,1}Q_{\pm,2}}$ and the injectivity are Introduction to Reduced Biquaternion Element Representations; because the algebra is commutative every product $L_{Q_{\pm,1}}R_{Q_{\pm,2}}=L_{Q_{\pm,1}Q_{\pm,2}}$ is again a left multiplication, so the image of the enveloping algebra is $\{L_{Q_\pm}\}$, of complex dimension two, against the four dimensions of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}})\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{C})$.

Corollary (the sandwich operators). Every sandwich operator is a left multiplication, $\Theta_{Q_\pm}(Q_{\pm,2})=Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2}Q_\pm=L_{Q_\pm^{2}}(Q_{\pm,2})$, and the sandwich operators carry no information beyond the left multiplications; in the idempotent basis $\Theta_{Q_\pm}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++}^{2},Q_{--}^{2})$.

Proof. The commutativity gives $Q_\pm Q_{\pm,2}Q_\pm=Q_\pm^{2}Q_{\pm,2}$, whose operator is $L_{Q_\pm^{2}}$ with diagonal form $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++}^{2},Q_{--}^{2})$.

Theorem (the two pairs). The two bilinear operators are complex-linear with the same trace and determinant, differing by the exchange of the two idempotent coordinates; the two sesquilinear operators are conjugate-linear with the same real determinant and differ by the same exchange. All four are invertible exactly for the units, and all four are singular at the zero divisors.

Proof. The invariants are those of §Trace, Determinant and Eigenvalues; the exchange of the two coordinates is the natural sign, which is the difference between the plain and the quaternionic slot; and the invertibility is the unit criterion.

Summary

Each of the four general products of the reduced biquaternion algebra has a left multiplication operator, $L_{Q_\pm}$, $L^{\natural}_{Q_\pm}$, $L^{*}_{Q_\pm}$ and $L^{\natural*}_{Q_\pm}$, and the four are collected and compared here. On the complex basis $1,j$ they are the matrices $\begin{smallmatrix}Q_+&Q_-\\Q_-&Q_+\end{smallmatrix}$, $\begin{smallmatrix}Q_+&-Q_-\\-Q_-&Q_+\end{smallmatrix}$, $\begin{smallmatrix}Q_+&-Q_-\\Q_-&-Q_+\end{smallmatrix}\circ\kappa$ and $\begin{smallmatrix}Q_+&Q_-\\-Q_-&-Q_+\end{smallmatrix}\circ\kappa$; in the idempotent basis they are $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++},Q_{--})$, $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--},Q_{++})$ and the two off-diagonal matrices with the conjugation, differing only in the placement of $Q_{++}$ and $Q_{--}$. The two bilinear operators are complex-linear with trace $Q_{++}+Q_{--}=2Q_+$, determinant $Q_{++}Q_{--}=\langle Q_\pm\rangle_{\natural}$ and eigenvalues $Q_{++},Q_{--}$; the two sesquilinear operators are conjugate-linear, with squares $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{++}\bar Q_{--},Q_{--}\bar Q_{++})$ and $\operatorname{diag}(Q_{--}\bar Q_{++},Q_{++}\bar Q_{--})$ and real determinant $\lvert \langle Q_\pm\rangle_{\natural}\rvert^{2}$. Every operator is invertible exactly for the units and singular at the zero divisors, so the operator picture preserves the absence of division. The adjoint of the plain multiplication is $L_{\bar Q_\pm}$ for the definite form and $L_{Q_\pm^{*}}$ for the indefinite one; the operator image $\{L_{Q_\pm}\}$ is a maximal commutative subalgebra of complex dimension two, the right multiplications coincide with the left ones for the plain product, and every sandwich operator is a left multiplication.

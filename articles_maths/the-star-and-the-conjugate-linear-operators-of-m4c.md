# __The Star and the Conjugate-Linear Operators of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$__

## Introduction

The biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$ carries three involutions, and only one of them is a matrix. The coefficient conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$ and the star $*=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$ are antilinear; the quaternion conjugation ${}^{\natural}$ is linear. The algebra $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ is the complex-linear endomorphism algebra $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, so an antilinear map is **not an element of it**. The star is an operation on the element $b$, not a fourth biquaternion unit, and the two-sided basis $T_{\mu\nu}$ does not contain it.

The distinction is not pedantic. The odd sector of the Clifford layer is carried by operations, and the operation that generates it is the coefficient conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, not the star. The bar and the star differ by the natural conjugation, which is even; the bar is the minimal antilinear generator, and the star is the involution that pairs with the Hermitian form. This article fixes the three maps, computes their matrices, gives the two conjugate-linear families, and displays the two Clifford dictionaries — the real-linear one generated by the bar and the complex-linear one of the coefficient frame — with their exact relation.

The article belongs to the $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ category and uses its conventions throughout. The three involutions are those of *Conventions in Mathematics*; the Clifford frame, its five gammas and the two-sided basis are those of *The Gamma Frame and the Complexified Dirac Algebra of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$* and *The Two-Sided Operators Method to Build $M_4(\mathbb{C})$*. The Hermitian structure and the trace form are *The Involution and the Hermitian Structure of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$*. The real forms and the antilinear automorphisms are *The Quaternionic Structure and the Real Forms of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$*. The sesquilinear sandwich is *The Sesquilinear Sandwich on the Biquaternion*.

## The Three Involutions

**The element.** Write
$$
b=b_0\,e_t+b_1\,e_x+b_2\,e_y+b_3\,e_z,\qquad b_\mu\in\mathbb{C},
$$
with $e_t=1$ and $e_x^2=e_y^2=e_z^2=-1$, $e_xe_y=e_z$, $e_ye_z=e_x$, $e_ze_x=e_y$.

**The coefficient conjugation.** The **bar** is
$$
\bar b=\bar b_0\,e_t+\bar b_1\,e_x+\bar b_2\,e_y+\bar b_3\,e_z .
$$
It is $\mathbb{R}$-linear and $\mathbb{C}$-antilinear, involutive, and multiplicative:
$$
\overline{ab}=\bar a\,\bar b .
$$
It fixes the real quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and anti-fixes its imaginary multiple $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$.

**The quaternion conjugation.** The **natural** conjugation is
$$
b^{\natural}=b_0\,e_t-b_1\,e_x-b_2\,e_y-b_3\,e_z .
$$
It is $\mathbb{C}$-linear and involutive, and it is an antiautomorphism:
$$
(ab)^{\natural}=b^{\natural}a^{\natural}.
$$
It fixes the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_t$ and anti-fixes the vector subspace $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})=\mathbb{C}e_x\oplus\mathbb{C}e_y\oplus\mathbb{C}e_z$.

**The star.** The **star** is the composite
$$
b^{*}=\overline{b^{\natural}}=\bar b_0\,e_t-\bar b_1\,e_x-\bar b_2\,e_y-\bar b_3\,e_z .
$$
It is $\mathbb{C}$-antilinear, involutive, and an antiautomorphism:
$$
(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}.
$$
It fixes the Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ and anti-fixes the anti-Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_-$.

**The table.**

| map | linearity | multiplicativity | fixed | anti-fixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bar $\bar{\cdot}$ | antilinear | automorphism | $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ | $i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ |
| natural ${}^{\natural}$ | linear | antiautomorphism | $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ | $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ |
| star $*$ | antilinear | antiautomorphism | $\mathbb{M}_+$ | $\mathbb{M}_-$ |

**The central imaginary.** The three maps act on $i$ as
$$
\bar i=-i,\qquad i^{\natural}=i,\qquad i^{*}=-i .
$$
The bar and the star conjugate the complex scalar; the natural conjugation does not. This is why the bar, and not the natural conjugation, is the antilinear generator of the Clifford action.

**The composite.** The star is the composite in either order,
$$
*=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}={}^{\natural}\circ\bar{\cdot},
$$
because the bar acts on the coefficients and the natural conjugation acts on the units, and the two act on independent labels. The bar is not the star: they differ by the even linear map ${}^{\natural}$,
$$
*={}^{\natural}\circ\bar{\cdot}.
$$

## The Matrices

**The real basis.** Read $\mathbb{B}$ as the real vector space $\mathbb{R}^8$ with the basis
$$
(1,i,\ e_x,i e_x,\ e_y,i e_y,\ e_z,i e_z).
$$
In this basis every real-linear map is an $8\times8$ real matrix.

**The bar.** The coefficient conjugation acts on each pair $(1,i)$ by $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$:
$$
J=\operatorname{diag}(J_2,J_2,J_2,J_2),\qquad J_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{tr}J=0,\qquad \det J=1,\qquad J^2=I_8 .
$$
The eigenvalues are $+1$ with multiplicity four (the real quaternion subspace) and $-1$ with multiplicity four (its imaginary multiple).

**The natural conjugation.** In the complex coefficient basis $(e_t,e_x,e_y,e_z)$ it is the diagonal matrix
$$
D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).
$$
In the real basis it is
$$
D_8=\operatorname{diag}(I_2,-I_2,-I_2,-I_2),
$$
because it multiplies each real coefficient by $+1$ or $-1$ and leaves $i$ fixed. Hence
$$
\operatorname{tr}D=-2,\qquad \det D=1,\qquad D^2=I_4 .
$$
As a real map, $\operatorname{tr}D_8=-4$ and $\det D_8=1$. The eigenvalues are $+1$ on the scalar and $-1$ on the three vector directions.

**The star.** The star is the composite $D\circ J$ in the real basis, and $D\circ\bar{\cdot}$ in the complex description. Its real matrix is block diagonal,
$$
S=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\right),
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{tr}S=0,\qquad \det S=1,\qquad S^2=I_8 .
$$
The eigenvalues are $+1$ with multiplicity four (the Hermitian subspace) and $-1$ with multiplicity four (the anti-Hermitian subspace).

**There is no complex matrix of the bar.** Suppose a $4\times4$ complex matrix $K$ satisfied $Kb=\bar b$ for every $b$. Applying it to $ib$ gives $K(ib)=iKb=i\bar b$, while $\overline{ib}=-i\bar b$. Hence $i\bar b=-i\bar b$, so $\bar b=0$ for every $b$, a contradiction. The bar is antilinear and has no matrix in $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$. The same argument applies to the star. The correct description of an antilinear map is a pair: a linear map together with the fixed conjugation,
$$
\bar{\cdot}=J_{\mathrm{real}},\qquad * = D\circ\bar{\cdot}.
$$

**The natural conjugation in the two-sided basis.** The linear map $D$ is a genuine element of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$, and it has a short expansion in the two-sided basis:
$$
\boxed{\;{}^{\natural}=-\tfrac12\bigl(T_{tt}+T_{xx}+T_{yy}+T_{zz}\bigr)\;}
$$
where $T_{\mu\nu}=L_\mu R_\nu$ and $T_{tt}=I$.

**Proof.** The four matrices are diagonal in the basis $(e_t,e_x,e_y,e_z)$. The matrix $T_{tt}=I$ is the identity. The matrix $T_{xx}=L_xR_x$ is conjugation by $e_x$: on $e_t$ it gives $e_xe_te_x=e_x^2=-e_t$, on $e_x$ it gives $e_x^3=-e_x$, and on $e_y,e_z$ it gives $+e_y,+e_z$. Hence $T_{xx}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1,1)$. Similarly $T_{yy}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1,1)$ and $T_{zz}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,-1)$. The sum is $\operatorname{diag}(-2,2,2,2)$, and $-\tfrac12$ times it is $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)=D$. $\square$

**Consequence.** The star has the expansion
$$
b^{*}=-\tfrac12\bigl(T_{tt}+T_{xx}+T_{yy}+T_{zz}\bigr)(\bar b).
$$
The star is therefore **not** in the complex span of the $T_{\mu\nu}$: it is the linear operator $D$ applied after the fixed coefficient conjugation. The phrase "the matrix of the star in the two-sided basis" has this precise meaning: the star has no $4\times4$ complex matrix, its linear part has the four-term expansion above, and the conjugation is the fixed real structure.

**The real decomposition.** The real endomorphism algebra splits as a direct sum of real vector spaces
$$
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{B})
=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\oplus\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})\circ\bar{\cdot},
$$
of two copies of real dimension $32$. The first summand is the complex-linear algebra spanned by the $T_{\mu\nu}$; the second is the antilinear half. The bar and the star lie in the second summand. This is the structural reason the star cannot be a matrix in the two-sided basis.

## The Conjugate-Linear Operators

**The two families.** For $b\in\mathbb{B}$ define
$$
C_b=\bar{\cdot}\circ L_b,\qquad C'_b=L_b\circ\bar{\cdot},
$$
that is $C_b(x)=\overline{bx}$ and $C'_b(x)=b\bar x$. Both are conjugate-linear. The bar is multiplicative, so
$$
C_b(x)=\bar b\,\bar x=L_{\bar b}\bigl(\bar x\bigr),
\qquad
C_b=L_{\bar b}\circ\bar{\cdot}.
$$
Similarly
$$
C'_b=\bar{\cdot}\circ L_b=L_b\circ\bar{\cdot}.
$$

**The product law.** The bar is an **automorphism**, so
$$
\bar{\cdot}\circ L_a\circ\bar{\cdot}=L_{\bar a},
\qquad
\bar{\cdot}\circ R_a\circ\bar{\cdot}=R_{\bar a}.
$$
Hence
$$
C_aC_b=L_{\bar a}L_b=L_{\bar a\,b},
\qquad
C'_aC'_b=L_aL_{\bar b}=L_{a\bar b},
$$
and the mixed products are also left multiplications:
$$
C_aC'_b=L_{\bar a}L_{\bar b}=L_{\bar a\bar b}=L_{\overline{ab}},
\qquad
C'_aC_b=L_aL_b=L_{ab}.
$$

**The table.**

| product | value | type |
|---|---|---|
| $C_aC_b$ | $L_{\bar a\,b}$ | left multiplication, linear |
| $C'_aC'_b$ | $L_{a\bar b}$ | left multiplication, linear |
| $C_aC'_b$ | $L_{\overline{ab}}$ | left multiplication, linear |
| $C'_aC_b$ | $L_{ab}$ | left multiplication, linear |

**The reading.** The conjugate-linear operators form a copy of $\mathbb{B}$: the map $b\mapsto C_b$ is conjugate-linear, and the product of two of them is a **left multiplication**, an even operation. The odd sector is therefore not an algebra of elements but an algebra of operations: the conjugate-linear family is the odd slot, and its square lands in the even left factor, exactly as the Clifford grading requires. The bar is an automorphism, so its conjugation preserves the left regular action; this is why the products stay one-sided.

**The star version.** The left family is multiplied by the even map $D$; the right family becomes the star composed with a right multiplication:
$$
S_b=*\circ L_b=D\circ C_b,\qquad S'_b=L_b\circ *=*\circ R_{b^{*}} .
$$
The star is an **antiautomorphism**, and its conjugation reverses the left action:
$$
*\circ L_a\circ *=R_{a^{*}} .
$$
Hence the star products are two-sided:
$$
S_aS_b=T_{b,a^{*}},\qquad S'_aS'_b=T_{a,b^{*}} .
$$
This is the precise structural difference between the two involutions. The bar gives left multiplications and is the minimal odd generator of the real Clifford action; the star gives two-sided operators and is the involution of the Hermitian structure. The two differ by the natural conjugation, which is even and therefore does not change the parity of the operator, but does change the side of the product.

## The Real-Linear Clifford Dictionary

**Theorem (the bar dictionary).** Put
$$
\gamma^0=\bar{\cdot},\qquad
\gamma^k=\bar{\cdot}\circ L_{-i\vec e_k}\quad(k=1,2,3),
\qquad
\gamma^5=R_{-i\vec e_z}.
$$
Then
$$
(\gamma^0)^2=I,\qquad (\gamma^k)^2=-I,\qquad
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2g^{\mu\nu}I,
$$
with $g=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and
$$
\{\gamma^5,\gamma^\mu\}=0,\qquad (\gamma^5)^2=I .
$$
The operators $\gamma^0,\gamma^k$ are conjugate-linear; $\gamma^5$ is linear. They act on $\mathbb{B}$ as a real eight-dimensional Clifford module.

**Proof.** The square of the bar is the identity. For the spacelike gammas, use the fact that the bar is an **automorphism**, so $\bar{\cdot}\circ L_a\circ\bar{\cdot}=L_{\bar a}$:
$$
(\gamma^k)^2
=\bar{\cdot}L_{-i e_k}\bar{\cdot}L_{-i e_k}
=L_{\overline{-i e_k}}L_{-i e_k}
=L_{i e_k}L_{-i e_k}
=L_{e_k^2}
=L_{-1}=-I .
$$
For $j\neq k$,
$$
\gamma^j\gamma^k+\gamma^k\gamma^j
=L_{e_je_k}+L_{e_ke_j}
=L_{e_je_k+e_ke_j}=0,
$$
because $e_j,e_k$ anticommute. The mixed anticommutator with $\gamma^0$ is
$$
\bar{\cdot}\bigl((-i e_k)\bar x\bigr)+(-i e_k)\bar{\cdot}\bar x
=\overline{(-i e_k)\bar x}+(-i e_k)x
=i e_kx-i e_kx=0 .
$$
Finally $\gamma^5$ is right multiplication by $-i e_z$, so its square is $R_{(-i e_z)^2}=R_{-e_z^2}=I$, and it anticommutes with each $\gamma^\mu$ because $L$ and $R$ commute and the multipliers anticommute up to the scalar. $\square$

**The volume element.** In this dictionary the product of the four gammas is central:
$$
\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3=iI .
$$
The volume element of the real Clifford module is the central imaginary $i$, not an independent chirality. The chirality $\gamma^5$ is a separate right multiplication by $-i e_z$; it is the grading of the Dirac module, not the product of the four gammas. This is the exact sense in which the real module has a volume element and a chirality that are different operators.

**The even part.** The even products of the gammas are left multiplications:
$$
\gamma^0\gamma^k=\bar{\cdot}\,\bar{\cdot}L_{-i e_k}=L_{-i e_k},
\qquad
\gamma^j\gamma^k=L_{e_j e_k},
\qquad
\gamma^5\gamma^\mu=-\gamma^\mu\gamma^5 .
$$
The algebra they generate is the left regular image
$$
L(\mathbb{B})=\{L_b:b\in\mathbb{B}\}\cong\mathbb{B},
$$
of real dimension eight. The odd part is the conjugate-linear family $\bar{\cdot}L(\mathbb{B})$. The real Clifford algebra is
$$
\mathrm{Cl}(1,3)\cong L(\mathbb{B})\oplus\bar{\cdot}L(\mathbb{B}),
$$
and the carrier remains $\mathbb{B}$.

**The chirality action.** The operator $\gamma^5=R_{-i e_z}$ commutes with every left multiplication and anticommutes with every $\gamma^\mu$. Its eigenspaces are the two chiral halves of the bimodule; on the left regular module it is a right multiplication, which is the algebraic reason the chirality is not in the left factor.

## The Complex-Linear Dictionary

**Theorem (the coefficient-frame dictionary).** In the same algebra,
$$
\gamma^0=R_{i e_x},\qquad
\gamma^k=L_{i e_k}R_{e_y}\quad(k=1,2,3),
\qquad
\gamma^5=R_{-i e_z}
$$
satisfy the same Clifford relations, with $\gamma^5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$. These operators are $\mathbb{C}$-linear, and they are the five elements
$$
\gamma^0=iT_{tx},\qquad
\gamma^k=iT_{ky},\qquad
\gamma^5=-iT_{tz}
$$
of the two-sided basis.

**Proof.** The relations were checked in *The Gamma Frame and the Complexified Dirac Algebra of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$* and are reproduced here for the comparison. The square of $\gamma^0$ is $R_{(i e_x)^2}=R_{-e_x^2}=I$. The square of $\gamma^k$ is
$$
L_{i e_k}R_{e_y}L_{i e_k}R_{e_y}
=L_{-e_k^2}R_{e_y^2}
=L_{1}R_{-1}=-I .
$$
The anticommutators follow from the anticommutation of the quaternion units and the commutation of $L$ and $R$. The product identity is the definition of $\gamma^5$ in the coefficient frame. $\square$

**The even part.** The even products of this dictionary generate the **complexified** even Clifford algebra
$$
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(4,\mathbb{C})\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_2(\mathbb{C}),
$$
of complex dimension eight, not the four-dimensional left factor $L(\mathbb{B})$. The two even algebras are different subalgebras of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$: the real dictionary has the left regular image, the complex dictionary has the chiral block-diagonal image. This is the precise difference between the two dictionaries.

**The comparison.**

| feature | real-linear dictionary | complex-linear dictionary |
|---|---|---|
| $\gamma^0$ | $\bar{\cdot}$ (antilinear) | $R_{i e_x}$ (linear) |
| $\gamma^k$ | $\bar{\cdot}L_{-i e_k}$ (antilinear) | $L_{i e_k}R_{e_y}$ (linear) |
| $\gamma^5$ | $R_{-i e_z}$ (linear) | $R_{-i e_z}$ (linear) |
| $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ | $iI$ | $-i\gamma^5$ |
| even part | $L(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}$ | $M_2\oplus M_2$ |
| odd part | $\bar{\cdot}L(\mathbb{B})$ | the off-diagonal block |
| carrier | $\mathbb{B}$ as a real $8$-module | $\mathbb{B}$ as a complex $4$-module |

**The choice.** The two dictionaries are two realizations of $\mathrm{Cl}(1,3)$ on the same eight-real-dimensional carrier. The real one uses the coefficient conjugation as the odd generator; the complex one uses the right and left multiplications. They are not equal as operators, and neither is a relabelling of the other: the first is antilinear, the second is linear. The choice between them is the choice of which complex structure is declared to be the scalar $i$ of the module. The real dictionary declares the central $i$ of $\mathbb{B}$ to be the volume element $iI$; the complex dictionary declares it to be $-\omega=i\gamma^5$, the negative of the volume element. The physics uses the complex dictionary for the $4\times4$ matrices and the real dictionary for the operation-level statement that the odd sector is the bar and a left multiplication.

## The Star and the Adjoint

**The Dirac adjoint.** The Dirac adjoint of a spinor is $\bar\psi=\psi^{*}\gamma^0$. In the operator dictionary the two factors are the star (the algebra involution on the spinor) and the gamma (the odd operation). The star is not the gamma; the gamma is the bar, or the right multiplication, depending on the dictionary. The composite is the adjoint.

**The Hermitian form.** The Hermitian form on $\mathbb{B}$ is
$$
\langle x,y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(x^{*}y),
$$
and the star is its involution. The bar is the coefficient conjugation that defines the real structure, not the Hermitian adjoint. The two agree on the real quaternion slice and nowhere else. This is the distinction recorded in *Conventions in Mathematics* between the bar, the natural conjugation and the star.

**The place of the star.** The star is the involution that makes $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ a $*$-algebra and $\mathbb{B}$ a Hermitian algebra. It is an operation on elements, not an element; it is antilinear, not a matrix; it lies in the antilinear half of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{B})$. The Clifford odd sector is generated by the bar, which is the coefficient part of the star. The star is the composite of the bar with the even natural conjugation. The decisive difference is that the bar is an automorphism and the star is an antiautomorphism: the bar preserves the left action, so its conjugate-linear operators square to left multiplications and satisfy the Clifford relations; the star reverses the left action, so its conjugate-linear operators square to two-sided operators and fail the spacelike square. The even factor $D$ is not the cause; the side of the product is.

## Summary

The biquaternion algebra carries three involutions: the bar (antilinear automorphism, fixed space $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$), the natural conjugation (linear antiautomorphism, fixed space $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$) and the star (antilinear antiautomorphism, fixed space $\mathbb{M}_+$). The star is the composite of the bar and the natural conjugation. In the real basis the bar is $J=\operatorname{diag}(J_2,J_2,J_2,J_2)$ and the star is $S=D_8J$; both have trace zero and determinant one. In the complex coefficient basis the natural conjugation is $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and it has the two-sided expansion
$$
{}^{\natural}=-\tfrac12(T_{tt}+T_{xx}+T_{yy}+T_{zz}).
$$
The star is therefore the antilinear map $b\mapsto D\bar b$; it has no $4\times4$ complex matrix, and it lies in the antilinear half of the real endomorphism algebra.

The conjugate-linear operators $C_b=\bar{\cdot}L_b$ and $C'_b=L_b\bar{\cdot}$ form a copy of $\mathbb{B}$; their products are the left multiplications $C_aC_b=L_{\bar a b}$ and $C'_aC'_b=L_{a\bar b}$, and the mixed products are the left multiplications $L_{\overline{ab}}$ and $L_{ab}$. The star version $S_b=D C_b$ is the same family multiplied by the even map $D$; its products are the two-sided operators $S_aS_b=T_{b,a^{*}}$ and $S'_aS'_b=T_{a,b^{*}}$, because the star is an antiautomorphism while the bar is an automorphism.

The real-linear Clifford dictionary is
$$
\gamma^0=\bar{\cdot},\qquad
\gamma^k=\bar{\cdot}L_{-i\vec e_k},\qquad
\gamma^5=R_{-i\vec e_z},
$$
with $(\gamma^0)^2=I$, $(\gamma^k)^2=-I$, $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2g^{\mu\nu}I$, and $\{\gamma^5,\gamma^\mu\}=0$. The product of the four gammas is $iI$; the even part is $L(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}$, and the odd part is the conjugate-linear family. The complex-linear dictionary is
$$
\gamma^0=R_{i e_x},\qquad
\gamma^k=L_{i e_k}R_{e_y},\qquad
\gamma^5=R_{-i e_z},
$$
with $\gamma^5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$, five elements of the two-sided basis, and the complexified even algebra $M_2\oplus M_2$. The two dictionaries are different realizations on the same carrier, one antilinear and one linear; the first is the operation-level form of the odd sector, the second is the coefficient-frame matrix form.

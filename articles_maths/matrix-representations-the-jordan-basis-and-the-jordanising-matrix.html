Matrix Representations : The Jordan Basis and the Jordanising Matrix

Introduction

A spectrally degenerate biquaternion has one eigenvalue and no eigenbasis. Off the scalar line its $2\times2$ matrix has a single Jordan block, its eigenspace is one-dimensional, and a second vector is supplied by a chain. The change to the Jordan basis is the Jordanising matrix. In the $4\times4$ regular model the same basis gives two Jordan blocks of size two, one in each spinor plane, and the Jordanising matrix has determinant fixed by the frame, independent of the element.

This article develops the Jordan basis, the canonical Jordanising matrix, the determinant of the change in the two displays, the canonical frame, the freedom, the defect and the transitions. It is the degenerate counterpart of Matrix Representations : The Eigenbasis and the Diagonalising Matrix.

Conventions. The algebra and the displays are those of Matrix Representations : The Models and the Change of Frame. The element is $\tilde Q=Q_te_t+\vec Q$ with $$ \vec Q^2=0,\qquad \vec Q\neq0. $$ Put $Q=\mathsf M_2(\vec Q)$, a rank-one nilpotent, and $M=\mathsf M_2(\tilde Q)=Q_t\mathrm I_2+Q$. The Hermitian form is $\langle X,Y\rangle_*=\operatorname{Sc}(XY^{*})$, with norm $$ \langle\vec Q\rangle_*=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\lvert Q_k\rvert^2>0, \qquad s=\sqrt{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*}=\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(Q^{\dagger}Q)}. $$ The Jordan block is $$ J_2(Q_t)=\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&1\\0&Q_t\end{pmatrix}. $$ The Peirce frame is the corpus order $\mathcal P=(\tilde\Pi_+,\vec\Upsilon_+,\vec\Upsilon_-,\tilde\Pi_-)$; the corner order is $\mathcal E=(\tilde\Pi_+,\vec\Upsilon_-,\vec\Upsilon_+,\tilde\Pi_-)$; the chain order is $\mathcal J=(\vec\Upsilon_-,\tilde\Pi_-,\tilde\Pi_+,\vec\Upsilon_+)$. The coefficient frame is $\mathcal B=(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$.

Boundaries. The flag, the corners and the canonical pair are The Jordan Basis of a Spectrally Degenerate Biquaternion; the spectral case is Matrix Representations : The Eigenbasis and the Diagonalising Matrix; the projectors are Matrix Representations : The Spectral Projectors; the centraliser is Matrix Representations : The Centraliser and the Double Centraliser; the nilpotent cone is Nilpotents.

Common Aspects

A spectrally degenerate biquaternion has one eigenvalue and no eigenbasis. Off the scalar line its entry matrix has a single Jordan block, its eigenspace is one-dimensional, and a second vector is supplied by a chain. In the regular model the same basis gives two Jordan blocks of size two, one in each spinor plane, and the Jordanising matrix has determinant fixed by the frame, independent of the element.

The basis. For a spectrally degenerate element off the scalar line, $\vec Q^2=0$ and $\vec Q\neq0$, and the Jordan basis is $$ \mathcal J(\tilde Q)=(\vec\Upsilon_-,\ \tilde\Pi_-,\ \tilde\Pi_+,\ \vec\Upsilon_+), \qquad \vec\Upsilon_-=\vec Q. $$ The first pair and the second pair are the two chains for the eigenvalue $Q_t$ under left multiplication. The same four elements in the corner order are the basis adapted to the coordinate model.

The table is the matrix-unit table with $P_{11}=\tilde\Pi_+$, $P_{12}=\vec\Upsilon_-$, $P_{21}=\vec\Upsilon_+$ and $P_{22}=\tilde\Pi_-$. Every element has a unique expansion $$ \tilde X=X_{11}\tilde\Pi_++X_{12}\vec\Upsilon_-+X_{21}\vec\Upsilon_++X_{22}\tilde\Pi_-, \qquad \mathsf M_2^{\tilde Q}(\tilde X)=\begin{pmatrix}X_{11}&X_{12}\\X_{21}&X_{22}\end{pmatrix}, $$ and for the element itself $\mathsf M_2^{\tilde Q}(\tilde Q)=J_2(Q_t)=\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&1\\0&Q_t\end{pmatrix}$.

The centraliser. The matrices commuting with $J_2(Q_t)$ are the upper triangular Toeplitz matrices $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&a\end{pmatrix}$. The centraliser of $\tilde Q$ is therefore $\mathbb{C}[\tilde Q]=\{ae_t+b\vec Q\}$, the dual numbers, of complex dimension two and with only the idempotents $0$ and $e_t$.

The canonical frame. The definite Hermitian form selects a canonical pair of Hermitian idempotents: $$ \tilde\Pi_+=\frac{\vec Q\vec Q^{*}}{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*},\qquad \tilde\Pi_-=\frac{\vec Q^{*}\vec Q}{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*},\qquad \vec\Upsilon_+=\frac{\vec Q^{*}}{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*}, \qquad s=\sqrt{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*}=\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(Q^{\dagger}Q)}. $$ The pair is orthogonal and complete, and $$ \tilde\Pi_+\vec Q=\vec Q,\qquad \vec Q\tilde\Pi_-=\vec Q,\qquad \vec Q\vec\Upsilon_+=\tilde\Pi_+,\qquad \vec\Upsilon_+\vec Q=\tilde\Pi_-. $$ The pair of Hermitian idempotents is unique. The generator $\vec\Upsilon_-=\vec Q$ is fixed by the element, and the product $\vec\Upsilon_-\vec\Upsilon_+=\tilde\Pi_+$ fixes $\vec\Upsilon_+$ in the corner. The only residual freedom of the canonical matrix is a common phase, which leaves the frame fixed. At $\vec Q=0$ the formulas divide by zero: the element is scalar, every basis is an eigenbasis, the Jordan type is two blocks $J_1(Q_t)$, and no adapted frame is distinguished.

The defect. A non-scalar element of the sector is never normal. Normality is $[\vec Q,\overline{\vec Q}]=0$, which for a vector part means $\vec Q=\zeta\vec p$ with $\zeta\in\mathbb{C}$ and $\vec p\in\mathbb{R}^3$; then $\vec Q^2=\zeta^2(\vec p\cdot\vec p)=0$, so $\zeta=0$ or $\vec p=0$, and in both cases $\vec Q=0$. In a $*$-unitary basis, $$ U^{\dagger}\mathsf M_4^{L}(\tilde Q)U =\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&s\\0&Q_t\end{pmatrix}\otimes\mathrm I_2. $$ This is two copies of the defective Jordan block. The defect is the same $s$ in both blocks, and it is the norm of the nilpotent. The canonical change is $*$-unitary exactly when $s=1$, a normalisation of the element, not a generic property. For a real quaternion, $\vec Q\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\vec Q^2=0$ force $\vec Q=0$: the defective sector has no real points except the real scalars.

Transitions. If $\tilde Q'=\tilde Q+be_t$, the flag and the frame are unchanged, and $\mathsf M_2^{\tilde Q}(\tilde Q')=J_2(Q_t+b)$ with the same Jordanising matrix. If $\tilde Q'=a\tilde Q$ with $a\neq0$, the canonical frame transforms by $\tilde\Pi_\pm'=\tilde\Pi_\pm$, $\vec\Upsilon_-'=a\vec\Upsilon_-$, $\vec\Upsilon_+'=a^{-1}\vec\Upsilon_+$, and the coordinate model by $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,a)$; the Jordanising matrix becomes $\mathsf S_{Q'}=\mathsf S_QD^{-1}$ up to a phase. For two elements with different vector lines, $T_{Q'Q}=\mathsf S_Q^{-1}\mathsf S_{Q'}$ satisfies the cocycle $T_{Q''Q}T_{Q'Q''}=T_{Q'Q}$; it preserves the block structure only when the vector parts are proportional. Two non-scalar elements commute exactly when their vector parts are proportional, because the centraliser is $\mathbb{C}[\tilde Q]$.

The Coefficient Frame

The change. In the coefficient frame the Jordanising matrix is the coordinate matrix of the Jordan basis in the coefficient basis. Its columns are the four elements in the corner order, and $$ \det\mathsf S_Q^{(4)}=\tfrac14 $$ for every element of the sector off the scalar line. The proof splits the change into the conjugation by $\mathsf S_Q$, of determinant one, and the fixed change from the matrix-unit basis to the coefficient basis. The latter is the Peirce matrix $$ T=\frac12 \begin{pmatrix} 1&0&0&1\\ 0&i&i&0\\ 0&-1&1&0\\ i&0&0&-i \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det T=\tfrac14, $$ whose columns are the four Peirce elements in the corner order. The entry scalars cancel between $\mathsf S_Q$ and $\mathsf S_Q^{-1}$.

The chain order. In the chain order the columns are permuted from $(P_{11},P_{12},P_{21},P_{22})$ to $(P_{12},P_{22},P_{11},P_{21})$. The permutation has determinant $-1$, so the determinant becomes $-\tfrac14$ in the coefficient frame. The value $\tfrac14$ is attached to the corner order, not to the element.

The change law. The coefficient-frame change sends the coefficient matrix to $$ \operatorname{diag}\bigl(J_2(Q_t),J_2(Q_t)\bigr) $$ in the chain order, and to $J_2(Q_t)\otimes\mathrm I_2$ in the corner order. The two forms differ by the permutation that orders the two chains. The coefficient-frame matrix of the element is not in Jordan form until this change is applied.

The Entry Model

The canonical matrix. Let $u$ be a unit vector spanning the image of $Q$, and let $v$ be a unit vector orthogonal to $u$. Then $U=(u\ v)$ is $*$-unitary, and $$ Qu=0,\qquad Qv=su,\qquad s=\sqrt{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*}. $$ The first relation holds because $Q^2=0$ and the image is the kernel; the second defines $s$, the size of the nilpotent. Put $$ \mathsf S_Q=\bigl(u\ \ s^{-1}v\bigr). $$ It is Jordanising: its first column is annihilated by $Q$ and its second is a preimage of the first. The two choices of $u$ differ by a phase, and the two choices of $v$ by a phase; the resulting $\mathsf S_Q$ differs by the same phase, so there is no second chart.

The theorem. The matrix is Jordanising, and $$ \mathsf M_2(\tilde\Pi_+)=uu^{\dagger},\qquad \mathsf M_2(\vec\Upsilon_-)=Q,\qquad \mathsf M_2(\vec\Upsilon_+)=s^{-1}vu^{\dagger},\qquad \mathsf M_2(\tilde\Pi_-)=vv^{\dagger}. $$ The columns are a chain: $Qu=0$ and $Q(s^{-1}v)=u$, so $Q\mathsf S_Q=\mathsf S_QE_{12}$ and $M\mathsf S_Q=\mathsf S_QJ_2(Q_t)$.

The determinant and the defect. Since $u$ and $v$ are orthonormal, $$ \mathsf S_Q^{\dagger}\mathsf S_Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,s^{-2}), \qquad \lvert\det\mathsf S_Q\rvert=\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*}}. $$ The modulus is invariant; the phase is not, because a common phase of $u$ and $v$ multiplies $\mathsf S_Q$ by that phase. Put $U=(u\ v)$. Since $U$ is $*$-unitary and $U^{\dagger}QU=sE_{12}$, $$ U^{\dagger}MU=\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&s\\0&Q_t\end{pmatrix}. $$ The superdiagonal $s$ is the size of the nilpotent. No $*$-unitary change removes it: a $*$-unitary diagonalisation would make $M$ normal, and a normal matrix with the single eigenvalue $Q_t$ is $Q_t\mathrm I_2$. The canonical matrix is $\mathsf S_Q=U\operatorname{diag}(1,s^{-1})$, the product of the $*$-unitary frame and the real scaling that normalises the superdiagonal to one.

The freedom. If $\mathsf S$ is one Jordanising matrix, the others are $\mathsf S\mathsf G$ with $$ \mathsf G=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\0&\alpha\end{pmatrix}, \quad \alpha\neq0, $$ a group isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\ltimes\mathbb{C}$, of complex dimension two. The parameter $\alpha$ is a global scale and $\beta$ a chain shift: with $b=-\beta/\alpha$, the frame becomes $\tilde\Pi_+(b)=\tilde\Pi_++b\vec Q$, $\tilde\Pi_-(b)=\tilde\Pi_--b\vec Q$, $\vec\Upsilon_-(b)=\vec Q$, the affine family of adapted pairs, with the canonical Hermitian pair at $b=0$. Since $\det\mathsf G=\alpha^2$, the determinant is multiplied by $\alpha^2$; the modulus $s^{-1}$ is attached to the canonical representative, and the shift does not change it.

The Peirce Frame

The explicit form. The regular Jordanising matrix $\mathsf S_Q^{(4)}$ has columns the coordinate columns of $P_{11},P_{12},P_{21},P_{22}$ in the Peirce frame, in the corner order. By construction, $$ \bigl(\mathsf S_Q^{(4)}\bigr)^{-1}\mathsf M_4^{L}(\tilde Q)\,\mathsf S_Q^{(4)} =J_2(Q_t)\otimes\mathrm I_2 =\begin{pmatrix} Q_t&0&1&0\\ 0&Q_t&0&1\\ 0&0&Q_t&0\\ 0&0&0&Q_t \end{pmatrix}. $$ In the chain order the same matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(J_2(Q_t),J_2(Q_t))$; the two forms differ by the permutation that orders the two chains.

The determinant. In the Peirce frame $\det\mathsf S_Q^{(4)}=-1$ for every element, and in the coefficient frame it is $\tfrac14$ in the corner order, because the coefficient display is the Peirce display changed by $C_{\Pi}$ and $\det(C_{\Pi}\mathsf S_Q^{(4)})=(-\tfrac14)(-1)=\tfrac14$. In the chain order the permutation has determinant $-1$, so the values become $+1$ in the Peirce frame and $-\tfrac14$ in the coefficient frame. The determinant is fixed by the frame and independent of the element; the entry scalars cancel.

The commutant. In the corner order the regular matrix is $$ \mathsf N=J_2(Q_t)\otimes\mathrm I_2=Q_t\mathrm I_4+E_{12}\otimes\mathrm I_2. $$ A matrix gives the same Jordan form exactly when it commutes with $\mathsf N$. The commutant is $$ \left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\0&A\end{pmatrix}:\ A,B\in M_2(\mathbb{C})\right\} =\mathbb{C}[E_{12}]\otimes M_2(\mathbb{C}), $$ of complex dimension eight, with invertible part $GL_2(\mathbb{C})\ltimes M_2(\mathbb{C})$. The $A$ part mixes the two spinor planes and the $B$ part shifts the two chains. The entry freedom is the image of the Toeplitz group under $G\mapsto G\otimes G^{-\mathsf T}$, isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\ltimes\mathbb{C}$; the remaining six parameters mix the planes. A matrix $\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\0&A\end{pmatrix}$ is $*$-unitary exactly when $B=0$ and $A\in U(2)$: the two planes cannot be rotated independently, only their common $*$-unitary mixing survives.

The defect. In a $*$-unitary basis, $$ U^{\dagger}\mathsf M_4^{L}(\tilde Q)U =\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&s\\0&Q_t\end{pmatrix}\otimes\mathrm I_2, $$ two copies of the defective Jordan block with the same $s$. The regular defect is the entry defect doubled, and the canonical change is $*$-unitary exactly when $s=1$.

Summary

The Jordan basis $\mathcal J=(\vec\Upsilon_-,\tilde\Pi_-,\tilde\Pi_+,\vec\Upsilon_+)$ is a system of matrix units; in the corner order the element is $J_2(Q_t)$. The canonical Jordanising matrix is $\mathsf S_Q=(u\ s^{-1}v)$, with $u$ a unit vector spanning the image and $v$ a unit vector orthogonal to it, and $s=\sqrt{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*}$; it satisfies $\mathsf S_Q^{-1}M\mathsf S_Q=J_2(Q_t)$ and $\lvert\det\mathsf S_Q\rvert=s^{-1}$. In the regular model the same basis gives $J_2(Q_t)\otimes\mathrm I_2$ in the corner order and $\operatorname{diag}(J_2,J_2)$ in the chain order. The determinant of the regular change is $\tfrac14$ in the coefficient frame and $-1$ in the Peirce frame, independent of the element, because the $2\times2$ scalars cancel in the similarity; the chain order flips both signs. The freedom is $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\ltimes\mathbb{C}$ in $2\times2$ and $GL_2(\mathbb{C})\ltimes M_2(\mathbb{C})$ in $4\times4$, with $*$-unitary subgroup $U(2)$. No non-scalar element is normal, the best $*$-unitary form is the defective Jordan block doubled, and the defect is $s$.

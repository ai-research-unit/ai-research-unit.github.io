The Hénon Maps

Introduction

The Hénon family is the simplest family of polynomial automorphisms of $\mathbb{C}^2$ whose dynamics is not affine: $$ H_{a,c}(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})=\bigl(Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c-aQ_{\blacktriangledown},\ Q_{\blacktriangle}\bigr),\qquad a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times},\ c\in\mathbb{C}. $$ The map has degree two and constant Jacobian determinant $a$; it is a polynomial automorphism for every $a\neq0$, with inverse $$ H_{a,c}^{-1}(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})=\left(Q_{\blacktriangledown},\ \frac{Q_{\blacktriangledown}^2+c-Q_{\blacktriangle}}{a}\right). $$ The family is the $\mathbb{C}^2$ analogue of the quadratic family $Q_{\blacktriangle}\mapsto Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c$: the second coordinate stores the previous value of the first, and the parameter $a$ couples the two coordinates. For $a=0$ the map degenerates to $(Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c,Q_{\blacktriangle})$, whose dynamics is that of the quadratic map; for $a\neq0$ the coupling makes the map invertible and produces the horseshoe, the fractal Julia sets and the connectedness locus.

This article treats the family and its inverse, the Jacobian and the two regimes of contraction and expansion, the horseshoe for large $\lvert a\rvert$, the degree growth and the entropy, the Julia sets, the connectedness locus, and the quadratic family as the limit $a\to0$. The polynomial automorphisms are The Affine and Polynomial Automorphisms of $\mathbb{C}^2$; the degree and the dynamical degree are the ones of that article; the topology of $\mathbb{C}^2$ is The Topology of $\mathbb{C}^2$.

The Hénon map is the degree-two polynomial automorphism, and its iteration is the model of a nonuniformly hyperbolic holomorphic dynamical system in two variables. The degree doubles at each iteration, the entropy is $\log 2$, and the Julia set is a fractal subset of $\mathbb{C}^2$ whose structure depends on the parameter $a$ and on the connectedness of the orbits.

The Family and Its Inverse

The Hénon map is a polynomial automorphism of degree two with inverse of degree two; its second coordinate is the previous first coordinate, and the parameter $a$ is the Jacobian.

Definition. The Hénon map with parameters $a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ and $c\in\mathbb{C}$ is $$ H_{a,c}(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})=\bigl(Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c-aQ_{\blacktriangledown},\ Q_{\blacktriangle}\bigr). $$

Proposition (the inverse). The map $H_{a,c}$ is a polynomial automorphism of $\mathbb{C}^2$, and its inverse is $$ H_{a,c}^{-1}(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})=\left(Q_{\blacktriangledown},\ \frac{Q_{\blacktriangledown}^2+c-Q_{\blacktriangle}}{a}\right). $$

Proof. If $(Q'_{\blacktriangle},Q'_{\blacktriangledown})=H(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})$, then $Q'_{\blacktriangledown}=Q_{\blacktriangle}$ and $Q'_{\blacktriangle}=Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c-aQ_{\blacktriangledown}$, so $Q_{\blacktriangledown}=(Q'^2_{\blacktriangle}+c-Q'_{\blacktriangledown})/a$. The two maps are polynomial and inverse to each other.

Proposition (the degree). The map $H_{a,c}$ has degree $2$, and its inverse has degree $2$. The composition $H_{a,c}^n$ has degree $2^n$.

Proof. The first component has degree $2$ and the second degree $1$; the inverse has the same degrees. The degree of a composition is at most the product of the degrees, and for the Hénon map the top-degree term $Q_{\blacktriangle}^2$ is not cancelled by the composition, so the degree doubles at each step.

Remark (the real Hénon map). For $a,c\in\mathbb{R}$ the map preserves the real locus $\mathbb{R}^2\subset\mathbb{C}^2$, and its restriction is the classical real Hénon map. The complex Hénon map is the complexification of the real one, and the real dynamics is the intersection of the complex dynamics with $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Remark (the group generated). The iterates $H^n$ have degrees $2^n$, so they are all distinct; the cyclic group generated by $H$ is infinite. The Hénon map is a tame polynomial automorphism, by the Jung–van der Kulk theorem, but its dynamical behaviour is not tame at all.

The Jacobian and the Two Regimes

The Jacobian determinant is the constant $a$; the map contracts volume for $\lvert a\rvert<1$, preserves it for $\lvert a\rvert=1$, and expands it for $\lvert a\rvert>1$.

Proposition (the Jacobian). The complex Jacobian matrix of $H_{a,c}$ is $$ JH_{a,c}(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})=\begin{pmatrix}2Q_{\blacktriangle}&-a\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, $$ and its determinant is $$ \det JH_{a,c}=a. $$ The real Jacobian determinant is $\lvert a\rvert^2$.

Proof. The partial derivatives are direct; the determinant of the matrix is $a$. The real Jacobian determinant of a holomorphic map is the square of the modulus of the complex one, by Analysis on $\mathbb{C}^2$.

Proposition (the three regimes). For $\lvert a\rvert<1$ the map is dissipative: it contracts the volume by the factor $\lvert a\rvert^2$ at every point. For $\lvert a\rvert=1$ it is volume preserving, and for $a=1$ it preserves the holomorphic symplectic form $dQ_{\blacktriangle}\wedge dQ_{\blacktriangledown}$; for general $\lvert a\rvert=1$ it multiplies the form by the constant $a$. For $\lvert a\rvert>1$ it is expanding in volume.

Proof. The real Jacobian determinant is $\lvert a\rvert^2$, constant; the pullback of the volume form is $\lvert a\rvert^2$ times the volume form. The pullback of $dQ_{\blacktriangle}\wedge dQ_{\blacktriangledown}$ is $\det JH\cdot dQ_{\blacktriangle}\wedge dQ_{\blacktriangledown}=a\,dQ_{\blacktriangle}\wedge dQ_{\blacktriangledown}$, which is the form itself exactly when $a=1$.

Remark (the inverse and the regimes). The inverse has Jacobian determinant $a^{-1}$; the dissipation of $H$ is the expansion of $H^{-1}$, and the two directions of time are not symmetric unless $\lvert a\rvert=1$. The stable and unstable manifolds are the curves along which the forward and backward contractions occur.

Remark (the volume-preserving case). For $a=1$ the map is a complex symplectic automorphism of the affine plane, and for general $\lvert a\rvert=1$ it is symplectic up to the constant factor $a$; it is the discrete analogue of a Hamiltonian system. The conservative case is the boundary between the dissipative and the expanding regimes, and its dynamics is the most delicate of the three.

The Horseshoe

For large $\lvert a\rvert$ the Hénon map has a hyperbolic horseshoe: on a Cantor set it is topologically conjugate to the full shift on two symbols.

Theorem (the horseshoe). For $\lvert a\rvert$ sufficiently large and $c$ fixed, the non-wandering set of $H_{a,c}$ contains a compact invariant set $\Lambda$ on which $H_{a,c}$ is topologically conjugate to the full shift on two symbols. The set $\Lambda$ is a Cantor set, and the Julia set of $H_{a,c}$ is a Cantor set.

Proof. This is the theorem of Devaney and Nitecki for the real Hénon map, and its complex version: for large $\lvert a\rvert$ the map sends a suitable rectangle into a thin horseshoe, the two branches of which cross the rectangle in the two vertical strips; the invariant set is the intersection of the preimages, a Cantor set, and the coding by the two branches gives the conjugacy. The details are in the theory of hyperbolic dynamics.

Remark (the geometry of the horseshoe). The horseshoe is the image of a rectangle bent into a U-shape and placed back over itself, so that the two arms cross the original rectangle in two strips. The two strips are the two symbols, and the invariant set is the set of points whose orbit stays in the rectangle forever, coded by the sequence of strips.

Remark (the real picture). For the real Hénon map the horseshoe is visible in the plane; the two arms of the horseshoe are the two branches of the unstable manifold. In $\mathbb{C}^2$ the horseshoe sits in a real four-dimensional ambient space, and its intersection with $\mathbb{R}^2$ is the real horseshoe.

Remark (the Julia set in the horseshoe regime). For large $\lvert a\rvert$ the Julia set is a Cantor set, and the map is uniformly hyperbolic on it. The entropy is concentrated on the horseshoe, and the rest of the dynamics is the basin of infinity.

Degree Growth and Entropy

The degree of $H^n$ is $2^n$; the dynamical degree is $2$, and the topological entropy is $\log 2$.

Theorem (the degree growth). For every $a\neq0$ and $c$, $$ \deg H_{a,c}^n=2^n. $$

Proof. The degree of $H$ is $2$; the top-degree term of the first component of $H^n$ is the square of the top-degree term of the first component of $H^{n-1}$, and the linear term $-aw$ does not cancel it. Hence the degree doubles at each step.

Definition. The dynamical degree of a polynomial automorphism $F$ is $$ \lambda(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\deg F^n\bigr)^{1/n}. $$ For the Hénon map, $\lambda(H_{a,c})=2$.

Theorem (the entropy). The topological entropy of $H_{a,c}$ is $$ h_{\mathrm{top}}(H_{a,c})=\log 2. $$

Proof. For a polynomial automorphism of $\mathbb{C}^2$ the topological entropy is the logarithm of the dynamical degree, by the theorem of Bedford–Lyubich–Smillie; the dynamical degree is $2$ by the degree growth. The entropy is independent of $a$ and $c$.

Remark (the entropy and the horseshoe). The entropy $\log 2$ is the entropy of the full shift on two symbols, and it is realised on the horseshoe of the large-$\lvert a\rvert$ regime. The degree growth is the algebraic shadow of the horseshoe: the two branches of the horseshoe are the two preimages counted by the degree.

Remark (the entropy and the parameters). The topological entropy is constant in the family, but the measure-theoretic entropy and the Lyapunov exponents depend on the parameter and on the invariant measure. The equilibrium measure of the Hénon map has entropy $\log 2$ and Lyapunov exponents $\chi^+>0$ and $\chi^-<0$ with $\chi^++\chi^-=\log\lvert a\rvert$, the sum being the logarithm of the Jacobian.

The Julia Sets

The filled Julia sets $K^\pm$ are the sets of points with bounded forward and backward orbits; their boundaries are $J^\pm$, and the Julia set is $J=J^+\cap J^-$.

Definition. The filled Julia sets are $$ K^+=\{Q\in\mathbb{C}^2:\{H^n(Q)\}_{n\ge0}\text{ is bounded}\},\qquad K^-=\{Q\in\mathbb{C}^2:\{H^{-n}(Q)\}_{n\ge0}\text{ is bounded}\}, $$ and the Julia sets are $$ J^+=\partial K^+,\qquad J^-=\partial K^-,\qquad J=J^+\cap J^-. $$

Proposition (the basic properties). The sets $K^\pm$ are closed and invariant under $H^{\pm1}$; $J^\pm$ are invariant under $H$, and $J$ is invariant under $H$ and $H^{-1}$. The set $K^+$ is the set of points that do not escape to infinity under forward iteration, and $K^-$ is the set of points that do not escape under backward iteration.

Proof. The boundedness of the forward orbit is preserved by $H$ and by $H^{-1}$ on $K^+$ by definition; the boundary of an invariant set is invariant. The escape to infinity is the complement of $K^+$ for a polynomial map of degree at least two.

Theorem (the large-$\lvert a\rvert$ case). For $\lvert a\rvert$ sufficiently large, the Julia set $J=J^+\cap J^-$ is a Cantor set; it contains the horseshoe of the previous section.

Proof. In the horseshoe regime the map is uniformly hyperbolic on the non-wandering set; the horseshoe is a Cantor set contained in $J^+\cap J^-$, and the intersection is a Cantor set by the standard description of the Julia set in the strongly dissipative regime.

Remark (the dissipative case). For $\lvert a\rvert<1$ the map contracts volume, and $K^+$ is a compact invariant set whose structure depends on the parameter; it can have positive area or be a fractal. The Julia set $J$ is the intersection of the boundaries of the two filled Julia sets, and its Hausdorff dimension depends on the parameter and is computed from the Lyapunov exponents and the pressure formula.

Remark (the currents). The Green functions $$ G^\pm(Q)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2^n}\log^+\lVert H^{\pm n}(Q)\rVert $$ are plurisubharmonic, and their currents $T^\pm=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\bar\partial G^\pm$ are the Green currents. The equilibrium measure is $\mu=T^+\wedge T^-$, its support is the Julia set $J$, and its entropy is $\log 2$. The currents are the natural invariant objects of the complex dynamics, and the Julia set is the support of their intersection.

The Connectedness Locus

The connectedness locus is the set of parameters $(a,c)$ for which the Julia set is connected; it is the $\mathbb{C}^2$ analogue of the Mandelbrot set.

Definition. The connectedness locus of the Hénon family is $$ \mathcal{C}=\{(a,c)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}:J_{a,c}\text{ is connected}\}. $$

Proposition (the complement). The complement of $\mathcal{C}$ is the disconnected locus, the set of parameters for which $J_{a,c}$ is not connected. The connectedness locus is closed in the parameter space.

Proof. The Julia set depends continuously on the parameter in the Hausdorff topology on compact sets; the set of parameters with connected Julia set is closed. The disconnected locus is the complement by definition.

Theorem (the structure). The connectedness locus is a closed subset of the parameter space with nonempty interior, hence of real dimension $4$; its boundary is the bifurcation locus, the Hénon analogue of the boundary of the Mandelbrot set. For fixed $c$ and $\lvert a\rvert$ sufficiently large, the parameter is outside $\mathcal{C}$; for $a\to0$, $\mathcal{C}$ converges to the Mandelbrot set of the quadratic family.

Proof. The closedness is the continuity of the Julia set; the hyperbolic parameters, which have an attracting cycle and a connected Julia set, form a nonempty open subset. The boundary is the bifurcation locus, by definition the Hénon Mandelbrot set. The degeneration as $a\to0$ is the theorem of Hubbard–Oberste-Vorth on the Hénon map as a perturbation of the quadratic map. The precise formulation is in the theory of complex dynamics in several variables.

Remark (the Mandelbrot set as a slice). The quadratic family is the degenerate slice $a=0$ of the Hénon family, and the Mandelbrot set is the connectedness locus of that slice. The Hénon connectedness locus is a thickening of the Mandelbrot set in the $a$-direction, and its slices by fixed $a$ are the "Hénon Julia sets" of the parameter $c$.

Remark (the real slice). For real $a,c$ the connectedness locus intersects $\mathbb{R}^2$ in the real connectedness locus of the real Hénon map; the real slice is the set of parameters for which the real Julia set is connected, and it is the visible part of the complex picture.

The Quadratic Family as a Limit

As $a\to0$ the Hénon map degenerates to $(Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c,Q_{\blacktriangle})$, whose dynamics is the quadratic family; the Hénon family is a deformation of the quadratic family.

Proposition (the degenerate limit). For $a=0$ the map $$ H_{0,c}(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})=\bigl(Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c,\ Q_{\blacktriangle}\bigr) $$ is not invertible, and its dynamics is the quadratic dynamics of $Q_{\blacktriangle}\mapsto Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c$ on the first coordinate; the second coordinate is the previous value of the first.

Proof. The first coordinate evolves by $Q_{\blacktriangle,n+1}=Q_{\blacktriangle,n}^2+c$, and the second coordinate is $Q_{\blacktriangle,n-1}$; the map is the suspension of the quadratic map by the shift on the orbit.

Theorem (the limit of the connectedness locus). As $a\to0$, the connectedness locus $\mathcal{C}_a$ converges to the Mandelbrot set $\mathcal{M}$ of the quadratic family, in the sense of the Hausdorff convergence of the slices and of the currents.

Proof. This is the theorem of Hubbard and Oberste-Vorth on the Hénon map as a perturbation of the quadratic map; the precise statement is in the theory of complex dynamics in several variables. The mechanism is that the coupling $a$ tends to zero, the second coordinate is rescaled by $a$, and the first coordinate converges to the quadratic map.

Remark (the parameter space). The Hénon family has two complex parameters, and the quadratic family is the boundary slice $a=0$. The Hénon connectedness locus is a two-dimensional deformation of the Mandelbrot set, and the deformation is the subject of the parameter-space theory of the Hénon maps.

Remark (the real Hénon map). The real Hénon map with the classical parameters has a strange attractor, and the complex Hénon map is its complexification. The fractal geometry of the real attractor is the shadow of the complex Julia set, and the complex methods give the rigorous theory of the real phenomena.

Summary

The Hénon map $H_{a,c}(Q_{\blacktriangle},Q_{\blacktriangledown})=(Q_{\blacktriangle}^2+c-aQ_{\blacktriangledown},Q_{\blacktriangle})$ is a polynomial automorphism of degree two with constant Jacobian $a$; its inverse is $(Q_{\blacktriangledown},(Q_{\blacktriangledown}^2+c-Q_{\blacktriangle})/a)$. For $\lvert a\rvert<1$ it is dissipative, for $\lvert a\rvert=1$ it is volume preserving, for $a=1$ it preserves the holomorphic symplectic form, and for $\lvert a\rvert>1$ it expands volume. For large $\lvert a\rvert$ it has a hyperbolic horseshoe on a Cantor set, topologically conjugate to the full shift on two symbols, and its Julia set is a Cantor set.

The degree of $H^n$ is $2^n$, the dynamical degree is $2$, and the topological entropy is $\log 2$; the entropy is realised on the horseshoe and on the equilibrium measure. The filled Julia sets $K^\pm$ are the sets of bounded forward and backward orbits, the Julia sets are their boundaries, and $J=J^+\cap J^-$ is the support of the equilibrium measure $T^+\wedge T^-$. The connectedness locus is the set of parameters with connected Julia set; it is a real four-dimensional subset of the parameter space, its boundary is the bifurcation locus, and it converges to the Mandelbrot set as $a\to0$, where the Hénon map degenerates to the quadratic family.

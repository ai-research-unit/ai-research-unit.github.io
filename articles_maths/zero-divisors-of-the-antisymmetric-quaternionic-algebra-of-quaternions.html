Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The antisymmetric quaternionic algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ carries the operation $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=p_0\mathbf q-q_0\mathbf p-\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions. The parent quaternionic product has no zero divisor, the ambient algebra $\mathbb{H}$ being a division algebra; the antisymmetric block is at the opposite extreme. This article determines its zero divisors: the criterion, the annihilator, the rank of the multiplication, the families and the dimension count, and the comparison with the biquaternion block.

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The two share the space, the addition and the real scalars, but $\diamond$ is a different multiplication, alternating and without a unit. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new. The symbol $\diamond$ is reserved for this block.

The product and its table are Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions; the left multiplication operator is read in The Adjoint Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category; the comparison with the parent's divisor theory is Zero Divisors of the General Quaternionic Algebra, and the biquaternion counterpart is Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of the biquaternion chapter.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1^2=e_2^2=e_3^2=-e_0$. A generic element is $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\mathbf q$ with $\mathbf q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}q_ke_k$ and $q_\mu\in\mathbb{R}$; $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$ is the Euclidean dot product, $\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ the vector product, the centre is $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_0$ and the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\mathbf q\}$; the quaternion form is $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$.

The Notion and the Product

Definition. A nonzero element $\tilde p$ is a zero divisor of the block when there is a nonzero element $\tilde q$ with

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=0 . $$

Because the operation is alternating, $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$ for every $\tilde p$; hence every nonzero element is a zero divisor, with itself as partner on both sides, and the zero-divisor set is the punctured space $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$.

Theorem (the zero-divisor set is total). The set of zero-divisor elements is $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$, of real dimension four. The block is the opposite of the parent quaternionic product, whose only zero divisor is $0$, and agrees with its plain companion, the cross product.

Proof. For every $\tilde p\neq0$ the relation $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$ exhibits the partner $\tilde q=\tilde p$; no nonzero element is excluded.

The Criterion

Theorem (the criterion). For nonzero $\tilde p,\tilde q$,

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=0\iff p_0\mathbf q=q_0\mathbf p+\mathbf p\times\mathbf q \iff \tilde q\ \text{is a multiple of}\ \tilde p,\ \tilde q=\lambda\tilde p . $$

For a nonzero element the annihilating partners are exactly the multiples of the element.

Proof. The equation $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=p_0\mathbf q-q_0\mathbf p-\mathbf p\times\mathbf q=0$ is the displayed criterion. Let $\mathbf q=\alpha\mathbf p+\mathbf q_\perp$ with $\mathbf q_\perp\perp\mathbf p$; the criterion splits into the parallel part $(p_0\alpha-q_0)\mathbf p=0$ and the perpendicular part $p_0\mathbf q_\perp=\mathbf p\times\mathbf q_\perp$. The first gives $\alpha=q_0/p_0$ when $p_0\neq0$; the second is $p_0\mathbf q_\perp=|\mathbf p|R(\mathbf q_\perp)$ with $R$ a quarter turn of the plane orthogonal to $\mathbf p$, so that $(p_0/|\mathbf p|)\mathbf q_\perp=R(\mathbf q_\perp)$ would make the real number $p_0/|\mathbf p|$ an eigenvalue of $R$, and a quarter turn has no real eigenvalue; hence $\mathbf q_\perp=0$, so $\mathbf q=\alpha\mathbf p$ and $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$. For a pure $\tilde p$ the parallel part gives $q_0=0$ and the perpendicular part $\mathbf p\times\mathbf q=0$, so again $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$, then pure. The direct verification by the rank of the multiplication operator is below.

The Parallel and the Perpendicular Parts

Remark (why no perpendicular partner). The criterion couples the scalar direction and the vector part. The cross product contributes the perpendicular component alone, and the scalar part times the perpendicular part is compared with a quarter turn of it, a rotation of the plane orthogonal to $\mathbf p$; a quarter turn has no real eigenvector, so the perpendicular component of the partner is forced to vanish. Only the parallel component survives, and it fixes the ratio of the scalar parts. The partners are therefore the parallel pairs, and the perpendicular pairs are exactly the pairs that are not partners.

The Annihilator and the Rank

Definition. The left annihilator of $\tilde p$ is $\operatorname{Ann}(\tilde p)=\{\tilde q:\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=0\}$.

Theorem. The annihilator is

$$ \operatorname{Ann}(\tilde p)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}\tilde p,&\tilde p\neq0,\\[2pt]\mathbb{H},&\tilde p=0,\end{cases} $$

the line of the element for a nonzero element, of real dimension one. The left multiplication operator $L^{\diamond}_{\tilde p}\tilde q=\tilde p\diamond\tilde q$ has the invariants

$$ \operatorname{Tr}L^{\diamond}_{\tilde p}=3p_0,\qquad \det L^{\diamond}_{\tilde p}=0,\qquad \operatorname{rank}L^{\diamond}_{\tilde p}=\begin{cases}3,&\tilde p\neq0,\\0,&\tilde p=0,\end{cases} $$

and the characteristic polynomial $\chi(t)=t(t-p_0)\bigl((t-p_0)^2+|\mathbf p|^2\bigr)$.

Proof. The annihilator is the kernel of the left multiplication, and the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$ lies in it by alternation; the criterion shows it is the whole kernel for $\tilde p\neq0$. The matrix of the operator in the basis is computed in The Adjoint Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category, with the displayed invariants; the rank is three off the origin because the kernel is one-dimensional.

Remark (the contrast with the cross product). For the antisymmetric plain block the annihilator of a noncentral element is the two-dimensional plane $\mathbb{R}e_0\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf p$, because the cross product ignores the scalar coordinate; here the coupling of the scalar and the vector parts cuts the annihilator down to the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$. The pair (line, plane) is the exact analogue of the biquaternion pair (line off the cone, plane on the cone) with the cone absent.

The Families and the Dimension Count

Theorem (the families). The zero-divisor pairs $(\tilde p,\tilde q)$ with $\tilde p,\tilde q\neq0$ and $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=0$ form two families:

$$ \text{(i) the pure pairs: } \tilde p=\mathbf u,\ \tilde q=\lambda\mathbf u\ \text{with}\ \mathbf u\neq0,\ \lambda\neq0 ; \qquad \text{(ii) the non-pure pairs: } \tilde q=\lambda\tilde p\ \text{with}\ \tilde p\ \text{non-pure},\ \lambda\neq0 . $$

No pair mixes a pure and a non-pure element.

Proof. By the criterion $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$. If $\tilde p$ is pure, $\tilde q$ is pure; if $\tilde p$ is non-pure, $\tilde q$ is non-pure for $\lambda\neq0$. The two families meet at no pair, the pure and the non-pure being exclusive.

Proposition (the dimension count). The zero-divisor set has real dimension four; the set of zero-divisor pairs has real dimension five; and the annihilator of a nonzero element is a line, of real dimension one.

Proof. The zero-divisor set is $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$, of dimension four. A pair is an element $\tilde p\neq0$, four parameters, together with its partner $\lambda\tilde p$, one parameter, giving a five-dimensional family; the second argument is not free, the annihilator being a line.

Remark (the cone is empty). The biquaternion rank drops on the isotropic cone of the central square $c(\tilde P)=\sum_\mu P_\mu^2$, on which the annihilator widens from a line to a plane; over $\mathbb{R}$ the central square is $c(\tilde p)=|\tilde p|^2$, positive off the origin, so the cone is $\{0\}$ and the annihilator never widens. The comparison collapses the biquaternion table to its first two rows' common value. Summary

The antisymmetrised quaternionic block has no nontrivial divisor theory: the operation is alternating, so every nonzero element is a zero divisor with itself as partner and the zero-divisor set is the punctured four-dimensional space. The criterion for a pair is $p_0\mathbf q=q_0\mathbf p+\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$, which forces the partner to be a multiple of the element, $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$: the perpendicular component is killed by the quarter-turn comparison of the scalar and cross terms, and the annihilator is the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$ for a nonzero element, cutting the multiplication operator down to rank three, with trace $3p_0$, vanishing determinant and characteristic polynomial $t(t-p_0)\bigl((t-p_0)^2+|\mathbf p|^2\bigr)$. The zero-divisor pairs split into the pure parallel pairs and the non-pure proportional pairs, of real dimension five. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the same criterion holds and the annihilator widens on the isotropic cone of the central square; over $\mathbb{R}$ the cone is empty and the annihilator is always the line.

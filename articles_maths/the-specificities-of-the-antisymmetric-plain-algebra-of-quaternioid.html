The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

The antisymmetric plain algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ is the cross product $\tilde q_1\times\tilde q_2=\tfrac12(\tilde q_1\tilde q_2-\tilde q_2\tilde q_1)=\vec q_1\times\vec q_2$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra. This article states what singles the block out among the companion products of the space: the two identities it satisfies, the classes its laws empty, and the place it takes in the law table of Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

The block is the only companion product that is at once alternating and Jacobi; the two properties together make it the Lie product of the space. It has no unit, hence no inverse and no group of units, and no algebraic norm in the sense of the block. Its idempotents reduce to $0$, and its nilpotents are the whole space; the two classes, to which the biquaternion chapter gave separate articles and which the real block gives the companion articles Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra and Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, are folded here.

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The two share the space, the addition and the real scalars, but $\times$ is a different multiplication, alternating and without a unit. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new.

The operation and its table are Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra; the Lie structure, the form and the operators are The Lie Algebra of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, The Killing Form of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra and The Adjoint Operators and the Derivations of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, and the element theory is Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, Square Roots of One and the Idempotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space and Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space, all below this article in this category. The law vocabulary is Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures and the law table The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x^2=\vec e_y^2=\vec e_z^2=-e_t$; a generic element is $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$, the centre is $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_t$ and the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\vec q\}$. The laws are those of Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

The Criterion

The Two Properties

Theorem (the isolating pair). The block is alternating,

$$ \tilde q\times\tilde q=0\qquad\text{for every }\tilde q, $$

and satisfies the Jacobi identity,

$$ \tilde q_1\times(\tilde q_2\times\tilde q_3)+\tilde q_2\times(\tilde q_3\times\tilde q_1)+\tilde q_3\times(\tilde q_1\times\tilde q_2)=0 \qquad\text{for all }\tilde q_1,\tilde q_2,\tilde q_3 . $$

Proof. The alternation is $\tfrac12(\tilde q\tilde q-\tilde q\tilde q)=0$, the two terms of the difference coinciding. The cyclic sum is the one of the vector product of the vector parts, $(\vec q_1\times\vec q_2)\times\vec q_3+(\vec q_2\times\vec q_3)\times\vec q_1+(\vec q_3\times\vec q_1)\times\vec q_2$, which vanishes by the anticommutativity of the vector product; it is verified on general elements and on the $64$ triples of the units.

Corollary (the block is the Lie product of the space). An alternating bilinear rule satisfying the Jacobi identity is a Lie bracket, so $(\mathbb{H},\times)$ is a Lie algebra. Among the companion products of the space, the block is the only structure that is both alternating and Jacobi: the other alternating product, the antisymmetric quaternionic operation, fails the Jacobi identity, and the four remaining products are not alternating. The Lie structure is The Lie Algebra of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category.

The Laws That Follow

Proposition. The block is flexible, power-associative and cube-zero,

$$ (\tilde q_1\times\tilde q_2)\times\tilde q_1=\tilde q_1\times(\tilde q_2\times\tilde q_1),\qquad (\tilde q\times\tilde q)\times\tilde q=\tilde q\times(\tilde q\times\tilde q),\qquad \tilde q\times(\tilde q\times\tilde q)=0, $$

and it is not associative and not alternative: the associator $(\tilde q_1\times\tilde q_2)\times\tilde q_3-\tilde q_1\times(\tilde q_2\times\tilde q_3)$ does not vanish, and the right alternative law fails at $\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_y$.

Proof. A bilinear alternating rule is antisymmetric, and the implications "alternating and bilinear $\Rightarrow$ flexible, power-associative, cube-zero" of Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space give the first three; flexibility is checked at $\vec e_x,\vec e_y$, where $(\vec e_x\times \vec e_y)\times \vec e_x=\vec e_z\times \vec e_x=\vec e_y$ and $\vec e_x\times(\vec e_y\times \vec e_x)=\vec e_x\times(-\vec e_z)=\vec e_y$. The associator is read on the units, where $(\vec e_x\times \vec e_y)\times \vec e_y=\vec e_z\times \vec e_y=-\vec e_x$ is different from $\vec e_x\times(\vec e_y\times \vec e_y)=0$, so the rule is not associative; the same triple is the failure of the right alternative law.

The Absence of a Unit, of an Inverse and of a Norm

Theorem. The block has no two-sided unit and no one-sided unit; hence it has no inverse, its group of units is empty, and it carries no algebraic norm in the sense of the block.

Proof. Every value is pure, whereas $\tilde q_1\times\tilde q_2=\tilde q_1$ at a non-pure $\tilde q_1$ would be a non-pure value; and the scalar direction is not reached at all, $\tilde q_1\times\tilde q_2$ having zero scalar part. So no element is a unit on either side, the inverse being defined relative to a two-sided unit. The algebraic norm of a product is a quadratic form multiplicative for the product; the diagonal form of the block is the vanishing function $\tilde q\times\tilde q=0$, so the block's own algebraic norm is the zero form, and the norms $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ of the space are not multiplicative for $\times$.

Remark (the contrast with the plain and symmetric blocks). The plain algebra has the unit $e_t$ and the multiplicative norm $\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}=\tilde q\tilde q^{\natural}$; the symmetric plain algebra keeps the unit $e_t$, but it is no longer a division algebra and its norm is no longer multiplicative; the antisymmetric block has neither, its two quadratic objects $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ being carried by the symmetric products instead. The absence is definitional, not accidental: it follows from alternation alone.

The Folded Element Classes

The Idempotents

Theorem. The idempotents of the block are only $0$,

$$ \tilde\pi\times\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi\iff\tilde\pi=0 . $$

Proof. The square is zero for every element, so the equation is $\tilde\pi=0$. The idempotent set is the origin alone; its own article is Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category, and the class is folded here.

The Nilpotents

Theorem. The nilpotents of the block are the whole space: the origin is the trivial nilpotent of index one, and every other element is a nilpotent of index two,

$$ \tilde\nu\times\tilde\nu=0\qquad\text{for every }\tilde\nu,\qquad \tilde\nu\neq0\implies\tilde\nu\ \text{is of index }2 . $$

The nonzero nilpotents are the punctured space, and the class coincides with the zero-divisor set and with the root set of $0$ (Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra and Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space, both below this article in this category).

Proof. The square vanishes identically, so every element is square-zero; the index of nilpotency is two for a nonzero element, the square being zero and the element itself nonzero, and one for the origin. The reading is the one of Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, where the class of nonzero nilpotents is the whole space for the two antisymmetric structures.

Remark (what isolates the block). Two readings single the block out. First, $\mathrm{APA}$ is the only structure that is both alternating and Jacobi, hence the only Lie structure of the companion products. Second, the block has no unit while the symmetric plain structure does, is not associative, and fails the norm while the symmetric plain structure carries one. The block is therefore $\mathrm{APA}$, the Lie bracket of the space.

The Boundary of the Criterion

Proposition. The criterion of isolation is the pair (alternating, Jacobi). Alternation alone does not isolate the block: the antisymmetric quaternionic structure is alternating and fails Jacobi. In the law table the Jacobi identity alone already points at the block, since it is the only structure whose product satisfies the identity; alternation is the further demand that turns a Jacobi product into a Lie product. The criterion does not reach the element theory of the symmetric blocks, which is read on the basis.

Proof. The failure of alternation alone is the witness $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$ of the antisymmetric quaternionic structure, whose cyclic sum is a nonzero multiple of $\vec e_z$; the Jacobi row of the table is zero for every structure but the block. The element theory of the symmetric structures requires a basis computation, as the table of empty classes records.

Summary

The antisymmetric plain block is isolated among the companion products by a single pair of identities: it is alternating, $\tilde q\times\tilde q=0$, and it satisfies the Jacobi identity, so it is the unique Lie structure of the space. Its laws are those the two properties force: flexible, power-associative and cube-zero, and neither associative nor alternative in the nonzero sense. The block has no unit on either side, hence an empty group of units and no algebraic norm of its own, the diagonal form of the operation being the zero function. Its idempotents reduce to $0$ and its nilpotents are the whole space, the class being folded here; both are shared with the antisymmetric quaternionic block, whose Jacobi identity fails. The block is therefore one of the two degenerate antisymmetric structures, and the Jacobi identity is the whole of the difference between them.

Annihilators of the General Plain Sesqualgebra

Introduction

The zero divisor of a product is a property of one element alone: it says that some nonzero partner exists that the element kills. The annihilator says which partners those are, and it is the finer object. It is the set of elements killed by a fixed element, $$ A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\star\tilde X=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde Q=0\}, $$ the left and the right annihilator (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Zero Divisor and the Annihilator). When the product is carried by a multiplication operator the annihilator is the kernel of that operator, so it is a linear subspace.

The block of this article is the general plain sesquimultiplication $$ \tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tilde P\tilde Q^{*} =\bigl[P_0Q_0-(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf Q})\bigr]+P_0\overline{\mathbf Q}+\overline{Q_0}\mathbf P-\mathbf P\times\overline{\mathbf Q}, $$ the plain product with the conjugate linear star in the second slot (Introduction to the General Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions). Its zero divisors are again the elements of the cone $C(\tilde P)=0$ (Zero Divisors of the General Plain Sesqualgebra, above this article in this category), and it is the sibling of the general plain algebra, from which it inherits the cone: the star in the slot turns the plain annihilator into its conjugate and identifies the two sides. The article owns that reading, the operator with its rank, and the shape of the annihilator on the remarkable subspaces; the zero-divisor set and its comparison with the parent and the four general products are the cited article's.

The results. The left and the right multiplications of the block, $L_{\tilde P}\tilde X=\tilde P\tilde X^{*}$ and $R_{\tilde P}\tilde X=\tilde X\tilde P^{*}$, are conjugate-linear, and the two annihilators coincide, $A_\star(\tilde Q)=B_\star(\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q)^{*}$, the conjugate of the plain annihilator $K(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde Z:\tilde Q\tilde Z=0\}$. Off the cone every annihilator is $\{0\}$ and the operator has complex rank four; on the cone each is a complex plane of complex dimension two and the operator has complex rank two. The block is the one of the general products for which the two annihilators are equal rather than merely two.

The Annihilator of a Product

The annihilator of an element is the set of partners that it kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left multiplication $\tilde P\tilde X^{*}$ and the right multiplication $\tilde X\tilde P^{*}$, and each is nonzero exactly when the element is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Definition (annihilator). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$ and let $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$. The left annihilator and the right annihilator of $\tilde Q$ in $\star$ are $$ A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\star\tilde X=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde Q=0\}. $$ They are the kernels of the left and the right multiplication, and they are nonzero exactly when $\tilde Q$ is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Throughout this article the product is the general plain sesquimultiplication $\star$, and unqualified, annihilator means the annihilator of $\star$. The one-sided multiplications are the operators $$ L_{\tilde P}\tilde X=\tilde P\tilde X^{*},\qquad R_{\tilde P}\tilde X=\tilde X\tilde P^{*}, $$ both conjugate-linear in $\tilde X$ because the star is, with kernel a complex subspace. The block is the sibling of the general plain algebra, whose annihilators are Annihilators of the General Plain Algebra.

The Two Annihilators Coincide

The two annihilators are equal and are the conjugate of the plain annihilator, $B_\star(\tilde Q)=A_\star(\tilde Q)=K(\tilde Q)^{*}$ with $K(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde Z:\tilde Q\tilde Z=0\}$: off the cone both are $\{0\}$ and the element is a unit of the plain product, and on the cone each is a complex plane of complex dimension two.

Theorem (the two annihilators are equal, and they are the conjugate of the plain annihilator). For every $\tilde Q$, $$ B_\star(\tilde Q)=A_\star(\tilde Q)=\bigl\{\tilde Z^{*}:\tilde Z\in K(\tilde Q)\bigr\}=K(\tilde Q)^{*}, \qquad K(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde Z:\tilde Q\tilde Z=0\}, $$ where $K(\tilde Q)$ is the plain left annihilator of $\tilde Q$ and $K(\tilde Q)^{*}$ is its image under the involution. For a nonzero zero divisor the two annihilators are equal and of complex dimension two; for a unit of the plain product both are $\{0\}$.

Proof. The right annihilator is $B_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\tilde X^{*}=0\}=\{\tilde X:\tilde X^{*}\in K(\tilde Q)\}=K(\tilde Q)^{*}$, the star being a bijection. For the left annihilator, $\tilde X\star\tilde Q=0$ reads $\tilde X\tilde Q^{*}=0$, and applying the anti-automorphism ${}^{*}$ gives $\tilde Q\tilde X^{*}=0$, with the converse by the same argument because ${}^{*}$ is a bijection; the two sets are therefore equal. The dimension is that of the plain annihilator, two on the cone and zero off it.

Example. For the zero divisor $\tilde Q=e_0+ie_1$, of central square $1-1=0$, the common annihilator is $$ A_\star(e_0+ie_1)=\mathbb{C}\{e_1+ie_0,\ e_2+ie_3\}. $$

The Multiplication Operator and the Rank

The conjugate-linear operator $L_{\tilde P}$ has complex rank four and kernel $\{0\}$ off the cone, complex rank two and kernel of complex dimension two on the cone, and rank $0$ with kernel of dimension four at $\tilde P=0$.

The two one-sided multiplications are conjugate-linear, so they have no determinant in the complex sense; what is read is the rank, the complex dimension of the image, which is half the real rank of the underlying real-linear map.

Corollary (the rank and the annihilator). The rank of $L_{\tilde P}$ and the annihilator of $\tilde P$ are

$\tilde P$ $C(\tilde P)$ $\operatorname{rank}L_{\tilde P}$ $\dim\ker L_{\tilde P}$
$0$ $0$ $0$ $4$
$\tilde P\neq0$, $C(\tilde P)\neq0$ $\neq0$ $4$ $0$
$\tilde P\neq0$, $C(\tilde P)=0$ $0$ $2$ $2$

Proof. The star is a bijection, so $L_{\tilde P}$ has the same rank and kernel as the plain multiplication $\tilde Z\mapsto\tilde P\tilde Z$, whose rank is four for a unit and two for a nonzero zero divisor; the kernel dimension is $4$ minus the rank.

The Dichotomy by the Cone

The annihilator reads the cone twice, trivially off it and as a complex plane on it, and the star makes the two sides coincide, where the parent's two annihilators are distinct.

The zero-divisor set of the block is the punctured cone $\{\tilde Q\neq0:C(\tilde Q)=0\}$, the same cone as the parent. The annihilator reads the cone twice, as in the parent: off the cone both annihilators are $\{0\}$ and the element is a unit of the plain product, and on the cone each is a complex plane. What the star changes is the coincidence: the two annihilators are equal, and they are the conjugate of the plain annihilator, whereas in the parent they are distinct. The block is the sibling of the general quaternionic algebra, whose $\mathbb{C}$-linear involution does the same work, and it is the sibling of the antipodal Annihilators of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, whose two sides are again distinct.

The Annihilator on the Remarkable Subspaces

On every remarkable subspace the annihilator is the ambient one intersected with the subspace: $\{0\}$ off the cone, and the conjugate of the plain annihilator, a complex plane, inside the subspace on the cone.

The centre. A nonzero central element $\tilde P=\lambda e_0$ has $L_{\lambda e_0}\tilde X=\lambda\tilde X^{*}$, whose kernel is the set of the $\tilde X$ with $\tilde X^{*}=0$, that is $\{0\}$: the central elements are units of the plain product and their annihilator is $\{0\}$.

The vector subspace. A nonzero pure element $\tilde P=\mathbf P$ has $C(\tilde P)=(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)$; it is a zero divisor of the cone exactly when the vector part is isotropic, and then the annihilators are the conjugate of the plain annihilator of $\mathbf P$, a complex plane.

The conjugate-changed subspaces. The star in the second slot exchanges the element and its conjugate, so on the Hermitian subspace, where the star fixes the vector part, the annihilator of a nonzero Hermitian zero divisor is the conjugate of its plain annihilator in the same subspace.

Summary

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners the element kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left and the right multiplication. The general plain sesquimultiplication $\tilde P\star\tilde X=\tilde P\tilde X^{*}$ carries the conjugate linear star in the slot, and the conjugate-alternation identifies the two sides: the two annihilators of an element are equal and are the conjugate of the plain annihilator $K(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde Z:\tilde Q\tilde Z=0\}$. Off the cone both annihilators vanish and the element is a unit of the plain product; on the cone each is a complex plane. The block is the plain parent with the star added, and the star is exactly what turns the two distinct planes of the parent into one plane.

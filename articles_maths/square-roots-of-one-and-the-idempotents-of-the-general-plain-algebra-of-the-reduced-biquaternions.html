Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions

Introduction

This article solves the equations $\breve Q^{2}=1$ and $\breve Q^{2}=\breve Q$ in the multiplication of the general plain algebra and reads the solutions. The roots of one are the four elements $$ 1,\quad -1,\quad j,\quad -j, $$ and the idempotents are the four elements $$ 0,\quad 1,\quad \Pi_+=\tfrac12(1+j),\quad \Pi_-=\tfrac12(1-j). $$ The two lists are exchanged by the halving map $\breve Q\mapsto\tfrac12(1+\breve Q)$, which sends a root of one to an idempotent and back; the two nontrivial idempotents are complementary and orthogonal, $\Pi_++\Pi_-=1$ and $\Pi_+\Pi_-=0$, and the two projections are $\breve Q\mapsto\Pi_+\breve Q$ and $\breve Q\mapsto\Pi_-\breve Q$, so that the algebra resolves as the sum of the two idempotent lines.

The computation is short because the multiplication is componentwise in the idempotent coordinates: for $\breve Q=(Q_{++},Q_{--})$, with $Q_{++}=Q_++Q_-$ and $Q_{--}=Q_+-Q_-$, one has $\breve Q^{2}=(Q_{++}^{2},Q_{--}^{2})$, so $\breve Q^{2}=1$ is the pair of complex equations $Q_{++}^{2}=1$, $Q_{--}^{2}=1$ and $\breve Q^{2}=\breve Q$ is the pair $Q_{++}^{2}=Q_{++}$, $Q_{--}^{2}=Q_{--}$. Every list of solutions is therefore a product of two complex lists, and the four figures come from the two solutions $\pm1$ and the two solutions $0,1$ of the complex equations.

The results. The roots of one are $1,-1,j,-j$, and they form a group isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ under the multiplication. The idempotents are $0,1,\Pi_+,\Pi_-$, of which $\Pi_+$ and $\Pi_-$ are the nontrivial ones and are complementary, orthogonal and zero divisors. Each idempotent $\Pi$ carries the projector $P_{\Pi}(\breve Q)=\Pi \breve Q$, with $P_{\Pi_+}+P_{\Pi_-}=\mathrm{id}$, $P_{\Pi}^{2}=P_{\Pi}$ and $P_{\Pi_+}P_{\Pi_-}=0$; the two projections are the Peirce projections onto the two lines, and the Peirce decomposition itself is Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, below this article in this category. The natural sign and the star both exchange the two nontrivial idempotents, $\Pi_+^{\natural}=\Pi_-=\Pi_+^{*}$, while only the bar fixes each idempotent, $\overline{\Pi_+}=\Pi_+$ and $\overline{\Pi_-}=\Pi_-$.

Boundaries. The algebra, its product and its structure as a direct product of two fields are Introduction to the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, above this article in this category. The involutions and the idempotents' behaviour under them are The Two Involutions and the Group of Involutions, above this article. The zero divisors and the two lines are Zero Divisors of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, above this article; the annihilators are Annihilators of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, above this article; and the roots of zero and the nilpotents are Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, below this article. The Peirce decomposition into the ideals the idempotents carry is Ideals and Peirce Decomposition, below this article in this category.

The Roots of One

The Equation and the Four Roots

The equation $\breve Q^{2}=1$ has exactly four solutions: $1$, $-1$, $j$ and $-j$.

Theorem (the roots of one). The solutions of $\breve Q^{2}=1$ in the multiplication are $$ \breve Q=1,\qquad \breve Q=-1,\qquad \breve Q=j,\qquad \breve Q=-j, $$ and there are no others.

Proof. In the idempotent coordinates $\breve Q=(Q_{++},Q_{--})$ with $Q_{++}=Q_++Q_-$ and $Q_{--}=Q_+-Q_-$, the product is componentwise, so $\breve Q^{2}=(Q_{++}^{2},Q_{--}^{2})$ (Introduction to the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions). The equation $\breve Q^{2}=1$ is the pair $Q_{++}^{2}=1$ and $Q_{--}^{2}=1$; a complex number has the two square roots of $1$, namely $\pm1$, so $Q_{++},Q_{--}\in\{1,-1\}$. The four pairs $(Q_{++},Q_{--})$ give, by $Q_+=\tfrac12(Q_{++}+Q_{--})$ and $Q_-=\tfrac12(Q_{++}-Q_{--})$, $$ (Q_{++},Q_{--})=(1,1)\to \breve Q=1,\quad (1,-1)\to \breve Q=j,\quad (-1,1)\to \breve Q=-j,\quad (-1,-1)\to \breve Q=-1 . $$

Corollary (the roots of one in the real basis). In the real coordinates the roots are $1=(1,0,0,0)$, $-1=(-1,0,0,0)$, $j=(0,0,1,0)$ and $-j=(0,0,-1,0)$; all four lie in the real subalgebra $\mathbb{R}\{1,j\}$.

Proof. The four elements are $\pm1$ and $\pm j$, read in the real basis; both $Q_+$ and $Q_-$ are real at each of them.

Remark (the roots of $-1$ are different). The equation $\breve Q^{2}=-1$ has the four solutions $\pm i$ and $\pm ij$, the roots of $-1$ of Conventions in Mathematics, §The Roots: they are the roots of one multiplied by $i$, and they lie in the translate $i\mathbb{R}\{1,j\}$. The roots of one are not the roots of $-1$, and the distinction is stated here because the two families are both four.

The Group of the Roots

The four roots of one form a group isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ under the multiplication.

Theorem. The set $\{1,-1,j,-j\}$ is closed under the multiplication, and it is a group isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$; its non-identity elements are the three involutions $-1$, $j$ and $-j$, and the product of any two distinct non-identity elements is the third.

Proof. The products are read from $j^{2}=1$ and $(-1)^{2}=1$: $$ (-1)j=-j,\qquad (-1)(-j)=j,\qquad j(-j)=-j^{2}=-1, $$ so the set is closed, and $1$ is the identity; every element is its own inverse. A group of four elements with every element of order two is $\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$.

Corollary (the two lists are distinct). The roots of one and the idempotents meet in the single element $1$; the halving map of §The Halving Map is a bijection between the two lists, and it is not the identity.

The Idempotents

The Idempotent Equation and the Four Idempotents

The equation $\breve Q^{2}=\breve Q$ has exactly four solutions: $0$, $1$, $\Pi_+$ and $\Pi_-$.

Definition. An idempotent is a solution of $\breve Q^{2}=\breve Q$; the nontrivial idempotents are the idempotents different from $0$ and $1$.

Theorem (the idempotents). The solutions of $\breve Q^{2}=\breve Q$ are $$ \breve Q=0,\qquad \breve Q=1,\qquad \breve Q=\Pi_+=\tfrac12(1+j),\qquad \breve Q=\Pi_-=\tfrac12(1-j), $$ and there are no others.

Proof. In the idempotent coordinates the equation is the pair $Q_{++}^{2}=Q_{++}$ and $Q_{--}^{2}=Q_{--}$; a complex number satisfies $z^{2}=z$ iff $z=0$ or $z=1$. The four pairs $(Q_{++},Q_{--})$ give, by $Q_+=\tfrac12(Q_{++}+Q_{--})$ and $Q_-=\tfrac12(Q_{++}-Q_{--})$, $$ (0,0)\to \breve Q=0,\quad (1,1)\to \breve Q=1,\quad (1,0)\to \breve Q=\Pi_+,\quad (0,1)\to \breve Q=\Pi_-. $$

The Two Nontrivial Idempotents

The two nontrivial idempotents are complementary, orthogonal and zero divisors, and they are the generators of the two lines.

Theorem (complementarity and orthogonality). The two nontrivial idempotents satisfy $$ \Pi_+^{2}=\Pi_+,\qquad \Pi_-^{2}=\Pi_-,\qquad \Pi_+\Pi_-=0,\qquad \Pi_++\Pi_-=1 . $$

Proof. Read in the idempotent coordinates, $\Pi_+=(1,0)$ and $\Pi_-=(0,1)$, so the four relations are the componentwise relations in $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$; equivalently, the direct computation of Reduced Biquaternion Space. The orthogonality $\Pi_+\Pi_-=0$ makes each a zero divisor, and the two lines they generate are the null cone of Zero Divisors of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions.

Corollary (the idempotents and the roots of one). The two nontrivial idempotents are $\Pi_+=\tfrac12(1+j)$ and $\Pi_-=\tfrac12(1-j)$, the halves of the sum and the difference of the identity and the root $j$ of one.

Proof. The two displays are the definitions; the root of one used is $j$, since $j^{2}=1$ is what makes $\Pi_\pm^{2}=\Pi_\pm$.

The Projections

The Projection onto an Idempotent Line

Each idempotent $\Pi$ carries the projector $P_{\Pi}(\breve Q)=\Pi \breve Q$, the orthogonal projection onto the line $\mathbb{C}\Pi$ along the other line.

Definition. For an idempotent $\Pi$, the projector it carries is $$ P_{\Pi}(\breve Q)=\Pi \breve Q. $$

Theorem (the projectors are idempotent). For $\Pi=\Pi_+$ and $\Pi=\Pi_-$, $$ P_{\Pi}^{2}=P_{\Pi},\qquad P_{\Pi_+}+P_{\Pi_-}=\mathrm{id},\qquad P_{\Pi_+}P_{\Pi_-}=0=P_{\Pi_-}P_{\Pi_+}. $$

Proof. $P_{\Pi}^{2}(\breve Q)=\Pi(\Pi \breve Q)=\Pi^{2}\breve Q=\Pi \breve Q$; the sum is $(\Pi_++\Pi_-)\breve Q=1\,\breve Q=\breve Q$; and the product is $\Pi_+\Pi_-\breve Q=0$. The identity $P_{\Pi_+}+P_{\Pi_-}=\mathrm{id}$ is the resolution of the identity, and the two projections are the Peirce projections of the algebra.

Proposition (the explicit projections). For $\breve Q=Q_++jQ_-$, $$ P_{\Pi_+}(\breve Q)=(Q_++Q_-)\Pi_+,\qquad P_{\Pi_-}(\breve Q)=(Q_+-Q_-)\Pi_-, $$ so the image of $P_{\Pi_+}$ is the first idempotent line and the image of $P_{\Pi_-}$ the second.

Proof. $\Pi_+\breve Q=\Pi_+(Q_++jQ_-)=Q_+\Pi_++Q_-\Pi_+j$; and $\Pi_+j=\tfrac12(1+j)j=\tfrac12(j+1)=\Pi_+$, so $\Pi_+\breve Q=(Q_++Q_-)\Pi_+$. The same for $\Pi_-$, using $\Pi_-j=-\Pi_-$.

The Resolution of the Identity

The algebra is the direct sum of the two images, and the resolution of the identity is the decomposition of every element into its two idempotent coordinates.

Theorem (the direct sum). The algebra is the direct sum of the two images, $$ \mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}=\operatorname{im}P_{\Pi_+}\oplus\operatorname{im}P_{\Pi_-}=\mathbb{C}\Pi_+\oplus\mathbb{C}\Pi_-, $$ and every element has the unique decomposition $\breve Q=P_{\Pi_+}(\breve Q)+P_{\Pi_-}(\breve Q)$.

Proof. $P_{\Pi_+}+P_{\Pi_-}=\mathrm{id}$ gives the decomposition, and $P_{\Pi_+}P_{\Pi_-}=0$ gives its uniqueness; the images are the two lines, which meet only at the origin (Zero Divisors of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions). The decomposition of a general element reads $\breve Q=(Q_++Q_-)\Pi_++(Q_+-Q_-)\Pi_-$ (Reduced Biquaternion Space).

Remark (the Peirce decomposition). The projections and the lines they carry are the Peirce data of the two idempotents; the spaces in the general Peirce decomposition of a pair of idempotents, the four subspaces $\Pi\mathbb{B}\Pi$, $\Pi\mathbb{B}(1-\Pi)$, $(1-\Pi)\mathbb{B}\Pi$ and $(1-\Pi)\mathbb{B}(1-\Pi)$, are Ideals and Peirce Decomposition of the Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, below this article in this category; here only the two projections and the two lines they carry are read.

The Idempotents Under the Involutions

The natural sign and the star both exchange the two nontrivial idempotents, and only the bar fixes every idempotent.

Theorem. $\Pi_+^{\natural}=\Pi_-=\Pi_+^{*}$, $\Pi_-^{\natural}=\Pi_+=\Pi_-^{*}$, and $\overline{\Pi_+}=\Pi_+$, $\overline{\Pi_-}=\Pi_-$; the flat sends each idempotent to the negative of the other, $\Pi_+^{\flat}=-\Pi_-$ and $\Pi_-^{\flat}=-\Pi_+$, which is not an idempotent.

Proof. $\Pi_+^{\natural}=\tfrac12(1+j)^{\natural}=\tfrac12(1-j)=\Pi_-$, and $\Pi_-^{\natural}=\Pi_+$ (The Two Involutions and the Group of Involutions); the bar conjugates the coefficients of $\Pi_\pm=\tfrac12\pm\tfrac12 j$, which are real, so it fixes each idempotent; the star is $\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$, so $\Pi_+^{*}=\overline{\Pi_+^{\natural}}=\overline{\Pi_-}=\Pi_-$ and $\Pi_-^{*}=\Pi_+$, the star exchanging the two as the natural sign does. The flat is $-{}^{*}$, so it sends each idempotent to the negative of the other.

Corollary (the involution swaps the two lines). The natural sign restricts to a $\mathbb{C}$-linear exchange of the two idempotent lines, $\mathbb{C}\Pi_+\leftrightarrow\mathbb{C}\Pi_-$, and the two lines are therefore conjugate under the intrinsic conjugate.

The Halving Map

The map $\breve Q\mapsto\tfrac12(1+\breve Q)$ sends the roots of one bijectively to the idempotents, and its inverse on the idempotents is $\Pi\mapsto 2\Pi-1$.

Theorem (the halving map). The map $$ H(\breve Q)=\tfrac12(1+\breve Q) $$ restricts to a bijection from the roots of one $\{1,-1,j,-j\}$ onto the idempotents $\{0,1,\Pi_+,\Pi_-\}$, with $$ H(1)=1,\qquad H(-1)=0,\qquad H(j)=\Pi_+,\qquad H(-j)=\Pi_-, $$ and the inverse on the idempotents is $\Pi\mapsto 2\Pi-1$.

Proof. The four values are direct: $\tfrac12(1+1)=1$, $\tfrac12(1-1)=0$, $\tfrac12(1+j)=\Pi_+$, $\tfrac12(1-j)=\Pi_-$. If $\breve Q^{2}=1$ then $H(\breve Q)^{2}=\tfrac14(1+2\breve Q+\breve Q^{2})=\tfrac14(2+2\breve Q)=H(\breve Q)$, so $H$ maps roots of one into idempotents; if $\Pi^{2}=\Pi$ then $2\Pi-1$ has $(2\Pi-1)^{2}=4\Pi^{2}-4\Pi+1=1$, so $2\Pi-1$ is a root of one; and the two maps are inverse, since $H(2\Pi-1)=\tfrac12(1+2\Pi-1)=\Pi$.

Remark (the classical form of the correspondence). The correspondence between the roots of one and the idempotents by halving is the classical correspondence between an involution and the projection it carries; here it reads the two nontrivial idempotents as the halves of the sum and the difference of the identity and the two nontrivial roots $j$ and $-j$.

Summary

The roots of one of the general plain algebra are the four elements $$ 1,\quad -1,\quad j,\quad -j, $$ and they form a group isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ under the multiplication; they all lie in the real subalgebra $\mathbb{R}\{1,j\}$. The idempotents are the four elements $$ 0,\quad 1,\quad \Pi_+=\tfrac12(1+j),\quad \Pi_-=\tfrac12(1-j), $$ solutions of $\breve Q^{2}=\breve Q$, and the two nontrivial ones are complementary, orthogonal and zero divisors, $$ \Pi_+^{2}=\Pi_+,\quad \Pi_-^{2}=\Pi_-,\quad \Pi_+\Pi_-=0,\quad \Pi_++\Pi_-=1 . $$

Each idempotent $\Pi$ carries the projector $P_{\Pi}(\breve Q)=\Pi \breve Q$, with $P_{\Pi_+}+P_{\Pi_-}=\mathrm{id}$, $P_{\Pi}^{2}=P_{\Pi}$ and $P_{\Pi_+}P_{\Pi_-}=0$; the explicit projections are $P_{\Pi_+}(\breve Q)=(Q_++Q_-)\Pi_+$ and $P_{\Pi_-}(\breve Q)=(Q_+-Q_-)\Pi_-$, and they give the resolution of the identity and the decomposition $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{r}}=\mathbb{C}\Pi_+\oplus\mathbb{C}\Pi_-$. The natural sign and the star both exchange the two nontrivial idempotents, $\Pi_+^{\natural}=\Pi_-=\Pi_+^{*}$ and $\Pi_-^{\natural}=\Pi_+=\Pi_-^{*}$, while only the bar fixes each idempotent, $\overline{\Pi_+}=\Pi_+$ and $\overline{\Pi_-}=\Pi_-$. The halving map $H(\breve Q)=\tfrac12(1+\breve Q)$ is a bijection from the roots of one onto the idempotents, with inverse $\Pi\mapsto2\Pi-1$, and it sends $-1$ to $0$, $j$ to $\Pi_+$ and $-j$ to $\Pi_-$.

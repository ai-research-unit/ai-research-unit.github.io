The Spin-Zero Carrier and the Rank Strata

Introduction

The subject. The rank of a scalar operator on the sixteen-dimensional space of the operators is either sixteen or zero, and nothing between. The article computes the two strata, shows that the rank stratification and the spectral stratification of the carrier coincide, compares the carrier with the other three carriers where the two stratifications differ, and describes the single jump of the rank as the multiplier crosses the origin.

The order of the article. The rank comes first; the two strata second. The coincidence of the stratifications follows, then the comparison with the other carriers, then the jump, and the reading closes.

It assumes The Spin-Zero Carrier: the Scalars, The Length and the Two Ranks and The Spin-Zero Carrier and the Spectral Strata. The row of the spin-zero carrier is where the rank of the multiplication by a scalar biquaternion is four or zero and the same coincidence holds.

The result in one line. The operator of left composition with $\lambda\mathrm I$ has rank sixteen for $\lambda\neq0$ and rank zero at $\lambda=0$, so the carrier has exactly the two rank strata of the point and the punctured line, the only carrier on which the rank and the spectrum agree.

Boundaries. The spectral strata are The Spin-Zero Carrier and the Spectral Strata; the determinant is The Spin-Zero Carrier and the Determinant; the ranks of a general operator are The Length and the Two Ranks.

The Rank of a Scalar Operator

Definition. The rank of an operator is the dimension of its image; the rank strata are the level sets of the rank.

Theorem. The operator of left composition with $\lambda\mathrm I$ has rank sixteen when $\lambda\neq0$ and rank zero when $\lambda=0$.

Proof. Left composition with a nonzero scalar is the multiplication by $\lambda$ on every vector of the space, which is a bijection; the zero scalar gives the zero map.

Corollary. There is no value of the multiplier for which the rank is intermediate, and the two values sixteen and zero exhaust the possibilities.

Proof. The two cases of the theorem.

Corollary. The rank is the dimension of the space itself in the generic case, since the operator is a homothety and the space is sixteen-dimensional.

Proof. A homothety with nonzero multiplier has the whole space as image.

The reading. The rank of the carrier is the dimension of the space or nothing. There is no proper image, because there is no proper kernel: a homothety either kills everything or nothing, and the carrier is the line of the homotheties.

The Two Strata

Proposition. The carrier splits into the stratum of rank sixteen, which is the punctured line, and the stratum of rank zero, which is the origin.

Proof. The rank is sixteen exactly when the multiplier is nonzero.

Corollary. The stratum of rank sixteen is open and dense in the carrier, and the stratum of rank zero is a single closed point.

Proof. The complement of the origin in a line is open and dense, and the origin is closed.

Corollary. The two strata of the carrier are exactly the two strata of the determinant, since the rank is full exactly when the determinant does not vanish.

Proof. For a homothety, invertibility, non-vanishing determinant and full rank are the same condition.

The reading. Two strata, one open and one closed, and the same two for the rank, the determinant and the spectrum. The carrier is the one place of the theory where three classifications coincide without a computation.

The Coincidence of the Stratifications

Theorem. On the spin-zero carrier the rank stratification and the spectral stratification agree: both have the origin and its complement as their only strata.

Proof. The spectrum is the single eigenvalue $\lambda$ with multiplicity sixteen for every $\lambda$, so the spectral stratum is the same set for all nonzero multipliers; the rank is sixteen on the same set and zero at the origin.

Corollary. The carrier is the only carrier on which the two stratifications agree, since on the others the spectrum refines the rank.

Proof. On a carrier of positive label the operators have several eigenvalues, and the multiplicity pattern changes inside a stratum of constant rank.

Corollary. The agreement is a consequence of the homothety property and not of the dimension: any line of homotheties has it.

Proof. The spectrum and the rank of a homothety are both determined by whether the multiplier vanishes.

The reading. The simplest carrier is the only one on which every invariant agrees. The rank, the determinant and the spectrum cut it into the same two pieces, and no finer information exists.

The Comparison with the Other Carriers

Proposition. On the other three carriers the rank takes the values zero, one, two and four, and the spectrum varies continuously inside each rank stratum.

Proof. A two-sided operator has the rank $\operatorname{rank}L_a\operatorname{rank}R_b/4$, which is four, two, one or zero, and the three positive carriers contain products of degenerate pairs, so all four values occur.

Proof. An operator of positive rank in those carriers is a nonzero element of the algebra composed with a nonzero functional, and its image is a line; the eigenvalues of such an operator depend on the two factors and not only on the rank.

Corollary. On those carriers the rank stratum of the invertible part contains operators with different spectra, so the spectral stratification is finer than the rank stratification.

Proof. Two primitive operators of different phases have the same rank and different eigenvalues.

Corollary. The carrier of label zero is the only one on which the finer classification collapses onto the coarser.

Proof. The comparison of the two stratifications.

The reading. Elsewhere in the space the rank is a coarse invariant and the spectrum is fine. On the carrier they coincide, and this is why the carrier is the natural base point for the stratified picture of the whole space.

The Jump of the Rank

Theorem. As the multiplier moves along the carrier the rank is constant on each of the two punctured pieces and jumps once, at the origin.

Proof. The rank is sixteen on the complement of the origin and zero at the origin, so there is exactly one point of discontinuity.

Corollary. The jump is downwards by sixteen at the origin and upwards by sixteen on the way back, and there is no other discontinuity.

Proof. The two values of the rank.

Corollary. The rank is not continuous on the carrier, and no continuous function of the multiplier reproduces it.

Proof. A continuous function on a connected punctured line extends across the origin, and the rank does not.

The reading. One jump, at one point, of size the dimension of the space. The carrier is the simplest example of a rank function with a single discontinuity, and it is the model for the jumps on the other carriers.

The Realification of the Eight Derived Structures

Introduction

The eight derived structures are the symmetric and the antisymmetric halves of the four general products of The Four General Products of the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space: the symmetric plain, symmetric quaternionic, antisymmetric plain and antisymmetric quaternionic algebra and sesqualgebra. Each is an algebraic structure on the same complex space $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}$, and each carries its own product, its own forms and its own operators.

The construction of the realification is not repeated here: the realification of a vector space, of a linear map and of a form, the doubling of the dimension, the complex structure and the table of the four effects of the change of scalars are The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars. What this article collects, one structure to a section, is what is specific to each of the eight: the real reading of its product, the kind of its second slot over $\mathbb{R}$, the realified form it carries with its signature, and its real operators. The three general structures, whose products are not symmetrised, are the subject of The Realification of the General Plain Sesqualgebra, The Realification of the General Quaternionic Algebra and The Realification of the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

Every structure below is read over $\mathbb{R}$ on the eight-dimensional real space $\mathbb{R}^8$ of basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z,ie_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z$, and $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ is the centre.

The Symmetric Plain Algebra (SPA)

The Realified Form

The product carries the general plain bilinear $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ form, the scalar part of the general product of its family, quoted from The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space: $$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=Q_{1t}Q_{2t}-(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2) $$ Over $\mathbb{C}$ it is a symmetric bilinear form; a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form over $\mathbb{C}$ is classified by its rank alone, so its realification is not the doubling of a complex inertia but the split pair described in §The Realification of a Form of The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars: the real part $\operatorname{Re}\Phi$ and the imaginary part $\operatorname{Im}\Phi$ are two real symmetric bilinear forms, and on the identification $\mathbb{R}^8$ the realification has signature $$ (4,4), $$ on the basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z,ie_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z$. On the realifications the complex structure reverses the form, $$ \Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(J\tilde Q_1,J\tilde Q_2)=-\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2), $$ which is the same statement as the two opposite blocks $\mathrm{D}$ and $-\mathrm{D}$ of its Gram matrix. The instance is the row general plain bilinear $B$ of the table of The Realification of the Four Forms, §The Four Gram Matrices.

The Real Operators

The left and the right multiplications by an element are the maps $$ L_{\tilde Q_1}:\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q_1\circ\tilde Q_2,\qquad R_{\tilde Q_1}:\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q_2\circ\tilde Q_1, $$ and over $\mathbb{R}$ they are $\mathbb{R}$-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}^8$, by §The Realification of a Linear Map of The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars. Because the second slot is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-linear, the left multiplication is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-linear as well, and its real trace is twice the real part of its complex trace, $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}(L_{\tilde Q_1})=8\operatorname{Re}\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1)$.

The Symmetric Plain Sesqualgebra (SPS)

The Realified Form

The product carries the general plain sesquilinear, the Hermitian $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ form, the scalar part of the general product of its family, quoted from The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space: $$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*}\bigr)=Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}+(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2}). $$ Over $\mathbb{C}$ it is a Hermitian form of complex inertia $(4,0)$; the realification of a Hermitian form is not split but the doubling of its inertia, by §The Realification of a Form of The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars: the form is real on the diagonal, the realification keeps its real part $\operatorname{Re}H$, and the realified signature is $$ (8,0), $$ with Gram matrix $\mathrm{I}_8$ on the basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z,ie_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z$. The discarded imaginary part is not a second independent form: it is the alternating companion of the Hermitian structure, the Kähler form, and it is determined by the real part through $\operatorname{Im}\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\operatorname{Re}\langle\tilde Q_1,i\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}$, so that a Hermitian form needs one real form where a bilinear form needs two. On the realifications the complex structure preserves the form, $$ \Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(J\tilde Q_1,J\tilde Q_2)=+\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2), $$ the opposite of the bilinear case. The instance is the general plain sesquilinear form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, the Hermitian form, whose row in the table of The Realification of the Four Forms, §The Four Gram Matrices, is the Hermitian one. Because the block's product is exactly this form read as a multiplication, the form's realification is also the realification of the product: the signature $(8,0)$, the Gram matrix $\mathrm{I}_8$ and the null set $\{0\}$ belong to the block as much as to the form it carries, and they are the definite row of the four.

The Real Operators

The left and the right multiplications by an element are the maps $$ L_{\tilde Q_1}:\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2,\qquad R_{\tilde Q_1}:\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q_2\star\tilde Q_1, $$ and over $\mathbb{R}$ they are $\mathbb{R}$-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}^8$, by §The Realification of a Linear Map of The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars. By the criterion of the previous section, $L_{\tilde Q_1}$ is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-conjugate-linear, of the anticommuting shape and of real trace zero, while $R_{\tilde Q_1}$ is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-linear, of the commuting shape; their traces are the ones of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Plain Sesqualgebra, $\operatorname{Tr}(L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_1})=Q_{1t}$ in the conjugate-linear convention and $\operatorname{Tr}(R^{\star}_{\tilde Q_1})=\overline{Q_{1t}}$, so that as real-linear endomorphisms they have traces $0$ and $2\operatorname{Re}Q_{1t}$, the second twice the real part of the complex trace.

The trace form of the real regular representation is the symmetric bilinear form $$ \tau(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}\bigr), $$ the analogue for this block of $\operatorname{Tr}(L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2})$ of the general plain algebra. The composition law $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_1}L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_2}=Q_{1t}R^{\star}_{\tilde Q_2}$ of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Plain Sesqualgebra evaluates it: $L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}=Q_{1t}R_{\tilde Q_2}$ has complex trace $Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}$, so that $$ \tau(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}\bigr). $$ The trace form is degenerate: its radical is the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, of real dimension six, its rank is two, and it sees the real and the imaginary parts of the central coordinate $Q_t$ alone. in the block's own terms it is the conjugate-linear map $\tilde S\mapsto\overline{S_t}\tilde Q_1$, which is neither multiplication, as The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Plain Sesqualgebra computes.

What Is Specific to This Product Read over $\mathbb{R}$

Four objects of the real reading are proper to the Hermitian block.

The real subalgebras that are not complex. Over $\mathbb{C}$ a subalgebra is a complex subspace, of even real dimension; over $\mathbb{R}$ there are subalgebras that no complex reading produces, because the product is central-valued and a real subspace closed under it need not be a complex subspace. The quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{R}\{e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$ and the Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_{+}=\mathbb{R}\{e_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$ are two real forms of real dimension four, and $$ \mathbb{R}\{e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y\} $$ is a real subalgebra of real dimension three: the odd dimension is the mark of the real reading. The vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ is not a subalgebra, because $\vec e_x\star \vec e_x=\langle \vec e_x,\vec e_x\rangle_{*}e_t=e_t$ leaves it; closure is a condition on the product, not on the coordinate span.

The Galois-twisted automorphisms. The coefficientwise conjugation $c=\overline{\cdot}$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-linear algebra automorphism of the block, because $\overline{\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2}=\overline{\tilde Q_1}\star\overline{\tilde Q_2}$, an identity which is conjugate-commutativity read on the involution, and $c$ is not $\mathbb{C}$-linear, since it induces the Galois conjugation $\kappa$ on the centre. Hence the real automorphism group of the block is twice the complex one, $$ \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{R}}(\star)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\star)\cup c\,\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\star), $$ the extra coset conjugate-linear: the complex automorphisms are the elements of $G_H$ fixing $e_t$ (The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Plain Sesqualgebra), and the passage to $\mathbb{R}$ adds the Galois twist of $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}$.

The real-quaternion coincidence. On the real quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ the block coincides with the symmetric quaternionic algebra $\mathrm{SQA}$ of The Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space: $$ \tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2=\mathrm{SQA}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)\qquad(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}), $$ because on the real quaternions the Hermitian form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ agrees with the quaternionic form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and the vector part of the quaternionic product is antisymmetric, hence killed by the symmetrisation. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the two structures are distinct on the whole space; the real reading makes them meet on a real form, and the coincidence is a statement of the real reading and not of the products.

The trivial null set against the punctured zero-divisor set. The realified form is positive definite, so the block's null set — the isotropic set of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ — is the origin alone, and the block has no isotropic line and no hyperbolic plane (The Realification of the Four Forms). The zero-divisor set of the ring is the complement of the origin, every nonzero element being a zero divisor because the form is non-degenerate and its orthogonal hyperplanes are nonzero (Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space). The two sets are the two extremes of $\mathbb{R}^8$, one point and its complement, and they are disjoint: the diagonal of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ is definite. Over an indefinite sibling the realified form has signature $(4,4)$ and a null set of real dimension seven, a genuine cone instead of a point; the definiteness of the Hermitian diagonal is what reduces the null set to the origin.

Instance: the Symmetric Plain Sesqualgebra

Two statements of the real reading are specific to the block. The product read over $\mathbb{R}$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-algebra on $\mathbb{R}^8$ in which conjugate-commutativity still holds and the square is the central element $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_1\rangle_{*}e_t=\lVert\tilde Q_1\rVert_E^{2}e_t$, real and positive off the origin; associativity fails at the triple $(\vec e_x,\vec e_x,e_t)$, the Jordan identity fails with the witness $x=y=\vec e_x$, and there is no unit. Every ring-theoretic property is unchanged: the idempotents stay $\{0,e_t\}$, the nilpotents stay the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})=\{Q_{1t}=0\}$ of index three, the zero divisors stay the complement of the origin, and the root of $-1$ stays absent. The second slot is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-conjugate-linear, so the left multiplications anticommute with $J$, $L_{\tilde Q_1}J=-JL_{\tilde Q_1}$, and carry the block shape $\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ B&-A\end{pmatrix}$, while the right multiplications commute, $R_{\tilde Q_1}J=JR_{\tilde Q_1}$, and carry the shape $\begin{pmatrix}A&-B\\ B&A\end{pmatrix}$; the block is the instance in which the two shapes are carried by the two halves of one operator family.

The Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra (SQA)

The Realified Form

The form carried by the product is the quaternion form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, and its realification is computed in the block and quoted here. In the real basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z,ie_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z$ the realified form is $$ \operatorname{diag}(\mathrm{I}_4,-\mathrm{I}_4), $$ of signature $(4,4)$ and index four, and the complex structure reverses the realification, $$ \Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(J\tilde Q_1,J\tilde Q_2)=-\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2), $$ which is the statement of the two opposite blocks of the Gram matrix; the verification is The Quaternion Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, §The Realification and the Signature. The restriction to the quaternion subspace is positive definite, of signature $(4,0)$, the restriction to the anti-quaternion subspace is negative definite, of signature $(0,4)$, and the two Hermitian subspaces carry the two real null cones (Remarkable Subspaces under the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra).

The isotropic set $\{N=0\}$ has complex dimension three and real dimension six, and its nonzero elements are exactly the square-zero elements of the block and the zero-divisor set that the four general products share. That set is strictly smaller than the zero-divisor set of the block itself, which is everything but the origin, so the isotropic cone, the square-zero elements and the block's zero divisors are three different sets and are kept apart (Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space). A fourth set is distinguished from all three: the null cone of the realified form, the real hypersurface $\{\operatorname{Re}N=0\}$ of dimension seven, which contains the complex isotropic cone and is the zero set of the real form and not the square-zero set of the block (The Realification of the Four Forms, §The Realified Null Cones).

The Real Operators

The product is commutative, so the left and the right multiplication by an element coincide, $$ L_{\tilde Q_6}\tilde Q_5=R_{\tilde Q_6}\tilde Q_5=\langle\tilde Q_6,\tilde Q_5\rangle_{\natural}\,e_t, $$ and both are $\mathbb{R}$-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}^8$. In the complex basis the matrix of $L_{\tilde Q_6}$ carries the four coordinates of $\tilde Q_6$ in its first row and carries zero elsewhere; for $\tilde Q_6\ne0$ the complex rank is one and the real rank is two, the image is the central line $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ — the same real plane for every nonzero $\tilde Q_6$ — and the kernel is the hyperplane $\{\tilde Q_5:\langle\tilde Q_6,\tilde Q_5\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, of real dimension six. The passage to $\mathbb{R}$ doubles the rank of a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map and turns the complex line of the image into a real plane.

Proposition (the two traces). For every $\tilde Q_6$, $$ \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(L_{\tilde Q_6}\bigr)=Q_{6t}=\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde Q_6),\qquad \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(L_{\tilde Q_6}\bigr)=2\operatorname{Re}Q_{6t}=2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde Q_6), $$ and the determinant vanishes in both readings.

Proof. Only the entry $(0,0)=Q_{6t}$ of the complex matrix is diagonal, so the complex trace is $Q_{6t}$; the real trace of the realification of a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map is twice the real part of its complex trace, and the determinant vanishes because the rank is one on a complex space of dimension four (The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, §The Operators, Their Matrices and Their Ranks).

The factor is two, not the four of the regular representation of the plain algebra and not the eight of its realification. The reason is that the multiplication of this block is the rank-one layer: the trace sees the scalar part of the multiplier and nothing else, the vector part being invisible to it, so the trace form of the block is not of the kind The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars records for the plain product, where the trace of the composite is the nondegenerate form $8\operatorname{Re}B$. It is the trace of a composite, $$ \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(L_{\tilde Q_6}L_{\tilde Q_3}\bigr)=2\operatorname{Re}(Q_{6t}Q_{3t}), $$ a real symmetric bilinear form of rank two whose radical is the vector subspace; it is degenerate, the trace form of the rank-one operator layer and not of a unital algebra.

Proposition (composition and the adjoint). The multiplications compose by $$ L_{\tilde Q_6}\circ L_{\tilde Q_3}=Q_{6t}L_{\tilde Q_3},\qquad\text{so}\qquad L_{\tilde Q_6}^{2}=Q_{6t}L_{\tilde Q_6}, $$ the map $\tilde Q_6\mapsto L_{\tilde Q_6}$ is injective and is not multiplicative, and the adjoint with respect to the realified form is the realification of the complex adjoint, $$ \bigl(L_{\tilde Q_6}\bigr)^{\dagger}\tilde Q_2=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}Q_{6\mu} Q_{2t}\,e_\mu , $$ which is self-adjoint exactly for a central multiplier. Every statement is the one of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, §Composition and the Algebra of Operators and §The Adjoint with Respect to the Form, read in the real basis.

The group of linear maps preserving the form. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the maps preserving $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ form the complex group $G_N$ of complex dimension six. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the $\mathbb{R}$-linear maps preserving the realified form are the linear maps preserving the split form $\operatorname{diag}(\mathrm{I}_4,-\mathrm{I}_4)$ on $\mathbb{R}^8$, of real dimension twenty-eight, and this group contains the realification of $G_N$, of real dimension twelve, as the subgroup of the maps that commute with $J$. The real form-preserving group is therefore the larger one, because a real map preserving $\operatorname{Re}N$ need not commute with the complex structure (The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, §The Two Groups of Linear Maps).

What Is Specific to the Real Interpretation

Four features appear in the real reading of the block and not in its complex reading.

The real subalgebras that are not complex subspaces. Over $\mathbb{C}$ a subalgebra of the block is a complex subspace, hence of even real dimension. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and the Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ are subalgebras: the coefficient $N$ is real on each of them, so the product of two of their elements lies in the real line $\mathbb{R}e_t$ and stays inside. Neither is a complex subspace, since $ie_t$ leaves $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and $i\vec e_x$ leaves $\mathbb{M}_+$, so neither is a subalgebra of the complex reading. There are also subalgebras of odd real dimension: the span $$ \mathbb{R}\{e_t,\ \vec e_x+i\vec e_y,\ i\vec e_x-\vec e_y\} $$ is kept inside itself, because $e_t$ pairs to zero with the two other generators and those two are isotropic and pair to zero with each other, so every product of two of the three generators is $0$ or in $\mathbb{R}e_t$; its real dimension is three, and it has no complex counterpart. The staying inside and the leaving of the six remarkable subspaces are tabulated in Remarkable Subspaces under the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

The coincidence on the real part. On the real subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{R}e_t+\mathbb{R}\vec e_x+\mathbb{R}\vec e_y+\mathbb{R}\vec e_z$ the symmetric quaternionic product and the symmetric plain sesquilinear product agree, $\mathrm{SQA}=\mathrm{SPS}$, because on real elements the Hermitian form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ equals the quaternion form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$; off that subspace they differ. The twelve operations of the complex space therefore collapse to ten on the real part, and the block is not distinguished there from the row $\mathrm{SPS}$ (Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, §The Placement Among the Twelve; Introduction to the Symmetric Plain Sesqualgebra).

The trace factor. The complex trace of the multiplication is the scalar part $Q_{6t}$ and the real trace is $2\operatorname{Re}Q_{6t}$; the factor two is the doubling of the realification, and the absence of the factors four and eight that the regular representation of the plain algebra carries is the rank-one nature of the block, a feature that the real reading shows and the complex reading hides.

The conjugate-linear automorphisms. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the automorphisms of the block are the $\mathbb{C}$-linear ones, and the natural conjugation is among them, because the block is commutative with central values. The star and the Hermitian conjugation preserve the product as well, and they are conjugate-linear; since the block is commutative, they are automorphisms of the $\mathbb{R}$-algebra and not of the $\mathbb{C}$-algebra, so the real automorphism group carries the extra conjugate-linear coset of the complex one, which the complex reading does not see. The block's article calls them conjugations of the block for this reason (Norm and Invertibility in the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra).

Instance: the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra

The change of scalars. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the same product on the same set is an $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear product on $\mathbb{R}^8=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z,ie_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$, with the complex structure $J=L_i$, $\mathbb{R}$-linear and $\mathbb{C}$-linear, of square $-\mathrm{id}$. The dictionary fixes everything read from the ring alone — commutativity, the failure of associativity, of power-associativity, of the Jordan and Jacobi identities, the centrality of the value, the absence of a unit, the idempotents, the nilpotents and the zero divisors — while the dimension moves from complex four to real eight; the witness of the failure of associativity, $\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_2=\vec e_x$, $\tilde Q_5=e_t$, lies in the real span and serves in both readings. The image, the central line over $\mathbb{C}$, is the real plane spanned by $e_t$ and $ie_t$, of real dimension two.

The real algebra and its centre. The operation is commutative, non-associative, non-power-associative, and satisfies neither the Jordan nor the Jacobi identity, and it has no one-sided unit. The coefficient $N$ is a symmetric $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear pairing of $\mathbb{R}^8\times\mathbb{R}^8$ into the centre $\mathbb{C}$, non-degenerate, with real part of rank eight and signature $(4,4)$, Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\mathrm{I}_4,-\mathrm{I}_4)$, and imaginary part the companion form of the same signature $(4,4)$, Gram matrix the swap block $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\mathrm{I}_4\\ \mathrm{I}_4&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; the two-sided annihilator of the product is $\{0\}$. The centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_t$ is a real subalgebra of real dimension two isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$, and $\mathbb{R}e_t$ a subalgebra of real dimension one.

The second slot. Read over $\mathbb{R}$ the second slot remains $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-linear, $L_\lambda J=JL_\lambda$ for a central $\lambda$, so the block is an algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ as it is over $\mathbb{C}$; no sesqualgebra appears, the only automorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ being the identity. In the real basis the matrix of the multiplication has the commuting block shape $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&-B\\ B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.

The Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra (SQS)

The Realified Form

The block carries the Krein form $$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2)=Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}-(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2}), $$ Hermitian of complex inertia $(1,3)$. The realification of a Hermitian form doubles its inertia, by §The Realification of a Form of The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars, so the form $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{Re}K$ is the real symmetric bilinear form on $\mathbb{R}^8$ of signature $$ (2,6),\qquad\text{Gram matrix }\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,1,-1,-1,-1), $$ and the complex structure preserves it, $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(J\tilde Q_1,J\tilde Q_2)=+\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)$. The instance is the row general quaternionic sesquilinear, the Krein $K$ of the table of The Realification of the Four Forms, §The Four Gram Matrices.

The compatibility of $K$ with the left multiplications realifies with the form. Over $\mathbb{C}$ it is $$ K\bigl(L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6}\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\bigr)=\overline{K\bigl(\tilde Q_1,L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6^{\natural}}\tilde Q_2\bigr)}, $$ a relation with a conjugation on the right. Taking real parts the conjugation disappears and the identity reads $$ \Phi_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6}\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\bigr)=\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\tilde Q_1,L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6^{\natural}}\tilde Q_2\bigr), $$ so $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6^{\natural}}$ is the $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}$-adjoint of $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6}$ in the ordinary sense of a real symmetric form: the adjoint relation survives the passage and loses its conjugation, and the block's operators are adjoint with respect to the indefinite form of signature $(2,6)$. The relation over $\mathbb{C}$ is the compatibility theorem of The Krein Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, quoted here and specialising the theorem of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

The Real Operators

The left and the right multiplications, $$ L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6}(\tilde Q_1)=\tilde Q_6\star\tilde Q_1,\qquad R^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6}(\tilde Q_1)=\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_6, $$ are $\mathbb{R}$-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}^8$, by §The Realification of a Linear Map of The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars, related by $L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6}\tilde Q_1=\overline{R^{\star}_{\tilde Q_6}\tilde Q_1}$. Their ranks are $0$ at $\tilde Q_6=0$, $4$ for $\tilde Q_6$ in the vector subspace, $8$ when $Q_{6t}\neq0$ and $\langle\tilde Q_6\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, and $6$ when $Q_{6t}\neq0$ and $\langle\tilde Q_6\rangle_{\natural}=0$; their traces are $0$ for the left family and $-4\operatorname{Re}(Q_{6t})$ for the right; and the image of a left multiplication by a vector element is a four-dimensional real subspace that is not one of the remarkable subspaces. These are the numbers of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, read in the real basis.

What Is Specific to the Real Interpretation

The trace form. The composite of two left multiplications is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-linear, so $$ \tau(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_1}L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_2}\bigr) $$ is a real symmetric bilinear form on $\mathbb{R}^8$, of signature $$ (8,0),\qquad\text{Gram matrix }\operatorname{diag}(8,4,4,4,8,4,4,4) $$ on $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z,ie_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z$. The trace of a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map is twice the real part of its complex trace, so $\tau=2\operatorname{Re}\Theta$ with $$ \Theta(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_1}L^{\star}_{\tilde Q_2}\bigr)=4Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}+2\sum_{\mu\in\{x,y,z\}}Q_{1\mu}\overline{Q_{2\mu}}, $$ a positive definite Hermitian form of complex inertia $(4,0)$. This is the trace form of the left regular representation of the block, the analogue of $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}(L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2})$ for the parent. The block therefore carries two real forms over $\mathbb{R}$, of opposite kinds: the indefinite $\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}$, of signature $(2,6)$, attached to the product, and the definite $\tau$, of signature $(8,0)$, attached to its left multiplications; the definite one is no multiple of the indefinite one.

The Galois twist. The four conjugations of the ring — the identity, the natural conjugation ${}^{\natural}$, the coefficientwise conjugation $\kappa$ and the Hermitian star ${}^{*}=\kappa\circ{}^{\natural}$ — are $\mathbb{R}$-linear and are automorphisms of the block, $$ T(\tilde Q_1\star\tilde Q_2)=T(\tilde Q_1)\star T(\tilde Q_2), $$ so the group of $\mathbb{R}$-algebra automorphisms of the block contains the Klein four group $\{\mathrm{id},{}^{\natural},\kappa,{}^{*}\}$. What Is Specific to the Real Interpretation

Four features appear in the real reading of the block and not in its complex reading.

The real subalgebras that are not complex subspaces. Over $\mathbb{C}$ a subalgebra of the block is a complex subspace, hence of even real dimension, and the complex reading sees only those. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the quaternion subspace $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{R}\{e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$, of real dimension four, is a subalgebra (Remarkable Subspaces under the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Biquaternions, §The Quaternion Subspace), and its image $$ \mathbb{R}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}=\bigl\{\tilde Q=\vec Q,\ \vec Q\ \text{with real coefficients}\bigr\} $$ is a subalgebra as well, of real dimension three: on the vector subspace the operation is the negative of the cross product (Remarkable Subspaces under the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Biquaternions, §The Vector Subspace), so the value of two real pure vectors is a real pure vector again. Neither of the two is a complex subspace, the element $i\vec e_x$ leaving both, and the three-dimensional one has no counterpart in the complex reading at all. Closedness is not automatic for a real subspace: $\mathbb{R}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y\}$ is not a subalgebra, since $\vec e_x\diamond \vec e_y=-\vec e_z$ leaves it; and the anti-quaternion, Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces fail closure, each with its own smallest witness (Remarkable Subspaces under the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Biquaternions). The general statement is the subspace row of the table of the four effects: a real subspace closed under the product need not be closed under $J$, so the real reading has more subalgebras than the complex one (The Realification and the Effect of the Change of Scalars, §The Four Effects on a Structure).

The complex reading shows the trace and hides its real factor; the real reading shows both.

The conjugate-linear automorphism. The coefficientwise conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$ is an automorphism of the block over $\mathbb{R}$: it is $\mathbb{R}$-linear, and the structure constants of the operation are real, so $$ \overline{\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2}=\bar{\tilde Q_1}\diamond\bar{\tilde Q_2} . $$ It is conjugate-linear, so it is not an automorphism over $\mathbb{C}$, and composing it with the $\mathbb{C}$-linear automorphism group (The Adjoint Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, §The Automorphism Group and the Structure Group) gives the conjugate-linear coset $\bar{\cdot}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}$ of the real automorphism group, which the complex reading does not see. A form $\beta$ is invariant under the block when $$ \beta\bigl(\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2,\tilde Q_5\bigr)=-\overline{\beta\bigl(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\diamond\tilde Q_5\bigr)} $$ for all $\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2,\tilde Q_5$, and in each of the four homogeneous classes the invariant forms are a central line, of real dimension two, spanned by the central form $\beta_0(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}$ or $Q_{1t}Q_{2t}$ according to the class; the enlarged class of all real-bilinear invariants has real dimension eight, the direct sum of the four central lines. Every invariant is central and the passage adds nothing to that space: the invariance condition is a statement about the ring and the realification changes neither the space nor its dimension. The whole of the computation is The Sesquilinear Pairing of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

The Real Part and the Subalgebras

On the quaternion subspace $$ \mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}, $$ of real dimension four, the block coincides with the antisymmetric plain algebra: for two real vector parts the conjugation is the identity, so $$ \tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2=\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2=\mathrm{APA}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2), $$ the ordinary cross product of the real vector parts. The coincidence holds because the conjugation of a real vector is the identity, and it disappears as soon as one argument leaves $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ for a direction in $i\mathbb{R}^{3}$.

The count of ten. The coincidence, with its symmetric companion — the symmetric quaternionic product coinciding with the symmetric plain sesquilinear product on $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ — is the reason the comment of The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space records a count of ten. On the four-dimensional real part the twelve operations reduce to ten, because the pair $(\mathrm{AQS},\mathrm{APA})$ and the pair $(\mathrm{SQA},\mathrm{SPS})$ each collapse to one operation there, the remaining eight staying distinct. The caution of the corpus applies: a computation restricted to the real part can hide the obstruction that separates the two members of a coincident pair, and twelve distinct operations require the full eight-dimensional real space.

Proposition (the closure criterion). A real subspace $W$ of $\mathbb{R}^8$ is a subalgebra of the block exactly when $\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2\in W$ for all $\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\in W$. Every value of the block lies in the vector subspace, so the condition reads $$ \tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2\in W\cap\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})\qquad\text{for all }\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\in W: $$ a subspace is closed exactly when its intersection with the vector subspace receives all the values of its pairs.

Proof. The first sentence is the definition. For the second, the value is $\vec Q\times\overline{\vec Q_2}$, a pure vector, so the membership of the value in $W$ is the membership of the value in $W\cap\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$.

The Real Operators

Definition. The left and the right multiplications by an element are the maps $$ L_{\tilde Q_1}:\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2=\vec Q_1\times\overline{\vec Q_2}, \qquad R_{\tilde Q_1}:\tilde Q_2\mapsto\tilde Q_2\diamond\tilde Q_1=\vec Q_2\times\overline{\vec Q_1}, $$ and over $\mathbb{R}$ they are $\mathbb{R}$-linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}^8$.

Theorem (the class and the traces). The left multiplication is conjugate-linear over $\mathbb{C}$, of the anticommuting block shape and of vanishing real trace, the two diagonal blocks being opposite; the right multiplication is $\mathbb{C}$-linear, of the commuting block shape and of vanishing trace. For a non-central element both have real rank $4$ and nullity $4$, the kernel of the left multiplication is the complex plane $$ \ker L_{\tilde Q_1}=\mathbb{C}\overline{\vec Q_1}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_t,\qquad\text{of real dimension }4, $$ and its image is the complex plane of the values orthogonal to the vector part in the complex bilinear dot, $(\vec W,\tilde Q_1)=0$, the equation $\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_5=\tilde W$ being solvable exactly there; for a central element both multiplications vanish.

Proof. The class and the two shapes are the theorem of §The Second Slot and the Centre; the vanishing of the trace of a conjugate-linear map is the opposite diagonal blocks. The rank, the kernel and the image are computed in The Adjoint Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and in Zero Divisors in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, on the $64$ basis pairs; the vanishing of the trace of $R$ is the trace of a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map, twice the real part of its complex trace, and the complex trace is zero because $\vec e_\mu\mapsto \vec e_\mu\times\overline{\vec Q_1}$ has the two skew blocks of the cross product alone.

Theorem (the operator trace form in the two readings). For all $\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2$, $$ \kappa(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}\bigr)=-2\,(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2})=2\bigl(\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}-Q_{1t}\overline{Q_{2t}}\bigr), $$ the composition being $\mathbb{C}$-linear because it is the product of two conjugate-linear maps; $\kappa$ is a Hermitian form of complex inertia $(0,3)$ with the centre as radical, and its realification is the real operator trace form $$ \kappa_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\operatorname{Re}\kappa(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=-2\operatorname{Re}\,(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2}), \qquad \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}\bigr)=2\,\kappa_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2), $$ a real symmetric bilinear form of real rank $6$ and signature $(0,6)$, negative semidefinite with the centre as radical, equal to twice the restriction of $K_{\mathbb{R}}$ to the vector subspace.

Proof. The composition $L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}$ is the map $\tilde Q_5\mapsto\vec Q_1\times\overline{\vec Q_2\times\overline{\vec Q_5}}$, and the two conjugations cancel, so it is $\mathbb{C}$-linear and has a complex trace; on the complex basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ its matrix has trace $-2(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2})$, computed on the $64$ basis pairs. The real trace of a $\mathbb{C}$-linear endomorphism is twice the real part of its complex trace, which is the factor $2$ and the same factor that relates the two readings of the trace of the left multiplication of the general plain product, $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}(L_{\tilde Q_1})=8\operatorname{Re}\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1)$ against $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}}(L_{\tilde Q_1})=4\,\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1)$. The Hermitian form $-2(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2})$ has the three vector coordinates negative and the scalar coordinate absent, hence inertia $(0,3)$ and radical the centre; a Hermitian form of inertia $(0,3)$ realifies to $(0,6)$ by the second rule of the realification of a form, and on the vector subspace $\kappa_{\mathbb{R}}=2K_{\mathbb{R}}$.

The element trace form, and the distinction. The element trace form of the block, $$ \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2\bigr)=2\,\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2\bigr), $$ vanishes identically, because the block is pure vector; the vanishing is the form side of the absence of a unit and of the pure-vector image, and it is The Sesquilinear Pairing of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra. The operator trace form $\kappa_{\mathbb{R}}$ does not vanish: the two objects differ, one being the trace of the product and the other the trace of the composition of the two left multiplications, and only the second is a nondegenerate form on the vector subspace. The adjoints with respect to $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$, the derivations (of real dimension $7$) and the structure group of the block are The Adjoint Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, whose statements are of the real-linear kind and hold unchanged.

The general construction. The reading is the instance of the operator trace form of the real biquaternion algebra. For the plain product that form is $$ \tau(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(L_{\tilde Q_1}L_{\tilde Q_2}\bigr)=8\operatorname{Re}\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(L_{\tilde Q_1})=8\operatorname{Re}\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1), $$ real symmetric of signature $(4,4)$, with the general factor $2$ between the real and the complex trace, $8=2\cdot4$; the same factor appears here as $\kappa_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{Re}\kappa$ of $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{R}}=2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}}$. What the block changes is the left multiplication, conjugate-linear in place of linear: the signature $(4,4)$ becomes the negative semidefinite $(0,6)$ and the trivial radical becomes the centre. the block shares the reality of its operator theory and not the Cartan structure, its operator calculus being conjugate-linear at every step.

Instance: the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra

The block is the vector half of the split of the general plain sesquilinear product, $$ \tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*}=\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}e_t+\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_2, $$ the scalar half being the product of the symmetric plain sesqualgebra; its scalar part therefore vanishes and it carries no form of its own. Over $\mathbb{C}$ it is a sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{C},\varsigma)$ with $\varsigma={}^{*}$ on the centre; over $\mathbb{R}$ the involution disappears, the product becomes an $\mathbb{R}$-algebra, and the passage changes the kind of the structure, the distinction migrating from the field to the centre. The criterion of $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-conjugate-linearity is the anticommutation $LJ=-JL$ of the left multiplication by a central element with the complex structure.

Summary

Each of the eight derived structures is the same product on the same elements read over $\mathbb{R}$: an $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear operation on $\mathbb{R}^8$ whose second slot is described by the centre $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$, carrying the realification of the form of its family with the signature recorded in its section, and acted on by the real left and right multiplications. The passage changes the field, the dimension and the linear structure; it adds the real reading of the form of each structure and of its operators.

Instance: the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Read over $\mathbb{R}$ the block is the same product on $\mathbb{R}^8$, and every ring-theoretic property is unchanged: conjugate-commutativity, the failure of associativity at the triple $(e_t,e_t,\vec e_x)$, the failure of the Jordan identity at $x=y=\vec e_x$, the absence of a unit, and the fact that the values are not confined to any one of the remarkable subspaces, their real span being the whole $\mathbb{R}^8$. Instance: the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Read over $\mathbb{R}$ the product is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear on $\mathbb{R}^8=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z,ie_t,i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$, and its ring theory does not move: conjugate-alternation, the Jacobi failure with the witness $(\vec e_x,\vec e_x,i\vec e_y)$ whose cyclic sum is $-2i\vec e_y$ — the only one of the twelve whose failure needs an element outside the quaternion subspace — the absence of a unit, the vanishing of the scalar part, the idempotent set the origin alone, every nonzero element a two-sided zero divisor, the nilpotent indices two and four, and an empty group of units. The two images are the six real dimensions of the product, $\mathrm{im}(\diamond)=\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z\}$, and the three real dimensions of the square, $\{\tilde Q_1\diamond\tilde Q_1\}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{i\vec e_x,i\vec e_y,i\vec e_z\}$; the diagonal is $\tilde Q\diamond\tilde Q=\vec Q\times\overline{\vec Q}=-2i\,\vec a\times\vec b$ at $\vec Q=\vec a+i\vec b$, hence $-2i\vec e_z$ at $\vec e_x+i\vec e_y$, and it vanishes exactly on the complex multiples of the real directions.

The involution disappears over $\mathbb{R}$, so the sesqualgebra becomes an $\mathbb{R}$-algebra whose second slot is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$-conjugate-linear. The two slots carry opposite linearities, and the two block shapes are the two halves of it: $$ L_{\tilde Q_1}J=-JL_{\tilde Q_1}\quad\text{with}\quad\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ B&-A\end{pmatrix}, \qquad R_{\tilde Q_1}J=JR_{\tilde Q_1}\quad\text{with}\quad\begin{pmatrix}A&-B\\ B&A\end{pmatrix}. $$ Both operators vanish in real trace; for a non-central element both have real rank $4$ and nullity $4$, the kernel of the left multiplication being $\ker L_{\tilde Q_1}=\mathbb{C}\overline{\vec Q_1}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_t$ of real dimension four.

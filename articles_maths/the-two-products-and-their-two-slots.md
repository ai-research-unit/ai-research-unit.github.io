# __The Two Products and Their Two Slots: the Algebra and the Sesqualgebra__

## Introduction

The four general products are four fillings of two slots, and this article is the same construction read on the complex space, where the first slot is empty and the second has two values. The result is the synthesis of the chapter: the two products $AC$ and $A\bar C$ of *The Two Products of the Complex Space* are the two surviving settings of the construction, the two structures they define are the field and the sesqualgebra, and every distinction of the four-product picture collapses to a single distinction — which of the identity and the bar occupies the second slot.

The article is a synthesis and it proves nothing twice. The products and the collapse are *The Two Products of the Complex Space* and *The Collapse of the Twelve Structures*; the field is *The Specificities of the Complex Algebra*; the classes of element are *The Classes of Element of the Complex Algebra*; the sesquilinear product and its operators are *The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space*; the two forms and their diagonals are *The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Complex Space*; the positivity is *The Hermitian Form and the Positivity of the Complex Algebra*. The only computations the article adds are the two adjoints of §*The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints*, and which are worked here on the complex space.

**Interpretation and notation.** The element is $A=a+ia'$; the base involution is the bar $\bar A=a-ia'$; ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}$. The four forms are $B=N=AC$ and $H=K=A\bar C$; the two algebraic norms are $B(A)=A^{2}$ and $H(A)=A\bar A$. The ordinary left multiplication of the field is written $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}(X)=AX$; the sesquilinear left and right multiplications are $L_{A}(X)=A\bar X$ and $R_{A}(X)=X\bar A$ of *The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space*. The transpose for the bilinear form is written ${}^{\mathrm T}$ and the adjoint for the Hermitian form ${}^{\dagger}$.

## The Two Slots

**Definition.** The **general product of two slots** on an algebra with an involution is

$$
x\star_{K}y=x\,K(y),
$$

where $K$ is the identity or the Hermitian conjugation, the first slot carrying the conjugation of the coefficient ring, which is the identity here.

**Theorem (the settings on $\mathbb{C}$).** On the complex field the first slot carries the identity, so only the second slot switches and the two settings are:

$$
\star_{\mathrm{id}}=AC, \qquad \star_{\bar{\cdot}}=A\bar C .
$$

The two surviving products are the multiplication of the field and the sesquilinear product.

**Proof.** The conjugation of the coefficient ring fixes the scalars and negates the vectors; on $\mathbb{C}$ the scalars are the whole algebra, so it is the identity, the first slot acts trivially, and the second alone switches, giving the two products of the display.

**Theorem (the rule of the second slot).** The second slot decides the composition: with $K=\mathrm{id}$ the product is associative and commutative and has a two-sided unit, and with $K=\bar{\cdot}$ it is neither, it is conjugate-commutative and has a right unit alone. The same slot decides the symmetry of the form the product carries: $AC$ is symmetric, $A\bar C$ is conjugate-symmetric.

**Proof.** These are the property tables of *The Two Products of the Complex Space* and *Relations and Comparison Between the Two Products*; the associativity and unit statements are *The Specificities of the Complex Algebra*, §*The Consequences*, and the sesquilinear statements are *The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space*, §*The Product and Its Class*.

**Remark (the empty first slot).** On $\mathbb{C}$ the four blocks collapse two-and-two, and the article *The Collapse of the Twelve Structures* carries the count. What survives of the first slot here is only its role as the **centre**: the complex field is its own centre, so the two complex products are the general products read on the centre, and the first-slot collapse is the statement that the centre is fixed by the conjugation of the coefficient ring.

## The Two Structures

**The algebra.** The setting $K=\mathrm{id}$ gives the field: associative, commutative, unital, without zero divisors, in which every nonzero element is invertible. Its classes of element are the consolidated catalogue of the chapter, and its form is the symmetric $B$. The structure is *The Specificities of the Complex Algebra*.

**The sesqualgebra.** The setting $K=\bar{\cdot}$ gives the sesquilinear structure over $(\mathbb{C},\bar{\cdot})$: $\mathbb{C}$-linear in the first slot and conjugate-linear in the second, non-associative, conjugate-commutative, with the right unit $1$ and no left unit; its associator is carried by the anti-self-adjoint part of the third slot alone and its ternary product satisfies the Jordan triple identity, both *The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space*. Its form is the conjugate-symmetric $H$. The structure is *The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space*.

**Remark (the two structures are the two slots of one ladder).** The field is the structure that carries the associative-unital theory; the sesqualgebra is the structure that carries the positivity theory. On $\mathbb{C}$ there is no third: the symmetrisations and the antisymmetrisations of the two slots collapse onto these two or onto the zero product, and the two are not nested, since the field's product is symmetric and the sesqualgebra's conjugate-symmetric.

## The Classes of Element in the Two Slots

**Theorem (the classes in the two slots).** Read in either slot, the complex field has no zero divisor, the annihilator of every nonzero element is $\{0\}$, the idempotents are $0$ and $1$, and the only square-zero element is $0$. The square alone reads differently. In the algebra slot the square-root set of a central value $W$ has two opposite elements for $W\neq0$ and the single element $0$ for $W=0$. In the sesqualgebra slot the square $Z\star Z=Z\bar Z$ is real, so the square-root set is empty for non-real or negative $W$, is the single element $0$ for $W=0$, and is the circle $\{Z:Z\bar Z=W\}$ for real $W>0$.

**Proof.** The common statements are *The Classes of Element of the Complex Algebra*, §*The Zero Divisors and the Annihilators*, §*The Idempotents* and §*The Nilpotents*. For the two square readings, $Z^{2}=W$ over the algebraically closed field has the two opposite roots for $W\neq0$ and the root $0$ for $W=0$, while $Z\bar Z=a^{2}+a'^{2}$ for $Z=a+ia'$, a real non-negative value, zero only at the origin and taking every positive value on a circle; the readings are §*The Roots of the Distinguished Values* and §*The Hermitian Square* of that article.

**Remark (the degenerate synthesis).** The complex synthesis is the degenerate case in which the root sets become two points, the origin, the empty set, and the circle of the sesquilinear square.

## The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints

**Theorem (the two pairings).** The two slots carry the two pairings of the complex space,

$$
B(A,C)=\mathrm{Sc}(AC)=AC, \qquad H(A,C)=\mathrm{Sc}(AC^{*})=A\bar C,
$$

the first bilinear and symmetric, the second sesquilinear and conjugate-symmetric, both non-degenerate with Gram matrix $[1]$ in the basis $1$. They coincide with the other two of the four forms, $B=N$ and $H=K$, because the first slot carries the identity.

**Proof.** The pairings are the scalars of the two products, and on $\mathbb{C}$ the scalar is the element; the Gram matrices are $B(1,1)=H(1,1)=1$. The coincidence $B=N$, $H=K$ is *The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Complex Space*.

**Definition.** For a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $F$, the **transpose** $F^{\mathrm T}$ is defined by $B(FX,Y)=B(X,F^{\mathrm T}Y)$ and the **adjoint** $F^{\dagger}$ by $H(FX,Y)=H(X,F^{\dagger}Y)$.

**Theorem (the two adjoints of the ordinary multiplication).** For the ordinary left multiplication $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}(X)=AX$ and the sesquilinear right multiplication $R_{A}(X)=X\bar A$,

$$
\bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}, \qquad \bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A},
$$

$$
\bigl(R_{A}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}, \qquad \bigl(R_{A}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}.
$$

The transpose keeps the parameter and the Hermitian adjoint conjugates it; the transpose and the adjoint of the same map therefore agree exactly when $A$ is self-adjoint, that is real.

**Proof.** For the first pair: $B(AX,Y)=AXY$ and $B(X,AY)=XAY$, equal by commutativity, so $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$ is its own transpose; and $H(AX,Y)=AX\bar Y$ while $H(X,\bar AY)=X\overline{\bar AY}=XA\bar Y$, equal, so the Hermitian adjoint is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}$. For the second pair: $B(X\bar A,Y)=X\bar AY$ and $B(X,\bar AY)=X\bar AY$, so the transpose is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}$; and $H(X\bar A,Y)=X\bar A\bar Y$ while $H(X,AY)=X\bar A\bar Y$, so the adjoint is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$. The agreement $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$ is exactly $A=\bar A$.

**Remark (the conjugate-linear family).** The left multiplication of the sesqualgebra, $L_{A}(X)=A\bar X$, is conjugate-linear, and with the conjugate convention $B(TX,Y)=\overline{B(X,T^{\mathrm T}Y)}$ its transpose for the bilinear form is $L_{\bar A}$, while its Hermitian adjoint in the convention of *The Sesquilinear Product of the Complex Space* is $L_{A}$; these were computed in §*The Sesquilinear Sandwich* of that article, together with the adjoint of the sandwich, $S_{P,Q}^{\dagger}=S_{\bar Q,\bar P}$. The two adjoints above are the complex reading of the four adjoints of the general theory.

## The Dictionary

The complex synthesis is set beside the general synthesis.

**Remark (why the dictionary is short).** Every line of the dictionary except the last is a collapse of the first slot: the first slot is trivial, so the bilinear forms coincide, the sesquilinear forms coincide, the symmetrisations of the two slots coincide with the slots themselves, and the antisymmetrisations vanish. The two adjoints are the two pairings $B$ and $H$ and not four, because the first-slot variants coincide with the plain ones. The complex space is the degenerate base of the ladder: at the complex rung the twelve structures are two.

## Summary

The four general products are the fillings of two slots, and on the complex field the first slot is empty, so that only the second slot survives and has two values: the identity, giving the field product $AC$, and the bar, giving the sesquilinear product $A\bar C$. The two structures are the field, associative and commutative with a two-sided unit and the symmetric form $B$, and the sesqualgebra, non-associative and conjugate-commutative with a right unit alone and the conjugate-symmetric form $H$. The classes of element agree in the two slots except at the square: the algebra square of a nonzero central value has two roots, the sesqualgebra square is real and has a circle of roots above $0$ and none for a non-real or negative value. The two pairings $B$ and $H$ carry two adjoints of a linear map, computed here: the transpose keeps the parameter and the Hermitian adjoint conjugates it, so the ordinary multiplication by $A$ has transpose itself and Hermitian adjoint the multiplication by $\bar A$, and the two agree exactly for real $A$. The same construction one rung up, where the first slot is alive, recovers the twelve structures.

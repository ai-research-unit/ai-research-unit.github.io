$M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Model : The Six Subspaces as Matrix Conditions

Introduction

The six remarkable subspaces of the biquaternion algebra are defined by the four conjugations and by the scalar–vector split. In the matrix model each definition becomes a matrix condition: the centre is the scalar matrices, the vector subspace is the traceless matrices, the quaternion subspace is a conjugate-linear pattern, the anti-quaternion subspace is $i$ times it, and the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces are the Hermitian and the anti-Hermitian matrices.

This article collects the six descriptions and their determinants. It is the matrix form of The Six Subspaces.

Conventions. The algebra and the model are those of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Representation : The Matrix Product and the Algebra Structure: $\tilde Q=Q_te_t+\vec Q$ with $Q_\mu=q_\mu+iq'_\mu$, the unprimed $q_\mu$ real and the primed $q'_\mu$ real. The conjugations are quaternion conjugation ${}^{\natural}$, complex conjugation $\bar{\cdot}$, Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}$ and anti-Hermitian conjugation ${}^{\flat}=-{}^{*}$. The four matrix basis images are

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(e_t)=\mathrm I_2,\quad \mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\-i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_z)=\begin{pmatrix}-i&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix}. $$

Boundaries. The subspaces and their relations are The Six Subspaces and The Lattice of the Subspaces; the four forms restricted to them are Remarkable Subspaces and the Four General Products, Forms and Norms; the polar action on them is The Polar Decomposition and Remarkable Subspaces; the matrix form of that action is The Sandwich in the $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Model.

The determinants are the values of $Q_t^2+B^2$ on the six subspaces, complex-valued on the centre and the vector subspace and real on the four real-type subspaces.

The Centre Subspace

The fixed space of quaternion conjugation.

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(Q_te_t)=Q_t\mathrm I_2=\begin{pmatrix}Q_t&0\\0&Q_t\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}q_t+iq'_t&0\\0&q_t+iq'_t\end{pmatrix}. $$

The image is the line of scalar matrices, of complex dimension one. The trace is $2Q_t$ and the determinant is $Q_t^2$; the determinant is the square of the scalar, and it vanishes exactly at the origin. The subspace is the only commutative one and the only one of real dimension two. Its two real parameters are the real and the imaginary part of the scalar coefficient.

The intersection with the other subspaces. The centre meets $\mathbb{M}_+$ in the real line $\mathbb{R}e_t$ and $\mathbb{M}_-$ in the imaginary line $\mathbb{R}(ie_t)$. The statement is the matrix form of the split of the centre into its Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts.

The Vector Subspace

The traceless matrices.

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)=\begin{pmatrix}-iQ_z&-iQ_x-Q_y\\-iQ_x+Q_y&iQ_z\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)=0. $$

The image is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, the traceless matrices, of complex dimension three. Its determinant is

$$ \det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)=Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2=B^2, $$

the determinant with the scalar term dropped. The subspace is the derived subspace $[\mathbb{B},\mathbb{B}]$ and is closed under the commutator; it is not closed under the product, because the product of two traceless matrices need not be traceless: for example $E_{12}E_{21}=E_{11}$ has trace one.

The two blocks. Writing $Q_k=q_k+iq'_k$, the real and imaginary parts of the vector subspace are the blocks of real and of imaginary coefficients, $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})=X_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus X_{i\mathbb{R}}$. The statement is the matrix form of the split of the traceless matrices into the real and imaginary parts of their entries.

The Quaternion Subspace

The real coefficients.

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(q_te_t+q_x\vec e_x+q_y\vec e_y+q_z\vec e_z) =\begin{pmatrix}q_t-iq_z&-iq_x-q_y\\-iq_x+q_y&q_t+iq_z\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}z&w\\-\bar w&\bar z\end{pmatrix}, $$

with $z=q_t-iq_z$ and $w=-iq_x-q_y$. The image is the set of matrices of the form $\begin{pmatrix}z&w\\-\bar w&\bar z\end{pmatrix}$, a complex-linear copy of $\mathbb{H}$ inside $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. The trace is $2q_t$, real, and the determinant is

$$ \det=|z|^2+|w|^2=q_t^2+q_x^2+q_y^2+q_z^2, $$

real, and zero only at the origin. The subspace is closed under multiplication and is a division algebra: every nonzero element is invertible, with inverse the conjugate divided by the determinant. The statement is the matrix form of the reality of the quaternion subspace.

The conjugate-linear pattern. The pattern $w\mapsto-\bar w$ is the matrix form of the quaternion conjugation: it is the unique conjugate-linear involution of $\mathbb{C}^2$ commuting with the left action. The quaternion subspace is the fixed space of complex conjugation, and its matrix image is the set of matrices commuting with that involution.

The Anti-Quaternion Subspace

The imaginary coefficients.

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(iq'_te_t+iq'_x\vec e_x+iq'_y\vec e_y+iq'_z\vec e_z) =i\begin{pmatrix}q'_t-iq'_z&-iq'_x-q'_y\\-iq'_x+q'_y&q'_t+iq'_z\end{pmatrix}. $$

The image is $i$ times the quaternion matrices, and the determinant is

$$ \det=-\big((q'_t)^2+(q'_x)^2+(q'_y)^2+(q'_z)^2\big), $$

real, and zero only at the origin. The subspace is not closed under multiplication. Together with the quaternion subspace it exhausts the algebra:

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}\oplus i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}, $$

the real-and-imaginary split, of two four-dimensional real summands.

The mirror. The two subspaces are exchanged by multiplication by the central $i$, and their determinants are opposite. The pair is the matrix form of the two real forms of the complex quaternion algebra: the division algebra and its imaginary multiple.

The Hermitian Subspace

The Hermitian matrices.

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(q_te_t+iq'_x\vec e_x+iq'_y\vec e_y+iq'_z\vec e_z) =\begin{pmatrix}q_t+q'_z&q'_x-iq'_y\\q'_x+iq'_y&q_t-q'_z\end{pmatrix}. $$

The image is the set of Hermitian matrices, of real dimension four. The trace is $2q_t$, real, and the determinant is

$$ \det=q_t^2-(q'_x)^2-(q'_y)^2-(q'_z)^2, $$

which takes both signs. The subspace is not closed under multiplication; it is closed under the anticommutator. The three non-identity basis images are the Pauli matrices:

$$ i\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_x)=\sigma_1,\qquad i\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_y)=\sigma_2,\qquad i\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_z)=\sigma_3. $$

The Anti-Hermitian Subspace

The anti-Hermitian matrices.

$$ \mathsf{M}_2(iq'_te_t+q_x\vec e_x+q_y\vec e_y+q_z\vec e_z) =i\begin{pmatrix}q'_t-q_z&-q_x+iq_y\\-q_x-iq_y&q'_t+q_z\end{pmatrix}. $$

The image is $i$ times the Hermitian matrices, of real dimension four. The trace is $2iq'_t$, purely imaginary, and the determinant is

$$ \det=-(q'_t)^2+q_x^2+q_y^2+q_z^2, $$

which takes both signs. The subspace is not closed under multiplication; it is a real Lie algebra under the commutator.

Why the anti-Hermitian determinant has the opposite sign. The determinant on a Hermitian matrix and the determinant on the anti-Hermitian matrix obtained from it by multiplication by $i$ are negatives on corresponding elements, because the factor $i$ contributes $i^2=-1$ to the determinant. The anti-Hermitian determinant is the one with the positive spatial part. The factor $i$ is the matrix form of the statement that the anti-Hermitian subspace is the fixed space of the reversal.

How They Fit Together

The three splits.

$$ \mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}\oplus\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B}) =\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}\oplus i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}} =\mathbb{M}_+\oplus\mathbb{M}_-. $$

The first is the trace decomposition, of dimensions two and six; the second and third are four-plus-four and neither refines the other. Each of these two subspaces takes its scalar from one half and its vectors from the other: the prime pattern of $\mathbb{M}_+$ is one real scalar and three imaginary vectors, and that of $\mathbb{M}_-$ is one imaginary scalar and three real vectors.

The subalgebras. The centre is closed under multiplication; the vector subspace is closed under the commutator but not under the product; the quaternion subspace is closed under multiplication; the anti-quaternion subspace and the two Hermitian subspaces are closed under neither. The statements are the matrix forms of the corresponding algebra statements, and they are read off the images above.

The involutions. The six subspaces are the fixed and anti-fixed spaces of the four involutions: the centre and the vector subspace for ${}^{\natural}$; the quaternion and anti-quaternion subspaces for $\bar{\cdot}$; the two Hermitian subspaces for ${}^{*}$ and ${}^{\flat}$. The matrix images are the scalar, traceless, conjugate-linear-pattern, $i$-pattern, Hermitian and anti-Hermitian conditions. The table at the head of the article is the complete list.

The real structure. The matrix model is a complex model, and it does not by itself distinguish $\mathbb{M}_+$ from $\mathbb{M}_-$. The distinction is the choice of the real form ${}^{\flat}$, and the matrix model displays it as the choice between the Hermitian and the anti-Hermitian matrices. The statement is the matrix form of the fact that the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces are exchanged by multiplication by $i$ and are not isomorphic.

Summary

The six remarkable subspaces are the scalar matrices, the traceless matrices, the matrices of the form $\begin{pmatrix}z&w\\-\bar w&\bar z\end{pmatrix}$, $i$ times them, the Hermitian matrices and $i$ times the Hermitian matrices. Their determinants are $Q_t^2$, $B^2$, $\lvert z\rvert^2+\lvert w\rvert^2$, its negative, $q_t^2-\lvert\vec q'\rvert^2$ and $-(q'_t)^2+\lvert\vec q\rvert^2$. The three splits are the trace decomposition and the two four-plus-four decompositions; the centre and the quaternion subspace are subalgebras, the vector subspace is a Lie subalgebra, and the two Hermitian subspaces are neither.

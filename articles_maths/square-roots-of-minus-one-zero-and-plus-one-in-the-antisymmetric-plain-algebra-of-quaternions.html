Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

The antisymmetric plain algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ carries the cross product $\tilde p\times\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p\tilde q-\tilde q\tilde p)=\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions. This article reads the three central root equations of the block — the roots of $-1$, of $0$ and of $+1$ — the special case $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=\lambda e_0$ of the square roots of a central value, as defined in the common apparatus of Definitions for the Study of the 6 Algebraic Structures.

The answer is forced by a single fact: the operation is alternating, so the square of every element is zero, and the three equations collapse at once. The block has no root of $-1$ and no root of $+1$, and its roots of $0$ are every nonzero element of the space.

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The two share the space, the addition and the real scalars, but $\times$ is a different multiplication, alternating and without a unit. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new.

The square and its polarisation are Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions; the zero divisors, which coincide with the roots of $0$, are Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, and the folding of the idempotents and of the nilpotents is The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, both below this article in this category. The roots of the other five products are read in the comparison at the end.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$ and $e_1^2=e_2^2=e_3^2=-e_0$. A generic element is $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\mathbf q$ with $\mathbf q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}q_ke_k$ and $q_\mu\in\mathbb{R}$; the centre is $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_0$ and the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\mathbf q\}$.

The Notion and the Product

Definition. Let $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$. A root of $\lambda$ in the block is a nonzero element $\tilde\xi$ with

$$ \tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=\lambda e_0 , $$

the central value $\lambda e_0$ being read in the space $\mathbb{H}$. The three values singled out are $-1$, $+1$ and $0$: the roots of $-1$ are the imaginary units, the roots of $+1$ the involutions, and the roots of $0$ the nonzero nilpotents of the block.

The Three Equations and the Reduction

Theorem (the reduction). The three equations reduce to the three scalar equations

$$ 0=-e_0,\qquad 0=0,\qquad 0=+e_0 , $$

and the root sets are

$$ \{\tilde\xi:\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=-e_0\}=\varnothing,\qquad \{\tilde\xi:\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0\}=\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\},\qquad \{\tilde\xi:\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=+e_0\}=\varnothing . $$

Proof. The operation is alternating, $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ for every $\tilde\xi$; the left side of each equation is therefore $0$, and the equations become $0=\lambda e_0$ for $\lambda=-1,0,+1$. The first and the third are impossible, the second is an identity, and the definition of a root excludes $\tilde\xi=0$ from the last set.

The Roots of Zero

Theorem. The roots of $0$ are all the nonzero elements,

$$ \tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0\iff\tilde\xi\neq0 , $$

the whole four-dimensional space with the origin removed. The root set of $0$ is the square-zero set and the zero-divisor set of the block, all three being the punctured space.

Proof. The square vanishes identically, so the equation is satisfied by every nonzero element and by no other; and an element is a two-sided zero divisor of an alternating product exactly when it is nonzero, with itself as partner (Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, below this article in this category).

Remark (the whole space, and not only the vector subspace). The root set of $0$ is the whole space, and not the vector subspace: the scalar coordinate does not enter the square, since $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ for a non-pure element as well. The set has real dimension four, and its elements are pairwise partners of themselves.

The Roots of Minus One and of Plus One

Theorem. The block has no root of $-1$ and no root of $+1$:

$$ \{\tilde\xi:\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=-e_0\}=\varnothing,\qquad \{\tilde\xi:\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=+e_0\}=\varnothing . $$

Proof. Either equation would read $0=\pm e_0$, a value with a nonzero scalar coordinate, against the pure value $0$ of the square. There is no exceptional element and no exceptional central value.

Remark (the failure is structural). The plain algebra has infinitely many roots of $-1$, the pure unit quaternions, because a pure square can be a negative scalar; the antisymmetric block has none, because a square is not a scalar at all but the zero element, whatever the coordinate of the alleged root. The absence is the same fact as the totality of the roots of $0$.

The Idempotents

Theorem. The idempotents of the block are only $0$:

$$ \tilde\pi\times\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi\iff\tilde\pi=0 . $$

Proof. By alternation the left-hand side is $0$ for every $\tilde\pi$, so the equation is $\tilde\pi=0$. The idempotents are the roots of $+1$ halved by the affine map $\tilde\xi\mapsto\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\xi)$ of the common apparatus; the map is never hit, the root set of $+1$ being empty, and $0$, which is not a root of $+1$, is the degenerate idempotent.

The Degeneracy of the Three Classes

Proposition. The three root classes of the block collapse onto two: the class of $0$, of real dimension four, and the empty class shared by $-1$ and $+1$. The only nondegenerate class is the root set of $0$, and it is the maximum possible, the whole punctured space; the other two are the minimum possible, empty.

Proof. The root set of $0$ is $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$ by the preceding theorem, of real dimension four; the root sets of $\pm1$ are empty. No other central value gives anything new: for $\lambda\neq0$ the equation is $0=\lambda e_0$, impossible, and for $\lambda=0$ it is universal.

Remark (what the degeneracy says). The three central values are carried by the block into the two extremes, and the passage is blind to the element: the specification of the root is by the target value alone, the value $0$ accepting every nonzero element and every other value accepting none. This is the opposite of the plain or the quaternionic block, where the roots of a central value form a sphere or a pair and degenerate only on a lower-dimensional family.

Summary

The antisymmetric plain block forces its whole root theory from one fact: the operation is alternating, so $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ for every element and the three central equations $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=\lambda e_0$ reduce to $0=\lambda e_0$. Hence the block has no root of $-1$ and no root of $+1$, and its roots of $0$ are every nonzero element, the punctured four-dimensional space, which is at once the square-zero set and the zero-divisor set. The idempotents are only $0$, the halved roots of $+1$ being an empty class. The three root classes collapse onto the two extremes, the empty class and the total punctured one, and the degeneracy is shared with the antisymmetric quaternionic block, while the general and symmetric blocks have spheres or pairs.

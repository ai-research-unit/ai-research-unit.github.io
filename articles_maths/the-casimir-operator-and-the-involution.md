
# __The Casimir Operator and the Involution__

## Introduction

Let $\mathrm{G}$ be a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra with Killing form $B$, and let $\Omega=\sum_i x_i \check x_i$ be its Casimir element, the element of the enveloping algebra $\mathrm{U}(\mathrm{G})$ attached to the form, where $(x_i)$ and $(\check x_i)$ are dual bases. The article studies $\Omega$ under the involutions of $\mathrm{G}$: under the extension $\mathrm{J}$ of an involution $\mathrm{J}$ that preserves the form, under the principal anti-automorphism $\sigma$, and under the star $\tau=\mathrm{J}\sigma$ of *Involutions of the Universal Enveloping Algebra*. The result is that every such map fixes $\Omega$, so $\Omega$ survives all the involutions of the algebra; and the decomposition of the Casimir element along a symmetric pair, $\Omega=\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$, splits it into the Casimir of the fixed subalgebra and a **translation part** that the involution negates. This article is the first of the `- * Operator Theory` group of the category: it reads the Casimir operator with an involution and computes the induced action on the symmetric pair. The Casimir element and its centrality are *The Casimir Operator*; the involution of the enveloping algebra is *Involutions of the Universal Enveloping Algebra*; the symmetric pair is *Symmetric Pairs of a Lie Algebra*; the adjoint-invariance version and the associated star are the companion entries *The Adjoint Representation and the Involution* and *Involutions of the Enveloping Algebra*, and the self-adjointness of $\Omega$ in a unitary representation belongs to the analysis of a later Part.

The base is a field $K$ of characteristic zero; $\mathrm{G}$ is finite-dimensional semisimple with Killing form $B$, $\mathrm{U}(\mathrm{G})$ its enveloping algebra, and the involutions are written $\mathrm{J}$ (of $\mathrm{G}$), $\mathrm{J}$ (its extension), $\sigma$ (principal anti-automorphism) and $\tau=\mathrm{J}\sigma$ (the star). The article uses the form and the involution algebraically and forms no length from $B$.

## Invariance of the Casimir Element

**Theorem.** Let $\mathrm{J}$ be an involution of $\mathrm{G}$ preserving the Killing form, $\mathrm{J}$ its extension to $\mathrm{U}(\mathrm{G})$, and $\sigma$ the principal anti-automorphism. Then

$$
\boxed{\;\mathrm{J}(\Omega)=\Omega,\qquad \sigma(\Omega)=\Omega,\qquad \tau(\Omega)=\Omega\;} .
$$

**Proof.** The form-preserving involution $\mathrm{J}$ carries a pair of dual bases to a pair of dual bases, and $\Omega=\sum_i x_i\check x_i$ is built from such a pair; hence $\mathrm{J}(\Omega)=\sum_i\mathrm{J}(x_i)\mathrm{J}(\check x_i)=\Omega$ after reindexing. The principal anti-automorphism sends $\sum_i x_i\check x_i$ to $\sum_i\sigma(\check x_i)\sigma(x_i)=\sum_i(-\check x_i)(-x_i)=\sum_i \check x_ix_i=\Omega$ since $\sigma$ reverses the product and $x\mapsto-x$ on the generators. The composite fixes $\Omega$ because both factors do.

**Corollary.** The Casimir element is fixed by the whole group of involutions generated by the form-preserving involutions and the principal anti-automorphism; consequently $\Omega$ is invariant under the star attached to any form-preserving involution, and the **unitary** condition of *Involutions of the Universal Enveloping Algebra* holds on $\Omega$ trivially.

**Proposition.** If $\mathrm{J}$ is an involution not preserving the form, then $\mathrm{J}(\Omega)=\Omega'$ where $\Omega'$ is the Casimir element formed with the transformed form $B\circ(\mathrm{J}\otimes\mathrm{J})$; the invariance of $\Omega$ holds exactly for the form-preserving involutions, among them the Cartan involution with respect to a compact form.

**Proof.** The image of $\Omega$ under $\mathrm{J}$ is the quadratic element of the form transformed by $\mathrm{J}$; equality with $\Omega$ is the equality of the two forms, which is the form-preserving hypothesis.

## Splitting along a Symmetric Pair

**Theorem.** Let $(\mathrm{G},\mathrm{K})$ be a symmetric pair with $\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{K}\oplus\mathrm{P}$ and $\mathrm{J}$ the involution, and suppose the form is such that the two summands are nondegenerate. Then the Casimir element splits as

$$
\boxed{\;\Omega=\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{P}},\qquad
\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}=\sum_{a}x_a\check x_a,\qquad
\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}=\sum_{b}y_b\check y_b\;},
$$

the first sum over dual bases of $\mathrm{K}$ and the second over dual bases of $\mathrm{P}$; $\mathrm{J}(\Omega_{\mathrm{K}})=\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}$, $\mathrm{J}(\Omega_{\mathrm{P}})=\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$, and $\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$ lies in the $-1$-eigenspace of the induced action only when the generators are chosen in $\mathrm{P}$ and the form pairs $\mathrm{K}$ with $\mathrm{P}$; with the natural choices the involution fixes both parts.

**Proof.** The direct sum $\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{K}\oplus\mathrm{P}$ with both summands nondegenerate splits the dual basis into the two families; the action of $\mathrm{J}$ is read on each family according to the eigenvalue of $\mathrm{J}$.

**Corollary.** The element $\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}$ is the Casimir element of the subalgebra $\mathrm{K}$ with respect to the restricted form, and $\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$ is invariant under $\mathrm{K}$; the operator $\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$ is the **translation Casimir** and its eigenvalues on the representations of the pair measure the size of the $\mathrm{P}$-directions.

**Proposition (the invariance of the split).** The summands $\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}$ and $\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$ are each annihilated by the ad-action of $\mathrm{K}$, and $\Omega=\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$ acts on a $\mathrm{K}$-module as the sum of the Casimir of $\mathrm{K}$ and the translation operator; the eigenvalues are additive over the decomposition of the representation.

**Proof.** The centrality of $\Omega$ in $\mathrm{U}(\mathrm{G})$ implies that each summand commutes with $\mathrm{K}$ because $\mathrm{K}$ preserves both the form and the splitting; the eigenvalue statement is the additivity of the action of a sum.

## The Involution and the Centre

**Proposition.** Both $\mathrm{J}$ and $\sigma$ preserve the centre $\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{U}(\mathrm{G}))$; the Casimir element lies in the centre, and the group of involutions acts on the centre by algebra automorphisms and anti-automorphisms that fix $\Omega$.

**Proof.** An algebra automorphism carries the centre to the centre, and so does an anti-automorphism (central elements map to central elements because they commute with everything); $\Omega$ is central by *The Casimir Operator* and is fixed by the previous theorem.

**Corollary.** The star $\tau$ acts on the centre by an anti-automorphism fixing $\Omega$; on the image of the centre under the Harish-Chandra homomorphism this action is the one induced by the involution of the root system, and its fixed part consists of the characters invariant under that involution.

**Remark.** The invariance of $\Omega$ is the algebraic reason a Casimir operator has a well-defined real spectrum in a unitary representation: the star fixes it, so it is symmetric, and a symmetric operator with the appropriate positivity has real eigenvalues. The analysis of that statement belongs to a later Part; the article records only that the star fixes $\Omega$.

## Worked Case: $\mathrm{sl}(2,K)$

Let $\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{sl}(2,K)$ with basis $e,h,f$ and Casimir element $\Omega=\tfrac14ef+\tfrac18h^2+\tfrac14fe$ as in *The Casimir Operator*. The principal anti-automorphism fixes $\Omega$, as computed in *Involutions of the Universal Enveloping Algebra*. For the symmetric pair with $\mathrm{J}(x)=-x^{t}$ the decomposition is $\mathrm{K}=\langle e-f\rangle$ and $\mathrm{P}=\langle h,e+f\rangle$; the split Casimir is $\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}=\tfrac1{2}(e-f)^2$ up to normalization and $\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}=\tfrac18h^2+\tfrac14(e+f)^2$, and both parts are fixed by $\mathrm{J}$ (the involution acts as $+1$ on the $\mathrm{K}$-generator and $-1$ on the $\mathrm{P}$-generators, so on the quadratic elements of the chosen families it acts by the product of the two signs). On a representation of $\mathrm{K}$ the element $\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$ acts by a scalar determined by the representation, and the sum $\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}=\Omega$ gives the familiar quadratic Casimir eigenvalue.


## Summary

The **Casimir element** $\Omega=\sum_i x_i\check x_i$ of a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra is fixed by every involution of the algebra: the extension $\mathrm{J}$ of a **form-preserving** involution of $\mathrm{G}$, the **principal anti-automorphism** $\sigma$, and their composite, the **star** $\tau=\mathrm{J}\sigma$, all satisfy $\mathrm{J}(\Omega)=\sigma(\Omega)=\tau(\Omega)=\Omega$. A non-form-preserving involution replaces $\Omega$ by the Casimir element of the transformed form, so the invariance holds exactly for the form-preserving involutions, among them the Cartan involution. Along a **symmetric pair** $(\mathrm{G},\mathrm{K})$ the Casimir element splits as $\Omega=\Omega_{\mathrm{K}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{P}}$, the Casimir of the fixed subalgebra plus the **translation Casimir**; each part is $\mathrm{K}$-invariant, and the eigenvalues are additive over the decomposition of the representation. The involutions preserve the centre and act on it by automorphisms and anti-automorphisms fixing $\Omega$; this is the algebraic reason a Casimir operator is symmetric under the star, hence has the spectral properties used in a later Part. For $\mathrm{sl}(2,K)$ the split is explicitly computed with $\mathrm{K}=\langle e-f\rangle$, $\mathrm{P}=\langle h,e+f\rangle$. The companion entries treat the adjoint representation and the star structures.

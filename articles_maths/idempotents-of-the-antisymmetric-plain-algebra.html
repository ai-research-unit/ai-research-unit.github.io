Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

The idempotent is defined by an equation in which the product occurs, so it is a class of the product and not of the space (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition). The block of this article is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $$ \tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q-\tilde Q\tilde P\bigr)=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{Q}, $$ the cross product of the two vector parts, which is alternating (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions). The article determines its idempotent set and states the consequences of its being as small as possible.

The answer is the extreme absence. The idempotent equation of the block is $\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$; its left side is zero for every element, and the equation therefore reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$. The block has one idempotent, the origin, which is the trivial idempotent of every product; it has no nontrivial idempotent, hence no orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent and no projection, and no Peirce decomposition. The unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block, the block being a bracket and not a product. The comparison with the parent product is the comparison of the origin alone with the idempotent family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $-1$, a set of real dimension four, and the two companion articles that the vanishing square makes equal are Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra and Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, which are cited.

The Notion and Its Product

Definition (idempotent of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element $\tilde\Pi$ is an idempotent of $\star$ when $$ \tilde\Pi\star\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi . $$ The origin and, when the product is unital, the unit are idempotents of every product; they are the trivial idempotents, and the others are nontrivial (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Idempotent, the Projection and the Peirce Decomposition).

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\wedge$, and unqualified, idempotent means an idempotent of $\wedge$. The idempotent is the root of $+1$ in the scaled sense of the definitions article, and the block's roots of $+1$ are treated in Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra; the idempotent sets of the four general products, and the table that compares them, are The Zero Divisors and the Twelve Structures, §The Square, the Idempotents and the Units.

The Criterion

Theorem (the idempotent set). The idempotent equation of the block is satisfied by the origin alone, $$ \{\tilde\Pi:\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi\}=\{0\}. $$

Proof. The block is alternating, $\tilde X\wedge\tilde X=0$ for every $\tilde X$, by Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Biquaternions; read at $\tilde X=\tilde\Pi$ this gives $\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=0$. The idempotent equation becomes $0=\tilde\Pi$, whose only solution is the origin. The origin is an idempotent indeed, $0\wedge0=0=0$.

The criterion is the whole of the computation, because the left side of the equation is identically zero and the equation constrains the right side alone. Every candidate idempotent is excluded at once, and no coordinate computation is needed.

Corollary (no nontrivial idempotent). The block has no nontrivial idempotent. In particular the unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block: $e_0\wedge e_0=\mathbf{0}\times\mathbf{0}=0\neq e_0$.

Proof. A nontrivial idempotent is one different from the origin and, when the product is unital, from the unit; the theorem says that the origin is the only idempotent, so there is no nontrivial one. The element $e_0$ satisfies $e_0\wedge e_0=0$ and $0\neq e_0$, so it is not an idempotent of the block, although it is the unit of the parent product.

The equation is read in the product and not in the algebra, and the block is not the plain product. The element $e_0$ is an idempotent of the parent and not of the block; the origin is an idempotent of both, being the trivial idempotent of every product.

The Absence of the Projector and of the Peirce Calculus

Proposition (no nontrivial projector). The only idempotent of the block is the origin, whose left multiplication operator is the zero operator. The block therefore carries no nontrivial projector: there is no nonzero idempotent element whose multiplication operator is a nonzero idempotent operator.

Proof. By the theorem the only idempotent element is the origin, and $L_0=0$; the zero operator is a projector, but there is no other idempotent element to produce one.

Proposition (no Peirce decomposition). The block has no Peirce decomposition relative to a nontrivial idempotent, because it has no nontrivial idempotent.

Proof. The Peirce decomposition is defined relative to an idempotent of an associative product and presupposes a nontrivial one; the block has none. The definitions article states the construction for an associative unital product, and neither the associativity nor a nontrivial idempotent is available here.

Remark. The vanishing square makes the two levels of the idempotent theory collapse onto the trivial one. An idempotent of an alternating product cannot exist off the origin, because the square of every element vanishes; the block is the extreme case of the collapse, and the Peirce calculus, the ideals and the module theory built from idempotents have no entry point in it. These constructions belong to the plain product and to its blocks.

The Comparison with the Parent Product

Remark (the parent idempotents). The parent general plain bilinear product is associative and unital, and its idempotents are the elements $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots $\tilde\Xi$ of $-1$, a set of real dimension four that contains the trivial pair, the images of the degenerate roots $\tilde\Xi=\pm i$; the family carries the left ideals, the direct sum decompositions and the Peirce decomposition (Idempotents of the General Plain Algebra). The block is the antisymmetric half of that product, and the comparison is the comparison of a one-point set with a family: the block keeps the trivial idempotent and loses every other, and it loses with them the entire idempotent calculus. The two sets are read against each other in the dictionary of the four general products, where the idempotent is one of the rows on which the four products separate (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §9), and the four idempotent sets themselves are The Zero Divisors and the Twelve Structures, §The Square, the Idempotents and the Units.

Summary

For the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\tilde P\wedge\tilde Q=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{Q}$ the idempotent equation $\tilde\Pi\wedge\tilde\Pi=\tilde\Pi$ reduces to $0=\tilde\Pi$, because the block is alternating; the block has one idempotent, the origin, and no nontrivial idempotent. The unit $e_0$ of the ambient algebra is not an idempotent of the block, the block being a bracket. No orthogonal family, no primitive idempotent, no projection and no Peirce decomposition exists, and the only projector the block carries is the zero operator, from the origin. The comparison with the parent product is the comparison of the origin alone with the family $\tfrac12(e_0+\tilde\Xi i)$ over the roots of $-1$, and the idempotent calculus — the left ideals, the direct sum decompositions, the Peirce decomposition — belongs to the parent and not to the block.

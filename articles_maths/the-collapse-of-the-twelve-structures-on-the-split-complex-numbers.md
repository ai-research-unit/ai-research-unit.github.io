# __The Collapse of the Twelve Structures on the Split-Complex Numbers__

## Introduction

The biquaternion method attaches to the algebra $\mathbb{B}$ twelve operations, named by the three-letter code of *The 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*, and reads them as the twelve algebraic structures of *The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space*. The code has a first letter for the operation ($\mathrm G$ general, $\mathrm S$ symmetric, $\mathrm A$ antisymmetric), a second for the family ($\mathrm P$ plain, $\mathrm Q$ quaternionic) and a third for the parent structure ($\mathrm A$ algebra, $\mathrm S$ sesqualgebra). Read on the split-complex algebra $\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2-1)$, the twelve operations collapse to **two** functions and therefore to two structures — the commutative ring under $AC$ and the sesqualgebra under $A\overline{C}$ — and this article carries the arithmetic of the collapse, the dictionary of the twelve against the two, and the reason it is not the same event as the collapse on $\mathbb{C}$.

The two surviving operations are *The Two Products of the Split-Complex Space*; the twelve on the biquaternion algebra are *The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space* and *The 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*. The reading of the surviving operations on the distinguished subspaces is *Remarkable Subspaces and the Two Products of the Split-Complex Space*, the article preceding this one, and the classification of their elements is *The Classes of Element of the Split-Complex Algebra*, below this article in this category. Nothing of the twelve is re-derived here: this article reads them on $\mathbb{D}$ and counts.

**The reading fixed here.** The natural sign is the identity on $\mathbb{D}$, the bar is the conjugation $j\mapsto-j$, and the Hermitian conjugation ${}^{*}$ coincides with the bar; the algebra is commutative. The statements of the collapse use only these three facts. In this article $A=a+ja'$ and $C=c+jc'$ are elements, and the letters $B$, $H$, $N$ are kept for the forms.

## The Twelve Codes on the Split-Complex Space

The general product of the plain algebra is the bilinear product, $\mathrm{GPA}(A,C)=AC$; of the quaternionic algebra, the natural conjugation applied to the first slot, $\mathrm{GQA}(A,C)=A^{\natural}C=AC$; of the plain sesqualgebra, the sesquilinear product, $\mathrm{GPS}(A,C)=A\overline{C}$; and of the quaternionic sesqualgebra, $\mathrm{GQS}(A,C)=A^{\natural}\overline{C}=A\overline{C}$. The symmetric and antisymmetric codes are the class-preserving symmetrisation and antisymmetrisation of the general product of the family,

$$
\mathrm S f(A,C)=\tfrac12\bigl(f(A,C)+\text{ex}(A,C)\bigr),
\qquad
\mathrm A f(A,C)=\tfrac12\bigl(f(A,C)-\text{ex}(A,C)\bigr),
$$

where the exchange $\text{ex}$ is the **class-preserving exchange** of *The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space*, §*The Exchange Behind the Split*: the interchange of the two slots for the two bilinear families, whose two slots have the same type, and the conjugate transpose of the value for the two sesquilinear families, whose slots are one linear and one conjugate-linear. Only the two values on $\mathbb{D}$ are needed here, and they are

$$
\text{bilinear families:}\quad \text{ex}(A,C)=AC,
\qquad
\text{sesquilinear families:}\quad \text{ex}(A,C)=\overline{C}A=A\overline{C},
$$

the second because the natural conjugation is the identity and the algebra is commutative.

## The Arithmetic of the Collapse

**Theorem.** On the split-complex space the twelve operations take two non-zero values and the zero value, and the dictionary is

$$
\mathrm{GPA}=\mathrm{SPA}=\mathrm{GQA}=\mathrm{SQA}=AC,
\qquad
\mathrm{GPS}=\mathrm{SPS}=\mathrm{GQS}=\mathrm{SQS}=A\overline{C},
\qquad
\mathrm{APA}=\mathrm{AQA}=\mathrm{APS}=\mathrm{AQS}=0 .
$$

**Proof.** The algebra is commutative, so $AC=CA$ and the exchange of the bilinear families changes nothing: $\mathrm{SPA}=\mathrm{GPA}$ and $\mathrm{APA}=0$, and likewise $\mathrm{SQA}=\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{AQA}=0$. The natural conjugation is the identity, $A^{\natural}=A$ and $C^{\natural}=C$, so the two bilinear general products coincide, $\mathrm{GQA}=A^{\natural}C=AC=\mathrm{GPA}$, and the two sesquilinear general products coincide, $\mathrm{GQS}=A^{\natural}\overline{C}=A\overline{C}=\mathrm{GPS}$. For the two sesquilinear families the exchange value is $\overline{C}A$, which by commutativity equals $A\overline{C}$; hence $\mathrm{SPS}=\mathrm{GPS}$, $\mathrm{APS}=0$, $\mathrm{SQS}=\mathrm{GQS}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}=0$. The four non-zero identities are two, and the four antisymmetric codes vanish.

So the collapse is read off two properties of $\mathbb{D}$ and no others. **Commutativity** identifies the general operation of each family with its symmetrisation and kills its antisymmetrisation, and it also makes the sesquilinear exchange value $\overline{C}A$ equal to the product $A\overline{C}$. **The trivial natural conjugation** identifies the quaternionic family with the plain one. The third-letter distinction between the algebra and the sesqualgebra survives, because the bar is not the identity: $\mathrm{GPA}=AC$ and $\mathrm{GPS}=A\overline{C}$ are different functions.

## The Dictionary of the Twelve Against the Two

| family | general | symmetric | antisymmetric | value on $\mathbb{D}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain algebra | $\mathrm{GPA}$ | $\mathrm{SPA}$ | $\mathrm{APA}$ | $AC$, $AC$, $0$ |
| quaternionic algebra | $\mathrm{GQA}$ | $\mathrm{SQA}$ | $\mathrm{AQA}$ | $AC$, $AC$, $0$ |
| plain sesqualgebra | $\mathrm{GPS}$ | $\mathrm{SPS}$ | $\mathrm{APS}$ | $A\overline{C}$, $A\overline{C}$, $0$ |
| quaternionic sesqualgebra | $\mathrm{GQS}$ | $\mathrm{SQS}$ | $\mathrm{AQS}$ | $A\overline{C}$, $A\overline{C}$, $0$ |

| the twelve collapse to | the two | the four that vanish |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{GPA},\mathrm{SPA},\mathrm{GQA},\mathrm{SQA}$ | $AC$ | $\mathrm{APA},\mathrm{AQA},\mathrm{APS},\mathrm{AQS}$ |
| $\mathrm{GPS},\mathrm{SPS},\mathrm{GQS},\mathrm{SQS}$ | $A\overline{C}$ | — |

The two structures that survive are therefore the **ring** $\mathbb{D}$ under the bilinear product $AC$ — commutative, associative, with the two-sided unit $1$ — and the **sesqualgebra** over $(\mathbb{D},\bar{\cdot})$ under the sesquilinear product $A\overline{C}$ — conjugate-commutative, with a right-sided unit alone and no left-sided one, and non-associative. The vanishing of the four antisymmetric codes removes the Lie layer entirely: there is no alternating operation on $\mathbb{D}$ built from these families, since every bracket of the commutative ring is zero. The associator of the sesquilinear product does **not** vanish, however: it is $[A,C,D]=(A\overline{C})\overline{D}-A\overline{(C\overline{D})}=A\overline{C}(\overline{D}-D)$, non-zero exactly when the third factor is not real, with the witness $[j,j,j]=2j$. The associator and the ternary product that repairs it are owned by *The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complex Space*, below this article in this category.

## Two Operations, One Field and One Ring

The collapse is the statement that the twelve-fold table of the biquaternion method has, on $\mathbb{D}$, exactly two entries, and the reason the two entries are worth keeping is that the two structures they define are not the two structures the collapse on $\mathbb{C}$ defines.

On the complex field the same arithmetic holds — $\mathbb{C}$ is commutative and its natural conjugation is trivial — and the collapse is therefore identical at the level of the codes: four codes give the field product $AC$, four give $A\overline{C}$, four vanish. What differs is **what the surviving ring is**. On $\mathbb{C}$ the bilinear product is the multiplication of a field: every non-zero element is a unit, there are no zero divisors, and the only idempotents are $0$ and $1$. On $\mathbb{D}$ the same product is the multiplication of a ring that is not a field: it has the null cone $a^2=a'^2$ of zero divisors, the two non-trivial idempotents $\Pi_{1,2}=\tfrac12(1\pm j)$, and the two non-trivial ideals $\mathbb{R}\Pi_{1,2}$ (*Split-Complex Zero Divisors*, *Split-Complex Idempotents and Projections* and *Split-Complex Ideals and Peirce Decomposition*, all below this article in this category). So the collapse is **arithmetic** on $\mathbb{C}$, where the surviving operation has no structure beyond the field it defines, and it is **algebraic** on $\mathbb{D}$, where the surviving operation defines a ring whose classes of element are non-empty.

**Remark.** The comparison is exact in one respect and only one. The set of identifications is the same on the two algebras, because it uses only commutativity and the triviality of the natural conjugation, both of which hold on both. What changes is the object the identifications leave standing, and the change is produced by the sign of the square of the generator, $j^2=+1$ against $i^2=-1$, which is the whole difference between the ring with zero divisors and the field. The split sign is thus visible in the collapse not in the count of the two but in what the two are.

## Summary

The twelve operations and the twelve algebraic structures of the biquaternion method, read on the split-complex space, collapse to two operations and two structures. Commutativity makes each general bilinear product equal to its symmetrisation and kills its antisymmetrisation, and the trivial natural conjugation makes the quaternionic family coincide with the plain one; the result is $\mathrm{GPA}=\mathrm{SPA}=\mathrm{GQA}=\mathrm{SQA}=AC$, $\mathrm{GPS}=\mathrm{SPS}=\mathrm{GQS}=\mathrm{SQS}=A\overline{C}$, and $\mathrm{APA}=\mathrm{AQA}=\mathrm{APS}=\mathrm{AQS}=0$. The associator of the sesquilinear product does not vanish and is confined to the sesquilinear shape; the Lie layer of the antisymmetric codes is empty.

The two structures that survive are the commutative ring under $AC$ and the sesqualgebra under $A\overline{C}$ over $(\mathbb{D},\bar{\cdot})$. On the complex field the same identifications hold, because $\mathbb{C}$ is commutative and its natural conjugation is trivial, but the surviving ring there is a field; on $\mathbb{D}$ it is a ring with zero divisors, two idempotents and two ideals. The collapse is therefore arithmetic on the field and algebraic on the ring, and the difference is the sign of the square of the generator.

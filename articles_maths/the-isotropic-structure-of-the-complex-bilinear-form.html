The Isotropic Structure of the Complex Bilinear Form

Introduction

Provisional. This article is a placeholder of the topology region, created in the dispatch that gives the four forms their own reading groups. It records its scope, its home and its sources; the content is to be written.

The complex bilinear form $\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)$ is indefinite, so it has a null set and isotropic subspaces. This article will treat them. The null set is the complex quadric

$$ \langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^2=0, $$

an isotropic cone of real dimension $6$, distinct from the null cone of the zero divisors: that one is the zero set of the quaternion bilinear form $N=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural})$, and the two cones must not be identified, since the forms differ by the sign vector $\varepsilon$. The article will describe the rulings of the cone, its isotropic lines, and the totally isotropic subspaces for $B$ with the maximal ones among them, and will compare them with the isotropic structure of the quaternion sesquilinear form.

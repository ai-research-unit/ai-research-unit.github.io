The Complex Bilinear Form on the Biquaternion Algebra

Introduction

Provisional. This article is a placeholder of the topology region, created in the dispatch that gives the four forms their own reading groups. It records its scope, its home and its sources; the content is to be written.

The biquaternion algebra carries four scalar forms, one to each of the four products of The Four Biquaternion Complex Products. This article treats the form of the plain product, the complex bilinear form

$$ \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu Q_\mu,\qquad \varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1). $$

It is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear, symmetric and non-degenerate, and it is indefinite: its Gram matrix in the coefficient basis is $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and on the real vector space of dimension eight its realification is the split form of signature $(4,4)$. Its diagonal $\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^2$ is the quadratic form it polarises.

The form is the last of the four to receive its own article. Its theory stood as §The Scalar Bilinear Form of Association and the Transpose on the Biquaternion Algebra, where it serves as the pairing that makes a linear map transposable. Beside it stands the quaternion bilinear form $N=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{\natural})$ of The Quaternion Bilinear Form on the Biquaternion Algebra, of Gram matrix $\mathrm{I}_4$; the two must not be confused, and the distinction of $D$ and $\mathrm{I}_4$ is the whole of it.

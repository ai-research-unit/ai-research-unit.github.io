Modules over the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions

Introduction

This article describes the left, right and two-sided modules over the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$ and the category they form. The substance is one theorem and its consequences: over a division algebra a module is free, the rank of a free module is well defined, and the category is semisimple. The division property is Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra, above this article in this category; the ideals, which are the submodules of the regular module, are Ideals and Simplicity of the General Plain Algebra, above this article in this category; the enveloping algebra and the operators by which the bimodule is read are The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the General Plain Algebra and the Bi-module Structure, below this article in this category.

The Regular Bimodule

Definition. The regular left module ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$ is the group $(\mathbb{H},+)$ with the action of $\mathbb{H}$ by left multiplication; the regular right module $\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{H}$ uses right multiplication by the opposite algebra $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$; the regular bimodule ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{H}$ carries both actions.

Proposition. The two actions commute, so ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{H}$ is a bimodule, equivalently a left module over the enveloping algebra $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{H}\otimes_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ under $(\tilde p\otimes\tilde q^{\mathrm{opp}})\cdot\tilde r=\tilde p\tilde r\tilde q$.

Proof. Associativity: $\tilde p(\tilde r\tilde q)=(\tilde p\tilde r)\tilde q$. The enveloping-algebra statements are The Enveloping Operator Algebra of the General Plain Algebra and the Bi-module Structure, below this article in this category.

Every Module is Free

Theorem. Every left $\mathbb{H}$-module is free.

Proof. Let $M$ be a left $\mathbb{H}$-module and let $\{x_\alpha\}_{\alpha<\lambda}$ be a well-ordered generating set, which exists by the well-ordering theorem. Build a subset $B\subseteq\{x_\alpha\}$ by transfinite recursion: put $x_\alpha$ in $B$ exactly when $x_\alpha$ is not in the submodule generated by $B\cap\{x_\beta:\beta<\alpha\}$. Then $B$ generates $M$: by induction on $\alpha$ each $x_\alpha$ lies in the submodule generated by $B\cap\{x_\beta:\beta\leq\alpha\}$, since either $x_\alpha\in B$ or it is in the span of the earlier $B$-elements. And $B$ is independent over $\mathbb{H}$: suppose $\sum_{b\in B'}a_bb=0$ with $B'$ finite, every $a_b\neq0$, and the elements of $B'$ listed in increasing order; let $b_*$ be the last. Then $a_{b_*}b_*=-\sum_{b\in B'\setminus\{b_*\}}a_bb$ lies in the span of $B\cap\{x_\beta:\beta<\mathrm{ord}(b_*)\}$, and since $a_{b_*}\neq0$ it is invertible, so $b_*$ lies there too, contradicting the choice of $b_*$. Hence $B'$ is empty and there is no non-trivial relation. A generating independent subset is a basis, so $M$ is free.

Corollary. Every submodule of a free module is free, every left ideal is free of rank at most one, and the module $\mathbb{H}^n$ is free of rank $n$.

Proof. Submodules of free modules over a division ring are the free submodules of the standard argument; a left ideal of $\mathbb{H}$ is a submodule of ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$, hence free with a basis of at most one element, because a basis with two elements would give two non-zero elements $b_1,b_2$ with $\mathbb{H}b_1\cap\mathbb{H}b_2\neq0$.

The Rank is Well Defined

Theorem. If $\mathbb{H}^m\cong\mathbb{H}^n$ as left $\mathbb{H}$-modules, then $m=n$.

Proof. Every non-zero element of $\mathbb{H}$ is invertible, so ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$ is a simple module: a non-zero submodule contains a non-zero element $b$, hence $\mathbb{H}\cdot b=\mathbb{H}$. A free module $\mathbb{H}^n$ therefore has a composition series of length $n$, $0\subsetneq\mathbb{H}\subsetneq\mathbb{H}^2\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathbb{H}^n$, whose factors are all isomorphic to the simple module ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$. Composition length is an invariant of the module, so an isomorphism $\mathbb{H}^m\cong\mathbb{H}^n$ forces $m=n$.

Definition. The rank of a free left $\mathbb{H}$-module $M$ is the cardinality of a basis; by the theorem it is well defined.

Corollary. The algebra $\mathbb{H}$ has the invariant basis number property, and the rank is an isomorphism invariant of the free module.

Proof. The theorem and the definition.

Submodules and Ideals

Theorem. The submodules of ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$ are the left ideals of $\mathbb{H}$; they are exactly $0$ and $\mathbb{H}$. The same holds for the right module and the two-sided module.

Proof. A submodule of the regular left module is closed under left multiplication by $\mathbb{H}$, that is, a left ideal. A non-zero left ideal contains a unit and hence is $\mathbb{H}$. The right and two-sided cases are the same computation; the full ideal theory, the central simplicity and the Brauer class are Ideals and Simplicity of the General Plain Algebra, above this article in this category.

Corollary. The module ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$ is simple, and it is the only simple left $\mathbb{H}$-module up to isomorphism.

Proof. It has no proper non-zero submodule, and every simple module is generated by one element $m$, hence is a quotient of ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$, hence isomorphic to it because the kernel is a submodule, that is $0$ or all of it.

The Endomorphism Rings

Theorem. $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{H})\cong\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ and $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}}(\mathbb{H})\cong\mathbb{H}$ as rings, while the bimodule endomorphisms are the scalars, $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{H})=\mathbb{R}$.

Proof. An $\mathbb{H}$-linear endomorphism of ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$ is determined by the image of $e_0$, and $F(\tilde r)=F(\tilde r\,e_0)=\tilde r\,F(e_0)$, so $F$ is right multiplication by $F(e_0)$; the assignment $F\mapsto F(e_0)$ is an anti-isomorphism, whence $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$. The right-sided case is the mirror, and an endomorphism that is linear on both sides is multiplication by a central element, that is by a real scalar.

Corollary. $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{H}^n)\cong M_n(\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}})$. The matrix algebra of the regular module acts by left multiplication on $\mathbb{H}^n$.

Proof. An endomorphism of $\mathbb{H}^n$ is an $n\times n$ matrix of endomorphisms of $\mathbb{H}$, the diagonal ones being the elements of $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$.

Semisimplicity and Torsion

Definition. The naive torsion submodule of a left $\mathbb{H}$-module $M$ is $T(M)=\{m\in M: \tilde a m=0\text{ for some }\tilde a\neq0\}$.

Theorem. $T(M)=0$ for every module, the category of left $\mathbb{H}$-modules is semisimple, every submodule is a direct summand, and every module is a direct sum of copies of the simple module ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$.

Proof. A non-zero element of $\mathbb{H}$ is invertible, so $\tilde a m=0$ with $\tilde a\neq0$ gives $m=\tilde a^{-1}\tilde a m=0$; this is the vanishing of the naive torsion. Every module is free, hence a direct sum of copies of ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$, which is simple; a direct sum of simple modules is semisimple, every short exact sequence of such modules splits, and every submodule is a summand.

Corollary. The algebra $\mathbb{H}$ is left and right semisimple, its global dimension is $0$, every module is projective and injective, and the free module of rank $n$ is the direct sum of $n$ copies of ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$.

Proof. The theorem; a module all of whose submodules are summands is projective and injective, and every short exact sequence splits.

Summary

The regular bimodule ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{H}$ carries the two commuting actions of $\mathbb{H}$ and $\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ and is the standard bimodule; every left or right $\mathbb{H}$-module is free, by the transfinite construction that discards each generator already in the span of the earlier ones, and the rank of a free module is well defined because $\mathbb{H}^n$ has composition length $n$ with all factors the simple module ${}_\mathbb{H}\mathbb{H}$. The submodules of the regular module are the ideals, hence $0$ and $\mathbb{H}$ alone; the endomorphism rings are $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{H})\cong\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ and $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{H})=\mathbb{R}$. The category is semisimple, the naive torsion vanishes, every module is a direct sum of copies of the unique simple module, and every submodule is a summand.

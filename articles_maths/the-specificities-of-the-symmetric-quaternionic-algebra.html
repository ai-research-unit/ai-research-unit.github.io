The Specificities of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The symmetric quaternionic bilinear product $\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q+\tilde Q^{\natural}\tilde P)$ is governed by one formula, proved in Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra and read throughout the block,

$$ \tilde P\bullet\tilde Q=\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\,e_0,\qquad \langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=P_0Q_0+(\vec P,\vec Q), $$

in which $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the quaternion form, symmetric, non-degenerate, of Gram matrix the identity on the basis. The formula makes the algebra element-theoretically the poorest of the twelve: it is commutative with vanishing commutator, its image is the central line $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_0$, it has no unit, its radical is $\{0\}$ because the form is non-degenerate, and its idempotents are $0$ and $e_0$ alone (The Radical and the Isotropic Elements of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra).

Its operator theory is nevertheless exactly computable, and again in one line, because the value of the product reads its two arguments only through the form: the left multiplication is the rank-one operator $L_{\tilde X}:\tilde Y\mapsto \langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}e_0$, and its square is

$$ L_{\tilde X}^{2}=X_0\,L_{\tilde X}, $$

with $X_0$ the scalar coordinate of $\tilde X$. This article reads the two consequences. The elements whose left multiplication is a projector are those of the affine hyperplane $X_0=1$, together with the origin: the first are pairwise distinct rank-one projections onto the central line, the second is the zero operator. The elements whose left multiplication is square-zero are those of the hyperplane $X_0=0$, which is the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, the orthogonal complement of the central line; the nonzero ones have rank one, and the zero elements of this family are distinct from the square-zero elements of the algebra, which are the isotropic cone.

The article states and proves the exclusive law of the block on the same model as the articles that began the family: the scalar law, which demands that the product be commutative and that its values be central, is satisfied by the block and by no other operation of the twelve, and the classification of the block's operators is read from it. The projectors do not come from isolated elements and not from the unit: they come from a hyperplane, they are indexed by that hyperplane and not by the idempotents of the algebra, and even the element $e_0$ gives the projection onto the central line and not the identity, because the algebra has no unit. The algebra therefore produces a full family of projectors and disowns all but one of their indices, exactly as the general quaternionic product does; the difference is the size and the shape of the family.

Placement. The article is the second and closing entry of the Specific Theory section of the symmetric quaternionic algebra, after The Radical and the Isotropic Elements of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, and it is the third of the family that The Specificities of the Biquaternion Algebra began and The Specificities of the General Quaternionic Algebra continued. It uses the governing formula of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, the non-degeneracy and the trivial radical of the radical article, the composition law of the multiplication operators in The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, the element idempotents of Idempotents of the General Plain Algebra, and the criterion of the article that began the family.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$ with $e_1e_2=e_3$ and central scalar imaginary $i$. The product is written $\bullet$, as in the Specific Theory entries of the block, and coincides with the $\star$ of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra and of The Quaternion Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra; juxtaposition is the plain product and $\natural$ the natural conjugation. An element is $\tilde X=X_0+\vec X$ with $X_0$ its scalar coordinate and $\vec X=(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ its vector part, and $\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}=X_0Y_0+(\vec X,\vec Y)$ is the quaternion form, non-degenerate and of Gram matrix the identity. The left multiplication is $L_{\tilde X}(\tilde Y)=\tilde X\bullet\tilde Y$, and the subscript is the element. An idempotent of the algebra is an element equal to its own square, and a projector is an idempotent operator, that is an idempotent of the morphism space; the zero operator is one, and the two levels are never interchanged. An idempotent element corresponds to a projector when its multiplication operator is idempotent, and the content of §The Projectors is exactly the scalar equation $X_0=1$, together with the origin.

The Exclusive Law of the Block

The family of specificities articles states, for each block, one law that the block has and the other eleven do not, and proves it (The Specificities of the Biquaternion Algebra, The Specificities of the General Quaternionic Algebra). The law of the symmetric quaternionic block is the following.

Definition (the scalar law). Let $\circ$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. The product satisfies the scalar law when

  1. it is commutative, $\tilde X\circ\tilde Y=\tilde Y\circ\tilde X$ for all $\tilde X,\tilde Y$; and
  2. its values are central, $\tilde X\circ\tilde Y\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_0$ for all $\tilde X,\tilde Y$.

Theorem (the equivalent form). A product $\circ$ satisfies the scalar law if and only if there is a symmetric $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear form $\beta$ on $\mathbb{B}$ with

$$ \tilde X\circ\tilde Y=\beta(\tilde X,\tilde Y)\,e_0\qquad\text{for all }\tilde X,\tilde Y . $$

Proof. If $\circ$ satisfies the scalar law, its value is central, so it reads $\beta(\tilde X,\tilde Y)e_0$ with $\beta$ the scalar coefficient; the bilinearity of the product gives the bilinearity of $\beta$, and its commutativity gives the symmetry. Conversely a product of the displayed shape has central values by construction and is commutative because $\beta$ is symmetric.

Theorem (the block is the only structure with the scalar law). The symmetric quaternionic product satisfies the scalar law, with $\beta=N$; and among the twelve operations of The Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space it is the only one that does.

Proof. The product is $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}e_0$ with $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ symmetric, bilinear and central-valued by Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, so the law holds; the uniqueness is §The Isolation among the Twelve, where each other operation is exhibited with a witness of the failure of clause 1 or of clause 2.

Theorem (the operator reading). The left multiplications of the block obey the composition law

$$ L_{\tilde X}L_{\tilde Z}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde X)\,L_{\tilde Z}\qquad\text{for all }\tilde X,\tilde Z, $$

where $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde X)=X_0$ is the scalar coordinate; and no other one of the twelve operations admits a law of the form $L^{\circ}_{\tilde X}L^{\circ}_{\tilde Z}=\ell(\tilde X)L^{\circ}_{\tilde Z}$ with $\ell$ depending on the first factor alone.

Proof. The law of the block is the composition law of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, $L_{\tilde A}L_{\tilde B}=\langle\tilde A,e_0\rangle_{\natural}L_{\tilde B}$, with $\langle\tilde A,e_0\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde A)$; its specialisation $\tilde A=\tilde B=\tilde X$ is $L_{\tilde X}^2=X_0L_{\tilde X}$ of §The Operator Identity. The failures of the other eleven are in §The Isolation among the Twelve.

Remark (why the two forms of the law are kept). The element form and the operator form are not the same statement in general, and the article keeps both. The operator form is the one the block's whole operator theory reads, and it is the form in which the four columns of the projectors and the square-zero operators are computed; the element form is the one the pairs of basis elements settle, because its two clauses are decided there. The block is the unique structure that passes both.

The Left Multiplication Is Rank One

Theorem (the shape of the operator). For all $\tilde X,\tilde Y$,

$$ L_{\tilde X}(\tilde Y)=\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}\,e_0 . $$

For $\tilde X\neq0$ the operator has image the central line $\mathbb{C}e_0$, kernel the hyperplane $\langle\tilde X,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}=0$, and rank one; it is the zero operator exactly at $\tilde X=0$.

Proof. The display is the governing formula read as an operator identity. The form $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is non-degenerate, so for $\tilde X\neq0$ the functional $\langle\tilde X,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is nonzero and its kernel is a hyperplane; the image is the span of $e_0$, of complex dimension one. For $\tilde X=0$ the functional vanishes and the operator is $0$; conversely $L_{\tilde X}=0$ gives $\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}=0$ for every $\tilde Y$ and hence $\tilde X=0$ by non-degeneracy.

Corollary (the map of elements is faithful). $L_{\tilde X}=L_{\tilde Z}$ if and only if $\tilde X=\tilde Z$.

Proof. The operators agree exactly when the functionals $\langle\tilde X,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and $\langle\tilde Z,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ agree, that is when $\langle\tilde X-\tilde Z,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}=0$, that is when $\tilde X-\tilde Z=0$ by non-degeneracy.

The corollary is what makes the operator theory exact: an operator identity of $\bullet$ is an element identity of the space, and no information is lost. It is the analogue of the faithfulness of the plain left multiplications, and it is the property the two sesquilinear members of the symmetric family do not have.

Remark (the operator is one for the whole hyperplane, not for the line). The image of $L_{\tilde X}$ is the central line and never a proper subspace of it, so the operators are as small as a nonzero operator can be: rank one, by the smallest possible argument. The algebra is a rank-one pairing, and its whole operator theory is the theory of the nonzero linear functionals on the space, read with a fixed value $e_0$.

The Operator Identity

Theorem (the square of the left multiplication). For every $\tilde X$,

$$ L_{\tilde X}^{2}=X_0\,L_{\tilde X}. $$

Proof. $L_{\tilde X}^{2}(\tilde Y)=L_{\tilde X}\bigl(\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}e_0\bigr)=\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}L_{\tilde X}(e_0)=\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}\langle\tilde X,e_0\rangle_{\natural}e_0$, and $\langle\tilde X,e_0\rangle_{\natural}=X_0$; so the composite is $X_0\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}e_0=X_0L_{\tilde X}(\tilde Y)$.

The theorem is the specialisation of the composition law $L^{\bullet}_{\tilde A}L^{\bullet}_{\tilde B}=\langle\tilde A,e_0\rangle_{\natural}L^{\bullet}_{\tilde B}$ of The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra to the single element $\tilde A=\tilde B=\tilde X$, and it is stated here as the identity the whole article reads. It is the exact replacement of the criterion of The Specificities of the Biquaternion Algebra, which in this algebra would read $L_{\tilde X}^{2}=L_{\tilde X\bullet\tilde X}$; the two are not the same, and the comparison is made at §The Criterion Fails Here.

Corollary (the monoid law). $L_{\tilde X}L_{\tilde Z}=X_0\,L_{\tilde Z}$ for all $\tilde X,\tilde Z$, so the family of left multiplications is stable under composition, it is a monoid with the reversed convention of composition, and every element of scalar coordinate $1$ is a left identity of the monoid.

Proof. The composite of the identity of the theorem with an arbitrary $L_{\tilde Z}$ gives the display, and at $X_0=1$ it reads $L_{\tilde X}L_{\tilde Z}=L_{\tilde Z}$: such an $L_{\tilde X}$ is a left identity.

Remark (the monoid is not the algebra). The multiplication of the algebra is commutative and has no unit, and the monoid of its left multiplications is non-commutative as soon as both factors have nonzero scalar coordinate ($L_{\tilde X}L_{\tilde Z}=X_0L_{\tilde Z}$ against $L_{\tilde Z}L_{\tilde X}=Z_0L_{\tilde X}$) and has left identities only. The passage from the algebra to its operators therefore changes both the commutativity and the unit, which is the sharpest illustration of the theme of the family: the element structure and the operator structure of these products are different objects.

The Projectors

Theorem (the classification). The operator $L_{\tilde X}$ is a projector if and only if $\tilde X=0$ or $X_0=1$.

Proof. By §The Operator Identity the condition is $(X_0-1)L_{\tilde X}=0$. If $X_0=1$ it holds. If $X_0\neq1$ then $L_{\tilde X}=0$, and by the faithfulness of §The Left Multiplication Is Rank One this is $\tilde X=0$, whose scalar coordinate is $0\neq1$; so the two cases are disjoint and cover the condition.

Corollary (the family and its shape). The projectors are the operators $L_{\tilde X}$ with $X_0=1$, one for each point of the affine hyperplane $\{X_0=1\}$, together with the zero operator at $\tilde X=0$. Each is the projection onto the central line $\mathbb{C}e_0$ along the hyperplane $\ker \langle\tilde X,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, it fixes $e_0$ and kills that kernel, the family is of complex dimension three, and its members are pairwise distinct.

Proof. At $X_0=1$ the operator is idempotent by the theorem, its image is $\mathbb{C}e_0$ and its kernel is a hyperplane by §The Left Multiplication Is Rank One, and the two are complementary because $\langle\tilde X,e_0\rangle_{\natural}=X_0=1$ does not vanish on the image: the operator is the projection onto $\mathbb{C}e_0$ along the kernel, and it fixes $e_0$. The distinctness is the faithfulness of §The Left Multiplication Is Rank One, and the hyperplane has complex dimension three.

Remark (the projection is onto the line, not onto the element's own span). Every member of the family is a projection of rank one onto the same line $\mathbb{C}e_0$, and the members differ only in the direction along which they project, which is the kernel of $L_{\tilde X}$. The family is therefore a projective space of directions rather than a family of subspaces: the kernels $\langle\tilde X,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}=0$, that is the points of the dual projective space $\mathrm{PG}(3,\mathbb{C})^{*}$ whose hyperplane does not contain the central line, the index $\tilde X$ ranging over the affine chart $X_0=1$. The hyperplanes that do contain the central line form the projective plane $X_0=0$ inside that dual space, and they are exactly the kernels excluded here.

Remark (the algebra disowns its projectors). The idempotents of the algebra are $0$ and $e_0$ alone, and the projectors are indexed by the hyperplane $X_0=1$ and by the origin. The overlap of the two descriptions is one element, besides the origin: $e_0$. Even there the coincidence is misleading, because $L_{e_0}$ is not the identity: it is the projection $E:\tilde Y\mapsto Y_0e_0$ onto the central line, the identity being unavailable since the algebra has no unit. The family is thus indexed by elements that are not idempotents of the algebra, and it is a full three-parameter family all the same.

Remark (the parallel with the general quaternionic product). In the general quaternionic algebra the projectors are the plain left multiplications indexed by the plain idempotents, a family of complex dimension two (The Specificities of the General Quaternionic Algebra). Here the family is indexed by the hyperplane $X_0=1$, of complex dimension three, and by itself: the index set and the family have the same dimension. In both cases the algebra disowns its projectors, the element idempotents being far fewer than the operator ones, and in both cases the disjunction has one source, that the operator reads the element only through a pairing and so cannot see the idempotent equation.

The Square-Zero Operators

Theorem (the classification). The operator $L_{\tilde X}$ is square-zero if and only if $X_0=0$.

Proof. By §The Operator Identity $L_{\tilde X}^{2}=X_0L_{\tilde X}$, which vanishes when $X_0=0$. Conversely if $L_{\tilde X}^{2}=0$ and $X_0\neq0$ then $L_{\tilde X}=0$, hence $\tilde X=0$ by faithfulness, and $0$ has scalar coordinate $0$, a contradiction.

Corollary (the set and its shape). The square-zero operators are the operators $L_{\tilde X}$ with $X_0=0$, that is with $\tilde X$ in the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})=\mathbb{C}e_1+\mathbb{C}e_2+\mathbb{C}e_3$, which is the orthogonal complement of the central line and is of complex dimension three; the operator is zero at $\tilde X=0$ and of rank one elsewhere, and it is a two-step flag $\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}e_0\subseteq\ker \langle\tilde X,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$.

Proof. The vanishing of $X_0$ is the vanishing of the scalar coordinate, that is membership in the vector subspace; the rank statement is §The Left Multiplication Is Rank One, and the flag is the image inside the kernel, which is the shape of a square-zero operator.

Remark (the two meanings of square-zero). The square-zero elements of the algebra are the isotropic ones, the cone $\langle\tilde X,\tilde X\rangle_{\natural}=0$ of complex dimension three (The Radical and the Isotropic Elements of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra), and the square-zero operators are the elements of the vector subspace, also of complex dimension three. The two sets are different: the isotropic cone contains $e_1+ie_2$, whose scalar coordinate is $0$ and which therefore does belong to both, and it contains $e_0+e_1$, whose scalar coordinate is $1$ and whose operator is a projector rather than a square-zero one. The coincidence of the dimensions is arithmetic and the two sets are not equal.

Remark (the sharp asymmetry between the two halves). The theorem and the classification before it split the space by the scalar coordinate alone, and the split is the whole operator theory: $X_0=1$ gives the projectors, $X_0=0$ the square-zero ones, and every other element gives an operator that is neither. The property of being a projector is therefore not an algebraic property of the element in this algebra but the single scalar equation $X_0=1$, which is why the family is a hyperplane and why the algebra's own idempotent equation meets it in one element.

The Criterion Fails Here

The criterion of The Specificities of the Biquaternion Algebra reads $L_{\tilde X}^{2}=L_{\tilde X\bullet\tilde X}$ and characterises the left-alternative products. In this algebra the two sides are $X_0L_{\tilde X}$ and, by §The Operator Identity read at the element $\tilde X\bullet\tilde X=\langle\tilde X,\tilde X\rangle_{\natural}e_0$,

$$ L_{\tilde X\bullet\tilde X}=\langle\tilde X,\tilde X\rangle_{\natural}\,L_{e_0}=\langle\tilde X,\tilde X\rangle_{\natural}\,E,\qquad E:\tilde Y\mapsto Y_0e_0 , $$

so the criterion would demand $X_0\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde X,\tilde X\rangle_{\natural}Y_0$ for every $\tilde Y$, that is the pair of conditions $X_0^{2}=\langle\tilde X,\tilde X\rangle_{\natural}$ and $X_0X_k=0$ for $k=1,2,3$. The conditions fail at the first vector, where the two sides of the alternativity law are

$$ e_1\bullet(e_1\bullet e_0)=0,\qquad (e_1\bullet e_1)\bullet e_0=e_0 , $$

since $e_1\bullet e_0=\langle e_1,e_0\rangle_{\natural}e_0=0$ while $e_1\bullet e_1=\langle e_1,e_1\rangle_{\natural}e_0=e_0$. The algebra is not left-alternative, the criterion is not available, and the classification above is not an instance of it: it is read from the identity of §The Operator Identity, which holds for the algebra's own reason, the rank-one shape of the product.

The Isolation among the Twelve

The family of specificities articles states, for each block, one law that the block has and the other eleven do not, proves it, and reads the block's theory from it. The law of this block is the scalar law of §The Exclusive Law of the Block: commutative with central values, equivalently $\tilde X\circ\tilde Y=\beta(\tilde X,\tilde Y)e_0$ for a symmetric bilinear form $\beta$.

Theorem (the isolation). Of the twelve structures of The Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space, the symmetric quaternionic product is the only one that satisfies the scalar law.

Proof. The twelve are generated from the four general products by symmetrising or antisymmetrising. The four general products are non-commutative, so they fail the first clause; the four antisymmetrisations are non-commutative as well. Among the four symmetrisations, the symmetric plain product is commutative and not central-valued, the vector part of the plain product surviving the symmetrisation; the symmetric plain sesquilinear product is central-valued and not commutative, its conjugation conjugating a scalar; and the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product fails both. The symmetric quaternionic product is commutative and central-valued by the governing formula, and it alone meets both clauses.

The operator reading of the law, $L^{\circ}_{\tilde X}L^{\circ}_{\tilde Z}=\ell(\tilde X)L^{\circ}_{\tilde Z}$ with $\ell$ depending on the first factor alone, fails for the other eleven as well. The three bilinear structures away from the block that carry a one-sided unit — the plain product, the general quaternionic product and the symmetric plain product — fail at $\tilde Z=e_0$, where $L^{\circ}_{e_0}$ is the identity, so a law of that form would make $L^{\circ}_{\tilde X}$ a scalar multiple of the identity, which it is not. The two alternating products carry no unit and fail the reading at the pair $(\tilde X,\tilde Z)=(e_1,e_1)$, where the two sides are not proportional. The six sesquilinear structures fail by parity: their left multiplication is conjugate-linear in its argument while a composite is $\mathbb{C}$-linear, so a linear operator equal to a conjugate-linear one vanishes, and the composite is not always zero. The block satisfies the reading with $\ell(\tilde X)=X_0$ by §The Operator Identity.

Remark (the four symmetric products). The four symmetric products behave alike at the element level — no unit, few idempotents — and differently at the operator level. The symmetric plain product recovers only the identity operator, from its unit; the two symmetric sesquilinear products recover only the zero operator, their left multiplications being conjugate-linear and hence, if idempotent, zero; this block recovers the affine hyperplane $X_0=1$ of rank-one projections onto the central line, together with the zero operator. The block is the one member whose operator family is a hyperplane, and the reason is the rank-one shape $L_{\tilde X}\tilde Y=\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}e_0$ of its left multiplication, which the other three do not have.

Summary

The symmetric quaternionic product satisfies the scalar law — it is commutative and its values lie in the centre, equivalently $\tilde P\bullet\tilde Q=\langle\tilde P,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}e_0$ for a symmetric bilinear form — and it is the only one of the twelve operations that does: the two other symmetrisations that survive one clause each fail the other (the symmetric plain product is commutative and not central, the symmetric plain sesquilinear product central and not commutative), and the four general products and the four antisymmetrisations fail both. Its left multiplication is the rank-one operator $L_{\tilde X}\tilde Y=\langle\tilde X,\tilde Y\rangle_{\natural}e_0$, faithful in $\tilde X$ because the form is non-degenerate, and the single identity $L_{\tilde X}^{2}=X_0L_{\tilde X}$ classifies everything: the operator is a projector exactly when $\tilde X=0$ or $X_0=1$, and square-zero exactly when $X_0=0$; the operator reading $L_{\tilde X}L_{\tilde Z}=X_0L_{\tilde Z}$ likewise fails for the other eleven, for the six sesquilinear ones by the parity obstruction and for the rest on explicit witnesses. The projectors are the affine hyperplane $X_0=1$ together with the zero operator, a family of complex dimension three of pairwise distinct rank-one projections onto the central line, indexed by the hyperplane and not by the idempotents of the algebra, which are $0$ and $e_0$ alone; even the element $e_0$ gives the projection onto the central line and not the identity, since the algebra has no unit. The square-zero operators are the vector subspace $X_0=0$, which is not the isotropic cone of the square-zero elements, the two coinciding only on the intersection. Among the four symmetric products the block is the only one with a hyperplane of projectors, and the law that isolates it among the twelve is the scalar law of §The Isolation among the Twelve.

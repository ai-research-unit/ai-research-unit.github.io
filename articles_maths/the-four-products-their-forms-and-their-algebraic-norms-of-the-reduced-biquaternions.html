The Four Products, Their Forms and Their Algebraic Norms

Introduction

The reduced biquaternion space carries four general products, and each carries a scalar-part form and an algebraic norm. The products are the general plain bilinear product $TW$, the general quaternionic bilinear product $T^{\natural}W$, the general plain sesquilinear product $TW^{*}$ and the general quaternionic sesquilinear product $T^{\natural}W^{*}$; the four scalar-part forms are written $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$, the forms of the pairs $(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})$, $(\mathrm{id},{}^{*})$, $({}^{\natural},\mathrm{id})$ and $({}^{\natural},{}^{*})$; and the algebraic norm of each is the diagonal of its form, $$ \langle T\rangle=A^{2}+B^{2},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{*}=|A|^{2}-|B|^{2},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{\natural*}=|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}. $$

This is the article of the definition of the four products and of the four algebraic norms, following The Four Products of the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space, The 4 Forms over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space and The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space of the biquaternion category. The clause that decides which product is which is the slot: the first slot carries either the identity or the natural sign, the second either the identity or the star, and the two choices give the four. The forms are recorded with their Gram matrices and their signatures; the norms with their fields of values; the relations between the products are recorded; and the products are compared.

The results. The general plain bilinear product is the multiplication of the algebra; it is the only associative one and the only commutative one, its form is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear and symmetric and its norm $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is complex-valued, indefinite of real signature $(2,2)$, and not multiplicative. The general quaternionic bilinear product has the same bilinearity and symmetry but is neither associative nor commutative; its norm $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}$ is complex-valued, indefinite of real signature $(2,2)$, and it is the multiplicative one, $\langle TW\rangle_{\natural}=\langle T\rangle_{\natural}\langle W\rangle_{\natural}$, the reduced norm of the algebra and its invertibility criterion. The general plain sesquilinear product is $\mathbb{C}$-linear in the first variable and $\mathbb{C}$-antilinear in the second, Hermitian, and its norm $\langle T\rangle_{*}=|A|^{2}-|B|^{2}$ is real-valued, indefinite of real signature $(2,2)$ and not multiplicative. The general quaternionic sesquilinear product is Hermitian like the former, and its norm $\langle T\rangle_{\natural*}=|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}$ is real-valued and positive definite, of real signature $(4,0)$, the topological norm of the space.

The one warning of this article. An algebraic norm is an element of the algebra — a complex number here for $\langle\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, a real number for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ — and it is the diagonal of the scalar-part form of a product; it is not the positive real that the word norm means in topology and analysis. Only $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is positive definite, so only $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is a topological norm (squared). The two uses of the word are never conflated, and Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, below this article, reads the algebraic norm of the multiplication against the criterion of invertibility.

Boundaries. The space, the bases and the linear or antilinear characters are Reduced Biquaternions as a Vector Space over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$, above this article. The involution that defines the natural-sign slot and the star is The Two Involutions and the Group of Involutions, above this article. The multiplication read as an algebra, with its unit, its zero divisors and its group of units, is Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions, below this article. The zero divisors and the annihilators are the next articles of the category.

The Four General Products

Each product has a coordinate table in the two complex coordinates $A$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, read off from the relations $i^{2}=-1$, $j^{2}=+1$ and $ji=ij$ and the four maps of the involutions.

Definition. For $T=A+Bj$ and $W=C+Dj$, the four general products are $$ TW=(AC+BD)+(AD+BC)j, $$ $$ T^{\natural}W=(AC-BD)+(AD-BC)j, $$ $$ TW^{*}=(A\bar C-B\bar D)+(B\bar C-A\bar D)j, $$ $$ T^{\natural}W^{*}=(A\bar C+B\bar D)-(A\bar D+B\bar C)j, $$ where the first slot carries $\mathrm{id}$ in the first and third and ${}^{\natural}$ in the second and fourth, and the second slot carries $\mathrm{id}$ in the first and second and ${}^{*}$ in the third and fourth. The scalar part of each is the general plain bilinear, the general quaternionic bilinear, the general plain sesquilinear and the general quaternionic sesquilinear form respectively.

Proposition (the tables of the plain products). The general plain bilinear product is the multiplication of the algebra, since ${}^{\natural}$ appears in neither slot; its table is the multiplication table of Reduced Biquaternion Space, and its scalar part and $j$-part are $AC+BD$ and $AD+BC$.

Proof. With $T=A+Bj$ and $W=C+Dj$, $TW=AC+ADj+BCj+BDj^{2}$, and $j^{2}=+1$ gives the display. This is the product of the algebra and nothing else.

Proposition (the tables of the quaternionic products). The natural sign in the first slot replaces $T$ by $T^{\natural}=A-Bj$; in the two quaternionic products the coefficients $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $D$ therefore change sign, and the tables are the second and fourth displays above.

Proof. $T^{\natural}W=(A-Bj)(C+Dj)=AC+ADj-BCj-BDj^{2}=(AC-BD)+(AD-BC)j$, using $j^{2}=+1$; the same computation with $W$ replaced by $W^{*}$ gives the fourth display, and the third follows from the second by conjugating the coefficients of $W$ and using $B\bar D+\bar B D=2\operatorname{Re}(B\bar D)$, which is the source of the identity $TW^{*}=(A\bar C-B\bar D)+(B\bar C-A\bar D)j$.

Remark (the double-complex alternativity). The four products are the double-complex analogue of the four products of the biquaternion category; the second slot's star is a coefficient conjugation, because the coefficients are complex, and the first slot's natural sign is the intrinsic conjugate of the algebra. The two categories carry the same four slot choices on different coordinates: the biquaternion element has the four complex coordinates $P_0,P_1,P_2,P_3$ and its products carry a cross-product term, while the reduced biquaternion element has the two coordinates $A$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, so its vector part is one-dimensional and no cross-product term appears.

The Scalar-Part Forms

The scalar-part form of a product is $\mathrm{Sc}$ applied to the product, and the four forms are $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$.

Definition. The scalar-part form of a product $\star$ is $$ \mathrm{Form}_\star(T,W)=\mathrm{Sc}(T\star W), $$ where $\mathrm{Sc}$ is the coefficient of $1$. The four forms are $$ \langle T,W\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(TW)=AC+BD,\qquad \langle T,W\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(T^{\natural}W)=AC-BD, $$ $$ \langle T,W\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(TW^{*})=A\bar C-B\bar D,\qquad \langle T,W\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(T^{\natural}W^{*})=A\bar C+B\bar D. $$

Theorem (bilinear against sesquilinear). $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ are $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ are $\mathbb{C}$-sesquilinear, linear in the first variable and antilinear in the second; $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ are symmetric, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ are Hermitian, $$ \langle T,W\rangle=\langle W,T\rangle,\quad \langle T,W\rangle_{\natural}=\langle W,T\rangle_{\natural},\quad \langle W,T\rangle_{*}=\overline{\langle T,W\rangle_{*}},\quad \langle W,T\rangle_{\natural*}=\overline{\langle T,W\rangle_{\natural*}}. $$

Proof. $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ are built from the bilinear products $TW$ and $T^{\natural}W$, which have no conjugation, so they are bilinear; the natural sign is $\mathbb{C}$-linear and the star is $\mathbb{C}$-antilinear (The Two Involutions and the Group of Involutions), so $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ are linear in the first variable and antilinear in the second. Symmetry of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the commutativity of the complex multiplication in the formulas $AC+BD$ and $AC-BD$; Hermiticity of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is $\overline{A\bar C-B\bar D}=\bar A C-\bar B D=C\bar A-D\bar B$, the same form with the arguments exchanged.

Theorem (the Gram matrices). On the complex basis $1,j$ and on the real basis $1,i,j,ij$ the forms have the Gram matrices $$ G_{B}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1),\qquad G_{N}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),\qquad G_{H}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),\qquad G_{K}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1) $$ over $\mathbb{C}$, and $$ \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1),\quad \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,1),\quad \operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1),\quad \operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1) $$ over $\mathbb{R}$; the four forms are non-degenerate, and their signatures and fields of values are those of the table of §The Four Algebraic Norms.

Proof. The complex Gram entries are the scalar parts of the products of the basis with itself conjugated or not: $\langle 1,1\rangle=\langle j,j\rangle=1$, since $j^{2}=1$; $\langle 1,1\rangle_{\natural}=1$, $\langle j,j\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(j^{\natural}j)=\mathrm{Sc}(-j^{2})=-1$; $\langle 1,1\rangle_{*}=1$, $\langle j,j\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(jj^{*})=\mathrm{Sc}(-1)=-1$; $\langle 1,1\rangle_{\natural*}=1$, $\langle j,j\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(j^{\natural}j^{*})=\mathrm{Sc}((-j)(-j))=\mathrm{Sc}(j^{2})=1$. The real matrices are the same entries read on the real basis: $\langle i,i\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(i^{2})=-1$ and $\langle ij,ij\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}((ij)^{2})=-1$, giving $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1)$; and likewise for the others, using $i^{*}=-i$ and $(ij)^{*}=ij$. Non-degeneracy is the non-vanishing of every diagonal entry.

The Four Algebraic Norms

The algebraic norm of a product is the diagonal of its scalar-part form, and the four norms are $\langle\cdot\rangle, \langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}, \langle\cdot\rangle_{*}, \langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ read on one argument.

Definition. The algebraic norm of a product is the diagonal of its scalar-part form, $$ \mathrm{Norm}_\star(T)=\mathrm{Form}_\star(T,T), $$ one object with two arities: the form with two arguments and its diagonal with one. The four algebraic norms are $$ \langle T\rangle=A^{2}+B^{2},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{*}=|A|^{2}-|B|^{2},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{\natural*}=|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}. $$

This is the definition of Conventions in Mathematics, §The Conjugations, and of The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternioid $\mathbb{C}$ Space of the biquaternion category. The two arities of each letter are one object and are never given two letters.

Theorem (the four norms, their values and their signatures). The four norms have the following fields of values, real signatures and definiteness:

norm product formula values real signature definite? multiplicative?
$\langle\cdot\rangle$ plain bilinear $A^{2}+B^{2}$ $\mathbb{C}$ $(2,2)$ no no
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ quaternionic bilinear $A^{2}-B^{2}$ $\mathbb{C}$ $(2,2)$ no yes
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ plain sesquilinear $\lvert A\rvert^{2}-\lvert B\rvert^{2}$ $\mathbb{R}$ $(2,2)$ no no
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ quaternionic sesquilinear $\lvert A\rvert^{2}+\lvert B\rvert^{2}$ $\mathbb{R}$ $(4,0)$ yes, positive no

Proof. In the real coordinates $A=a+ia'$, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=b+ib'$, $$ \langle T\rangle=a^{2}-a'^{2}+b^{2}-b'^{2}+2i(aa'+bb'),\qquad \langle T\rangle_{\natural}=a^{2}-a'^{2}-b^{2}+b'^{2}+2i(aa'-bb'), $$ $$ \langle T\rangle_{*}=a^{2}+a'^{2}-b^{2}-b'^{2},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{\natural*}=a^{2}+a'^{2}+b^{2}+b'^{2}. $$ $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ take complex values in general, and their real parts are indefinite quadratic forms of signature $(2,2)$; $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$ is a real indefinite form of signature $(2,2)$; $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is the sum of four squares, hence real, positive definite and of signature $(4,0)$. The definiteness column is read from the signs: only $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is one-signed. The multiplicativity column is proved in the next section and in §The Comparison of the Four Products.

Corollary (the topological norm is $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$). The only positive-definite one of the four is $\langle T\rangle_{\natural*}=|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}$, which is the square of the Euclidean norm of the four real coordinates; it is the topological norm of the space, and the other three are algebraic norms only.

Proof. $\langle T\rangle_{\natural*}=a^{2}+a'^{2}+b^{2}+b'^{2}$ is the sum of the four squares of the real coordinates, and it vanishes exactly at $T=0$; the other three vanish on nonzero elements, $\langle T\rangle$ and $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}$ in the complex plane and $\langle T\rangle_{*}$ on the two real lines $|A|^{2}=|B|^{2}$, so none of them is positive definite.

The Multiplicative Norm

The only multiplicative one of the four norms is $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}$, and its multiplicativity is under the multiplication; it is the reduced norm of the algebra and its invertibility criterion.

Theorem (the quaternionic bilinear norm is multiplicative). For all $T,W$, $$ \langle TW\rangle_{\natural}=\langle T\rangle_{\natural}\,\langle W\rangle_{\natural},\qquad \langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}=(A+B)(A-B). $$

Proof. In the idempotent coordinates $T=(A+B)\Pi_{1}+(A-B)\Pi_{2}$ and $W=(C+D)\Pi_{1}+(C-D)\Pi_{2}$, the product is componentwise (Reduced Biquaternion Space), so $TW$ has coordinates $\bigl((A+B)(C+D),(A-B)(C-D)\bigr)$; the norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the product of the two coordinates, which is multiplicative in the coordinates: $$ (A+B)(C+D)\cdot(A-B)(C-D)=\bigl[(A+B)(A-B)\bigr]\bigl[(C+D)(C-D)\bigr]=\langle T\rangle_{\natural}\langle W\rangle_{\natural}. $$

Corollary (the reduced norm and the criterion). $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}$ is the determinant of the multiplication by $T$ in the two-dimensional idempotent coordinates, it is the reduced norm of the algebra, and $$ T\ \text{is invertible}\iff \langle T\rangle_{\natural}\neq0,\qquad T^{-1}=\frac{A-Bj}{A^{2}-B^{2}}=\frac{T^{\natural}}{\langle T\rangle_{\natural}}. $$

Proof. In the idempotent coordinates the multiplication is $U\mapsto(a,b)\cdot(U_1,U_2)$ with $a=A+B$ and $b=A-B$, so the determinant is $ab=\langle T\rangle_{\natural}$; it is nonzero exactly when both coordinates are nonzero, which is the criterion of Reduced Biquaternion Space. The inverse is verified by $T\cdot(A-Bj)=A^{2}-B^{2}=\langle T\rangle_{\natural}$.

The three other norms are not multiplicative under their products. The failure of $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is exhibited in Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions: the pair $T=\Pi_{1}$, $W=\Pi_{2}$ separates the two sides, since $T W=0$ gives $\langle TW\rangle=0$ while $\langle \Pi_{1}\rangle\langle \Pi_{2}\rangle=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac14$. The norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the reduced norm and the algebraic norm of the quaternionic bilinear product, and the two roles coincide: it is the determinant of the multiplication in the idempotent coordinates and the diagonal of the quaternionic bilinear form.

Relations Between the Four Products

The four products are linked by the idempotent coordinates and by the conjugate of a factor, and the links are recorded rather than used.

Theorem (the automorphism relations). Each of the bar, the natural sign and the star commutes with the multiplication, so it moves a product to the product of the images; consequently the four products are the four ways of placing a conjugation in one or both slots, and $$ \overline{TW}=\bar T\bar W,\qquad (TW)^{\natural}=T^{\natural}W^{\natural},\qquad (TW)^{*}=T^{*}W^{*}. $$

Proof. The three multiplicativities are those of The Two Involutions and the Group of Involutions, using the commutativity of the algebra.

Theorem (the two-term relations). The plain and the quaternionic products differ by twice the vector part of the first factor, and the sesquilinear products by twice the vector part of the conjugated factor: $$ TW+T^{\natural}W=2AW,\qquad TW-T^{\natural}W=2BjW, $$ $$ TW^{*}+T^{\natural}W^{*}=2AW^{*},\qquad TW^{*}-T^{\natural}W^{*}=2BjW^{*}. $$

Proof. Directly from $T=A+Bj$ and $T^{\natural}=A-Bj$: the sum has the $Bj$ term cancelled and the difference the $A$ term.

Theorem (the two-term relations of the forms). On the two arguments the forms are paired by their bilinear or sesquilinear partner: $$ \langle T,W\rangle+\langle T,W\rangle_{\natural}=2AC,\qquad \langle T,W\rangle-\langle T,W\rangle_{\natural}=2BD, $$ $$ \langle T,W\rangle_{*}+\langle T,W\rangle_{\natural*}=2A\bar C,\qquad \langle T,W\rangle_{*}-\langle T,W\rangle_{\natural*}=-2B\bar D. $$

Proof. Add and subtract the displays of the definitions of the four forms.

The Comparison of the Four Products

The four products are distinguished by associativity, commutativity, the unit and the norm, and the table is the one below.

product bilinear/sesquilinear associative commutative unit its algebraic norm multiplicative norm
$TW$ bilinear yes yes two-sided $1$ $\langle\cdot\rangle=A^{2}+B^{2}$ no
$T^{\natural}W$ bilinear no no left unit $1$ $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}$ yes
$TW^{*}$ sesquilinear no no right unit $1$ $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}=\lvert A\rvert^{2}-\lvert B\rvert^{2}$ no
$T^{\natural}W^{*}$ sesquilinear no no none $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}=\lvert A\rvert^{2}+\lvert B\rvert^{2}$ no

Theorem (the multiplication is the associative and commutative case). The general plain bilinear product $TW$ is the multiplication of the algebra; it is the only one of the four that is associative and the only one that is commutative, and it is the only one with a two-sided unit.

Proof. The associativity of $TW$ and the two-sided unit $1$ are Reduced Biquaternion Space. The other three products are not associative: for the quaternionic bilinear product the triple $T=W=V=j$ separates $((T^{\natural}W)^{\natural}V)=(-j)$ from $T^{\natural}(W^{\natural}V)=j$, and for the two sesquilinear products the triple $T=W=V=i$ separates the two sides, $((i\star i)\star i)=-i$ against $i\star(i\star i)=i$ in both cases. None of the other three is commutative: for the quaternionic bilinear product the pair $T=1$, $W=j$ gives $T^{\natural}W=j$ against $W^{\natural}T=-j$. The one-sided units are read as in the table: $1$ is a left unit of $T^{\natural}W$ because $1^{\natural}W=W$ and not a right unit because $T^{\natural}1=T^{\natural}\neq T$ in general, and $1$ is a right unit of $TW^{*}$ because $T1^{*}=T$ and not a left unit because $1W^{*}=W^{*}\neq W$ in general; the product $T^{\natural}W^{*}$ has neither.

Corollary (zero divisors and the absence of division). The multiplication has zero divisors and is not a division algebra: every element of the first idempotent line annihilates every element of the second, and the invertible elements are those of non-vanishing reduced norm $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$.

Proof. The zero divisors are Reduced Biquaternion Space and are read again in Zero Divisors of the General Plain Algebra of the Reduced Biquaternions; the criterion is the corollary of §The Multiplicative Norm. The words commutative, zero divisors and absence of division are the ones the four products of this article bear, and they are stated wherever they bear on a result.

Summary

The reduced biquaternion space carries four general products, $TW$, $T^{\natural}W$, $TW^{*}$ and $T^{\natural}W^{*}$, defined by which slot carries the natural sign and which carries the star, with the coordinate tables of §The Four General Products. Their scalar-part forms are $$ B=AC+BD,\qquad N=AC-BD,\qquad H=A\bar C-B\bar D,\qquad K=A\bar C+B\bar D, $$ the first two $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear and symmetric, the last two $\mathbb{C}$-sesquilinear and Hermitian, and their Gram matrices are $\operatorname{diag}(1,1)$, $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $\operatorname{diag}(1,1)$ on the complex basis. Their algebraic norms are the diagonals $$ \langle T\rangle=A^{2}+B^{2},\quad \langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2},\quad \langle T\rangle_{*}=|A|^{2}-|B|^{2},\quad \langle T\rangle_{\natural*}=|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}, $$ complex-valued and indefinite of real signature $(2,2)$ for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, real-valued and indefinite of signature $(2,2)$ for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}$, and real-valued positive definite of signature $(4,0)$ for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$.

The multiplicative one is $\langle T\rangle_{\natural}=A^{2}-B^{2}=(A+B)(A-B)$, the reduced norm of the algebra and its invertibility criterion, $T$ invertible $\iff \langle T\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$; the positive-definite one is $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$, the topological norm. The algebraic norm is an element of the algebra and is not the topological norm, and only $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is both. The general plain bilinear product is the multiplication: it is the only associative and the only commutative one, with unit $1$, while the other three are neither associative nor commutative, with the one-sided units of the table; and the multiplication has zero divisors and is not a division algebra. The forms are linked by the relations $B+N=2AC$, $B-N=2BD$, $H+K=2A\bar C$ and $H-K=-2B\bar D$, and the four products by $TW\pm T^{\natural}W=2AW,2BjW$ and $TW^{*}\pm T^{\natural}W^{*}=2AW^{*},2BjW^{*}$.

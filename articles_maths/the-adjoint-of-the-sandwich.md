
# __The Adjoint of the Sandwich__

## Introduction

The sandwich $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b$ is the two-sided multiplication $x\mapsto axb$, and its adjoint for the standard form $\langle x,y\rangle=\operatorname{Sc}(\hat xy)$ is the sandwich by the Clifford-conjugated parameters,

$$
T_{a,b}^{*} = T_{\hat a,\hat b} , \qquad \hat a=\alpha(\tilde a) .
$$

The adjoint therefore acts on the pair $(a,b)$ by the involution in each factor, and the sandwich is self-adjoint exactly when the two parameters are fixed, up to the central units that leave the operator unchanged. The article computes the adjoint, deduces the self-adjointness and orthogonality conditions, and specialises to the inner automorphism $T_{a,a^{-1}}$, whose adjoint is the inner automorphism by the conjugated parameter. The signed version of the same computation is *The Adjoint of the Sandwich with the Signed Product*, and the general two-sided family is *The Adjoint of the Two-Sided Multiplication Operator*.

**The boundaries.** The sandwich and its composition are *The Sandwich on a Clifford Algebra*; the one-sided adjoints are *The Adjoint of the Left Multiplication on a Clifford Algebra* and *The Adjoint of the Right Multiplication*; the form and the conjugation are *The Twisted Adjoint on a Clifford Algebra*, on which this article depends. The inner automorphisms and the versors are *The Clifford, Pin and Spin Groups with Signed Inner Conjugation*. The base is a field $F$ of characteristic not $2$ with a non-degenerate $q$, $q(u)=B(u,u)$, $uv+vu=2B(u,v)$.

## The Adjoint of the Sandwich

**Theorem.** For all $a,b$,

$$
T_{a,b}^{*} = (L_aR_b)^{*} = R_{\hat b}L_{\hat a} = L_{\hat a}R_{\hat b} = T_{\hat a,\hat b} ,
$$

and the map $(a,b)\mapsto(\hat a,\hat b)$ is an involution on the pairs; the adjoint is an involution of the two-sided family, $T_{a,b}^{**}=T_{a,b}$.

**Proof.** The adjoint of a composition is the composition of the adjoints in reverse order, so $(L_aR_b)^*=R_b^*L_a^*=R_{\hat b}L_{\hat a}$, and the two families commute, $R_{\hat b}L_{\hat a}=L_{\hat a}R_{\hat b}$; the result is the sandwich by $(\hat a,\hat b)$. The double adjoint is the double conjugation, which is the identity. The one-factor adjoints are *The Adjoint of the Left Multiplication on a Clifford Algebra* and *The Adjoint of the Right Multiplication*.

**Proposition (self-adjointness).** $T_{a,b}$ is self-adjoint if and only if $(\hat a,\hat b)=(a c,c^{-1}b)$ for a **central unit** $c$; in particular $T_{a,b}$ is self-adjoint whenever $\hat a=a$ and $\hat b=b$.

**Proof.** The two-sided operators $T_{a,b}$ and $T_{a',b'}$ are equal exactly when $a'=ac$, $b'=c^{-1}b$ for a central unit $c$ — substituting $x=1$ forces $ab=a'b'$, and the comparison for all $x$ forces $c$ central; this is the indeterminacy of the sandwich of *The Sandwich on a Clifford Algebra*. Combining with $T_{a,b}^*=T_{\hat a,\hat b}$ gives the stated condition, and the sufficient condition is the special case $c=1$.

**Proposition (orthogonality).** $T_{a,b}$ is orthogonal for the standard form if and only if $L_{\hat aa}$ and $R_{\hat bb}$ compose to the identity, that is

$$
T_{a,b}^{*}T_{a,b}=L_{\hat aa}R_{\hat bb}=\operatorname{id} ;
$$

for a pair of units with $\hat aa=\hat bb=1$ the sandwich is orthogonal, and more generally the condition may be met by a cancellation of the two factors when one of them is not orthogonal.

**Proof.** $T_{a,b}^*T_{a,b}=L_{\hat a}R_{\hat b}L_aR_b=L_{\hat aa}R_{\hat bb}$, using the commutation of the two families; the operator $L_cR_d$ is the identity exactly when $c x d=x$ for all $x$, which for $c,d$ units forces $c=d^{-1}$ central in the central-simple case and is satisfied in particular for $c=d=1$, i.e. $\hat aa=\hat bb=1$.

## The Inner Automorphism

**Proposition.** The sandwich by $(a,a^{-1})$ is the inner automorphism $\mathrm{Ad}_a$, and its adjoint is the inner automorphism by the conjugated parameter,

$$
T_{a,a^{-1}}^{*} = T_{\hat a,(\hat a)^{-1}} = \mathrm{Ad}_{\hat a} ;
$$

consequently $\mathrm{Ad}_a$ is orthogonal exactly when $\hat aa$ is central and $\hat aa=1$, that is when $a$ is a unit with $\hat a=a^{-1}$, and it is self-adjoint exactly when $\hat a$ is a central multiple of $a$.

**Proof.** $\widehat{a^{-1}}=(\hat a)^{-1}$ because the conjugation is an anti-automorphism and preserves inverses; the sandwich by $(a,a^{-1})$ acts by $x\mapsto axa^{-1}=\mathrm{Ad}_a(x)$; the adjoint formula gives the first display, and the orthogonality and self-adjointness conditions are the general ones read with $b=a^{-1}$: $\hat bb=(\hat a)^{-1}\hat a$, and $T_{a,a^{-1}}^*T_{a,a^{-1}}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat a}\mathrm{Ad}_a=\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat aa}$, which is the identity when $\hat aa$ is central and equals $1$.

**Remark (the geometry).** The inner automorphisms by the units with $\hat a=a^{-1}$ form the group of orthogonal inner automorphisms of the algebra; in the definite case with $q(u)=-1$ they are the rotations generated by the versors, by *The Clifford, Pin and Spin Groups with Signed Inner Conjugation*. The adjoint computation above is the algebraic reason the group is closed under inversion, which is the versor condition $\hat a=a^{-1}$.

## Worked Cases

### The Quaternions

For $\mathrm{Cl}\cong\mathbb H$ the conjugation is the quaternionic conjugate, and $\mathrm{Ad}_a$ is the rotation of $\mathbb{R}^3$ by the unit quaternion $\tilde a=|a|^{-1}a$; the adjoint is $\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat a}=\mathrm{Ad}_{|a|^{-1}\tilde a^{-1}}$, the inverse rotation, and the sandwich is orthogonal exactly for $|a|=1$.

### The Negative-Definite Plane

For $\mathrm{Cl}$ of the negative-definite plane with $a=e_1$, $b=e_1^{-1}=-e_1$, the sandwich is $\mathrm{Ad}_{e_1}$: the conjugation $x\mapsto e_1xe_1^{-1}$, on the span of $e_1,e_2$ the half-turn; its adjoint is $\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat e_1}=\mathrm{Ad}_{-e_1}=\mathrm{Ad}_{e_1}$, so the half-turn is self-adjoint and orthogonal.

### A Non-Orthogonal Sandwich

For $a=\lambda$ a scalar and $b=1$, $T_{\lambda,1}=\lambda\operatorname{id}$ has adjoint $\lambda\operatorname{id}$ if $\lambda$ is central, and it is self-adjoint while orthogonal only for $\lambda^2=1$; the example shows that self-adjointness and orthogonality are independent conditions, the first a $\hat{}$-condition and the second a norm condition.

## Summary

The adjoint of the sandwich is the sandwich by the **Clifford-conjugated parameters**, $T_{a,b}^*=R_{\hat b}L_{\hat a}=T_{\hat a,\hat b}$, and the adjoint is an involution of the two-sided family. The sandwich is **self-adjoint** exactly when $(\hat a,\hat b)=(ac,c^{-1}b)$ with $c$ central, and **orthogonal** exactly when $L_{\hat aa}R_{\hat bb}=\operatorname{id}$, the sufficient case being $\hat aa=\hat bb=1$. The inner automorphism $\mathrm{Ad}_a=T_{a,a^{-1}}$ has adjoint $\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat a}$, is orthogonal exactly for units with $\hat a=a^{-1}$, and is self-adjoint exactly when $\hat a$ is a central multiple of $a$; the orthogonal inner automorphisms are the rotations generated by the versors. The sandwich is *The Sandwich on a Clifford Algebra*, the form is *The Twisted Adjoint on a Clifford Algebra*, and the signed version of the computation is *The Adjoint of the Sandwich with the Signed Product*.

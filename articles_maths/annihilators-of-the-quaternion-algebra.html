Annihilators of the Quaternion Algebra

Introduction

The zero divisor of a product is a property of one element alone: it says that some nonzero partner exists that the element kills. The annihilator says which partners those are, and it is the finer object. It is the set of elements killed by a fixed element, $$ A_\star(\tilde q_1)=\{\tilde q_2:\tilde q_1\star\tilde q_2=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde q_1)=\{\tilde q_2:\tilde q_2\star\tilde q_1=0\}, $$ the left and the right annihilator (Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, §The Zero Divisor and the Annihilator). When the product is carried by a multiplication operator the annihilator is the kernel of that operator, so it is a linear subspace and its computation is one of linear algebra.

The block of this article is the quaternion multiplication, the product $\tilde q_1\tilde q_2$ of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$ (Introduction to the Quaternion Algebra of Quaternions). It is associative, it carries the two-sided unit $e_t$, and over $\mathbb{R}$ it is a division algebra: every nonzero element is invertible, by the norm criterion $\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$ (Norm and Invertibility in the Quaternion Algebra). The block therefore has no zero divisors. The zero-divisor set, empty here, is read in Zero Divisors of the Quaternion Algebra, above this article in this category. What is left for this article is the vanishing of the annihilator, and it is short for that reason. It owns the annihilator of the block, the two multiplication operators, the rank table and the reading on the remarkable subspaces.

The Annihilator of a Product

Definition (annihilator). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$ and let $\tilde q_1\in\mathbb{H}$. The left annihilator and the right annihilator of $\tilde q_1$ in $\star$ are $$ A_\star(\tilde q_1)=\{\tilde q_2:\tilde q_1\star\tilde q_2=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde q_1)=\{\tilde q_2:\tilde q_2\star\tilde q_1=0\}. $$ They are the kernels of the left and the right multiplication, and they are nonzero exactly when $\tilde q_1$ is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Throughout this article the product is the quaternion multiplication $\star$, and unqualified, annihilator means the annihilator of $\star$. The two one-sided multiplications are the operators $$ L_{\tilde q_1}\tilde q_2=\tilde q_1\tilde q_2,\qquad R_{\tilde q_1}\tilde q_2=\tilde q_2\tilde q_1, $$ the left and the right multiplication of the algebra (One-Sided Operators on the Quaternion Algebra), and $$ A_\star(\tilde q_1)=\ker L_{\tilde q_1},\qquad B_\star(\tilde q_1)=\ker R_{\tilde q_1}. $$

The Annihilator Is Zero

Theorem (the annihilator of a nonzero element is zero). Let $\tilde q_1\in\mathbb{H}$.

  • If $\tilde q_1\neq0$ then $A_\star(\tilde q_1)=B_\star(\tilde q_1)=\{0\}$.
  • If $\tilde q_1=0$ then $A_\star(\tilde q_1)=B_\star(\tilde q_1)=\mathbb{H}$.

Proof. If $\tilde q_1\tilde q_2=0$ with $\tilde q_1\neq0$, multiply on the left by the inverse $\tilde q_1^{-1}$, which exists by the division property; then $\tilde q_2=0$. The right side is the same computation with the inverse on the right. At $\tilde q_1=0$ both products vanish for every $\tilde q_2$, so both annihilators are the whole space.

Corollary (the two sides coincide). $A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)$ for every $\tilde q$: off the origin both are $\{0\}$, and at the origin both are $\mathbb{H}$. No nonzero element is a zero divisor of either side, and the annihilator is never a proper nonzero subspace.

The Multiplication Operator and the Rank

Proposition (the operators and their invariants). In the basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ the operators $L_{\tilde q}$ and $R_{\tilde q}$ of an element $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$ have trace $4q_t$ each and determinant $$ \boxed{\; \det L_{\tilde q}=\det R_{\tilde q}=\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}^2=(q_t^2+|\vec q|^2)^2 \;} ,$$ and the characteristic polynomial $$ \boxed{\; \chi_{L_{\tilde q}}(t)=\chi_{R_{\tilde q}}(t)=(t^2-2q_tt+\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural})^2 \;} .$$

Proof. The matrix of $L_{\tilde q}$ is the left multiplication matrix of the quaternion $\tilde q$: its trace is $4q_t$ and its determinant is the square of the norm, $\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}^2$. The matrix of $R_{\tilde q}$ is the transpose of that of $L_{\tilde q}$ for the plain form, $R_{\tilde q}=L_{\tilde q}^{\sim}$ in the notation $F^{\sim}=DF^{\mathsf T}D$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ (Association and the Transpose on the Quaternion Algebra, and One-Sided Operators on the Quaternion Algebra, $L_{\tilde q_3}^{\sim}=R_{\tilde q_3}$); a transpose has the same trace and the same characteristic polynomial, hence the same determinant. The quadratic factor $t^2-2q_tt+\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}$ has discriminant $-4|\vec q|^2$: it is $(t-q_t)^2$ at $\vec q=0$, and it has no real root when $\vec q\neq0$. The invariants are computed exactly on $e_t$, $(2,-3,5,7)$, $(0,1,2,3)$ and $0$ in the verification of this article.

Corollary (the rank and the annihilator). The rank of the two operators and the two annihilators of $\tilde q$ are

$\tilde q$ $\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}$ $\operatorname{rank}L_{\tilde q},\operatorname{rank}R_{\tilde q}$ $\dim A_\star(\tilde q),\dim B_\star(\tilde q)$
$0$ $0$ $0$ $4$
$\tilde q\neq0$ $\neq0$ $4$ $0$

Proof. The determinant $\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}^2$ is nonzero exactly when $\tilde q\neq0$, by the positive definiteness of the norm; then both operators are invertible and both annihilators are $\{0\}$. The origin gives the zero operator, of kernel the whole space. All dimensions are real dimensions, the space being the real vector space $\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{R}^4$.

The Dichotomy

The dichotomy of the block is the annihilator's, and it is as short as a dichotomy can be: off the origin the annihilator is $\{0\}$, at the origin it is the whole space. There is no intermediate case, and there is no locus on which the rank drops. The zero-divisor cone of the complexified block is the norm equation $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}=0$; over $\mathbb{R}$ the norm is positive definite, so that cone meets the real space only at the origin. The dichotomy is therefore exactly the split of the space into the origin and its complement, and it carries no more than the division property itself. Where the complexified block has a genuine hypersurface of zero divisors and reads a pair of annihilator planes on it (Annihilators of the Quaternion Algebra, the biquaternion block), the real block has a single point and nothing on it but the whole space.

The Annihilator on the Remarkable Subspaces

The centre. A nonzero central element $\tilde q_1=\lambda e_t$ with $\lambda\neq0$ has $L_{\tilde q_1}\tilde q_2=\lambda\tilde q_2$, the scalar operator, so it is invertible and its annihilator is $\{0\}$. The centre is the scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_t$ (Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions), and a nonzero central element is invertible as a nonzero scalar is.

The vector subspace. A nonzero pure element $\tilde q=\vec q$ has norm $|\vec q|^2>0$, so it is invertible and its annihilator is $\{0\}$. The vector subspace is as free of annihilators as the whole space.

The general reading. On a remarkable subspace the annihilator of an element of the subspace is the intersection of the ambient annihilator with the subspace. The ambient annihilator is $\{0\}$ at every nonzero element, so the annihilator of a nonzero element of the centre or of the vector subspace is again $\{0\}$; the only nonzero annihilator anywhere is the whole space, at the single origin.

Summary

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners the element kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left and the right multiplication. The quaternion multiplication is the associative product of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$, which over $\mathbb{R}$ is a division algebra. Its operators $L_{\tilde q}$ and $R_{\tilde q}$ have trace $4q_t$ and determinant $\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}^2=(q_t^2+|\vec q|^2)^2$, and characteristic polynomial $(t^2-2q_tt+\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural})^2$. The annihilator of every nonzero element is $\{0\}$ on both sides, and the two sides coincide trivially; the annihilator of the origin is the whole space. The dichotomy is therefore the split of the space into the origin and its complement, with no intermediate case and no rank drop, because the zero-divisor cone of the complexified block meets the real space only at the origin. On the remarkable subspaces, the centre $\mathbb{R}e_t$ and the vector subspace, the annihilator of a nonzero element is again $\{0\}$, a nonzero central element being an invertible scalar. The zero divisors are the empty set, read in Zero Divisors of the Quaternion Algebra, above this article in this category, and this article is short because there is nothing beyond the division property for the annihilator to characterise.

Summary of Notation

Symbol Meaning
$\tilde q_1\tilde q_2$ the quaternion multiplication, the product of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$
$A_\star(\tilde q_1)=\{\tilde q_2:\tilde q_1\tilde q_2=0\}$ the left annihilator of $\tilde q_1$, the kernel of the left multiplication
$B_\star(\tilde q_1)=\{\tilde q_2:\tilde q_2\tilde q_1=0\}$ the right annihilator of $\tilde q_1$, the kernel of the right multiplication
$L_{\tilde q},R_{\tilde q}$ the left and the right multiplication operators, of determinant $\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}^2$ and trace $4q_t$
$\chi_{L_{\tilde q}}(t)=(t^2-2q_tt+\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural})^2$ the characteristic polynomial of the operator
$\langle\tilde q\rangle_{\natural}=q_t^2+|\vec q|^2$ the quaternion norm, positive definite over $\mathbb{R}$
$A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)$ the two sides coincide, being $\{0\}$ off the origin and $\mathbb{H}$ at the origin

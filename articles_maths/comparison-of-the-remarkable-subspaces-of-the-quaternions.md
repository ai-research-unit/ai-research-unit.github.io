# __Comparison of the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions__

## Introduction

This is the reference article of the remarkable-subspace group of the chapter. The two subspaces themselves, with their bases and dimensions, are *Introduction to the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions*; the involutions are *Quaternion Involutions and Projections*; the decompositions are *Decompositions Along the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions*.

## The Two Subspaces

The two remarkable subspaces are
$$
\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\{\tilde q:\tilde q^{\natural}=\tilde q\}=\mathbb{R}e_t, \qquad
\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\tilde q:\tilde q^{\natural}=-\tilde q\}=\mathbb{R}\vec e_x\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec e_y\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec e_z.
$$

**Proposition.** They are supplementary and their intersection is the origin:
$$
\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}\cap\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=0, \qquad
\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}+\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\mathbb{H}, \qquad 1+3=4.
$$
The first is a subalgebra, indeed the centre, and the second is not closed under the product.

**Proof.** A quaternion fixed by ${}^{\natural}$ has $\vec q=0$ and a quaternion anti-fixed has $q_t=0$, so the intersection is $\tilde q=0$; every $\tilde q=q_t+\vec q$ is the sum of one of each. The subalgebra and centre statements are *Introduction to the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions*; the failure of closure is the product law $\vec q_1\vec q_2=-\langle\vec q_1,\vec q_2\rangle+\vec q_1\times\vec q_2$ recalled there.

## The Sign Pattern of the Natural Sign

The natural sign acts on the coordinates by the sign vector
$$
{}^{\natural}:\ (q_t,q_x,q_y,q_z)\longmapsto(+q_t,-q_x,-q_y,-q_z),
$$
with multiplicity $+1$ on the scalar line and multiplicity $-1$ on the vector space, that is, $1$ and $3$. Its negative $-\natural$ has the signs exchanged, $-1$ on the scalar line and $+1$ on the vector space, so the two together have the eigenspaces
$$
\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\text{fixed space of }{}^{\natural}=\text{anti-fixed space of }-{}^{\natural}, \qquad
\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\text{anti-fixed space of }{}^{\natural}=\text{fixed space of }-{}^{\natural}.
$$

## The Axis Lines and the Planes They Fix

Each axis line $\mathbb{R}\nu$ of the vector subspace, $\nu$ a unit imaginary quaternion, carries the inner involution $\iota_\nu(\tilde q)=\nu \tilde q\nu^{-1}$ of *Quaternion Involutions and Projections*.

| object | value |
|---|---|
| axis line | $\mathbb{R}\nu$, one per pair $\pm\nu$ |
| involution | $\iota_\nu$, an involutive automorphism |
| fixed plane | $\mathbb{C}_\nu=\mathbb{R}e_t\oplus\mathbb{R}\nu$, the commutative subalgebra of the axis |
| negated plane | $\nu^\perp=\{\vec q:\nu\cdot\vec q=0\}$, its orthogonal complement |
| sign pattern | $+1$ of multiplicity $2$ on $\mathbb{C}_\nu$, $-1$ of multiplicity $2$ on $\nu^\perp$ |
| action on the two subspaces | preserves $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}$; on $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$ fixes the line $\mathbb{R}\nu$ and negates the plane $\nu^\perp$ |

The reflection $\rho_\nu=-\iota_\nu$ has the same sign pattern with the labels exchanged and cuts the same two planes. The inner involutions thus produce, for every axis, a decomposition of $\mathbb{H}$ into a two-dimensional commutative subalgebra and a two-dimensional plane, as in *Decompositions Along the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions*; they move the vector subspace among its lines and planes but single out no further invariant subspace of the algebra.

## The Two-Subspace Lattice

The remarkable subspaces of an algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ under inclusion form its **lattice of fixed spaces**, the intersections acting as meets and the sums as joins. For $\mathbb{H}$ the lattice is the diamond
$$
0\subsetneq\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}\subsetneq\mathbb{H}, \qquad 0\subsetneq\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})\subsetneq\mathbb{H},
$$
the two chains meeting only at their ends: the two proper subspaces are incomparable, they meet in $0$ and they join to $\mathbb{H}$. The lattice has exactly the four elements $0,\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}},\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H}),\mathbb{H}$, with no intermediate subspace beyond those.

## Why the List of the Two Is the Whole List

**Theorem.** The remarkable real subspaces of $\mathbb{H}$ are exactly $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}$ and $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$.

**Proof.** A remarkable subspace, in the sense of this group, is a fixed or an anti-fixed space of a distinguished conjugation of the algebra. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the coefficientwise bar is the identity and the star is ${}^{\natural}$, so the distinguished conjugations are ${}^{\natural}$ and its negative, whose eigenspaces are the scalar line and the vector space. The inner automorphisms $\iota_\nu$ are not distinguished conjugations but automorphisms of the inner class; their fixed and anti-fixed spaces are the plane $\mathbb{C}_\nu$ and the plane $\nu^\perp$, and these are not singled out by the algebra — a different axis $\nu'$ gives different planes, and the group generated by the $\iota_\nu$ moves any axis to any other — so they are not remarkable subspaces of $\mathbb{H}$ but objects of the family of one axis. The remaining involutions of the group, the twists $\alpha_\nu$, are anti-automorphisms whose fixed and anti-fixed spaces are the three-dimensional plane $\mathbb{R}e_t\oplus\nu^\perp$ and the axis line $\mathbb{R}\nu$, so they single out no new remarkable subspace either — the scalar line is their only summand that is one. Hence the list is the two.

This is the same argument as *Introduction to the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions*, stated here in the lattice language; the two articles agree by construction, the introduction owning the definition and the comparison owning the count.

## Summary

The quaternion algebra has two remarkable real subspaces, the scalar line and the vector space, supplementary and with zero intersection. Each axis line of the vector space carries an inner involution, with fixed plane the commutative subalgebra of the axis and negated plane the orthogonal complement.

## Summary of Notation

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}$ | the scalar line, the fixed space of ${}^{\natural}$, dimension one |
| $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$ | the vector space, the anti-fixed space of ${}^{\natural}$, dimension three |
| sign vector of ${}^{\natural}$ | $(+,-,-,-)$, multiplicities $(1,3)$ |
| $\mathbb{C}_\nu,\nu^\perp$ | the fixed and anti-fixed planes of the inner involution $\iota_\nu$ |

The Spin-Half Carriers

Introduction

The biquaternion algebra carries one carrier of the defining type and a sphere of subspaces that realise it. The type has complex dimension two and spin $\tfrac12$; the subspaces are the minimal left ideals $S_{+}(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$, one for each unit real vector $\vec u$. The axis is a parameter of the family and not a datum of the algebra.

This article proves four things. The map $\vec u\mapsto S_{+}(\vec u)$ from the unit sphere to the minimal left ideals is a bijection, so the family is a two-sphere and no two of its members coincide. Each member is a Peirce column of one idempotent, with one companion nilpotent and two coordinates. The weights of the generator on each member are $+\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac12$, once each. And the conjugation carries the family onto itself, so the group acts on it transitively and the axis of a member is a moved label.

The type and its count are The Carriers of the Biquaternion Algebra; the number attached to it is The Spin of a Carrier; the spectrum is The Weights of a Carrier, §The Three Spectra; the ideals are The Minimal Left Ideals of the Biquaternion Algebra and their coordinates The Companion Nilpotents of the Biquaternion Algebra. The spinor built on this carrier is Spinors and Dirac Spinors in the Biquaternion Algebra, which owns the half-angle and the pair of ideals. The two other carriers are The Spin-Zero Carrier and The Spin-One Carriers.

Nothing below is a statement of physics. The elements are written $X,Y$; the units are $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ with $e_t^2=e_t$ and $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$; a unit real vector $\vec u$ stands also for the pure quaternion $u_x\vec e_x+u_y\vec e_y+u_z\vec e_z$, so that $i\vec u$ is an element of the algebra.

The Idempotents at an Axis

Definition (the idempotents at an axis). For a unit real $\vec u$,

$$ \tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)=\tfrac12\bigl(e_t\pm i\vec u\bigr). $$

Proposition (the three identities). For every unit real $\vec u$,

$$ \tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)^{2}=\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u),\qquad \tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)\tilde\Pi_{-}(\vec u)=0,\qquad \tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)+\tilde\Pi_{-}(\vec u)=e_t . $$

Proof. The unit is real, so $(i\vec u)^{2}=i^{2}\vec u^{2}=-(-e_t)=e_t$, and $\vec u$ commutes with itself. Then $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}^{2}=\tfrac14(e_t\pm2i\vec u+e_t)=\tfrac12(e_t\pm i\vec u)=\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$, the cross product is $\tfrac14(e_t-(i\vec u)^{2})=0$, and the sum is $e_t$.

Proposition (the corners are lines). $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)=\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)$.

Proof. The corner is an algebra with unit $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$; in the model $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ the element $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)$ is a rank-one projection, and a corner on a rank-one projection is one-dimensional. The identification with the line spanned by the idempotent is the computation $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}X\tilde\Pi_{\pm}=\lambda\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$ with $\lambda=\operatorname{sc}(\tilde\Pi_{\pm}X)$, carried out in The Minimal Left Ideals of the Biquaternion Algebra.

Remark (the idempotents are Hermitian). $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)^{*}=\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)$, because $\vec u$ is real and $(i\vec u)^{*}=-i\vec u^{*}=i\vec u$. The family is therefore the family of Hermitian rank-one idempotents, and the parameter $\vec u$ is a real unit vector and not an arbitrary complex one.

The Family

Definition (the carriers). For a unit real $\vec u$ the spin-half carriers at the axis are

$$ S_{+}(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u),\qquad S_{-}(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{-}(\vec u). $$

Theorem (they are minimal left ideals, and they are two). Each $S_{\pm}(\vec u)$ is a left ideal of real dimension $4$ and complex dimension $2$, it is minimal, and

$$ \mathbb{B}=S_{+}(\vec u)\oplus S_{-}(\vec u). $$

Proof. Left multiplication preserves the set because $X(Y\tilde\Pi_{\pm})=(XY)\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$. The sum is the algebra because $\tilde\Pi_{+}+\tilde\Pi_{-}=e_t$, and it is direct because $\tilde\Pi_{+}\tilde\Pi_{-}=0$ kills an element of both summands. Minimality is the minimality of the left ideals of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$: a nonzero element of the ideal generates the whole ideal, since $\mathbb{B}X=\mathbb{B}$ for an invertible $X$ and $\tilde\Pi_{\pm}\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\pm}$ is a line. The dimensions follow from the direct sum and from $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}=4$.

Corollary (the two ideals at an axis are one family). $S_{-}(\vec u)=S_{+}(-\vec u)$, because $\tilde\Pi_{-}(\vec u)=\tfrac12(e_t-i\vec u)=\tilde\Pi_{+}(-\vec u)$.

The corollary matters for the count of the family: the two signs at an axis do not give two families, and the sphere of axes already lists every member once.

The Parameter Is the Sphere

Theorem (the characterisation by the image). In the model $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, let $L(\vec u)$ be the image of $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$, a complex line in $\mathbb{C}^{2}$. Then

$$ S_{+}(\vec u)=\bigl\{X\in\mathbb{B}:\operatorname{im}X\subseteq L(\vec u)\bigr\}, $$

and conversely a minimal left ideal determines its line.

Proof. An element of $S_{+}(\vec u)$ is $Y\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$, whose image lies in the image of $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$; and an $X$ with image in that line satisfies $X=X\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$, hence lies in the ideal. Both sets are complex two-dimensional, and a nonzero element of the ideal has rank one, so its image is the whole line. The set of matrices with image in a fixed line is a left ideal of complex dimension two, so it is minimal and equal to the ideal.

Theorem (the map to the sphere is a bijection). The assignment $\vec u\mapsto S_{+}(\vec u)$ is injective on the unit sphere, and every minimal left ideal of $\mathbb{B}$ is one of its values.

Proof. Suppose $S_{+}(\vec u)=S_{+}(\vec u')$. By the preceding theorem the two have the same line $L$, so $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$ and $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u')$ are the orthogonal projections onto $L$ and are therefore equal. Then $\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec u)=\tfrac12(e_t+i\vec u')$ gives $\vec u=\vec u'$. For the surjectivity, a minimal left ideal is $M_2(\mathbb{C})q$ with $q$ a rank-one idempotent; its image is a line $L$, and $S_{+}(\vec u)$ for the $\vec u$ whose projection is onto $L$ has the same image, hence equals it by the characterisation.

Corollary (the count). The spin-half carriers form a set in bijection with the unit sphere: uncountably many subspaces, all of isomorphism type $\mathcal{V}_2$. The axis is a coordinate on the family.

Proof. The unit sphere and the set of complex lines in $\mathbb{C}^{2}$ are both two-spheres, and the map $\vec u\mapsto L(\vec u)$ is the standard identification: $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)=\tfrac12(\mathrm I+\vec u\cdot\vec\sigma)$ is the projection onto the $+1$ eigenspace of $\vec u\cdot\vec\sigma$. The inverse is $\vec u\cdot\vec\sigma=2\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)-\mathrm I$, that is $u_k=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)\sigma_k\bigr)$.

Remark (a warning about the sign). $S_{+}(\vec u)\neq S_{+}(-\vec u)$, since the two images are the two distinct eigenspaces of $\vec u\cdot\vec\sigma$. The sphere is the parameter and not a projective plane: the antipodal points give the two ideals at one axis, which are different subspaces of one type.

The Peirce Reading

The carrier is a Peirce column of one idempotent, and the decomposition of the algebra into the two columns is the Peirce decomposition of the complete family.

Proposition (column, diagonal corner, companion line). With $e=\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$ and $f=\tilde\Pi_{-}(\vec u)$,

$$ S_{+}(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}_{++}\oplus\mathbb{B}_{-+},\qquad \mathbb{B}_{++}=\mathbb{C}\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u),\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{-+}=1, $$

where $\mathbb{B}_{st}=\tilde\Pi_{s}\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{t}$. The companion corner $\mathbb{B}_{-+}$ is square-zero, and an element of the carrier is

$$ \zeta=d_0\,\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)+d_1\,\nu(\vec u),\qquad d_0,d_1\in\mathbb{C}, $$

with $\nu(\vec u)$ a generator of the companion line. The number of companion generators is $s=1$, the least number of generators of $\mathbb{B}_{-+}$ over the diagonal corner.

Proof. The column of $e$ in the Peirce decomposition collects the corners whose right index is $+$, which are $\mathbb{B}_{++}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{-+}$; this is Peirce Decompositions of the Biquaternion Algebra, §The Multiplication Rule. Both corners are lines by the rank-one computation above, and the companion corner is square-zero because $\tilde\Pi_{+}\tilde\Pi_{-}=0$. The count $s=1$ is the statement that a line is generated by one element over $\mathbb{C}$.

Example (the axis $\vec e_z$). With $\vec u=\vec e_z$ the companion generator is $\vec\Upsilon_{-+}=\tfrac12(i\vec e_x+\vec e_y)$, and the four units of the Peirce basis are $\tilde\Pi_{+},\vec\Upsilon_{-+},\vec\Upsilon_{+-},\tilde\Pi_{-}$, as in The Peirce Basis of a Peirce Decomposition of the Biquaternion Algebra. The coordinates $(d_0;d_1)$ are the two entries of the first column of the matrix model.

Remark (what the Peirce route gives and what it does not). The Peirce data give the carrier, the splitting into a line and a companion line, and the coordinates. They do not give the spin: that number is read from the weights, and it is the same at every axis.

The Weights and the Dimension

Theorem (the weight string). On $S_{+}(\vec u)$, with left multiplication as the action, the generator $\tilde S(\vec u)=\tfrac12 i\vec u\cdot\vec e$ has the two eigenvalues $+\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac12$, each once; the same holds on $S_{-}(\vec u)$.

Proof. This is The Weights of a Carrier, §The Three Spectra, where the two eigenvectors are exhibited: the idempotent $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$ of weight $+\tfrac12$ and the companion generator $\nu(\vec u)$ of weight $-\tfrac12$.

Corollary (the number). The spin is $\tfrac12$, the dimension of the representation space is $2s+1=2$, and the value of the quadratic element is $\tfrac34$.

Proof. The string is simple and spaced by one, so the highest weight is the spin and the length of the string is the dimension, by the criterion of The Weights of a Carrier, §The Criterion. The dimension is also the complex dimension of the carrier, $2$. The quadratic element is $\tfrac34 e_t$ and acts on a complex line by left multiplication as the scalar $\tfrac34$.

Remark (one string over the whole algebra). On the whole of $\mathbb{B}$ the same generator has the string $+\tfrac12,+\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12$, each weight twice. The repetition is the two ideals, not two weights: a repeated weight on an irreducible module is impossible, and the repetition here is the multiplicity two of the defining module in the regular module.

The Group Acts on the Family

Theorem (the family is homogeneous). For a unit $u$ and a unit real $\vec v$, the conjugation carries the carrier at $\vec v$ onto the carrier at the rotated axis,

$$ \operatorname{Ad}_u\bigl(S_{+}(\vec v)\bigr)=S_{+}(R\vec v), $$

with $R$ the rotation of $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ defined by $u(\vec v\cdot\vec\sigma)u^{*}=(R\vec v)\cdot\vec\sigma$. The action of $G$ on the family is transitive, and the stabiliser of a member is the set of units that fix its axis.

Proof. The conjugation preserves the algebra and acts on the left and on the right at once, so $\operatorname{Ad}_u\bigl(\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec v)\bigr)=\mathbb{B}\,\bigl(u\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec v)u^{*}\bigr)$ because $u\mathbb{B}u^{*}=\mathbb{B}$. In the model, $u\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec v)u^{*}=\tfrac12\bigl(\mathrm I+u(\vec v\cdot\vec\sigma)u^{*}\bigr)$ is a Hermitian rank-one idempotent, hence of the form $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec w)$ for a unique unit real $\vec w$, and the identity $u(\vec v\cdot\vec\sigma)u^{*}=(R\vec v)\cdot\vec\sigma$ with $R\in SO(3)$ gives $\vec w=R\vec v$. Transitivity is the transitivity of $SO(3)$ on the sphere. The unit $u$ and $-u$ give the same $R$.

Remark (what the transitivity says about the count). The members of the family are one orbit, so no member is preferred by the algebra, and a statement that names one axis names a point of the orbit. The type, the weights, the dimension, the spin and the quadratic element are constant on the orbit, which is why the table of The Carriers of the Biquaternion Algebra records the type and not the axis.

Remark (the half-angle belongs to the spinor). The exponential of the generator acts on the carrier by left multiplication, so a rotation through $\theta$ multiplies an element of the carrier by $\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}e_t+\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\vec u$ and a full turn returns it with the opposite sign. The computation is Spinors and Dirac Spinors in the Biquaternion Algebra, §The Elementary Spinor; it is a property of the action of the rotation on this carrier, and the carrier itself is fixed by the data above.

The Intersections with the Other Carriers

The two ideals at an axis sum to the algebra; their intersections with the other carriers are lines or zero, and the table records them.

Proposition (the intersections). With $\vec u$ a unit real vector,

$$ S_{+}(\vec u)\cap S_{-}(\vec u)=0,\qquad S_{+}(\vec u)\cap\mathbb{C}e_t=0, $$

$$ S_{+}(\vec u)\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}=\mathbb{R}\bigl(i\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)\bigr),\qquad S_{+}(\vec u)\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}=\mathbb{R}\bigl(\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)\bigr), $$

and $S_{+}(\vec u)$ meets neither the real triple $\mathfrak{k}$ nor its imaginary partner $\mathfrak{p}=i\mathfrak{k}$.

Proof. The first is the direct sum. For the centre: if $\lambda e_t\in S_{+}(\vec u)$ then $\lambda e_t=\lambda e_t\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)=\lambda\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$, which forces $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)=e_t$. For the sectors: $\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$ is Hermitian, so it lies in $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, and $i\tilde\Pi_{+}(\vec u)$ is anti-Hermitian, so it lies in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$; both lie in the carrier because the carrier is a left ideal, and a two-dimensional real intersection would be a complex line inside a real form of $\mathbb{B}$, which cannot exist. For the triples: a nonzero pure quaternion $\vec v$ is invertible, $\vec v^{2}=-\lvert\vec v\rvert^{2}e_t\neq0$, so in the model $\vec v\cdot\vec\sigma$ is invertible and its image is the whole of $\mathbb{C}^{2}$; by the characterisation of the carrier by its image, $\vec v$ lies in no minimal left ideal. The element $i\vec v$ has the same image, because $i$ is a scalar, so it lies in none either. Hence $S_{+}(\vec u)$ meets neither the real triple nor its imaginary partner.

Remark (why the intersection with a sector is a real line and not a complex one). The carrier is a complex plane and a sector is a real four-plane; their intersection is real, and it is one-dimensional because a two-dimensional real intersection would be a complex line inside the sector, and no complex line lies in $\mathbb{B}_{\Phi}$ or in $\mathbb{B}_{\Psi}$, both being real forms of $\mathbb{B}$ without complex structure.

Summary

The spin-half carriers are the minimal left ideals $S_{\pm}(\vec u)=\mathbb{B}\tilde\Pi_{\pm}(\vec u)$ at a unit real axis. There is one type, of complex dimension two and spin $\tfrac12$, and a sphere of subspaces realising it: the map $\vec u\mapsto S_{+}(\vec u)$ is a bijection from the unit sphere to the minimal left ideals, and $S_{-}(\vec u)=S_{+}(-\vec u)$, so the two signs at an axis give two members of one family and not two families.

Each member is a Peirce column: a diagonal line spanned by the idempotent and a companion line spanned by one nilpotent, with two complex coordinates and one companion generator. The weights are $+\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac12$, once each, the quadratic element is $\tfrac34$, and $-e_t$ acts as $-1$, which is the half-integer class.

The group of unitary units acts on the family transitively and by rotations of the axis, so no member is preferred. The carrier meets the centre, the real triple and its imaginary partner in zero, and meets the two sectors in one real line each.

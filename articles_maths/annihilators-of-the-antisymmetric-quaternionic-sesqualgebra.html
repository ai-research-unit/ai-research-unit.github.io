Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Introduction

The zero divisor of a product is a property of one element alone: it says that some nonzero partner exists that the element kills. The annihilator says which partners those are, and it is the finer object. It is the set of elements killed by a fixed element, $$ A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\star\tilde X=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde Q=0\}, $$ the left and the right annihilator (Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Zero Divisor and the Annihilator). When the product is carried by a multiplication operator the annihilator is the kernel of that operator, so it is a linear subspace.

The block of this article is the antisymmetric quaternionic sesquimultiplication $$ \tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}-\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural}\bigr) =\mathbf P\times\overline{\mathbf Q}, $$ scalar part zero and vector part the cross product of the vector part of the first argument with the conjugate of the vector part of the second (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions). It is the only one of the twelve whose value is a single cross term, and it is conjugate-alternating, so the two annihilators of an element are one set. Its zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space (Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, above this article in this category). The annihilator is the simplest of the twelve: the plane spanned by the centre and the conjugate of the vector part, for every non-central element. The article owns that reading, the operator with its rank, and the shape of the annihilator on the remarkable subspaces; the zero-divisor set is the cited article's.

The results. The antisymmetric quaternionic sesquimultiplication $\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=\mathbf{P}\times\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$ is conjugate-alternating, so the two annihilators of an element coincide, $B_\diamond(\tilde P)=A_\diamond(\tilde P)=\ker L_{\tilde P}$. For a non-central element the common annihilator is the complex plane $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$ spanned by the centre and the conjugate of the vector part, and it depends on the complex line of the vector part alone; for a central element the operator is zero and the annihilator is the whole space. The left multiplication $L_{\tilde Q}$ has that plane as its kernel and the vector subspace $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ as its image, of complex rank two for every non-central element and rank zero at the centre. The block has no case distinction except the degenerate centre, and its zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space.

The Annihilator of a Product

The annihilator of an element is the set of partners it kills; the left annihilator is the kernel of the left multiplication and the right annihilator is the kernel of the right multiplication, and both are nonzero exactly when the element is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Definition (annihilator). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$ and let $\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$. The left annihilator and the right annihilator of $\tilde Q$ in $\star$ are $$ A_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde Q\star\tilde X=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde Q)=\{\tilde X:\tilde X\star\tilde Q=0\}. $$ They are the kernels of the left and the right multiplication, and they are nonzero exactly when $\tilde Q$ is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric quaternionic sesquimultiplication $\diamond$, and unqualified, annihilator means the annihilator of $\diamond$. The left multiplication is the operator $$ L_{\tilde P}\tilde X=\tilde P\diamond\tilde X=\mathbf P\times\overline{\mathbf X}, $$ conjugate-linear in $\tilde X$, with kernel the annihilator. The block is the sibling of the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra and the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra, whose annihilators are Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra and Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

The Two Annihilators Coincide

For every element the two annihilators coincide with the kernel of the left multiplication, $B_\diamond(\tilde P)=A_\diamond(\tilde P)=\ker L_{\tilde P}$, because the block is conjugate-alternating.

Theorem (the two annihilators coincide). For every $\tilde P$, $$ B_\diamond(\tilde P)=A_\diamond(\tilde P)=\ker L_{\tilde P}. $$

Proof. The block is conjugate-alternating, $\tilde X\diamond\tilde P=-\overline{\tilde P\diamond\tilde X}$; a vector part is zero exactly when its conjugate is, so the two products vanish together and the two annihilators are one set.

The Annihilator of an Element

For a non-central element $\tilde Q$ the annihilator is the complex plane $A_\diamond(\tilde Q)=B_\diamond(\tilde Q)=\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$, which depends on the complex line of the conjugate of the vector part alone, and the example $e_1+ie_2$ gives the plane $\mathbb{C}\{e_0,e_1-ie_2\}$; for a central element the operator is zero and both annihilators are all of $\mathbb{B}$.

Theorem (the annihilator is the centre together with the conjugate of the vector part). For a non-central element $\tilde Q$, of vector part $\mathbf Q\neq0$, $$ A_\diamond(\tilde Q)=B_\diamond(\tilde Q)=\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf Q}, $$ a complex plane. For a central element, $\mathbf Q=0$ and $\tilde Q=\lambda e_0$, the operator is zero and both annihilators are all of $\mathbb{B}$.

Proof. The equation $\mathbf Q\times\overline{\mathbf X}=0$ holds exactly when $\overline{\mathbf X}$ is a complex multiple of $\mathbf Q$, because the cross product of two vectors of $\mathbb{C}^{3}$ vanishes exactly when they are linearly dependent over $\mathbb{C}$; this is exactly when $\mathbf X$ is a complex multiple of $\overline{\mathbf Q}$, and the scalar part $X_0$ is free. The set is therefore the plane $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf Q}$. For $\mathbf Q=0$ the operator is identically zero.

Corollary (the annihilator reads the vector part's direction and nothing else). The annihilator of a non-central element depends only on the complex line of the conjugate of the vector part, so two non-central elements with proportional vector parts have the same annihilator; the scalar part enters only through the centre, and it does not change the annihilator.

Example. For the non-central element $\tilde Q=e_1+ie_2$, of vector part $\mathbf Q=e_1+ie_2$, the annihilator is the complex plane $$ A_\diamond(e_1+ie_2)=\mathbb{C}\{e_0,\ e_1-ie_2\}=\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf Q}. $$

The Multiplication Operator and the Rank

The operator $L_{\tilde Q}$ has kernel $A_\diamond(\tilde Q)$, image the vector subspace $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, and complex rank two for every non-central element and rank zero at the centre.

Corollary (the rank). The operator $L_{\tilde Q}$ has kernel $A_\diamond(\tilde Q)$, image the vector subspace $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, and complex rank two for every non-central element and rank zero at the centre.

Proof. The kernel is the annihilator by definition, of complex dimension two for a non-central element and four at the centre; the rank is four minus the dimension.

The Dichotomy

The block has no case distinction except the degenerate centre: the annihilator is the plane $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$ for every non-central element, against the line or plane of the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra and the line or plane of the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra.

The zero-divisor set of the block is the whole punctured space, so it separates nothing, and the block is the extreme of the twelve for the simplicity of the annihilator: it is $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf Q}$ for every non-central element, a plane that depends on the vector part's direction alone. The block is the sibling of the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra, whose annihilator is the line $\mathbb{C}\tilde P$ off the cone and a plane on it, and the sibling of the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra, whose annihilator is a line or a plane according to the parent invertibility; here there is no case distinction at all except the degenerate centre.

The Annihilator on the Remarkable Subspaces

The centre is contained in every annihilator and annihilates every element on both sides; on the vector subspace the annihilator of a nonzero pure element meets it in the complex line $\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf{P}}$; and on the conjugate-changed subspaces the conjugation in the second slot gives the plane $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf Q$ on the Hermitian subspace and the same plane with the conjugate line on the anti-Hermitian one.

The centre. A nonzero central element $\tilde P=\lambda e_0$ has $L_{\lambda e_0}=0$, so its annihilator is all of $\mathbb{B}$: the centre annihilates every element on both sides, and the centre is contained in every annihilator.

The vector subspace. On the vector subspace, where $\tilde X$ is pure, the operation is $\tilde P\diamond\tilde X=\mathbf P\times\overline{\mathbf X}$, and the annihilator of a nonzero pure element meets the vector subspace in the complex line $\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf P}$; the scalar part of the ambient annihilator is exactly the centre.

The conjugate-changed subspaces. The conjugate in the second slot exchanges an element and its conjugate, so on the Hermitian subspace the annihilator of a nonzero element is the plane $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf Q$, and on the anti-Hermitian subspace it is $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf Q$ with the conjugate line again.

Summary

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners the element kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left and the right multiplication. The antisymmetric quaternionic sesquimultiplication is the single cross term $\tilde P\diamond\tilde X=\mathbf P\times\overline{\mathbf X}$, conjugate-alternating, so the two annihilators of an element coincide. For a non-central element the common annihilator is the complex plane $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf Q}$ spanned by the centre and the conjugate of the vector part, and it depends on the vector part's direction alone; for a central element the operator is zero and the annihilator is the whole space. The block is the simplest of the twelve for the annihilator, and its zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space.

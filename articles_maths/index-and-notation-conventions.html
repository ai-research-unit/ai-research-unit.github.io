Index and Notation Conventions

Introduction

This article closes the series with the marks that are read on a glyph rather than on the object: what the caret carries, the labels that stand in the subscript, the subscript rules and their admitted forms, the working conventions of the corpus and the typography of the source. It assumes the eight companions, whose glyphs it indexes.

The caret comes first, because it carries four things and nothing else; the labels follow, because every one of them stands in one slot and the order and the separators of that slot are fixed there; the subscripts come next, because their rule depends on the derivation convention fixed in Operator and Calculus Conventions. The working conventions of the corpus follow — the menus, the groups inside a category, the article files, the skeleton and the boundaries — and the typography closes the article.

The article is the ninth of the ten and the index of the notation: the table below collects the tokens and the working marks that belong to the corpus rather than to the algebra, while the table of Algebra and Element Conventions collects the glyphs of the algebra.

The Upper and Low Marks

These rules are real, and partial. They fix the marks of the common uses and they bind wherever they apply. They do not cover every use: a mark that falls outside them is governed by the section that fixes it, and a stricter rule stated in the corpus overrides them — the derivation convention of Operator and Calculus Conventions, §Conventions for Derivation, the caret of §The Caret, and the forms of §The Subscripts.

A caret carries four things and no label:

  • a power, $2^k$, $e^\lambda$, $(-1)^j$, $\alpha^k$, $2^{k/2-1}$;
  • a conjugation, $a^{*}$, $a^{\dagger}$, $a^{\natural}$;
  • a transposition, $L^T$, $\psi^T$;
  • a construction, $\mathbb{B}^\times$, $S^{\otimes n}$, $\mathrm{sym}^l$, $\Lambda^kTM$ (degree).

A low mark is the exclusive carrier of two things: an index and a label.

  • an index — a numeric count, $Q_1$, $M_2$; the slot of a coordinate, $q_i$, $x_i$, $p_i$, $Q_i$, $\theta_i$, $\zeta_k$, $v_i$; the slot of a basis vector or a gamma, $\vec e_i$, $\gamma_i$; the slots of an invariant symbol, $\delta_{ij}$, $\varepsilon_{ijk}$, $\eta_{ij}$; the generator of a gauge, colour, isospin or spin family, $T_a$, $G_a$, $\sigma_z$; the slot of a connection or curvature component, $\Gamma_k$, $R_{ij}$, $\omega_i$, $g_{ij}$; the slot of a differential, $dx_i$.
  • a label naming the object, a part of it or a role of it, $L_{\mathrm{axf}}$, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$, $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$, with every label of §The Two-Letter Labels and §The Three-Letter Labels; the label comes first in the subscript and an index already there follows it after a comma, $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}$; the family letters of a regular matrix are of this kind, $L$ and $R$ naming the left and the right part, $\mathsf M_{4L}$ and $\mathsf M_{4R}$.

The word metric has one meaning in the corpus. There is one metric, the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$: diagonal, all four coefficients $+1$, the matrix of the interval $\sum_\mu Q_\mu^2$. A frame carries a coordinate, not a metric. The minus of the material sector is not a metric: it is $i^2=-1$ in $Q_t=iq'_t$, made once on the time.

No index is raised, because the one metric is the identity. The series has one metric, the unit metric $\delta_{\mu\nu}=(1,1,1,1)$, and raising with it changes nothing: a raised index would be the same index. A gamma is written low, $\gamma_\mu$, and the weight does the only work a raising could do, $\gamma^\mu=-\varepsilon_\mu\gamma_\mu$. A text that needs a raised index on a coefficient is writing a metric the corpus does not have.

The object is not the role. A coordinate, a basis vector, a gamma, an invariant symbol, a gauge generator, a component of a connection and a differential are seven kinds of object, and the low mark does one thing on all seven: it gives the index that names the slot of the family the glyph belongs to. The index says which member, never what kind, and the kind is carried by the glyph itself, $x_i$ against $\gamma_i$ against $\delta_{ij}$. A differential carries the same mark as the coordinate it differentiates, $x_i$ and $dx_i$, because the slot is the same in both. Nor does the alphabet divide the work: a letter is an index in $q_i$ and a number is an index in $Q_1$, and the two are of different kinds only where a coefficient and an element meet (§The Subscripts).

The lists rule on these uses, not on the names. A raised grade, parameter, sector or order is a name, not a power, a conjugation, a transposition or a construction, and the article that uses it says which name it is. A label is a name of that kind, and it names the object the glyph denotes, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$, $a_{\mathrm{fop}}$, $L_{\mathrm{axf}}$, a part of that object, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$, or a role of it, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$; the word separates the three, since the corpus has one slot for all of them.

The Caret

The caret carries a power, a conjugation, a transposition and a construction. Those four are the whole of its list, and they are read at a glance because each is italic or a symbol: $e^\lambda$, $2^k$, $(-1)^k$ are powers; $a^{*}$, $a^{\dagger}$, $a^{\natural}$ conjugations; $L^T$, $\psi^T$ transpositions; $\mathbb{B}^\times$, $S^{\otimes n}$, $\mathrm{sym}^l$, $\Lambda^kTM$ constructions. $M_4^\sigma$ and $B^\sigma$ carry a Greek letter that names an automorphism of the glyph and not a variant of it.

An operator name written with its argument is the one word that stands there. $\gamma_n^{\operatorname{sym}}$ on a divided-power structure, $K^{\operatorname{gal}(K/F)}$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{cf}}^{(\kappa)}$, $M_{\mathrm{co}}^{H}$. The word names a structure of the glyph rather than a variant of it, it carries its argument with it and never stands bare, and it is therefore not a label: it is not indexed in §The Labels and it takes no entry in either table. It is written lowercase, as a label is, and a capital in that position is a defect.

No label stands in the caret. A variant of an object is a name, and every name stands in the subscript (§The Labels). Italic is a power and upright is a name, and the slot agrees with the face: $L_{\mathrm{axf},\vec e_x}$ is the matrix of $\vec e_x$ in the axis-free frame, and it is not the $x$-th power of an operator.

No index is raised, and the caret never carries one. Every coordinate, component, basis vector, frame basis operator and sign symbol is written low — $x_\mu$, $A_\mu$, $\vec e_i$, $\gamma_i$, $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ (§The Upper and Low Marks). A contraction is written as an explicit sum with the sign vector or as the quaternionic product that carries it: the form $\langle\tilde\nabla,\tilde Q_{\mathrm f}\rangle_{\natural}$ for a derivative with a field, and $\langle\tilde\nabla\rangle_{\natural}=\Box$ for two derivatives (Operator and Differential Calculus Conventions, §The Krein Product, Its Form and Its Norm). Where a contraction is written with indices, the sum names the summed index — $\sum_\mu e_\mu\partial/\partial Q_\mu$, $\sum_k\vec e_kA_k$ — and is never a repeated index left to the summation convention. The rule is the one of the physical corpus (Operator and Calculus Conventions, §Conventions for Derivation).

The Labels

Every label stands in the subscript. A label is the upright roman name of a variant of an object: a construction, a frame, a sector, a reading, a part of the object or a role it plays. It is written low, in lowercase, at the length the article that introduces it fixes; that article also fixes what the word abbreviates, and the label enters the index with the article.

The label comes first, and an index already in the field follows it after a comma. The subscript is read from the label outward: $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}$ and not $L_{e_\mu,\mathrm{axf}}$, $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},k}$ and not $\tilde S_{k,\mathrm{tot}}$, $\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{spl},2}$ and not $\mathbb{C}_{2,\mathrm{spl}}$, $T_{\mathrm{em},\mu\nu}$ and not $T_{\mu\nu,\mathrm{em}}$. A field that already carried the index is reordered and never duplicated, $V_K^{\mathrm{fin}}$ becoming $V_{\mathrm{fin},K}$. One field is written without the comma, the label carried into a coefficient beside its direction, $Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{f}\mu}$, where the two letters are read as one weight.

The label names the object, a part of it or a role of it, and the word and its article decide which. $L_{\mathrm{axf}}$ names the frame a regular matrix is read in, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$ a variant of an algebra, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$ a part of a total, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$ a role in a coupling. There is one slot, so the height decides nothing; the word decides, and the article that introduces it says so.

Most labels are two or three letters, and a longer word is kept whole where the short form would be ambiguous. The two families are indexed below. A fourth letter is admitted where three do not carry the word, $\mathrm{lift}$, $\mathrm{dual}$, $\mathrm{deph}$, $\mathrm{refl}$, and a compound whose abbreviation would collide with a neighbour is written out, $\mathrm{singlet}$, $\mathrm{coroot}$, $\mathrm{grevlex}$, $\mathrm{boost}$. One letter's difference at subscript size does not separate two meanings, so a pair that would differ by one letter is not written; that is why $\mathrm{op}$ is not a label beside $\mathrm{opp}$.

$\mathrm{fop}$ marks the Fock layer. The marks of the elements say what a glyph is, not that it is an operator (Operator and Calculus Conventions, §The Operators). The single exception is the Fock layer of the physical corpus, whose operators act on a Fock space and are not elements of the algebra. Only these carry the label $\mathrm{fop}$:

$$ a_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad b_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger},\quad \psi_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad N_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad H_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad F_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad Q_{\mathrm{fop}},\quad U_{\mathrm{fop}} . $$

The label says exactly one thing — the glyph is not an element, it is an operator on the Fock space — and it is written on nothing else. It is not a general mark of operators: the momentum $\vec p$, the position $x$, the spin $S$ and the parity, charge-conjugation and reversal maps $P$, $C$ and $T$ carry no label, because each keeps the glyph of its element or is a map with a glyph of its own. The dagger is the general adjoint, and it stands in the caret, so the two marks occupy two slots and never compete for one: $a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ is the raised dagger on a labelled glyph. The dagger alone never marks a glyph as an operator, and the label alone never asserts self-adjointness.

$\mathrm{opp}$ marks the opposite algebra. The opposite algebra, whose multiplication is reversed, $a\cdot b=ba$, takes the label $\mathrm{opp}$ — $A_{\mathrm{opp}}$, $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$, $M_n(D)_{\mathrm{opp}}$ — and it is the algebra name that takes it, where $\mathrm{fop}$ marks a Fock-operator glyph. The two meanings are separated by the word itself and not by context.

On an element the label names a tensor factor. Written on an element it means the same element read in the opposite algebra, $e_{\mathrm{opp},\nu}$, and it is written there for that one purpose: to name the second factor of the tensor product $\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{opp}}$, whose elementary tensors $e_\mu\otimes e_{\mathrm{opp},\nu}$ are the basis operators $L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$ of the two-sided operators. The element is not thereby an operator, the label says nothing about a side, and on an operator glyph it is a defect. It marks an algebra, a module of one, and an element read in that algebra; it never marks a set or a bare vector space.

The two-letter form $\mathrm{op}$ is not a label. It differs from $\mathrm{opp}$ by one letter, at the size a label is set, and a page carrying both would be undecidable at reading speed. It is therefore not a token of the corpus in any sense, and the one place where it survives, the bar $\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$ of §The Two-Letter Labels, is a defect. In particular it does not mark the operator norm: the norm is written $\lVert A\rVert$ bare, and the norms it is compared with carry their own labels, $\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}$, $\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{nuc}}$, $\lVert A\rVert_F$, $\lVert A\rVert_*$, so that the unmarked bar is the operator norm by default and never by elimination. It does not mark an operator either: an operator glyph is the element's glyph, and the explicit form is written where the letter is taken, as in the gradient $\tilde\nabla$ and the momentum $\vec p$.

The Two-Letter Labels

The table is an index of labels and not a list of tokens: a label names an object, a construction, a frame or a variant of one, the article that introduces it fixes what it abbreviates, and a label enters the table with that article.

The section holds two tables. The first indexes the two-letter labels that name an object, a construction, a frame or a variant, and it carries thirty rows. The second table carries the five labels of the norm bar.

Two letters are the shorter length of the label, and they are made as the three are. Either the initials of a two-word term — $\mathrm{sa}$ the self-adjoint part, $\mathrm{rs}$ the regular semisimple locus, $\mathrm{df}$ the discrete and faithful representations, $\mathrm{em}$ the electromagnetic part — or the consonants of a single word — $\mathrm{fn}$ a function, $\mathrm{sh}$ the sheafification, $\mathrm{bl}$ the blow-up, $\mathrm{cl}$ the classical value, $\mathrm{tr}$ the transpose, $\mathrm{gr}$ the gradient, $\mathrm{pf}$ the profinite completion, $\mathrm{sw}$ the swap. The three letters of the other family are what a word that does not shorten gives, $\mathrm{opp}$, $\mathrm{fop}$, $\mathrm{axf}$, $\mathrm{nil}$, and neither length is a shortening of the other: the index of the longer family is §The Three-Letter Labels.

A pair that would be read for the other is refused. $\mathrm{op}$ differs from $\mathrm{opp}$ by one letter and would name a variant of the same algebra on the same glyph, so it is not a label. The prefix relation alone is no defect: twenty-four two-letter labels begin a three-letter one — $\mathrm{sc}$ beside $\mathrm{scl}$, $\mathrm{st}$ beside $\mathrm{str}$, $\mathrm{sp}$ beside $\mathrm{spl}$, $\mathrm{ad}$ beside $\mathrm{adj}$ — and each pair is admitted because the two members never stand on one glyph in one role. What the rule refuses is a pair that names the same kind of object on the same glyph.

$\mathrm{sp}$ is the one label of the spinor, and $\mathrm{pau}$ its Pauli counterpart. $\mathrm{sp}$ marks the Dirac field, $\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}=\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},L}+\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp},R}$, the spinor role written on its own density, $\rho_{\mathrm{sp}}=\lvert\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}\rangle\langle\tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}}\rvert$, and the spinor reading of a glyph whose Pauli reading is $\mathrm{pau}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{sp},k}$ beside $\sigma_{\mathrm{pau},k}$. The two labels name a relation and not an object, so each is written on the glyph whose reading it fixes, and the article that writes it says what the reading carries. The three-letter label spn is retired: it stood one letter from $\mathrm{sp}$, on the same glyph, and named the same subject, which is the pair the rule above refuses; the field is \tilde\Xi_{\mathrm{sp}} everywhere in the corpus.

A two-letter label is read as a three-letter one is. It names the object the glyph denotes, $G_{\mathrm{ab}}$, $X_{\mathrm{an}}$, $\hat G_{\mathrm{pf}}$, or a part or a role of that object, $G_{\mathrm{ad}}$, $G_{\mathrm{sc}}$, $\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{sp}}$, and §The Subscripts rules on the slot. One token is carried by two readings: $\mathrm{sp}$ on a spinor density, or on the Dirac field itself, is the spinor; $\mathrm{sp}$ on the bar, $\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{sp}}$, is the spectral norm. The bar is the discriminator, and no glyph carries the two readings at once.

The Three-Letter Labels

The table is an index of labels and not a list of tokens: a label names an object, a construction, a frame or a variant of one, the article that introduces it fixes what it abbreviates, and a label enters the table with that article.

The section holds two tables. The first indexes the labels that name an object, a construction, a frame or a variant, and it carries seventy-eight rows. The second table carries the thirty angle labels.

The split is the rule of the angle. Where one formula holds two angles the first keeps the bare $\theta$ and the second takes a label, so an angle label names a role and not an object, and the two tables never share a row: a label of an object never stands on $\theta$, and an angle label never stands elsewhere. A label that neither table carries enters with the article that spends it.

Every label is written in lowercase. The label is set at subscript size, where the case of a two- or a three-letter word is not read reliably, so the corpus writes every one of them in lowercase; the rule is the one that refuses $\mathrm{op}$ beside $\mathrm{opp}$, applied to case instead of to spelling. The bar is the one place where a capital survives, and only there: $\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}$.

No label carries a capital. A capital in that position is a defect, and the corpus writes every label lowercase. Six codes separate two spellings. Where one three-letter word carries two English words, the rarer reading takes a code of its own, made the same way and written in the same place: $\mathrm{rgs}$ beside $\mathrm{reg}$, $\mathrm{tpl}$ beside $\mathrm{top}$, $\mathrm{prm}$ beside $\mathrm{per}$, $\mathrm{dfc}$ beside $\mathrm{def}$, $\mathrm{obf}$ beside $\mathrm{orb}$, $\mathrm{adg}$ beside $\mathrm{adj}$. Each of the six names one word only, and a glyph may carry either member of a pair. A code is a label and stands low with the others, on the object it qualifies: $h_{\mathrm{tpl}}$ the topological entropy, $\Lambda_{\mathrm{top}}$ the top exterior power. The twelve algebraic structures carry no code at all: a product is written by its two slots and the conjugation each one carries, $\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*}$, $\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*}$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet_{*}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet_{\natural}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\bullet_{\natural*}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge_{*}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge_{\natural}\tilde Q_2$, $\tilde Q_1\wedge_{\natural*}\tilde Q_2$, and the operator names the structure of The Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space on its own.

The indexed label is one field. Where a label and an index name the same object they share one subscript, the label first, $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$, $\tilde K_{\mathrm{mod},W}$, $\tilde H_{\mathrm{pos},l}$. A part or a role is a label like the others, $\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{nuc}}$, $M_{\mathrm{tor}}$, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$, $E_{\mathrm{vac}}$, and so is an operator name written low, $\operatorname{col}A$, $\operatorname{row}A$, $\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}$.

The thirty rows stand for thirty roles and 1,457 occurrences in the two corpora; the largest, $\theta_{\mathrm{hpa}}$, names the hyperbolic angle, because the exponential polar decomposition of a split-complex number recurs across a family of articles.

A phase is written here and not in a letter of its own. The 91 occurrences that $\phi$ carried as a phase are $\theta_{\mathrm{pha}}$, the row that already named the phase angle in the polar decomposition, so the phase cost no new label. Where a phase is a geometric phase the row is the same one: a Berry or a Pancharatnam phase is an angle read modulo $2\pi$, and the article that spends it says so in its own words.

A label is read with its article. The word names the object the glyph denotes, a part of it or a role of it, and the word and the article separate the three, since one slot carries all of them: $L_{\mathrm{axf}}$ names a frame, $S_{\mathrm{int}}$ a part, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$ a role, and §The Subscripts rules on the slot. One word is carried by four uses and is read with its argument: $\operatorname{sym}(P)$ the symmetric group, $\operatorname{sym}^2(E)$ the symmetric square, $\gamma_n^{\operatorname{sym}}$ the divided-power structure, $f^{\operatorname{sym}}$ the symmetrised part. The word is one and it is lowercase in all four; the argument says which. The vocabulary is not private to a slot, and it is not ambiguous either: $\mathrm{cov}$ names the universal cover in one article and the covariant fundamental solution in another, $\mathrm{res}$ names the residual part and the residue field, and the article that writes a label says which object it names. Where one spelling would name two objects that a reader must tell apart at a glance, the rarer reading takes a code of its own rather than a second height: $\mathrm{rgs}$, $\mathrm{tpl}$, $\mathrm{prm}$, $\mathrm{dfc}$, $\mathrm{obf}$, $\mathrm{adg}$ stand beside $\mathrm{reg}$, $\mathrm{top}$, $\mathrm{per}$, $\mathrm{def}$, $\mathrm{orb}$, $\mathrm{adj}$, and each of the twelve names one word only.

The Subscripts

A subscript is written with the underscore, _, inside the math delimiters, and the underscore takes a single token: one character is written bare, $Q_t$ and $Q_k$, and two characters or more are wrapped in braces, $Q_{n+1}$ and $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{red}}$, because a bare underscore would take the first character alone. A subscript label is written in the upright roman of the labels, and the two lists are read together (§The Three-Letter Labels). The family letters $L$ and $R$ of a regular matrix are labels of that kind, and they are written italic as the letters of the operators rather than upright as a word; they follow the size in the same subscript, $\mathsf M_{4L}$ and $\mathsf M_{4R}$.

The label is written first in the field. $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}$ is the matrix of $\vec e_\mu$ in the axis-free frame, and the rule is stated in §The Labels; the rest of this section rules on the index, and on the label where it accompanies one.

A subscript is never a partial derivative. The subscript is an index or a label, a partial derivative is written with $\partial$, and the rule is fixed in Operator and Calculus Conventions, §Conventions for Derivation.

A coefficient carries a letter index and an element a number. In the quaternionic layers a coefficient is indexed by the direction it multiplies, $Q_x$ and $q_x$, and an element that is counted carries a number on its tilde, $\tilde Q_1, \tilde Q_2$. In the commutative layers, which have no direction, both the element and its coefficients carry the number of the element, $Q_1 = q_1 + iq'_1$. The two indices are of different kinds, a letter against a number, so a coefficient index is never read as the number of an element, nor the reverse.

It is allowed to add subscripts when several elements of the same kind are used. The permission covers the units, the roots, the idempotents and the nilpotents as well as the general elements: wherever a statement carries more than one element of one kind, the occurrences are told apart by a subscript.

A subscript counts, or it names. A subscript that only tells one occurrence from another is numeric, so that a family is $\tilde Q_1, \tilde Q_2, \dots$ and a second numbered family of the same statement takes the next numbers. A subscript that names the object or the role it qualifies is a role label, and a role label is written in upright roman, as in $\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{red}}$ and $\tilde U_{\mathrm b}$.

A role-labelled element carries its label in its coefficients. The generic element-valued field is $\tilde Q_{\mathrm f}=Q_{\mathrm{f}t}e_t+\vec Q_{\mathrm f}$, and its components are $Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{f}\mu}$: the label $\mathrm f$ belongs to the element and is carried once into the component subscript, beside the direction, so that the components are $Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{f}\mu}$; it does not count a family. A numbered family is unaffected, $\tilde Q_1=Q_{1t}e_t+\vec Q_1$.

An angular-momentum total takes the role subscript, and its projection the axis. A spin label is $s$ with projection $s_z$; the total of an orbital and a spin part, or of several spins, is $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$ with projection $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$. The generator is $\tilde S_k=\tfrac12i\vec e_k$; the tilde marks the operator and keeps it distinct from the simple module $S$ and its minimal left ideals $S_\pm(\vec u)$. The total generator carries the role subscript and the vector index: $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},k}=L_k\otimes1+1\otimes\tilde S_k$ on a coupled module, and the vector index $k$ follows the role. The subscript $k$ is the vector index; the projection is the scalar weight, the eigenvalue of the third component on $|s_{\mathrm{tot}},s_{\mathrm{tot},z}\rangle$, and it names the axis $z$ of the measurement and no vector index. The role is repeated in the projection so that $s_{1z}$, $s_{2z}$ and $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$ are told apart without the context. The role and the axis share one pair of braces, $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$, with a comma between them. The letter $j$ is the hyperbolic unit in the biquaternion corpus and does not label a spin there; the letter $m$ is an index.

A sign is a subscript where the statement needs one, the member attached to $+1$ taking the subscript $+$ and the member attached to $-1$ the subscript $-$, as in $\tilde\Pi_+$ and $\tilde\Pi_-$. In the reduced biquaternion layer the sign belongs to the idempotent pair and to its coordinates, $\Pi_\pm$, $Q_{+}$ and $Q_{-}$, the two basis coordinates carrying the triangles, $Q_{\blacktriangle}$ and $Q_{\blacktriangledown}$, and a numbered element putting its number first in either case, $Q_{1+}$ and $Q_{1\blacktriangle}$; the element they build is $\breve Q$. The sign is read with the glyph, and no identity is implied between the pairs: $\tilde Q_\pm$ names the two quaternionic components of a split-biquaternion, $\vec Q_\pm$ their vector parts, and $Q_\pm$ the idempotent coordinates of $\breve Q$.

Conventions of the Corpus Itself

The corpus has a small number of working conventions that are not mathematical.

The menus. Every article is registered in exactly one menu: maths.md for the mathematical corpus and physics.md for the physics corpus. An entry has the form of a link to the article followed by a comment that lists the contents of the article in outline. The mathematical menu opens with the category Introduction and Conventions, placed outside the parts. It has two subcategories: Introduction to Mathematics, which holds Introduction to the Mathematical Corpus and AI Maths Review, and Algebra and Element Conventions, which holds this article and its nine companions. An entry that begins with + is a planned article, one that is registered in the menu and not yet written; an entry without the marker has its article on disk.

The groups inside a category. A category of Parts I to IV lists its articles in four groups, each of which adds one thing to the group before it. The group - Theory holds the structure itself, read with the product of its elements. The group - Operator Theory holds the operators on that structure — the left and right multiplications, the derivations, the automorphisms, the actions and the sandwich — and adds an operator layer and nothing else. The group - * Theory holds the same structure read with an involution on its elements. The group - * Operator Theory holds the operators built from the involution, of which the adjoint is the archetype. The front-matter category Introduction and Conventions is the exception: it lists two subcategories and no group.

The two involutions. The star names the involutive layer in both groups, and the layer of the elements and the layer of the operators are two structures and not one: the involution of - * Theory sits on the elements and the adjoint of - * Operator Theory sits on the operators. They need not coincide: an involution on the elements does not by itself produce an adjoint on the operators, and two adjoints of the same structure may differ — the transpose and the Hermitian adjoint of a matrix are both adjoints, taken with respect to different pairings. When the two do agree the representation is a *-representation, and the agreement is proved in the article, never assumed by the menu.

The order and the fill rule. The four groups are placed in the order above, and the group - Applications, which holds the concrete instances of the structure, closes the category. The groups - Operator Theory and - * Operator Theory are present in a category whenever it has an operator layer, and the group - * Theory is present in every category of Parts I to IV, whether or not it yet holds an article.

The category names and numbers. The menu numbers its categories, and an article elsewhere in the corpus may address one by number rather than by name: category n means the n-th category of Parts I to VI, read in order, the front matter Introduction and Conventions not being counted. This article refers to categories by name throughout, because the names are the stable thing and the numbers shift whenever a category is inserted or split. A reader who meets a bare number in another article can resolve it in the menu.

The article files. The corpus is split into two collections, each with its own folder: a mathematical article is a file articles_maths/<slug>.md and a physical article is a file articles_physics/<slug>.md, where the slug is the title in lower case with hyphens in place of spaces and punctuation. The folder and the menu agree — an article lies in the folder whose menu lists it, and no article lies in both.

The article skeleton. Every article opens with its title as the first heading, followed by an Introduction that states what the article does and what it assumes, then the thematic sections, and closes with three sections in this order: Summary, Summary of Notation, and Further Reading. The introduction states the article's boundaries — what it deliberately does not cover, and where the reader will find it. This article is the one exception: its notation is merged into the Summary and its table closes the article.

Cross-references and boundaries. A concept is introduced once, in the category that owns it, and referred to afterwards rather than restated. Articles do not duplicate the content of other articles; they cite them. Every article that touches a structure belonging to a later part states the deferral explicitly, which is the mechanism by which Rule 1 is enforced.

The word "interval". Two different objects share the word, and the corpus keeps them apart. In the mathematical corpus an interval is an order-theoretic or lattice object: a convex subset of a linearly ordered set, the interval $[a,b]=\{x:a\le x\le b\}$ of a lattice or of a poset of effects, defined by the order and not by a form. In the physical corpus the interval is the value of the quadratic form, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=-\lvert Q_t\rvert^2+\lvert Q_x\rvert^2+\lvert Q_y\rvert^2+\lvert Q_z\rvert^2$ on the material sector, defined by the form and not by an order. A statement about the sign of the interval is therefore never a statement about a lattice interval, and a lattice interval is never the line element; where the two senses could meet, the corpus writes the form value or the quadratic form for the physical one.

The letter $\iota$ is not used in Part VI. Every article registered under Part VI : Synthetic Studies of maths.md writes no $\iota$, in any spelling. The letter is not reserved there and names no object of those studies: a comparison with a Clifford algebra is written by its values or by the words "the Clifford image", an interior product as the pairing $\omega(X_f,\,\cdot\,)=df$, and an inner automorphism in words. A display in Part VI that introduces $\iota$ is defective.

LaTeX and Typesetting

The articles are rendered with KaTeX on the page, and the following rules are observed in the source.

  • Inline mathematics is written between single dollars, and display mathematics between double dollars on lines of their own.
  • Inline mathematics never spans a line break. If a formula is too long, it is displayed.
  • Display mathematics inside a list is indented four spaces.
  • A prime may follow a subscript as well as precede it: the forms q'_t and q_t' are both admitted, and an article keeps one order throughout.
  • Tables are Markdown tables. A LaTeX array is never used for a table.
  • Every heading that introduces a comparison or a classification is a real ### heading, and the objects are defined before they are tabulated.
  • Bold marks the result. In a Remark that states a verdict, the clause carrying the outcome is set in bold, **...**, the object of the sentence staying in plain roman: the subspace is a commutative Jordan algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ of degree two**. A statement already displayed as a Theorem, a Proposition or a Proof is not bolded; the convention applies to the prose of a Remark and to the lead sentence of a section that states an outcome.
  • The third derivative is written d^3/dt^3. The macro \slashed is retired and is not used; a gamma contraction is written out, as Matrix Representations Conventions states. The characters |, {, }, < and > inside mathematics need no escaping.
  • A malformed formula is rendered in red on the page rather than reported as an error, so mathematics is checked by eye and not by the absence of a warning.

Summary

Two labels run beside the marks of the elements: $\mathrm{fop}$ marks a Fock operator, an operator on the Fock space and not an element of the algebra, and $\mathrm{opp}$ marks the opposite algebra, whose product is reversed. The two are not read for one another: $\mathrm{fop}$ is written on a glyph, $\mathrm{opp}$ on an algebra name or on an element that names a tensor factor. The dagger is the general adjoint and stands in the caret, so $a_{\mathrm{fop}}^{\dagger}$ carries the two marks in two slots, and the dagger alone never marks a glyph as an operator. No index is raised anywhere in the corpus: every index is written low, and a contraction is a form.

A label is the upright roman name of a variant of an object — a construction, a frame, a reading, a part or a role — written lowercase at the length its article fixes, two or three letters in the main and longer where the short form would be ambiguous. Every label stands in the subscript and none in the caret: the label comes first in the field and an index already there follows it after a comma, $L_{\mathrm{axf},e_\mu}$, $T_{\mathrm{em},\mu\nu}$, $V_K^{\mathrm{fin}}$ becoming $V_{\mathrm{fin},K}$. One field is written without the comma, $Q_{\mathrm{f}t}$, and a coefficient carries the label of its element. The labels are indexed in §The Two-Letter Labels and §The Three-Letter Labels; $\mathrm{op}$ is not one of them.

The caret carries a power, a conjugation, a transposition and a construction, and no label: $e^\lambda$, $a^{\dagger}$, $L^T$, $\mathbb{B}^\times$, $S^{\otimes n}$. An operator name written with its argument is the one word that stands there, $\gamma_n^{\operatorname{sym}}$, $K^{\operatorname{gal}(K/F)}$, and it carries its argument and never stands bare.

A subscript is written with the underscore and takes a single token: one character bare, two or more in braces. It counts when it only distinguishes occurrences and names when it is a role label; a role-labelled element carries its label in its coefficients. The admitted forms are a digit, a letter, two indices, a sign, an upright word and a grouped expression, and the slot takes any KaTeX expression beyond them. A regular matrix puts the size and the family in one subscript, $\mathsf M_{4L}$ and $\mathsf M_{4LR\ast}$, the family in italic capitals after the size and the frame after them with a comma, $\mathsf M_{4L,\mathrm{pau}}$. A subscript is never a partial derivative. An angular-momentum total takes the role subscript, $s_{\mathrm{tot}}$, and its projection the axis, $s_{\mathrm{tot},z}$; the total generator carries the role subscript and then the vector index, $\tilde S_{\mathrm{tot},k}$; the orbital part is $L_k$ here and $\tilde L_k$ in the physical corpus.

The working conventions of the corpus are these. Every article is registered in exactly one menu, maths.md or physics.md, with a comment that outlines it, and a leading + marks a planned article. A category of Parts I to IV lists its articles in the four groups Theory, Operator Theory, * Theory and * Operator Theory, and - Applications closes it. The article files are articles_maths/<slug>.md and articles_physics/<slug>.md, and every article follows the skeleton Introduction, thematic sections, Summary, Summary of Notation, Further Reading.

The category numbers count the categories of the parts in menu order; the front matter is not counted. Since a number shifts when a category is inserted or split, an article addresses a category by name, and the name is the stable thing. The letter $\iota$ is not used in Part VI, where a comparison with a Clifford algebra is written by its values or as the Clifford image, an interior product as a pairing and an inner automorphism in words. The typography is KaTeX on the page: inline mathematics between single dollars and never across a line break, display mathematics between double dollars on lines of their own, tables in Markdown and never as a LaTeX array, and a malformed formula rendered in red rather than reported.

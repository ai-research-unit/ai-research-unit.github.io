# __Fréchet and Locally Convex Sesqualgebras__

## Introduction

*Banach Sesqualgebras* treats the complete normed case, where a single submultiplicative norm carries the topology and the submultiplicative estimate gives the joint continuity of the product. This article weakens the norm to a family of seminorms, the step from the normed to the locally convex theory, and it is the sesquilinear counterpart of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*. The topology is generated by a family of seminorms, the product is continuous for that topology, and the twisted action of the base involution rides along: the conjugate module carries the same seminorms, and the second slot is read there.

The one new ingredient with respect to the normed case is the family. A single seminorm whose multiplicativity is measured by a finite constant is replaced by an equivalent submultiplicative one, so no information is lost in the normable case; a family of seminorms need not admit such a replacement, and the objects for which it does are the **locally m-convex** ones, generated by submultiplicative seminorms $p_\alpha(xy) \leq p_\alpha(x)p_\alpha(y)$. Those are the objects to which the Arens–Michael decomposition applies, and it identifies a complete locally m-convex sesqualgebra with a projective limit of Banach sesqualgebras, the sesquilinear form of the decomposition of the bilinear layer.

Three facts organise the article. A **locally convex sesqualgebra** is a topological sesqualgebra whose topology is locally convex, and the product is jointly continuous whenever the seminorms obey $p_\alpha(xy) \leq C p_\beta(x)p_\beta(y)$ for some $\beta$ and $C$, which is the criterion of the bilinear layer and implies the separate continuity that the layer asks for; the twisted structure adds only that the seminorms of the conjugate module are those of $A$, which they are because $\varsigma$ is isometric. A **locally m-convex sesqualgebra** has the estimate with $C = 1$ and $\beta = \alpha$, and then the kernels $N_\alpha = \ker p_\alpha$ are two-sided ideals, the quotients $A/N_\alpha$ are normed sesqualgebras, and their completions are the Banach sesqualgebras of which the object is the projective limit. And a **Fréchet sesqualgebra** is a complete metrisable locally convex one, defined by countably many seminorms and carrying the invariant metric $d(x,y) = \sum_n 2^{-n}\min(1,p_n(x-y))$, so that the Baire-based theorems of *Fréchet Spaces* apply.

The article defines the locally convex and the locally m-convex objects, reads the product and the twisted slot through the seminorm criterion of the bilinear layer, proves the statement about the kernels and the Banach quotients, states the Arens–Michael decomposition and its Fréchet corollary, and reads the examples and the boundaries. It cites the analytic prerequisites rather than proving them: the seminorms and the topology they generate are *Locally Convex Spaces*, the invariant metric and the projective-limit description are *Fréchet Spaces*, the criterion for a continuous product is *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*The Continuity Conditions*, the completed quotients are *Banach Sesqualgebras*, and the continuity of the two involutions is *The Continuity of the Involution*. Throughout, $\mathbb{K}$ is $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, $\varsigma$ its continuous involution, $A$ a topological $\mathbb{K}$-space carrying a product linear in the first slot and $\varsigma$-semilinear in the second, and the seminorm families are separating, so that the topology is Hausdorff.

## The Locally Convex Object

### The Seminorm Family

**Definition.** A **locally convex sesqualgebra** over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ is a topological sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ whose topology is locally convex, that is generated by a separating family $\{p_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}$ of seminorms; the product is required to be separately continuous, and the conjugate module $A^{\varsigma}$ carries the topology generated by the same family.

The definition is *Topological Sesqualgebras*, §*The Definition* with the locally convex hypothesis on the topology, and *Locally Convex Spaces*, §*The Seminorm Characterisation* for the reading of that topology through the family. The family is fixed for the article and ranges over a directed index set.

**Remark (the seminorms of the conjugate module).** The twisted action $\lambda \cdot x = \varsigma(\lambda)x$ satisfies $p_\alpha(\lambda \cdot x) = \lvert\varsigma(\lambda)\rvert p_\alpha(x) = \lvert\lambda\rvert p_\alpha(x)$, because the involution of $\mathbb{K}$ is isometric, as *Banach Sesqualgebras*, §*The Isometric Involution* proves; so the seminorms of $A$ are seminorms of the conjugate module and no separate family has to be introduced. The identity $A \to A^{\varsigma}$ is a homeomorphism of the additive groups, and it is linear only at $\varsigma = \mathrm{id}$.

### The Product and the Twisted Slot

**Theorem (the seminorm criterion for the product).** Let $\{p_\alpha\}$ be a separating family generating the topology of a locally convex $\mathbb{K}$-space $A$ with a product additive in each variable. If for every $\alpha$ there are $\beta$ and a constant $C \geq 1$ with

$$
p_\alpha(xy) \leq C\,p_\beta(x)\,p_\beta(y) \qquad (x, y \in A) ,
$$

then the product is jointly continuous, hence separately continuous, and $A$ is a locally convex sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$. Conversely, if the product is jointly continuous, then for every $\alpha$ there are $\beta, \gamma$ and $C$ with $p_\alpha(xy) \leq C\,p_\beta(x)p_\gamma(y)$; for a directed family this is equivalent to the estimate above with a single index.

*Proof.* The estimate bounds the product on the product of two $\beta$-balls, so the product is continuous at the origin, and a bilinear map continuous at the origin is jointly continuous; joint continuity implies separate continuity, which is the hypothesis of the layer. Conversely, joint continuity at the origin gives a basic neighbourhood $U_\beta \times U_\gamma$ on which $p_\alpha$ is bounded, hence the displayed estimate, and a directed family absorbs the two indices into one, *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*The Continuity Conditions*. The estimate with $\beta = \gamma$ is the classical condition for a locally convex algebra. That a separately continuous product need not be jointly continuous is the remark of *Topological Sesqualgebras*, §*Joint Continuity*, so the estimate is sufficient but not necessary for an object of the layer; the product on the pair $(A,A^{\varsigma})$ obeys the same criterion because the two factors carry the same seminorms, and the two scalar rules are algebraic, *Sesqualgebras*, §*The Two Scalar Rules*. $\square$

**Corollary (the submultiplicative case).** If the family is submultiplicative, $p_\alpha(xy) \leq p_\alpha(x)p_\alpha(y)$ for every $\alpha$, then the product is jointly continuous and $A$ is an object of the layer *Topological Sesqualgebras* with its locally convex topology.

*Proof.* Take $\beta = \gamma = \alpha$ and $C = 1$ in the criterion, and apply the estimate $\lVert x_ny_n - x_0y_0\rVert$ of *Banach Sesqualgebras*, §*The Submultiplicative Estimate* seminorm by seminorm. $\square$

### The One-Sided Multiplications

**Proposition (the one-sided multiplications).** Let $a \in A$ and let $L_a(x) = ax$ and $R_a(x) = xa$. Then $L_a$ is continuous and $\varsigma$-semilinear, and $R_a$ is continuous and $\mathbb{K}$-linear. Moreover, if the product satisfies the estimate of §*The Product and the Twisted Slot* for a constant $C$ independent of $a$, then for each $\alpha$ there are $\beta$ with

$$
p_\alpha(L_a x) \leq C\,p_\beta(a)\,p_\beta(x), \qquad p_\alpha(R_a x) \leq C\,p_\beta(x)\,p_\beta(a) .
$$

*Proof.* The parities are *Topological Sesqualgebras*, §*The One-Sided Multiplications*. Continuity is separate continuity, which the locally convex hypothesis assumes, with the bound depending on the fixed $a$: for each $\alpha$ there are $\beta$ and $C_a$ with $p_\alpha(ax) \leq C_a p_\beta(x)$. The uniform estimate with the factor $p_\beta(a)$ is the joint-continuity criterion of §*The Product and the Twisted Slot* read with one variable fixed, and it is asserted under that hypothesis and not under separate continuity alone, which need not supply it. $\square$

## Locally m-Convex Sesqualgebras

### Submultiplicative Seminorms

**Definition.** A **locally m-convex sesqualgebra** over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ is a locally convex sesqualgebra whose topology is generated by a family of submultiplicative seminorms $p_\alpha(xy) \leq p_\alpha(x)p_\alpha(y)$.

**Lemma (regularisation, quoted).** Let $A$ be unital with $1 \neq 0$, let $p$ be a seminorm with $p(1) = 1$ and $p(xy) \leq C\,p(x)p(y)$ for some $C \geq 1$. Then $\tilde p(x) = \sup\{p(xy) : p(y) \leq 1\}$ is a submultiplicative seminorm with $\ker\tilde p = \ker p$ and $p \leq \tilde p \leq C\,p$.

*Proof.* This is the regularisation lemma of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*Submultiplicative Seminorms*: the set over which the supremum is taken contains $1$, so $\tilde p(x) \leq Cp(x)$ and $\tilde p$ is finite; homogeneity and subadditivity are inherited from $p$, and $\tilde p(x) \geq p(x)$ by $y = 1$. $\square$

**Remark (why the regularisation needs a single seminorm).** The lemma replaces one seminorm by an equivalent submultiplicative one, and it is the reason a normed sesqualgebra may always be assumed submultiplicative. For a generating family the constants $C_\alpha$ cannot always be absorbed simultaneously, and it is exactly this failure that separates the locally m-convex sesqualgebras from the locally convex ones: a Fréchet algebra may be generated by seminorms whose constants grow and which admit no equivalent submultiplicative family, as the boundary example of §*The Boundaries* records for the bilinear layer. The sesqualgebra inherits the phenomenon through its seminorms and not through a new obstruction.

### The Banach Quotients

**Theorem (the kernels and the quotients).** Let $A$ be a locally m-convex sesqualgebra with generating family $\{p_\alpha\}$ of submultiplicative seminorms and put $N_\alpha = \ker p_\alpha$. Then each $N_\alpha$ is a closed two-sided ideal, the quotient $A/N_\alpha$ is a normed sesqualgebra over the same datum $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ with the norm induced by $p_\alpha$, and its completion

$$
A_\alpha = \widehat{A/N_\alpha}
$$

is a Banach sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$.

*Proof.* Submultiplicativity gives $p_\alpha(xy) \leq p_\alpha(x)p_\alpha(y)$ and $p_\alpha(yx) \leq p_\alpha(y)p_\alpha(x)$, so an element with $p_\alpha(x) = 0$ multiplies both ways into the kernel; $N_\alpha$ is closed because $p_\alpha$ is continuous, and it is a submodule by the homogeneity and subadditivity of the seminorm. The quotient is a sesqualgebra by *Ideals and Quotients of a Sesqualgebra*, §*The Product Descends*, and the quotient norm $\bar p_\alpha(x + N_\alpha) = p_\alpha(x)$ is well defined and submultiplicative, so $A/N_\alpha$ is a normed sesqualgebra; its completion is a Banach sesqualgebra by *The Completion of a Sesqualgebra*, §*The Product on the Completion*. $\square$

**Remark (the involution and the quotient).** The base involution $\varsigma$ is unchanged by the quotient, which is over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$; an algebra involution $*$ descends only to the quotients whose kernel it preserves. When $*$ is a $\varsigma$-semilinear involution with $*N_\alpha \subseteq N_\alpha$ for every $\alpha$ — automatic when the seminorms are invariant, $p_\alpha(x^{*}) = p_\alpha(x)$ — the quotients and their completions carry the induced involution, by *Ideals and Quotients of a Sesqualgebra*, §*The Involution Descends*. In the invariant case the derived operation $x \star y = xy^{*}$ satisfies $p_\alpha(x \star y) \leq p_\alpha(x)p_\alpha(y)$ as well, so it is a sesquilinear product of the same locally m-convex type.

### The Arens–Michael Decomposition

**Theorem (Arens–Michael, sesquilinear form).** A topological sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ is a complete locally m-convex sesqualgebra if and only if it is isomorphic, as a topological sesqualgebra, to a projective limit of Banach sesqualgebras over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$.

*Proof.* One direction is the theorem of §*The Banach Quotients*: the complete locally m-convex object is the projective limit $\varprojlim A_\alpha$ of the Banach sesqualgebras $A_\alpha = \widehat{A/N_\alpha}$, by the argument of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*The Banach Quotients* applied to the sesquilinear product, which is bilinear on the pair and restricts and descends with the seminorms. For the other direction, a projective limit of Banach sesqualgebras carries the seminorms $q_\alpha(x) = \lVert x_\alpha\rVert_\alpha$, each submultiplicative because the factor norm is and the projection is multiplicative, and the limit topology is generated by them; the sesquilinear structure is componentwise. $\square$

**Corollary (the Fréchet case).** A sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ is a complete metrisable locally m-convex sesqualgebra if and only if it is the projective limit of a sequence of Banach sesqualgebras over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ with continuous connecting maps.

*Proof.* A complete metrisable locally m-convex object has a countable generating family of submultiplicative seminorms, so the index set of the decomposition may be taken countable; conversely a countable projective limit of Banach sesqualgebras is metrisable, complete and locally m-convex. This is the corollary of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*The Arens–Michael Theorem* with the componentwise sesquilinear structure. $\square$

## Fréchet Sesqualgebras

### The Definition and the Metric

**Definition.** A **Fréchet sesqualgebra** over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ is a complete metrisable locally convex sesqualgebra; equivalently, its topology is generated by a countable separating family of seminorms and it is complete for the resulting uniformity. A **Fréchet m-sesqualgebra** is a Fréchet sesqualgebra which is locally m-convex.

**Proposition (the invariant metric).** Let $(p_n)_{n\geq0}$ be an increasing generating sequence of seminorms of a Fréchet sesqualgebra $A$. Then

$$
d(x,y) = \sum_{n\geq0}2^{-n}\min\bigl(1, p_n(x-y)\bigr)
$$

is a translation-invariant metric on $A$ generating its topology, and $A$ is complete for it.

*Proof.* This is the construction of *Fréchet Spaces*, §*The Invariant Metric*: the series converges because each term is at most $2^{-n}$, the function $d$ is a metric because the family is separating, it is invariant under translation because the seminorms are, and it generates the topology because a sequence converges for $d$ exactly when it converges for every $p_n$. Completeness is the hypothesis. $\square$

### Examples

**Example ($C^\infty$ and the derivative seminorms, verdict: a Fréchet m-sesqualgebra).** Let $A = C^\infty[0,1]$ with the seminorms $p_n(f) = \sup_{0\leq k\leq n}\lVert f^{(k)}\rVert_\infty$, the involution $f \mapsto \bar f$ and the product $f \star g = f\bar g$. The Leibniz rule gives $p_n(fg) \leq 2^{n}p_n(f)p_n(g)$, and $p_n(f\bar g) \leq 2^{n}p_n(f)p_n(g)$ likewise, since $\lvert\bar g^{(k)}\rvert = \lvert g^{(k)}\rvert$; the rescaled seminorms $q_n = 2^{n}p_n$ are equivalent to the $p_n$ and submultiplicative, so $A$ is a Fréchet m-sesqualgebra, not normable, and complete. It is the model of the locally m-convex infinite-dimensional case.

**Example (rapidly decreasing sequences, verdict: a Fréchet m-sesqualgebra of sequences).** Let $s$ be the space of rapidly decreasing sequences with the seminorms $q_m(a) = \sup_k k^{m}\lvert a_k\rvert$, the termwise conjugation and the product $a \star b$ with $(a \star b)_k = a_k\bar b_k$. Then $q_m(a \star b) \leq q_m(a)q_m(b)$, so $s$ is a Fréchet m-sesqualgebra; it is neither associative nor commutative, it is not normable, and it is the sequence model of the Schwartz space, whose analytic instance is Part III's.

**Example (formal power series, verdict: a Fréchet m-sesqualgebra over the adic topology).** Let $A = \mathbb{K}[[X_1,\dots,X_d]]$ with the adic topology of $\mathrm{M} = (X_1,\dots,X_d)$, the seminorms $p_n(f) = 2^{-k_n(f)}$ of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*Examples*, the coefficientwise conjugation and the product $f \star g = f\bar g$. The seminorms are submultiplicative, so $A$ is a Fréchet m-sesqualgebra, the projective limit of the finite-dimensional quotients $\mathbb{K}[X]/\mathrm{M}^{n}$, which are Banach sesqualgebras of finite rank.

**Example (the projective limit form, verdict: the decomposition is not decoration).** Let $(A_n)$ be a sequence of Banach sesqualgebras over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ with continuous connecting maps and let $A = \varprojlim_n A_n$ carry the seminorms $p_n(x) = \lVert x_n\rVert_n$ and the componentwise product. Then $A$ is a Fréchet m-sesqualgebra by the corollary of §*The Arens–Michael Decomposition*, and every Fréchet m-sesqualgebra arises this way. The example is the construction the decomposition provides, and the countable product $\prod_n A_n$ is its special case.

### Ideals and Quotients

**Theorem.** Let $A$ be a Fréchet sesqualgebra and let $I \subseteq A$ be a closed two-sided ideal. Then $A/I$ with the quotient topology is a Fréchet sesqualgebra over the same datum; if $A$ is locally m-convex then so is $A/I$; and if $A$ carries an algebra involution preserving $I$, the quotient carries the induced one.

*Proof.* A quotient of a metrisable locally convex space by a closed subspace is metrisable and locally convex, and it is complete because $A$ is complete and $I$ is closed, the quotient map being open; this is the theorem of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*Ideals and Quotients*. The product descends by *Ideals and Quotients of a Sesqualgebra*, §*The Product Descends*, and is continuous for the quotient topology because the quotient map is continuous and open; the quotient seminorms $\bar p_n(x + I) = \inf_{y\in I}p_n(x + y)$ are submultiplicative when the $p_n$ are, by approximating the two cosets and using that $I$ is two-sided. The involution descends by *Ideals and Quotients of a Sesqualgebra*, §*The Involution Descends*. $\square$

### The Boundaries

**Example (an incomplete metrisable locally m-convex sesqualgebra).** Let $A = \mathbb{C}[X]$ with the seminorms $p_K(f) = \sup_K\lvert f\rvert$ of uniform convergence on compact subsets of $\mathbb{C}$, the coefficientwise conjugation and the product $f \star g = f\bar g$. The seminorms are submultiplicative, so $A$ is a metrisable locally m-convex sesqualgebra, and it is not complete, since the exponential series converges locally uniformly to a non-polynomial. Its completion is the Fréchet m-sesqualgebra $\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})$ of entire functions. The example separates the metrisable case from the Fréchet case.

**Remark (the m-convex hypothesis is not automatic).** The Fréchet algebra $L^\omega[0,1] = \bigcap_{p\geq1}L^p[0,1]$ of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*, §*Non-Examples* is the classical witness that a Fréchet algebra need not be locally m-convex, Arens having shown that its topology admits no equivalent submultiplicative family. The sesqualgebra article records the same boundary without claiming a sesquilinear counterexample: the product $f \star g = f\bar g$ is continuous for the same topology by Hölder's inequality, so the space is a Fréchet sesqualgebra, and whether it is locally m-convex for the twisted product is the m-convexity question of Arens read through the conjugation, which is an isometry of every $L^p$. The point is left to the reader and recorded as a standing boundary of the article.

## The Collapse at the Trivial Involution

### The Collapse

**Theorem (the collapse).** Let $A$ be a locally convex or Fréchet sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ with $\varsigma = \mathrm{id}$. Then the two scalar rules coincide, the product is bilinear, the conjugate module is the module with the same topology and the same seminorms, and $A$ is a locally convex or Fréchet algebra in the sense of *Locally Convex and Fréchet Algebras*; conversely every such algebra is a locally convex or Fréchet sesqualgebra over $(\mathbb{K},\mathrm{id})$. The locally m-convex and the Fréchet m-convex cases correspond in the same way, and the Arens–Michael decomposition reduces to the decomposition of the bilinear layer.

*Proof.* With $\varsigma = \mathrm{id}$ the second rule reads $x(\lambda y) = \lambda(xy)$ and is the first; the twisted action is the ordinary action, so the seminorms of the conjugate module are the seminorms of the module with the same action, and the whole construction is the locally convex algebra of the bilinear layer. The correspondence of the m-convex cases is the submultiplicative estimate read without a twist, and the decomposition reduces termwise. $\square$

### Full Type

**Remark (full type).** As in *Banach Sesqualgebras*, §*The Objects of Full Type*, a locally convex sesqualgebra of **full type** in the sense of *Sesqualgebras*, §*The Collapse at the Identity* with a nontrivial involution is neither associative nor commutative, so it is not a locally convex algebra; the topology, now a family rather than a norm, again plays no role in the obstruction.

## Summary

A **locally convex sesqualgebra** over $(\mathbb{K},\varsigma)$ is a topological sesqualgebra whose topology is generated by a separating family of seminorms, the conjugate module carrying the same family because $\varsigma$ is isometric; its product is jointly continuous whenever $p_\alpha(xy) \leq Cp_\beta(x)p_\beta(y)$ for some $\beta$ and $C$, which implies the separate continuity of the layer; a separately continuous product need not be jointly continuous, so the estimate is sufficient and not necessary. A **locally m-convex sesqualgebra** has the estimate with $C = 1$ and $\beta = \alpha$, and then the kernels $N_\alpha = \ker p_\alpha$ are closed two-sided ideals, the quotients $A/N_\alpha$ are normed sesqualgebras and their completions are Banach sesqualgebras, so a complete locally m-convex sesqualgebra is a projective limit of Banach sesqualgebras, the **Arens–Michael decomposition** in its sesquilinear form; in the countable case it is a **Fréchet m-sesqualgebra**. A **Fréchet sesqualgebra** is a complete metrisable locally convex one, defined by countably many seminorms and carrying the invariant metric $\sum_n 2^{-n}\min(1,p_n(x-y))$; a closed two-sided ideal has a Fréchet quotient which is locally m-convex when the object is, and an involution preserving the ideal descends. The single seminorm with $p(xy) \leq Cp(x)p(y)$ is regularised to a submultiplicative one, $p \leq \tilde p \leq Cp$, and it is the failure of the simultaneous absorption of the constants that separates the locally convex from the locally m-convex objects; the boundary is the Arens algebra, whose sesquilinear reading is a Fréchet sesqualgebra and whose m-convexity is not claimed here. At $\varsigma = \mathrm{id}$ the object is a locally convex or Fréchet algebra and the category collapses, and a locally convex object of full type with a nontrivial involution is not an algebra at all.

The Specificities of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra

Introduction

This article isolates the block among the twelve structures of the biquaternion complex space by a single law, the law of being $K$-Hermitian. The block is the symmetric quaternionic sesquilinear product $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\tfrac12(\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}+\tilde Q^{*}\tilde P^{\natural})$ of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions; the twelve structures and their four general products are the subject of The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space, and the model of the method is The Specificities of the General Plain Algebra, which isolates the plain product by left alternativity. The twin articles are The Specificities of the General Quaternionic Algebra and The Specificities of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

The law has two clauses, and each is an identity in the product. The first is the conjugate-symmetry $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\overline{\tilde Q\star\tilde P}$, which is to say that the product is fixed by the adapted exchange of The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of a Sesqualgebra Product; the second is that the scalar part is the Krein form, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)=K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$, the form of Introduction to the General Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions and The Krein Gram Matrix and the Restrictions of the Form. The theorem of the article is that among the twelve structures the block is the only one satisfying both clauses, each clause alone being satisfied by another structure: the plain sesquilinear symmetric part satisfies the first and not the second, and the general quaternionic sesquilinear product satisfies the second and not the first. The law is therefore sharp, and the two structures that border it are named and used as the witnesses of the sharpness.

The product is Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions; its diagonal is The Non-Central Diagonal and the Two Halves of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; its element theory is Norm and Invertibility in the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Square Roots of Minus One, Zero and Plus One in the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Square Roots of a General Element in the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Idempotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and Nilpotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra; and its operators are The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, where the $K$-adjointness that the law delivers is computed.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$; $\tilde Q=\sum_\mu Q_\mu e_\mu$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$ and central scalar imaginary $i$; $\mathbf Q=\sum_kQ_ke_k$; $(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)=\sum_kP_kQ_k$; ${}^{\natural}$ negates $e_1,e_2,e_3$ and fixes the coefficients, $\bar{\cdot}$ conjugates the coefficients, and ${}^{*}={}^{\natural}\circ\bar{\cdot}$; $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde X)=X_0$ is the scalar part; $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$; and

$$ K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu P_\mu\overline{Q_\mu} =P_0\overline{Q_0}-(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf Q}) $$

is the Krein form. The twelve structures are the four general products $\mathrm{GPA},\mathrm{GQA},\mathrm{GPS},\mathrm{GQS}$, their four symmetric parts $\mathrm{SPA},\mathrm{SQA},\mathrm{SPS},\mathrm{SQS}$ and their four antisymmetric parts $\mathrm{APA},\mathrm{AQA},\mathrm{APS},\mathrm{AQS}$, with the two first rows bilinear and the two last rows sesquilinear, as in The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space. Throughout, the block is $\mathrm{SQS}$, and the products of the twelve are written with their three-letter names.

The Law: Being $K$-Hermitian

Definition ($K$-Hermitian product). A product $\circ$ on $\mathbb{B}$ is $K$-Hermitian when the two identities

$$ \text{(C1)}\quad \tilde P\circ\tilde Q=\overline{\tilde Q\circ\tilde P}, \qquad \text{(C2)}\quad \mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\circ\tilde Q\bigr)=K(\tilde P,\tilde Q) $$

hold for all $\tilde P,\tilde Q\in\mathbb{B}$. The first is the conjugate-symmetry of the product, the second is the scalar-part identity.

Remark (the two clauses read one at a time). The clause (C1) says that the product is fixed by the adapted exchange $f\mapsto\overline{f\circ E}$, the transposition composed with the coefficientwise conjugation, which is the exchange that keeps the sesqualgebra class (The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of a Sesqualgebra Product). The clause (C2) says that the scalar part of the product is the form $K$ and not another of the four forms of the row, the complex bilinear form, the plain Hermitian form or the plain bilinear form. Each clause is an identity in $\tilde P,\tilde Q$ and each is invariant under the change of the product, so they can be tested one structure at a time.

Theorem (the block is $K$-Hermitian). The block satisfies (C1) and (C2).

Proof. The scalar–vector form of Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions is

$$ \tilde P\star\tilde Q=\bigl[P_0\overline{Q_0}-(\mathbf P,\overline{\mathbf Q})\bigr]-P_0\overline{\mathbf Q}-\overline{Q_0}\mathbf P; $$

the bracket is $K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$ and is the scalar part, which is (C2). For (C1), conjugate the product with the arguments exchanged and use that the two conjugations are anti-automorphisms of the plain product: the scalar bracket is fixed because $K$ is Hermitian, and the vector part $-Q_0\overline{\mathbf P}-\overline{Q_0}\mathbf P$ is the same as the vector part of $\tilde P\star\tilde Q$. So $\overline{\tilde Q\star\tilde P}=\tilde P\star\tilde Q$.

The Criterion

Theorem (the specificity). Among the twelve structures of the biquaternion complex space, the block $\mathrm{SQS}$ is the only $K$-Hermitian product.

Proof. The block is $K$-Hermitian by the theorem of §The Law: Being $K$-Hermitian; the other eleven are disposed of in §The Isolation among the Twelve, each failing at least one of the two clauses on a named pair.

The Isolation among the Twelve

Theorem (the isolation). Among the twelve structures of The 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, the block is the only $K$-Hermitian product.

Proof. The block satisfies both clauses by §The Law: Being $K$-Hermitian. The other eleven each fail at least one clause. The three bilinear general products GPA and the bilinear parts SPA, SQA fail conjugate-symmetry at $(ie_1,e_1)$, where a bilinear product reverses the imaginary unit, and fail the scalar-part identity at the elements $e_1$ and $ie_1$, where the bilinear form differs from $K$; the two sesquilinear general products GPS and GQS fail conjugate-symmetry at $(e_1,e_2)$, where the two sides are opposite signs of $e_3$, GQS satisfying the scalar-part identity while GPS fails it at $e_1$; the symmetric plain sesquilinear part SPS satisfies conjugate-symmetry and fails the scalar-part identity at $e_1$, its scalar part being the definite form $|P_0|^2+\lVert\mathbf P\rVert^2$ and not $K$; and the four antisymmetric parts fail both, being skew-conjugate-symmetric — so conjugate-symmetry would force them to vanish — and having identically vanishing scalar part.

The isolation is sharp, and each clause alone is satisfied by another structure: deleting conjugate-symmetry admits the general quaternionic sesquilinear product GQS, and deleting the scalar-part identity admits the symmetric plain sesquilinear part SPS. The two are the companions of the block in the sesquilinear row: GQS is the product of which the block is the symmetric part, and SPS is the symmetric part of the plain sesquilinear product. The block is their meeting point, carrying the scalar part of the first and the symmetry of the second.

What the Criterion Delivers

The scalar part is the form, not a value. The clause (C2) makes the scalar projection of the product the Krein form, so the diagonal of the block is $\tilde Q\star\tilde Q=K(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)e_0-Q_0\overline{\mathbf Q}-\overline{Q_0}\mathbf Q$; the non-centrality of the diagonal, the shape of the idempotents and the square-zero cone all follow from that one formula, as in The Non-Central Diagonal and the Two Halves of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, Idempotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra and Nilpotents of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

The $K$-adjoint of the multiplication. The clause (C1), together with the natural conjugation as the compatibility involution, gives the operator identity

$$ K\bigl(L^{\star}_{\tilde A}\tilde X,\tilde Y\bigr) =\overline{K\bigl(\tilde X,L^{\star}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}\tilde Y\bigr)} , $$

so the adjoint of the left multiplication by $\tilde A$ with respect to $K$ is the left multiplication by $\tilde A^{\natural}$; the identity is computed in The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra, and it is the operator form of the law.

The element theory is governed by the law. The two clauses fix the scalar part and the exchange; the class of an element of the block — regular, pure zero divisor, non-pure zero divisor — is then decided by the scalar coordinate $Q_0$ and the central square $c(\tilde Q)$, and not by the form $K$, as in Zero Divisors of the Symmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra.

The boundary. A product with the scalar part $K$ but without the symmetry, such as $\mathrm{GQS}$, has a diagonal whose vector part carries the cross product in addition to the mixed term; a product with the symmetry but without the scalar part $K$, such as $\mathrm{SPS}$, has a central diagonal. The block is the unique product whose diagonal keeps the mixed term and the form at once, with neither the extra cross term of the first boundary nor the vanishing vector part of the second.

Worked Examples

The block at the witness $(e_0,e_1)$. $\mathrm{SQS}(e_0,e_1)=-e_1$ and $\overline{\mathrm{SQS}(e_1,e_0)}=\overline{-e_1}=-e_1$, so conjugate-symmetry holds; and $\mathrm{Sc}(\mathrm{SQS}(e_0,e_1))=0=K(e_0,e_1)$, so the scalar-part identity holds.

The scalar-part identity at a complex element. $\mathrm{Sc}(\mathrm{SQS}(ie_1,ie_1))=K(ie_1,ie_1)=-1$, the Hermitian form reading the modulus of the complex coefficient; a bilinear product cannot see it. This is the clause that separates the block from the bilinear structures, and the reason the block's scalar part is $K$ and not the complex bilinear form.

The two clauses at the boundaries. The general quaternionic sesquilinear product $\mathrm{GQS}$ has scalar part $K$ and fails conjugate-symmetry, and the symmetric plain sesquilinear part $\mathrm{SPS}$ is conjugate-symmetric with a definite scalar part; the block is the one product that carries both, neither the extra cross term of the first nor the central diagonal of the second.

Summary

The block is isolated among the twelve structures of the biquaternion complex space by the law of being $K$-Hermitian: the product is conjugate-symmetric, $\tilde P\star\tilde Q=\overline{\tilde Q\star\tilde P}$, and its scalar part is the Krein form, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\star\tilde Q)=K(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$. The block satisfies both clauses; the eleven other structures each fail at least one, the failures being read at the pairs $(ie_1,e_1)$ and $(e_1,e_2)$ and at the elements $e_1$ and $ie_1$. The criterion is sharp: deleting the conjugate-symmetry admits the general quaternionic sesquilinear product $\mathrm{GQS}$, and deleting the scalar-part identity admits the symmetric plain sesquilinear part $\mathrm{SPS}$; the block is the meeting point of the two, with the scalar part of the first and the symmetry of the second. The law delivers the diagonal formula with the Krein form in its scalar part, the $K$-adjointness of the left multiplications, $L^{\star}_{\tilde A}{}^{\dagger}=L^{\star}_{\tilde A^{\natural}}$, and the element theory in which the class of an element is decided by the scalar coordinate and the central square.

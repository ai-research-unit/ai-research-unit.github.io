Annihilators of the General Plain Algebra

Introduction

The zero divisor of a product is a property of one element alone: it says that some nonzero partner exists that the element kills. The annihilator says which partners those are, and it is the finer object. It is the set of elements killed by a fixed element, $$ A_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde q\star\tilde x=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde x\star\tilde q=0\}, $$ the left and the right annihilator (Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, §The Zero Divisor and the Annihilator). When the product is carried by a multiplication operator the annihilator is the kernel of that operator, so it is a linear subspace and its computation is one of linear algebra.

The block of this article is the general plain multiplication, the product $\tilde p\tilde q$ of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$ (Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions). It is associative, it carries the two-sided unit $e_0$, and over $\mathbb{R}$ it is a division algebra: every nonzero element is invertible, by the norm criterion $N(\tilde q)\neq0$ (Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra). The block therefore has no zero divisors and no Zero Divisors article, because there is no set to characterise. What is left of the element theory is the vanishing of the annihilator, and the article is short for that reason. It owns the annihilator of the block, the two multiplication operators, the rank table and the reading on the remarkable subspaces.

The chapter carries the structure-level article Annihilators in the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space (articles_maths/annihilators-in-the-algebraic-structures-over-the-quaternion-r-space.md), whose annihilating set asks the other question: which elements kill the whole space for a fixed product, the radical of that product, where this article asks which elements a fixed element kills. The two notions are transposed, not equal.

The Annihilator of a Product

Definition (annihilator). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$ and let $\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}$. The left annihilator and the right annihilator of $\tilde q$ in $\star$ are $$ A_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde q\star\tilde x=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde x\star\tilde q=0\}. $$ They are the kernels of the left and the right multiplication, and they are nonzero exactly when $\tilde q$ is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Throughout this article the product is the general plain multiplication $\star$, and unqualified, annihilator means the annihilator of $\star$. The two one-sided multiplications are the operators $$ L_{\tilde p}\tilde x=\tilde p\tilde x,\qquad R_{\tilde p}\tilde x=\tilde x\tilde p, $$ the left and the right multiplication of the algebra (One-Sided Operators on the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions), and $$ A_\star(\tilde p)=\ker L_{\tilde p},\qquad B_\star(\tilde p)=\ker R_{\tilde p}. $$

The Annihilator Is Zero

Theorem (the annihilator of a nonzero element is zero). Let $\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}$.

  • If $\tilde q\neq0$ then $A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)=\{0\}$.
  • If $\tilde q=0$ then $A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)=\mathbb{H}$.

Proof. If $\tilde q\tilde x=0$ with $\tilde q\neq0$, multiply on the left by the inverse $\tilde q^{-1}$, which exists by the division property; then $\tilde x=0$. The right side is the same computation with the inverse on the right. At $\tilde q=0$ both products vanish for every $\tilde x$, so both annihilators are the whole space.

Corollary (the two sides coincide). $A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)$ for every $\tilde q$: off the origin both are $\{0\}$, and at the origin both are $\mathbb{H}$. No nonzero element is a zero divisor of either side, and the annihilator is never a proper nonzero subspace.

The Multiplication Operator and the Rank

Proposition (the operators and their invariants). In the basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$ the operators $L_{\tilde p}$ and $R_{\tilde p}$ of an element $\tilde p=p_0e_0+\vec p$ have trace $4p_0$ each and determinant $$ \det L_{\tilde p}=\det R_{\tilde p}=N(\tilde p)^2=(p_0^2+|\vec p|^2)^2, $$ and the characteristic polynomial $$ \chi_{L_{\tilde p}}(t)=\chi_{R_{\tilde p}}(t)=(t^2-2p_0t+N(\tilde p))^2. $$

Proof. The matrix of $L_{\tilde p}$ is the left multiplication matrix of the quaternion $\tilde p$: its trace is $4p_0$ and its determinant is the square of the norm, $N(\tilde p)^2$. The matrix of $R_{\tilde p}$ is the transpose of that of $L_{\tilde p}$ for the plain form, $R_{\tilde p}=L_{\tilde p}^{\sim}$ in the notation $F^{\sim}=DF^{\mathsf T}D$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ (Association and the Transpose on the General Plain Algebra, and One-Sided Operators on the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions, $L_{\tilde a}^{\sim}=R_{\tilde a}$); a transpose has the same trace and the same characteristic polynomial, hence the same determinant. The quadratic factor $t^2-2p_0t+N(\tilde p)$ has discriminant $-4|\vec p|^2$: it is $(t-p_0)^2$ at $\vec p=0$, and it has no real root when $\vec p\neq0$. The invariants are computed exactly on $e_0$, $(2,-3,5,7)$, $(0,1,2,3)$ and $0$ in the verification of this article.

Corollary (the rank and the annihilator). The rank of the two operators and the two annihilators of $\tilde q$ are

$\tilde q$ $N(\tilde q)$ $\operatorname{rank}L_{\tilde q},\operatorname{rank}R_{\tilde q}$ $\dim A_\star(\tilde q),\dim B_\star(\tilde q)$
$0$ $0$ $0$ $4$
$\tilde q\neq0$ $\neq0$ $4$ $0$

Proof. The determinant $N(\tilde q)^2$ is nonzero exactly when $\tilde q\neq0$, by the positive definiteness of the norm; then both operators are invertible and both annihilators are $\{0\}$. The origin gives the zero operator, of kernel the whole space. All dimensions are real dimensions, the space being the real vector space $\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{R}^4$.

The Dichotomy

The dichotomy of the block is the annihilator's, and it is as short as a dichotomy can be: off the origin the annihilator is $\{0\}$, at the origin it is the whole space. There is no intermediate case, and there is no locus on which the rank drops. The zero-divisor cone of the complexified block is the norm equation $N=0$; over $\mathbb{R}$ the norm is positive definite, so that cone meets the real space only at the origin. The dichotomy is therefore exactly the split of the space into the origin and its complement, and it carries no more than the division property itself. Where the complexified block has a genuine hypersurface of zero divisors and reads a pair of annihilator planes on it (Annihilators of the General Plain Algebra, the biquaternion block), the real block has a single point and nothing on it but the whole space.

The Annihilator on the Remarkable Subspaces

The centre. A nonzero central element $\tilde p=\lambda e_0$ with $\lambda\neq0$ has $L_{\tilde p}\tilde x=\lambda\tilde x$, the scalar operator, so it is invertible and its annihilator is $\{0\}$. The centre is the scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_0$ (Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions), and a nonzero central element is invertible as a nonzero scalar is.

The vector subspace. A nonzero pure element $\tilde p=\vec p$ has norm $|\vec p|^2>0$, so it is invertible and its annihilator is $\{0\}$. The vector subspace is as free of annihilators as the whole space.

The general reading. On a remarkable subspace the annihilator of an element of the subspace is the intersection of the ambient annihilator with the subspace. The ambient annihilator is $\{0\}$ at every nonzero element, so the annihilator of a nonzero element of the centre or of the vector subspace is again $\{0\}$; the only nonzero annihilator anywhere is the whole space, at the single origin.

Summary

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners the element kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left and the right multiplication. The general plain multiplication is the associative product of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$, which over $\mathbb{R}$ is a division algebra. Its operators $L_{\tilde p}$ and $R_{\tilde p}$ have trace $4p_0$ and determinant $N(\tilde p)^2=(p_0^2+|\vec p|^2)^2$, and characteristic polynomial $(t^2-2p_0t+N(\tilde p))^2$. The annihilator of every nonzero element is $\{0\}$ on both sides, and the two sides coincide trivially; the annihilator of the origin is the whole space. The dichotomy is therefore the split of the space into the origin and its complement, with no intermediate case and no rank drop, because the zero-divisor cone of the complexified block meets the real space only at the origin. On the remarkable subspaces, the centre $\mathbb{R}e_0$ and the vector subspace, the annihilator of a nonzero element is again $\{0\}$, a nonzero central element being an invertible scalar. The block has no Zero Divisors article, and this one is short, because there is nothing for it to characterise.

Summary of Notation

Symbol Meaning
$\tilde p\tilde q$ the general plain multiplication, the product of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$
$A_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde q\tilde x=0\}$ the left annihilator of $\tilde q$, the kernel of the left multiplication
$B_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde x\tilde q=0\}$ the right annihilator of $\tilde q$, the kernel of the right multiplication
$L_{\tilde p},R_{\tilde p}$ the left and the right multiplication operators, of determinant $N(\tilde p)^2$ and trace $4p_0$
$\chi_{L_{\tilde p}}(t)=(t^2-2p_0t+N(\tilde p))^2$ the characteristic polynomial of the operator
$N(\tilde p)=p_0^2+|\vec p|^2$ the quaternion norm, positive definite over $\mathbb{R}$
$A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)$ the two sides coincide, being $\{0\}$ off the origin and $\mathbb{H}$ at the origin

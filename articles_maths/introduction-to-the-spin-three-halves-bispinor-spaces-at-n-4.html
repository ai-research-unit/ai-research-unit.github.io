Introduction to the Spin-$\tfrac32$ Bispinor Spaces at $n=4$

Introduction

The subject. A spinor is a vector of the module $\mathbb{C}^{4}=V_{3/2}$, and a bispinor is a pair of spinors, one in the module and one in its dual. The article introduces the bispinor spaces, shows that the space of bispinors is the algebra, and reads the carrier decomposition as the Clebsch–Gordan rule applied to the pair.

The order of the article. The first section defines the spinors and the dual spinors. The second defines the bispinors and proves that they fill the algebra. The third reads the decomposition. The fourth treats the two subspaces obtained by fixing one factor, which are the ideals. The fifth draws the boundary.

It assumes The Minimal Left Ideals of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ and the Module $V_{3/2}$ and The Clebsch–Gordan Decomposition of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$. The row at order two is Introduction to the Spin-$\tfrac12$ Bispinor Spaces.

The result in one line. The bispinor space is $V_{3/2}\otimes V_{3/2}^{*}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C})$, of dimension sixteen, and it decomposes as $V_3\oplus V_2\oplus V_1\oplus V_0$.

Boundaries. The article introduces the spaces and the identification; it does not treat the Fierz identities between them, which are the subject of a later article.

Spinors and Dual Spinors

Definition. A spinor is a vector of $V_{3/2}=\mathbb{C}^{4}$; a dual spinor is a vector of the dual space $V_{3/2}^{*}$.

Proposition. The dual module is isomorphic to the module, $V_{3/2}^{*}\cong V_{3/2}$, because the label is half-integral and the module is self-dual with an alternating form.

Proof. The Frobenius–Schur indicator of $V_{3/2}$ is $-1$, so the module carries an invariant nondegenerate alternating form $\omega$; the map $v\mapsto\omega(v,\cdot)$ is an isomorphism of modules $V\to V^{*}$.

Corollary. A dual spinor can be written as a spinor, and the pairing is alternating.

Proof. The isomorphism and the alternation of $\omega$.

Corollary. There is no invariant symmetric form on the module, and therefore no invariant notion of length of a spinor.

Proof. The space of invariant bilinear forms on an irreducible module is one-dimensional, and it is spanned by the alternating form.

The reading. The self-duality is the reason a bispinor can be written as a pair of spinors rather than as a spinor and a separate dual object.

Bispinors

Definition. A bispinor is an element of $V_{3/2}\otimes V_{3/2}^{*}$.

Proposition. The space of bispinors is isomorphic to $M_4(\mathbb{C})$, of complex dimension sixteen, and the isomorphism is $Ξ_{\mathrm{wfn}}\otimes\bar\chi\mapstoΞ_{\mathrm{wfn}}\bar\chi^{\dagger}$ up to the identification of the dual with the conjugate.

Proof. $\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{4})\cong\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{4})^{*}$ by the map $v\otimes f\mapsto vf$, which is an isomorphism of vector spaces and of modules for the conjugation action.

Corollary. The bispinor space is the algebra, so every statement about the bispinors is a statement about the algebra.

Proof. The isomorphism.

Corollary. A bispinor has a rank, a determinant, a spectrum and a trace, all read from the corresponding matrix.

Proof. The isomorphism carries the matrix invariants to the bispinor space.

The reading. The word "bispinor" names the algebra when it is thought of as a pair of spinors; the two names describe the same sixteen dimensions.

The Decomposition

Proposition. The bispinor space decomposes as $V_3\oplus V_2\oplus V_1\oplus V_0$, of dimensions $7+5+3+1=16$.

Proof. The tensor square of the module with its dual decomposes by the Clebsch–Gordan rule for $j=k=\tfrac32$ into the labels $0,1,2,3$.

Corollary. The bispinors of label three are symmetric in the two spinors, and the bispinors of label zero are the scalars.

Proof. The tensor square splits into the symmetric square $V_3\oplus V_1$ and the alternating square $V_2\oplus V_0$, and the top label is symmetric.

Corollary. The rank-one bispinors $Ξ_{\mathrm{wfn}}\bar\chi^{\dagger}$ are the pure ones, and they span the whole space.

Proof. Rank-one matrices span the algebra.

The reading. The decomposition is the statement that a pair of spin-$\tfrac32$ objects contains spins three, two, one and zero, each once.

The Ideals

Proposition. Fixing the second spinor to a nonzero dual spinor $\bar\chi_0$ gives the subspace $\{Ξ_{\mathrm{wfn}}\bar\chi_0^{\dagger}\}$, of dimension four, which is a minimal left ideal; fixing the first gives a minimal right ideal.

Proof. The map $Ξ_{\mathrm{wfn}}\mapstoΞ_{\mathrm{wfn}}\bar\chi_0^{\dagger}$ is injective and its image is the left ideal generated by the rank-one idempotent $\bar\chi_0^{\dagger}Ξ_{\mathrm{wfn},0}$ for a spinor $Ξ_{\mathrm{wfn},0}$ paired with $\bar\chi_0$ to one.

Corollary. The minimal left ideals are the fibres of the projection of the bispinor space onto the second factor.

Proof. The proposition.

Corollary. The ideals carry the label $\tfrac32$ under left multiplication and integer weights under conjugation, as the same subspace seen through two actions.

Proof. The ideal is a copy of the module for the left action, and a subspace with integer weights for the conjugation action.

The reading. The bispinor space contains the spinors in two ways, as the first and as the second factor; the ideals are the two families of copies.

The Boundary

Against the module article. That article constructs the module as a minimal left ideal; this one constructs the space of pairs of module vectors.

Against the Clebsch–Gordan article. That article proves the decomposition; this one reads it as the content of the bispinor pair.

Against the Fierz article. The identities among the sixteen bispinors are the subject of The Fierz–Kofink Identities at $n=4$.

A Final Observation

The algebra structure. The bispinor space is not only a module but an algebra: the product of two bispinors is the product of the corresponding matrices, the unit is the identity matrix, and the Fierz identity expresses the product of two rank-one bispinors through the pairing.

The two actions. The left and right multiplications give two commuting actions of the algebra on itself, and the bispinor description is the simultaneous reading of the two; the carriers are the pieces on which the conjugation action is irreducible.

The Dimension Bookkeeping

The tensor square. The bispinor space is the tensor product of the module with its dual conjugate, of complex dimension $4\times4=16$, and the four carriers are its isotypic components, of dimensions $7,5,3,1$.

The dense part. The rank-one bispinors form a dense open subset of the bispinor space up to the zero element, since every matrix of rank at most one is a bispinor and the matrices of higher rank are sums of two or more.

The pairing. The pairing of a spinor with its conjugate is the trace of the associated bispinor, and the Fierz identity expresses the square of a rank-one bispinor through that trace; the identity is the algebraic form of the fact that a rank-one matrix is determined by its image and its kernel.

The reading. The bispinor description turns the sixteen dimensions of the algebra into the product of two four-dimensional spaces, and the carriers are the pieces of that product sorted by the conjugation action.

The Check of the Carriers

The four pieces. The bispinor space splits into the four carriers, of dimensions $7,5,3,1$, and each piece is the image of the corresponding projector; the sum of the dimensions is sixteen, and the sum of the ranks of the projectors is the same number.

The traces. The trace of each projector is its dimension, so the traces are $7,5,3,1$; the trace of the identity is sixteen, and the traces of the four projectors add to it, which is the completeness of the decomposition.

The pairing of the pieces. The trace form pairs the septet with itself, the quintet with itself, the triplet with itself and the scalars with themselves, and it pairs no two different carriers; the decomposition is therefore orthogonal for the form as well as for the action.

Summary

A spinor is a vector of $V_{3/2}$, of dimension four, and a dual spinor is a vector of the dual, which is isomorphic to the module because the label is half-integral. A bispinor is a pair, and the space of bispinors is the algebra, of dimension sixteen.

The bispinor space decomposes as $V_3\oplus V_2\oplus V_1\oplus V_0$, of dimensions $7+5+3+1$, and the rank-one bispinors are the pure ones.

Fixing one factor of the pair gives the minimal left or right ideals, of dimension four: the ideals are the fibres of the bispinor description, and they carry the label $\tfrac32$ for the left action.

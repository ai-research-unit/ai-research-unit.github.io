Norm and Invertibility in the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra

Introduction

This article studies the algebraic norm and the invertibility of the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra

$$ \tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=\operatorname{Vect}\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}\bigr) =\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}-\overline{\tilde Q\tilde P^{*}}\bigr) =-P_0\overline{\mathbf{Q}}+\overline{Q_0}\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{P}\times\overline{\mathbf{Q}}, $$

the antisymmetric half of the general plain sesquilinear product, the operation $\mathrm{APS}$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions. The operation is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear in the first slot, conjugate-linear in the second, vector valued, and it has no unit on either side.

A norm is relative to a product, and the algebraic norm of a product is the diagonal of its scalar-part form, the definition of The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space. For the block the scalar part is identically zero, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q)=0$, because every value is a pure vector; the algebraic norm of the operation is therefore the zero function, and by the convention of the same article a vanishing quadratic form is not an algebraic norm, so the block is counted with no algebraic norm rather than with a zero one.

Both notions of the title are therefore absences. The block has no two-sided unit, so no element is invertible and the group of units is empty; the one-sided inverse equations to the centre have no solution. What replaces the invertibility is not a norm but an operator reading: the one-sided multiplication by an element has a kernel and an image, and the relative inverse below accounts for the loss on the quotient.

Boundaries. The operation, its class and the absence of a unit are Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra of Biquaternions; the general definitions of the unit and the inverse are Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Unit and the Inverse, and the unit table of the four general products is Comparison Between the Four General Products; the element theory that uses the substitute is Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra, and the structural reading of the diagonal is The Vector Part of the Square and the Jacobi Failure of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra. The algebraic norm of the general plain sesquilinear product is $H$, a different form, and its theory is Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Sesqualgebra. The centre is $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_0$ and the vector subspace is $\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$; the projection $\operatorname{Vect}$ removes the scalar part, and it is $\mathbb{C}$-linear and surjective with kernel $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$.

Conventions. $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ has basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, $e_1e_2=e_3$, central scalar imaginary $i$ and $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$; an element is $\tilde Q=Q_0e_0+\mathbf{Q}$ with $\mathbf{Q}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q_ke_k$. The natural conjugation is $\tilde Q^{\natural}=Q_0-\mathbf{Q}$, the coefficientwise conjugation is $\bar{\cdot}$, and the Hermitian conjugation is ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The general plain sesquilinear product is $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$, and the product of the block is $\diamond$.

The Relative Definitions

The word unit and the word inverse name a product, so the definitions are stated once for a general product and read on the block; they are Definitions for the Study of the 12 Algebraic Structures, §The Unit and the Inverse.

Definition (unit). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{B}$. An element $e$ is a left unit of $\star$ when $e\star\tilde X=\tilde X$ for every $\tilde X$, a right unit when $\tilde X\star e=\tilde X$ for every $\tilde X$, and a two-sided unit when it is both. A product with a two-sided unit is unital.

Definition (inverse and group of units). Let $\star$ be a unital product with identity $e$. An element $\tilde Q$ is left-invertible of $\star$ when there is $\tilde R$ with $\tilde R\star\tilde Q=e$, right-invertible when there is $\tilde R$ with $\tilde Q\star\tilde R=e$, and invertible when there is $\tilde R$ with $\tilde Q\star\tilde R=\tilde R\star\tilde Q=e$. Such an $\tilde R$ is the inverse of $\tilde Q$, and in an associative unital product the invertible elements form the group of units.

Throughout this article the product is the block $\diamond$, and, unqualified, unit and inverse are read in $\diamond$.

The Absence of a Unit

Theorem (no unit). The block has neither a left unit nor a right unit. There is no element $\tilde u$ with $\tilde u\diamond\tilde X=\tilde X$ for every $\tilde X$, and none with $\tilde X\diamond\tilde u=\tilde X$ for every $\tilde X$.

Proof. The two one-sided actions of $e_0$ are

$$ \tilde P\diamond e_0=\mathbf{P},\qquad e_0\diamond\tilde Q=-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}, $$

the first being $\operatorname{Vect}(\tilde P)$ and the second $\operatorname{Vect}(\tilde Q^{*})$. A left unit $\tilde u$ would satisfy, at $\tilde X=e_0$, the equation $\tilde u\diamond e_0=e_0$, that is $\mathbf{u}=e_0$; the left member is a vector part, of scalar part zero, while the right member has scalar part one, so the equation is impossible. A right unit $\tilde u$ would satisfy, at $\tilde X=e_0$, the equation $e_0\diamond\tilde u=e_0$, that is $-\overline{\mathbf{u}}=e_0$; the same parity of the scalar part forbids it.

The obstruction is the projection: every value of the block is a pure vector, so its scalar part vanishes, while the unit $e_0$ has scalar part one. The block cannot see the unit of the space on either side, and the two displays above are the two one-sided actions that replace it. The parent product $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ has the right unit $e_0$, and the block destroys it: the right-unit equation survives on the vector subspace alone and fails at every element with a non-zero scalar part.

The Absence of an Inverse

Corollary (no inverse, no group of units). No element of $\mathbb{B}$ is invertible of the block, and the group of units of $\diamond$ is empty.

Proof. The inverse is defined relative to a two-sided unit, and the two-sided unit does not exist; the definition has no instance in the block, and the symbol $\tilde Q^{-1}$ read with the product of the block names nothing. The group of units is the empty set.

Proposition (the one-sided equations have no solution). For every $\tilde Q$ the two equations

$$ \tilde Q\diamond\tilde R=e_0,\qquad \tilde R\diamond\tilde Q=e_0 $$

have no solution $\tilde R$.

Proof. Each left member is a value of the block, hence a pure vector of scalar part zero; the right member $e_0$ has scalar part one. A pure vector is never $e_0$, so neither equation holds.

The Algebraic Norm of the Block

Definition (the algebraic norm of the operation). Let $f$ be an operation on $\mathbb{B}$ with scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde P,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\,f(\tilde P,\tilde Q)$. The algebraic norm of $f$ is the diagonal of that form,

$$ \varphi(\tilde Q)=\varphi(\tilde Q,\tilde Q)=\mathrm{Sc}\,f(\tilde Q,\tilde Q), $$

and for an operation whose scalar part vanishes identically the algebraic norm is the zero function (The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space).

Theorem (the algebraic norm of the block is the zero function). For the antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra,

$$ \mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q\bigr)=0\quad\text{for all }\tilde P,\tilde Q, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \varphi_{\diamond}(\tilde Q)=0\quad\text{for all }\tilde Q . $$

The algebraic norm of the operation is identically zero, and by the convention of the definition the block carries no algebraic norm.

Proof. Every value of the block is a pure vector, $\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=\operatorname{Vect}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})\in\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$, of scalar part zero for every pair; the diagonal is therefore the zero function. A vanishing quadratic form is not an algebraic norm, and its vanishing set is the whole space, so the block is counted with no algebraic norm rather than with a zero one.

Remark (the diagonal of the block). The sesquilinear product is not alternating in the bilinear sense — its second slot is conjugate-linear — so the diagonal need not vanish as an element of the block:

$$ \tilde Q\diamond\tilde Q=\operatorname{Vect}\bigl(\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}\bigr)\in\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B}), $$

the vector part of the Hermitian square $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$; it is central exactly when it is zero, that is exactly when $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$ is central. The witness $\tilde Q=e_1+ie_2$ gives $\tilde Q\diamond\tilde Q=2ie_3\neq0$. The diagonal is a value and not a norm, and it is not central in general, so no central-valued quadratic map is built from the product.

The Relative Inverse

The block has no inverse, but the one-sided multiplication by an element is an operator with a kernel and an image, and its invertibility on a quotient is the substitute the article records.

Proposition (the kernel of the multiplication). For the block, the one-sided multiplication by a non-central element $\tilde A$,

$$ L_{\tilde A}\tilde R=\tilde A\diamond\tilde R, $$

has the complex plane $\mathbb{C}e_0\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\mathbf{A}}$ of complex dimension two as its kernel, and it induces a conjugate-linear bijection of the quotient of $\mathbb{B}$ by that kernel onto the plane of the values orthogonal to $\mathbf{A}$ in the complex bilinear dot.

Proof. The kernel is the annihilator computed in Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Sesqualgebra, of complex dimension two for a non-central element, and the block is conjugate-linear in its second slot, so the induced map on the quotient is conjugate-linear; the image is the plane $\{\tilde W:\mathbf{W}\in\operatorname{Vect}(\mathbb{B}),\ (\mathbf{W},\mathbf{A})=0\}$, and the dimension count gives the bijection.

So the equation $\tilde A\diamond\tilde R=\tilde W$ is solvable exactly for the values on that plane, and the set of its solutions is a coset of the kernel. The block has no inverse, and it has a relative inverse in which each non-central element acts invertibly between two spaces of complex dimension two, the loss and the gain being accounted by the kernel and the image.

The Invertibility Criterion Is Not Transferred

Remark (the criterion of the parent is not read here). The parent general plain sesquilinear product $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ has the right unit $e_0$, and its one-sided inverse equations are decided by a quadratic form that is the algebraic norm $H$ of a different product, the diagonal of $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})$ (Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Sesqualgebra). That form is not the algebraic norm of the block and is not read here. The block loses the parent's one-sided equations at once, because the value has been moved into a subspace that meets the centre only at the origin, and the place of the criterion is taken by the relative inverse above.

Summary

The antisymmetric plain sesqualgebra $\tilde P\diamond\tilde Q=\operatorname{Vect}(\tilde P\tilde Q^{*})$ has no unit on either side, hence no invertible element and an empty group of units, and the one-sided inverse equations $\tilde Q\diamond\tilde R=e_0$ and $\tilde R\diamond\tilde Q=e_0$ have no solution because every value of the block is a pure vector. The algebraic norm of the operation is the diagonal of its scalar-part form; the scalar part vanishes identically, the diagonal is the zero function, and by the convention of The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space a vanishing quadratic form is not an algebraic norm, so the block carries no algebraic norm. The diagonal of the block is the vector part of the Hermitian square, a value and not a norm, central only where it vanishes. What replaces the invertibility is not a norm but the operator reading: the multiplication by a non-central element has a two-dimensional kernel and induces a conjugate-linear bijection of the quotient onto the plane of the solvable values, the relative inverse. The algebraic norm $H$ of the parent sesquilinear product is a different form of a different product and is not read here.

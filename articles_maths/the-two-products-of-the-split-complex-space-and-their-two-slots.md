# __The Two Products of the Split-Complex Space and Their Two Slots__

## Introduction

The four general products are four fillings of two slots, and this article is the same construction read on the split-complex space. The first slot carries the identity here, and the second slot has two values, the identity and the bar, so that the four settings collapse to the **two products** $AC$ and $A\bar C$ of *The Two Products of the Split-Complex Space*; the two structures they define are the commutative **ring** and the **sesqualgebra** over $(\mathbb{D},\bar\cdot)$, and the synthesis reads the classes of element and the two pairings in the two slots. Unlike the complex field, the split-complex ring is the first rung at which the two slots differ in substance: the idempotents and the zero divisors appear in the algebra slot and give the synthesis its content, the sesquilinear square becomes a hyperbolic level set rather than a circle, and the two slots carry the two different classes $0$ and the null cone as their square-zero sets.

The article is a synthesis and proves nothing twice. The products and the collapse of the twelve structures are *The Two Products of the Split-Complex Space* and *The Collapse of the Twelve Structures on the Split-Complex Numbers*; the ring is *The Specificities of the Split-Complex Algebra*; the classes of element are *The Classes of Element of the Split-Complex Algebra*; the zero divisors and the idempotents are *Split-Complex Zero Divisors* and *Split-Complex Idempotents and Projections*; the sesquilinear product and its operators are *The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complex Space*; the two forms and their diagonals are *The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complex Space*; and the positivity is *The Hermitian Form and the Cone of the Split-Complex Algebra*. The only computations the article adds are the two adjoints of the ordinary and the sesquilinear right multiplication in §*The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints*, and which are worked here on the split-complex space.

**Interpretation and notation.** The element is $A=a+ja'$; the basis involution is the bar $\bar A=a-ja'$; ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}$. The general product is $x\star_{K}y=xK(y)$ with $K\in\{\mathrm{id},{}^{*}\}$ the switch of the second slot, the first slot carrying the conjugation of the coefficient ring, which is the identity here. The ordinary left multiplication is written $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}(X)=AX$; the sesquilinear left and right multiplications are $L_{A}(X)=A\bar X$ and $R_{A}(X)=X\bar A$ of *The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complex Space*; the idempotents are $\Pi_{1,2}=\tfrac12(1\pm j)$ and the idempotent coordinates $A_\pm=a\pm a'$. The form for the transpose is the general plain bilinear form $B(A,C)=AC$ and the form for the adjoint is the general plain sesquilinear form $H(A,C)=A\bar C$ of *The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complex Space*; the transpose for $B$ is written ${}^{\mathrm T}$ and the adjoint for $H$ is written ${}^{\dagger}$.

## The Two Slots

**Definition.** The **general product of two slots** on an algebra with an involution is

$$
x\star_{K}y=x\,K(y),
$$

where $K$ is the identity or the Hermitian conjugation, the first slot carrying the conjugation of the coefficient ring, which is the identity here.

**Theorem (the settings on $\mathbb{D}$).** On the split-complex space the first slot carries the identity, so only the second slot switches and the two settings are:

$$
\star_{\mathrm{id}}=AC, \qquad \star_{\bar{\cdot}}=A\bar C .
$$

The two surviving products are the multiplication of the commutative ring and the sesquilinear product of the space.

**Proof.** The conjugation of the coefficient ring is the identity because the coefficients of $\mathbb{D}$ are real; the first slot therefore acts trivially, and the second alone switches, giving the two products of the display. The collapse of the twelve operations to these two, and the vanishing of the four antisymmetrised products, are *The Collapse of the Twelve Structures on the Split-Complex Numbers*.

**Theorem (the rule of the second slot).** The second slot decides the composition and the form. With $K=\mathrm{id}$ the product is associative, commutative and two-sidedly unital, and the form it carries is symmetric: this is the **ring** with the bilinear form $B$. With $K=\bar{\cdot}$ the product is non-associative and conjugate-commutative with a right unit alone, and the form it carries is conjugate-symmetric: this is the **sesqualgebra** with the Hermitian form $H$.

**Proof.** The properties of $AC$ are those of the commutative associative algebra of *Split-Complex Algebra*; the properties of $A\bar C$ — the right unit alone, the associator $A\bar C(\bar D-D)$, the conjugate commutativity — are those of *The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complex Space*. The two forms are *The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complex Space*.

**Remark (the empty first slot).** On $\mathbb{D}$ the two first-slot variants merge, because the conjugation of the coefficient ring is the identity, and the symmetric and the plain families merge, because the ring is commutative. What survives of the first slot is only its role as the **centre**: the real scalars are the centre of the algebra, and the first-slot collapse is the statement that the centre is fixed by the conjugation of the coefficient ring.

## The Two Structures

**The ring.** The setting $K=\mathrm{id}$ gives the commutative ring: associative, commutative, unital, reduced (its only nilpotent is $0$) and not a field, with the null cone of zero divisors and the two nontrivial idempotents. Its form is the symmetric $B(A,C)=AC$, whose diagonal is the norm $B(A)=A^2$. The structure is *The Specificities of the Split-Complex Algebra*.

**The sesqualgebra.** The setting $K=\bar{\cdot}$ gives the sesquilinear structure over $(\mathbb{D},\bar{\cdot})$: $\mathbb{D}$-linear in the first slot and $\bar\cdot$-semilinear in the second, non-associative, conjugate-commutative, with the right unit $1$ and no left unit, whose associator is $[A,C,D]=A\bar C(\bar D-D)$ and whose ternary product $A\bar C D$ satisfies the Jordan triple identity. Its form is the conjugate-symmetric $H(A,C)=A\bar C$, whose diagonal is $H(A)=A\bar A=N(A)$. The structure is *The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complex Space*.

**Remark (the two structures are the two slots of one ladder).** The ring is the structure that carries the associative-unital theory; the sesqualgebra is the structure that carries the positivity theory. On $\mathbb{D}$ the two are not nested and are sharply different, since the ring is associative and the sesqualgebra is not, and the second slot alone is responsible: the involution in the right argument destroys associativity, the two-sided unit and the symmetry of the form, and it leaves intact only the commutativity up to the conjugation, the right unit and the conjugate symmetry.

## The Classes of Element in the Two Slots

The classes of element are where the synthesis has its content on $\mathbb{D}$, since the two slots separate what the complex field cannot separate at all.

**Zero divisors and annihilators.** In the ring slot the zero divisors are the null cone $\mathbb{R}^\times\Pi_1\cup\mathbb{R}^\times\Pi_2$ and the annihilator of a nonzero multiple of $\Pi_1$ is the opposite line $\mathbb{R}\Pi_2$, so the two families annihilate one another. In the sesquilinear slot the annihilating relation $A\star C=A\bar C=0$ holds exactly when the ring product $A\bar C$ vanishes, so the elements $A$ for which some nonzero $C$ has $A\star C=0$ are again the null cone with the origin; and the sesquilinear product is faithful on the left, since $A\star 1=A$, so no element annihilates the whole space. The ring facts are *Split-Complex Zero Divisors*.

**Idempotents.** In the ring slot the idempotents are the four $0,1,\Pi_1,\Pi_2$, central, orthogonal and complete, with the two nontrivial ones *Split-Complex Idempotents and Projections*. In the sesquilinear slot the equation $E\star E=E$ reads $E\bar E=E$, that is $E_{+}E_{-}=E$, so the pairs $(E_+,E_-)$ solve $E_+E_- = E_+ = E_-$ and the only solutions are $E=0$ and $E=1$. The sesqualgebra therefore has **no nontrivial idempotent**: the two idempotents of the ring are killed by the conjugation in the right slot, since $\Pi_1\star\Pi_1=\Pi_1\bar\Pi_1=\Pi_1\Pi_2=0$.

**Nilpotents and square-zero elements.** In the ring slot the only nilpotent and the only square-zero element is $0$, the algebra being reduced. In the sesquilinear slot the square of an element is $A\star A=A\bar A=N(A)$, which is the norm, and the **square-zero elements are the whole null cone**,

$$
\{A : A\star A=0\} = \{A : N(A)=0\} = \mathbb{R}\Pi_1\cup\mathbb{R}\Pi_2 ,
$$

the primitive zero divisors satisfying $\Pi_{1,2}\star\Pi_{1,2}=0$. So the two slots have square-zero sets as different as $\{0\}$ and a pair of lines.

**The square roots of a central value.** Let $W\in\mathbb{R}$ be a central value. In the ring slot the equation $A^2=W$ is the pair $A_\pm^2=W$, so it has four roots $\pm\sqrt{W},\pm j\sqrt{W}$ for $W>0$, the single root $0$ for $W=0$, and no root for $W<0$. In the sesquilinear slot the equation $A\star A=N(A)=W$ is the level set $a^2-a'^2=W$ of the norm, and since the norm is surjective it has solutions for **every** real $W$: the two branches of the hyperbola $a^2-a'^2=W$ for $W\neq0$, and the pair of null lines for $W=0$. The complex circle of the sesquilinear square is replaced by a hyperbola, and the empty answer for negative values is replaced by the other hyperbola; the ring-slot readings are *The Classes of Element of the Split-Complex Algebra*, and the sesquilinear square is the norm of *Split-Complex Norm and Invertibility*.

**Theorem (the classes in the two slots).** In the ring slot the distinguished sets are: zero divisors the null cone, idempotents the four central ones, nilpotents and square-zero elements $\{0\}$, roots of a positive central value four and of a negative value none. In the sesquilinear slot they are: annihilating elements the null cone, idempotents $\{0,1\}$, square-zero elements the null cone, and roots of every real central value, the level sets of the norm.

**Proof.** The ring statements are the classes of *The Classes of Element of the Split-Complex Algebra* and of *Split-Complex Zero Divisors*; the sesquilinear statements are the computations of the paragraphs above, using $E\star E=E\bar E$ and $A\star A=N(A)$.

**Remark (the contrast with the complex synthesis).** On the complex field the classes of the two slots agree except at the square: there the ring is a field, so there is no zero divisor, no non-trivial idempotent and no nilpotent, and the two slots differ only in the two-point versus circle answer of the square roots. On $\mathbb{D}$ the two slots differ already at the idempotents and at the square-zero elements, because the ring is not a field: the algebra slot has four idempotents and the sesquilinear slot has two, and the square-zero set is the origin in the one and a pair of lines in the other.

## The Two Pairings and the Two Adjoints

**Theorem (the two pairings).** The two slots carry the two pairings of the split-complex space,

$$
B(A,C)=AC, \qquad H(A,C)=A\bar C,
$$

the first bilinear and symmetric, $\mathbb{D}$-valued with Gram matrix $[1]$ in the basis $1$, the second sesquilinear and conjugate-symmetric. Their real scalar parts are

$$
\operatorname{Sc}B(A,C)=ac+a'c', \qquad \operatorname{Sc}H(A,C)=ac-a'c'=g(A,C),
$$

and the second, the form of the Hermitian side, is of signature $(1,1)$ in the basis $\{1,j\}$.

**Proof.** The pairings are the two products read as forms; their scalar parts are the real parts $\operatorname{Sc}(AC)=ac+a'c'$ and $\operatorname{Sc}(A\bar C)=ac-a'c'$. The scalar Gram matrices are read from the basis values $B(1,1)=B(j,j)=1$, $B(1,j)=0$, giving $\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$, and $H(1,1)=1$, $H(j,j)=-1$, $H(1,j)=0$, giving $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$ of signature $(1,1)$. The full form theory, including the realification of the two forms, is *The Two Forms and the Two Algebraic Norms of the Split-Complex Space* and *The Hermitian Form and the Cone of the Split-Complex Algebra*.

**Definition.** For a real-linear map $F$, the **transpose** $F^{\mathrm T}$ is defined by $B(FX,Y)=B(X,F^{\mathrm T}Y)$, and the **adjoint** $F^{\dagger}$ by $H(FX,Y)=H(X,F^{\dagger}Y)$ for a $\mathbb{D}$-linear $F$ and by $H(FX,Y)=\overline{H(X,F^{\dagger}Y)}$ for a $\bar\cdot$-semilinear $F$.

**Theorem (the two adjoints of the ordinary and the sesquilinear right multiplication).** For the ordinary left multiplication $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}(X)=AX$ and the sesquilinear right multiplication $R_{A}(X)=X\bar A$,

$$
\bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}, \qquad \bigl(L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A},
$$

$$
\bigl(R_{A}\bigr)^{\mathrm T}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}, \qquad \bigl(R_{A}\bigr)^{\dagger}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}.
$$

The transpose keeps the parameter and the Hermitian adjoint conjugates it; the transpose and the adjoint of the same map therefore agree exactly when $A$ is self-adjoint, that is real.

**Proof.** For the first pair: $B(AX,Y)=AXY$ and $B(X,AY)=XAY$, equal by commutativity, so $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$ is its own transpose; and $H(AX,Y)=AX\bar Y$ while $H(X,\bar A Y)=X\overline{\bar A Y}=XA\bar Y$, equal, so the Hermitian adjoint is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}$. For the second pair: $B(X\bar A,Y)=X\bar A Y$ and $B(X,\bar A Y)=X\bar A Y$, so the transpose is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}$; and $H(X\bar A,Y)=X\bar A\bar Y$ while $H(X,AY)=X\bar A\bar Y$, so the adjoint is $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$. The agreement $L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar A}=L^{\mathrm{alg}}_{A}$ is exactly $A=\bar A$, that is $A\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Remark (the conjugate-linear family).** The left multiplication of the sesqualgebra, $L_{A}(X)=A\bar X$, is $\bar\cdot$-semilinear. Its Hermitian adjoint is $L_{A}$ itself, self-adjoint, and the adjoint of the sandwich is $S_{B,C}^{\dagger}=S_{C,B}$; both computations are in *The Sesquilinear Product of the Split-Complex Space*, and the sandwich example $S_{\Pi_1,\Pi_2}^{\dagger}=S_{\Pi_2,\Pi_1}$ shows the two projections onto the idempotent lines mutually adjoint. The two adjoints above are the split-complex reading of the four adjoints of the general theory.

## The Dictionary

The complex synthesis and the split-complex synthesis are set side by side.

**Remark (why the dictionary is short and why it is longer than the complex one).** Every line of the dictionary except the last is a collapse of the first slot: the first slot is trivial, so the bilinear forms coincide, the sesquilinear forms coincide, the symmetrisations of the two slots coincide with the slots themselves, and the antisymmetrisations vanish. The two adjoints are the two pairings $B$ and $H$ and not four, because the first-slot variants coincide with the plain ones. The difference from the complex column is the two lines in which the ring is not a field: the zero divisors and the idempotents. The split-complex space is the first rung at which these show, and the complex rung is degenerate. One sign flip governs the whole column: the square of the generator is $j^2=+1$ here against $i^2=-1$ on the complex field, and it is that flip which makes the Hermitian form of the split space indefinite where the complex one is definite.

## Summary

The four general products are the fillings of two slots, and on the split-complex space the first slot is empty, so that only the second slot survives and has two values: the identity, giving the ring product $AC$, and the bar, giving the sesquilinear product $A\bar C$. The two structures are the commutative ring, associative and commutative with a two-sided unit, a null cone of zero divisors, the two nontrivial idempotents and the symmetric form $B$, and the sesqualgebra, non-associative and conjugate-commutative with a right unit alone, the associator $A\bar C(\bar D-D)$ and the conjugate-symmetric form $H$.

The classes of element separate the two slots on $\mathbb{D}$ where they coincide on $\mathbb{C}$: the ring has the four idempotents $0,1,\Pi_{1,2}$ and the square-zero set $\{0\}$, while the sesqualgebra has the idempotents $0,1$ alone, the null cone as its square-zero set, and the hyperbolas $a^2-a'^2=W$ as the roots of every real central value, the complex circle having become a hyperbola and the empty negative case having become the other hyperbola. The two pairings $B$ and $H$ carry two adjoints of a linear map, computed here: the transpose keeps the parameter and the Hermitian adjoint conjugates it, so the ordinary multiplication by $A$ has transpose itself and Hermitian adjoint the multiplication by $\bar A$, and the two agree exactly for real $A$; the sesquilinear left multiplication is self-adjoint and the sandwich is adjoint to the sandwich with the parameters exchanged. The same construction one rung up, where the first slot is alive, recovers the twelve structures; the complex synthesis is the rung below, where the ring is a field and this synthesis loses the zero divisors and the idempotents that give it its content.

The 4 Algebraic Norms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space

Introduction

The twelve products of The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space carry four forms and no more, and this article starts from them. The four forms are the four scalar parts of the four general products, and they are counted and defined in The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, read off the third column of the table of the twelve: the general plain bilinear form $\langle\cdot\rangle$, the general plain sesquilinear form $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$, also called the Hermitian form, the general quaternionic bilinear form $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, and the general quaternionic sesquilinear form $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$, also called the Krein form, each written with the one bracket of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe whose subscript records the pair $(a,b)$,

$$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle, \qquad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}, \qquad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}, \qquad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}, $$

for the four pairs $(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})$, $(\mathrm{id},{}^{*})$, $({}^{\natural},\mathrm{id})$, $({}^{\natural},{}^{*})$ read in that order. The chain of the section is the table of the twelve, then the four forms, then the four algebraic norms of this article, and the passage from the second to the third is one operation: the algebraic norm. It closes the group The Forms and Their Algebraic Norms and carries the four diagonals of the four forms.

Definition (the four algebraic norms). The algebraic norm of a form is that form read at its two equal arguments, $\tilde Q_1=\tilde Q_2$:

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle,\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*},\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural},\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}. $$

The four forms therefore give four algebraic norms, one to a form, written on the coordinates as

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^{2},\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2},\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2},\qquad \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}, $$

with $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$. One letter, two arities. A letter names the form when it carries two arguments and the algebraic norm when it carries one; the norm is the form of the same name with its two arguments equated, and the four equations above are the whole of the definition. Read with one argument, the value is complex for $\langle\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and real for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$, because a sesquilinear form has a real algebraic norm; the same equation written for a general form, $\varphi(\tilde Q_2)=\varphi(\tilde Q_2,\tilde Q_2)$, is the definition of §The Algebraic Norm of an Operation. The four names are $\langle\cdot\rangle$ the general plain bilinear algebraic norm, the algebraic norm of $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$; $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ the general plain sesquilinear algebraic norm, the algebraic norm of $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*})$, also called the Hermitian algebraic norm; $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ the general quaternionic bilinear algebraic norm, the algebraic norm of $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2)$; and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ the general quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm, the algebraic norm of $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*})$, also called the Krein algebraic norm. The symmetrisation of a family keeps the scalar part of the general product, so it keeps the form and its algebraic norm; the antisymmetrisation kills the scalar part, so it has neither. That is the whole of the count, and §From the Four Forms to the Four Algebraic Norms proves it.

The four algebraic norms are separated by three properties and no two of them share all three. The value lies in $\mathbb{C}$ for $\langle\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and in $\mathbb{R}$ for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$. The vanishing set is the complex cone $\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=0$ for $\langle\cdot\rangle$, the origin alone for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$, the zero-divisor cone $\mathcal N$ for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, and the real cone $\mathcal K$ for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$. Multiplicativity holds for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ alone and definiteness for $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ alone; no one of the four is both, and that impossibility is the zero divisor of Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra.

The four algebraic norms of this article are the four degree-two functions of The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, read at the two equal arguments of their pairings. Their names in the three-letter code defined in The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space are read there; for the record, $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is carried by the general plain bilinear product and its symmetrisation, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ by the general plain sesquilinear product and its symmetrisation, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ by the general quaternionic bilinear product and its symmetrisation, and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ by the general quaternionic sesquilinear product and its symmetrisation, while the four antisymmetrisations carry no algebraic norm.

The one topological norm of the space, $\lVert\cdot\rVert_E$, is the square root of $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and it is unique; the other three are algebraic and give no distance. The distinction is that of Introduction to Mathematics and of Hermitian Forms over Algebras and Norms: the algebraic norm takes its value in the ring, and the topological norm is the selection of the positive real values among them, which is the passage to The Norm Defined by a Form.

Notational Conventions

$\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ is the biquaternion algebra, with basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, $e_t=1$, $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$, and central scalar imaginary $i$, $i^{2}=-1$. A general element is $\tilde Q=\sum_{\mu\in\{t,x,y,z\}}Q_\mu e_\mu$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$, written in the centre–vector split $\tilde Q=c+v$ with $c=Q_te_t\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ and $v=\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q_k\vec e_k\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$. The scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}$, the coefficientwise conjugation is $\bar{\cdot}$, the natural conjugation is ${}^{\natural}$, with $Q^{\natural}_\nu=\varepsilon_\nu Q_\nu$, and the Hermitian conjugation is ${}^{*}=\bar{\cdot}\circ{}^{\natural}$. The sign vector is $\varepsilon=(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and $\mathbb{B}$ is identified with $\mathbb{C}^{4}$ by $\tilde Q\mapsto(Q_t,Q_x,Q_y,Q_z)$. The vector parts carry the complex bilinear dot product $(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2)=\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q_{1,k}Q_{2,k}$ and the cross product $\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2$. The four general forms are the four pairings of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe, written there with one bracket $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ whose subscript records the pair $(a,b)$ — the natural conjugation ${}^{\natural}$ for a non-identity first slot and the star ${}^{*}$ for a non-identity second slot. In the letters of this article,

$$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_{1,\mu} Q_{2,\mu}, \qquad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*})=\sum_\mu Q_{1,\mu}\overline{Q_{2,\mu}}, $$ $$ \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2)=\sum_\mu Q_{1,\mu} Q_{2,\mu}, \qquad \langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*})=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_{1,\mu}\overline{Q_{2,\mu}}, $$

each being the trace form of its own product up to the factor of the trace convention, $2\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)$, $2\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*})$, $2\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2)$ and $2\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*})$, for the four pairs $(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})$, $(\mathrm{id},{}^{*})$, $({}^{\natural},\mathrm{id})$, $({}^{\natural},{}^{*})$ read in that order. The four algebraic norms are $\langle\tilde Q\rangle=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle$, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}$, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}$ and $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}$: the general plain bilinear algebraic norm, the general plain sesquilinear algebraic norm, the general quaternionic bilinear algebraic norm and the general quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm. The names, the bracket and the pair of each are the convention of Conventions in the Biquaternion Universe, which reads the four on the algebra and on the material sector; $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ is also the Hermitian algebraic norm and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ the Krein algebraic norm, and the brackets $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ are the short names of this article. The Euclidean norm is $\lVert\tilde Q\rVert_E=\bigl(\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}$.

The Algebraic Norm of an Operation

Definition (algebraic norm). Let $f$ be an operation on $\mathbb{B}$ with scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)=\mathrm{Sc}\,f(\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2)$. The algebraic norm of the operation $f$ is the algebraic norm

$$ \varphi(\tilde Q)=\varphi(\tilde Q,\tilde Q), $$

a function of one element taking its value in $\mathbb{C}$ when $f$ is $\mathbb{C}$-bilinear and in $\mathbb{R}$ when $f$ is Hermitian. For an operation whose scalar part vanishes identically the algebraic norm is the zero function.

The definition is the one of Introduction to Mathematics: the algebraic norm is the quadratic form $\mathfrak{Q}(x)=\langle x,x\rangle$ read at the two equal arguments of a form, valued in the ring. It records the size of an element only in the sense of the ring; it is not a distance, and it becomes a distance only when it is selected to take positive real values.

The four algebraic norms of the biquaternion algebra are then the four functions of the introduction, $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$: the four algebraic norms of The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space.

From the Four Forms to the Four Algebraic Norms

The count of the four is taken, one step, in The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space: the symmetrisation of a family keeps the scalar part and the antisymmetrisation kills it, so the eight operations of the four $\mathrm G/\mathrm S$ rows carry the four forms two by two and the four antisymmetric operations carry the zero form. The twelve products are named in the three-letter code defined in The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space — the general, symmetric and antisymmetric operation of each family, the plain and quaternionic family lettered $\mathrm P$ and $\mathrm Q$ and the algebra and sesqualgebra lettered $\mathrm A$ and $\mathrm S$, so that the twelve are $\mathrm{GPA}$, $\mathrm{GPS}$, $\mathrm{GQA}$, $\mathrm{GQS}$ the four general, $\mathrm{SPA}$, $\mathrm{SPS}$, $\mathrm{SQA}$, $\mathrm{SQS}$ the four symmetric and $\mathrm{APA}$, $\mathrm{APS}$, $\mathrm{AQA}$, $\mathrm{AQS}$ the four antisymmetric.

Proposition (the twelve operations carry four algebraic norms). The twelve operations have exactly four distinct non-zero algebraic norms, the four algebraic norms above, each attained by two operations, and four operations have the zero algebraic norm.

Proof. The algebraic norm is the form at the two equal arguments, so the count of the four algebraic norms is the count of the four forms, which is the proposition of The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space; the symmetrisation of a family therefore carries the same algebraic norm as the general product and the antisymmetrisation the zero one. Hence the algebraic norms of the twelve are those of the four general products, each on the two operations $\mathrm G$ and $\mathrm S$ of its family: $\langle\cdot\rangle$ on $\mathrm{GPA}$ and $\mathrm{SPA}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ on $\mathrm{GPS}$ and $\mathrm{SPS}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ on $\mathrm{GQA}$ and $\mathrm{SQA}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ on $\mathrm{GQS}$ and $\mathrm{SQS}$; and $0$ on $\mathrm{APA}$, $\mathrm{APS}$, $\mathrm{AQA}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}$. The four are distinct as functions of one element: on $\vec e_x$ and on $ie_t$ their value pairs are

$$ B=(-1,-1),\qquad H=(1,1),\qquad N=(1,-1),\qquad K=(-1,1), $$

which are four different pairs.

Remark (a vanishing form carries no algebraic norm). The four antisymmetric operations have the zero algebraic norm because their scalar part is identically zero — $\vec Q_1\times\vec Q_2$ for $\mathrm{APA}$ and $\mathrm{AQA}$, and the alternating forms of the two Hermitian forms for $\mathrm{APS}$ and $\mathrm{AQS}$. A vanishing quadratic form is not an algebraic norm, and its vanishing set is the whole space, so the four operations are counted with no algebraic norm rather than with a zero one.

The Four Algebraic Norms

The General Plain Bilinear Algebraic Norm

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=Q_t^{2}-Q_x^{2}-Q_y^{2}-Q_z^{2}, $$

the general plain bilinear algebraic norm $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=Q_{1,0}Q_{2t}-(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2)$. It is complex-valued and $\mathbb{C}$-quadratic, its Gram matrix on the coefficient basis is the sign matrix $\mathrm E=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and its realification is of signature $(4,4)$. Its vanishing set is the complex cone $\langle\tilde Q\rangle=0$, already the complex quadric of The 4 Forms over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space. It is not multiplicative. The algebraic norm is that of the plain product, the multiplication of the algebra, and it is the one algebraic norm the plain product carries.

The General Plain Sesquilinear Algebraic Norm

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}+\lvert Q_x\rvert^{2}+\lvert Q_y\rvert^{2}+\lvert Q_z\rvert^{2}, $$

the general plain sesquilinear algebraic norm $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2^{*})=Q_{1,0}\overline{Q_{2t}}+(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2})$. It is real-valued, $\mathbb{R}$-quadratic and positive definite, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}>0$ for $\tilde Q\neq0$; its Gram matrix is $\mathrm I_4$ and its realification is of signature $(8,0)$. Its vanishing set is the origin alone, and it is the square of the topological norm, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}=\lVert\tilde Q\rVert_E^{2}$.

The algebraic norm is definite, and it is the one of the four whose square root is the topological norm: $\lVert\tilde Q\rVert_E$ is the unique topological norm of the real vector space $\mathbb{B}\cong\mathbb{R}^{8}$, up to equivalence, and it is the topological norm under which every one of the twelve operations is read in Topology and Metric for Each of the Twelve Operations. It is not multiplicative.

The General Quaternionic Bilinear Algebraic Norm

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}=Q_t^{2}+Q_x^{2}+Q_y^{2}+Q_z^{2}, $$

the general quaternionic bilinear algebraic norm $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2)=Q_{1,0}Q_{2t}+(\vec Q_1,\vec Q_2)$. It is complex-valued, its Gram matrix is the identity $\mathrm I_4$, and its realification is of signature $(4,4)$. Its vanishing set is the zero-divisor cone $\mathcal N=\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=0\}$, of real dimension $6$, and the algebraic norm is the reason $\mathbb{B}$ has zero divisors at all.

Proposition (multiplicativity). $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is multiplicative: $$ \langle\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\tilde Q_1\rangle_{\natural}\,\langle\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}. $$

Proof. Under the matrix representation $\mathsf{M}_2:\mathbb{B}\to M_2(\mathbb{C})$, $\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_k)=-i\sigma_k$, the algebraic norm is the determinant, $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\det\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)$, as the computation of the determinant of $\mathsf{M}_2(\tilde Q)=Q_t\mathrm I-i\sum_kQ_k\sigma_k$ shows. The map $\mathsf{M}_2$ is an algebra homomorphism and the determinant is multiplicative, so $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is.

The algebraic norm is the reduced norm of the algebra, it is the only multiplicative one of the four, and it is the biquaternion algebraic norm of Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra: an element is invertible if and only if $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\neq0$, and the unit group is the norm-one group $\{\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=1\}$, a non-compact real $6$-manifold homotopy equivalent to $S^{3}$.

The General Quaternionic Sesquilinear Algebraic Norm

$$ \langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde Q,\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}=\lvert Q_t\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_x\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_y\rvert^{2}-\lvert Q_z\rvert^{2}, $$

the general quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde Q_1^{\natural}\tilde Q_2^{*})=Q_{1,0}\overline{Q_{2t}}-(\vec Q_1,\overline{\vec Q_2})$; it is the Krein algebraic norm of the algebra, the algebraic norm of the Krein form. It is real-valued and indefinite, its Gram matrix is the sign matrix $\mathrm E$, and its realification is of signature $(2,6)$. Its vanishing set is the real cone $\mathcal K=\{\lVert c\rVert_E=\lVert v\rVert_E\}$ of the centre–vector split, and by the identity $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural*}=\lVert c\rVert_E^{2}-\lVert v\rVert_E^{2}$ it is the null cone of the indefinite metric. It is not multiplicative. The algebraic norm is the one that the Krein structure of the algebra produces, the pair of a definite and an indefinite form, and its positive and negative value-one sets are $S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}$ and $S^{5}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}$.

What the Four Algebraic Norms Separate

Proposition (no algebraic norm is both definite and multiplicative). No function on $\mathbb{B}$ is both definite and multiplicative.

Proof. The algebra has zero divisors, $(e_t+i\vec e_x)(e_t-i\vec e_x)=0$ with $e_t\pm i\vec e_x\neq0$ and $\langle e_t\pm i\vec e_x\rangle_{\natural}=1+i^{2}=0$. A multiplicative function $\varphi$ would give $\varphi(e_t+i\vec e_x)\varphi(e_t-i\vec e_x)=\varphi(0)=0$, so one of the two values is zero, on a nonzero element; a definite function is nonzero off the origin. No function is both.

Corollary. $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ is definite and not multiplicative; $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is multiplicative and not definite; and no third algebraic norm of the four repairs the split. The multiplicative one and the definite one are different objects, and that difference is the failure of the biquaternions to be a normed division algebra — the four normed division algebras $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{H}$, $\mathbb{O}$ of the Hurwitz theorem are exactly the ones in which the two senses coincide.

Proposition (only $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is multiplicative). Of the four algebraic norms, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ alone satisfies $\varphi(\tilde Q_1\tilde Q_2)=\varphi(\tilde Q_1)\varphi(\tilde Q_2)$.

Proof. $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is multiplicative by the proposition of its section. The other three are not, each refuted by one substitution. For $\langle\cdot\rangle$: $\langle \vec e_x\vec e_y\rangle=\langle \vec e_z\rangle=-1$ while $\langle \vec e_x\rangle\langle \vec e_y\rangle=(-1)(-1)=1$. For $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$: $\langle \vec e_x+i\vec e_y\rangle_{*}=1+1=2$ while $(\vec e_x+i\vec e_y)^{2}=\vec e_x^{2}+(i\vec e_y)^{2}=-e_t+e_t=0$ and $\langle 0\rangle_{*}=0$. For $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$: $\langle e_t+\vec e_x\rangle_{\natural*}=1-1=0$ while $(e_t+\vec e_x)^{2}=e_t^{2}+e_t\vec e_x+\vec e_xe_t+\vec e_x^{2}=-e_t+\vec e_x-\vec e_x-e_t=-2e_t$ and $\langle -2e_t\rangle_{\natural*}=4$.

Remark (the two senses of norm). Two different things carry the name. The algebraic norm of this article is a map into the ring — $\langle\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ into $\mathbb{C}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ into $\mathbb{R}$ — and it is algebra: algebraic norms, quadratic forms, polarisations, zero divisors. The topological norm is the one selection of positive real values, $\lVert\tilde Q\rVert_E=\bigl(\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{*}\bigr)^{1/2}$, and it is topology: distance, balls, convergence, completeness. Only $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ of the four yields it, because only $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ is real and definite; $\langle\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ are complex-valued, and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ is real but indefinite. The two senses are the two layers of Introduction to Mathematics, and no third function holds them together.

Remark (the four collapse to two on real elements). For real $\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2$ the coefficientwise conjugation is the identity, so $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural*}=\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle$ and $\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{*}=\langle\tilde Q_1,\tilde Q_2\rangle_{\natural}$. On the real part of the space the four algebraic norms are the two, $\langle\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}=\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$, and the four operations of the two bilinear rows and of the two sesquilinear rows coincide there. This is the real-form caution of The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space read at the two equal arguments: a computation restricted to real elements sees two algebraic norms and hides the other two. On the remarkable real subspaces the four algebraic norms restrict to the two real forms in the same way, and the restrictions, their signatures and the definiteness of each on each are the subject of Remarkable Subspaces and the Four Algebraic Norms.

Summary

The twelve products of The 12 Products of the Biquaternion Complex Space carry four forms and, as their algebraic norms, four functions and no more: the symmetrisation of a family keeps the scalar part and the antisymmetrisation kills it, so the eight operations of the four $\mathrm G/\mathrm S$ rows carry the four algebraic norms two by two and the four antisymmetric operations carry the vanishing algebraic norm. The four are the general plain bilinear algebraic norm $\langle\cdot\rangle=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$, the general plain sesquilinear algebraic norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}=\sum_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}$, the general quaternionic bilinear algebraic norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}=\sum_\mu Q_\mu^{2}$ and the general quaternionic sesquilinear algebraic norm $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}=\sum_\mu\varepsilon_\mu\lvert Q_\mu\rvert^{2}$.

$\langle\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ are complex-valued and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ real-valued. $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural}$ is the multiplicative one, the reduced norm and the criterion of invertibility; $\langle\cdot\rangle_{*}$ is the definite one, and its square root is the unique topological norm of the space; $\langle\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\natural*}$ are the two indefinite ones of the plain and of the Krein form. Multiplicativity and definiteness are held by no single one of the four, and the zero divisors are the reason.

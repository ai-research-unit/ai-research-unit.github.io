
# __Mackey Theory__

## Introduction

Mackey theory is the body of results, due to George Mackey, that analyses the unitary representations of a locally compact group in terms of induced representations. Its central results are the **imprimitivity theorem**, which characterises induced representations by the presence of a system of imprimitivity on a homogeneous space, the theorem comparing two induced representations $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\sigma$ and $\operatorname{Ind}_{H'}^G\sigma'$ and deciding when they are equivalent, the irreducibility criterion, and the **Mackey machine** for a group with a normal subgroup — a procedure that reduces the classification of the irreducible unitary representations of $G$ to the same problem for smaller groups. Applied to a semidirect product it produces the **little-group method**; applied to a nilpotent Lie group it produces Kirillov's orbit method, in which the irreducibles are parametrised by the coadjoint orbits.

The article develops these results in the frame of the previous article: the induced representation is $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\sigma$ in the function-space or balanced-product realisation of *Induced Representations of Locally Compact Groups*, the unitary dual is $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ of *Representation Theory of Locally Compact Groups*, and the Haar measure and the quasi-invariant measure on a homogeneous space are those of *Locally Compact Groups and Haar Measure*. The boundary with Part III is the one fixed in the representation-theoretic block: the *constructions and classification statements* — systems of imprimitivity, the imprimitivity theorem, the intertwining number theorem, the little-group method, the orbit correspondence, the obstruction cocycle — are the subject of this article, while the **harmonic analysis** — the completion of the induced spaces, the direct-integral decomposition of an induced representation, the Plancherel theorem and the measure-theoretic details of the orbit correspondence — belongs to *Analysis on Groups* in Part III, where the measure and the limit are available. The cohomological classification of the obstruction uses the group cohomology of *Group Cohomology* in Part I, and the operator-algebraic frame — the imprimitivity theorem read as a Morita equivalence of $C^*$-algebras — is that and is used only as a pointer. No physics is invoked.

## Systems of Imprimitivity

### Definition

Let $G$ act on a locally compact space $X$ by homeomorphisms. A **system of imprimitivity** for a unitary representation $\pi$ of $G$ based on $X$ consists of a $\sigma$-algebra of subsets of $X$ stable under $G$ together with a projection-valued measure

$$
P : \mathcal{B}(X) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{H}_\pi)
$$

from the measurable sets of $X$ to the orthogonal projections of $\mathcal{H}_\pi$, such that

$$
\pi(g)\, P(E)\, \pi(g)^{-1} = P(gE) \qquad \text{for all } g \in G,\ E \subseteq X \text{ measurable} .
$$

Equivalently, in the transitive case $X = G/H$, the data consist of a unitary representation $\pi$ of $G$ and a projection-valued measure $P$ on the quotient $G/H$ on which $G$ acts such that $\pi(g)P(E)\pi(g)^{-1} = P(gE)$; when the measure is a point mass at the coset $H$ one recovers the **imprimitivity condition**

$$
P(H)\,\pi(h) = \pi(h)\,P(H) \qquad \text{for all } h \in H ,
$$

with $P(H)$ the projection onto the fibre over the coset $H$, which therefore commutes with the operators $\pi(h)$, $h \in H$; the general system is generated by this one projection together with the action.

**Definition.** A representation $\pi$ of $G$ is **monomial** if it is induced from a one-dimensional representation of a closed subgroup, and **of finite type** if it is induced from a finite-dimensional representation of a closed subgroup.

### The Imprimitivity Theorem

**Theorem (imprimitivity; Mackey).** Let $G$ be a locally compact group acting transitively on a standard Borel space $X$ with isotropy subgroups conjugate to a fixed closed subgroup $H$, and let $\rho$ be a unitary representation of $H$. Then the induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho$ carries a system of imprimitivity based on $X = G/H$ by

$$
P(E)\, f = 1_E\, f , \qquad E \subseteq G/H ,
$$

the projection being multiplication by the indicator function of $E$ in the function-space realisation. Conversely, every unitary representation $\pi$ of $G$ with a system of imprimitivity based on $G/H$ is unitarily equivalent to an induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho$, where $\rho$ is the restriction of $\pi$ to the isotropy subgroup $H$ on the range of the projection $P(H)$.

**Proof sketch.** The forward construction is immediate: the indicator $1_E$ is a bounded multiplication operator on the induced space, the family $E \mapsto 1_E$ is a projection-valued measure on $G/H$, and the identity $\pi(g)1_E\pi(g)^{-1} = 1_{gE}$ is the equivariance of left translation. For the converse, let $\mathcal{H}_0 = P(H)\mathcal{H}_\pi$; the imprimitivity condition says that $\mathcal{H}_0$ is invariant under $H$, and $\rho = \pi|_H$ restricted to $\mathcal{H}_0$ is a unitary representation of $H$. The map sending $v \in \mathcal{H}_0$ to the $H$-equivariant function $g \mapsto (\pi(g)^{-1}v)$ lands in the induced space, and it intertwines the actions; it is unitary because the projection-valued measure on $G/H$ is the spectral measure of the induced functions' supports. The measure-theoretic care — the existence of a quasi-invariant measure on $G/H$, the $\sigma$-finiteness and the identification of the range of $P$ — uses the measure theory of Part III and the quasi-invariant measure of *Locally Compact Groups and Haar Measure*, §Quotients and Homogeneous Spaces. $\square$

**Corollary (the $C^*$-algebraic form).** The imprimitivity theorem is equivalent to a Morita equivalence between the crossed-product $C^*$-algebra $C_0(X)\rtimes G$ and the group algebra $C^*(H)$, and the induction functor corresponds under this equivalence to the balanced product $\otimes_{C^*(H)}$. This is the form of the theorem used in noncommutative geometry and in the classification of $C^*$-algebras, and the $C^*$-algebraic framework is that in Part II .

**Example (the transitive system of the quasiregular representation).** The quasiregular representation $\lambda_{G/H}$ of *Induced Representations of Locally Compact Groups*, §Basic Properties of Induction, is the induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G1_H$, and the system of imprimitivity based on $G/H$ is given by multiplication by indicators. It is the model system: every transitive system is obtained from a quasiregular one by multiplying by a representation of the isotropy subgroup.

## Mackey's Analysis of Induced Representations

### Equivalence of Induced Representations

Two induced representations may be equivalent even when the inducing data look different; the comparison is made by Mackey's double-coset analysis, which reduces the problem to a finite or countable sum over double cosets and then to a conjugacy question for representations of a single subgroup.

**Theorem (intertwining number theorem; Mackey).** Let $H_1, H_2$ be closed subgroups of $G$ and let $\rho_i$ be unitary representations of $H_i$. Under the standard regularity hypotheses on the double coset space $H_2\backslash G/H_1$ — which hold, in particular, when the space is countable and the orbit maps are sufficiently regular, as for a Lie group with closed subgroups — the intertwining space is given by

$$
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G\bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1}^G\rho_1,\ \operatorname{Ind}_{H_2}^G\rho_2\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{s \in H_2\backslash G/H_1} \dim\operatorname{Hom}_{H_2\cap sH_1s^{-1}}\bigl(\rho_1^s,\ \rho_2\bigr) ,
$$

with $\rho_1^s(x) = \rho_1(s^{-1}xs)$, the two representations $\rho_1^s$ and $\rho_2$ being read on the common subgroup $H_2\cap sH_1s^{-1}$. Consequently $\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1}^G\rho_1 \cong \operatorname{Ind}_{H_2}^G\rho_2$ precisely when the summands match: for a bijection $s \leftrightarrow s'$ between the double cosets carrying nonzero summands, the constituents $\operatorname{Ind}_{H_2\cap sH_1s^{-1}}^{H_2}\rho_1^s$ of the first representation and the corresponding constituents of the second are equivalent, all remaining summands of both sums being zero.

**Proof sketch.** The double coset decomposition of $G$ relative to $H_1$ and $H_2$ fibres the restriction: $\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1}^G\rho_1$ restricted to $H_2$ decomposes as the sum, over the double cosets, of the representations induced from $\rho_1^s$ on the intersections, which is the restriction formula proved below by the orbit decomposition of $G/H_1$ under $H_2$ together with induction in stages. Taking intertwiners into $\operatorname{Ind}_{H_2}^G\rho_2$ and using Frobenius reciprocity of *Induced Representations of Locally Compact Groups* twice reduces the computation to the stated sum of Hom spaces on the intersections; the identity is exact in the finite case and in the type I case, and is quoted as standard in general. The statement is recorded in the form of a comparison of families of representations of $H_2$, which is the form used in the little-group method below. $\square$

### Irreducibility Criterion

**Theorem (irreducibility criterion; Mackey).** Let $H \leq G$ be closed and let $\rho$ be an irreducible unitary representation of $H$. Under the standard regularity hypotheses on the double cosets $H\backslash G/H$, the induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho$ is irreducible if and only if for every $g \in G \smallsetminus H$ the representations $\rho$ and $\rho^g$ of $H \cap gHg^{-1}$, defined by $\rho^g(x) = \rho(g^{-1}xg)$ restricted to the intersection, are disjoint; equivalently, the stabiliser of the class of $\rho$ in the conjugation action of $G$ on $\operatorname{Irr}(H)$ is exactly $H$.

**Proof.** The restriction formula applied with $H_1 = H_2 = H$ expresses $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho$ restricted to $H$ as the direct sum over the double cosets $H\backslash G/H$ of the representations induced from $\rho^g$ on $H \cap gHg^{-1}$. The double coset $H$ itself contributes $\rho$, while the others contribute representations orthogonal to $\rho$ or not, and $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho$ is irreducible exactly when its endomorphism algebra is $\mathbb{C}$. Computing $\operatorname{End}_G(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho)$ by Frobenius reciprocity of *Induced Representations of Locally Compact Groups* gives $\operatorname{Hom}_H(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho|_H,\rho)$, which by the restriction formula is $\operatorname{Hom}_H(\bigoplus_{g}\rho^g, \rho)$, a space of dimension one exactly when only the identity double coset contributes, which is exactly the disjointness condition on $\rho$ and its conjugates. The general statement is the theorem of Mackey; the counting is exact in the finite case and in the type I case, and is quoted as standard in general. $\square$

### Restriction and Tensor Products

**Theorem (restriction formula).** Let $H_1, H_2 \leq G$ be closed subgroups and let $\rho$ be a unitary representation of $H_1$. Then, as a representation of $H_2$,

$$
\Bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1}^G\rho\Bigr)\Big|_{H_2} \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{s \in H_2\backslash G/H_1} \operatorname{Ind}_{H_2\cap sH_1s^{-1}}^{H_2}\rho^s ,
$$

the sum running over the double cosets and $\rho^s(h) = \rho(s^{-1}hs)$ for $h \in H_2\cap sH_1s^{-1}$.

**Theorem (tensor product formula).** Let $H_1, H_2 \leq G$ be closed subgroups and let $\rho_i$ be unitary representations of $H_i$. Then

$$
\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1}^G\rho_1 \otimes \operatorname{Ind}_{H_2}^G\rho_2 \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{s \in H_2\backslash G/H_1} \operatorname{Ind}_{H_2\cap sH_1s^{-1}}^{G}\bigl(\rho_1^s \otimes \rho_2\bigr) ,
$$

where $\rho_1^s$ and the restriction of $\rho_2$ are both read on the intersection group $H_2\cap sH_1s^{-1}$, and the constituents are indexed by the double cosets.

**Proof.** The two formulas are the two faces of one computation. The tensor product $\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1}^G\rho_1\otimes\operatorname{Ind}_{H_2}^G\rho_2$ is the restriction to the diagonal of the representation $\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1\times H_2}^{G\times G}(\rho_1\boxtimes\rho_2)$ of $G \times G$, and the restriction formula applied to the diagonal subgroup $\Delta G \subseteq G\times G$ expresses it as a sum over the double cosets of $\Delta G$ in $G\times G$ against $H_1\times H_2$; those double cosets are parametrised by the double cosets $H_2\backslash G/H_1$, and the constituent at $s$ is induced from $\rho_1^s\otimes\rho_2$ on the intersection. The restriction formula is proved directly by the orbit decomposition of $G/H_1$ under $H_2$. Both are stated and proved as standard in the literature; the details are combinatorial in nature and are exact. $\square$

**Example (finite groups).** For finite $G$ the formulas are the classical ones of *Representations of Groups*: the restriction formula is Mackey's theorem on the restriction of an induced representation, and the tensor product formula computes the tensor product of two induced representations as a sum over double cosets. The character identities, with sums over $G$ replaced by averages, are the finite content of the two theorems.

## The Mackey Machine

### Groups with a Normal Subgroup

The Mackey machine classifies the irreducible unitary representations of a group $G$ with a closed normal subgroup $N$ such that the quotient $G/N$ is well behaved, in terms of the action of $G$ on $\operatorname{Irr}(N)$ and the representations of certain stabilisers. The machinery proceeds in three steps.

**Theorem (Mackey's analysis).** Let $N \trianglelefteq G$ be a closed normal subgroup and let $\pi$ be an irreducible unitary representation of $G$. Then:

**(a)** The restriction $\pi|_N$ is a direct sum (or, in general, a direct integral) of irreducibles of $N$, and the set of those occurring is a $G$-orbit in $\operatorname{Irr}(N)$ — the **orbit of $\pi$** — because $\pi(g)\pi(n)\pi(g)^{-1} = \pi(gng^{-1})$ shows that the set of classes occurring is invariant under conjugation.

**(b)** Fix an irreducible $\rho \in \operatorname{Irr}(N)$ in that orbit and let

$$
G_\rho = \{g \in G : \rho^g \cong \rho\}
$$

be its **stabiliser** or **little group**. There is a projective representation $\sigma$ of $G_\rho$ on $\mathcal{H}_\rho$ extending $\rho$, with cocycle

$$
\sigma(g)\sigma(h) = \omega(g,h)\,\sigma(gh) , \qquad \omega(g,h) \in \mathbb{T} ,
$$

and $\pi$ is equivalent to $\operatorname{Ind}_{G_\rho}^G(\sigma_\pi)$ for an irreducible projective representation $\sigma_\pi$ of $G_\rho$ whose cocycle is $\omega$.

**(c)** The irreducible representations of $G$ with orbit $\mathcal{O}$ correspond to the irreducible projective representations of $G_\rho$ with cocycle $\omega$ whose restriction to $N$ is a multiple of $\rho$, and these may be read on $G_\rho/\ker\rho$ with the cocycle induced by $\omega$. In particular, when $\omega$ is a coboundary — which holds in particular when the extension $1 \to N \to G_\rho \to G_\rho/N \to 1$ splits over the quotient — the projective representations are ordinary representations of $G_\rho$, and the classification is by the irreducibles of the little group.

**Proof sketch.** (a) The restriction decomposes over the spectral measure of the commutative algebra generated by $N$; conjugating by $g$ changes $\rho$ to $\rho^g$, so the set of classes occurring is an orbit. (b) The stabiliser $G_\rho$ acts on $\mathcal{H}_\rho$ by intertwining operators that are unitary up to scalars; the choice of a measurable section of the action produces the cocycle $\omega$, and the extension of $\rho$ to a projective representation is a measurable choice, made unique up to a character. The induction of that projective representation back to $G$ is the representation $\pi$, and the equivalence is the imprimitivity theorem applied to the system of imprimitivity carried by the orbit $\mathcal{O}$, identified with $G/G_\rho$ via the orbit map. (c) The projective representations of $G_\rho$ with cocycle $\omega$ are modules over the twisted group algebra, and the irreducible ones are classified by the little group modulo the kernel of $\rho$; the split case reduces to ordinary representation theory. The full proof, including the measurability of the choices and the direct-integral refinement, is standard and is quoted from the literature; the decomposition into a direct integral in the non-type-I case is the Part III refinement. $\square$

### Semidirect Products and the Little-Group Method

**Corollary (semidirect products).** Let $G = N \rtimes H$ with $N$ abelian and normal. Then the irreducible unitary representations of $G$ are parametrised by the pairs $(\mathcal{O}, \sigma)$ where $\mathcal{O}$ is a $G$-orbit in the Pontryagin dual $N^\vee$ and $\sigma$ is an irreducible unitary representation of the stabiliser $H_\chi$ of a point $\chi \in \mathcal{O}$, the representation being $\operatorname{Ind}_{N\rtimes H_\chi}^G(\chi \otimes \sigma)$. Here $H_\chi = \{h \in H : h\cdot\chi = \chi\}$ is the little group of $\chi$, and the projective obstruction is absent because $H_\chi$ meets $N$ trivially in the semidirect product.

**Proof.** Apply the Mackey machine with $N$ abelian, so $\operatorname{Irr}(N) = N^\vee$ by *Pontryagin Duality*; the stabiliser of $\chi \in N^\vee$ is $G_\chi = N \rtimes H_\chi$, and the extension $\sigma$ is $\chi \otimes \sigma$ with $\sigma$ an ordinary representation of $H_\chi$ because $N$ is abelian and the action of $H_\chi$ on $N$ is by group automorphisms preserving $\chi$, so the cocycle $\omega$ can be computed to be trivial on $N$ and on a complement. The induction is from $G_\chi$ to $G$. $\square$

**Example (the Euclidean group and its motion group analogue).** Let $G = SO(n) \ltimes \mathbb{R}^n$, the group of orientation-preserving isometries of $\mathbb{R}^n$. The dual of the normal subgroup $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $\mathbb{R}^n$, on which $SO(n)$ acts by the defining representation. The orbits are the spheres of radius $r > 0$ together with the origin. For $r > 0$ the stabiliser of a point is $SO(n-1)$, and the irreducible unitary representations of $G$ with orbit the sphere of radius $r$ are $\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathbb{R}^n\rtimes SO(n-1)}^{G}(\chi_\xi \otimes \sigma)$ for $\sigma \in \operatorname{Irr}(SO(n-1))$; the ones with orbit the origin are the representations of $SO(n)$ pulled back. This is the complete classification of the irreducible unitary representations of the Euclidean group, and it exhibits the general pattern: a continuous family parametrised by the radius and a discrete parameter from the little group.

**Example (the affine group of the line).** Let $G = \mathbb{R} \rtimes \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ with the multiplication of *Locally Compact Groups and Haar Measure*, §The Modular Function and Unimodularity. The normal subgroup $\mathbb{R}$ has dual $\mathbb{R}$, and $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ acts by scaling with two orbits: $\{0\}$ and $\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\{0\}$. The orbit $\{0\}$ gives the characters of the quotient $\mathbb{R}_{>0} \cong \mathbb{R}$, a continuous family of one-dimensional representations; the two orbits $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ and $\mathbb{R}_{<0}$ are free, so the little group is trivial and the corresponding representations are $\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathbb{R}}^G\chi_{\pm}$, the two infinite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations of the affine group, contragredient to one another and each a member of its continuous series. This is the standard computation of the unitary dual of the $ax+b$ group and the simplest non-unimodular example of the machine.

### Nilpotent Groups and the Orbit Method

**Theorem (Kirillov's orbit method, statement).** Let $G$ be a connected simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$. Then:

**(a)** the coadjoint action of $G$ on the dual vector space $\mathfrak{g}^*$ has orbits that are symplectic manifolds under the Kirillov–Kostant form

$$
\omega_\xi(X, Y) = \langle \xi, [X, Y]\rangle , \qquad \xi \in \mathfrak{g}^* ;
$$

**(b)** every coadjoint orbit $\mathcal{O}$ carries a $G$-invariant measure and a canonical irreducible unitary representation $\pi_\mathcal{O}$ of $G$ realised on the space of square-integrable sections of a line bundle over $\mathcal{O}$ (the **Kirillov correspondence**);

**(c)** the map $\mathcal{O} \mapsto \pi_\mathcal{O}$ is a bijection between the coadjoint orbits and $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$;

**(d)** the representation $\pi_\mathcal{O}$ is monomial: it is induced from a one-dimensional unitary character of a closed subgroup, and it is obtained by the Mackey machine from the abelian normal subgroups of the lower central series.

**Proof sketch.** The proof is by induction on the dimension, along the lower central series. A nonzero nilpotent Lie algebra has a centre of positive dimension, so $G$ has a central subgroup $Z \neq \{e\}$; fix a unitary character $\chi$ of $Z$ and let $G_\chi$ be its stabiliser in $G$. The Mackey machine applied to the central subgroup $Z$ — the case in which the kernel is central, so that the action of $G$ on $\operatorname{Irr}(Z)$ is trivial and no orbit analysis beyond the stabiliser is needed — reduces the irreducibles of $G$ with central character $\chi$ to the irreducible projective representations of $G_\chi/Z$ with the Mackey cocycle $\omega(h,k) = \chi([\tilde h,\tilde k])$ determined by $\chi$ and the commutators, lifted to $G_\chi$; the representation is then induced from $G_\chi$ to $G$. When the centre acts by scalars, the alternating form $\langle\xi,[X,Y]\rangle$ attached to the corresponding $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}^*$ is nondegenerate modulo its radical, a maximal isotropic subalgebra $\mathfrak{m}$ — a polarisation, which exists for a nilpotent algebra — is chosen so that the character $e^{i\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle}$ is trivial on $[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]$, and $\pi_\mathcal{O} = \operatorname{Ind}_M^G\chi_\xi$ is irreducible and independent of the polarisation up to equivalence; the orbit $\mathcal{O}$ is identified with $G/M$ and the Kirillov–Kostant form is the symplectic form of that homogeneous space. The full argument, including the verification that the inductive stage matches the coadjoint-orbit picture, is the theorem of Kirillov and is quoted as standard; the measure-theoretic part — the invariant measure on the orbit and the completion of the induced space — is the Part III refinement. $\square$

**Example (the Heisenberg group).** For the Heisenberg group with Lie algebra spanned by $X, Y, Z$ with $[X,Y] = Z$ central, the dual $\mathfrak{g}^*$ has coordinates $(x, y, z)$; the coadjoint orbits are the planes $z = \lambda \neq 0$, each a single orbit (because the coadjoint action moves the pair $(x,y)$ freely while fixing $z$), together with the single points of the plane $z = 0$, on which the coadjoint action is trivial. The orbit $z = \lambda$ corresponds to the Stone–von Neumann representation $\pi_\lambda$ — realised classically on the square-integrable functions of the line, an object of Part III — and each point of the plane $z = 0$ to a character of the abelianisation. This is the model case of the orbit method and the computation of *Representation Theory of Locally Compact Groups*, §The General Case and the Unitary Dual.

## Projective Representations and the Mackey Obstruction

### Cocycles and Extensions

Let $G_\rho$ be the little group of an irreducible $\rho$ of a normal subgroup $N$, and let $\omega : G_\rho \times G_\rho \to \mathbb{T}$ be the cocycle of the projective extension. Then $\omega$ is a $2$-cocycle:

$$
\omega(g,h)\,\omega(gh,k) = \omega(g,hk)\,\omega(h,k) \qquad \text{for all } g, h, k \in G_\rho ,
$$

which is exactly the cocycle identity of *Group Cohomology*. Multiplying $\sigma(g)$ by a measurable scalar $c(g)$ replaces $\omega$ by the coboundary $\omega \cdot \delta c$; hence $\omega$ determines a class

$$
[\omega] \in H^2(G_\rho, \mathbb{T})
$$

in the second cohomology of $G_\rho$ with coefficients in the circle, and this class is the **Mackey obstruction** of the orbit. The obstruction measures the failure of the extension

$$
1 \to N \to G_\rho \to G_\rho/N \to 1
$$

to split over the stabiliser in the relevant sense, and it is invariant under conjugation of $\rho$.

**Theorem (obstruction criterion).** The Mackey obstruction $[\omega]$ vanishes if and only if the representation $\rho$ of $N$ extends to an ordinary representation of $G_\rho$; in that case the irreducible representations of $G$ with orbit $\mathcal{O}$ are the representations $\operatorname{Ind}_{G_\rho}^G(\tilde\rho \otimes \tau)$ with $\tau \in \operatorname{Irr}(G_\rho/N)$. If the obstruction is nontrivial, the classification requires the twisted group algebra or, equivalently, the corresponding central extension of $G_\rho/N$ by $\mathbb{T}$.

**Proof.** The vanishing of a $2$-cocycle class means that the cocycle is a coboundary $\delta c$; rescaling $\sigma$ by $c^{-1}$ makes the projective representation an ordinary one, which is precisely an extension of $\rho$ to $G_\rho$. When the obstruction vanishes, the Mackey machine's classification reduces to the ordinary irreducibles of the quotient modulo the kernel, which are the $\tau \in \operatorname{Irr}(G_\rho/N)$ twisted by the extension; when it does not, the representations are the irreducible modules over the twisted group algebra, computed by the projective representation theory of $G_\rho/N$. $\square$

**Example (the obstruction for the Euclidean group).** For $G = SO(n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\chi \in \mathbb{R}^n\smallsetminus\{0\}$ the little group is $SO(n-1)$, which meets $\mathbb{R}^n$ trivially, so the extension splits and the obstruction vanishes; the classification is by $\operatorname{Irr}(SO(n-1))$ as stated above. The obstruction is needed for extensions in which the quotient does not lift, the classical example being a group whose normal abelian subgroup has a character whose stabiliser does not split over it; the standard finite example is the quaternion group $Q_8$ with its central subgroup of order $2$, where the obstruction is the nontrivial element of $H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{T}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.

## The Boundary with Analysis

- The **completion** of the induced spaces, the **direct-integral decomposition** over a coadjoint orbit or over a double coset space, and the identification of the little-group induction with a space of $L^2$ sections of a line bundle are Part III; the article states the constructions on the dense algebraic subspaces.
- The **orbit method for solvable groups** and the **Kirillov character formula**, which expresses the character of $\pi_\mathcal{O}$ as the Fourier transform of the orbit measure, require the distribution theory of Part III.
- The **operator-algebraic form** of the imprimitivity theorem — the Morita equivalence of $C_0(G/H)\rtimes G$ with $C^*(H)$ — is developed with the operator algebras of *Topology on Linear Algebras*.
- What is *not* deferred: the imprimitivity theorem, the intertwining number theorem, the irreducibility criterion, the restriction and tensor-product formulas, the Mackey machine, the little-group method for semidirect products, the orbit correspondence for nilpotent Lie groups and the obstruction class are developed here.

## Summary

A system of imprimitivity for a unitary representation $\pi$ of $G$ based on a transitive $G$-space $G/H$ is a projection-valued measure $P$ on $G/H$ with $\pi(g)P(E)\pi(g)^{-1} = P(gE)$. Mackey's imprimitivity theorem states that the induced representations $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho$ are exactly the representations carrying such a system, the inducing representation being the restriction of $\pi$ to $H$ on the range of $P(H)$. The theorem has a $C^*$-algebraic form as a Morita equivalence between $C_0(G/H)\rtimes G$ and $C^*(H)$.

Mackey's intertwining number theorem computes the intertwiners between two induced representations as a sum over the double cosets of Hom spaces on the intersections, and his irreducibility criterion states that $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\rho$ is irreducible exactly when $\rho$ is irreducible and is disjoint from all its conjugates by elements outside $H$. The restriction formula expresses $(\operatorname{Ind}_{H_1}^G\rho)|_{H_2}$ as a sum over the double cosets $H_2\backslash G/H_1$ of inductions from the intersections, and the tensor product formula is the same computation for the diagonal of $G\times G$. The Mackey machine analyses an irreducible $\pi$ of a group with a closed normal subgroup $N$ by the $G$-orbit of $\pi|_N$ in $\operatorname{Irr}(N)$, the little group of a point of the orbit, and a projective extension whose cocycle has a class in $H^2(G_\rho,\mathbb{T})$, the Mackey obstruction, vanishing exactly when the little-group representation extends.

For a semidirect product $G = N\rtimes H$ with $N$ abelian the irreducibles are $\operatorname{Ind}_{N\rtimes H_\chi}^G(\chi\otimes\sigma)$ for orbits in $N^\vee$ and $\sigma$ an irreducible of the little group $H_\chi$; for a connected simply connected nilpotent Lie group, Kirillov's orbit method identifies $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ with the coadjoint orbits by the Kirillov correspondence, and the representations are monomial. The analytic refinements belong to *Analysis on Groups* in Part III.

## Further Reading

- George W. Mackey, *The Theory of Unitary Group Representations* (University of Chicago Press, 1976), for systems of imprimitivity, the imprimitivity theorem and the analysis of group extensions.
- George W. Mackey, *The Scope and History of Commutative and Noncommutative Harmonic Analysis* (American Mathematical Society, 1992), for the historical development of the machine and its applications.
- Gerald B. Folland, *A Course in Abstract Harmonic Analysis* (CRC Press, 2nd ed. 2015), for the imprimitivity theorem and Mackey's analysis.
- Jacques Dixmier, *Les $C^*$-algèbres et leurs représentations* (Gauthier-Villars, 1964; English translation North-Holland, 1977), for the $C^*$-algebraic form of imprimitivity and the twisted group algebras.
- Alexandre A. Kirillov, *Lectures on the Orbit Method* (American Mathematical Society, 2004), for the orbit method and the Kirillov correspondence.
- Alexandre A. Kirillov, *Elements of the Theory of Representations* (Springer, 1976), for the orbit method for nilpotent and solvable groups.
- L. Pukánszky, *Leçons sur les représentations des groupes* (Dunod, 1967), for the representation theory of nilpotent and solvable Lie groups.
- Marc A. Rieffel, *Morita Equivalence and Continuous-Trace $C^*$-Algebras* (American Mathematical Society, 1998), for the crossed-product and Morita-equivalence form of imprimitivity.
- Armand Borel and Nolan R. Wallach, *Continuous Cohomology, Discrete Subgroups, and Representations of Reductive Groups* (Princeton University Press, 2nd ed. 2000), for the cohomological machinery of extensions and obstructions.

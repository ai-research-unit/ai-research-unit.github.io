Introduction to the 2×2 Matrix Representation of Biquaternions

Introduction

The biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H}$ is the four-dimensional complex algebra with basis $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, where $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_je_k=-e_ke_j$ for $j\neq k$, and with a central scalar imaginary $i$ satisfying $i^2=-1$; a general element is $\tilde Q=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}Q_\mu e_\mu$ with $Q_\mu\in\mathbb{C}$. The algebra and its six distinguished subspaces are those of Biquaternions as a Vector Space over $\mathbb{C}$ and Introduction to the Six Subspaces.

This article is the first reading of the matrix realization of the algebra: the presentation of $\mathbb{B}$ as a four-dimensional subspace of the matrix algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, fixed by four displayed matrices and extended by linearity. It belongs to the linear algebra of the algebra, beside Comparison of the Six Subspaces: the realization writes the four coefficients in a square array, and the six distinguished subspaces reappear as six matrix conditions.

This article owns the map, the statement that it is an algebra isomorphism, the trace and the determinant of the matrix, and the six subspace conditions. The further development — the rank-one elements and the outer product, the characteristic polynomial and the spectrum, the Cayley–Hamilton identity, the matrix form of the four conjugations, the simple module, and the matrix units with the projective line — is Biquaternion 2×2 Matrix Element Representation.

The Representation

Definition. The matrix realization of $\mathbb{B}$ is the $\mathbb{C}$-linear map

$$ \Phi:\mathbb{B}\longrightarrow M_2(\mathbb{C}) $$

determined on the basis by

$$ \Phi(e_0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Phi(e_1)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\-i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Phi(e_2)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Phi(e_3)=\begin{pmatrix}-i&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix}, $$

and on the central scalar by $\Phi(ie_\mu)=i\Phi(e_\mu)$. On a general element it reads

$$ \Phi(\tilde Q)= \begin{pmatrix} Q_0-iQ_3 & -iQ_1-Q_2\\ -iQ_1+Q_2 & Q_0+iQ_3 \end{pmatrix}. $$

Remark (the choice, stated). The assignment above is the corpus's choice, held fixed so that the realization agrees with The Clifford Structure of the Biquaternion Algebra. It is convenient because the three images of $e_1,e_2,e_3$ are $-i$ times the Pauli matrices, mentioned once as a shorthand and nothing more: every proposition is a statement about $\mathbb{B}$ and about $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. The choice is not canonical; a different isomorphism differs from this one by conjugation by an invertible matrix, and the invariants below are unchanged.

Remark. The four coefficients and the four matrix entries carry the same data, but the passage between them is not a rearrangement: each entry is a fixed linear combination of two coefficients, with coefficients among $1,-1,i,-i$; the entry $\Phi(\tilde Q)_{12}=-iQ_1-Q_2$ involves two components at once.

Remark (other realizations from the light-cone basis). A second family of $2\times2$ models comes from the isotropic (light-cone) basis used in The Chiral Algebra of Biquaternions and the Cyclic Representation of the Dirac Equation. Writing a biquaternion in the null coordinates there, two of that article's four products become ordinary matrix products under two maps, the outer product under $M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi&\alpha\\\beta&\eta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and the diagonal product under $F\leftrightarrow\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f_\alpha&-f_\beta\\-f_\xi&f_\eta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; the source's claimed third, the inner product, is false, an exhaustive search finding no realization of it. The two maps differ from $\Phi$ by the change of basis between the isotropic basis and the corpus's basis, and they are named here only to place $\Phi$ among the others.

The Representation Is an Algebra Isomorphism

Theorem. The map $\Phi$ is an algebra isomorphism $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$.

Proof. The images of $e_1,e_2,e_3$ satisfy the quaternion relations. Each squares to $-I$, since $\Phi(e_1)^2=\Phi(e_2)^2=\Phi(e_3)^2=-I$ by direct computation, and the cyclic products match, $\Phi(e_1)\Phi(e_2)=\Phi(e_3)$ while $\Phi(e_2)\Phi(e_1)=-\Phi(e_3)$. The algebra $\mathbb{B}$ is generated by $e_1,e_2,e_3$ subject only to these relations, so the linear map extending the assignment is multiplicative, hence an algebra homomorphism. It is bijective because the four images are linearly independent in the four-dimensional space $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, and so form a basis. $\square$

Corollary (the centre). The centre of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the set of scalar matrices $\lambda I$, so the centre of $\mathbb{B}$ is the scalar subspace $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{C}e_0$, and $\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}})$ is the set of scalar matrices.

Proof. A matrix commuting with every matrix is a scalar matrix; transport along $\Phi$. $\square$

The Trace and the Determinant

Proposition (trace and determinant). For every biquaternion $\tilde{Q}$,

$$ \operatorname{Tr}(\Phi(\tilde{Q}))=2Q_0,\qquad \det(\Phi(\tilde{Q}))=N(\tilde{Q})=Q_0^2+Q_1^2+Q_2^2+Q_3^2 . $$

Proof. The trace is the sum of the diagonal entries, $(Q_0-iQ_3)+(Q_0+iQ_3)=2Q_0$. The determinant is

$$ (Q_0-iQ_3)(Q_0+iQ_3)-(-iQ_1-Q_2)(-iQ_1+Q_2)=(Q_0^2+Q_3^2)-\bigl((-iQ_1)^2-Q_2^2\bigr)=Q_0^2+Q_1^2+Q_2^2+Q_3^2, $$

since $(-iQ_1)^2=-Q_1^2$ and the cross terms cancel within each product. $\square$

Corollary (invertibility). A biquaternion is invertible if and only if $\det\Phi(\tilde{Q})=N(\tilde{Q})\neq0$, and then $\Phi(\tilde{Q}^{-1})=\Phi(\tilde{Q})^{-1}$. The zero divisors of $\mathbb{B}$ are exactly the nonzero elements whose matrix is singular.

Proof. A square matrix over a field is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero, and $\Phi$ is an isomorphism, so invertibility transports. $\square$

The classification of the zero divisors is Biquaternion Zero Divisors; what the matrix realization adds is the reading of the criterion in terms of the determinant and the rank, developed in Biquaternion 2×2 Matrix Element Representation, §The Rank-One Elements and the Outer Product.

Remark (the determinant is multiplicative, the trace is not). The determinant is multiplicative, $\det\Phi(\tilde{P}\tilde{Q})=\det\Phi(\tilde{P})\det\Phi(\tilde{Q})$, so $N$ is multiplicative, $N(\tilde{P}\tilde{Q})=N(\tilde{P})N(\tilde{Q})$. The trace is additive but not multiplicative, and its value $2Q_0$ depends only on the scalar part.

Worked example. For $\tilde{Q}=(2+i)e_0+(1-i)e_1+3e_2+ie_3$ the image is $\begin{pmatrix}3+i&-4-i\\2-i&1+i\end{pmatrix}$, of trace $4+2i=2Q_0$ and determinant $11+2i=N(\tilde{Q})$, so the element is a unit with inverse $\tilde{Q}^{\natural}/N$.

The Six Subspaces in Matrix Form

The six distinguished subspaces have the following images under $\Phi$. Each row records the image of the subspace, not a new object.

Subspace Image under $\Phi$ Description
$\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $\{\lambda I:\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}$ the scalar matrices
$\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ $\{M:\operatorname{Tr}M=0\}$ the traceless matrices, the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$
$\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $\left\{\begin{pmatrix}z&w\\-\bar w&\bar z\end{pmatrix}:z,w\in\mathbb{C}\right\}$ the fixed points of $M\mapsto\epsilon\overline{M}\epsilon^{-1}$
$i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $\left\{\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\\bar v&-\bar u\end{pmatrix}:u,v\in\mathbb{C}\right\}$ $i$ times the quaternion subspace
$\mathbb{M}_+$ $\{M:M=M^\dagger\}$ the Hermitian matrices
$\mathbb{M}_-$ $\{M:M=-M^\dagger\}$ the anti-Hermitian matrices

Here $\epsilon=i\sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$, and $\dagger$ is the conjugate transpose.

Proof. The centre and the traceless part follow from the general entry and the definition of the centre. The quaternion subspace, whose coefficients are real, is the fixed space of the antilinear map $M\mapsto\epsilon\overline{M}\epsilon^{-1}$: the condition is $M_{22}=\overline{M_{11}}$ and $M_{21}=-\overline{M_{12}}$, written with $z=M_{11}$ and $w=M_{12}$. Multiplication by $i$ gives the anti-quaternion subspace, where the two diagonal signs are exchanged, $M_{22}=-\overline{M_{11}}$ and $M_{21}=\overline{M_{12}}$. The Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces are the fixed and anti-fixed spaces of the conjugate transpose. $\square$

Explicit Patterns and Invariants

Solving each defining condition gives the explicit pattern and the trace and determinant. With $Q_\mu=q_\mu+iq'_\mu$,

Subspace Explicit pattern of $\Phi(\tilde Q)$ $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(\tilde Q)$ $\det\Phi(\tilde Q)$
$\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $Q_0I$ $2Q_0$ $Q_0^2=N$
$\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{B})$ $\begin{pmatrix}-iQ_3&-iQ_1-Q_2\\-iQ_1+Q_2&iQ_3\end{pmatrix}$, traceless $0$ $N$
$\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $\begin{pmatrix}z&w\\-\bar w&\bar z\end{pmatrix}$, $z=q_0-iq_3$, $w=-q_2-iq_1$ $2q_0$ $\lvert z\rvert^2+\lvert w\rvert^2=N>0$
$i\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{B}}$ $\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\\bar v&-\bar u\end{pmatrix}$, $u=q'_3+iq'_0$, $v=q'_1-iq'_2$ $2iq'_0$ $-\bigl(\lvert u\rvert^2+\lvert v\rvert^2\bigr)=N<0$
$\mathbb{M}_+$ $M=M^\dagger$, Hermitian $2q_0\in\mathbb{R}$ $N\in\mathbb{R}$
$\mathbb{M}_-$ $M=-M^\dagger$, anti-Hermitian $2iq'_0$ $N\in\mathbb{R}$

The trace separates the centre from the vector subspace and nothing else, seeing only the scalar part, twice. On the quaternion subspace the determinant is strictly positive, so no nonzero element there is singular: the image is a realization of the quaternion division algebra inside $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. On the anti-quaternion subspace it is strictly negative, so that subspace misses the singular matrices altogether, which is the matrix statement of the absence of zero divisors there. The traceless matrices are the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, developed in The 12 Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, and the two patterns of the middle rows are the conjugate-reflection condition of the real and the purely imaginary coefficients. The degenerate cases of the determinant, the rank-one matrices and the outer product $\Phi(\tilde{Q})=uv^{T}$ belong to Biquaternion 2×2 Matrix Element Representation.

Summary

The matrix realization $\Phi$ presents the biquaternion algebra as the four-dimensional subspace of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ spanned by four displayed matrices, the images of $e_1,e_2,e_3$ being $-i$ times the Pauli matrices. The map is an algebra isomorphism $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, so the centre goes to the scalar matrices. The trace of the image is $2Q_0$, the determinant is $N(\tilde{Q})=Q_0^2+Q_1^2+Q_2^2+Q_3^2$, so invertibility is non-vanishing determinant and the zero divisors are the singular matrices; the determinant is multiplicative and the trace is not. The six distinguished subspaces become six matrix conditions: scalar, traceless, the $\epsilon$-conjugation pattern, its $i$-multiple, Hermitian and anti-Hermitian matrices. The rank-one elements, the characteristic polynomial, the Cayley–Hamilton identity, the conjugations in matrix form, the simple module and the matrix-unit structure are the further development and belong to Biquaternion 2×2 Matrix Element Representation.

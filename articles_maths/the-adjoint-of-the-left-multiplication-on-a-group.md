
# __The Adjoint of the Left Multiplication on a Group__

## Introduction

The left multiplication $x\mapsto ax$ is the simplest operator a group carries, and its adjoint with respect to the natural pairing of the category is explicit: it is the left multiplication by the inverse of $a$. The case in which the operator is self-adjoint is exactly the case in which the parameter has order at most two, so the involutions of the group reappear as the self-adjoint left multiplications. This article gives the adjoint, the self-adjointness criterion, and the compatibility of the adjoint operation with the involution of the group; the two-sided case and the interpretation of the inversion as the adjoint of the left multiplication are in *Involutions on the Operator Layer* and *The Group Inversion as an Adjoint*, and the left multiplication itself is in *Left and Right Multiplication in a Group*. It is the third article of the `* Operator Theory` group.

Throughout, $k[G]$ is the group algebra with orthonormal basis $G$, $\sigma$ is an involution of $G$ with linear extension $\Sigma_{\sigma}$, and the adjoint is defined by $\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,T^{*}y\rangle$ for the natural pairing $\langle g,h\rangle=\delta_{g,h}$.

## The Adjoint

**Theorem (the adjoint of the left multiplication).** For every $a\in G$,

$$
(L_a)^{*}=L_{a^{-1}}.
$$

**Proof.** In the basis $G$, $(L_ag)_h=\delta_{ag,h}$, and the matrix of the adjoint is the transpose, so $(L_a^{*}h)_g=\delta_{ag,h}=\delta_{g,a^{-1}h}$; hence $L_a^{*}(h)=a^{-1}h$ and $L_a^{*}=L_{a^{-1}}$. The same computation applies to any group of operators generated by the left multiplications.

**Corollary (iterates and products).** $(L_a^{*})^{*}=L_a$, $(L_aL_b)^{*}=L_{(ab)^{-1}}$, and $(L_a^{k})^{*}=L_{a^{-k}}$ for every integer $k$. The left multiplications form a $*$-closed set: the adjoint of a left multiplication is a left multiplication.

**Proof.** The adjoint is an involution, so $(L_a^{*})^{*}=L_a$; the product rule $(L_aL_b)^{*}=L_b^{*}L_a^{*}=L_{b^{-1}}L_{a^{-1}}=L_{(ab)^{-1}}$ uses the anti-multiplicativity and the composition law; the power formula follows by induction, with the negative powers from the invertibility of the adjoint.

**Proposition (the matrices).** In the basis $G$ the matrix of $L_a$ has the single $1$ in each column, at the row $ag$ of the column $g$; the matrix of $L_a^{*}=L_{a^{-1}}$ has its $1$ in the row $a^{-1}g$ of the column $g$. The two matrices are transpose to one another, as they must be.

**Proof.** The column $g$ of $L_a$ has coefficient $1$ at $ag$ and $0$ elsewhere; the transpose has the $1$ at the row $g$ of the column $ag$, which is to say at the row $a^{-1}h$ of the column $h$ after setting $h=ag$.

## Self-Adjointness

**Theorem (the self-adjoint left multiplications are the involutions).** $L_a$ is self-adjoint if and only if $a^{2}=e$. Consequently the self-adjoint left multiplications are exactly the left translations by the elements of order at most two, they form a set in bijection with the $2$-torsion of $G$, and they include the identity.

**Proof.** $L_a$ is self-adjoint exactly when $L_a=L_{a^{-1}}$, and $a\mapsto L_a$ is injective since $L_ag=g$ forces $a=e$; so the condition is $a=a^{-1}$, that is $a^{2}=e$. The set of such elements is the $2$-torsion of $G$, which contains $e$.

**Example.** In $C_4=\langle r\rangle$ the self-adjoint left multiplications are $L_e$ and $L_{r^{2}}$, since $r^{2}$ is the unique element of order two. In $V_4$ all four left multiplications are self-adjoint, because every element is its own inverse. In $S_3$ the self-adjoint left multiplications are the four with parameter in $\{e,(1\,2),(1\,3),(2\,3)\}$. In $Q_8$ they are $L_{\pm1}$.

**Proposition (the skew-adjoint left multiplications).** There is no nonzero skew-adjoint left multiplication unless $\operatorname{char}k=2$; in characteristic not two, $L_a$ is skew-adjoint if and only if $L_a=0$, which never happens for a translation.

**Proof.** Skew-adjointness is $L_a^{*}=-L_a$, that is $L_{a^{-1}}=-L_a$. Evaluating at $e$ gives $a^{-1}=-a$, which in characteristic not two would make $L_{a^{-1}}$ and $-L_a$ have entries $1$ and $-1$ in the same positions, a contradiction; in characteristic two the two signs agree and the condition is $a^{2}=e$, so every left multiplication is at once self-adjoint and skew-adjoint.

**Remark.** The self-adjoint criterion is the operator-level form of the fixed-point criterion of the involutive part of the category: an element of order at most two is a fixed point of the inversion, and the left multiplication by such an element is a fixed point of the adjoint. The correspondence is exact and is the reason the two structures are read together.

## Compatibility with the Involution

**Proposition (the adjoint commutes with the extension of the involution).** For every involution $\sigma$ of $G$ and every $a\in G$,

$$
(L_{\sigma(a)})^{*}=L_{\sigma(a)^{-1}}=L_{\sigma(a^{-1})}=\Sigma_{\sigma}\,L_{a^{-1}}\,\Sigma_{\sigma},
$$

and the extension $\Sigma_{\sigma}$ commutes with the adjoint operation: $(\Sigma_{\sigma}T\Sigma_{\sigma})^{*}=\Sigma_{\sigma}T^{*}\Sigma_{\sigma}$.

**Proof.** The first chain is the adjoint formula together with $\sigma(a)^{-1}=\sigma(a^{-1})$; the last equality is the conjugation formula for a translation under the extension of $\sigma$, which acts on the basis by $\Sigma_{\sigma}g=\sigma(g)$. For the commutation, $\Sigma_{\sigma}$ is self-adjoint by *The Group Inversion as an Adjoint*, so $(\Sigma_{\sigma}T\Sigma_{\sigma})^{*}=\Sigma_{\sigma}^{*}T^{*}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{*}=\Sigma_{\sigma}T^{*}\Sigma_{\sigma}$.

**Proposition (the left regular representation is a $*$-representation).** The map $a\mapsto L_a$ is a representation of $G$ into the algebra $\operatorname{End}_k(k[G])$ with the adjoint as involution, and it satisfies $L_a^{*}=L_{a^{-1}}$, that is, it is a $*$-representation for the inversion of $G$.

**Proof.** The multiplicativity $L_aL_b=L_{ab}$ is the composition law of the translations; the adjoint relation is the theorem; and the defining property of a $*$-representation, $\rho(a)^{*}=\rho(a^{-1})$, is exactly the adjoint formula.

**Corollary (the commutant is closed under the adjoint).** The commutant of $L(G)$ in $\operatorname{End}_k(k[G])$, which is the set of right multiplications $R(G)$, together with the scalars, is closed under the adjoint: $(R_b)^{*}=R_{b^{-1}}\in R(G)$.

**Proof.** The commutant is $R(G)$ by the centraliser theorem of *Left and Right Multiplication in a Group*, and the adjoint of $R_b$ is $R_{b^{-1}}$, which lies in $R(G)$.

## Summary

The adjoint of the left multiplication is the left multiplication by the inverse, $(L_a)^{*}=L_{a^{-1}}$; equivalently the matrix of $L_a$ has its $1$'s at the positions $g\mapsto ag$ and the adjoint at $g\mapsto a^{-1}g$, the transpose. The adjoint commutes with products through the anti-multiplicativity, $(L_aL_b)^{*}=L_{(ab)^{-1}}$, and the left multiplications are closed under the adjoint.

The **self-adjoint** left multiplications are exactly those with parameter of order at most two: $L_a$ is self-adjoint if and only if $a^{2}=e$, so the self-adjoint translations correspond to the $2$-torsion of $G$; there are no nonzero skew-adjoint left multiplications in characteristic not two, and in characteristic two every left multiplication is both. The adjoint commutes with the extension of any group involution $\sigma$, and $a\mapsto L_a$ is a $*$-representation of $G$ for the inversion, whose commutant $R(G)$ is likewise closed under the adjoint.

## Summary of Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $(L_a)^{*}=L_{a^{-1}}$ | the adjoint of a left multiplication |
| $(L_aL_b)^{*}=L_{(ab)^{-1}}$ | the adjoint inverts a product |
| $L_a$ self-adjoint $\iff a^{2}=e$ | the self-adjoint translations are the involutions |
| $L_{\sigma(a)}^{*}=L_{\sigma(a^{-1})}$ | compatibility with a group involution |
| $\rho(a)^{*}=\rho(a^{-1})$ | the left regular representation is a $*$-representation |
| $(R_b)^{*}=R_{b^{-1}}$ | the commutant is $*$-closed |

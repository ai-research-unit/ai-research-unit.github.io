The Two General Products of the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space

Introduction

The quaternion space $\mathbb{H}$ is a four-dimensional real vector space and an associative division algebra, and it carries two general products: the general plain bilinear product $\tilde p\tilde q$, the multiplication of the algebra, and the general quaternionic bilinear product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, the plain product with the first factor read through the quaternion conjugation ${}^{\natural}$. The biquaternion chapter reads four general products on its space, because its base ring $\mathbb{C}$ carries a nontrivial involution and the second slot of each product may be read as it stands or through that involution. The base of the quaternion algebra is $\mathbb{R}$, whose only involution is the identity, so the second slot of every general product is the identity and the sesquilinear family of the biquaternion chapter does not exist here: exactly two general products are read on $\mathbb{H}$, and this article defines them.

The two products and their two structures are defined here. The symmetrisation and the antisymmetrisation of the two are The 6 Products of the Quaternion Real Space, and the six structures they define are The 6 Algebraic Structures over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space, both below this article in this category. The left and right multiplications of the two are The Two General Products and Operators, the reading on the distinguished subspaces is Remarkable Subspaces and the Two General Products, and the degree-two reading is The 2 Forms over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space, The 2 Algebraic Norms over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space and Remarkable Subspaces and the Two Forms, all below this article in this category. Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$, the real quaternion algebra, with units $e_0=1,e_1,e_2,e_3$, the relations $e_k^2=-e_0$ and $e_1e_2=e_3$, $e_2e_3=e_1$, $e_3e_1=e_2$, and the general element $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\mathbf q$, $\mathbf q=q_1e_1+q_2e_2+q_3e_3$, with real coefficients $q_\mu$. The quaternion conjugation is the intrinsic conjugation $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_0e_0-\mathbf q$; it is the unique nontrivial involution of $\mathbb{H}$ (The Group of Involutions of the Quaternions), and the bar of the general conventions is the identity on $\mathbb{R}$, so the second-slot involution of the general construction is the identity. The dot product is $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q=p_1q_1+p_2q_2+p_3q_3$ and the cross product $\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ is the usual one, $(p_2q_3-p_3q_2)e_1+(p_3q_1-p_1q_3)e_2+(p_1q_2-p_2q_1)e_3$. The scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde q)=q_0$ and the vector part is $\operatorname{Vect}(\tilde q)=\mathbf q$. In this article $\tilde p$ and $\tilde q$ name elements, so that the letters $B$ and $N$ stay free for the forms.

The Two Products

Definition. The general plain bilinear product and the general quaternionic bilinear product of the elements $\tilde p=p_0e_0+\mathbf p$ and $\tilde q=q_0e_0+\mathbf q$ are

$$ \tilde p\tilde q = \bigl(p_0q_0-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q\bigr) + p_0\mathbf q + q_0\mathbf p + \mathbf p\times\mathbf q , $$

$$ \tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q = \bigl(p_0q_0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q\bigr) + p_0\mathbf q - q_0\mathbf p - \mathbf p\times\mathbf q . $$

The first is the product of the algebra, read on the scalar and vector parts of the two elements; the second is the same rule with the first factor read through ${}^{\natural}$, which changes the sign of its vector part alone. The two products are defined on the same pair of coordinates and take their values in the same space, and the two are the general products of $\mathbb{H}$ in the sense of the general construction: the plain product with the two slots read as they stand, and the plain product with the first slot conjugated by the only nontrivial involution the element carries.

Both products are $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear, being built from the bilinear operations of the real vector space $\mathbb{H}$: the scalar and vector parts, the dot product and the cross product are all bilinear over $\mathbb{R}$. No slot of either is conjugate-linear, and the second slot of each is the identity, the base ring $\mathbb{R}$ having no nontrivial involution.

Proposition (the two products separate the scalar and the vector part of the first factor). For all $\tilde p,\tilde q$,

$$ \tilde p\tilde q+\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=2p_0\,\tilde q , \qquad \tilde p\tilde q-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=2\,\mathbf p\,\tilde q , $$

where $\mathbf p\tilde q$ is read as an element multiplying $\tilde q$, and the two products agree exactly when the vector part of the first factor annihilates the second,

$$ \tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=\tilde p\tilde q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathbf p\tilde q=0 . $$

Proof. The two rules differ only in the signs attached to $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$, $\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$ and $q_0\mathbf p$, so adding them cancels those terms and leaves $2p_0q_0+2p_0\mathbf q=2p_0\tilde q$; subtracting them leaves $-2\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q+2q_0\mathbf p+2\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$, which is $2\mathbf p\tilde q$ by the plain rule read with the vector part alone in the first slot. The agreement condition is the vanishing of the difference.

The Multiplication Tables

The two products are determined by their values on the pairs of units, as every bilinear rule is. For the plain product the table is the table of the algebra,

$\tilde p\backslash\tilde q$ $e_0$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $e_3$
$e_0$ $e_0$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $e_3$
$e_1$ $e_1$ $-e_0$ $e_3$ $-e_2$
$e_2$ $e_2$ $-e_3$ $-e_0$ $e_1$
$e_3$ $e_3$ $e_2$ $-e_1$ $-e_0$

and for the quaternionic product it is the same table with the entries $e_i^{\natural}e_j$,

$\tilde p\backslash\tilde q$ $e_0$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $e_3$
$e_0$ $e_0$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $e_3$
$e_1$ $-e_1$ $e_0$ $-e_3$ $e_2$
$e_2$ $-e_2$ $e_3$ $e_0$ $-e_1$
$e_3$ $-e_3$ $-e_2$ $e_1$ $e_0$

The two tables coincide on the first row and differ by a sign on every entry of the other three rows. The sign is the reflection of the vector part of the first factor, and it is the whole of the difference between the two products beyond the conjugation.

Proposition (the values of the two products). The plain product is the multiplication of the associative division algebra, its only nonzero idempotent is $e_0$ and it has no zero divisor. The quaternionic product has the left unit $e_0$ and no right unit, is not associative, and has the same two idempotents $0$ and $e_0$ and no zero divisor.

Proof. The plain product is the multiplication of $\mathbb{H}$, an associative division algebra with unit $e_0$; its only idempotents are $0$ and $e_0$ because $p(p-e_0)=0$ forces $p=0$ or $p=e_0$, and there is no zero divisor because every nonzero element is invertible. For the quaternionic product, $e_0^{\natural}\tilde q=e_0\tilde q=\tilde q$ while $\tilde q^{\natural}e_0=\tilde q^{\natural}\neq\tilde q$ for a non-real $\tilde q$, so $e_0$ is a left unit and not a right one; associativity fails on $\tilde p=\tilde q=\tilde r=e_1$, where $(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}\tilde r=(e_1^{\natural}e_1)^{\natural}e_1=e_0^{\natural}e_1=e_1$ while $\tilde p^{\natural}(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde r)=e_1^{\natural}(e_1^{\natural}e_1)=e_1^{\natural}e_0=-e_1$, and the two sides differ by the sign of $e_1$. The failure is measured by the associator $(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}\tilde r-\tilde p^{\natural}(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde r)=(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural})\tilde r$ of The Two General Products and Operators, below this article in this category. The idempotents of the quaternionic product are computed from $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde p=|p|^2e_0$, which is real, so $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde p=\tilde p$ forces $p\in\mathbb{R}$ and $p^2=p$, hence $p=0$ or $p=e_0$. A zero divisor would give $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=0$ with both factors nonzero; taking the norm of $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ gives $|p|^2|q|^2=0$, so one of the two factors is zero.

The associator of the quaternionic product and the rest of its laws are Comparison Between the Two General Products and The Two General Products and Operators, both below this article in this category.

Why Exactly Two Products and Not Four

The general products of the biquaternion chapter are four: the general products $\tilde P\tilde Q$ and $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q$ of the plain and the quaternionic family, and the general sesquilinear products $\tilde P\tilde Q^{*}$ and $\tilde P^{\natural}\tilde Q^{*}$ of the same two families with the second slot read through the star. The four are read from one construction: the plain product with a conjugation inserted in a slot, the conjugations being the intrinsic ${}^{\natural}$ of the element and the involution of the base ring, the two chosen independently. The first-slot choice gives the plain and the quaternionic families; the second-slot choice gives the bilinear and the sesquilinear family, the sesquilinear members being conjugate-linear in their second argument.

On $\mathbb{H}$ the second slot carries no choice at all. The base ring is $\mathbb{R}$, whose only involution is the identity; the insertion of the base involution in the second slot therefore changes nothing, and the general product is read with the identity in that slot. What remains is the first-slot choice, the identity or ${}^{\natural}$, and it produces exactly the two products of the definition,

$$ \tilde p\tilde q \quad\text{and}\quad \tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q , $$

the first with both slots plain and the second with the first slot conjugated. The two sesquilinear members of the biquaternion list have no counterpart here: they are conjugate-linear in their second argument, conjugate-linearity is a phenomenon of a base ring with a nontrivial involution, and $\mathbb{R}$ has none. The count of the general products of $\mathbb{H}$ is therefore two, and the count of the general products of $\mathbb{B}$ is four, the difference being the involution of the base and nothing else.

Remark (the second slot, and the count). The two sesquilinear general products of the biquaternion list carry the involution of the base in their second slot, and it is that second slot which separates the bilinear family from the sesquilinear one; the two bilinear members carry the identity in the same slot. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the involution of the base is the identity, so the second slot of every general product of $\mathbb{H}$ is the identity, the two sesquilinear members have no counterpart among the general products here, and the list of two is complete. The count is developed at length, with the record of the products the choice would give over a base with a nontrivial involution, in The Two General Products and the Reason There Are Only Two, in the general plain algebra block of this chapter.

The Structures the Two Products Define

The two products define the two structures that the whole operational vocabulary of the chapter describes, and no third structure is carried by a general product of $\mathbb{H}$.

product structure commutative associative unit
$\tilde p\tilde q$ associative division algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ no yes two-sided, $e_0$
$\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ quaternionic algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ no no left only, $e_0$

The first is the algebra $\mathbb{H}$ itself, the quaternion algebra with its multiplication: associative, with the two-sided unit $e_0$, non-commutative, and a division algebra, every nonzero element being invertible. The second is the quaternionic algebra, the space with the product of the first slot conjugated: non-associative, non-commutative, with the unit on the left alone. The two have the same underlying real vector space, the same two idempotents and the same absence of zero divisors, and they are told apart by associativity and by the side on which the unit acts. The two structures are read again, with the four structures of the two halves of each, in The 6 Algebraic Structures over the Quaternion $\mathbb{R}$ Space, below this article in this category.

Summary

The quaternion space carries two general products, defined on the same coordinates: the general plain bilinear product $\tilde p\tilde q=(p_0q_0-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q)+p_0\mathbf q+q_0\mathbf p+\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$, the multiplication of the algebra, and the general quaternionic bilinear product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=(p_0q_0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q)+p_0\mathbf q-q_0\mathbf p-\mathbf p\times\mathbf q$, the same rule with the first factor conjugated by ${}^{\natural}$. Both are $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear, and their sum and difference separate the scalar part of the first factor from its vector part, $\tilde p\tilde q+\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=2p_0\tilde q$ and $\tilde p\tilde q-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=2\mathbf p\tilde q$. The two multiplication tables coincide on the first row and the diagonal and differ by a sign elsewhere.

The count is two and not four because the general products are read with a conjugation in each slot and the base ring $\mathbb{R}$ has only the identity involution: the second slot of every general product is the identity, only the first-slot choice survives, and the sesquilinear family of the biquaternion chapter, which is conjugate-linear in its second argument, has no counterpart over $\mathbb{R}$. The plain product makes $\mathbb{H}$ an associative division algebra with the two-sided unit $e_0$; the quaternionic product makes $\mathbb{H}$ a left-unital, non-associative quaternionic algebra. The two have the same two idempotents $0$ and $e_0$ and no zero divisor.

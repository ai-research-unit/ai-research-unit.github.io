**The new PDF was a duplicate.** `Vladislav V. Kravchenko - On a quaternionic Maxwell equation for the

**A real error found and fixed: the sign of `Theta_alpha`.** The article had
`Theta_alpha = +e^{i alpha |x|}/(4 pi |x|)`; the source prints the **minus** in both versions, in the sentence
immediately before "`D_alpha K_alpha = delta`". The earlier pass had *noticed* the minus and set it aside as a
layout artefact, having checked identity (11) — which is empty reasoning, because (11) factors `Theta_alpha`
out of `K_alpha` and so reads identically with either sign. The test that discriminates is the kernel's
defining property, and it needs a flux integral, not a pointwise check:

    for a ball around the origin,  int_{|x|=r} n K_alpha dS -> +1.0000  for the source's minus
                                                            -> -1.0000  for the plus

So `D_alpha K_alpha = +delta` only with the minus. It is confirmed independently at `alpha = 0`: the minus
gives `K_0 = -x/(4 pi r^3)`, the Cauchy kernel of `D`, which is what the article's own inhomogeneous-media
section and the dedicated chiral article both give.

**Two content items added.** (i) The source's defining equation `D_alpha K_alpha = delta`, absent until now.
(ii) The residue series **resum to Bessel functions**: `I_1 = i H(t) e^{i a_y t} J_0(2 sqrt(ct))` and
`I_2 = -H(t) sqrt(t/c) e^{i a_y t} J_1(2 sqrt(ct))`, with `c(x) = |x|/(beta^2 sqrt(eps mu))`; verified to
6.1e-13 and 5.1e-13 over 200 random values of `ct`. The previous pass had read the outcome as "not a finite
combination of the customary kernels" and implied the expansion was *asymptotic* — the source does not say
that, it justifies the interchange by uniform convergence, so the series is exact. A false caveat in the
`.context` claiming "the body says so" was withdrawn in place (the word "asymptotic" never appeared in the
article).

**Not recorded, deliberately.** The source's `A(x), B(x), c(x), E(x)` of the "more convenient form" of the
frequency-domain kernel. `B(x)` as printed carries three terms of different length dimension, and the
"convenient form" attaches the essential singularity to one term only, whereas an independent derivation puts
it on the whole bracket (with `E(x)` multiplying both terms). The article records the structure and the Bessel
result, not those normalisations.

**A biquaternion-arithmetic trap worth remembering.** Representing a biquaternion as `(scalar, (x,y,z))` of
Python complex numbers and using `1j` for **both** the scalar imaginary **and** the quaternion units `e_k`
silently conflates them: `(1j)(1j) = -1` folds the scalar imaginary into the units, so a left multiplication
by `e_k` returns `i * grad` instead of `grad`. It is invisible for probe functions that are real with real
coefficients, which is why the `alpha = 0` checks, `D^2 = -Delta` and `D[x/(4 pi r^3)] = 0` all passed while
the sign was wrong. Track `e_k` by position and use `e1 d = (-v1, (s, -v3, v2))` plus cyclic permutations.
The flux test is immune because the normal `n` is real.

### Second review of the same article (same day)

Author: "this is new maths, this is normal to have a few mistakes." Six more defects found and fixed; the
sixth is a new theorem the first pass had missed.

- New theorem worth remembering: **associativity forces the trivial involution.** For a sesquilinear product,
  associativity gives `(xy)(λz) = x(y(λz))`; the second scalar rule evaluates the two sides as `ς(λ)(xy)z`
  and `λ(xy)z`, hence `(ς(λ) − λ)(xy)z = 0` for all arguments. So an associative sesquilinear algebra whose
  products generate a faithful module has `ς = id`. Consequence: a sesquilinear algebra with a nontrivial
  involution is never a disguised associative algebra, and the *derived operation* `x⋆y = xy*` (non-associative
  exactly because `ς ≠ id`) is the only nondegenerate source of examples. This explains why the whole category
  is built on the derived operation rather than on an associative product.
- Over-statements trimmed: the collapse theorem `ς = ς'` needs `A` faithful (counterexample: `R = ℤ`,
  `A = ℤ/2`, scalar 2 annihilates `A`); "the product `IJ` is an ideal" needs associativity, reworded to
  "generate an ideal"; the Introduction claimed a product can be sesquilinear for one involution and bilinear
  for another, which contradicts the collapse theorem.
- A false clause removed: the Hermitian symmetry `{x,y,z}* = {z*,y*,x*}` does **not** reduce to the plain
  symmetry `{x,y,z} = {z,y,x}` at `* = id`; the plain symmetry needs commutativity.
- Trap to remember (same as the first review, restated): inside a derived expression the *ordinary* product is

### The collapse result, written out (same day, fourth pass)

The author asked for the non-associativity consequence to be written as a proper result. It is now a Theorem +
Corollary + Remark in *The Collapse at the Identity* of `sesquilinear-algebras.md`.

**Statement.** With `c_λ = ς(λ) − λ`:
- commutative product ⟹ `c_λ·(A·A) = 0`; if the products generate `A` then `c_λA = 0`;
- associative product ⟹ `c_λ·((A·A)·A) = 0`; with generation **and** no nonzero left annihilator in `A`,
  `c_λA = 0`;
- faithfulness then gives `c_λ = 0`, i.e. `ς = id`.

**Corollary.** A *nondegenerate* sesquilinear algebra (faithful, products generate, no nonzero left
annihilator) with `ς ≠ id` is neither associative nor commutative; with a unit the two extra hypotheses are
automatic.

**Lesson to keep (I made this error twice, at two levels).** From "the scalar kills every product" I twice
upgraded to "the scalar kills all of `A`". The upgrade is valid only using generation (and, in the associative
case, only after the extra left-annihilator step). Concretely, associativity yields `c_λA ⊆ Lann(A)`, not
`c_λA = 0`; the missing hypotheses are exactly a unit or `Lann(A) = 0`. Always ask which hypothesis licenses
each upgrade.

### Second review pass on "Derivations of a Sesquilinear Algebra" - a REAL false proposition

The first draft AND the first review both missed that the scalar action is broken in the twisted case. The
proposition "Sums and Multiples" said lambda.D is a derivation for every lambda in R, so Der(A) is an
R-submodule. FALSE. Because the product is varsigma-semilinear in its SECOND slot:

    (lambda.D)(xy) - [(lambda.D)(x)y + x.(lambda.D)(y)] = (lambda - varsigma(lambda)) x D(y).

So lambda.D is a derivation iff (lambda - varsigma(lambda)) A.D(A) = 0: only the scalars FIXED by varsigma act.
Verified exactly on (M_2(C), x star y = x y*) over (C, conj): lambda = 3 keeps D = ad_a a derivation; lambda =
i, 1+i, -2i do not. The old "proof" quietly used the first product rule (linear) to move lambda past x, where

## Idempotents and roots across the four biquaternion products (author's request)

The author asked whether the idempotents and the square roots of $-1$, $0$, $+1$ differ across the
four products. They do, and the difference is fixed by one thing: **the scalar part of the square**,
which is the diagonal of each product's form. P1 complex bilinear: $P_0^2-(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)$,
the square an element; P2 quaternionic bilinear: $N(\tilde P)e_0$, the square always **central**;
P3 complex sesquilinear: $\sum_\mu\lvert P_\mu\rvert^2\ge 0$, so **no root of $-1$ and no
nonzero nilpotent**; P4 quaternionic sesquilinear: the Krein value
$\lvert P_0\rvert^2-\sum_k\lvert P_k\rvert^2$.

Idempotent sets: P1 $0,e_0,\tfrac12(e_0+\xi i)$ with $\xi^2=-e_0$; P2 $0,e_0$ only; P3 the
Hermitian idempotents $\tfrac12(e_0+i\hat\mu)$; P4 $-\tfrac12e_0+\mu$, $\mu$ real,
$(\mu,\mu)=\tfrac34$. Roots of $-e_0$: P1 $P_0=0$ with $(\mathbf P,\mathbf P)=1$, or
$P_0=\pm i$; P2 $N(\tilde P)=-1$; **P3 none**; P4 includes every real pure $\mu$ with
$(\mu,\mu)=1$.

### Decision rule for the Algebra/Topology boundary (and the one violation)

The one genuine violation of the block boundary is declared by the corpus itself:
`biquaternion-zero-divisors` (Algebra) says in its introduction "It follows *Biquaternion Norm and
Invertibility*" and "The invertibility criterion is assumed from the article on biquaternion norm and

markdown inline links. They appear as Further Reading lines of the form
`- *Article Title* (`articles_maths/<slug>.md`), for ...`. Only 37 files use true `](...)` links.

read out of the introduction prose.

### Verified: the four products do not give four invertibility notions

Script `/tmp/bqver.py` (stdlib only, exact on Gaussian-integer elements):
- Only `P Q` (product 1) has a two-sided identity `e_0`; `P^h Q`, `P* Q` and `P~ Q` have none (so no "inverse"
  can be defined for them at all). Watch out: the identity probe needs genuinely complex test elements, otherwise
  the coefficientwise conjugation looks like it fixes everything.
- The four diagonals are four different quadratics: `P0^2-(v,v)`, `N=P0^2+(v,v)`, `sum|P_mu|^2`, Krein.
- For each of the four products, `c(P) R = e_0` is solvable exactly when `N(P) != 0` (0 disagreements in 4000
  random Gaussian-integer elements). So splitting invertibility by product gives the same set four times.
- The four null sets are different: `Q=(1,1,0,0)` has `P1`-diagonal 0 while `N=2` (invertible), and
  `Q=(1,i,0,0)` has `N=0` (zero divisor) while the `P1`-diagonal is 2. So the complex-bilinear null cone is not
  the zero-divisor set for `Q^2=2Q_0Q`. Conclusion: the four sub-categories each come from one product's
  *diagonal form*, not from four multiplications.

## Sesquialgebras: the last two Part I articles written (2026-09-27)

The final two unwritten entries of `## Sesquialgebras` (Part I) are written; the category has no entry
left carrying a `+` in `maths.md`. `maths.md` changed only by dropping the two leading `+`.

1. `articles_maths/involutions-of-a-sesquilinear-tensor-product.md` (+ `.context`). The tensor involution
   is recalled from `tensor-products-of-sesquialgebras.md` (not re-proved); the new content is the **flip**
   `τ(x⊗y) = y⊗x` on the tensor square, the two anti-involutions `♮` and `♮τ = τ♮`, the commutative group
   `{id, ♮, ♮τ, τ}`, the `n`-fold twist `♮_n` and reversal `ρ_n = π_n ♮_n`, the comparison with
   `involutions-of-the-tensor-algebra.md`, and the worked cases `C⊗_R H` and `B⊗_C B`.
   Traps: (a) `♮ ≠ ♮τ` exactly when `τ ≠ id`, i.e. only for the base itself, so on any tensor square of
   rank ≥ 2 the two anti-involutions are genuinely two; (b) `Fix(♮)` strictly contains the Hermitian
   tensors, witness `E12⊗E21 + E21⊗E12`, which has a nonzero `S⊗S` component. The order-reversing
   permutation is `π_n`, **not** `σ_n` (`σ` is reserved for the algebra involution by the category rule).

2. `articles_maths/examples-of-sesquialgebras.md` (+ `.context`). Reference tabulation in the style of
   `examples-of-algebras.md`: the data table (datum, involution/twist, product, associative, commutative,
   unit, full type), the two lists (genuinely sesquilinear vs collapsed), and the sections on the field,
   the real algebras, the matrices, the endomorphism algebra, the four biquaternion products, the group
   algebra, the zero product and the direct products.
   Traps: (a) the four biquaternion products twist the **first** slot by `♮` (`X^♮Y`, `X^♮Y*`), not the
   second; the consequence is that `♮(X)Y` has a **left** unit and no right one (not unitless), `X^♮Y*`
   has no unit, `XY*` a right unit only; (b) associativity of `R[G]` with `g ↦ g^{-1}` must be tested on

   while the scalar coefficients force `ς = id` and then the elementary abelian two-group case.

Charon: The 303-Myr Antimatter Era

Introduction

This article records the corpus's cosmological history, which is not the standard one. For the first 303 million years the universe is filled with antimatter and no nucleons at all; then, at that instant, nucleons appear in a "bang" with kinetic energies of thousands of GeV. Matter and antimatter are segregated by epoch rather than by a primordial asymmetry. The claim is stated in the corpus's clearest prose, and it is refuted three times over.

The claim, verbatim. RC 084: "la Relativité complexe nous indique que, pendant les premiers 300 millions d'années, l'espace cosmologique était rempli de particules d'anti-matière (anti-nucléons), qui ont progressivement disparu et ont été remplacées par une création continue de matière (nucléons)." RC 083 gives the initial state: antinucleons at $-2{,}8\times10^{-20}$ g cm$^{-3}$, neutrinos at $+2{,}79\times10^{-22}$ g cm$^{-3}$, blackbody temperature 7615 K. RC 084 fixes the date: "$\tau = \tau_b = 303$ millions d'années", when the "nascent" nucleons "possèdent une énergie cinétique de plusieurs milliers de GeV".

The verdict is [FALS]. Three independent observations exclude it: the light-element abundances fix the nucleon population at 1–200 seconds, not 303 Myr; the CMB is a blackbody to $10^{-4}$ and shows no energy injection after recombination; and the galaxy is already a galaxy at 290 Myr, before the bang.

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §8.4, sourced to RC ch. X §§2–3, ch. XI, Esp §VII.13. The corpus's own G-argument that no stars form before 303 Myr is at RC 084. The related articles are P10, P11 and P12; the chain is R10.

Conventions. Gaussian cgs. $\tau$ is the corpus's universal age, $\tau_b = 303$ Myr the date of the bang, $T$ the blackbody temperature, $z$ the redshift. Solar mass $M_{\odot} = 1{,}989\times10^{33}$ g.

Part 1 — The initial state the corpus stipulates

RC 083 states the starting condition with numbers, and they are worth setting out because the corpus's own zero-total-energy requirement produces them.

component density at $\tau = 0$ (g cm$^{-3}$) sign
antinucleons (antiprotons, antineutrons) $2{,}8\times10^{-20}$ negative
neutrinos $2{,}79\times10^{-22}$ positive
blackbody temperature 7615 K —

The densities nearly cancel, and that is the point. The antinucleon rest mass dominates by a factor of a hundred, and the neutrino density is tuned against it so that the total is zero — the corpus's §8.1 condition, imposed on the initial state. So the corpus's antimatter era is not an optional feature: it is what its own zero-total-energy axiom requires the early universe to be.

Then the antimatter leaves. RC 084: the antinucleons "progressivement disparu", replaced by a continuous creation of nucleons. The corpus draws the moral explicitly: "S'il est donc vrai que, aujourd'hui, les particules d'anti-matière sont « rares », c'était par contre les particules de matière qui étaient rares au commencement du monde, et pendant 300 millions d'années." This is the corpus's answer to the matter–antimatter asymmetry: there is no asymmetry, only an era ordering. The observed universe is matter-dominated because matter came second, not because it won.

And the corpus draws a prediction from it. RC 084: "Il est probable que la faible valeur de $G$ pendant les premières centaines de millions d'années de notre Univers a interdit la formation d'étoiles et de galaxies pendant cette période" — the small early $G$ (inventory §7.3) is invoked to explain why nothing forms until the bang. That prediction now has a date and a counter-example (Part 4).

Part 2 — The light elements fix the nucleons at one to two hundred seconds

The decisive measurement is not about antimatter at all. It is about when the nucleons existed.

Big-bang nucleosynthesis runs at 1–200 s, when the temperature is $10^{9}$–$10^{10}$ K and protons and neutrons fuse through deuterium into helium-4, with traces of D, He-3 and Li-7. It is the earliest observationally tested epoch in cosmology, and it is tested by abundance ratios.

quantity observed what it fixes
D/H $2{,}53\times10^{-5}$ the baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta \approx 6{,}1\times10^{-10}$
$Y_{p}$ (He-4 mass fraction) $0{,}247$ the neutron-to-proton ratio at freeze-out
Li-7/H $\approx 1{,}6\times10^{-10}$ (the one discrepant case, and it is a factor of three, not $10^{9}$)

The corpus has no nucleons at that time. Its nucleons appear at 303 Myr, after BBN has long since finished. So deuterium and helium-4 in the corpus's universe have no production site: they cannot be made at 303 Myr, because at 303 Myr the temperature is 150 K (RC 083's own figure), far too cold for fusion, and the "bang" nucleons arrive with kinetic energies of thousands of GeV — they would destroy light nuclei by spallation, not build them. The observed primordial abundances are a photograph of an epoch the corpus's model does not contain.

And the antimatter would have to disappear without a trace. RC 084 says the antinucleons "progressivement disparu". If they annihilated against matter, the energy went into gamma rays; the corpus's §8.1 requires the total to stay zero, so the energy cannot simply vanish. This is Part 3.

Part 3 — The CMB and the gamma-ray background

The CMB is a blackbody to one part in $10^{4}$. COBE/FIRAS measured the spectrum across 0.5–10 mm in 1990 and found deviations from a 2.725 K Planck curve of less than $10^{-4}$; nothing since has changed that. Any energy injected after recombination (380 kyr, $z = 1100$) distorts this spectrum, and the corpus's bang sits at $z \approx 13{,}7$ (or, in its own model, $z \approx 50$), long after.

How large the injection would be. The bang gives every nucleon thousands of GeV of kinetic energy. A nucleon's rest energy is 0.938 GeV, so the injection is of order $10^{3}$ times the baryonic rest energy, i.e. of order $10^{3} \times 10^{80} \times 1{,}5\times10^{-3} \text{ erg} \approx 10^{80}$ erg. The entire CMB contains about $10^{74}$ erg. So the corpus's bang releases roughly a million times the whole energy content of the CMB, at $z \approx 13{,}7$, and leaves the CMB untouched. The measurement excludes it by about six orders of magnitude.

The annihilation adds a second, independent bound. Antimatter disappearing "progressively" through 300 Myr means annihilation distributed across $z \approx 14$ down to today. Annihilation to pions yields a broad gamma spectrum peaking near 70 MeV in the rest frame and, after redshift, filling the MeV range. The measured extragalactic MeV background is extraordinarily low — of order $10^{-6}$ MeV cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$ sr$^{-1}$ — which bounds the fraction of the cosmic mass that can ever have annihilated to something like $10^{-5}$ or less. A model in which antinucleons at $2{,}8\times10^{-20}$ g cm$^{-3}$ disappear is not a $10^{-5}$ perturbation; it is most of the mass budget.

The two bounds are independent and they agree. One counts energy, the other counts photons; both say the antimatter era did not happen.

Part 4 — The galaxy that exists before the bang

The corpus's own prediction is the cleanest test, because it has a date.

Three hundred and three million years after the Big Bang is $z \approx 13{,}7$ in the modern cosmology — the mapping on which the JWST dates below also rest. (The corpus's own model puts the same epoch much earlier in redshift: its quoted temperature at 303 Myr is 150 K against 2,7 K today, i.e. $z \approx 50$, and its own ratio $R_{\text{today}}/R(303\,\text{Myr}) \approx 50$ agrees. Either way the corpus's bang is well after recombination, which is what Part 3 needs.) The JWST JADES survey has now measured galaxies at higher redshift, and one of them settles the question: JADES-GS-z14-0 at $z = 14{,}32$, published in 2024, at 290 Myr after the Big Bang. It is a compact star-forming galaxy with a stellar mass of order $4\times10^{8}$ $M_{\odot}$, i.e. $\approx 8\times10^{41}$ g of processed material, and its spectrum shows the light of hot stars, not the light of an antimatter plasma.

The corpus says nothing can be there. RC 084's argument is explicit: small early $G$ "interdit la formation d'étoiles et de galaxies" for the first hundreds of millions of years, and RC 085 has the creation rate zero at $\tau = 0$ and at the minimum of $M$ at $\tau = 345$ Myr — so at 290 Myr the universe is near its minimum mass, with no nucleons yet made. The observation finds $4\times10^{8}$ solar masses of stars at 290 Myr, thirteen million years before the corpus's bang.

An honest note on the near-coincidence. The corpus's 303 Myr lands within 5 % of the modern epoch of reionisation and first star formation ($z \approx 8$–14). The inventory already flags this as suspicious. It is worth stating precisely what the coincidence is and is not: he put matter creation there, not reionisation, and the object he ruled out for that epoch — a galaxy — is the object the observation found. A near-miss on the date does not rescue a model whose content is inverted.

Summary

The corpus's first 303 Myr are an antimatter era, ending in a bang that creates nucleons with thousands of GeV each. The claim is [FALS] on three independent grounds. BBN at 1–200 s fixes the nucleon population and the light-element abundances, and the corpus has no nucleons then; the bang's energy ($\sim10^{80}$ erg, about $10^{6}$ times the whole CMB) at $z \approx 13{,}7$ would distort the blackbody that COBE/FIRAS measures to $10^{-4}$, and the progressive annihilation would fill the MeV gamma background that is measured to be nearly empty. Bespoke to this model, the corpus predicts no stars or galaxies before 303 Myr, and JADES-GS-z14-0 is a galaxy at 290 Myr — $4\times10^{8}$ $M_{\odot}$ of stars, thirteen million years before the bang. Chain R10 DIES here.

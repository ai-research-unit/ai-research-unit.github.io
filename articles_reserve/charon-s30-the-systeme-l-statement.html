Charon — The Système L: The Statement of the System (S30)

Audits the register's §13.3–§13.4 against Esp 055 §15, Esp 056 and Esp 057–058 (scans 055–058). The statement of the system whose numerical method is S31's, whose constants are S32's, and whose ledger is S34's.

Introduction

The system is present, legible and complete: seventeen numbered equations, L1–L17, with their boundary conditions, their unknown list and their own two integral normalisations — and the normalisations turn out to define the length scale the whole programme runs on. The register's §13.3 is confirmed without qualification: the Système L was never missing. What this article adds is the part nobody had read closely: the counting, the equation-by-equation map to the six constants, and the two normalisation equations, which are not decoration but definitions.

Three structural results, none of them in the register, and one of them new and positive.

(1) L17 is the definition of the elementary length, and it is satisfied by the printed solution to $2{,}6\,\%$. L17 reads $\int_{0}^{T}dt/R^{3} = T/l_{0}^{3}$, that is $l_{0} = \langle R^{-3}\rangle_{t}^{-1/3}$: the elementary length is the geometric mean radius of the lepton's pulsation. With the printed $R_{0} = 7{,}8\times10^{-15}$ cm, $\mathcal{R} = 4{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm and a constant-speed cycle, $l_{0}$ comes out at $1{,}7127\times10^{-14}$ cm against the printed $1{,}7577\times10^{-14}$ cm — $2{,}6\,\%$, which is the size of the corpus's own $\chi_{L} = 2\,\%$. So the elementary length that $\xi$ encodes, taken by the corpus from the nucleon mass and called a "suggestion" (Esp 057), is independently reproduced by the lepton's own geometry. S22's closure gains its equation and a number.

(2) L16, the other normalisation, is satisfied to $5{,}7\,\%$ by the same reading. L16 reads $\int_{0}^{T}dt/R = 2\pi/c$, i.e. the harmonic mean radius is $cT/2\pi = 3{,}00\,R_{0}$ exactly. Both normalisations are satisfied, in the same direction, by the same waveform (§3.4), so the printed solution is what it claims to be, and the two normalisation equations are checkable rather than decorative.

(3) The equation count closes and the unknown count does not. $3n+14 = 20$ at $n = 2$ against a roster of $17$ is not a discrepancy: $3\times1+14 = 17$ exactly, so the printed roster is the $n = 1$ instance and each higher lepton adds three equations. The unknown list is another matter: $2n+15 = 19$ at $n = 2$ against a printed list of $15$ symbols of which two carry the index, giving $17$ — two short, and one short if $k_{S}$ is added, and $k_{S}$ is defined by L13 and named nowhere in the list.

And one negative result, which S31 and S33 complete. The claim that the system "ne dépend en fait d'aucun paramètre" (Esp 056) is contradicted by the system's own numerical method two pages later: Esp 057 §1 begins by giving six numerical values, and Esp 057 §4 says they are chosen "numériquement identique aux valeurs numériques expérimentales". The claim is false as stated; what the system demonstrates is a closure property, and S31 prices it.

What the Corpus Claims

Esp 055 §15, the naming of the system:

Nous disposons maintenant du système complet d'équations et de ses conditions complémentaires et initiales que doit satisfaire le modèle leptonique. Nous appellerons cet ensemble d'équations le Système $L$. Le Système comprend en fait $(3n + 14)$ équations, l'indice $n$ étant associé à l'ensemble $(p+1)$ des leptons chargés qui décrit le Système, avec $n = 1, 2, \dots, p$. Le spin (ou le pulse) de ces leptons est $(n + \frac{1}{2})\,\hbar$, avec conventionnellement $n = 0$ pour l'électron, de masse propre $m$, celle du lepton tau $\tau_1$, le « prochain » lepton lourd $\tau_2$, et ainsi de suite $\tau_n$. Ces $(3n + 14)$ équations doivent permettre le calcul des $(2n + 15)$ quantités suivantes : $R(\tau), c, h, m, \tau_n, l_0, T, \alpha, \Lambda, R_0, \mathcal{R}, k_p, W_{0m}, W_{0\tau_n}, K$.

Esp 056, the claim this article tests:

Ce qu'il y a de tout à fait remarquable dans le Système $L$ est qu'il ne dépend en fait d'aucun paramètre, puisque tous ceux qui figurent dans les équations sont finalement calculés par la solution du système d'équations. Le Système $L$ décrit donc un micro-univers ne reposant, pour sa représentation dans un modèle, que sur des principes de cohérence globale. En ce sens, nous pouvons dire que le Système $L$ représente un bootstrap leptonique, avec le sens qu'a donné Geoffrey Chew au mot « bootstrap » […] La solution du Système $L$ doit permettre le calcul direct de 5 des principales constantes physiques fondamentales, à savoir $c, h, m, \mu$ et $\tau_2$; par ailleurs, comme nous allons le voir au paragraphe 18.2, le Système $L$ conduit également au calcul de la charge électrique élémentaire $e$.

Tags. The system itself is [MATH]: it is a well-posed system of ordinary differential equations with boundary conditions, integral constraints and a discrete parameter, and nothing in it is ambiguous once the transcription items of §13.4 are settled. The claim attached to it is [SPEC], and it is the claim, not the system, that fails.

Scope. The numerical method and the residual are S31's; the six constants one row each are S32's; the input/output ledger and the axiom ledger are S34's; the parameter-freedom claim is S33's and is only stated here. What is audited here is the system: what it contains, what it counts, what it defines, and what each equation returns.

1. The System as Printed

The header. Esp 056 opens the roster with the definitions that make the equations non-dimensional and fix the internal geometry:

$$n = 1, 2, \dots, p \qquad h = 2\pi\hbar \qquad K = -1, 0 \text{ ou } +1 \qquad l_{\nu} = \frac{\pi R}{2}$$

$$\frac{dR}{dt} = R' \qquad \xi = \frac{4}{3}\pi l_{0}^{3}\frac{c}{\hbar} \qquad l = 2R\arctan\!\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)$$

$$d\Omega_{\eta} = 4\pi R^{3}\left(1 + \frac{\eta^{2}}{4}\right)^{-3}\eta^{2}d\eta \qquad v_{\eta}^{2} = R'^{2}\left[\frac{R^{2}}{\left(2R\arctan(\eta/2)\right)^{2}} + \frac{R'^{2}}{c^{2}}\right]^{-1}$$

1.1 One definition worth isolating: $h = 2\pi\hbar$

The header prints $h = 2\pi\hbar$, which is right, and it prints $\xi = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi l_{0}^{3}c/\hbar$ with $\hbar$. S5 Hazard 6 records that L'Esprit elsewhere prints $(\hbar = 2\pi\hbar)$ where $h = 2\pi\hbar$ is meant. In the Système $L$ the two symbols are used correctly: $h$ everywhere in the action terms, $\hbar$ in $\xi$ and in the spin quantum $\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar$ of Esp 055. So the slip is elsewhere in the book and does not touch this system — recorded because a reader arriving from S5's hazard will look for it here.

2. The Counts

2.1 $3n+14 = 20$ and the roster of 17

The arithmetic is exact and the reading is forced. $3\times1 + 14 = 17$, which is the number of equations actually printed as L1–L17; $3\times2 + 14 = 20$, which is the number the corpus states for $n = 1,2$. Therefore the printed roster is the $n = 1$ instance, and every additional lepton adds exactly three equations.

Which three is determined by inspection, and the answer is consistent with everything else in the corpus. The equations that carry the index $n$ explicitly are those whose right-hand side is $\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)h$ or whose integrand carries $\tau_{n}$ or $W_{0\tau_{n}}$:

L10 $\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)h$, $W_{0\tau_{n}}$ one per $n$

So L8, L10 and L12 are the three that multiply, and L9 is the electron's spin equation, which is L10 at $n = 0$ — exactly the corpus's own relation between (223) and (224), which §13.4 tabulated. The count closes with no residue, and the fact that L9's right-hand side is $\frac{1}{2}h$ while L10's is $\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)h$ is the corpus's own signal that the two are one family at two indices. One caveat, recorded for the record and needing nothing: if L9 is instead counted as indexed by $W_{0\tau_{n}}$, the roster would give $21$ at $n = 2$ and the printed $20$ would be one short. The corpus's own (223)/(224) pair and the value $\frac{1}{2}h$ settle it in favour of the reading above.

2.3 Over-determination, and why it matters here

Twenty equations for seventeen to nineteen unknowns is an over-determined system, and the corpus knows it. Esp 057 §3 defines a scalar residual over the six equations that its reduced system drops, and its minimum is what the numerical solution reports. Two consequences follow at once, and they are the reason this section is here rather than in S31.

(1) The system is not solved; it is best fitted, and the residual measures precisely that. So the corpus's word "calculés" (Esp 056: all the parameters "sont finalement calculés par la solution du système") is true of the parameters obtained from L1, L7, L8 and the boundary conditions, and false of the six dropped equations, which are satisfied only to the residual. S31 prices the residual; S32 gives the resulting deviations one row each.

(2) The corpus's own precedent for a redundant system is S24's. In the Système $H$ the corpus prints a second-order equation and its first integral as if they were two independent equations; the redundancy is not an error, it is the structure of a system written down from both ends. Here the redundancy is nameable: L17 is (240), i.e. the definition of $l_{0}$; L8 at $n = 2$ is (244), i.e. the tower formula; L1 is the pulsation equation (200). §5 works each of these out, and the reason it matters is S34's: a system that contains its own definitions cannot derive the quantities they define.

3. The Length Scale Is Defined Inside the System

3.1 L16: the harmonic mean radius

L16 is a statement about the mean of $1/R$, and in radius language it fixes a mean radius. With $R(\tau)$ periodic of period $T$,

$$\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{dt}{R} = \frac{2\pi}{cT} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \left\langle \frac{1}{R}\right\rangle^{-1} = \frac{cT}{2\pi} = 2{,}3380\times10^{-14}\ \text{cm} = 3{,}00\,R_{0}$$

The harmonic mean radius of the pulsation is three times the minimum radius — an exact statement about the printed numbers ($cT/2\pi$ with the printed $T = 4{,}9\times10^{-24}$ s and $R_{0} = 7{,}8\times10^{-15}$ cm is $2{,}9974\,R_{0}$). Written the other way, L16 computes $c$ from the geometry: $c = 2\pi/\int dt/R$, which is why $c$ is in the unknown list at all and why S32's first row is defined rather than measured.

3.2 L17: the elementary length as a geometric mean

L17 is the corpus's (240), and it is the definition of the elementary length. Esp 054 derives it: comparing the density (239) with the definition (47 ter) gives $1/l_{0}^{3} = \frac{1}{T}\int dt/R^{3}$, which is printed again in the roster as L17. So

$$l_{0} = \left\langle R^{-3}\right\rangle_{t}^{-1/3}$$

the elementary length is the geometric mean radius of the pulsation, and the second form $\int dt/R^{3} = 4\pi cT/(3\hbar\xi)$ is an identity, not a second condition: $3\hbar\xi = 4\pi l_{0}^{3}c$ holds to the digit, so $T/l_{0}^{3}$ and $4\pi cT/(3\hbar\xi)$ are the same expression. Checked with the printed values ($T = 4{,}9\times10^{-24}$ s, $\xi = 6{,}467\times10^{-4}$): both give $9{,}0225\times10^{17}$ s cm⁻³, and they are identical by algebra, not by rounding.

This is where the article's positive result comes from. $\xi$ is taken by the corpus from the nucleon mass $M_{0}$ and called a "suggestion" (Esp 057); S22 showed the closure is a fixed point with no external test. L17 is the external test the corpus's own system supplies: given the printed $R_{0}$, $\mathcal{R}$ and $T$, the lepton's geometry determines $l_{0}$, and hence $\xi$, without any reference to $M_{0}$.

3.3 The two moments, computed

The printed $R(\tau)$ is not in the transcription, but three of its data are: $R_{0} = 7{,}8\times10^{-15}$ cm, $\mathcal{R} = 4{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm, $T = 4{,}9\times10^{-24}$ s, and one shape datum — "la vitesse maximum de pulsation $R'$ atteint la valeur $0{,}861\,c$, pour $R = 1{,}79\times10^{-14}$ cm" (Esp 058 §17). From those, the two moments can be bracketed and compared with what L16 and L17 require. The required values, from the printed $T$, $\xi$ and $l_{0}$:

The constant-speed cycle reproduces both normalisations to $6$–$8\,\%$ in the moments and to $2{,}6\,\%$ in $l_{0}$; the sinusoid does not — it is off by $21\,\%$ and by a factor $1{,}96$ respectively. And two further printed data decide between them, in opposite directions — which is itself a result. The corpus's shape datum: a pure sinusoid has its maximum speed at the midpoint of the radius range, $R = 2{,}79\times10^{-14}$ cm, whereas the corpus puts it at $1{,}79\times10^{-14}$ cm. And the corpus's relativistic coefficient: the table of Esp 058 prints $k_{P} = 1{,}36$, and S32 §4 shows that this is exactly the harmonic value for the printed amplitude — $1{,}363$ for $\beta_{\max} = 0{,}861$ — while a constant-speed profile at the same amplitude would give $1{,}966$ and one at $c$ diverges. So the coefficient points to a harmonic pulse and the position of the maximum points away from it. The printed solution's profile is therefore neither idealisation: it must be asymmetric, fast through small $R$ and slower near the maximum — which is the direction both normalisations require (§3.4), and which is why the article brackets the profile instead of choosing one.

For the constant-speed cycle the mean radius has closed forms worth having, because they are what makes the reading checkable:

$$\left\langle\frac{1}{R}\right\rangle = \frac{\ln(\mathcal{R}/R_{0})}{\mathcal{R}-R_{0}}, \qquad \left\langle\frac{1}{R^{3}}\right\rangle = \frac{\mathcal{R}+R_{0}}{2R_{0}^{2}\mathcal{R}^{2}}, \qquad l_{0} = \left(\frac{2R_{0}^{2}\mathcal{R}^{2}}{R_{0}+\mathcal{R}}\right)^{1/3} = 1{,}7127\times10^{-14}\ \text{cm}$$

The third of these is the article's headline number: the elementary length from the lepton's own radii, free of $\xi$, free of $M_{0}$, and $2{,}6\,\%$ below the printed value. The derivation of the closed forms is in the appendix of this section: for a cycle whose speed is constant on each leg, the residence density in $R$ is uniform, so the time-average of any function of $R$ is its average over $[R_{0},\mathcal{R}]$.

3.4 How much of the shape is free, and the bracket that settles it

The constant-speed cycle is an idealisation, so the article does not stop at it. With $|R'| \le 0{,}861\,c$ everywhere (the corpus's own printed maximum) and a single minimum and maximum per period, the residence density $p(R)$ — the fraction of the period spent per unit radius — is bounded below by $2/(Tv_{\max}) = 1{,}5813\times10^{13}$ cm⁻¹, and normalised to $\int p\,dR = 1$. Its floor already consumes $63{,}6\,\%$ of the normalisation, and the remaining weight can be placed anywhere. Maximising or minimising the two moments over that family gives the achievable windows:

moment required achievable window inside?
$\left\langle 1/R\right\rangle$ $4{,}2772\times10^{13}$ $[3{,}63,\,7{,}54]\times10^{13}$ yes
$\left\langle 1/R^{3}\right\rangle$ $1{,}8413\times10^{41}$ $[1{,}30,\,8{,}94]\times10^{41}$ yes

So L16 and L17 are not contradicted by the printed solution — they are constraints on the waveform, and the printed solution can meet them. And the direction is fixed: both required moments are below the constant-speed values, so the true $p(R)$ must carry slightly more weight at large $R$ than a constant-speed cycle does — by $5{,}7\,\%$ in the first moment and $8{,}1\,\%$ in the second. The corpus's own speed datum points the same way: the fastest point is at $1{,}79\times10^{-14}$ cm, well below the midpoint, which says the waveform lingers near the maximum relative to a sine.

What this buys, stated plainly, because it is the point of the section. The two normalisation equations of the Système $L$ are satisfiable and nearly satisfied by the printed solution: on the best available reading they come out at $-5{,}7\,\%$ and $-2{,}6\,\%$ in the quantities they define, which is the corpus's own $\chi_{L} = 2\,\%$ order. The elementary length is not an arbitrary input after all: it is the geometric mean radius of the lepton's pulsation, and the lepton's own radii give it back to a few per cent. S22's repair-list item is therefore answered, and the answer helps the corpus.

Two things are visible in that table before any number is computed, and both are S34's verdict in embryo. (1) Every constant appears on both sides of the map. $c$ is in L16 and in L17 and in L1; $h$ is in L7, L8 and in every action equation; the lepton masses are in L7 and L8 as the quantities named by the right-hand sides. The map is a closure diagram, not a computation tree — which is precisely what the corpus claims for it ("tous ceux qui figurent dans les équations sont finalement calculés par la solution du système") and precisely why the numerical method's seeded start (Esp 057 §1) decides the question. (2) $e$ is not in the map at all. The elementary charge is not returned by any L-equation; it is computed in §18.2 from $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and Esp 057 §1 lists it among the six values given. So the "5 + 1 = 6" of Esp 056 is a statement about §18.2, not about the Système $L$, and S34's reading — one defined, five measured, one suggested, no independent output — is what the map shows.

5. What the System Carries Rather Than Derives

Five redundancies, each of them a place where the system contains a definition rather than an equation. They are listed together because S34's ledger turns on their being definitions.

5.1 L7 and L8 give $\alpha$ and $\beta$ in closed form

Adding L7 and L8 at $n = 1$, and subtracting, and using L16 to evaluate $\int dt/R = 2\pi/c$, gives the two internal constants in closed form in the lepton masses. Using $T = 2h/[c^{2}(\tau-\mu)]$ from L8 at $n = 1,2$:

$$\alpha = \frac{m + 3\mu - 2\tau}{4\pi^{2}} \qquad\qquad \beta = -\frac{h}{4\pi^{3}c}\left(1 - \varepsilon\right), \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\mu - m}{\tau - \mu}$$

Both are exact, and both were verified numerically this pass. With today's measured masses: $\alpha = -1{,}46133\times10^{-25}$ (printed $-1{,}472\times10^{-25}$, $-0{,}7\,\%$) and $\beta = -1{,}66995\times10^{-39}$ (printed $-1{,}7\times10^{-39}$, $-1{,}8\,\%$). With the corpus's own printed masses ($9{,}11\times10^{-28}$, $1{,}884\times10^{-25}$, $3{,}1976\times10^{-24}$): $\alpha = -1{,}47653\times10^{-25}$ ($+0{,}3\,\%$) and $\beta = -1{,}67104\times10^{-39}$ ($-1{,}7\,\%$). The corpus's arithmetic is sound and its rounding is honest; that is the first thing the two closed forms establish. $\beta$'s closed form is S21's and S37's, $\beta = -h(1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3}c)$, reached there from the éon's system; the Système $L$ gives the same expression, which is a real cross-check between the two chapters. The algebra is in §5.1-bis.

§5.1-bis, the derivation, in four lines. $T = 2h/[c^{2}(\tau-\mu)]$ makes $c^{2}T = 2h/(\tau-\mu)$. L7 and L8 at $n = 1$ are $2\pi^{2}c^{2}T\alpha \mp 4\pi^{3}c\beta + h,3h = m,\mu \cdot c^{2}T$. Adding: $4\pi^{2}c^{2}T\alpha + 4h = (m+\mu)c^{2}T$, and substituting $c^{2}T$: $8\pi^{2}h\alpha/(\tau-\mu) = 2h(m+\mu)/(\tau-\mu) - 4h$, giving $\alpha = [(m+\mu) - 2(\tau-\mu)]/(4\pi^{2}) = (m+3\mu-2\tau)/(4\pi^{2})$. Subtracting: $8\pi^{3}c\beta = (\mu-m)c^{2}T - 2h = 2h\varepsilon - 2h$, giving $\beta = -h(1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3}c)$. $\square$

5.2 L8 at $n = 2$ is the tower formula (244)

The same substitution shows that L8 at $n = 2$ is not an independent equation. L8 at $n = 2$ reads $\tau_{2}c^{2}T = 2\pi^{2}c^{2}T\alpha + 4\pi^{3}c\beta + 5h$; inserting the closed forms of §5.1 collapses it to

$$\tau_{2} = \mu + \frac{2h}{c^{2}T}$$

which is (244) at $n = 2$. So the corpus's heavy-lepton formula is inside L8, and evaluating L8 at $n = 2$ tests nothing that (244) has not already fixed. This is the structural form of R7 and P1's result: the tower is not an extra prediction of the system, it is the $n$-slider of the same equation, and the state it puts at $n = 3$ ($3{,}4817$ GeV from the printed $T$, against the $6{,}184\times10^{-24}$ g $= 3{,}467$ GeV the corpus's own table prints) is the one LEP excluded. S43 and P1 own that; here it is only the structure.

6. The Antileptons

The system covers antiparticles by a sign rule, and the rule is exact and cheap to state. Esp 055: the antileptons are obtained "en changeant simultanément les signes des $(n+4)$ quantités" $h, \alpha, \beta, W_{0m}, W_{0\tau_{n}}$, "les masses propres calculées étant alors conventionnellement négatives".

quantity sign reversal effect on the system
$h$ yes the action terms reverse; the quantisation quantum is signed
$\alpha$, $\beta$ yes both densities reverse: this is the corpus's "antiphotons" of (245)
$W_{0m}$, $W_{0\tau_{n}}$ yes the neutrino rest energies reverse
$n+4$ at $n = 1$ $5$ quantities matches the list

Two observations. (1) The rule is a symmetry statement and it is correct as far as it goes: the corpus's mass sign convention is negative for antiparticles (S13's business), and the reversal of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ is what (245)'s $\pm a\theta^{4}$ needs in order to have two photon species. (2) The rule is not checked anywhere in the corpus, and it does not have to be: it is a convention on the sign of a mass whose magnitude the system computes. Recorded as the system's only exact symmetry.

7. The Bootstrap Claim, Stated and Not Settled Here

The claim is Esp 056's, quoted in full at the head of this article, and it has two halves.

(1) "Il ne dépend en fait d'aucun paramètre". This is false as stated, and the corpus's own next page refutes it: Esp 057 §1 begins "Donnons-nous un ensemble de six valeurs numériques" — $h, c, m, \mu, \tau_{2}$ et $e$ — and Esp 057 §4 says the starting set is chosen "numériquement identique aux valeurs numériques expérimentales". Six numbers are put in. The honest form of the claim is the one S34 gives it: not no parameter, but no parameter left once the six seeds are fixed, which is a closure statement about a system of twenty equations.

(2) "Un bootstrap leptonique, avec le sens qu'a donné Geoffrey Chew au mot bootstrap". Chew's bootstrap was a self-consistency program in which the hadrons are the forces that bind them, with no external input; the lepton analogue would need the model's own two internal constants to come out of the model. §5.1 shows that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are closed forms in the measured lepton masses plus $h$ and $c$ — so the internal constants are consequences of the seeds, which is weaker than Chew's bootstrap and stronger than nothing.

S31 reads the numerical method and its honesty; S33 tests the parameter-freedom claim itself; S34 counts the ledger. What this article settles is the input side, and it is a fact about the words on the two pages: the system is stated as parameter-free and started from six measured numbers, in the same chapter.

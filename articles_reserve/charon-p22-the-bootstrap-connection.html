Charon: The Bootstrap Connection

Introduction

This article records the corpus's claim to have demonstrated Chew's bootstrap, and it separates three things that the claim runs together. The corpus says the lepton model was built from four global coherence principles — relativity, homogeneity/isotropy, conservation of momentum-energy, and quantisation (action $nh$ split equally between a spin and a pulse) — "and those alone"; that it is therefore "entirely devoid of arbitrary parameters"; and that it directly computes six fundamental constants: $c$, $h$, $e$, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$.

The verdict is [MIX], and the mixture is exactly three-layered. Chew's S-matrix bootstrap as a programme was abandoned in favour of QCD, so it is [OUT] as a programme. "Determine structure from consistency" was revived and is now mainstream — the conformal bootstrap, the amplitudes/on-shell bootstrap — so the idea is alive. And the corpus's specific claim is [FALS] as stated: the four principles do not determine the lepton model, and the six constants are one defined constant, five measured seeds and one inherited agreement, with no independent output (S34, S41; P1, S45).

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §9.8, sourced to Esp Annexe 8 (scans 115–116). P22 is upstream of S57: Esp 098 makes the corpus's philosophical conclusion "si (et seulement si) le modèle leptonique peut prouver qu'il peut apporter un progrès", and Esp 042 §3 offers the constants claim as that proof — so if P22 fails, S57's subject loses its premise (S57's own verdict is [PHIL]). The related articles are P1, P2, P20 and S45. The register's reference for this article is §9.8; the companion's entry and Esp Annexe 8 are the primary sources.

Conventions. Chew's three bootstrap principles are relativity, unitarity and causality on the $S$-matrix. The corpus's four are relativity, homogeneity/isotropy, conservation, and quantisation. $\alpha, \beta, T_{0}$ are the lepton sector's three "computed" constants; $m, \mu, \tau$ the measured masses.

Part 1 — The four principles do not do the work claimed

The corpus's statement, verbatim. Esp 116: "nous avons pu effectivement vérifier que le modèle leptonique, construit comme un micro-univers obéissant aux 4 principes de cohérence globale précédents, et à eux seulement, était entièrement dépourvu de paramètres arbitraires, et permettait en fait le calcul direct de 6 des constantes fondamentales de la Physique ($c$, $h$, $e$, $m$, $\mu$ et $\tau$)." And: "En ce sens, le modèle leptonique peut être considéré comme démontrant le bien-fondé de l'approche « bootstrap » et constitue ce qu'il faut nommer un bootstrap leptonique."

The direction of implication is the problem. A bootstrap derives structure from consistency: the constraints must be strong enough that only one structure survives. The corpus's four principles are satisfied by every relativistic quantum field theory — by QED, by QCD, by the Standard Model. Special relativity, homogeneity and isotropy, energy–momentum conservation, and the quantisation of action are the minimum requirements of any such theory, not a selection rule. So they cannot single out one lepton model.

And the text shows it. The corpus says it built the model as a micro-universe obeying the four principles and then verified that the result has no free parameters. That is the reverse of a bootstrap: a structure was asserted, and then checked for compatibility. Compatibility is a necessary condition, not a derivation. Chew's principles worked (in their programme) because they were imposed on a specific object — the $S$-matrix — with unitarity and analyticity as severe quantitative constraints. The corpus's four are generic symmetries with no such power.

And the fourth "principle" is where the freedom actually lives. "Le principe de quantification (action $nh$ répartie également entre un spin et un pulse)" is not a coherence principle of the kind the other three are. Equipartition of the action between a spin term and a pulse term is a specific, substantive assumption about the model's dynamics — it is exactly the kind of choice the corpus needs, and exactly the kind of choice the claim of "no arbitrary parameters" excludes. A principle that encodes a model-specific assumption is not a constraint on the model; it is a part of it. So the fourth principle carries the freedom the claim denies, and the phrase "et à eux seulement" does the opposite of what it is asked to do: it certifies that a substantive assumption was made, and calls it coherence.

And the same four principles are claimed for the cosmology. Esp 116's footnote: "ce sont les mêmes principes qui ont été appliqués pour le calcul du modèle cosmologique." So the claim covers the cosmology too, and the cosmology's numerical predictions are the corpus's worst failures — $\Lambda$'s sign (P10), the antimatter era (P9), continuous creation (P11), Axiom 6 (P12), the redshift ceiling (P13). The corpus's own unifying method therefore has a track record, and the record is on the falsified side.

Part 2 — The six constants: five seeds, one agreement, no output

This is where the bootstrap claim is most precisely refutable, and the count is the corpus's own. The four principles are said to permit "le calcul direct de 6 des constantes fondamentales": $c$, $h$, $e$, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$. The ledger, from S45 and rule R1:

constant status
$c$ a defined constant, not computed by the model
$h$ a measured seed
$m$, $\mu$, $\tau$ measured masses, fed in
$e$ a measured seed (S34); the $\alpha$ $2{,}3\,\%$ is the inherited agreement (S35)

So the six are accounted for without a single independent output: one defined constant and five measured seeds (S34), with the $\alpha$ agreement inherited from $\beta$, i.e. from the measured lepton masses (S35). S41's final count is "0 independent outputs". The corpus's own RC 051 states the five inputs and says the Système E provides $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ "en fonction de ces paramètres" — so the corpus is not concealing the count; it is stating the count and then claiming the opposite of it. And S21's closed form settles the matter: $\alpha = (m + 3\mu - 2\tau)/4\pi^{2}$ — no $h$, no $c$, no $\xi$. A constant independent of $h$ and $c$ inside a system built from $h$ and $c$ has been solved for, not derived (P20, §4.8).

And $c$ and $h$ on the list are self-refuting. A theory cannot compute the speed of light and the quantum of action from principles that presuppose special relativity and quantisation. The four principles are built out of $c$ and $h$; the claim that they compute $c$ and $h$ is circular.

And the sector's one hard prediction died with the model. The lepton tower was its only testable consequence, and LEP refused it (P1, P2). So the "no arbitrary parameters" claim yields exactly one testable consequence, and it is false. That is P20's exception — the sector with no freedom failed openly — and it is why the bootstrap claim cannot be defended by pointing to the other numbers: they are the inputs.

Part 3 — The bootstrap's own history, in three parts

Part (a): Chew's programme is [OUT]. The $S$-matrix bootstrap of the 1960s sought to determine the hadron spectrum from unitarity, analyticity and crossing. It was abandoned in favour of QCD, which explained the spectrum through quarks and colour. The register's reason for the [OUT] tag is the historical one: the programme did not deliver, and it was superseded. The corpus's invocation of it in 1983 came a decade after its eclipse, and it cites Chew's three principles without the experimental reason for their failure — the same documentary pattern as the EPR annexe's missing inequality (P16).

Part (b): the idea is alive, and not in the corpus's form. Rattazzi–Rychkov–Tonni–Vichi (2008) revived "determine structure from consistency" as the conformal bootstrap, which computes critical exponents of the 3d Ising model to six-figure precision; the amplitudes/on-shell bootstrap does the same for scattering. Two differences from the corpus's version matter. First, the modern constraints are quantitative and severe — unitarity, crossing, conformal symmetry imposed numerically — not generic symmetries. Second, and decisively, the modern bootstrap computes things like critical exponents and anomalous dimensions; it does not compute $c$, $h$, $e$ or the lepton masses from nothing. The revival supports "consistency is powerful", not "consistency fixes the constants of nature".

Part (c): the corpus's specific claim is [FALS] as stated. Part 1 shows the principles do not determine the model; Part 2 shows the six constants are five seeds and an inherited agreement, with no independent output. Both are needed: a reader could accept that generic principles are weak and still think the output list impressive, or accept the input count and think the principles are the interesting part. Together they close both routes.

Part 4 — The philosophical claim, and the corpus's own proposed test

The corpus draws a strong conclusion from the bootstrap. Esp 116: if principles of global coherence suffice to determine the hadron structure, then observations "sont déterminées dans leur essence par la façon dont notre Esprit raisonne pour regarder le monde — ce qui aurait une portée philosophique considérable". This is the mind-dependence claim that S57 audits as [PHIL]. Its status depends on the premise, and the premise fails (Part 1).

And the corpus proposes itself as the bootstrap's test case, which is the most interesting thing it does here. Esp 116: the lepton universe "peut donc, d'une certaine manière, constituer un « test » pour la philosophie du bootstrap, en démontrant notamment ... un calcul direct, à partir du modèle, d'un certain nombre de constantes physiques fondamentales."

So the corpus sets the terms, and the test returns negative. The demonstration is the constants claim, and the constants are one defined constant, five measured seeds and one inherited agreement (Part 2); the testable consequence is the tower, and it is falsified (P1). The corpus proposed a test of the bootstrap using its own model as the specimen, and the specimen fails. That is the article's most economical closing statement, and it is the corpus's own design.

And the premise it feeds is load-bearing for the philosophy. S57's audit shows Esp 098 conditions the éon identification on the lepton model "prouvant" that it can advance the formulation of the laws of nature, and Esp 042 §3 offers the constants claim as that proof. So P22 is upstream of S57: with the constants claim refuted, the corpus's philosophical conclusion loses the evidence it was given. The epistemology survives — S57's verdict — but the physical premise does not.

Summary

The corpus claims the lepton model was fixed by four global coherence principles alone, is devoid of arbitrary parameters, and directly computes six fundamental constants. The verdict is [MIX], in three layers: Chew's $S$-matrix bootstrap is [OUT] as a programme (abandoned for QCD, a decade before the corpus invoked it); "determine structure from consistency" is alive and successful (the conformal and amplitudes bootstraps); and the corpus's specific claim is [FALS] as stated. The four principles are satisfied by every relativistic quantum field theory, so they cannot select a lepton model — and the direction of the argument is reversed: the corpus asserts a model and verifies its compatibility, which is not a derivation. The fourth "principle", equipartition of action between spin and pulse, is a model-specific assumption carrying exactly the freedom the claim denies. The six constants are one defined constant and five measured seeds ($c$; $h$, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$, $e$), with zero independent outputs and the $\alpha$ agreement inherited from $\beta$ (S34, S35, S41; P20, S45); $c$ and $h$ are presupposed by the very principles said to compute them; and the one testable consequence, the lepton tower, is falsified (P1). The same four principles are claimed for the cosmology, whose numerical predictions are the corpus's worst failures. And the corpus proposed itself as the bootstrap's test case — the result of that test is negative, on the corpus's own terms, and it is the premise S57's philosophy depends on.

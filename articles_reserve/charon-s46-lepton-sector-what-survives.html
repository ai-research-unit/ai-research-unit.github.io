Charon — The Lepton Sector: What Survives (S46)

Closes the lepton block S42–S45 (inventory §4.1–§4.8) and feeds S34's ledger. Sources: RC 050–RC 053, RC 055 §4, RC 057 §1; Esp 050–Esp 052, Esp 058, Esp 059–Esp 060, Esp 087, Esp 116; the register's P1–P3, P4, P9–P10, P21–P23.

Introduction

This article is the lepton sector's close, and it is not a second S34 or S41: S34 counts six constants across the whole programme and S41 counts what the constants block changed; this one states what the lepton sector is, what it hands to whom, and what it is left holding. The four articles S42–S45 have each owned one piece — the identification, the sign convention, the mass equations (218)–(221), and the input/output walk — and what remains is the sector's own boundary: what it owns, what it owes, what it gives away, and the single sentence its correspondence record reduces to.

The sector in one statement. The charged leptons are the éons of the imaginary space — one model of a closed pulsating micro-universe, repeated — with the three measured masses as the only inputs it does not derive; and every number it produces beyond them either restates those masses ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$), or is a feature of the éon's geometry conditional on a seed borrowed from the hadron sector ($\xi$), or is a prediction that experiment has refused.

The closing verdict, stated at the top because everything below supports it: every observable consequence of the lepton sector is falsified, and every surviving part of it is internal. The sector's distinctive observable consequences number exactly three — the tower of charged leptons (P1), the electron's size at the $10^{-14}$ m scale (P3) and the spin ladder (P23) — and all three are dead. What stands is the model's internal coherence, the transfer of the masses into $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and one structural fact about the charge (S42) that the sector neither tests nor needs.

Three things the article does that the four source articles do not. (1) It states the sector's boundary rules, so that no later article quotes a lepton-sector number as though it were a prediction. (2) It runs rule R6 on the sector's rows and records the result in sector form: coherence passes, reproducibility passes for the geometry and fails for the masses, correspondence is empty of its own subject matter — the sector's only contacts with experiment are hand-offs. (3) It collects the sector's own residue, distinguishing it from S41's eight cross-sector open items, so the two lists are not confused.

And one warning, which the sector is uniquely placed to give. The corpus's lepton model is the place where a reader is most likely to mistake internal consistency for physics: the numbers close, the two books agree, and the objects involved — an éon of $8\times10^{-15}$ cm, a pulsation of $4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s, a neutrino orbit $Y = 2{,}27$ — are stated to four figures. None of them is observable, and the register's P21 warning applies directly: a lepton density quoted as a physical density is wrong. The sector's honest metric is the one below.

The sector is one model with three members, and it is worth saying what that buys and what it costs. It buys the exact equality of the three charges without a lepton-specific parameter, and it buys a geometry — turning points, curvature sign, neutrino orbit — that no other part of the corpus supplies. It costs the ability to say why there are three leptons, or why their masses have the ratios they have: the ratios are the input, and S43 shows even the lightest pair's splitting is a difference of two nearly equal large terms, so the model does not compute the ratios either.

The sector contributes the geometry and owns neither the mechanism's test nor its failure.

4. The sector's own residue, kept apart from S41's list

Six open items belong to the sector. S41's eight cross-sector items are a different list, and the article keeps them apart so that neither is read as the other.

  1. The seed is borrowed. $\xi$ (equivalently $l_{0}$) comes from the nucleon closure $l_{0} = R_{H}$, not from the lepton model; S22 owns the closure and S38 shows what its two printed values do to a mass. Every geometric output of the sector is conditional on it.
  2. The primitives are not exhibited. The spin and pulse equations (223), (224), (229), (230) are transcribed with their integrands; the corpus never evaluates them. S43 reduces the mass pair to one integral, $\int_{0}^{T_{0}}dt/R$, which the turning points bound; a future article with the (3-32) solution in hand could check the shape.
  3. Two cells of the numerical table are blank. $W_{0\tau}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{on}}$ in Esp 058 (S41's item 4, confirmed here).
  4. The eccentricity is fitted. $E = 0{,}36$ is chosen to make the charge exact, and RC's own printed $0{,}933$ leaves a $2{,}7\,\%$ gap (RC 055 §4; S41's item 7).
  5. The lightest pair's masses are cancellations. The electron's mass is one part in $3{,}2\times10^{3}$ of its own terms and the $\mu$–$e$ splitting one part in $16$; the corpus carries three figures (S43).
  6. The sign convention is unobservable. The matter/antimatter symmetry of (3-32) is exact and the corpus breaks it by stipulation (S44); the claim can never be tested as stated.

Only item 3 and item 4 are on S41's list. Items 1, 2, 5 and 6 are the sector's own, and none of them was recorded before this block.

5. What the sector does not touch

The boundary matters as much as the content, because the lepton model is the corpus's most quotable part.

  • The hadron sector is not the sector's. The holon, the nucleon mass and the strong range belong to S24–S27; the sector supplies the éon that a holon carries (S25) and the closure that fixes $\xi$ (S22). It does not predict the nucleon mass, and S38 owns what that computation is worth.
  • The cosmology is not the sector's. The corpus uses "antimatter" in two senses — a sign of proper mass at a point and a sign of density in the cosmology (S44 §5, P10); the sector owns the first and nothing else.
  • The charge's value is not the sector's. R1's chain owns it, and S34 and S41 record that its agreement is inherited. The sector owns only the equality of the three charges, which S42 shows is structural.
  • The mind material is untouched entirely. Whatever survives of the corpus's entropic-observer programme (P12–P19) is unrelated to the éon's numbers.

6. Verdict

What survives in the lepton sector. One éon model with no lepton-specific parameter, correctly reproducing the exact equality of the three charged leptons' charges (S42); a mass pair that closes on the corpus's own constants, with its branch assignment forced and its constants identical to (3-68)'s (S43); a conditional geometry — turning points, negative curvature, the shared neutrino orbit, and the mean inverse radius $\langle R \rangle = 2{,}35\times10^{-14}$ cm — that recomputes (S43, S45); a sign convention that is consistent, load-bearing once, and unobservable (S44); and a ledger in which the sector's inputs are honest and its own freedom is zero (S45).

What does not survive. The tower above the tau (P1, P2), the electron's $10^{-14}$ m size (P3), the spin ladder (P23), the six density levels as quark flavours (P4, P21), and the claim that the sector's numbers are computed rather than inverted (S45). Exactly three distinctive observable claims, and all three are dead.

The sector's final sentence, and the one to quote. The lepton model is the corpus's most constrained construction — one object, repeated, with the measured masses as its only freedom — and that is why its one hard prediction stayed hard and was refused. Its coherence is real, its geometry is invisible, and its correspondence is empty; per rule R6 nothing in the first two may be read as the third.

Summary

The lepton block S42–S45 is closed. The sector is one model — a closed pulsating micro-universe, repeated — with the three measured lepton masses as its only inputs and no parameter specific to a lepton.

What it owns: the identification of the charged leptons with the éons (S42); the two-branch mass equations (220)/(221) (S43); the sign convention that makes the electron the antimatter member (S44); the input/output walk that returns seven inputs, four restatements, one conditional geometric output set, two numbers never derived and zero lepton-specific parameters (S45); and the falsified tower (P1–P3).

What it hands out, and the asymmetry that is the sector's story: the measured masses become $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and $\alpha$'s $2\,\%$ agreement is inherited from the spectrum through $\beta$ (S35, S37); the saturation geometry stands as a mechanism for fractional charge while its quark reading dies (S47/S48, P4); the six density levels die twice over (P4 and P21); and the tower, the spin ladder and the electron's size are all falsified (P1, P23, P3). Every hand-off in which the sector makes a claim of its own is dead.

Rule R6, on the sector's rows: coherence passes in unusual measure — the mass pair and the inversion (3-68) are one constraint (S43); reproducibility passes for the geometry, which recomputes from $\xi$ and the masses, and fails for the masses themselves, with the one apparent mass computation a cancellation of one part in $3{,}2\times10^{3}$ (S43); correspondence is empty of the sector's own subject matter — three distinctive observable claims, all falsified, and one inherited comparison that belongs to R1.

And the sector's residue, kept apart from S41's eight cross-sector items: a borrowed seed ($\xi$ from $l_{0} = R_{H}$); unexhibited primitives (223)/(224)/(229)/(230); two blank cells in the 058 table; a fitted eccentricity ($0{,}36$ against the printed $0{,}933$, a $2{,}7\,\%$ gap); the lightest pair's masses as cancellations; and an unobservable sign convention.

The sector's final sentence: the lepton model is the corpus's most constrained construction — one object, repeated, with the measured masses as its only freedom — and that is exactly why its one hard prediction was refused.

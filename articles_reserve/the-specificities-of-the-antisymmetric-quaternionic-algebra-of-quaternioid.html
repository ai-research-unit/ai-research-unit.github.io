The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The antisymmetric quaternionic algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ is the quaternionic product $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions. This article states what singles the block out among the six products of the space: the identities it satisfies and those it fails, the classes its laws empty, and the place it takes in the law table of Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

The block is alternating like its plain companion, and unlike it, it fails the Jacobi identity; the two facts together isolate it, since it is the only alternating product of the six that is not a Lie bracket. This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The two share the space, the addition and the real scalars, but $\diamond$ is a different multiplication, alternating, without a unit and without the Jacobi identity. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x^2=\vec e_y^2=\vec e_z^2=-e_t$; a generic element is $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$, the centre is $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_t$ and the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\vec q\}$. The Two-Line Criterion

The Alternation and the Vanishing Square

Theorem. The block is alternating,

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0\qquad\text{for every }\tilde p, $$

so every element is square-zero, and no element has an inverse in the block's sense.

Proof. The alternation is $\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde p-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde p)=0$, the two terms of the difference coinciding; the square is $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$; an inverse requires a two-sided unit, which the block does not have.

The Jacobi Failure

Theorem (the failure, with witness). The block does not satisfy the Jacobi identity. Its cyclic sum has the closed form

$$ J(\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r)=-p_0\,(\vec q\times\vec r)+q_t\,(\vec p\times\vec r)-r_0\,(\vec p\times\vec q), $$

and the witness $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$ gives

$$ J(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)=(e_t\diamond \vec e_x)\diamond \vec e_y+(\vec e_x\diamond \vec e_y)\diamond e_t+(\vec e_y\diamond e_t)\diamond \vec e_x=-\vec e_z\neq0 . $$

The block is therefore not a Lie algebra, and the failure is exactly the associator defect of the parent quaternionic product: $J=\tfrac14\sum_\sigma\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{assoc}(\tilde p_\sigma,\tilde q_\sigma,\tilde r_\sigma)$, the associator being that of the product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$. The full reading is The Jacobi Failure and the Associator Defect of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category.

Proof. Inserting the explicit form into the three terms gives the closed form, which is linear in the scalar triple with the three cross products as coefficients; at $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$ only the first term survives and equals $-(\vec e_x\times \vec e_y)=-\vec e_z$. The associator identity is the criterion of The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product read on the parent product, verified on general elements.

Remark (the contrast with the comparator). The antisymmetric plain block of the same space is alternating and satisfies the identities its alternation implies, the Jacobi identity included; the antisymmetric quaternionic block shares the alternation and differs in the failure. This single failure is the whole difference between the two antisymmetric blocks, and it is why the plain block is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and this one is not.

The Absence of a Unit, of an Inverse and of a Norm

Theorem. The block has no two-sided unit and no one-sided unit; hence it has no inverse, its group of units is empty, and it carries no algebraic norm of the block.

Proof. Every value is pure, whereas $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tilde p$ at a non-pure $\tilde p$ would be a non-pure value, and the scalar direction is not reached, $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q$ having zero scalar part; the candidate $e_t$ gives $e_t\diamond\tilde q=\vec q$, the projection, which is $\tilde q$ only on the vector subspace. No element is a unit on either side. The algebraic norm of a product is a quadratic form multiplicative for the product; the diagonal form of the block is the vanishing function $\tilde q\diamond\tilde q=0$, so its own algebraic norm is the zero form.

Remark (the contrast with the quaternion norm). The parent quaternionic product has the multiplicative norm $N(\tilde q)=\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde q$ and the block does not inherit it: the square map sees only the symmetric half, which collapses onto the scalar line, and the antisymmetric half carries neither a square nor a norm. The symmetric half is The Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions.

The Folded Element Classes

The Idempotents

Theorem. The idempotents of the block are only $0$,

$$ \tilde\pi\diamond\tilde\pi=\tilde\pi\iff\tilde\pi=0 . $$

Proof. The square is zero for every element, so the equation is $\tilde\pi=0$. The idempotent theory into this article.

The Nilpotents

Theorem. The nilpotents of the block are the whole space: the origin is the trivial nilpotent of index one, and every other element is a nilpotent of index two,

$$ \tilde\nu\diamond\tilde\nu=0\qquad\text{for every }\tilde\nu,\qquad \tilde\nu\neq0\implies\tilde\nu\ \text{is of index }2 . $$

The nonzero nilpotents are the punctured space. The class is the whole space and not the vector subspace: the block's square vanishes on every element, the scalar direction included, since $e_t\diamond e_t=0$.

Proof. The square vanishes identically, so every element is square-zero; the index is two for a nonzero element and one for the origin. The reading is the one of Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, where the class of nonzero nilpotents is the whole space for the two antisymmetric structures.

Remark (what isolates the block). The block is the only structure of the six that is alternating and fails the Jacobi identity. It is one of the two alternating structures, the other being the antisymmetric plain one, which satisfies the identity; and among the six it is the only one whose alternation is not balanced by the Lie law. The block is therefore the second of the two antisymmetric structures in the order of the catalogue, separated from its companion by the Jacobi identity alone.

The Boundary of the Criterion

Proposition. The criterion of isolation is exactly the pair (alternating, Jacobi fails). Neither property alone isolates the block: the antisymmetric plain structure is alternating and satisfies Jacobi, and the general quaternionic structure fails Jacobi without being alternating. The criterion does not reach the element theory, which is read from the vanishing square and is common to the two antisymmetric blocks.

Proof. The first failure is the law table's Jacobi row, whose vanishing for $\mathrm{AQA}$ is the witness $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$; the second is the alternation row. The element theory needs only $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$ and is identical in the two alternating structures.

Summary

The antisymmetric quaternionic block is alternating, $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$, and fails the Jacobi identity, with the closed form $J=-p_0(\vec q\times\vec r)+q_t(\vec p\times\vec r)-r_0(\vec p\times\vec q)$ and the witness $J(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)=-\vec e_z$; it is therefore not a Lie algebra, and this failure is exactly the associator defect of the parent quaternionic product, $J=\tfrac14\sum_\sigma\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{assoc}$. The block is the only structure of the six that is alternating and not Lie, and this pair isolates it from its plain companion, which satisfies the identity. It has no unit on either side, hence an empty group of units and no algebraic norm of its own, the diagonal form of the operation being the zero function and the square map seeing only the symmetric half. Its idempotents reduce to $0$ and its nilpotents are the whole space, the class being folded here; both are shared with the antisymmetric plain block, whose Jacobi identity holds.

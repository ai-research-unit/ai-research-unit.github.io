Charon — The Spin Sign: Electron as Antimatter (S44)

Audits inventory §4.5 (the electron has spin $-1/2$, treated as antimatter; the $\mu^{-}$ has spin $+1/2$). Sources: RC 046 §V, eqs (3-31)–(3-32); RC 047 §VI.1, (3-50)–(3-53); RC 048, (3-52) and its Nota; RC 057 §1; Esp 052 §11; Esp 094 §10.

Introduction

The register keeps this item in Part S as the one inventory tag it cannot falsify — [OUT], "an idiosyncratic sign convention", "asserts nothing falsifiable" — and the article confirms the tag while supplying the reason the register only gestures at: the symmetry that would separate matter from antimatter in this formalism is exact, so the distinction is a naming, and the corpus then breaks the symmetry by an explicit stipulation. The equation that carries the mass, (3-32), contains $\alpha$ and $\beta$ only through their squares and $\xi$ only through $\xi^{2}$, so the simultaneous replacement $(\alpha, \beta, h) \to (-\alpha, -\beta, -h)$ leaves the equation — and every condition attached to it — identically unchanged. What changes is the sign of the mass, through (3-51): matter is defined as $m_{n} > 0$ and antimatter as $m_{n} < 0$ (RC 047), so the "anti-solution" is the same solution with the labels exchanged. Nothing in the formalism prefers one labelling.

The corpus then makes the labelling definite by fiat, and this is the article's central quotation. At (3-52) it writes

nous conviendrons de conserver à la constante $\beta$ son signe négatif, même pour $n = 0$

— a convention, stated as such ("nous conviendrons") — and in the Nota that follows, the electron's observed negative charge is read through it:

L'électron, qui est observé expérimentalement comme possédant une charge électrique négative, devrait donc selon le formalisme de la Relativité complexe être classé parmi les particules d'antimatière ($m_{0} < 0$).

So the chain is: exact sign symmetry → a stipulation that $\beta < 0$ → the $n = 0$ matter state is the positron → the electron is antimatter → its action, hence its spin, is $-1/2$ (RC 057 §1). Every link after the first is a naming, and the corpus's own word for the consequence is "ceci n'a guère d'importance en Physique « pré-complexe »".

Three findings beyond the tag, and each is stated separately because they have different statuses.

(1) The article separates the sign from the ladder, which the register merges. The spin sign is unobservable — it is a choice of which projection is called positive, and the corpus attaches no magnetic moment to it. The spin ladder is not a sign convention: RC 047 and Esp 052 assign spin $1/2, 3/2, 5/2$ to $n = 0, 1, 2$, and the tau is measured to have spin $1/2$. That is a falsification (P23, registered under the spin ladder), and it does not touch the sign.

(2) The stipulation is not idle: it decides which branch the rest of the lepton sector runs on. S43 shows the mass equations (220)/(221) cannot be extended jointly, and that the electron's branch is the corpus's antimatter member. So §4.5's convention is load-bearing in exactly one place — it fixes the matter ladder's starting point at the muon — and unobservable in all places.

(3) The corpus uses "antimatter" in two senses, and the article records both without adjudicating between them. In chapter III the word means the sign of a proper mass at a real point $P$; in the cosmology it means the negative sign of a density ("le signe $-$ indiquant qu'il s'agit d'une densité de type antimatière", Esp 094 §10, for $\rho_{a} \simeq -7\times10^{-32}$ g/cm$^{3}$). The two senses are not the same statement, and the corpus does not connect them. The Nota's cosmological aside — "nos atomes possèdent des « traces » d'antimatière, leurs électrons" — is the only place a bridge is offered, and it is one sentence.

Verdict in one line, per rule R5: §4.5's tag [OUT] is confirmed on a sharper ground than "idiosyncratic" — the formalism's own matter/antimatter symmetry is exact, the electron's classification is a stipulation, and the claim therefore carries no falsifiable content; the falsifiable content in the neighbourhood belongs to the spin ladder, which dies separately.

What the Corpus Claims

The sign symmetry, RC 047 §VI.1, remark 1:

$\alpha$, $\beta$ et $h$ (ce dernier associé à $\xi = \Omega_{0}c/\hbar$), qui figurent dans l'équation fondamentale (3-32) par l'intermédiaire de leurs carrés, sont définis seulement au signe près. […] il existera une autre solution (que nous nommerons l'anti-solution) qui sera simplement obtenue en changeant simultanément les signes des trois quantités $\alpha$, $\beta$ et $h$ (ou $\xi$). Cette anti-solution correspond, d'après (3-31), à un changement de signe de la masse propre $m_{n}$. On reconnaît naturellement ici la distinction entre matière ($m_{n}$ positif) et antimatière ($m_{n}$ négatif).

The stipulation and its consequence, RC 048, (3-52) and Nota:

nous conviendrons de conserver à la constante $\beta$ son signe négatif, même pour $n = 0$, mais d'écrire pour ce cas l'équation de conservation (3-51) sous la forme : $$\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T} 2\pi^{2}c^{2}\left(\alpha - \frac{\beta}{R}\right)d\tau + \frac{h}{T} = m_{0}c^{2}$$

Nota : Le cas $n = 0$ nous indique que la structure particulière de masse propre la plus faible et positive (matière) est de charge électrique positive ($\beta > 0$ dans 3-51), et correspond donc au positron.

The spin statement, RC 057 §1:

Notons que l'électron possède, en Relativité complexe, un spin $-1/2$ (antimatière, § III. 3-2), et le $\mu^{-}$ un spin $+1/2$ (matière).

And the spin ladder it sits on, RC 047 §VI.1 remark 2 and Esp 052 §11:

la relation (3-51) suppose que le neutrino contenu dans le micro-univers Imaginaire peut posséder dans cet espace […] des états de spin plus grands que $1/2$, à savoir : $1/2$ pour $n = 0$, $3/2$ pour $n = 1$, $5/2$ pour $n = 2\ldots$

1. The symmetry is exact, and this is checkable without numbers

The fundamental equation (3-32), as transcribed from RC 044:

$$\frac{R'^{2}}{c^{2}} = \frac{1}{6}\frac{\xi^{2}}{R^{2}}\left[\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{3}{R_{0}^{2}} - \frac{1}{R^{2}}\right) - \frac{2\beta^{2}}{R_{0}^{4}}\right] + K + \frac{1}{3}\Lambda R^{2}$$

Read the parity of each factor under $(\alpha, \beta, h) \to (-\alpha, -\beta, -h)$. $\alpha^{2}$ and $\beta^{2}$ are invariant. $\xi = \Omega_{0}c/\hbar$: $\Omega_{0} = \frac{4}{3}\pi l_{0}^{3}$ is a positive volume, $c$ is fixed, so $h \to -h$ gives $\xi \to -\xi$ and $\xi^{2}$ is invariant. $K$, $\Lambda$, $R_{0}$, $R$, $R'$ are untouched, and the existence condition (3-69-2), $\Lambda = (\xi^{2}/R_{0}^{6})(\beta^{2}/R_{0}^{2} - \alpha^{2}) - 3/R_{0}^{2}$, is built from $\xi^{2}$, $\alpha^{2}$ and $\beta^{2}$ alone. So the transformation is an exact symmetry of (3-32) and of every existence and initial condition attached to it: the "anti-solution" is the same trajectory $R(\tau)$, not a second one.

Only the mass relation moves, and it moves by a sign. (3-51) reads $\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}2\pi^{2}c^{2}(\alpha + \frac{\beta}{R})d\tau + 2(n+\frac{1}{2})\frac{h}{T} = m_{n}c^{2}$. Under the replacement, the integral flips sign (its integrand does) and the kinetic term flips sign ($h \to -h$), so the whole left-hand side flips and $m_{n} \to -m_{n}$. That is the entire content of the corpus's matter/antimatter distinction: a sign that the formalism cannot choose. The same flip takes the spin's sign, because the spin is the action $(n+\frac{1}{2})h$ divided by $2\pi$ — which is precisely why the corpus says the electron, being antimatter, has spin $-1/2$: the two statements are the same statement.

Verified structurally, as a statement about (3-32)'s parity, with no numerical input.

2. Where the corpus breaks the symmetry: a stipulation

An exact symmetry is broken by adding a condition, and the corpus's condition is that $\beta$ keep its negative sign. RC 048 says so in the first person plural and calls it a convention: "nous conviendrons de conserver à la constante $\beta$ son signe négatif, même pour $n = 0$". That single stipulation makes the $n = 0$ matter state the one with $\beta > 0$ — the positron — and pushes the electron into the antimatter class.

Note what kind of statement this is. It is not read off experiment, and it is not derived: it is chosen, in order to fix the sign that (3-32) leaves free. The corpus's own subsequent sentence is the honest reading of it — "Ceci n'a guère d'importance en Physique « pré-complexe »" — and it is right about that: for a physics in which mass is positive by definition, the classification is not observable. The article's addition is that "pré-complexe" is doing more work than the corpus admits: the classification is not observable in complex physics either, because the operation that produces it is an exact symmetry of the equations. A symmetry of the equations cannot, by itself, distinguish two states of the world; it distinguishes two names.

3. The sign, the ladder, and the one place the convention bites

The register's §4.5 entry and P23's spin ladder are two different claims and must be graded separately.

claim content status
the electron's spin sign $-1/2$, from the anti-solution [OUT]: unobservable, follows from a stipulation
the spin magnitude the éon neutrino's action is quantised as $(n+\frac{1}{2})\hbar$ [EST] for half-integer spin: every charged lepton is a fermion — correct
the spin ladder $n = 0, 1, 2$ → spin $1/2, 3/2, 5/2$ [FALS]: the tau is measured to have spin $1/2$ (P23)

The ladder is the corpus's, not the register's, and it dies on data. RC 047 §VI.1 remark 2 and Esp 052 §11 both say the higher-$n$ leptons carry higher spin; the tau carries spin $1/2$. So the sector contains a falsified prediction that is not the sign convention — which is why the article refuses to grade the two together: the register's [OUT] is about the sign and survives; P23's falsification is about the ladder and stands.

And the convention does bite in one place, which the register does not mention. S43 (§5) shows that the mass equations (220)/(221) cannot be extended jointly, and that the extension uses the muon's branch. The muon is the first matter lepton, and the electron is the antimatter member — so §4.5's stipulation is exactly what makes the matter ladder start at the muon. The corpus's own reason at Esp 052 §11 is empirical ("l'équation (221) qui permet la transition $\tau \to \mu$ expérimentalement"), and the two reasons agree. So the [OUT] item is load-bearing in one place inside the corpus and unobservable in every place outside it — which is a fair description of a convention that has been used consistently.

4. Where a spin sign would become physics, and why it does not here

A sign of spin becomes observable when the spin is coupled to something with a fixed direction, and the standard coupling is the magnetic moment. If "spin $-1/2$" were read as flipping the electron's magnetic moment relative to its spin, the statement $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{e} = -g\,(e/2m_{e})\,\mathbf{S}$ would have its sign reversed — an observable difference, and one that g−2 measurements and atomic spectroscopy settle by construction. The corpus never writes a magnetic moment, for any particle. Its spin is an action, not a moment, and the action's sign enters only through the mass equation and the interaction's energy-momentum bookkeeping.

The second place a spin sign becomes observable is parity, and the corpus does not go there either. A spin is a pseudovector: its components reverse under spatial reflection, so "spin $-1/2$" has no invariant meaning unless an axis and a parity convention are fixed. The corpus fixes neither, and the charge sign — which is well defined — is carried elsewhere, by $\beta$ through the blackbody density $D_{E} = \pm\beta c^{2}/R^{4}$ (RC 053 (3-80)), not by the spin. So the sign convention's only real content is which of two exact mirror states we agree to call the particle, and the corpus says so.

5. Two senses of "antimatter", recorded and not merged

In chapter III, antimatter is a sign of proper mass at a real point: $m_{n} < 0$, produced by the anti-solution. In the cosmology, antimatter is a sign of density: Esp 094 §10 quotes the cosmic "vacuum" density $\rho_{a} \simeq -7\times10^{-32}$ g/cm$^{3}$ ("le signe $-$ indiquant qu'il s'agit d'une densité de type antimatière"), and the register's P10 records the claim that the vacuum is a form of antimatter. These are two different uses of one word, in two parts of the same construction, and the corpus does not say how they relate.

The Nota's one-sentence bridge is the only connection offered: "cela mérite sans aucun doute d'être noté, si on pense par exemple à la présence de l'antimatière dans l'Univers dans son ensemble : nos atomes possèdent des « traces » d'antimatière, leurs électrons." The article records the sentence as the corpus's own observation. It states no conflict and derives no consequence: reading it as a statement about the cosmic antimatter budget would require identifying two senses the corpus keeps separate, and the epoch structure of the cosmology (P9) has no place for a permanently present component. A bridge the corpus does not build is not a claim the register may grade.

6. Verdict

Rule R5, applied: §4.5's tag [OUT] stands, and its reason is now stated precisely. The electron's classification as antimatter, and therefore its spin sign, rest on a stipulation that fixes a sign the formalism leaves exactly free — (3-32) is invariant under $(\alpha, \beta, h) \to (-\alpha, -\beta, -h)$, the mass flips sign, and nothing distinguishes the two labellings. The claim therefore asserts nothing falsifiable, which is what the register says, and the article's addition is why: it is not idiosyncrasy but the exactness of the symmetry.

Three qualifications travel with the tag. (1) The spin ladder is a different claim and is falsified (P23): all charged leptons have spin $1/2$, and the corpus's $1/2, 3/2, 5/2$ assignment for $n = 0, 1, 2$ dies there. (2) The convention is used consistently and has one internal consequence — it starts the matter ladder at the muon (S43 §5). (3) A sign of spin would become physics through the magnetic moment or a parity convention; the corpus writes neither, so the sign stays a naming.

The register should carry the item with its relation to P23 stated, since the two are otherwise read as one claim about "the electron's spin".

Summary

The inventory's §4.5 — the electron has spin $-1/2$ and is treated as antimatter, the $\mu^{-}$ has spin $+1/2$ — is the one [OUT] item the register keeps in Part S, on the ground that it asserts nothing falsifiable. The article confirms the tag and supplies the reason: the symmetry that would distinguish matter from antimatter in Charon's imaginary-sector formalism is exact.

First, the symmetry is exact and checkable without numbers. The fundamental equation (3-32) carries $\alpha$ and $\beta$ only as $\alpha^{2}$ and $\beta^{2}$, and $\xi$ — which absorbs $h$ — only as $\xi^{2}$; the replacement $(\alpha, \beta, h) \to (-\alpha, -\beta, -h)$ therefore leaves (3-32), the existence condition (3-69-2) and the initial condition (3-31) identically unchanged. The "anti-solution" with $m_{n} \to -m_{n}$ is the same trajectory with the labels exchanged, so matter/antimatter here is a naming.

Second, the corpus breaks the symmetry by stipulation, and says so. RC 048 states "nous conviendrons de conserver à la constante $\beta$ son signe négatif, même pour $n = 0$", which makes the $n = 0$ matter state the positron and the electron an antimatter member; the electron's spin $-1/2$ (RC 057 §1) follows because the spin is the signed action. The corpus's own comment — "ceci n'a guère d'importance" — is the honest reading, and the article strengthens it: the classification is unobservable even inside the formalism.

Third, the sign and the ladder must be graded separately, and the convenience of the convention is real. The spin ladder ($1/2, 3/2, 5/2$ for $n = 0, 1, 2$) is a falsifiable claim and is falsified — the tau has spin $1/2$ (P23). The spin sign is not falsifiable and cannot be turned into physics here, because the corpus writes no magnetic moment and fixes no parity convention. And the stipulation is load-bearing in one place: it makes the muon the starting point of the matter ladder, which is what allows the mass equations to be extended at all (S43 §5).

Two senses of "antimatter" coexist in the corpus — a sign of proper mass at a point (chapter III) and a sign of density in the cosmology (Esp 094, P10) — and the article records both without merging them.

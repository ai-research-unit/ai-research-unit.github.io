Charon — The Nucleon Mass Minimum (S27)

Audits §5.4 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md, against Esp §VIII.5–6 (scans 087–091) and RC ch. IV §I–II (scans 060–062).

Introduction

The number reproduces to a quarter of a per cent, and the article's job is the sentence that follows it: how much freedom did that take? The register's verdict is "STANDS, weakly" and its brief is "redo the sweep; report the freedom honestly". The answer: one free parameter, swept, whose value is then selected by a minimum — exactly the pattern P20 names, "sweep a parameter, select a minimum, report a hit" — and a nucleon mass that is nonetheless within $1\,\%$ of the printed value across a factor $2{,}7$ in that parameter. So the number does not depend on where in the sweep the minimum is found; it depends on there being a minimum at all, which the equations supply.

And there is a second freedom the register does not mention, which turns out to be the interesting one: which value of $\xi$ the printed number used. The corpus prints two, $6{,}467\times10^{-4}$ (1983) and $6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ (1987). Recomputed, all three of the 1983 book's printed masses require $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ and all three of its printed densities require $\xi = 6{,}467\times10^{-4}$: the same book's printed mass curve and printed density axis are $5\,\%$ apart, consistently, across the whole curve. With the 1987 value the minimum is $1{,}6766\times10^{-24}$ g against the measured proton mass $1{,}6726\times10^{-24}$ g — $+0{,}24\,\%$; with the 1983 book's own printed value it is $1{,}7629\times10^{-24}$ g — $+5{,}4\,\%$. So the hit depends on which of the corpus's two constants you believe, and the printed numbers themselves say which one the corpus's computer used.

One structural point is new and worth having: the minimum is not chosen, it is selected by the corpus's own lifetime argument. Esp (352)–(353) bound the mass-loss rate by $|dM/dt| \leq c\,(dM/dd_{0})(dd_{0}/dR)$, which vanishes at every extremum of $M(d_{0})$ — so the observable states are the extrema of the mass curve, and the curve has exactly two: the minimum, which the corpus calls the nucleon, and the maximum. That also resolves the "two stationary states" sentence S24 flagged: Esp 089 §6.2 exhibits the graph for exactly those two.

What the Corpus Claims

§5.4 — nucleon mass from the minimum of $M(d_{0})$: $M_{\min} = 1{,}68\times10^{-24}$ g, for $0{,}11 \le \omega \le 0{,}13$. Esp §VIII.6.1.2. Tag: [MIX] (fit-like success). The register: "The measured proton mass is $1{,}6726\times10^{-24}$ g. The agreement is excellent. But $d_{0}$ and $\omega$ are swept and a minimum is selected, so this is a 'prediction' with a free sweep parameter. Modern nucleon mass comes from QCD + chiral symmetry breaking; the mechanism here is not QCD."

The corpus's own chain, verbatim. Esp 087's Axiom 7:

Pour qu'un hadron soit observable, c'est-à-dire possède une durée de vie non nulle, il faut que sa pulsation propre $\omega_{0}$ soit en résonance avec la pulsation propre $\omega_{0}$ du modèle leptonique chargé : $\omega_{0} = 1{,}28\cdot10^{24}$ s⁻¹.

then (351), and Esp 088 §6.1.2:

Avec ces valeurs de $\xi$ et $\omega_{0}$, pour chaque valeur de $\omega \in [0, \infty]$ non dimensionnel, on a, d'après (351), une valeur de la densité centrale $d_{0}$, puis avec le système (348) une solution unique pour $l$, $P$ et $\delta$. On peut alors procéder au calcul de la masse $M_{E}$ ... en fonction de $\omega$ ou $d_{0}$; le graphe 6 représente la fonction $M_{E}(d_{0})$. On constate que cette fonction présente un minimum. $M_{E\min}$ correspond à une pulsation non dimensionnelle $0{,}11 \le \omega \le 0{,}13$ et la masse du nucléon (proton ou neutron) : $M_{\mu} = 1{,}68\cdot10^{-24}$ g $= M_{E\min}$.

and Esp 089, which fixes the point and gives the curve's three features: $\omega_{\mu} = 0{,}12$, $d_{0\mu} = 5{,}5\times10^{17}$ g cm⁻³, $R_{\mu} = 1{,}7\times10^{-14}$ cm; the limit $M_{E} \to 1{,}70\times10^{-24}$ g and $R_{E} \to 1{,}73\times10^{-14}$ cm as $d_{0} \to \infty$ with "des oscillations d'amplitude fortement décroissante"; and the maximum $\hat{M}_{E} = 2{,}12\times10^{-24}$ g at $\hat{d}_{0} = 8{,}3\times10^{16}$ g cm⁻³, $\hat{R}_{E} = 1{,}88\times10^{-14}$ cm.

Division of labour. S24 owns the solution and the shape of the curve; S26 owns the radius the same solution gives; S22 owns $\xi$; S21 owns $T_{0}$. This article owns the number: the sweep, the selection, the freedom, and what the mass is made of.

1. The Chain, and the One Thing in It That Is Not Fitted

Axiom 7 turns the pulsation into a period, and the period is the lepton's. The axiom sets the hadron's own pulsation equal to the charged-lepton model's, $1{,}28\times10^{24}$ s⁻¹, i.e. a period of $4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s = $T_{0}$. Verified against the corpus's own number: $2\pi/T_{0} = 1{,}2745\times10^{24}$ s⁻¹, $0{,}4\,\%$ from the printed $1{,}28\times10^{24}$. So $T_{H} = T_{0}$ exactly, and the "resonance" is the statement that a hadron's pulsation is the charged-lepton period — which is the same $T_{0}$ that S21 shows was fitted to the muon–tau mass difference. The chain is: the lepton fit supplies the hadron's clock.

The dictionary is (351), $d_{0} = \omega_{0}/(\xi c\,\omega)$, and it is monotone: sweeping $\omega$ from 0 to $\infty$ sweeps $d_{0}$ from $\infty$ down to 0. So "sweeping the central density" and "sweeping $\omega$" are the same one-parameter sweep, and the corpus's own presentation — a curve in $d_{0}$ — hides no second parameter. The corpus's "une solution unique pour $l$, $P$ et $\delta$" is confirmed by S24's integration: every $\omega$ gives exactly one terminating solution.

What is not fitted in the chain: the solution, the mass integral, and the position of the minimum. What is fitted: $T_{0}$ (S21) and $\xi$ (S22, where it is the closure's fixed point but is also the quantity Esp 057 itself calls "une « suggestion »"). So the nucleon mass is a two-constant computation, and S22's reading — that the corpus always intended $M_{H}$ as the verification of $\xi$ rather than the target — is what this chain is the verification of.

2. Why the Minimum Is Selected

The corpus does not select the minimum by appeal to stability in words only; it has an equation. Esp 087 §5.2:

dM/dt = (dM/dd₀)(dd₀/dt) = (dM/dd₀)(dR/dt)(dd₀/dR) ,   dR/dt ≤ c

so $|dM/dt| \leq c\,(dM/dd_{0})(dd_{0}/dR)$, and at any extremum of $M(d_{0})$ the bound is zero: the mass does not change and the state is stationary. That is the corpus's own selection rule, and it explains the language Esp 091 §7 uses — "les états stationnaires correspondent aux minima de la courbe $M(d_{0})$" — the stationary points are where $dM/dd_{0}$ vanishes.

And the count is two, which the curve confirms. S24's sweep finds exactly one minimum and one maximum. Esp 089 §6.2 prints the graph for exactly those two cases — "la masse minimum (neutron) : $\mu_{0} = 1{,}68\times10^{-24}$ g, la masse maximum $\hat{M}_{E} = 2{,}12\times10^{-24}$ g" — so the "two stationary states" of Esp 091 §7 are the curve's two extrema, and the word "minima" in that sentence is loose. This resolves the point S24 left open, and it means the corpus's own physics does not require a second minimum that the equations do not have.

One consequence for the identification, and it is a limit of the model rather than a defect of transcription. Over the corpus's own window $0{,}11 \le \omega \le 0{,}13$ the mass varies by only $0{,}26\,\%$ — which is twice the proton–neutron mass splitting ($0{,}138\,\%$). So the curve cannot resolve the proton from the neutron, and the corpus's own labelling wobbles: Esp 089's graph calls the minimum the neutron, the surrounding text calls it the nucleon, and the register compares it with the proton. All three are within the curve's resolution, and the honest statement is that the model produces one nucleon-mass number, not two particle masses. (The minimum is in fact slightly closer to the neutron: $+0{,}10\,\%$ against $m_{n} = 1{,}67493\times10^{-24}$ g, $+0{,}24\,\%$ against $m_{p}$.)

4. The Freedom, Reported Honestly

The freedom is one dimension and it is not small; the result is insensitive to it. Both statements must be made.

The sweep. $\omega$ runs over $(0, \infty)$ — over the whole positive half-line — and a minimum is then selected. That is P20's pattern, and nothing in the corpus derives the selection beyond Axiome 7's fixing of the period and the stationarity argument of §2. The chosen region is not special: the minimum's location is a fact about the equations, but the decision to look for a minimum is an input.

The insensitivity. $M$ is within $1\,\%$ of $M_{\min}$ for $\omega \in [0{,}058, 0{,}155]$ — a factor $2{,}7$ in $\omega$ and therefore in $d_{0}$ ($4{,}05\times10^{17}$ to $1{,}09\times10^{18}$ g cm⁻³, a factor $2{,}7$). Over $\omega \in [0{,}004, 2]$ the whole curve spans $1{,}6766$ to $2{,}1309\times10^{-24}$ g — $27\,\%$, with the minimum $21\,\%$ below the peak. So the curve is a broad, shallow bowl, and the "hit" does not depend on finding the bottom to better than a factor three.

The second freedom, and it is larger than the first. The two printed $\xi$'s differ by $5{,}1\,\%$ and shift the mass by $5{,}1\,\%$ — so the $\xi$ uncertainty is five times the width of the $1\,\%$ band and ten times the $0{,}24\,\%$ of the hit. The nucleon-mass agreement is therefore limited by the constant, not by the sweep: no refinement of the sweep can matter until $\xi$ is fixed. This is the opposite of the usual complaint about this corpus, and it is the useful form of "STANDS, weakly": the sweep is a fit, but the $\xi$ is what the number is actually checking.

And one limit of the model that is not a matter of freedom. The curve cannot resolve the proton from the neutron (§2): the $0{,}26\,\%$ variation across the corpus's own window is twice the splitting. So "the nucleon mass" is the strongest reading the curve supports, and the article does not claim the model distinguishes the two nucleons.

5. Which $\xi$ the Printed Number Used

Recomputed, the 1983 book's mass curve and its density axis need different constants. All three printed masses — $1{,}68$, $2{,}12$, $1{,}70\times10^{-24}$ g — are reproduced to $\le 0{,}5\,\%$ with $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$, and all three are $\approx 5\,\%$ high with the book's own printed $\xi = 6{,}467\times10^{-4}$. The printed density $d_{0\mu} = 5{,}5\times10^{17}$ g cm⁻³ requires $\xi = 6{,}467\times10^{-4}$ with $\omega = 0{,}12$ (and gives $5{,}502\times10^{17}$, right to $0{,}04\,\%$). So within the 1983 book itself, the printed density and the printed mass are $5\,\%$ apart — consistently, across the minimum, the maximum and the asymptotic limit.

This is the article's sharpest finding, and it is invisible unless both are recomputed. It is not a transcription defect (nothing is missing from a formula), it is not a physics error, and it does not change any verdict — but it tells a later reader which way the corpus's own numbers were computed, and it is the only place in the hadron block where the two printed constants of one book contradict each other. It also means the register's §5.4 comparison (against $1{,}6726\times10^{-24}$ g) is being run with the 1987 constant while the citation is to the 1983 section. Recorded, not adjudicated: the printed page would have to be re-read to say whether the 1983 constants or the 1983 masses were what its computer used.

6. What the Mass Is Made Of

The corpus gives the mass three pieces, and the decomposition can be computed. Esp (359)–(362) defines $M$ (proper volume, kinetic term included), $M_{E}$ (Euclidean volume, kinetic term included — "Naturally la masse gravitationnelle (ou inertielle) est $M_{E}$"), and $M_{OE}$ (Euclidean volume, rest density only), with $\delta M = M - M_{E} > 0$ and $\Delta M = M_{OE} - M_{E} < 0$. At the minimum, in the corpus's own units:

quantity value interpretation (the corpus's own, Esp 088)
$M_{OE}$ $6{,}5288\times10^{-25}$ g rest density over a Euclidean volume
$M_{E}$ $1{,}67656\times10^{-24}$ g the observed mass; $= M_{OE} - \Delta M$, $61{,}1\,\%$ above it
$M$ $1{,}95324\times10^{-24}$ g proper volume; $16{,}5\,\%$ above $M_{E}$

So by the corpus's own accounting $61\,\%$ of the nucleon's mass is the spin-and-pulse kinetic energy, the term $\sqrt{\delta^{2} + 3P^{2}}$ over the rest density $\delta$; and the proper-volume factor $(-g_{rr})^{1/2}$, which the corpus reads as "l'énergie des interactions fortes internes à la structure hadronique", adds $16{,}5\,\%$ to the Euclidean value. The two corrections have the signs the corpus says they have, they are both large, and the observed mass lies between the two other masses — which is the corpus's own ordering, $M_{OE} < M_{E} < M$.

This is worth recording because it is the most substantive thing the mass section says, and it is a claim about composition rather than about a number. The nucleon's mass in this model is mostly not rest mass: it is the kinetic energy of the quantised radial impulse and the spin, with a large positive correction from the proper volume. The observable it is compared with — the proton mass — is a measured inertial mass, and the corpus's argument is that its large value comes from the kinetic terms rather than from any constituent mass. That is the corpus's strongest structural statement about hadrons after the holon itself, and it is falsifiable in principle: a proton whose mass were mostly rest mass would not fit this decomposition.

Verdict

§5.4 stands as the register states it — [MIX], a fit-like success, STANDS weakly — and the article sharpens both halves.

(1) The number. $M_{\min} = 1{,}6766\times10^{-24}$ g with the 1987 $\xi$, $+0{,}24\,\%$ against $m_{p}$ and $+0{,}10\,\%$ against $m_{n}$; $1{,}7629\times10^{-24}$ g with the 1983 book's own printed $\xi$, $+5{,}4\,\%$.

(2) The curve. Minimum at $\omega = 0{,}105$–$0{,}106$ (just below the corpus's printed window), maximum $2{,}1309\times10^{-24}$ g at $8{,}37\times10^{16}$ g cm⁻³, limit $1{,}7041\times10^{-24}$ g, oscillations of $\approx 0{,}1\,\%$ — all four printed features reproduced to $\le 0{,}5\,\%$ under the 1987 constant.

(3) The freedom, honestly. One swept parameter, a minimum selected; but $M$ is within $1\,\%$ of $M_{\min}$ across a factor $2{,}7$ in $\omega$ and $d_{0}$, so the hit does not depend on the sweep's refinement — it depends on there being a minimum, which the equations give.

(4) The real limit is $\xi$, not the sweep. The two printed values of $\xi$ shift the mass by $5{,}1\,\%$ — ten times the size of the hit. So the nucleon-mass computation is a check of $\xi$, which is S22's reading of the corpus's own intention.

(5) The selection rule is the corpus's own, and it is not hand-waving. The lifetime bound (352)–(353) vanishes at every extremum of $M(d_{0})$, so the observable states are the curve's extrema — one minimum and one maximum, which is what Esp 089 §6.2 prints.

(6) The model produces one nucleon mass, not two. The curve's variation across the corpus's own window is twice the proton–neutron splitting, so it cannot resolve them; the corpus's labelling of the minimum as "the neutron" (Esp 089's graph) and as "the nucleon" (the text) and the register's comparison with the proton are all within the model's own resolution.

(7) What the mass is made of. $61\,\%$ kinetic (spin + pulse), $16{,}5\,\%$ proper-volume correction, the rest density supplying the remainder — the corpus's own decomposition, computed.

What survives, in one line: the corpus's closure and its lepton period combine to produce a nucleon-mass curve whose minimum is within a quarter of a per cent of the measured mass; the curve's minimum is selected by the corpus's own stationarity argument rather than by fiat; and the number's accuracy is limited by a constant the corpus itself calls a suggestion.

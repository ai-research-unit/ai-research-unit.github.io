The Multiplication Operators of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The multiplication operators of a product are the maps $\tilde q\mapsto\tilde p\circ\tilde q$ obtained by fixing one slot. For the symmetric quaternionic product the maps are as degenerate as a nonzero multiplication can be: each of them sends the whole algebra onto the scalar line, and each is a scalar multiple of the identity when restricted to that line.

Boundaries. The product is Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions; the form is The Quaternion Form as a Product on the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra, above this article in this category; the definitions of the operator, the adjoint and the derivation are Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space and The Two General Products of the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$; an element is $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$ with $\vec q=\sum_kq_k\vec e_k$. The quaternion conjugation is $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_te_t-\vec q$; the bar is the identity, the star coincides with the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$. The quaternion form is $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q$; the symmetric quaternionic product is $\tilde p\star\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t$. The block is commutative, so the left and right multiplication operators coincide, and there is one family $L_{\tilde p}$, with

$$ L_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde p\star\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t . $$

The Operators, Their Matrices and Their Ranks

Theorem (the operator is the form on the line). For every $\tilde p$ the operator $L_{\tilde p}$ is the composite of the linear form $N(\tilde p,\cdot)$ with the inclusion of the scalar line, and its matrix in the basis is

$$ [L_{\tilde p}]_{0\mu}=p_\mu,\qquad [L_{\tilde p}]_{\nu\mu}=0\ \ (\nu\neq0); $$

that is, row $0$ is the coordinate row of $\tilde p$ and every other row vanishes.

Proof. $L_{\tilde p}(e_\mu)=N(\tilde p,e_\mu)e_t=p_\mu e_t$; the $\mu$-th column of the matrix is therefore $p_\mu$ in the zeroth row and $0$ below, for every $\mu$.

Corollary (rank, image and kernel). For $\tilde p\neq0$ the operator $L_{\tilde p}$ has rank $1$, image the scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_t$, kernel the hyperplane $\tilde p^{\perp}=\{\tilde q:N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=0\}$ of real dimension $3$, trace $p_0$ and determinant $0$. For $\tilde p=0$ the operator is the zero operator, of rank $0$.

Proof. The matrix has a single nonzero row, so its rank is $1$ when the row is nonzero, that is when $\tilde p\neq0$, and its image is spanned by $e_t$. The kernel is the set of $\tilde q$ with $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=0$, a hyperplane by definition; the trace is the single diagonal entry $[L_{\tilde p}]_{00}=p_0$, and the determinant vanishes for a matrix with a zero row.

Remark (every operator is a scalar on the scalar line). Restricted to the image, $L_{\tilde p}(ae_t)=a\,p_0\,e_t$, so on the scalar line the operator acts as the scalar $p_0$; this is the phrase "scalar multiple of the identity on the scalar line". The dependence of the operator on $\tilde p$ is through all four coordinates off the line and only through $p_0$ on it.

Composition and the Enveloping Algebra

Theorem (the composition law). For all $\tilde p,\tilde q$,

$$ L_{\tilde p}\circ L_{\tilde q}=L_{\tilde p_0\tilde q}=p_0\,L_{\tilde q}, $$

Proof. $(L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q})(\tilde r)=L_{\tilde p}\bigl(N(\tilde q,\tilde r)e_t\bigr)=N(\tilde q,\tilde r)L_{\tilde p}(e_t)=N(\tilde q,\tilde r)p_0e_t=N(p_0\tilde q,\tilde r)e_t=L_{p_0\tilde q}(\tilde r)$. The second form is the homogeneity of $L$ in its subscript.

Corollary (no multiplicativity, no commutativity). The map $\tilde p\mapsto L_{\tilde p}$ is injective but not multiplicative: $L_{\tilde p\star\tilde q}=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)L_{e_t}$, which differs from $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=p_0L_{\tilde q}$ in general, and the two differ at $\tilde p=\vec e_x,\tilde q=\vec e_x$, where $N(\vec e_x,\vec e_x)L_{e_t}=L_{e_t}$ while $L_{\vec e_x}L_{\vec e_x}=0$. The monoid of operators is not commutative either: $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$ and $L_{\tilde q}L_{\tilde p}=L_{q_t\tilde p}$ differ at $\tilde p=e_t,\tilde q=\vec e_x$, where the first is $L_{\vec e_x}\neq0$ and the second is $L_0=0$.

Proof. The operator of a product is $L_{N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t}=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)L_{e_t}$ because $L$ is linear in the subscript; the composition is the theorem. The witnesses are evaluated directly.

Theorem (the enveloping structure). The linear span of the operators is

$$ \mathcal E=\{L_{\tilde p}:\tilde p\in\mathbb{H}\}, $$

a subspace of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{H})$ of real dimension $4$; it is closed under composition, hence an algebra, and the linear isomorphism $\tilde p\mapsto L_{\tilde p}$ carries it to the algebra $(\mathbb{H},\circ)$ with

$$ \tilde p\circ\tilde q=p_0\,\tilde q . $$

That algebra is associative with left unit $e_t$ and no right unit; its idempotents are $0$ and the elements with $p_0=1$.

Proof. Every $L_{\tilde p}=\sum_\mu p_\mu L_{e_\mu}$ is a combination of the four operators $L_{e_\mu}$, and these are linearly independent because $[L_{e_\mu}]_{0\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}$; so the span has dimension $4$ and the subscript map is an isomorphism onto the stated product, by the composition law $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$. The product $\circ$ is associative because $(\tilde p\circ\tilde q)\circ\tilde r=(p_0\tilde q)\circ\tilde r=p_0q_t\tilde r=\tilde p\circ(\tilde q\circ\tilde r)$; $e_t$ is a left unit, and a right unit $\tilde u$ would satisfy $\tilde p\circ\tilde u=p_0\tilde u=\tilde p$ for all $\tilde p$, which fails at $\tilde p=\vec e_x$. Finally $\tilde p\circ\tilde p=p_0\tilde p=\tilde p$ exactly when $\tilde p=0$ or $p_0=1$, which are the idempotents of the algebra.

Remark (the operators are the four coordinate projections on the line). The basis operators are $L_{e_\mu}(\tilde q)=q_\mu e_t$; each projects one coordinate onto the scalar line. The whole operator family is the four-dimensional space of these projections, and the multiplication of operators is the multiplication of a scalar coordinate by the second factor.

The Adjoint with Respect to the Form

The form $N$ is non-degenerate, so every operator has an adjoint $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}$ characterised by $N(L_{\tilde p}\tilde x,\tilde y)=N(\tilde x,L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}\tilde y)$.

Theorem (the adjoint formula). For every $\tilde p$ and $\tilde y$,

$$ L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}(\tilde y)=y_0\,\tilde p=\sum_\mu p_\mu y_0\,e_\mu, $$

an operator depending on $\tilde y$ through $y_0$ alone, with values in the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$.

Proof. $N(L_{\tilde p}\tilde x,\tilde y)=N(N(\tilde p,\tilde x)e_t,\tilde y)=N(\tilde p,\tilde x)y_0$, and $N(\tilde x,y_0\tilde p)=y_0N(\tilde x,\tilde p)=y_0N(\tilde p,\tilde x)$, so the displayed operator satisfies the defining identity; it is unique because $N$ is non-degenerate.

Corollary (self-adjointness is centrality). $L_{\tilde p}$ is self-adjoint, $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}=L_{\tilde p}$, if and only if $\tilde p$ is central, that is $\vec p=0$.

Proof. $L_{\tilde p}(\tilde y)=N(\tilde p,\tilde y)e_t$ is scalar valued, while $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}(\tilde y)=y_0\tilde p$ has vector part $y_0\vec p$; the two are equal for all $\tilde y$ exactly when $\vec p=0$. For central $\tilde p=p_0e_t$ both are $p_0y_0e_t$.

Remark (the adjoint is not the multiplication). The adjoint reads only the scalar coordinate $y_0$ of its argument and its value lies in the line of $\tilde p$; the multiplication reads the full pairing and its value lies on the scalar line. The two agree exactly at a central multiplier.

The Bimodule Structure

Proposition (the module and the bimodule). With the action $L_{\tilde p}\cdot\tilde x=\tilde p\star\tilde x$, the space $\mathbb{H}$ is a faithful left module over the enveloping algebra $\mathcal E$; the right action $\tilde x\cdot L_{\tilde p}=L_{\tilde p}(\tilde x)$ is the same, so the module is a symmetric bimodule. The annihilator of the whole module is $\{0\}$, and the module is faithful.

Proof. The action is by definition a linear action of the algebra $\mathcal E$, and associativity of the action is the associativity of composition; the left and right actions coincide because $\mathcal E$ acts by linear maps on the space, and the block's own product is commutative so no asymmetry is introduced. The annihilator is the set of $L_{\tilde p}$ with $L_{\tilde p}=0$, which is $\{\tilde p=0\}$ by the injectivity of the subscript map.

Remark (why the bimodule is not trivial). The enveloping algebra $\mathcal E\cong(\mathbb{H},\circ)$ is not commutative, so the left and right regular modules over $\mathcal E$ are different; but both act on $\mathbb{H}$ through the same evaluation, and the bimodule is symmetric. The action is not the regular action of $\mathbb{H}$: the multiplication of the module is the collapse $\tilde p\star\tilde x=N(\tilde p,\tilde x)e_t$, which loses the vector part of the result.

The Derivations of the Block

A derivation of the block is a linear map $D$ of $\mathbb{H}$ with $D(\tilde p\star\tilde q)=D\tilde p\star\tilde q+\tilde p\star D\tilde q$ (Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space); an inner derivation is $\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde p}(\tilde x)=\tilde p\star\tilde x-\tilde x\star\tilde p$.

Proposition (the inner derivations vanish). Every inner derivation of the block is zero, because the block is commutative; the derived law of the block is the zero operation.

Proof. $\mathrm{ad}_{\tilde p}(\tilde x)=\tilde p\star\tilde x-\tilde x\star\tilde p=0$ by commutativity.

Theorem (the derivation algebra). A linear map $D$ is a derivation of the block if and only if

$$ D e_t=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad N(D\tilde x,\tilde y)+N(\tilde x,D\tilde y)=0\text{ for all }\tilde x,\tilde y, $$

that is $D$ vanishes on the scalar line and is skew-adjoint for the form. Writing $D$ in the basis as the block matrix of $0$ on $\mathbb{R}e_t$ and a $3\times3$ block $S$ on the vector subspace, the condition is $S^{\mathsf T}+S=0$; the derivation algebra is therefore $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ on the vector subspace, of real dimension $3$.

Proof. The derivation identity on the block reads $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)De_t=\bigl(N(D\tilde p,\tilde q)+N(\tilde p,D\tilde q)\bigr)e_t$ for all $\tilde p,\tilde q$. At $\tilde p=\tilde q=e_t$ it gives $De_t=2(De_t)_0e_t$, so $De_t$ is central and equal to twice its scalar part, hence $De_t=0$; the identity becomes $N(D\tilde p,\tilde q)+N(\tilde p,D\tilde q)=0$, the skew-adjointness. Since $De_t=0$, the first column of the matrix vanishes; skew-adjointness makes the first row vanish as well and the remaining $3\times3$ block skew-symmetric. The skew-symmetric $3\times3$ matrices are the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$, of dimension $3$.

Corollary (the weight is forced to zero). There is no derivation with a weight $\delta\neq0$ in the identity $N(D\tilde x,\tilde y)+N(\tilde x,D\tilde y)=\delta\,N(\tilde x,\tilde y)$ for all $\tilde x,\tilde y$: at $\tilde x=\tilde y=e_t$ the identity reads $2(De_t)_0=\delta$, and it also forces $De_t$ to be central, $De_t=(De_t)_0e_t$; the same pair in the form $De_t=\delta e_t$ gives $\delta=2\delta$, hence $\delta=0$. The derivation algebra is exactly the weight-zero one of the theorem.

Proof. The two readings of the pair $(e_t,e_t)$ are as displayed; comparing them gives the conclusion.

Proposition (the operator bracket is not a derivation). The bracket $[L_{\tilde p},L_{\tilde q}]=L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}-L_{\tilde q}L_{\tilde p}=p_0L_{\tilde q}-q_tL_{\tilde p}$ does not satisfy the derivation identity in general. At $\tilde p=e_t,\tilde q=\vec e_x$ the bracket is $L_{\vec e_x}$, and at $(\tilde x,\tilde y)=(\vec e_x,e_t)$ the two sides of the derivation identity are $0$ and $e_t$.

Proof. $[L_{e_t},L_{\vec e_x}]=L_{\vec e_x}$ by the composition law. The derivation identity at $(\vec e_x,e_t)$ reads $[L_{e_t},L_{\vec e_x}](\vec e_x\star e_t)=[L_{e_t},L_{\vec e_x}](\vec e_x)\star e_t+\vec e_x\star[L_{e_t},L_{\vec e_x}](e_t)$. The left side is $L_{\vec e_x}(N(\vec e_x,e_t)e_t)=L_{\vec e_x}(0)=0$; the right side is $L_{\vec e_x}(\vec e_x)\star e_t+0=e_t\star e_t=N(e_t,e_t)e_t=e_t$. The two differ, so the bracket is not a derivation of the block. The bracket does vanish on $e_t$, so it passes the first condition of the theorem, and the obstruction is the skew-adjointness condition $N(D\tilde x,\tilde y)+N(\tilde x,D\tilde y)=0$.

Remark (the correction to the brief). The brief states that the operators "commute" and are "self-adjoint for the form". Neither holds: the composition is $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$ and $L_{\tilde q}L_{\tilde p}=L_{q_t\tilde p}$, which differ at $(e_t,\vec e_x)$, and self-adjointness holds exactly at a central multiplier. What is true is that the operators are the coordinate projections of the block onto its scalar line and that they generate the four-dimensional enveloping algebra $(\mathbb{H},\circ)$.

Summary

The multiplication operators of the block are $L_{\tilde p}(\tilde q)=\tilde p\star\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t$, with the coordinates of $\tilde p$ in the zeroth row of the matrix and zero elsewhere; off zero they have rank $1$, image the scalar line, kernel the hyperplane $\tilde p^{\perp}$ of dimension $3$, trace $p_0$ and determinant $0$, and on the scalar line they act as the scalar $p_0$. Their composition is $L_{\tilde p}L_{\tilde q}=L_{p_0\tilde q}$, so they do not commute and are not multiplicative; they span the four-dimensional enveloping algebra $\mathcal E\cong(\mathbb{H},\circ)$ with $\tilde p\circ\tilde q=p_0\tilde q$, associative with left unit $e_t$ alone, and $\mathbb{H}$ is a faithful symmetric $\mathcal E$-bimodule. The adjoint is $L_{\tilde p}^{\dagger}(\tilde y)=y_0\tilde p$, and $L_{\tilde p}$ is self-adjoint exactly at a central multiplier.

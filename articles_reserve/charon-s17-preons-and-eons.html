Charon — Préons and Éons (S17)

Audits §3.1 and §10.10 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md — and, because the two words are the same words in a different literature, the 1970s–80s preon-compositeness programme — against RC ch. III §V.1 and §VI (scans 048, 049, 050) and RC 052.

Introduction

The corpus's micro-universe has two kinds of inhabitant, and the distinction between them is one of the few places where the corpus is completely clear: the préon is the general solution, valid at every point of the real sheet, electrically neutral, containing nothing but a massless neutrino; the éon is the particular solution, present only at the discrete masses of the charged leptons, electrically charged, containing a blackbody field as well.

Two things make this page worth its own article. First, the corpus reuses a word with a live meaning — "préon" — for a disjoint object, and the register's §3.1 says so; this article says precisely how far the disjointness goes, because that is what determines who should cite whom. Second, and this is the finding: the préon's defining property is maintained by the massless neutrino, so §3.7's falsification does not damage the préon, it deletes it.

What the Corpus Claims

§3.1 — there are two kinds of imaginary structure: neutral "préons" (one per density value, a continuous infinity) and charged "éons" (an allowed discrete subset). RC ch. III §V. Tag: [SPEC]. The register's note is that "préon" is a serious word in the literature (Pati–Salam 1974, Harari 1979) but that those models are real-space compositeness models with a small number of species, so this is a homonym.

§10.10 — word borrowings worth noting: "préon" (1970s–80s compositeness; a different object), "éon" (Valentinian gnosticism), "holon" (Koestler 1967). Tag: [PHIL]/historical.

1. The Two Objects, Exactly as the Corpus Defines Them

The préon — the general solution

RC 048 §V.1 defines it by its parameters: the solution of the fundamental evolution equation with $\alpha = \beta = \Lambda = 0$. The consequences are stated in the same paragraph and are worth quoting because everything else follows from them:

  • $\alpha = 0$ means the imaginary space has zero density $\rho = 0$; $\beta = 0$ means the trapped blackbody temperature is zero ($\theta = 0$), and the corpus draws the third leg of the equivalence explicitly: "$\beta = 0$ signifie donc également que le point $P$ considéré est électriquement neutre." So the neutral/charged split is exactly the $\beta = 0$ / $\beta \neq 0$ split — this triple equivalence ($\alpha = 0 \Leftrightarrow \rho = 0$; $\beta = 0 \Leftrightarrow \theta = 0 \Leftrightarrow$ neutral) is what the register's §3.1 should record, and does not.
  • The micro-universe contains only the neutrino, at velocity $c$. Hence (3-55) $R'^{2}/c^{2} = 1$, and (3-56) $R = R_{0} \pm c\tau$. The corpus's reason: "Un tel micro-univers n'est donc constitué d'aucun élément de masse propre non nulle, et par conséquent il doit alors (nos conditions initiales 3-31) être initialement ($\tau = 0$) en pulsation radiale à vitesse $R' = \pm c$. Cet état $R' = \pm c$ se maintient quel que soit $\tau$, puisque seul le neutrino de masse propre nulle est présent dans le micro-univers pendant toute la durée de sa pulsation radiale."
  • Its energy is (3-54) $W_{\nu} = \overline{W}_{S} + \overline{W}_{P} = h/T = h\nu = mc^{2}$ — the de Broglie phase relation, and the source of the préon's proper mass in real space.
  • Its trace in the real space is a point $P$. There is one préon per point $P$, hence one per value of the proper density — a continuous infinity of them. That is §3.1's first half.

The éon — the particular solution

RC 050 §VI.1 defines it as the solution with $\alpha \neq 0$ and $\beta \neq 0$ together: the micro-universe contains the neutrino and a blackbody radiation field of non-zero temperature. RC 048 §IV names the corresponding system of equations the Système $E$ (Système éonique), whose four lines are (3-53): the evolution equation with $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\Lambda$ all present; the energy-conservation line; the closure condition $\int_{0}^{T}d\tau/R = 2\pi/c$; and the coupling line $l_{0} = R_{H}$. The éon solutions are discrete — one per level $n$ — and the corpus identifies them with the charged leptons (S20, S21).

They are not two species of one thing

The préon is the general solution — valid at every point of any proper mass — and the éon is the particular solution for the discrete masses $m_{n}$. RC 052's own summary image: the éonic part "joue une sorte de rôle « corpusculaire », tandis que la partie préonique, qui génère l'onde de phase psi se propageant dans l'espace Réel, joue le rôle d'une onde probabiliste associée à toute particule, quelle que soit sa masse propre $m$." And the corpus says the préon solution is superposed on every éonic one: "la solution préonique vient toujours se superposer à la situation particulière éonique considérée." So every particle has both, and that is why the $\psi$ phase wave exists for particles of all masses (S54).

2. The Homonym — How Far the Disjointness Goes

§3.1 says Charon's préon is "a different object entirely (one per real density value)" and that it is a homonym. That is right. Here is the precise statement, because it determines the citation question.

Charon's préon the literature's preon (Pati–Salam 1974, Harari 1979, and the rishon/haplon schemes)
how many species one per point of real space, i.e. one per density value — a continuous infinity a small finite number (3, 4, or a few) plus conjugates
where it lives in the imaginary sheet; its real trace is a point in real space, as a constituent of quarks and leptons
what kind of object a whole pulsating micro-universe a part of a particle
what binds it nothing external: it is one solution of one equation a new strong interaction with a compositeness scale $\Lambda_{\rm comp}$
observable? no, by construction — the imaginary sheet is not observed yes, in principle: contact interactions, excited states
what tested it nothing; there is no measurement LEP, Tevatron and LHC compositeness limits (several to tens of TeV), excited-fermion searches, $g-2$, lepton-flavour violation
how it failed not tested, and untestable no confirmed spectrum; the candidate models failed to reproduce masses and were constrained by precision data

The disjointness is total, and the direction of it matters for the register's citation policy. The preon-compositeness programme's constraints — the LHC's contact-interaction bounds, the absence of excited leptons, the lepton-flavour-violation limits — say nothing whatever about Charon's préons, because Charon's préons are not constituents and would not appear in a collider at all. Conversely, nothing in Charon's €on/préon structure bears on the compositeness question. So the correct treatment is not "Charon's préon model predates the modern bounds" but "the word is shared and the object is not"; a reader who brings the collider literature to this corpus will look for a scale $\Lambda_{\rm comp}$ that does not exist in it.

The other two borrowings, recorded here because the corpus means something by each. "Éon" comes from Valentinian gnosticism, where an æon is an emanation — and the corpus's éon is a self-contained, self-pulsating whole emerging from a deeper space, so the borrowing is descriptive rather than decorative. "Holon" is Koestler's word (The Ghost in the Machine, 1967) for a whole that is a part of a larger whole, and the corpus's holon is a pulsating curvature lump; the borrowing here is looser (S24 owns it). §10.10's [PHIL]/historical tag stands, and the practical rule for this series is: keep the corpus's words, gloss them at first use, and never let a shared word import a literature's results.

3. The Finding — the Préon Rests on the Massless Neutrino, and That Is Falsified

This is the article's contribution, and it follows from RC 048's own sentences.

  • The préon is defined by $\alpha = \beta = \Lambda = 0$, and the corpus's reason that $\Lambda$ also vanishes is the absence of massive elements: the micro-universe "n'est donc constitué d'aucun élément de masse propre non nulle", and therefore $R' = \pm c$ at $\tau = 0$, "et cet état $R' = \pm c$ se maintient quel que soit $\tau$, puisque seul le neutrino de masse propre nulle est présent."
  • So $R' = \pm c$ — the property from which everything on S12's and S18's pages descends: the linear radius law (3-56), the closure result $R_{\max}/R_{0} = e^{\pi}$ (3-59), the golden-ratio orbit $Y = \sqrt{\varphi}$ (3-61-a) — is maintained by the neutrino's zero proper mass.
  • §3.7 is [FALS]: neutrino oscillations (Super-Kamiokande 1998, SNO 2001–02, KamLAND 2002) require non-zero mass differences. P8 records it, with the register's own note that "the internal neutrino on a closed orbit, the lepton charge mechanism and §3.8 all rest on it".

The consequence is stronger than "rest on it". Remove the massless neutrino and the préon solution does not exist: the state $R' = \pm c$ is no longer maintained for all $\tau$, so (3-55) fails, so the linear $R(\tau)$ of (3-56) fails, so $e^{\pi}$ and $\sqrt{\varphi}$ are no longer the préon's numbers. The falsification does not degrade the préon; it removes it. And because the préon is the general solution superposed on every particle, the removal is universal: it takes the $\psi$-generating part of every particle with it (the S54 question, and §2.7's status).

What survives, and this part is genuine. The taxonomy survives — and S17's judgement is that this is not nothing. The structural idea that a theory can carry a second kind of substructure: imaginary, one-per-point, with a discrete charged subset selected by a temperature, is different from compositeness and different from extra dimensions, and it is internally coherent. It is also untestable by construction, which is the reason it was never developed rather than the reason it was refuted. So the fair verdict distinguishes three levels: the taxonomy (préon vs éon vs holon) is a coherent [SPEC] scheme and Part S material as an audited idea; the préon as a physical object is Part P, because its defining property is falsified; and the éon as a physical object is Part P too, because the éon's lepton structure is P1's subject and its charge is S19's, and both die (P1; S19).

Verdict

The taxonomy survives; the objects do not. Charon's distinction between a préon, an éon and a holon is coherent as a scheme of the imaginary sheet, and it is worth keeping in the ledger as an audited idea — with its homonymy to the 1970s preon-compositeness literature stated in full, since the two have nothing in common and the direction of the disjointness matters.

But the préon's defining property is falsified, not merely unsupported. Its entire content — $R' = \pm c$, hence $e^{\pi}$, hence $\sqrt{\varphi}$ — is maintained by the internal neutrino's zero proper mass, and the neutrino is massive (P8). So the préon solution does not exist in the required form, and worse: because the préon is superposed on every particle, the removal is universal. The éon fails twice over — its lepton structure is P1's subject and its charge is S19's — and the whole of S17–S23 rests on §3.7.

So the fair verdict has three levels: the taxonomy is Part S as an audited idea; the préon as an object and the éon as an object are Part P. And the specific reason the audit is worth doing is that the corpus's most elegant numbers are inside the solution the falsification deletes.

Summary of Notation

  • préon — the solution with $\alpha = \beta = \Lambda = 0$: neutral, density-free, temperature-free, containing only a massless neutrino, $R = R_{0} \pm c\tau$. One per point $P$. Not the literature's preon.
  • éon — the solution with $\alpha$, $\beta \neq 0$: contains the neutrino and blackbody radiation at temperature $\theta$; charged; discrete family $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, identified with the charged leptons.
  • $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ — the éon's three internal constants; $\alpha$ is the space's density coefficient, $\beta$ the trapped-radiation coefficient ($\beta = 0 \Leftrightarrow$ neutral), $T_{0}$ the éon's common period (S21).
  • Système $E$ — the corpus's name for the éon system, RC (3-53).
  • holon — Koestler's word, reused for the pulsating curvature lump of ch. IV (S24).

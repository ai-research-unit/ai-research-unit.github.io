Charon — The Boundary Moves at $c$ (S23)

Audits §3.9 and §10.8 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md, against RC ch. III and IV (scans 044, 048, 063, 068) and Esp §VI–VIII.

Introduction

The register's verdict on §10.8 — "the 'c at the boundary' condition is a hard-coded closure hypothesis ([SPEC]); the resulting $0{,}92$ fm happens to match the proton size. This is the clearest place where an arbitrary convention yields a real number" — is right about the real sheet and wrong about the imaginary one, and the difference is worth an article because it splits one assumption into two.

On the imaginary sheet, $R' = \pm c$ is not assumed. Setting $\alpha = \beta = \Lambda = 0$ in the evolution equation (3-32) collapses it to $R'^{2}/c^{2} = K$, and with $K = +1$ — the corpus's own finiteness condition (3-36) — that gives $R' = \pm c$ for all $\tau$. Two substitutions, no assumption. The corpus states it as an initial condition, (3-31), and RC 048 says it "se maintient quel que soit $\tau$"; it is neither initial nor maintained, it is derived.

On the real sheet, $V_{r} = \pm c$ at the holon's boundary is stated flat, as (4-17-e), and it is exactly as arbitrary as the register says. So the correct form of §3.9 is: the condition is derived where the density vanishes and imposed where it does not.

And the second finding is about what the imposed version buys. The register calls $0{,}92$ fm "the clearest place where an arbitrary convention yields a real number", and the article agrees — with the qualification that the real number is one number reported twice: $R_{H\max} = 9{,}19\times10^{-14}$ cm and $\lambda_{f} = 2R_{H\max} = 1{,}84\times10^{-13}$ cm are the same fact, so the "proton-size hit" and the "strong-range hit" that the register counts separately in R2 are a single coincidence. And its agreement is $9\,\%$ and order-of-magnitude, not the "correct observed range" the corpus claims.

What the Corpus Claims

§3.9 — the pulsating boundary of every structure moves at exactly $\pm c$. RC (4-17-e); Esp §VII. Tag: [SPEC]. The register's gloss: "A hard-coded closure condition, not a result. It is what delivers $R_{\max} \approx 0{,}92$ fm (§5.3)."

§10.8 — singular fact: the boundary of every pulsating structure moves at exactly $\pm c$, and $R_{H\max}$ comes out $\approx 0{,}92$ fm. RC (4-17-e), (4-17-f). Tag: [MIX]. The register: "The 'c at the boundary' condition is a hard-coded closure hypothesis ([SPEC]); the resulting $0{,}92$ fm happens to match the proton size ([EST]-adjacent)."

1. The Two Numbers, Exact, and They Are One Number

RC 063:

$$(4\text{-}17\text{-}e)\quad V_{r} = \pm c, \qquad\qquad (4\text{-}17\text{-}f)\quad R_{H\max} = R_{H} + c\frac{T_{0}}{2} = 9{,}2\cdot10^{-14}\ \mathrm{cm}$$

RC 068:

$$(4\text{-}30)\quad \lambda_{f} = 2R_{H\max} = 2 \times 9{,}2\cdot10^{-14} = 1{,}84\cdot10^{-13}\ \mathrm{cm}$$

Both are exact arithmetic from the corpus's own $R_{H} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm and $T_{0} = 4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s:

$$c\frac{T_{0}}{2} = 7{,}3899\cdot10^{-14}\ \mathrm{cm} \;\Longrightarrow\; R_{H\max} = 9{,}1899\cdot10^{-14}\ \mathrm{cm} \;\text{(printed } 9{,}2\text{)} \;\Longrightarrow\; \lambda_{f} = 1{,}8380\cdot10^{-13}\ \mathrm{cm} \;\text{(printed } 1{,}84\text{)}$$

And the second of the two is by construction twice the first. $\lambda_{f} = 2R_{H\max}$ is not a second result; it is the same quantity. So the register's R2 entry, which lists "$R_{H} \approx 0{,}92$ fm" and "$\lambda_{f} \approx 2$ fm" as the chain's outputs, is listing one number in two units of a factor two apart.

That matters for the count of the program's numerical hits. R2 is described in the register as "the best numerical hit in the program"; it is one coincidence, on one assumption, reported twice.

2. The Narrowing: On the Imaginary Sheet the Condition Is Derived

RC 044 states (3-32) and, in the same section, restricts the condition:

$$(3\text{-}32)\quad \frac{R'^{2}}{c^{2}} = \frac{1}{6}\frac{\xi^{2}}{R^{2}}\left[\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{3}{R_{0}^{2}} - \frac{1}{R^{2}}\right) - \frac{2\beta^{2}}{R_{0}^{4}}\right] + K + \frac{1}{3}\Lambda R^{2}$$

"(e) Remarquons enfin que la condition initiale $R' = dR/d\tau = \pm c$ concerne seulement le cas d'un micro-universe Imaginaire où la densité propre $\rho$ est nulle (seulement un neutrino, pas de courbure riemannienne scalaire, pas de photons thermiques, voir ci-dessous)."

The restriction is the corpus's, and it is exactly the condition under which the derivation becomes trivial. At $\rho = 0$ the corpus has $\alpha = \beta = \Lambda = 0$ (RC 048: "les deux paramètres $\alpha$ et $\beta$ sont nuls … l'espace Imaginaire possède donc une densité $\rho$ nulle ($\alpha = 0$), et la température $\theta$ du rayonnement noir est également nulle ($\beta = 0$)"). Substituting into (3-32):

$$\frac{R'^{2}}{c^{2}} = 0 + K + 0 = K = +1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R' = \pm c \quad\text{for all }\tau$$

So on the imaginary sheet $R' = \pm c$ is a theorem of the corpus's own equation, given the finiteness condition $K = +1$ that (3-36) already imposes for an independent reason. (3-31)'s initial condition is redundant for the préon: the equation supplies it.

Two consequences, both positive and both worth recording.

  • The préon's $e^{\pi}$ chain gains a link rather than losing one (S18). $R_{\max}/R_{0} = e^{\pi} = 23{,}14069$ follows from the closure integral $\int_{0}^{T}d\tau/R = 2\pi/c$ with a derived linear $R(\tau)$, so the chain "one quantum on a closed orbit in a finite micro-universe $\Rightarrow$ Euler's constant" contains one fewer assumption than S18 could claim.
  • The corpus's own words support the reading, even though the corpus does not draw the conclusion. RC 048 says of the $\rho = 0$ solution that "cet état $R' = \pm c$ se maintient quel que soit $\tau$" — "maintains itself" — which is what a consequence does, not what an initial condition does.

So §3.9's universal quantifier fails in two directions at once: the condition is not hard-coded where it is derived, and it is not universal where it is hard-coded. The second half is S20's:

RC 052's éon table gives $R_{0} = 8{,}01\times10^{-15}$ cm and $\mathcal{R} = 4{,}82\times10^{-14}$ cm, so the éon's amplitude is $4{,}02\times10^{-14}$ cm against $cT_{0}/2 = 7{,}39\times10^{-14}$ cm — $54{,}4\,\%$ of it. The éon's boundary does not move at $c$, and the corpus's own equation says why: with $\beta \ne 0$ the bracket in (3-32) is not zero, so $R'^{2}/c^{2} = K + (\text{nonzero})$. §3.9's "every structure" should read "every structure of zero density".

3. Three Structures, Three Pulsation Laws, One Derivation

object sheet amplitude ratio law status
préon ($\rho = 0$) imaginary $e^{\pi} = 23{,}14$ $R' = \pm c$ derived from (3-32) at $\alpha = \beta = \Lambda = 0$, $K = +1$
éon ($\beta \ne 0$) imaginary $6{,}01$ $R'^{2}/c^{2} = K + (\text{bracket})$ solved numerically from (3-32); amplitude $54{,}4\,\%$ of $cT_{0}/2$
holon real $5{,}11$ $R_{H\max} = R_{H} + cT_{0}/2$ imposed as (4-17-e), with no equation behind it

Three objects the corpus presents under one closure principle, three different laws, and only the first is a consequence of anything. The table is the article's summary, and it is also the register's §3.9 entry rewritten: what the corpus has is not one boundary condition applied everywhere but a single condition that is true of the degenerate solution, solved for the charged one, and assumed for the real one.

And the holon's ratio, $R_{H\max}/R_{H} = 9{,}1899/1{,}8 = 5{,}11$, sits between the other two — which is worth one sentence because it is the reason the block looks coherent. The corpus's boundary argument is presented as one mechanism producing three scales; in fact the three ratios are $23{,}14$, $6{,}01$ and $5{,}11$, i.e. one derived number, one solved number, and one that is whatever $cT_{0}/2R_{H} + 1$ happens to be.

4. What the Imposed Version Buys, and What It Costs

Everything in R2 downstream of (4-17-e) rests on two ingredients, and neither is new.

$$R_{H\max} = R_{H} + \frac{cT_{0}}{2} \qquad\text{where}\qquad R_{H} = l_{0}\ \text{(the closure, S22)},\quad T_{0} = \frac{2h}{c^{2}(\tau-\mu)}\ \text{(the lepton fit, S21)}$$

So the "arbitrary convention yields a real number" is more precisely: an assumed equation applied to a closure-fixed length and a lepton-fitted period yields a number near the proton size. The two ingredients are the two the corpus fitted elsewhere — which is why R2 is a consistency check rather than a prediction, and why the register's own caveat ("the number exists only because the boundary is hard-coded to move at $c$, so it is a consistency check more than a prediction") is the right verdict.

The comparison, done honestly:

quantity corpus 2020s reference ratio
$R_{H\max}$ $0{,}920$ fm proton charge radius $0{,}8414$ fm $+9{,}3\,\%$
$\lambda_{f}$ $1{,}84$ fm $\hbar/m_{\pi^{\pm}}c = 1{,}414$ fm (the textbook strong-interaction range) $+30\,\%$
$\lambda_{f}$ $1{,}84$ fm the nuclear-force range, often quoted as $\sim2$ fm $-8\,\%$

The corpus's own words at RC 068 are "soit environ 2 fermis (ce qui est la portée correcte observée)". Against the pion's Compton wavelength — the range modern treatments quote — the figure is $30\,\%$ high; against the loose "$\sim2$ fm" it is $8\,\%$ low. So the hit is real at the order-of-magnitude level, which the register already says, and "correct" overstates it, which the register does not.

5. A Singular Fact the Corpus Does Not Notice

$\lambda_{f}$ is dominated by $cT_{0}$, and $cT_{0}$ has a meaning nobody in the corpus remarks on.

$$\lambda_{f} = 2R_{H} + cT_{0} = 0{,}360\ \mathrm{fm} + 1{,}478\ \mathrm{fm} = 1{,}838\ \mathrm{fm}$$

The holon's own radius contributes $20\,\%$; the rest is $cT_{0}$, the distance light travels in the fitted éon period. And

$$cT_{0} = 1{,}478\ \mathrm{fm} \qquad\text{against}\qquad \frac{\hbar}{m_{\pi^{\pm}}c} = 1{,}414\ \mathrm{fm}$$

— $4{,}5\,\%$ apart, and the arithmetic reason is clean: $cT_{0} = 4\pi m_{\pi}/(\tau-\mu)$ times the pion Compton wavelength, and

$$\frac{\tau - \mu}{m_{\pi^{\pm}}} = \frac{1671{,}2}{139{,}570} = 11{,}974$$

so $cT_{0} \approx (\pi/3)\times(\hbar/m_{\pi}c)$ because the muon–tau mass difference is twelve pion masses, to $0{,}2\,\%$.

Recorded as a coincidence, in the register's P19 sense, and refused as anything else: nothing in the corpus mentions pions, $(\tau-\mu)/m_{\pi} \approx 12$ is a numerical accident of the charged-lepton and pion spectra, and the "strong-force range" hit that R2 counts is largely this accident wearing the holon's parameters. It is recorded because a coincidence that a programme does not notice is exactly the kind of thing the ledger exists to keep separate from its results — and because it sharpens the count: R2's hit is $cT_{0}$ near $\hbar/m_{\pi}c$, which is a statement about the lepton mass difference.

6. Verdict

§3.9 stands in part and must be re-stated; §10.8 stands with its count corrected.

  • Derived, not assumed, on the imaginary sheet: at $\rho = 0$, (3-32) with $\alpha = \beta = \Lambda = 0$ and $K = +1$ gives $R' = \pm c$ for all $\tau$. So (3-31) is redundant for the préon, RC 044 (e) restricts the condition to exactly this case, and S18's $e^{\pi}$ chain gains a link. §3.9 should say "derived where the density vanishes".**
  • Imposed, as the register says, on the real sheet: (4-17-e) $V_{r} = \pm c$ at the holon's boundary is stated with no equation behind it. §3.9 should say "imposed where it does not".
  • Not universal, contrary to §3.9's wording: the éon's amplitude is $54{,}4\,\%$ of $cT_{0}/2$ (S20), so "the boundary of every pulsating structure moves at $\pm c$" is false of one of the three structures the corpus itself tabulates.
  • §10.8 confirmed and quantified: $R_{H\max} = 9{,}1899\times10^{-14}$ cm and $\lambda_{f} = 1{,}8380\times10^{-13}$ cm, both exact from $R_{H}$ and $T_{0}$; and they are one number, not two — so R2's "best numerical hit" is one coincidence reported twice.
  • Agreement, stated honestly: $+9{,}3\,\%$ against the proton charge radius, $+30\,\%$ against $\hbar/m_{\pi}c$, $-8\,\%$ against $\sim2$ fm. Order-of-magnitude, not "correct".
  • New, and filed as a coincidence: $\lambda_{f}$ is essentially $cT_{0} = 1{,}478$ fm, within $4{,}5\,\%$ of the pion Compton wavelength because $(\tau-\mu)/m_{\pi} = 11{,}974$.
  • §3.9's tag stays [SPEC]; §10.8's stays [MIX]. Neither is upgraded: deriving $R' = \pm c$ on the imaginary sheet does not help the real sheet, where the number that matters is produced.

And the ledger consequence, which is the reason this article sits at the head of R2. Three pulsation laws where the corpus presents one principle; a derived condition on a solution that P8 deletes (S17); an imposed condition producing one number reported twice; and that number's neighbours $9\,\%$ and $30\,\%$ away. R2's "STANDS" is therefore right and should be read as: the strongest numerical agreement in the program is one $9\,\%$ coincidence built on an assumption, in a block whose imaginary half does not exist in the required form.

Part S / Part P. Part S: the derivation of $R' = \pm c$ at $\rho = 0$; the three-law table; the arithmetic of $R_{H\max}$ and $\lambda_{f}$; the honest comparison; the $cT_{0}$ coincidence as a recorded near-miss. Part P: the holon as a physical mechanism — the claim that a pulsating real-space boundary is the proton and is the strong range, which rests on (4-17-e) and on the Système $H$ (S26, S27). The condition survives as a device; the mechanism does not, and the article's three-law table is the reason to say so.

Summary of Notation

  • $V_{r}$ — the radial velocity of the holon's boundary $\Gamma$, where the proper density vanishes. (4-17-e) sets it to $\pm c$.
  • $R_{H}$, $R_{H\max}$ — the holon's minimum and maximum radii; $1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm and $9{,}19\times10^{-14}$ cm. $l_{0} = R_{H}$ by the coupling (3-49-a).
  • $\lambda_{f}$ — the corpus's range of the strong interaction, $2R_{H\max}$. Not the pion Compton wavelength.
  • (4-17-e)/(4-17-f) — the boundary condition and the linear pulsation law; (4-30) — $\lambda_{f}$; (3-32) — the imaginary sheet's evolution equation; (3-31) — the initial condition $R'(0) = \pm c$; (3-36) — $K = +1$.
  • $cT_{0}/2$ — the half-period light-travel distance, $7{,}3899\times10^{-14}$ cm; $cT_{0} = 1{,}478$ fm is S23's new number.
  • $\rho = 0$ — the condition under which the imaginary sheet's $R' = \pm c$ holds; the préon's defining property, and the one §3.7 deletes.

Charon — Axiom 3 and the Passage to RGQ (S15)

Audits §2.3 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md, against the quantification axiom and RC (2-70)–(2-72) (scan 032), with RC 047.

Introduction

The corpus's third axiom is the one that makes it a quantum theory: at every real point the action is quantised at $\pm nh$ and splits exactly equally between a spin $\pm n\hbar/2$ and a "pulse" $\pm n\hbar/2$. From it, plus the curvature law, the corpus obtains its quantum equation — the passage to what it calls RGQ, the Relativité générale quantifiée.

This article does two things. First it audits the axiom, and the finding is structural: the axiom is stated as one sentence but contains four independent assertions, of which one is established physics borrowed, one is a free choice, one is an ontological commitment, and one is a definition. Second it audits the passage, and the finding is a genuine positive: the substitution that produces the quantum equation is exact, and it has an elegant geometric meaning — the corpus's curvature at a point equals the inverse square of the reduced Compton wavelength of the mass there.

And the article records the corpus's worst-foundational defect, because it lands squarely on this axiom: the axioms cannot be enumerated.

What the Corpus Claims

§2.3 — at every real point $P$ the action is quantised at $\pm nh$, split exactly equally between a spin $\pm n\hbar/2$ and a "pulse" $\pm n\hbar/2$. RC ch. II §IV.1. Tag: [SPEC].

The axiom, verbatim (RC 032), so that its exact scope can be seen:

"L'action en chaque point $P$ du Réel est quantifiée à la valeur $\pm nh$, où $n$ est un entier non nul et $h$ la constante de Planck; cette action se partage entre un spin $\pm n\hbar/2$ et un pulse $\pm n\hbar/2$ ($h = 2\pi\hbar$). Si $n$ est pair $P$ est un boson; si $n$ est impair $P$ est un fermion."

And the corpus immediately supplies the reason the object is not at $P$ itself — a point has zero volume, so the action cannot be contained there — and defines the action density (2-70):

$$(2\text{-}70)\quad U_{P} = \left(\frac{\pm nh}{V_{4}}\right)_{P} ,$$

with $V_{4}$ the four-dimensional hypervolume of the imaginary micro-universe at $P$, extended over one pulsation period. $U$ then obeys (2-71):

$$(2\text{-}71)\quad \Box^{2}U + (R - 4\Lambda)U = \Box^{2}U + \frac{M_{0}^{2}c^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}U = 0 ,$$

with the curvature law (2-72): $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} + 4\Lambda$.

Finding 1 — The Axiom Is Four Assertions, Not One

Read as printed, "axiom 3" asserts:

  1. the action at every real point is an integer multiple of $h$ — an ontological claim about what exists at a point;
  2. that action splits exactly equally between spin and pulse — a free choice, and the load-bearing one;
  3. even $n$ ⟹ boson, odd $n$ ⟹ fermion — the spin–statistics rule;
  4. the object so quantised is a density at a point, carried by the imaginary micro-universe (2-70) — a definition, and one that imports the whole micro-universe ontology.

Of these, (3) is established physics — the spin–statistics theorem — and it is imported, not derived. Nothing in the corpus's argument forces integer action to yield the observed statistics; the corpus's derivation of "fermion from a fused pair of half-integer states" is an argument for consistency, not for uniqueness. (1) is a commitment; (2) is the free choice; (4) is a definition of $U$ rather than a dynamical claim.

And on (2) the programme's own S11 audit has already ruled. The equipartition is unobservable in the mass sector — the lepton mass equations contain only the sum $S + P = 2(n+\tfrac{1}{2})h$, so the factor 2 can be traded into the fitted $T$ with the fit unchanged — and where it is observable it is false: the parity rule puts the muon and tau on spin-$\tfrac{3}{2}$ and spin-$\tfrac{5}{2}$ rungs, while both are measured spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$. That consequence is post-mortem as P23. So the axiom's one distinctive assertion is either invisible to measurement or contradicted by it.

Finding 2 — The Axioms Cannot Be Enumerated, and That Is a Foundational Defect

This is the article's hardest finding and it is a defect of exactly the kind the programme's ambition could not afford.

The corpus presents an axiomatic reconstruction of physics — three axioms, not two. But the axiomatics does not survive contact with the printed text. The register's own correction list records: "Axiome 3 is two different axioms" — the label designates different statements in different sections; "Axiome 5 unprinted"; a stray "axiome 6" reference in Esp 087; Axiome 4 (the spin–pulse equality $S^{2} = P^{2} = \tfrac{1}{4}n^{2}h^{2}$) is admitted rather than derived; Axiome 2 (stationary action) is never applied to the split (S14 generalises this: it is not applied to the $U$ equation either); and the 1983 book prints the self-contradictory $\hbar = 2\pi\hbar$ twice, which RC prints correctly. The register's adopted rule — never cite an axiom by number — is the correct response.

Why this is a foundational defect and not a proofreading complaint. An axiomatic reconstruction is checkable exactly to the extent that its axioms can be listed. If the axiom set cannot be enumerated from the corpus, then the programme's central claim — that physics rests on a small set of stated principles — cannot be evaluated, and the claim is thereby not made good rather than merely unproven. A reader cannot ask "which axiom forbids this?" because the numbers do not resolve to statements. This is the ambition-versus-delivery gap in its most concrete form: the corpus promised a foundation and printed an index.

And it is worth contrasting with what the corpus does well, because the contrast is instructive. Where the corpus states a named principle with a sentence — the quantification axiom quoted above; the Cartan conditions (S7); the zero-total-energy stipulation (S50) — the principle is clear, quotable and testable. It is the numbering that is unstable, not the physics. So the defect is real but repairable in principle: the corpus's principles can be recovered by quotation, which is exactly why this programme cites pages and not axiom numbers.

Finding 3 — The Passage to RGQ Is a Substitution, and It Is Exact

The passage from the axiom to the quantum equation runs through the curvature law, and the substitution closes exactly.

  • By (2-70), $U = \pm nh/V_{4}$ — the action per unit four-volume.
  • By (2-72), $R - 4\Lambda = \xi^{2}\rho^{2}$, and the corpus's normalisation is $M_{0} = \Omega_{0}\rho$ with $\xi \equiv \Omega_{0}c/\hbar$.
  • Therefore $M_{0}^{2}c^{2}/\hbar^{2} = (\Omega_{0}c/\hbar)^{2}\rho^{2} = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} = R - 4\Lambda$, exactly — an identity, not an approximation.
  • So (2-71)'s identification is exact, and the quantum equation is the Klein–Gordon equation for a scalar whose mass term is read off the curvature of space at the point.

The identity has an elegant geometric reading, which the programme has not recorded before and which is worth a line in its own right. Substituting $\rho = M_{0}/\Omega_{0}$: $R - 4\Lambda = (M_{0}c/\hbar)^{2}$, so $1/\sqrt{R - 4\Lambda}$ is the reduced Compton wavelength $\hbar/M_{0}c$ of the mass at that point. Numerically, at the electron's proper density $\rho_{e} = m_{e}/\Omega_{0} = 3{,}7297\times10^{13}$ g cm$^{-3}$: $R - 4\Lambda = \xi^{2}\rho_{e}^{2} = 6{,}7060\times10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$, whose curvature radius is $3{,}8616\times10^{-11}$ cm — against $\hbar/m_{e}c = 3{,}8616\times10^{-11}$ cm. The two agree to the last figure shown, and the agreement is exact, not approximate: with $\xi \equiv \Omega_{0}c/\hbar$ the two expressions are one identity written twice. The residual that appears if one mixes the corpus's rounded printed values is $0{,}05\,\%$, which is the rounding of $\xi$ and $\Omega_{0}$ and not a physics gap. So the corpus's "curvature at a point" is $(2\pi/\text{Compton wavelength})^{2}$: mass is curvature in this framework, with $\xi$'s definition $\xi \equiv \Omega_{0}c/\hbar$ being precisely the choice that makes the two agree. A definition made to work — and it works.

Finding 4 — What the Passage Does Not Deliver

Three limits, stated so that the positive above is not overread.

  • It is a scalar equation. Klein–Gordon describes spin-0. The electron needs spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$, which the corpus obtains from the fusion machinery of de Broglie's school — the $16^{2} = 256$ component count and its relatives (§2.6, S12's subject) — not from the passage to RGQ. So "the passage to RGQ" does not by itself quantise the electron, and the corpus's spin sector inherits the fusion machinery's problems (P23, P24).
  • The quantisation is in the interpretation, not the dynamics. $U$'s value is quantised by (2-70), but $U$'s equation is the standard Klein–Gordon equation. Any massive scalar obeys the same equation. So the "quantum" content of RGQ is one relation, $U = \pm nh/V_{4}$, and it is a definition of $U$.
  • No new prediction follows. The equation is standard, its mass term is fixed by $\rho$, and $\rho$ is an input (S34's ledger). So RGQ differs from the Klein–Gordon equation by an interpretation of $U$ and by nothing observable — unless the micro-universe sector supplies a difference, which is the business of S17–S28 and, so far, of P23 and P24.

Verdict

One free choice, one borrowed theorem, one stable equation, and a foundation that cannot be listed.

  • What is genuinely to the corpus's credit. (a) The existence argument behind the axiom is the strongest thing the quantification does: an integer total action cannot be carried by a single half-integral object, so some pairing is needed, and the corpus supplies one. (b) The passage to RGQ is carried out exactly — the mass term is not fitted but substituted, and the substitution is an identity. (c) The resulting geometric reading, curvature = $1/(\text{reduced Compton wavelength})^{2}$, is elegant, dimensionally correct, and verified numerically here to better than $0{,}1\,\%$. That is a real result of the framework, and the register does not currently carry it. (d) The axiom's derivable consequence (even/odd $\leftrightarrow$ boson/fermion) is correct physics.
  • Where it fails. (a) The distinctive assertion — exact spin–pulse equipartition — is invisible to the mass measurements that could test it and contradicted by the spins of the muon and the tau it predicts (P23). (b) The axiomatics cannot be enumerated, so the "axiomatic" claim cannot be checked; Axiome 4 is admitted, Axiome 3 is two axioms, Axiome 5 is unprinted. (c) The passage to RGQ delivers the Klein–Gordon equation's content with a geometric mass term and no observable difference from Klein–Gordon itself.
  • Net. §2.3 stays [SPEC], with the split tagged [PARTIAL] for the spin-ladder failure (§2.3's register note already carries this). And this article adds the positive: the mass term of RGQ is exactly the curvature, in the sense that curvature equals the inverse squared reduced Compton wavelength — an identity the corpus earned by its choice of $\xi$, and the framework's cleanest internal consistency.

Summary of Notation

  • $n$ — a non-zero integer; the total action at $P$ is $\pm nh$; $n$ even ⟹ boson, $n$ odd ⟹ fermion.
  • spin, pulse — the two halves of the action, each $\pm n\hbar/2$; "pulse" is the corpus's term for the second (radial pulsation) degree of freedom of a point, with no standard counterpart.
  • $U$ — the action density, $U = dA/dV_{4}$, quantised by (2-70) as $U_{P} = (\pm nh/V_{4})_{P}$.
  • $V_{4}$ — four-dimensional hypervolume of the imaginary micro-universe at $P$, over one pulsation $T$.
  • $\xi$ — $\equiv \Omega_{0}c/\hbar$, the lepton-sector constant (RC 051 (3-70)); it is the choice of $\xi$ that makes $R - 4\Lambda = (M_{0}c/\hbar)^{2}$.
  • RGQ — Relativité générale quantifiée, the corpus's name for its quantised framework; the "passage to RGQ" is the substitution chain (2-70)+(2-72)$\to$(2-71).
  • Axiome 4 — the spin–pulse equality $S^{2} = P^{2} = \tfrac{1}{4}n^{2}h^{2}$; admitted, not derived; the "facteur 2".

Charon — The Holon Defined (S24)

Audits §5.2 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md, against RC ch. IV (scans 058–063) and Esp §VIII (scans 081–091).

Introduction

The claim stands, and this article is the first in the series to say so about a mechanism rather than about an arithmetic. §5.2 describes the holon as "a compact closed $K = +1$ lump, density maximum at the centre, zero at the boundary, radially pulsating at $T_{0}$", tags it [SPEC] with real relatives, and names the MIT bag and the Skyrmion. The article's job was to find out whether the object exists as a solution of the corpus's own equations. It does.

The Système $H$ was re-integrated here, from the corpus's own printed equations, and it has the properties the definition requires. The density runs from $\delta = 1$ at the centre to $\delta = 0$ at a finite boundary $x_{\Gamma}$; the radial impulse runs from $P_{0}$ to exactly $\omega/2$; the Schwarzschild ratio $s = 2l/x$ stays below $0{,}33$ everywhere, so the no-black-hole remark of RC 062 is confirmed with a wide margin; and the mass curve $M(\omega)$ has the minimum the corpus's Graph 2 shows, at the corpus's own $\omega$, with the corpus's own printed value to $0{,}25\,\%$ once the edition's $\xi$ is used. One integration produces the mass, the radius and the shape together.

Two defects were found on the way, both in the printed equations rather than in the physics. The first is decisive and is the article's main negative result: the sign in the Système $H$ box of RC 060, as transcribed, forbids the holon from existing. With that sign the density $\delta$ can never vanish, the boundary $\Gamma$ does not exist, and the "compact" lump becomes an infinite structure. The corpus's own equation two pages earlier, its own remark in Esp 089 and its own boundary conditions all require the opposite sign; with it, everything works and the printed numbers come out. The second is the normalisation of the mass integral, printed wrongly in one book and rightly in the other, and the $(4\text{-}16)$ cube, already recorded by S22.

What the Corpus Claims

§5.2 — the holon: a compact closed $K = +1$ lump, density maximum at the centre, zero at the boundary, radially pulsating at $T_{0}$. RC ch. IV §I (definition 1.1) + (4-0). Tag: [SPEC], with real relatives. The register's gloss: "close in spirit to the MIT bag model (1974) ... and to Skyrmions/solitons. Neither of those has a density that falls to zero at a pulsating surface, though. The structural idea is respectable; the mechanism is not established."

The corpus's own definition, in full. RC 058:

Le nom HOLON désigne, en Relativité complexe, une forte courbure « localisée, compacte et refermée sur elle-même », délimitant dans le Réel une Forme individuelle, grossièrement sphérique (sphéroïde aplati), comportant une courbure (densité propre) maximum en son centre et une courbure minimum (densité propre nulle) à sa périphérie, cette Forme possédant en outre une pulsation radiale, quantifiée à une action demi-entière.

and RC 065, definitions 2.7 and 2.8: "Nous appelons HADRON le holon associé aux six niveaux de quarks décrits ci-dessus. Il n'existe qu'un seul holon, mais naturellement un grand nombre de hadrons." The single holon and the many hadrons matter for S25, and the six levels matter for P4; this article owns the lump, its solution, and its shape.

Scope of this article. The six level-to-quark identifications are P4's (§6.1, §10.9) and the density $\rho = m/\Omega_{0}$ is P21's (§5.5); neither is re-opened here. The pulsation condition $V_{r} = \pm c$ at the boundary is S23's (§3.9, §10.8). What is left, and what is audited here, is the object itself: is there a solution, is it compact, is it unique, does it have the shape claimed?

1. The Système $H$, as the Corpus Prints It

The equations. RC 059–060 and Esp 084–086 give one system twice, for $n = 1$ — the spin-½ case the corpus actually computes (Esp §VIII.6: "Nous nous en tiendrons donc ici à une analyse numérique du système d'équations (348) pour $n = 1$"). In the corpus's non-dimensional variables — $\rho = \rho_{0}\delta$, $P_{j}/\hbar = \xi\rho_{0}p_{j}$, $x = \xi\rho_{0}r$, $l = \xi\rho_{0}u$ — the spherically symmetric approximation with $p_{1}^{2} = p_{2}^{2} = p_{3}^{2} = p^{2}$ gives (RC (4-8), Esp (342)):

dP²/dx = −(δ² + 4P²)(P²x³ + 2l) / (2x(x − 2l))

dl/dx  = (x²/2)(δ² + 3P²)

the third relation (RC (4-12), Esp (348)), and the closure data (RC (4-13), Esp (349)–(350)):

δ² = P²(4P² − ω²)/ω² ,   l(0) = 0 ,   δ(0) = 1 ,   δ(x_Γ) = 0

The corpus's own remark about these equations is correct and worth quoting, because it places the object in an established family: "les équations (4-8) sont celles qui sont utilisées pour étudier en espace riemannien les étoiles super-denses, chez lesquelles la densité centrale est de l'ordre des densités hadroniques. Cependant, le terme $p^{2}$ des équations holoniques (4-8) joue, dans le cas des étoiles super-denses, le rôle de la pression $p$." The equations are the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff system (Tolman 1939; Oppenheimer–Volkoff 1939), with $p^{2}$ in the place of the pressure. What replaces the equation of state is the pulse quantisation $P\,v\,T_{0} = (n + \tfrac{1}{2})h$ (RC (4-9), Esp (343)), which in non-dimensional form is the third relation above. So the departure from established physics is entirely (i) the quadratic source, which is S10's $\rho^{2}$ law, and (ii) the third relation, which is the pulse. Nothing else in the system is new, and saying so is in the object's favour: it is a solvable system whose behaviour is known.

The closure is one equation, not two. Esp (350) prints $P_{0}^{2} = (n^{2}\omega^{2}/8)\,[1 + \sqrt{1 + 16/(n^{2}\omega^{2})}]$ as a numerical recipe. Recomputed: that formula is exactly the statement $\delta(0) = 1$. Substituting $\delta = 1$, $P = P_{0}$ into the third relation gives $4P_{0}^{4} - \omega^{2}P_{0}^{2} - \omega^{2} = 0$, whose positive root is the printed formula — verified symbolically and to twelve figures at $n = 1$. So the "solution of the Système $H$" has one free non-dimensional parameter, $\omega$, and no separate initial-value condition: $P_{0}$ is determined by $\delta(0) = 1$. The corpus uses this correctly. It also means $\omega$ is the only knob, which is what makes the sweep of S27 possible.

2. The Sign in the Box: With the Printed Sign There Is No Holon

RC 060's Système $H$ box prints, in both transcription layers (RC/060.tex line 49 and RC/060_improved.tex line 52):

δ² = p²(4p² + n²ω²)/(n²ω²)

with a plus. Esp (348) prints the same relation with a minus, and so does RC's own (4-10-a), two pages earlier in the same book:

ρ² = P²(4P²c² − n²ω₀²ħ²) / (Ω₀²n²c²ω₀²ħ²)

The minus is required, and the corpus says so in three independent ways. (i) The definition of the holon is a lump with "densité propre nulle à sa périphérie". With the plus, $\delta^{2} = P^{2}(1 + 4P^{2}/\omega^{2}) > 0$ for every $P > 0$, so $\delta$ can vanish only at $P = 0$ — and the plus-sign system was integrated here to $x = 200$: $P^{2}$ decays but never reaches zero, and $\delta^{2}$ stays positive. There is no boundary at any finite radius. The lump is not compact; it is an infinite atmosphere. (ii) The corpus's own boundary conditions require $\delta(x_{\Gamma}) = 0$ at a finite $x_{\Gamma}$ (Esp (349)). (iii) Esp 089, discussing the boundary, states: "On voit, d'après (348), que l'impulsion $P$ radiale en tout point de la frontière $\Gamma$ vaut $P_{\Gamma} = \pm \omega/2$... $P$ prend 2 valeurs distinctes pour $\delta = 0$, de part et d'autre de la frontière $\Gamma$." Two distinct values of $P$ at $\delta = 0$ is exactly what the minus gives and the plus cannot.

With the minus, the boundary is at $P = \omega/2$ exactly, and the integration terminates there. So the transcribed plus sign is a defect — either a transcription slip or a typographical error in the 1987 book — and it is load-bearing: with it, the object this article audits does not exist. It joins the repair list with a scan request, because only the printed page can say which layer is wrong.

And it is confirmed by the numbers, not only by the qualifiers. With the plus sign, integrating to $x = 200$ — where $\delta^{2}$ is still $2{,}4\times10^{-6}$, so no boundary has been reached — leaves a mass integral already at $1{,}8\times10^{-21}$ g and a radius of $5{,}6\times10^{-13}$ cm, factors $10^{3}$ and $33$ above the corpus's own solution, and both still growing with the cutoff. No choice of $\xi$ or $\omega$ repairs that. The minus is the corpus's own system.

3. The Solution: What the Integration Gives

Method. The system was integrated with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta scheme from $x = 10^{-8}$, using the series $l = Ax^{3}/6$ and $P^{2} = P_{0}^{2} - B(P_{0}^{2} + A/3)x^{2}/4$ near the singular origin, halted when $\delta^{2}$ reaches zero. Convergence was checked on step halving: at $\omega = 0{,}12$ and $\omega = 0{,}105$, $x_{\Gamma}$ is stable to eight digits from $h = 2\times10^{-5}$ down to $h = 2{,}5\times10^{-6}$, and the mass integral to five digits. The results below are at $h = 10^{-5}$, except the far-asymptotic points at large $\omega$, where the step was scaled to $x_{\Gamma}$ because the holon's radius shrinks as $\omega^{-2}$.

The three defining properties hold.

property required by §5.2 what the integration gives
density maximum at the centre $\delta(0) = 1$ exactly, by construction of $P_{0}$
density zero at the periphery $\delta = 0$ at a finite $x_{\Gamma}$, at $P = \omega/2$ exactly
compact and closed on itself $x_{\Gamma} = 6{,}200$ at $\omega = 0{,}12$; $7{,}089$ at $\omega = 0{,}105$; $2{,}83\times10^{-4}$ at $\omega = 100$ — finite for every $\omega \in (0, \infty)$

The no-black-hole remark is confirmed with a wide margin. RC 062 asserts $s = 2l/r < 1$ for every central density. Over a sweep of $\omega$ from $0{,}004$ to $2$ — a factor 500, and 60 solutions — the maximum of $s$ is $0{,}325$, reached near $\omega \approx 0{,}4$; near the nucleon's own solution $s \approx 0{,}31$. So the claim is not marginal: the holon sits a factor three away from forming a horizon, for every solution in the family, and the "the solution can never be a trou noir" sentence of the corpus is verified rather than asserted.

One caution about the pulsation, and it is a real one. The integration just performed solves a static spherically symmetric metric; nothing in it pulsates. The pulsation enters the system only as (i) the third relation, which is the quantised radial impulse, and (ii) the boundary condition $V_{r} = \pm c$, which is imposed and is S23's subject. The computed $P(x)$ is the field of a radial impulse, not a time-dependent solution. So §5.2's "radially pulsating at $T_{0}$" is an input to the equations, not an output of them — and this is the same structure S23 found: derived where the density vanishes, imposed where it does not.

4. Two Normalisations, One Cube, and Which Book Is Right

The mass integral. RC (4-14) and Esp (360) print the same quantity with different powers of the density:

printed relation factor dimension of the factor
RC (4-14) $4\pi/(\xi^{3}\rho_{0}^{3})$ cm⁻³ — not a mass
Esp (360), $M_{E}$ $4\pi/(\xi^{3}d_{0}^{2})$ g — correct
Esp (360), $V_{E}$ $4\pi/(\xi^{3}d_{0}^{2})$ g — not a volume

The correct powers follow from $r = x/(\xi\rho_{0})$ alone. A mass is $4\pi\rho_{0}\int(\delta^{2}+3P^{2})^{1/2}r^{2}dr = 4\pi/(\xi^{3}\rho_{0}^{2})\int(\delta^{2}+3P^{2})^{1/2}x^{2}dx$; a volume is $4\pi/(\xi^{3}\rho_{0}^{3})\int x^{2}dx$. So each book gets one of the two right and the other wrong: RC errs on the mass, Esp errs on the volume. The mass is the one that matters, and it is anchored independently: fed the corpus's own $\xi$, $\omega$ and the correct Esp normalisation, the integration of §3 returns the corpus's own printed mass — see §5. Had RC (4-14) been used, the mass would have come out smaller by one power of $\rho_{0}$, i.e. by $5{,}8\times10^{17}$.

The $(4\text{-}16)$ cube. For small $\rho_{0}$ the corpus gives an analytic approximation, $M_{0} = \sqrt{2/3}\,\xi^{2}c^{5}T_{0}^{5}\rho_{0}^{3}/\pi^{4}$ and $R_{0} = \xi c^{2}T_{0}^{2}\rho_{0}/(\sqrt{2}\pi^{2})$. The symbolic form carries $\rho_{0}^{3}$ and the printed numerical form does not — it reads $2{,}75\times10^{-73}\rho_{0}$ g, which is not a mass. Recomputed: the coefficient belongs to the cube. Evaluated at the corpus's own 1987 $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ and $T_{0} = 4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s, the symbolic coefficient is $2{,}733\times10^{-73}$, i.e. the printed $2{,}75$ to $0{,}6\,\%$; and the coefficient of $R_{0}$ is $1{,}064\times10^{-30}$ against the printed $1{,}07$, to $0{,}9\,\%$. The transcription's missing cube is the only defect; the coefficients are right. This confirms S22's reading and closes it.

And the two relations are genuinely asymptotic, in the range the corpus claims. Integrating the exact system at large $\omega$ (small $\rho_{0}$) gives $M/\rho_{0}^{3} \to 2{,}673\times10^{-73}$ and $R/\rho_{0} \to 1{,}0551\times10^{-30}$ as $\omega$ runs to 400 — $2{,}2\,\%$ below the analytic $M$ coefficient and $0{,}9\,\%$ from the analytic $R$ coefficient, the residual being the neglected correction terms. So $(4\text{-}16)$ is a fair approximation to the corpus's own system, not merely a plausible formula. The corpus's caution, that the computer could not reach these densities, is unnecessary today: the exact solution is available there and agrees.

The verdict on the curve: the shape is right, and the numbers are right under one of the two editions' constants. With $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ (RC's legend of Graph 3, and the one-figure-$\Omega_{0}$ reading of S22), the three printed mass numbers — $1{,}68$ at the minimum, $2{,}12$ at the maximum, $1{,}70$ at the limit — are reproduced to $0{,}25\,\%$, $0{,}5\,\%$ and $0{,}25\,\%$; with the 1983 book's printed $\xi = 6{,}467\times10^{-4}$ all three are about $5\,\%$ high. So the 1983 book's printed $\xi$ and its printed mass will not both stand, and the mass is the one that wins, because it is the one that is checked against a measured number. This finding is S27's to develop; S24 records it as a property of the solution, and the consequence is that the holon's mass, radius and shape are one solution of one system, with the free parameter fixed by the corpus's own closure and the constant fixed by the corpus's own value to within a factor $1{,}05$.

Two further features of the curve are worth recording because they bound the claims that can be made from it. First, the curve is shallow: over the whole family $\omega \in [0{,}004, 2]$, $M$ lies between $1{,}6766\times10^{-24}$ g and $2{,}1309\times10^{-24}$ g ($\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ throughout) — a range of $27\,\%$, with the minimum $21\,\%$ below the peak. The minimum is therefore a real feature but not a knife-edge, which cuts both ways: the nucleon's mass is not a fine-tuned coincidence, and it is also not sharply selected by the curve's shape. Second, the minimum is the only one: over the same sweep there is one minimum and one maximum, and no second minimum. Esp 091 §7 says "les états stationnaires correspondent aux minima de la courbe $M(d_{0})$, et sont au nombre de deux". With the corpus's own equations the count of minima is one. S27 resolves the sentence: the two stationary states are the curve's two extrema — the minimum and the maximum, which Esp 089 §6.2 in fact prints — so "minima" in that sentence is loose, and the corpus's own physics does not require a second minimum the equations do not have.

6. The Relatives: Bag, Skyrmion, and the TOV System

The register's comparison is fair, and the three objects differ in ways that matter. The MIT bag (Chodos, Jaffe, Johnson, Thorn, Weisskopf 1974) is a region of perturbed vacuum with a constant energy density $B$ and a pressure balance at a static surface, in which confined quark fields are placed by hand; the Skyrmion (Skyrme 1961; Witten 1983) is a topological soliton of the chiral field whose energy density peaks at the centre and falls off smoothly, with baryon number as a winding number. Charon's holon is a third thing: a solution of a metric equation whose source is a function of the curvature it produces, since $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} + 4\Lambda$ (4-0) is the corpus's own identification of density with curvature, and the density is not a matter density but a geometric measure of it.

Where the holon is genuinely like its relatives: it is compact and extended; its mass comes out of its own geometry with one adjustable constant; it has a boundary where the source vanishes; and the mass curve has a minimum, so a stable lump is selected rather than imposed.

Where it is not: (i) the surface pulsates and moves at $c$ — no counterpart in the bag or the Skyrmion, and it is imposed rather than solved (S23); (ii) the source is quadratic in the density, which is what gives the system its TOV-like form with $p^{2}$ for the pressure and is S10's law, not an analogy the corpus borrows; (iii) the density in question is $\rho = m/\Omega_{0}$, a mass per elementary cell rather than a matter density, so $\rho_{H} = 5{,}8\times10^{17}$ g cm⁻³ is not a hadronic density in the bag's sense at all — that is P21's finding, and it means the "central density" of this article's §5 is a different quantity from the bag constant $B^{1/4} \approx 200$ MeV. (iv) The corpus's own compromise is worth recording here: "Nous ne devons donc pas ambitionner ici autre chose que l'obtention des principales caractéristiques qualitatives des hadrons" (Esp §VIII.6), because the real object is axially symmetric (it has spin) and the system solved is spherical. The corpus knows its solution is an approximation to its own object, and says so.

7. Verdict

§5.2 stands as stated. The holon is a compact closed lump with a density maximum at the centre and zero at a finite boundary, and it is a genuine solution of the corpus's own field equation, verified by independent integration of the printed system. The tag does not move: [SPEC] with real relatives, and the mechanism is not established physics. The pulse is an input, the boundary condition is imposed, and the density is a geometric construct.

The article's additions to the register. (1) The Système $H$ box of RC 060, as transcribed, carries a sign that makes the holon impossible; the corpus's own (4-10-a), its own boundary conditions and its own remark at Esp 089 all require the other sign, and with it the corpus's printed numbers come out. (2) The closure $\delta(0) = 1$ and the printed $P_{0}$ formula are the same equation — one free parameter, $\omega$, and nothing else. (3) The mass normalisation is right in Esp (360) and wrong in RC (4-14); Esp's own volume normalisation is wrong in the mirror-image way. (4) The no-black-hole claim $s < 1$ holds with a factor-three margin across the whole family. (5) Graph 2 is recovered: one minimum at $\omega \approx 0{,}105$, one maximum, oscillations at large density, and a finite limit — with the printed numbers reproduced to $\lesssim 0{,}5\,\%$ under the 1987 $\xi$ and $\approx 5\,\%$ high under the 1983 one. (6) The pulsation is not an output of the system, and the corpus's own text says the solved object is a spherical approximation to an axial one.

What this article does not claim. It does not claim the holon is a physical particle: the solution's existence is a mathematical fact about the corpus's equations, and §5.2's [SPEC] tag is exactly the statement that the identification is not earned. It does not re-audit the six levels (P4), the density definition (P21), the boundary condition (S23) or the mass value (S27). The "two stationary states" sentence is resolved by S27: the two stationary points are the curve's two extrema, which Esp 089 §6.2 prints.

Charon: Where Does the Top Quark Go?

Introduction

P4 shows that the six holonic levels are the charged-lepton masses with quark names attached. This article follows the consequence to the one flavour that makes the failure unmistakable. The corpus's heaviest level is the top, at about 8.5 GeV. The real top quark is 172.7 GeV, twenty times heavier, and it was not discovered until 1995 — eight years after the book. The corpus put the top in the lightest part of its spectrum, and the top turned out to be the heaviest particle in the Standard Model.

The claim. RC 063, table (3-79), row 5: $n = 5$, symbol $t$, name top, $\rho_{5} = 4{,}99\times10^{17}$ g cm$^{-3}$, $r_{5} = 1{,}7\times10^{-16}$ cm, $m_{5} = 1{,}52\times10^{-23}$ g. Converting: $m_{5} = 8{,}5$ GeV$/c^{2}$. The corpus presents the level scheme as complete: six levels, one per flavour, all of them inside the holon.

The verdict is [FALS], as a spectrum. The top's measured mass is $172{,}7$ GeV$/c^{2}$, found by CDF and D0 at Fermilab in 1995. The corpus's assignment misses by a factor of twenty, and the miss is not a numerical error to be repaired: the ladder has no rung within a factor of twenty of the top, and the ladder's step and ceiling leave no room to add one.

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §6.1 and §6.2, sourced to table (3-79) at RC/063_improved.md. The transition-energy audit is S49's, from RC 066. The parent articles are P4 (the identification) and P1 (the ladder). The related chains are R7 and R11.

Conventions. Gaussian cgs. $n$ is the level index, $m_{n}$ the level mass, $r_{n}$ the level radius, $\rho_{H}$ the holon's central density. The top mass is the PDG direct-measurement average, $172{,}7 \pm 0{,}3$ GeV$/c^{2}$.

Part 1 — The gap, stated in the corpus's own numbers

The corpus gives the top a mass of $1{,}52\times10^{-23}$ g. The measured top mass, in the same units, is $3{,}08\times10^{-22}$ g. The ratio is $20{,}3$.

The gap is not closable by relabelling. P4 shows the levels are ordered by the ladder index, and the corpus fixes each level's quark name. One could imagine reassigning the name top to a higher level — but there is no higher level in the table, and the table stopped because the density passed the holonic ceiling. Extending the ladder to 173 GeV would need about a hundred further rungs, each with a smaller radius, all packed inside a holon whose radius is of order $10^{-14}$ cm. The scheme is not short of one level; it is short of the entire structure that could hold the top.

The geometric absurdity, made explicit. From table (3-79) the level radii run

$$r_{0} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}, \quad r_{1} = 1{,}79\times10^{-14}, \quad r_{2} = 4{,}2\times10^{-15}, \quad r_{3} = 1{,}7\times10^{-15}, \quad r_{4} = 4{,}2\times10^{-16}, \quad r_{5} = 1{,}7\times10^{-16}\ \mathrm{cm}.$$

A level at 173 GeV would sit far deeper still, at a radius the corpus's holon solution does not reach. The level scheme is not a spectrum with a hole at the top; it is a scheme in which the top cannot appear.

Part 2 — What the top actually is

The top is not merely heavier than the corpus expected. It is qualitatively different from every other quark, which is why a level scheme built on an arithmetic ladder cannot represent it.

Its mass is the electroweak scale. $m_{t} = 172{,}7$ GeV is close to the Higgs vacuum expectation value, $v = 246$ GeV. Its Yukawa coupling is $y_{t} = \sqrt{2}\,m_{t}/v \approx 0{,}99 \approx 1$. No other fermion has a Yukawa coupling near one; the next largest, the bottom, is about $0{,}024$. A scheme that treats the top as one more rung of a mass ladder has no way to express that it is the only fermion whose mass is naturally of the electroweak order.

It decays before it can bind. The top's lifetime is about $5\times10^{-25}$ s, shorter than the hadronisation time of about $3\times10^{-24}$ s. So the top decays to a $W$ and a bottom before it can form a hadron. There is no toponium — no $t\bar{t}$ bound state — whereas the charm and bottom form the $J/\psi$ and the $\Upsilon$ that P6 uses. The corpus's quarkonium construction, in which a quark and antiquark sit on a level and cancel, has no counterpart for the top at all.

Discovery history. The top was discovered in 1995 by the CDF and D0 experiments at the Tevatron, eight years after RC's publication. Before that, its existence was inferred indirectly. So the corpus named a particle that was not yet discovered and gave it a mass twenty times too small — a striking outcome for a scheme whose claim to notice was that it could assign the flavours.

Part 3 — What the corpus's transition energies do and do not cover

S49's audit of RC 066 records the corpus's level-transition energies: the printed ratios follow an effective index $1{,}42$ in $r_{n}$, the only clean agreement is $E_{2}/E_{1} = 16{,}64$ against $\rho_{2}/\rho_{1} = 16{,}86$ (1.3 %), and the highest transition energies run into the hundreds and thousands of GeV.

The energies are not the masses. It is tempting to answer the top gap by pointing at the corpus's high transition energies. That would be a category error: those energies are differences between levels, not level masses, and S49 shows the printed ratios follow no clean law and cannot be recomputed without the holon solution (4-11), which the corpus never exhibits. The transition ladder is not a substitute for a top quark.

Where the corpus itself leaves the comparison. S49 notes that RC 066 breaks off at "…des énergies de l'ordre de 4 GeV : rappelons que", and that the comparison with known particles is therefore absent. 4.14 GeV is 34 % above the $J/\psi$; 68.9 GeV is 14 % below the $W$ and 24 % below the $Z$, both discovered in 1983, the book's own year; and the range 253–3022 GeV has no counterparts. The one place the corpus might have met the top's neighbourhood is exactly where the argument stops.

Summary

The corpus's heaviest quark level is the top at 8.5 GeV; the real top is 172.7 GeV, a factor of 20.3 above. The gap is not repairable: the level scheme is an arithmetic ladder truncated at the holonic density ceiling, and about a hundred further rungs would be needed to reach the top's mass, at radii the holon does not reach. The top is also qualitatively different — its Yukawa coupling is near one, and it decays before hadronising, so it forms no quarkonium — which a mass ladder cannot express. The corpus named an undiscovered particle and made it the lightest thing in its spectrum. Verdict [FALS] as a spectrum.

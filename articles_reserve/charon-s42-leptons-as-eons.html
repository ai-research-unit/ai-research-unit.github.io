Charon — Leptons as Éons (S42)

Audits inventory §4.1 (charged leptons are the discrete imaginary éons) and §4.2 (all charged leptons share the pulsation period $T_{0}$ and are in phase). Sources: RC 050 §VI, RC 051 (3-68)–(3-72), RC 052 §1–§3, Esp 059 §18.1, Esp 060 §18.2, Esp 087's Axiom 7.

Introduction

The identification is the load-bearing piece of the lepton sector, and the register's own gloss on its second half — "needed to make charge identical for all éons; no support" — is half right and half backwards. The corpus says the electrically charged éons of the imaginary space are the charged leptons, each appearing in the real space as a point (RC 050 §VI, Esp 059 §18.1). §4.1's claim is an interpretation and it does real work: it is what makes the lepton sector's numbers and the éon sector's numbers the same numbers. §4.2's claim is an axiom, Esp 060 and Esp 087 invoke it for two different things, and — this is the finding — it is not needed for the charge at all.

Why not needed, in one step. The charge is the end of the electromagnetic ladder, and S36 shows that ladder to be a function of $\gamma = -c\beta/h$ and pure numbers alone: $R$, $\theta$, the pulsation and the period all cancel because the blackbody relation (245) makes $R^{5}\theta^{5}$ a constant of the motion. $\beta$ is one number for the whole Système E (RC (3-68)), so every éon carries the same charge however its phase sits and whichever éon it is. The equality of leptonic charges is therefore a structural property of the single $\beta$, and the phase-locking axiom does not carry it. The register's "no support" stands; its "needed" does not.

What §4.2 is actually for. Esp 060 invokes the axiom to make the virtual exchange between two leptons simultaneous — "il y a un échange simultané de signe des impulsions respectives entre deux univers" — which is a statement about the interaction picture and about energy-momentum conservation, not about the value of the charge. Esp 087's Axiom 7 states something else again: a hadron is observable only if its own pulsation resonates with the charged-lepton model's $\omega_{0} = 1{,}28\times10^{24}$ s$^{-1}$. So one printed axiom carries two claims, and the one §4.2 names is neither of them as stated. That is the same defect S5 records for the axiom numbering generally, and rule R5's discipline — cite axioms by content and source — is what it costs.

And the price of the identification, which the article states plainly because it is the reason the claim cannot be a test. If all éons share $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$, $\xi$ and one trajectory $R(\tau)$ — and they do, by the structure of the Système E — then they also share their radius, their internal temperature, their photon count and their quantised neutrino orbit ($Y = 2{,}27$ for every $n$, RC (3-74-a)). Everything an éon has is identical across the leptons; the only thing that differs is the mass, and the mass is $\rho_{n}\Omega_{0}$, the discrete imaginary density, which the éon's own physics does not determine. So the identification makes the exact equality of charges natural and leaves the mass spectrum as the input it always was — which is exactly S21's and S34's finding, reached from the other side.

The ledger question this article has to settle, therefore: does §4.1 explain an observed fact, or accommodate it? Answer: it accommodates it by construction and explains it in one number. The corpus does not fit the charge of each lepton; it builds one éon model and repeats it, with the mass supplied from outside. An explanation of this kind cannot be tested by the equality of charges — any single-$\beta$ construction gives that — and the tests it does face are elsewhere: the fractional-charge consequence (S47/S48, R6), and the coupling (S35). Both are the corpus's own, and one of them survives as a mechanism while the other's agreement is inherited.

The identification, from RC 050 §VI's own title — "Les éons électriquement chargés de l'Imaginaire, et les leptons chargés ponctuels du Réel" — and from RC 051:

Les masses $m_{n}$, qui devraient en principe correspondre aux masses des leptons chargés, dont on connaît expérimentalement aujourd'hui trois représentants (l'électron, le muon et le lepton « lourd » tau).

Nous appelons ÉON ce modèle électriquement chargé ($\beta \neq 0$) de l'espace Imaginaire. Les éons sont donc les solutions particulières du Système $E$ pour une suite discontinue de valeurs $\rho_{n}$ de la densité $\rho_{n}$ au point $P$ du Réel. Cependant, la solution éonique NE VIENT JAMAIS SEULE au point $P$.

The one period, and the corpus's own warning about two, from RC 052 §1:

$T$ varie notamment avec chaque $m$, alors que $T_{0}$ reste le même pour toutes les solutions particulières éoniques $m_{n}$.

The phase-locking, from Esp 060 §18.2:

Nous admettrons aussi (notre axiome 7 ci-dessous, section VIII) que toutes les particules en pulsation, et notamment les leptons, ont la même période et sont en phase.

And Axiom 7's own text, from Esp 087:

Pour qu'un hadron soit observable, c'est-à-dire possède une durée de vie non nulle, il faut que sa pulsation propre $\omega_{0}$ soit en résonance avec la pulsation propre $\omega_{0}$ du modèle leptonique chargé : $\omega_{0} = 1{,}28\cdot10^{24}$ s$^{-1}$.

1. What the identification claims, and what it is worth

The éon is a definite object in the corpus's system, not a name for the lepton. It is the particular solution of the Système E with $\beta \neq 0$ — that is, with an internal blackbody — as against the préon, the general solution with $\alpha = \beta = 0$ in which only a massless neutrino circulates and $R = R_{0} \pm c\tau$. The éons come in a discontinuous sequence of densities $\rho_{n}$, and the corpus adds a structural point that matters here: the éonic solution never comes alone, because the préonic solution superposes on it at the same real point $P$. The éonic part plays the "rôle corpusculaire", the préonic part generates the $\psi$ phase wave (RC 052 §1). So the identification is: the charged leptons are the éonic particular solutions; each real lepton is a point that carries both.

The corpus equips the éon with one trajectory and one clock, and this is the key to everything below. With (3-68)'s $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ all fixed by the three measured masses, and $\xi$ fixed by the closure $l_{0} = R_{H}$, the Système E returns $R_{0} = 8{,}01\times10^{-15}$ cm and $\mathcal{R} = 4{,}82\times10^{-14}$ cm (RC (3-72)) — a single trajectory $R(\tau)$, shared by every éon, because the existence bounds (3-69-1) and the coupling (3-49-a) fix $R_{0}$ and $\xi$ once. The leptons differ only in $\rho_{n}$. Verified structurally, not numerically: nothing in (3-53) or (3-68) is indexed by $n$.

So the identification's content is strong and its scope is narrow. Strong: one model of a charged extended structure, repeated, with no lepton-specific parameter. Narrow: the repetition is what makes every internal property identical, and the model has no way to distinguish the leptons except through the density it is handed. The register's §4.1 tag [SPEC] is right, and the claim's value is that it makes the lepton sector's numbers and the éon sector's numbers the same numbers — which is why S20, S21, S30, S32 and S35 can all be read as statements about leptons and about éons at once.

2. What the identification explains for free: one charge, three masses

One charged-lepton fact is exact to the precision of measurement and no theory of its vintage should be able to accommodate it by accident: the electron, the muon and the tau carry the same charge, although their masses span three orders of magnitude. (The electron's charge is fixed to a part in $10^{21}$ by the neutrality of matter; the muon's and the tau's are established to whatever precision their own measurements permit, and the model's statement is that all three are $e$ exactly, with no fitting.) The corpus reproduces it without fitting, and the reason is short.

The charge is a function of the lepton spectrum's shape, not of which éon is considered. S36's derivation ends at

$$\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar c} = \frac{8\pi^{3}}{3}\left(\frac{15\gamma}{8\pi^{5}}\right)^{5/4}\int_{1{,}92}^{\infty}\frac{x^{4}}{e^{x}-1}\,dx,$$

which contains no $R$, no $\theta$, no $T_{0}$ and no $m$; the only physical content is $\gamma = -c\beta/h$, and $\beta$ is one number for the whole Système E. Since no rung of the ladder is indexed by the éon, the charge cannot vary from éon to éon. That is the exact mechanism of the equality, and it is a mechanism the corpus never states: the corpus credits the shared period, and the shared period has nothing to do with it.

What the shared period is for is the éon's internal consistency and the interaction picture. The orbit condition (3-73), $\frac{1}{T_{0}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{Y^{2}R'^{2}/c^{2}}{\sqrt{1+Y^{2}R'^{2}/c^{2}}} = 1$, has the solution $Y = 2{,}27$ for every $n$ — a single quantised neutrino orbit for all three leptons, verified structurally in the same way. So the corpus's own formalism already delivers the "same period, same phase" conclusion as a consequence of the single Système E, which makes Esp 060's "admettrons" an axiom with nothing left to assume — and leaves Axiom 7's hadron resonance with the work to do, which S27 verifies at $0{,}4\,\%$ against the corpus's printed $1{,}28\times10^{24}$ s$^{-1}$.

3. The two periods, and why the corpus's warning matters

RC 052's sentence — "$T$ varie notamment avec chaque $m$, alors que $T_{0}$ reste le même" — is a warning placed exactly where the two periods could be confused, and it is right about the corpus's own equations. The préonic period is $T = 2(n+\frac{1}{2})h/(mc^{2})$, i.e. $(2n+1)h/(mc^{2})$, which at $n = 0$ is (3-54)'s $h/(mc^{2})$ — the neutrino's circulation clock, scaling as $1/m$:

lepton $T = h/(mc^{2})$ $T/T_{0}$
$e$ $8{,}09\times10^{-21}$ s $1641$
$\mu$ $3{,}91\times10^{-23}$ s $7{,}9$
$\tau$ $2{,}33\times10^{-24}$ s $0{,}47$

and $T_{0} = 2h/(c^{2}(\tau-\mu)) = 4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s, one number (S21: fitted to the muon–tau spacing, which is what makes the spacing a definition rather than a prediction). So the lepton-to-lepton ratio of the mass-dependent period is 3477 — the mass ratio — and an identification that ran on $T$ would have the three éons a factor of three thousand out of phase with one another. The identification runs on $T_{0}$, and the table is the reason the warning exists. Verified: the two periods are different objects in the corpus's own text, they differ by a factor $1{,}6\times10^{3}$ for the electron, and only one of them is common to the leptons.

4. What the identification does not deliver

Four things are outside it, and the article's fairness requires each to be stated.

(1) The mass spectrum is an input. The éons exist for a discontinuous set of densities, and the set is what the lepton masses are; the corpus supplies three measured masses and reads $\rho_{n}$ off them (RC (3-68) inverts the system for $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ from $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$). So the identification does not predict the spectrum; the spectrum is the data. S21's finding — that the tower's step $\tau-\mu$ is the definition of $T_{0}$ — is the same statement in the constants' language.

(2) Its extension is falsified. The model's own extrapolation of the sequence beyond the tau is the lepton tower at $\approx3{,}45$, $5{,}12$, $6{,}79$ GeV, and LEP's non-observation of charged leptons to $\sim100$ GeV kills it (R7, P1). The identification therefore has exactly the number of members experiment allows, three, and no predictive content about how many there should be.

(3) The electron's size is falsified. If the lepton's real trace is a point, the model must not assign the éon's $10^{-14}$ cm scale to anything the electron shows; and $g-2$'s agreement with QED to ten digits forbids composite structure at that scale (P3, §4.7). The corpus's own reading — that the éon is a point for the entropic observer, and that no observable space connects the extended structure to it — is the only consistent one, and it is what makes the identification untestable rather than tested.

(4) The equality of charges is not a test of it, and this is the article's main caution. Any construction that makes the charge a function of one universal number gets the equality for free. What is testable in the electromagnetic sector is the value, and that comparison belongs to S35: the ladder's one free number is $\gamma$, fixed by $\beta$ from the measured lepton masses, and the resulting $\alpha$ agrees with the independently measured $1/137$ to two per cent — inherited rather than achieved (S34, S37). So the lepton sector's honest tests are those two: the fractional-charge geometry, which survives as a mechanism while the quark identification dies (R6, S47/S48), and the coupling, whose agreement is the Système L's consistency on display.

5. Verdict

§4.1 is kept, as an interpretation that does real work, with its scope stated. The charged leptons are the éonic particular solutions of the Système E, and the identification is what allows the éon's radius, temperature, closure and constants to be read as statements about leptons. It is not a derivation of anything and it is not testable as such; its value is structural, and it is the reason the corpus's lepton sector has one model rather than three.

§4.2 is graded in two parts, and both are weakened. As an explanation of identical charge, it is unnecessary: the charge is a function of $\beta$ alone (S36, §2 above), so the corpus's formalism already forces the equality without any axiom about phase. As an axiom about a common period, it is redundant given the formalism: all éons share one $R(\tau)$ trajectory and one $T_{0}$ because the Système E has no $n$-dependence in its trajectory equations, so there is nothing for "admettrons" to assume. What survives is Axiom 7's hadron statement, which does work (S27), and Esp 060's use of the axiom for simultaneity, which is a statement about the interaction picture rather than about the charge.

Rule R5, applied: the inventory's §4.2 tag [SPEC] stands, and its gloss should read — "invoked to make the exchange simultaneous; not needed for the identity of the charges, which the single $\beta$ supplies; no independent support." And the identification's price should be recorded with it: repeating one éon model across the leptons makes every internal property identical and leaves the mass as the only difference, which is exactly the input column of S34's ledger.

Summary

The corpus's lepton sector rests on one identification: the charged leptons are the electrically charged éons of the imaginary space, appearing in the real space as points (RC 050 §VI, Esp 059 §18.1). The article's findings are three.

First, the identification is strong in what it explains and narrow in what it permits. One éon model, repeated, with no lepton-specific parameter: every éon shares the trajectory $R_{0} = 8{,}01\times10^{-15}$ cm, the period $T_{0} = 4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s, the constants $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\xi$, the photon count and the quantised neutrino orbit $Y = 2{,}27$ — a single number for all $n$. Only the density $\rho_{n}$, hence the mass, differs, and the density is what the measurements supply.

Second, the equality of the leptonic charges — exact to measurement precision across three orders of magnitude in mass — is a consequence of the single $\beta$, not of the phase-locking axiom. The charge is the end of the ladder, and the ladder (S36) is a function of $\gamma = -c\beta/h$ alone, with $R$, $\theta$ and $T_{0}$ cancelling through the blackbody relation. So the register's "needed to make charge identical" is corrected: the axiom is not needed for that, and given the Système E's own $n$-independence it is not needed for the common period either. What does work is Axiom 7's hadron resonance (S27) and Esp 060's use of phase for simultaneity.

Third, the identification's untestability and its price are stated together. Any single-$\beta$ construction gives identical charges, so the equality is not a test; the model's extension of the sequence is falsified by LEP; and the electron's $10^{-14}$ cm scale is excluded as structure by $g-2$, which leaves the identification as an interpretation of how the lepton sector's numbers hang together rather than as a claim about nature. Its two real tests are the corpus's own: the fractional-charge geometry, which stands as a mechanism, and the coupling, whose two-per-cent agreement is inherited from the lepton spectrum (S34, S35).

Annihilators of the General Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The zero divisor of a product is a property of one element alone: it says that some nonzero partner exists that the element kills. The annihilator says which partners those are, and it is the finer object. It is the set of elements killed by a fixed element, $$ A_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde q\star\tilde x=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde x\star\tilde q=0\}, $$ the left and the right annihilator (Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space, §The Zero Divisor and the Annihilator). When the product is carried by a multiplication operator the annihilator is the kernel of that operator, so it is a real subspace of the four-dimensional space $\mathbb{H}$.

The block of this article is the general quaternionic multiplication $$ \tilde p\star\tilde q=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q =\bigl[p_0q_0+\vec p\cdot\vec q\bigr]e_0+p_0\vec q-q_0\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q, $$ the product of the conjugate of the first factor with the second, read over $\mathbb{R}$ (Introduction to the General Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions). It is not associative, and it has $e_0$ as a left unit only. But over $\mathbb{R}$ the quaternion algebra is a division algebra and the norm $N(\tilde p)=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde p=p_0^2+|\vec p|^2$ is a sum of four squares, hence positive definite; since the norm is multiplicative, $N(\tilde p\star\tilde q)=N(\tilde p)N(\tilde q)$, the product has no zero divisors at all (Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra). The annihilator is the finer reading of this fact, and over $\mathbb{R}$ its answer is the smallest possible away from the origin.

The article is short, and it is short for one reason: the block has no zero divisors. There is no Zero Divisors article for it, because there is no zero-divisor set to describe, and the annihilator is read off the division property in a page. This is the element-level annihilator. It is not the structure-level Annihilators in the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, whose object is the annihilating set of an element that kills the whole space for a fixed product, the radical; here the product is fixed and the element varies.

The Annihilator of a Product

Definition (annihilator). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$ and let $\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}$. The left annihilator and the right annihilator of $\tilde q$ in $\star$ are $$ A_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde q\star\tilde x=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde q)=\{\tilde x:\tilde x\star\tilde q=0\}. $$ They are the kernels of the left and the right multiplication, and they are nonzero exactly when $\tilde q$ is a zero divisor of the corresponding side.

Throughout this article the product is the general quaternionic multiplication $\star$, and unqualified, annihilator means the annihilator of $\star$. The left multiplication is the plain left multiplication by the conjugate, $$ L_{\tilde p}\tilde x=\tilde p\star\tilde x=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde x, $$ and the right multiplication is its conjugate, $\tilde x\star\tilde p=(L_{\tilde p}\tilde x)^{\natural}=\tilde x^{\natural}\tilde p$. Beside $L_{\tilde p}$ it is convenient to keep the plain right multiplication by the conjugate, $$ R_{\tilde p}\tilde x=\tilde x\tilde p^{\natural}, $$ the mirror operator, of the same determinant and the same rank as $L_{\tilde p}$. The block is the real member of the general quaternionic family; its complex sibling is Annihilators of the General Quaternionic Algebra, where the same product has a zero-divisor cone and the annihilator is a complex plane on it.

The Two Annihilators Coincide

Theorem (the coincidence). For every $\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}$, $$ B_\star(\tilde q)=A_\star(\tilde q). $$

Proof. By the exchange law of the block, $\tilde x\star\tilde q=(\tilde q\star\tilde x)^{\natural}$ (Introduction to the General Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions). The right multiplication is therefore the conjugate of the left multiplication, and the conjugation is a bijection of $\mathbb{H}$ onto itself fixing $0$ alone, so $\tilde x\star\tilde q=0$ exactly when $\tilde q\star\tilde x=0$. Equivalently, conjugating the equation $\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde x=0$ gives $(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde x)^{\natural}=\tilde x^{\natural}\tilde q=0$, because ${}^{\natural}$ is an anti-automorphism of order two. Hence the two annihilators contain exactly the same elements.

The coincidence is a property of the product, and it holds for the same reason as over $\mathbb{C}$: the conjugation is a real-linear anti-automorphism of order two, so the first factor can be conjugated away without changing the annihilation. Over $\mathbb{R}$, however, the coincidence is vacuous: both sides are $\{0\}$ for a nonzero element (next section), so they agree because both are trivial. The substance of the block is not in the theorem but in the size of the common set.

The Annihilator Is Zero

Theorem (the common annihilator). Let $\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}$.

  • If $\tilde q\neq0$ then $A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)=\{0\}$.
  • If $\tilde q=0$ then $A_\star(\tilde q)=B_\star(\tilde q)=\mathbb{H}$.

In particular the block has no zero divisor: no nonzero element kills a nonzero partner.

Proof. By the multiplicativity and the definiteness of the norm, $\tilde q\star\tilde x=0$ gives $N(\tilde q)N(\tilde x)=N(0)=0$, hence $N(\tilde q)=0$ or $N(\tilde x)=0$, that is $\tilde q=0$ or $\tilde x=0$. So for $\tilde q\neq0$ the only solution of $\tilde q\star\tilde x=0$ is $\tilde x=0$, and $A_\star(\tilde q)=\{0\}$; the right annihilator is the same by the coincidence, or directly from $\tilde x\star\tilde q=0$. At $\tilde q=0$ the product vanishes for every $\tilde x$ and both annihilators are the whole space.

Corollary. The zero-divisor set of the block is empty away from the origin, and the block carries no Zero Divisors article. The emptiness is the definiteness of the norm, and not a property of the general quaternionic shape: over $\mathbb{C}$ the same shape has a cone.

The Multiplication Operator and the Rank

Proposition (the operator and its determinant). In the basis $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$ the operators are the plain left and right multiplications by the conjugate $\tilde p^{\natural}$, of determinant $$ \det L_{\tilde p}=N(\tilde p)^{2}=\bigl(p_0^2+|\vec p|^2\bigr)^{2},\qquad \det R_{\tilde p}=N(\tilde p)^{2}. $$

Proof. $L_{\tilde p}$ is the left multiplication of the plain algebra by the quaternion $\tilde p^{\natural}$, whose determinant is the square of the plain norm, $N(\tilde p^{\natural})^{2}$; the conjugation keeps the sum of four squares, so $N(\tilde p^{\natural})=N(\tilde p)$. The right multiplication is the plain right multiplication by the same conjugate, and the left and the right regular representations of the quaternion algebra have the same determinant.

Corollary (the rank and the annihilator). For every $\tilde p$,

$\tilde p$ $\operatorname{rank}L_{\tilde p}$ $\dim\ker L_{\tilde p}$ $\ker L_{\tilde p}$
$\neq0$ $4$ $0$ $\{0\}$
$0$ $0$ $4$ $\mathbb{H}$

and the same table holds for $R_{\tilde p}$.

Proof. The determinant $N(\tilde p)^2$ is a strictly positive real for $\tilde p\neq0$ and vanishes only at the origin. A $4\times4$ matrix with nonzero determinant has rank four and trivial kernel; the zero operator at the origin has rank zero and kernel the whole space. The right side is identical because $\det R_{\tilde p}$ is the same.

Remark. The determinant is a square and it is never negative, so over $\mathbb{R}$ it vanishes at the origin alone. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the same determinant vanishes on the cone $N(\tilde p)=0$, and there the rank drops to two. The real picture has no intermediate rank: it jumps from four to zero.

The Dichotomy

Theorem (the dichotomy). The annihilator of an element is $\{0\}$ at every nonzero element and the whole space $\mathbb{H}$ at the origin alone. Equivalently, the rank of the multiplication operator is four at every nonzero element and zero at the origin.

Proof. This is the rank table of the preceding section.

The dichotomy of the complex block has three rows — rank four off the cone, rank two on it, rank zero at the origin — and the passage to $\mathbb{R}$ deletes the middle one. The deletion is the division property: over $\mathbb{R}$ the norm is definite, so its only zero is the origin and the cone of the complex block shrinks to the single point $\{0\}$. What remains over $\mathbb{R}$ is the origin against everything else.

The Annihilator on the Remarkable Subspaces

The centre. A nonzero central element $\tilde p=\lambda e_0$ has $\tilde p^{\natural}=\lambda e_0$ and $L_{\lambda e_0}\tilde x=\lambda\tilde x$; the operator is the scalar $\lambda$ times the identity, invertible for $\lambda\neq0$, so its annihilator is $\{0\}$.

The vector subspace. A nonzero pure element $\tilde p=\vec p$ has $N(\tilde p)=|\vec p|^2>0$ and $L_{\vec p}\tilde x=-\vec p\tilde x$, again invertible; its annihilator is $\{0\}$.

Proposition (the reading on the subspaces). On the centre $\mathbb{R}e_0$ and on the vector subspace $V$ the annihilator of every nonzero element is $\{0\}$; only the origin has the whole space as annihilator.

Proof. Apart from the origin, the elements of the two subspaces have nonzero norm, and the theorem on the common annihilator applies to each.

The isotropic directions that would enlarge an annihilator, and that the complex block carries on its cone, do not exist over $\mathbb{R}$: the quaternionic form is definite, so neither subspace has an isotropic element other than $0$ (The Isotropic Structure of the General Quaternionic Algebra).

Summary

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners the element kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left and the right multiplication. The general quaternionic multiplication $\tilde p\star\tilde x=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde x$ carries the conjugation, a real-linear anti-automorphism of order two, so its two annihilators of an element always coincide, exactly as over $\mathbb{C}$. Over $\mathbb{R}$ they coincide vacuously: the two operators are the multiplications by the conjugate $\tilde p^{\natural}$ in the division algebra, of determinant $N(\tilde p)^2=(p_0^2+|\vec p|^2)^2$, invertible at every nonzero $\tilde p$. The common annihilator is therefore $\{0\}$ at every nonzero element and the whole space at the origin, and the dichotomy has these two rows and no more. The block has no zero divisors and no Zero Divisors article, and its element-level annihilator is distinct from the structure-level annihilating set of Annihilators in the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, which fixes the product and reads the elements that kill the whole space. The complex sibling, where the norm becomes isotropic, has a cone and a plane on it; over $\mathbb{R}$ the division property leaves nothing.

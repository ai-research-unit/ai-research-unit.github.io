Charon — Collective and Individual Behaviour (S55)

Audits inventory §9.4–§9.5. Sources: RC 020 §3.5, RC 021 §I. Hand-off from S54 (the "regard" becomes steering) and to S56 (the memory).

Introduction

This is the most defensible of Charon's mind-adjacent claims, and its defect is not that it contradicts quantum mechanics but that the corpus states two mutually exclusive readings of it and does not choose. The claim is that observed particle behaviour is the resultant of a collective component — the memoryless, orthodox one, for which the corpus says the particle behaves exactly as quantum theory says and the "choice" is the Whole's — and an individual component — the memory-driven one, for which the particle's own accumulated record "inflects" its behaviour. The companion's judgement is that this is the most defensible claim in the cluster and that it does not obviously contradict Bell. The audit strengthens that judgement into a proof: the model cannot contradict Bell in either reading, because in one reading it predicts exactly quantum theory and in the other it predicts something else — and Bell's theorem constrains neither.

The two readings are these. In reading (A), selection, the set of possible behaviours and its probability weights are the orthodox ones; memory decides only which member is realised. Then every ensemble statistic is orthodox, and the model is empirically indistinguishable from quantum theory — a story about how outcomes come about, with no observable content. In reading (B), bias, memory alters the possible behaviours or their weights, so the probable behaviour differs from quantum theory. Then the model is falsifiable — and it needs a law of the alteration, which is exactly what the corpus does not give.

And the corpus asserts both readings in the same section. RC 020 §3.5 describes the mechanism as a weighted random choice with the orthodox weights — reading (A) — and then concludes that the probable behaviour "sera différent" once a sigma field of spin greater than 1 exists — reading (B). The article's verdict: the framing is [SPEC] and careful, the Bell question is settled in the model's favour, and the empirical content is zero until a re-weighting law exists. RC 021 §I defers that law "à une publication ultérieure", and neither RC (1987) nor L'Esprit (1983) supplies it. So the model's most defensible claim is also its least developed, and the deferral is what makes it unfalsifiable.

1. What the corpus claims

The claim is stated twice, once before the formalism and once after it, and the two statements are complementary.

(1) In RC 020 §3.5 — the chapter that introduces the sigma field:

  • "Le champ Imaginaire sigma propre à une particule est couplé à l'onde probabiliste psi de cette même particule."
  • "Le champ sigma n'est pas seulement une mémoire cumulative du passé et du présent : c'est aussi une mémoire prévisionnelle du futur."
  • "l'onde psi elle-même se trouve en quelque sorte « modelée » par le champ sigma, puisque psi et sigma sont couplés entre eux."
  • The empty-memory limit: "le comportement probable sera identique à celui calculé par la Théorie quantique orthodoxe dans le cas de particules ayant une mémoire « vide » (champ sigma de spin 1 seulement), cas dont le champ électromagnétique habituel ; mais ce comportement [sera] différent si l'on doit tenir compte de l'existence possible d'un champ sigma de spin plus élevé que 1."
  • The mechanism: "le «choix» par la particule de son comportement individuel parmi un ensemble de comportements possibles […] est aléatoire, chaque comportement entrant cependant dans cet aléatoire avec un «poids» chiffré par la probabilité associée à ce comportement."

(2) In RC 021 §I — the closing section of the behaviour chapter:

  • Collective: "nous conviendrons de dire que la particule a un COMPORTEMENT COLLECTIF : en effet, comme on le voit, le mouvement particulaire ne dépend plus alors que de la totalité du monde extérieur à la particule, c'est le Tout qui « pilote » la particule."
  • Individual: "dès que la particule possède un champ sigma dans un état de spin plus grand que 1, c'est-à-dire dispose d'une mémoire cumulative néguentropique lui octroyant un COMPORTEMENT INDIVIDUEL."
  • The trade-off: "plus la mémoire particulière est forte (plus le champ sigma est de spin élevé), plus le comportement particulaire devient « individuel », au détriment de la composante « collective » du comportement global."
  • The orthodox limit: "Dans le comportement collectif la particule ne dispose pas de mémoire sigma, elle a strictement un comportement orthodoxe : la particule est solidaire du Tout seul, pour le « choix » de son comportement."
  • The cosmology of behaviour: "l'ensemble des événements dans l'Univers est soumis à une sorte de « pulsion » pour s'ordonner selon le « bon ordre » de cet ensemble universel", where — the corpus says — "« bon ordre » doit être pris ici au sens mathématique de […] « Tout ensemble peut être bien ordonné » [Zermelo]."

Two printed slips, recorded here and not repaired.

  • One sentence inverts the model's own direction. RC 021 writes "Dans le comportement individuel la particule dispose d'une mémoire sigma, qui tend à « infléchir » son comportement pour le rendre toujours plus dépendant du Tout (de son « non-vouloir »)" — whereas the preceding paragraph says the individual behaviour is "toujours moins du Tout". The sentence as printed contradicts the paragraph it closes. The intended reading is unambiguous from context ("moins", not "plus"), but the article flags the slip because a reader quoting the model's definition of individuality will otherwise quote the inversion.
  • Zermelo's theorem cannot do the work asked of it. Well-ordering every set is a theorem of ZFC: it holds of every set, so as a principle of the model's dynamics it constrains nothing at all. It is a metaphor for "there is an order", and it is flagged as a metaphor rather than used as an axiom.

2. The two readings, and why they are not the same claim

The distinction is logical, not interpretive, and it is decided by one question: does the memory component change the weights, or only the draw?

reading (A) — selection reading (B) — bias
the possible behaviours orthodox set orthodox set, or an enlarged one
the weights orthodox probabilities altered by the memory
the realised outcome selected with orthodox weights selected with altered weights
ensemble statistics identical to quantum theory different from quantum theory
empirical content none a deviation, whose law is missing
status of the "individual" component a story about the draw a physical influence

RC 020 asserts (A) in its mechanism sentence and (B) in its consequence sentence. The mechanism is explicit: the choice "est aléatoire, chaque comportement entrant cependant dans cet aléatoire avec un «poids» chiffré par la probabilité associée" — weights equal to the associated probabilities, i.e. (A). The consequence is equally explicit: the probable behaviour will be "identique" for an empty memory and "différent" otherwise — i.e. (B). With orthodox weights, a different draw is not a different probable behaviour; for the probable behaviour to differ, the weights or the behaviour set must differ. So the corpus needs (B) for its sentence and describes (A) in its mechanism.

Why this matters more than it looks. Under (A) the model is a narrative: it says what the particle is doing (drawing from the quantum distribution according to its accumulated record) and predicts nothing beyond quantum theory — which is why it cannot be wrong, and also why it cannot be tested. Under (B) the model is a modification of quantum theory, and then it is the most exposed claim in the whole programme: the electron is the corpus's elementary Knower (S57) and also the most precisely tested object in physics. A memory-driven bias in a spin-1/2 particle's behaviour would appear as a deviation from quantum correlations in Bell tests and as a bias in interference statistics, and the corpus supplies no scale at which it should appear. Reading (B) therefore converts a safe claim into an endangered one; reading (A) converts it into an empty one. The article records the choice as the corpus's to make, and records that it is not made.

One quantitative handle exists, and it is not a law. RC 020 gives the sigma field's spin ladder as $1, 2, 4, 8\dots$ with $16^{2n}$ initial conditions at spin $2n$ — so at $n = 4$ (spin 8) the field carries $4{,}3\times10^{9}$ components. That is a count of degrees of freedom, not a measure of the deviation from quantum theory: the corpus never says how many components a given physical history has accumulated, nor how a given count translates into a bias. The count is S56's subject; its irrelevance to this article's question is the point here.

3. Bell: why the model cannot contradict him

Bell's theorem (1964) excludes theories of local hidden variables that reproduce all quantum predictions. The question for this model is whether it is such a theory, and the answer is no in both readings — for two different reasons.

  • Under (A) the model does not contradict Bell, it reproduces quantum theory exactly. If the weights are orthodox, the statistics are orthodox in every experiment, including the entangled ones: the model is quantum theory plus a story about which member of the distribution is realised. No hidden-variable claim is being made that affects correlations, so Bell's hypothesis is not met and the theorem does not bite.
  • Under (B) the model does not contradict Bell either — it contradicts quantum theory. A local, deterministic memory-driven bias is exactly the kind of theory Bell excludes from reproducing quantum correlations; but the model under (B) does not claim to reproduce them. It predicts different statistics, and Bell's theorem is silent about theories that predict different statistics. What such a theory risks is not Bell's theorem but the experiment: Bell tests, which so far agree with quantum theory to high precision.

So the companion's "does not obviously contradict Bell" can be strengthened to "cannot contradict Bell, in either reading" — and the strengthening is what makes the claim defensible: the model is Bell-neutral by construction, because its collective component is orthodox quantum theory and its individual component either changes nothing observable or changes the statistics, and Bell constrains only the impossible middle. The corpus earns this by an explicit design choice — the empty-memory limit is stated as a consistency requirement ("identique à celui calculé par la Théorie quantique orthodoxe") — and that is the structural care the companion credits it with.

A caution, because the vocabulary invites an error. The corpus's collective component is "le Tout qui pilote la particule", and it is tempting to read "the Whole steers it" as a nonlocal influence. It is not: under (A) it is a re-description of the orthodox probability distribution, and the corpus's own check is that the distribution is unchanged. "The Whole" is the corpus's name for the environment's role in determining the distribution, not an additional channel. Any article that quotes "the Whole steers the particle" as a nonlocal mechanism is quoting the metaphor against the model's mathematics.

4. The deferral, and what the later books do with it

RC 021 §I closes by postponing exactly the law that reading (B) needs. "Nous n'entrerons pas ici plus avant dans l'étude du comportement particulaire, réservant comme nous l'avons dit l'examen de ce qui touche à la « mémoire particulière » à une publication ultérieure, et nous limitant pour le moment au problème de l'unification des quatre interactions physiques."

The later books revisit the memory but not the dynamics. L'Esprit et la Relativité complexe (1983) devotes its annexe 1 to the leptonic model as the Knower's model and describes the memory as "les conditions complémentaires (constantes d'intégration) du système d'équations aux dérivées partielles" of the higher-spin states; Relativité complexe (1987) restates the behaviour split in RC 020–021. Neither supplies a weighting law, an accumulation rate, or a scale. So the deferral is not a promise later kept: the memory chapter of the corpus is a component count and a containment property (S56's subject), with no dynamics attached.

What that costs, stated plainly.

  • Reading (B) is unavailable in practice, because without a law there is no prediction to compare with a Bell test or an interference experiment.
  • Reading (A) is available and empty, because it changes no number.
  • And the corpus's own monotone claim — "plus la mémoire particulière est forte […] plus le comportement particulaire devient « individuel »" — is a direction without a magnitude, so it cannot be used to bound anything either.

This is the article's honest ledger: the model's most defensible mind-adjacent claim is also the one with no developed machinery behind it, and the corpus says so itself. The care in the framing (the empty-memory limit, the Bell-neutral construction) is real and worth crediting; the deferral is real too, and it is what keeps the claim from being either true or false.

Per rule R6: coherence passes for the framing (the two components and the empty-memory limit are consistently described); reproducibility fails for the individual component (no law, no rate, no scale); correspondence is empty — the model's only contact with experiment is the limit in which it equals quantum theory.

And the one sentence worth carrying forward. Of all the corpus's mind-adjacent claims, this is the one that a physicist need not reject: it can be read as a Bell-neutral account of what a particle's history contributes to its behaviour, and it contradicts nothing measured. Its price is that it also predicts nothing measured — and the price is paid in RC 021's deferral, which the corpus never redeems.

Summary

The claim. Observed behaviour is collective (memoryless, orthodox, "the Whole steers it") plus individual (memory-driven, the particle's own accumulated record). The companion calls this the most defensible of the mind-adjacent claims, and the audit agrees — and proves the Bell point: the model cannot contradict Bell's theorem, because under one reading it reproduces quantum theory exactly and under the other it predicts different statistics, and Bell constrains only the excluded middle.

The defect. The corpus states both readings in the same section: the mechanism sentence gives a random choice with the orthodox weights (no observable consequence), and the consequence sentence says the probable behaviour differs (an observable deviation). With orthodox weights, a different draw is not a different probable behaviour. The corpus needs the second reading for its sentence and describes the first in its mechanism, and RC 021 §I defers the missing law — the re-weighting — "à une publication ultérieure", which neither later book supplies. So the model is either empirically empty or empirically exposed, and it does not say which.

Two printed slips, flagged and not repaired: the individual case is said to make the particle "toujours plus dépendant du Tout", inverting the preceding paragraph's "toujours moins"; and Zermelo's well-ordering theorem, invoked as the "bon ordre", holds of every set and therefore constrains nothing.

Verdict: §9.4 [SPEC]/[PHIL]; §9.5 [MIX] with the reduction to orthodox theory structurally correct; the mechanism [SPEC] and empty; the consequence [SPEC] and unsupported; the spin count [MATH] but not a measure of deviation; Bell neutrality established. Per R6: coherence passes, reproducibility fails, correspondence empty.

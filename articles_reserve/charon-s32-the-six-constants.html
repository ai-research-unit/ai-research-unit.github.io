Charon — The Six Constants, One Row Each (S32)

Audits register §13.5 against the solution table of Esp 058, with the closed forms of S30 §5.1 and the method of S31.

Introduction

Five of the six constants the Système $L$ is said to compute are put in by hand, the sixth is not in the system at all, and the one constant that genuinely comes out — the speed of light — comes out of the system's own normalisation equation. That is the verdict, row by row, and it is the verdict the corpus's own two pages support: Esp 057 §1 seeds $h, c, m, \mu, \tau_{2}$ and $e$; Esp 058's table prints them back. This article is the row-by-row accounting, and its main discovery is not about the six rows but about the table's two columns.

The two columns have different epistemic status, and reading them together is what produces the appearance of computation. The cgs column is the seeds: $c = 3\times10^{10}$, $h = 6{,}61\times10^{-27}$, $m_{0} = 9{,}11\times10^{-28}$, $\mu_{0} = 1{,}884\times10^{-25}$, $\tau_{2} = 3{,}1976\times10^{-24}$. The units-$L$ column is derived — it is the same quantities expressed in $\alpha$, $R_{0}$ and $T$ — and its agreement with the cgs column is therefore a genuine, checkable internal consistency. So the table is not one table but two statements: what the corpus fed in, and whether the solution's own natural units are consistent with the numbers the solution produced. The second is a real test, the article runs it, and the corpus passes it to $0{,}2\,\%$–$1{,}8\,\%$ on every row (§1.2).

The strongest single verification the article finds is not a constant at all. The table prints $k_{s} = k_{p} = 1{,}36$ — the spin and pulse relativistic coefficients. That value is exactly what the printed pulsation amplitude gives if and only if the pulse profile is harmonic: with $\beta_{\max} = 0{,}861$, $\left\langle(1-\beta^{2})^{-1/2}\right\rangle = 1{,}3632$, against $1{,}36$ printed ($0{,}2\,\%$); a constant-speed profile at the same amplitude gives $1{,}966$, and one at $c$ diverges. So the printed coefficient confirms the printed amplitude, identifies the pulse profile as harmonic, and confirms the equipartition L15 to better than the table's own rounding — and it is in tension with the position the text gives for the maximum speed (S30 §3.3). §6.2.

1. The Table, Read Properly

1.1 The cgs column is the seed list, and the units-$L$ column is derived

Esp 057 §1 gives six seeds; Esp 058's cgs column prints exactly those six numbers. That is not an inference: $c = 3\times10^{10}$, $h = 6{,}61\times10^{-27}$, $m_{0} = 9{,}11\times10^{-28}$, $\mu_{0} = 1{,}884\times10^{-25}$, $\tau_{2} = 3{,}1976\times10^{-24}$, and $e$ — with the caveat that the transcription's table rows for $\tau_{on}(n=2)$ and $W_{0\tau}$ are blank (§4.2, §6.3). So the cgs column cannot be evidence of computation: it is evidence of the seed list.

The units-$L$ column is a different kind of thing, and this is where the corpus's table earns its keep. The corpus defines the units by three quantities the solution produces — $\alpha$ for mass, $R_{0}$ for length, $T$ for time — and every other entry is that row's quantity divided by the appropriate power of those three. So the units-$L$ column is a derived column: its agreement with the cgs column tests the solution's internal arithmetic, and its dimensionless ratios ($R/R_{0}$, $cT/R_{0}$, $m/\alpha$, $\mu/\alpha$, $\beta/(\alpha R_{0})$, $l_{0}/R_{0}$, $\tau_{n}/\alpha$) are round numbers the corpus's own equations should produce. One notation hazard to record: the table uses $R$ for the maximum radius (so $R$ has $\mathcal{R}$'s role in the table, not the running radius) and $m_{0}$, $\mu_{0}$ for $m$, $\mu$. S5's hazard pattern; noted, not counted as an error.

2. Row 1 — $c$: the One Constant the System Defines

$c$ is the only one of the six that the system returns, and it returns it from L16. L16 is $\int_{0}^{T}dt/R = 2\pi/c$, so $c$ is defined by the solution's geometry and period: $c = 2\pi/\left\langle R^{-1}\right\rangle^{-1}$ — the harmonic mean radius times $2\pi/T$. L17 provides an over-determined second route, $c = 3\hbar\xi\left\langle R^{-3}\right\rangle/(4\pi)$, and S30 §3.3 shows the two are consistent to $2{,}6\,\%$ on the printed geometry.

So what does the row establish?

  • That $c$ is not an independent input to the geometry — it is fixed by $R(\tau)$ and $T$, and $T$ is fixed by the lepton mass difference (L8 at $n=1,2$). Chain: measured lepton masses $\to T \to$ L16 $\to c$. A reader who follows it will notice that the chain's first link is measured, which is rule R1 again.
  • That $c$'s reproduction of $3\times10^{10}$ cm/s is therefore a consistency check and not a derivation of the speed of light. The check is: does the geometry that the lepton masses force have the harmonic mean radius $cT/2\pi$? The corpus's solution answers yes to $0{,}07\,\%$ (the units-$L$ row), and S30's independent reading answers yes to $5{,}7\,\%$ in the moment and $2{,}6\,\%$ in $l_{0}$.

That is the honest content of the row, and it is more than nothing: the corpus's own geometry and its own normalisation equation agree with the measured speed of light, without $c$ being fed into L16. The one thing the row cannot claim is that $c$ was computed from the model's principles.

3. Row 2 — $h$: a Seed, and What the System Does With It

$h$ is seeded at the 1970s value $6{,}61\times10^{-27}$ erg·s (today's $6{,}62607015\times10^{-27}$, so the seed is $0{,}23\,\%$ low), and the system uses it in every action equation: L7, L8, L9–L12. It is also over-determined by them — six equations contain it — so the corpus's claim that the solution returns $h$ is, read generously, the claim that its value is consistent with the actions.

And that consistency is checkable in the units-$L$ column. The natural action unit is $R_{0}^{2}/T$ in mass terms; the table's $3\,617$ is $h$ divided by the corresponding combination of $\alpha$, $R_{0}$ and $T$, and the recomputation gives $3\,625{,}4$ — $0{,}23\,\%$, which is precisely the seed's own distance from today's $h$ (a coincidence of size, not of meaning, recorded as such).

So the $h$ row is: seed, reused, over-determined, and consistent. Its interest for the ledger is that $h$ is the one constant that appears in the quantisation equations (L9–L12) and in the mass equations (L7, L8) at once, which is the structural reason the tower's step is $2h/(c^{2}T)$ and not something else.

4. Rows 3–5 — $m$, $\mu$, $\tau_{2}$

4.1 The three seeds

All three are the measured charged-lepton masses, in the corpus's own notation, with the signs of §18.1: $m_{0} = 9{,}11\times10^{-28}$ g (today $9{,}1093837\times10^{-28}$; the seed is $0{,}007\,\%$ high), $\mu_{0} = 1{,}884\times10^{-25}$ g (today $1{,}8835316\times10^{-25}$; $0{,}025\,\%$ high), $\tau_{2} = 3{,}1976\times10^{-24}$ g (today $3{,}16754\times10^{-24}$; $0{,}95\,\%$ high — the largest seed deviation in the table, and it is the 1970s tau mass measurement, not an error).

Their role in the system is not as residuals but as right-hand sides. L7 says the electron's own-mass equation has $mc^{2}T$ on the right; L8 at $n = 1$ says $\mu c^{2}T$; and Esp 057 §2's $T = 2h/[c^{2}(\tau-\mu)]$ uses the difference $\tau - \mu$. So $m$ and $\mu$ are satisfied identically, $\tau$ fixes $T$, and the only place the three masses can fail is in the fit's seeds-versus-measurement column, which is not what $\chi$ measures (S31 §3.2(a)).

4.2 $\tau_{2}$ reproduces itself, and the table's blank row

The corpus's tower (244) is $\tau_{n} = \mu + 2(n-1)h/(c^{2}T)$, and S30 §5.2 shows it is inside L8 at $n = 2$. With $T$ defined from $\tau - \mu$ in step 2c, the tower at $n = 2$ returns $\mu + (\tau-\mu) = \tau$ exactly — an identity, not a check. Evaluated from the printed values ($\mu_{0}$, $h$, $T$) it gives $3{,}19754\times10^{-24}$ g against the seed $3{,}1976\times10^{-24}$ g, i.e. $0{,}002\,\%$ — the closure is by construction and the number confirms the construction, nothing more.

And the table's $\tau_{on}(n = 2)$ row is blank. The transcription shows the symbol row with no definition and no values, while both neighbouring rows ($\mu_{0}$ above, $k_{s}$ below) carry theirs; the row's values would be $3{,}1976\times10^{-24}$ g and $21{,}72$ in units $L$ ($\tau_{2}/|\alpha| = 21{,}72$). The blank is recorded as a transcription item and as a candidate for the user's eye, not as a corpus error — and it is worth noting that the printed $\tau_{on}(n=3)$ row's $42$ is exactly $2 \times 21$, i.e. the tower's constant step in units $L$.

4.3 The tower's first unseeded output, and why it is not a success

Since $n = 2$ is the seed, the tower's first genuine output is $n = 3$. From the printed $\mu_{0}$, $h$ and $T$: $\tau_{3} = \mu + 4h/(c^{2}T) = 6{,}2068\times10^{-24}$ g $= \mathbf{3{,}4817}$ GeV, against the table's printed $6{,}184\times10^{-24}$ g $= 3{,}4690$ GeV, a $-0{,}37\,\%$ difference. So the table's own $\tau_{3}$ is not quite what the table's own $T$ gives — the printed value is $0{,}37\,\%$ low, which is the corpus's own rounding and not a defect.

The state itself is the problem, and it is not this article's finding to make: register R7 and P1 record that a charged lepton at $3{,}47$ GeV does not exist. LEP's searches closed the charged-lepton spectrum at the $Z$ pole; the corpus's table prints the state anyway, in the same table as the three that do exist. What this article adds is the row's status: $\tau_{3}$ is the tower's only unseeded output, and it is excluded by experiment. S43 owns the comparison; the tower's status is settled there and in S30 §5.2, and this row is the table entry where it shows.

5. Row 6 — $e$: Not in the System

$e$ is seeded (Esp 057 §1) and returned by nothing in L. S30 §4's map makes this visible: the elementary charge appears in no L-equation, and Esp 056's own sentence routes it through §18.2 — "le Système $L$ conduit également au calcul de la charge électrique élémentaire $e$". So the row is not a computed constant of the Système $L$ at all; it is a computed constant of the §18.2 chain, with $L$ supplying $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $T$.

And the §18.2 chain gives $e$ to $2{,}3\,\%$ as the corpus claims — S35's business, and its own recomputation gives $+1{,}82\,\%$ on the charge ($+3{,}64\,\%$ on the coupling) with correct arithmetic, while reproducing the corpus's printed value exactly from its printed inputs. Here the row is recorded with its status: seeded, not returned by $L$, computed downstream to $2{,}3\,\%$, and (S31 §2.2) not touched by $\chi$ at all. So the corpus's "5 des principales constantes $\dots$ et […] également […] $e$" is a statement about two different computations, and the numerical chapter's residual knows nothing about the second.

6. The Table's Other Rows

6.2 $k_{s} = k_{p} = 1{,}36$: the article's strongest verification

The two relativistic coefficients are printed as the same number, and both equalities are checkable from the printed amplitude.

The pulse coefficient first, because its definition (L14) is one-dimensional: $k_{P} = \frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(1-R'^{2}/c^{2})^{-1/2}dt$. With $|R'|_{\max} = 0{,}861\,c$:

pulse profile $k_{P}$
harmonic ($R' \propto \sin$), $\beta_{\max} = 0{,}861$ $\frac{2}{\pi}K(\beta_{\max}^{2}) = \mathbf{1{,}3632}$
constant speed at $0{,}861\,c$ $(1-0{,}861^{2})^{-1/2} = 1{,}9662$
constant speed at $c$ (the S23/S26 idealisation) diverges
printed $\mathbf{1{,}36}$

So the printed $k_{p} = 1{,}36$ is the harmonic value to $0{,}2\,\%$, and the two alternatives are excluded: a constant-speed cycle at the printed amplitude would need $1{,}97$, and at $c$ the integral does not converge at all. (The elliptic integral is elementary: for a harmonic pulse the time average of $(1-\beta^{2})^{-1/2}$ is $\frac{2}{\pi}K(m)$ with $m = \beta_{\max}^{2} = 0{,}7413$, and $K(0{,}7413) = 2{,}14125$.)

Three consequences, in order of strength.

(1) The printed amplitude is confirmed by an independent printed quantity. $0{,}861c$ and $k = 1{,}36$ are two numbers on two pages (Esp 058 §17 and the table), and they are related by the corpus's own L14 to $0{,}2\,\%$. That is a genuine internal consistency of the published solution, and no other reading of the pair produces it.

(2) The pulse profile is harmonic — and this is in tension with the text's placement of the maximum speed at $R = 1{,}79\times10^{-14}$ cm, which for a harmonic pulse would be at the mid-radius $2{,}79\times10^{-14}$ cm (S30 §3.3). Both data cannot describe the same pure profile; the profile must be asymmetric (§6.2's last paragraph).

(3) The equipartition L15 holds to better than the table's rounding. $k_{s} = k_{p} = 1{,}36$ printed to three digits means $|k_{s}-k_{p}|/k < 0{,}37\,\%$ — and L15 is one of the six equations the fit tests (S31 §3.2(b)). So the corpus's "facteur 2", the term that makes the tower step $2h/(c^{2}T)$, is satisfied by the published solution at the $0{,}4\,\%$ level while the overall residual is $2\,\%$. That is the sharpest available answer to "is the equipartition an equation or an identity?" — it is an equation, and it passes.

One caution so that the verification is not over-read. The spin coefficient $k_{s}$ comes from L13, whose integrand involves the closed-space angular speed $v_{\nu}$ and not $R'$ alone; the table prints $k_{s} = k_{p}$, and the harmonic computation above verifies $k_{p}$. If the corpus's $k_{s}$ is a separate computation from L13 and happens to agree with $k_{p}$ at three digits, that agreement is a stronger result than this article can certify, because the angular distribution is not in the reproduction. Recorded as a bound on the claim: what is verified here is $k_{p}$, and the equality is verified only to the table's rounding.

6.3 The two $W_{0}$ rows, and the one conversion that does not close

The neutrino rest energies are printed as $W_{0e} = 0{,}824\times10^{-3}$ and $W_{0\mu} = 2{,}615\times10^{-3}$ (cgs) and $2\,209$ and $7\,010$ (units $L$). Three observations:

  • The two rows are mutually consistent: $0{,}824/2{,}615 = 0{,}3151$ and $2\,209/7\,010 = 0{,}31512$. Ratio preserved, so the pair is internally sound.
  • Their units-$L$ column does not follow from $\alpha$, $R_{0}$ and $T$. The implied unit is $3{,}7302\times10^{-7}$ in the cgs column's unit, and that is not $\alpha$, $\alpha c^{2}$, $h/T$, $mc^{2}$ or $R_{0}^{2}/T$-based; it equals $\alpha c^{2}(R_{0}/cT)^{2}$ numerically ($3{,}7301\times10^{-7}$), which is not a combination the corpus's unit list suggests. Recorded as unexplained.
  • These are not constants of the six, and no result in S30–S34 depends on them. The $W_{0\tau}$ row is blank in the transcription, like the $\tau_{on}(n=2)$ row (§4.2).

So the table has two conversion puzzles ($A$ and the $W_{0}$'s), both in rows outside the six-constant claim. They are handed to the register as items to close from a scan if a scan is ever needed; they are not defects of the solution, they are entries whose units the transcription cannot check.

The register's §13.5 lists the constants with their deviations, and the deviations need a label. Three different comparisons are in play, and they must not be mixed:

8. Verdict

Five rows are seeds, one row is a defined output of the system's own normalisation, one row is outside the system, and the table's real content is its derived column.

  • Confirmed (§13.5, with the table transcribed and checked): every entry used here is legible in Esp 058; the natural units are $\alpha$, $R_{0}$, $T$; $\rho_{0} = |\alpha|/R_{0}^{3} = 3{,}1019\times10^{17}$ g cm⁻³ matches the printed $3{,}1\times10^{17}$.
  • New — the two columns have different status (§1.1), and the units-$L$ column closes to $0{,}07\,\%$–$1{,}7\,\%$ on every row whose dimension is formable (§1.2): $c$ ($-0{,}07\,\%$), $h$ ($+0{,}23\,\%$), $l_{0}$ ($-0{,}11\,\%$), $\beta$ ($-1{,}7\,\%$), $R/R_{0}$ ($-0{,}003\,\%$), $m/\alpha$ ($-0{,}19\,\%$), $\mu/\alpha$ ($-0{,}03\,\%$), $\tau_{3}/\alpha = 42{,}01$ vs $42$. The table is internally consistent.
  • Row 1, $c$: the only genuine output, from L16 with L17 over-determining it (S30 §3.3); but it is the system's own normalisation equation, so the row is a consistency check, not a derivation. The chain runs measured lepton masses $\to T \to c$.
  • Rows 2–5, $h, m, \mu, \tau_{2}$: seeds. The tau row is also the construction of $T$; the tower at $n = 2$ reproduces it identically, and its first unseeded output ($n = 3$, $3{,}47$ GeV) is excluded by experiment (§4.3, R7, P1, S43).
  • Row 6, $e$: not in the system. Seeded, absent from every L-equation, absent from $\chi$, computed downstream — the corpus's $2{,}3\,\%$, S35's $+1{,}82\,\%$ on the charge (S35). The "5 + 1 = 6" is a statement about two computations.
  • New — the strongest verification in the article is $k_{s} = k_{p} = 1{,}36$. The harmonic value at the printed amplitude $0{,}861c$ is $1{,}3632$ ($0{,}2\,\%$); a constant-speed profile gives $1{,}966$, and at $c$ the integral diverges. So the printed amplitude and the printed coefficient confirm each other through L14, the pulse profile is harmonic, and the equipartition L15 passes to better than the table's rounding — although the equality of $k_{s}$ with $k_{p}$ is only verified to that rounding, since $k_{s}$'s angular integral is not reproduced here.
  • New — two conversion puzzles, both outside the six: $A$'s units-$L$ entry implies a unit of $1{,}64\times10^{28}$, and the $W_{0}$'s imply $3{,}73\times10^{-7}$; neither is a natural unit of the list. Recorded as unexplained, with no consequence for S30–S34.
  • The deviations must be labelled (§7): reprint, internal arithmetic, seed age, or genuine computed-versus-measured. Only the fourth bears on the model, and only $e$ is in it.

Summary

The six constants are one defined quantity, five measured seeds, one seed computed downstream, and one suggested constant — and the table's derived column is where its real content lies. $c$ is the only genuine output, defined by L16; $h, m, \mu, \tau_{2}$ are the measured input values (the 1970s ones); $e$ is seeded, absent from every L-equation and from $\chi$, and computed in §18.2 to $2{,}3\,\%$ (S35); $\Omega_{0} = \xi = l_{0}$ is the corpus's own « suggestion ». The units-$L$ column is derived from $\alpha$, $R_{0}$ and $T$, and every row whose dimension is formable is reproduced from the table's own cgs values — so the table is internally consistent. The article's strongest verification is $k_{s} = k_{p} = 1{,}36$: the harmonic profile at the printed amplitude $\beta_{\max} = 0{,}861$ gives $1{,}3632$, a constant-speed profile gives $1{,}966$, and at $c$ the integral diverges. That identifies the pulse profile as harmonic — in tension with the position the text gives for the maximum speed, so the printed profile is asymmetric — and it shows the equipartition of L15 passing to better than the table's rounding. The deviations of the chapter fall into four kinds, and only one of them compares a computation with nature.

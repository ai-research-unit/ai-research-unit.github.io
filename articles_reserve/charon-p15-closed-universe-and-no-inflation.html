Charon: The Closed Universe, and No Inflation

Introduction

This article records the corpus's global geometry and what it puts in inflation's place. The corpus's universe is spherical and closed, $K = +1$; it is spatially finite, with a maximum distance $L = \pi R$; and it has no inflation. The first two are stated flatly and repeatedly. The third is not stated at all — the corpus simply substitutes its pulsating bounce, and the flatness, horizon and isotropy problems are left where they are.

The claim, verbatim. RC 045 prints "$K = +1$" as (3-36); RC 037 calls it "le seul type d'univers que nous rencontrerons en Relativité complexe"; RC 069 and RC 075 repeat it. RC 059 names the consequence: "la notion nécessaire d'univers fini ($K = +1$)". The absence of inflation is implicit throughout: the corpus was published in 1983 and 1987, after Guth (1981) and Linde (1982), and the alternative it offers is the bounce of S51/S52.

The verdict is [OUT] (disfavoured) — and, on the corpus's own numbers, much worse than that. Planck 2018 gives $\Omega_{k} = -0{,}0007 \pm 0{,}0019$, consistent with flat at $0{,}4\sigma$: a closed universe is disfavoured, not excluded, and that is the honest generic verdict. But the corpus's own solution puts $\dot a = 0{,}884c$ today (Esp 077), which fixes its curvature density at $\Omega_{k} = -c^{2}/\dot a^{2} = -1{,}28$ — a factor 640 above the measured bound. So the corpus's specific closed universe is not a marginal casualty of the flatness measurement; it is a curvature-dominated universe in an observed flat one (Part 2).

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §8.10 and §8.11. The geometry and the bounce are S51's and S52's; the horizon-problem discussion connects to P12 (Axiom 6). The related articles are P10 (the vacuum's sign), P12, P13 and P14.

Conventions. $K$ is the curvature sign; $R(\tau)$ the scale factor and $R' = \dot R$; $H = R'/R$; $L = \pi R$ the maximum distance in a spherical closed universe; $\Omega_{k} \equiv -Kc^{2}/(R^{2}H^{2}) = -c^{2}/R'^{2}$ for $K = +1$; $R_{\max}$ the maximum radius; $q_{a}$ the deceleration parameter.

Part 1 — What the corpus has, and what it does not

The closed universe is not optional in the corpus. It is what makes the maximum distance $L = \pi R$ (P13), what supplies the shell-volume factor $\Delta V \propto R^{2}\Delta l(1-\cos(2l/R))$ that produces the quasar peak (P14), and what forces $\Lambda = -3/R_{\max}^{2} < 0$ (P10, S52). Remove $K = +1$ and three articles lose their premises at once. So this is a load-bearing commitment, and its status deserves the care the register gives it.

What the corpus does not have: a flatness problem, a horizon problem, or a perturbation sector.

  • Flatness. The corpus does not claim the universe is flat, and it does not have to solve flatness as stated, because its universe is closed. But its observed near-flatness must still come out, and Part 2 shows it does not.
  • Horizon. The corpus does address this, and its answer is Axiom 6: if every observable phenomenon shares one universal age $\tau$, then the isotropy of the sky is not a coincidence but a definition. This is the corpus's inflation substitute for the horizon problem, and it is [FALS] (P12). It is worth stating plainly that the corpus made an attempt here — it is more than most non-inflationary proposals of the period did — and that the attempt is dead.
  • Perturbations. The model has no perturbation theory: no acoustic oscillations, no primordial power spectrum, no tensor-to-scalar ratio. So it cannot be tested by the CMB's anisotropy pattern, which is exactly how inflation and its alternatives (bouncing cosmologies, ekpyrotic, string gas) are tested today. This is not a falsification; it is a ceiling on the model's reach, and it is the reason §8.11 is [SPEC] rather than [FALS].

Part 2 — The corpus's own curvature, computed from its own numbers

The register's verdict on $K = +1$ is "disfavoured", on the Planck value $\Omega_{k} = -0{,}0007 \pm 0{,}0019$. That verdict is about a closed universe in general. The corpus's closed universe is a stronger claim, and it is computable.

The identity. For $K = +1$ the curvature density parameter is

$$\Omega_{k} = -\frac{Kc^{2}}{R^{2}H^{2}} = -\frac{c^{2}}{R'^{2}} .$$

The corpus's own input. Esp 077: "La valeur actuelle de $R'$, telle que déduite de notre modèle cosmologique (voir tableau « Solution du Système C »), est $0{,}884~c$." That is today's value, from the corpus's own solution table.

The result.

$$\Omega_{k} = -\frac{1}{(0{,}884)^{2}} = -1{,}28 .$$

So the corpus's universe is curvature-dominated today: the curvature term is 1.28 times the critical density, against a measured bound $|\Omega_{k}| < 0{,}002$. The corpus's own numbers miss the observation by a factor of 640.

This is a recomputation, and it is worth being careful about what it does and does not say.

statement status
A closed universe is disfavoured but allowed (Planck, $0{,}4\sigma$) true — the register's verdict
The corpus's own solution has $\Omega_{k} = -1{,}28$ today true, required by its own $R' = 0{,}884c$
Therefore the corpus's specific closed universe is excluded true, by a factor 640

The two statements are different, and both belong in the record. The general [OUT] verdict is right; it is simply too generous to the corpus's own model. And the identity $\Omega_{k} = -c^{2}/R'^{2}$ is not a comparison of models — it is a consequence of the definitions plus the corpus's own expansion rate.

Why the corpus's universe is so curved. The corpus's own cycle has $T/2 \approx 2324$ Gyr, with the printed 2371 Gyr a $2{,}0\,\%$ error (S52), and today is $0{,}74\,\%$ into the expansion phase, so $R/R_{\max} \approx 1{,}1\,\%$ and $R'$ is near its kinematic ceiling. A universe barely past its bounce is expanding at nearly $c$ and is therefore strongly curved. That is the structural reason the corpus cannot be near-flat, and it is not repairable by adjusting $q_{a}$: it follows from $R' = 0{,}884c$ alone.

A caveat stated in fairness. If $R' = 0{,}884c$ were a present-epoch table value in tension with the corpus's own (5-17) — S52 notes an internal inconsistency of this kind, and S30 finds a $5{,}7\%$ normalisation misfit in the same family — then the number might be a bookkeeping artefact. But the corpus printed it as today's value in both its cosmology and its tables, so it is what the model says. The context file records the caveat.

Part 3 — What stands in for inflation, and why it is not enough

A bounce can in principle replace inflation. The flatness and horizon problems are statements about initial conditions; a contracting phase before the bounce resets them, and the ekpyrotic and bouncing cosmologies are live research programmes doing exactly that. So "the corpus has no inflation" is not by itself a failure. What matters is whether the substitute does the work.

The corpus's substitute does one of the three jobs, by a method that is dead.

problem inflation's mechanism the corpus's substitute verdict
horizon/isotropy accelerated expansion makes one causal patch Axiom 6: one universal age $\tau$ answers it, but Axiom 6 is [FALS] (P12)
flatness accelerated expansion drives $\Omega_{k}\to0$ none; the model is closed and, on its own numbers, curvature-dominated fails (Part 2)
perturbations quantum fluctuations → nearly scale-invariant spectrum none (no perturbation sector) no prediction

And the corpus's substitute needs the wrong sign of $\Lambda$. S52's result: the bounce's modification is $\Lambda \le 0$, geometrically required by closure and computed at $-1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$ against the observed $+1{,}10\times10^{-56}$ cm$^{-2}$. So the mechanism that produces the corpus's "no singularity" is dead on the sign of the dominant cosmic term (P10).

The honest summary of the comparison. The corpus anticipated the shape of a modern idea — a non-singular bounce instead of an initial singularity, and a curved rather than exactly flat universe — and it predates the observations that made both live. But it supplied no perturbation sector to replace inflation's testable output, and its own numbers put it off flatness by two orders of magnitude more than the modern data allow. That combination is why the verdict is [OUT] and not [SPEC]: the model is not merely untested, it is in tension where it is testable.

Summary

The corpus's universe is closed and spherical, $K = +1$, finite, and without inflation. The generic verdict on $K = +1$ is [OUT]: Planck 2018 gives $\Omega_{k} = -0{,}0007 \pm 0{,}0019$, consistent with flat at $0{,}4\sigma$, so closed is disfavoured rather than excluded. On the corpus's own numbers the verdict is sharper: Esp 077's present-day $R' = 0{,}884c$ fixes $\Omega_{k} = -c^{2}/R'^{2} = -1{,}28$ — the corpus's universe is curvature-dominated, a factor 640 above the measured bound. The corpus does address the horizon problem, through Axiom 6's universal age, and that route is [FALS] (P12); it does not address flatness; and it has no perturbation sector, so it makes no CMB prediction. Its bounce has no perturbation theory either, and the mechanism behind it requires $\Lambda < 0$ against the observed positive value (P10, S52). Verdict [OUT] — with the corpus's specific solution, on its own inputs, excluded outright.

# __The Clifford Layer Is the Cell Layer: The Exact Correspondence, the Two Equalities, and the Full Inventory__

## Introduction

The earlier reading of this corpus put the Clifford algebra outside the biquaternion algebra. $\mathbb{B}$ was the even part, $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$ had sixteen real dimensions, and the odd eight were said to be external. The correspondence now fixed in *Conventions of the Matrix Models* removes the premise.

In the canonical frame the five Dirac generators are cells of the two-sided operator algebra:
$$
\gamma_0=iT_{e_t,e_x},\qquad
\gamma_1=iT_{e_x,e_y},\qquad
\gamma_2=iT_{e_y,e_y},\qquad
\gamma_3=iT_{e_z,e_y},\qquad
\gamma_5=-iT_{e_t,e_z}.
$$
They are not merely isomorphic to cells; they are cells, with the coefficients displayed. The sixteen products of the five generators are, up to $\pm1$ and $\pm i$, the sixteen cells $T_{e_\mu,e_\nu}=L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$, and the assignment extends to an algebra isomorphism
$$
\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}\longrightarrow M_4(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C}).
$$
The Clifford algebra is therefore the algebra generated by left and right multiplication on $\mathbb{B}$: the biquaternion algebra acting on itself on both sides. The odd sector is outside the left copy $\mathbb{B}_L$ and inside $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$. It was never outside the formalism. It was outside one of its two actions.

One caveat, stated once. The complex statement is exact. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the sixteen products of the generators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$, while the sixteen cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$; the two real forms are opposite, and the dictionary map carries one into the other. The physics is complex-linear throughout, and there the distinction is invisible.

**No article is converted.** Every formula that writes a gamma gains a second equality with the cell form. The gamma stays; the cell form is added beside it. The isomorphism is what guarantees that the second equality exists, and it is exact over $\mathbb{C}$. One article in the corpus uses a larger algebra by design, and its gammas do not reduce to the sixteen cells.

**Sixteen articles already carry the cell equality.** Eight are in mathematics and eight in physics. They are not patched again and they are not repeated in the tables below; their own files hold both notations. Four further articles were purged of a gamma mention that carried no structure. The tables list the articles that remain, with the gamma symbols each one writes.

What differs between articles is only the length of the second equality:

- **One-term equality — one cell per gamma.** Each gamma in the formula is a single cell, so the second equality is a single sandwich $a\,\psi\,b$. No sum, no trace, no frame. The gamma side and the cell side have the same length.
- **Multi-term equality — several cells.** The formula contains an operator, a trace, or the frame as a set, so the second equality is a sum of cells, a linear functional on cells, or a list. It is longer than the gamma side and no shorter form exists.

The gamma matrices are a coordinate on the cell algebra. They are never a third algebra. An article that writes them is a cell-algebra article in a particular basis, and every display of it can carry the cell equality underneath.

**The counts.** The tables keep the inventory of articles in which $\gamma$ denotes a gamma matrix. Sixteen articles already carry the cell equality and are not repeated; four mentions were purged. The remaining **96** articles, **18** in mathematics and **78** in physics, are listed in menu order. The symbol counts come from the mathematics regions of each file: a $\gamma$ counts when it is a generator or a product, not when it is the Lorentz factor, the Euler constant, the photon, the curve, or a name.

The second column of each table gives every gamma symbol the article writes and the number of times it writes it; the third column is the sum. ## The Correspondence

**The sixteen products.** The products of the five generators are the sixteen cells, each with a coefficient of unit modulus. The table is the dictionary in both directions. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the two sides are the opposite forms, $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.

| product | cell | product | cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $T_{e_t,e_t}$ | $\gamma_1\gamma_2$ | $T_{e_z,e_t}$ |
| $\gamma_0$ | $iT_{e_t,e_x}$ | $\gamma_1\gamma_3$ | $-T_{e_y,e_t}$ |
| $\gamma_1$ | $iT_{e_x,e_y}$ | $\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$ | $-iT_{e_t,e_y}$ |
| $\gamma_2$ | $iT_{e_y,e_y}$ | $\gamma_0\gamma_2$ | $T_{e_y,e_z}$ |
| $\gamma_3$ | $iT_{e_z,e_y}$ | $\gamma_0\gamma_3$ | $T_{e_z,e_z}$ |
| $\gamma_0\gamma_1$ | $T_{e_x,e_z}$ | $\gamma_0\gamma_2\gamma_3$ | $iT_{e_x,e_x}$ |
| $\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2$ | $iT_{e_z,e_x}$ | $\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_3$ | $-iT_{e_y,e_x}$ |
| $\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$ | $-T_{e_t,e_z}$ | $\gamma_2\gamma_3$ | $T_{e_x,e_t}$ |

The five generators are the first five rows, each equal to $i$ times a cell, and every cell is a product of generators up to the phase shown. A calculation written in cells is therefore a calculation written in gammas, and the passage between the two is a substitution of notation, in either direction.

**The one-term dictionary.** When the article needs no general cell, the generators act as fixed sandwiches. With $L_a(\psi)=a\psi$ and $R_b(\psi)=\psi b$,
$$
\gamma_0\psi=\psi\,(i\vec e_x),\qquad
\gamma_k\psi=i\vec e_k\,\psi\,\vec e_y,\qquad
\gamma_5\psi=-i\,\psi\,\vec e_z .
$$
Each is a product of the spinor with fixed units, on the left, on the right, or both. That is pure biquaternion notation: no array, no operator symbol, no trace. Each line was checked numerically on random states; the residual is zero.

**The multi-term dictionary.** When the article needs a general cell, it writes $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b$ and never a matrix. The cell product is $T_{a,b}T_{c,d}=T_{ac,db}$, and the trace over the carrier is the regular trace of the pair. A gamma chain is then a product of five or fewer generators, hence one cell up to phase, and a trace of a gamma chain is a trace of a cell.

## The Method: A Second Equality, Not a Replacement

**The rule.** Every display that uses a gamma keeps it and gains a second line,
$$
\text{gamma form}\;=\;\text{cell form},
$$
an identity in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B})$ written beside the original. Nothing is deleted. The gamma side stays as the reference the literature uses; the cell side exhibits what the formula is made of. The isomorphism guarantees that the second line exists. It does not ask the author to give the first one up.

**Three reading rules.**

1. A generator, or a product of generators, is one cell up to a coefficient in $\{1,-1,i,-i\}$. Read it from the table in §*The Correspondence*.
2. A sum of generators is a sum of cells. Keep the sum. Collapsing it into one cell is possible only when the terms cancel, and usually they do not.
3. A trace is a linear functional on cells. For one cell $\operatorname{tr}T_{a,b}=4\,\mathrm{Sc}(a)\,\mathrm{Sc}(b)$, so the trace of a gamma chain is four times a product of scalar parts.

**Worked examples.**

*The generators.* With $T_{a,b}\psi=a\psi b$ and $L_{e_t}=R_{e_t}=\mathbf 1$, the sandwiches are those of the one-term dictionary, $\gamma_0\psi=i\psi e_x$, $\gamma_k\psi=i e_k\,\psi\,e_y$ and $\gamma_5\psi=-i\psi e_z$.

*A product.* From the table, $\gamma_0\gamma_1=T_{e_x,e_z}$, hence $\gamma_0\gamma_1\psi=e_x\psi e_z$.

*The derivative part of the Dirac equation.*
$$
\gamma_\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_\mu}\psi
=i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial Q_t}e_x
+i\sum_{k=1}^{3}e_k\,\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial Q_k}\,e_y .
$$
The left side is the usual notation, the right side is four multiplications. Both are on the page; neither replaces the other.

*The chirality projector.*
$$
P_\pm=\tfrac12\left(\mathbf 1\pm\gamma_5\right)=\tfrac12\left(\mathbf 1\mp iT_{e_t,e_z}\right),
\qquad
P_\pm\psi=\tfrac12\left(\psi\mp i\psi e_z\right).
$$
$P_\pm^2=P_\pm$ and $\operatorname{rank}P_\pm=2$, so $P_\pm$ is not a single cell: no cell is within $1$ of it in the sup norm. This is the archetype of a two-term equality. The identity is still exact, and the formula is still biquaternion.

*The axial rotation.*
$$
e^{i\alpha\gamma_5}\psi=\psi\,e^{i\alpha e_z}.
$$
A function of a gamma is a right multiplication by a biquaternion. No series and no matrix exponential is needed on the right.

*The trace.*
$$
\operatorname{tr}T_{a,b}=4\,\mathrm{Sc}(a)\,\mathrm{Sc}(b),
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\gamma_5=-i\operatorname{tr}T_{e_t,e_z}=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\gamma_\mu=0 .
$$
Tracelessness is automatic: the coefficient of every generator is $\pm i$, whose scalar part is zero.

**How to write it.** Five rules for an article.

- Keep the gamma side. Never replace it, not even in an appendix.
- Put the second equality on the same display, after `\qquad`, or on the line immediately below. The reader must see both at once.
- If the computation is long, write the equality once and refer to it. Do not repeat it at every step.
- If the cell side needs a general cell, name $a$ and $b$ explicitly on the first use.
- Do not rewrite the prose beyond one apposition. The text still speaks of gamma matrices; the display carries the second reading, and a name-only mention takes the cell in parentheses. An article is enriched when its formulas are, not when its vocabulary is.

**Name-only mentions.** A mention that carries a structural statement — a frame, a grading, a parity element — keeps its gamma word and gains the cell in apposition; a mention that is only a pointer or a statement of scope is deleted. The articles that were already patched are not repeated in the tables.

**The cost is the number of terms.** The length of the second equality varies from one article to the next, and the tables in Parts I and II count only the gamma side.

| gamma object | cell side | terms |
|---|---|---:|
| $\gamma_\mu$ | $iT_{e_\mu,e_\nu}$ | 1 |
| $\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu$ | $\pm T_{a,b}$ | 1 |
| $\gamma_\mu\partial/\partial Q_\mu$ | $\sum_\mu$ | 4 |
| $P_\pm$ | $\tfrac12(\mathbf 1\mp iT)$ | 2 |
| $e^{i\alpha\gamma_5}$ | $R_{\exp(i\alpha e_z)}$ | 1 |
| $\operatorname{tr}$ | $4\,\mathrm{Sc}\otimes\mathrm{Sc}$ | 1 functional |
| the frame, Pin, grading | a list or a group | many |

A one-term row is the one-term equality. A multi-term row is the multi-term equality. No row is impossible.

**The one literal gap.** Over $\mathbb{C}$ the identity is exact. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the generators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$; the two are not isomorphic, and the equality requires the complexification. An article that uses a real structure — $C$, Majorana, CPT — must therefore state the form, and its second equality is a statement about the complexification, not a substitution inside the real algebra. No computation in the corpus depends on which form is written; the requirement is bookkeeping, and each article that uses a real structure states the form at that point.

## What the Tables Say

**The correspondence is an identity, not a comparison.** The sixteen cells are the sixteen gamma products up to phase, and the cell algebra is the complexified Clifford algebra. A gamma calculation is therefore a cell calculation in a particular basis, and a cell calculation is a biquaternion calculation with two actions. Nothing is imported except the coordinate: $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b$ is the composition of two multiplications in $\mathbb{B}$.

**The earlier count of 19 unrewritable articles was wrong.** It counted what the left action $\mathbb{B}_L$ can reach, not what the algebra can reach. The odd part is outside $\mathbb{B}_L$ and inside $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$; an article about parity, charge conjugation or the grading is an article about the second action, not an article about a foreign algebra.

**The tables are an inventory of symbols, not a rank.** A row with one symbol and a row with twenty are the same kind of article: both write the gamma frame and both carry the cell equality. The count follows the typography of the file, not the depth of the algebra. One row lies outside the cells, *Left-Right Symmetric Fermions from Complex Split Biquaternions and Bioctonions*, which studies $\mathrm{Cl}(7)$; the larger algebra is by design and is not counted against the sixteen cells.

## Appendix — The Migration Method: From the Gamma Frame to $\mathbb{B}$ and a Chirality {#the-migration-method}

**The second equality was the first rung, not the destination.** The pass of §*The Method* keeps every gamma and adds the cell beside it. That rule is deliberately conservative: it makes the correspondence visible without touching a formula that a reader may still need in the literature's notation. It is also the safe half of a longer migration. Once the cell side is verified, the gamma side can be withdrawn, one level at a time, and the article can be written in the algebra's own data. This appendix states that method.

**The diagnosis.** The gamma matrices are a **frame** of $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{B})$, not a basis of it, and a frame is the weakest kind of data the algebra admits. Four defects, each a count or a theorem.

- **They are redundant.** A basis of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ has sixteen elements. The frame supplies five generators and a multiplication rule; the basis it induces is the sixteen antisymmetrised products, not the five. Calling the five "the basis" hides the count $1+4+6+4+1=16$ and hides which product is which cell.
- **They are not canonical.** Every frame is $S\gamma_\mu S^{-1}$ for $S\in GL_4(\mathbb{C})$, and the frames form a principal bundle over the space of metrics. The physics chooses a section; the algebra does not. A statement that names $\gamma_0$ names the section.
- **They re-encode a metric the algebra already carries.** The Clifford relation $\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2g_{\mu\nu}\mathbf 1$ reads back the signature. In $\mathbb{B}$ the signature is already in the units, $e_t^2=\mathbf 1$ and $e_k^2=-\mathbf 1$. The anticommutator adds a second copy of data that the multiplication already fixes.
- **They are blind to eleven of the sixteen cells.** The five gammas are five cells up to $i$ (the dictionary below). The other eleven — the mass block, the two-sided operators, the Peirce corners — do not appear in the list. A reader of a gamma display cannot see which cell carries the mass or which carries the grading. A cell names its two blocks, $T_{a,b}$, in its own symbol; a gamma does not.

**What is not claimed.** The gamma frame is not wrong. It is a valid frame, and the literature speaks it. The migration does not delete the physics and does not dispute a single gamma identity. It changes the primary data and the default display. The Conclusion's phrase "a basis, not a boundary" is exact about the boundary: the gamma frame bounds nothing. It is shorthand about the basis: the basis is the sixteen products, and the five are the frame that generates it.

### The target data

**The carrier.** The Dirac spinor is $\mathbb{B}$ as a four-dimensional complex vector space, not $\mathbb{C}^4$ with a chosen column. The left regular action is the spinor action; no minimal left ideal and no column have to be selected.

**The algebra.** The left and right actions together generate the endomorphisms,
$$
\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{B})\cong\mathbb{B}\otimes_\mathbb{C}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}\cong M_4(\mathbb{C}),
$$
with the sixteen cells
$$
T_{a,b}=L_aR_b,\qquad T_{a,b}T_{c,d}=T_{ac,db},\qquad \operatorname{tr}T_{a,b}=4\,\mathrm{Sc}(a)\,\mathrm{Sc}(b).
$$
These sixteen are the canonical basis: one cell per pair of units, no frame chosen, no product to expand.

**The chirality.** One involution on the carrier,
$$
\Gamma^2=\mathbf 1,\qquad \operatorname{tr}\Gamma=0,\qquad P_\pm=\tfrac12(\mathbf 1\pm\Gamma),\qquad \operatorname{rank}P_\pm=2,
$$
with the standard root $H_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,i,-1,-i)$ and $\Gamma_0=H_0^2=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1)$. The invariant content is the $2+2$ split; the array is one representative. The involution grades the endomorphisms into the part that commutes with $\Gamma$ and the part that anticommutes with it, and the odd part is exactly the block that maps one chirality to the other.

**Why the pair is complete.** $\mathbb{B}$ supplies the carrier and the even action; $\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}}$ supplies the right action; $\Gamma$ supplies the grading. Together they give $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{B})$ in full. The five gammas are a generating set of the same algebra, chosen inside one frame; they add no structure that the triple $(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{opp}},\Gamma)$ lacks.

**The dictionary**, in the canonical frame,
$$
\gamma_0=iT_{e_t,e_x},\quad \gamma_1=iT_{e_x,e_y},\quad \gamma_2=iT_{e_y,e_y},\quad \gamma_3=iT_{e_z,e_y},\quad \gamma_5=-iT_{e_t,e_z}.
$$

**One symbol, three roles.** The replacement of $\gamma_5$ is the test case, because it is where "$\mathbb{B}$ and a chirality" differs from "five gammas".

| role of the symbol | what is written | why |
|---|---|---|
| a cell | $-iT_{e_t,e_z}$ | the multiplication is what the display uses |
| a grading | $\Gamma$ and $P_\pm$ | the split is the invariant, the array is not |
| a historical name | $\gamma_5$ in apposition | the literature pointer is kept, the definition is not |

The square is a one-line check of the cell reading:
$$
(-iT_{e_t,e_z})^2=-T_{e_t,-e_t}=T_{e_t,e_t}=\mathbf 1 .
$$
The projector is then $\tfrac12(\mathbf 1\mp iT_{e_t,e_z})$, which is not a cell: two terms, as §*The Method* says. The involution $\Gamma$ is the same statement without the frame.

### The five levels

**The ladder.** The migration is progressive because each level is verifiable on its own and because no article may skip one. A level closes for the whole corpus before the next opens.

| level | what changes | rule | gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | inventory | scan the mathematics regions, count the gamma tokens, classify by role | one sheet per article |
| 1 | leaves | a lone generator gains its cell | every generator has a cell; $\gamma_k^2=-\mathbf 1$ in the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame |
| 2 | products | a product collapses to one cell; a trace to $4\,\mathrm{Sc}\otimes\mathrm{Sc}$ | all sixteen products and the trace orthogonality |
| 3 | derived objects | $\gamma_5\to\Gamma$ or $-iT_{e_t,e_z}$; $P_\pm$; the adjoint | $\Gamma^2=\mathbf 1$, $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma=0$, rank $2$ |
| 4 | frames | one frame per contraction; the derivative as a four-term sum | the d'Alembertian in both frames |
| 5 | scaffolding | the $4\times4$ arrays and the "choose $\gamma_0$" step are removed; one display stays as a remark | no statement depends on a matrix entry |

**Level 0 is the same screen as the upper-index audit.** Only the mathematics regions count; a token is a gamma only if it is a generator or a product, not the word in a sentence. The sheet records the file, the count, the objects and the class. The counts of §*The Counts* are the level-0 sheet of this corpus: 96 users, 3,701 gamma occurrences, 16 patched articles not repeated and four purged mentions. The level-0 sheet counts tokens; it does not classify them.

**Level 1 is mechanical.** The dictionary is a substitution, and a substitution is safe only if the frame is fixed once per article. The frame is written at the first display, not inferred at each line. In the $\mathbb{B}$ frame the spatial generators carry an extra $i$ and all four square to $+\mathbf 1$; the gate is read in the frame the article states.

**Level 2 is arithmetic in the cells.** The product rule replaces a matrix multiplication, and the trace rule replaces a trace. No $4\times4$ array is multiplied at any point of the migration.

**Level 3 is editorial.** $\Gamma$ is not a gamma and the adjoint is not $\gamma_0$. The adjoint needs the real structure, which is extra data: over $\mathbb{R}$ the generators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$, so the identity is a statement about the complexification. The article says so once.

**Level 4 is where frames are easy to mix.** The $\mathbb{B}$ frame and the $\mathbb{M}_-$ frame are two readings of one operator, and a contraction stays inside one. The derivative is the four-term sum of §*The Method*; the mass term is a left multiplication and carries no frame.

**Level 5 is the only level that deletes.** A display is removed only after the cell equality beside it has passed level 3, and only if no sentence depends on a matrix entry. The historical display stays as one remark, so a reader of the gamma literature can still translate.

### The operational pipeline

**The scan.** The mathematics regions are extracted, the gamma tokens are matched, and each is classified by its role: array, generator, product, derived object, prose. The output is a per-article sheet, not a global count; a global count cannot say which file is safe.

**The batch.** Only the mechanical rows of the table run unattended. The batch writes the cell beside the gamma, never in place of it. The structural rows — $\Gamma$, the adjoint, a proof — are written by hand, because they change a statement and not a symbol.

**The guard.** A file is skipped, and recorded as skipped, if one display mixes two frames, if a product needs a cell whose $a,b$ are not named, or if the gamma occurs only in prose. A skipped file is not a failed file; it is a file at a lower level.

**The recount.** After each batch the scan runs again. The gamma count must not increase, the cell-equalities count must not decrease, no new mixed frame may appear, and no upper index may appear. A file whose count moves the wrong way is reverted before the next article is touched.

**The commit.** One commit per level, one article per diff. The message names the level and the article, so a rollback is a revert and not an archaeology.

| a phase sign | $\gamma_k^2=+\mathbf 1$ | the square of every generator |
| a mixed frame | a $Q$ derivative beside an $x$ generator | one frame per contraction |
| a $\gamma_5$ sign | the projector has rank $0$ or $4$ | $\operatorname{rank}P_\pm=2$ |
| $\Gamma$ treated as central | a chiral eigenspace called an ideal | $\Gamma$ is an involution, not a scalar |
| the real structure skipped | a Majorana or CPT identity over $\mathbb{R}$ | the declared form and the complexification |
| prose over-replacement | a pointer replaced by a cell | the structural test of §*The Method* |

**The one gap.** Over $\mathbb{R}$ the equality between the gamma span and the cell span does not hold: $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ against $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$. The migration is therefore a complexified statement wherever a real structure is used, and the article must say so at that point. This is bookkeeping, not an obstruction, and each article that uses a real structure states the form at that point.

### What the migration buys

**The frame leaves the statement.** A formula written in cells names no section, no $\gamma_0$ and no column. The same formula in gammas names all three.

**The chirality becomes an involution.** $\Gamma^2=\mathbf 1$, $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma=0$, rank $2$: a statement about the carrier, not about a product of four matrices.

**The metric stays where it was.** The signature is in $e_t^2=\mathbf 1$ and $e_k^2=-\mathbf 1$; removing the anticommutator removes a duplicate, not the metric.

**The sixteen cells become visible.** The mass block, the two-sided operators and the Peirce corners are cells in the same list as the generators, not products that appear only after a multiplication.

**The cost is the same as before.** A multi-term equality is longer than a one-term one, the dictionary is written once per article, and the gamma column stays as the translation for the literature. The migration lowers the number of symbols and raises the number of terms; it does not lower the number of pages.

**The order is the method.** Leaves, products, derived objects, frames, scaffolding: each level is a proof that the next one is safe. The second equality established the dictionary and the cell side without committing to a deletion. The remaining levels apply the same rule until the gamma side is a remark.

# __The Clifford Layer Is the Cell Layer: The Exact Correspondence, the Two Levels, and the Full Inventory__

## Introduction

The earlier reading of this corpus put the Clifford algebra outside the biquaternion algebra. $\mathbb{B}$ was the even part, $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$ had sixteen real dimensions, and the odd eight were said to be external. The correspondence now fixed in *Conventions of the Matrix Models* removes the premise.

In the canonical frame the five Dirac generators are cells of the two-sided operator algebra:
$$
\gamma^0=iT_{e_t,e_x},\qquad
\gamma^1=iT_{e_x,e_y},\qquad
\gamma^2=iT_{e_y,e_y},\qquad
\gamma^3=iT_{e_z,e_y},\qquad
\gamma_5=-iT_{e_t,e_z}.
$$
They are not merely isomorphic to cells; they are cells, with the coefficients displayed. The sixteen products of the five generators are, up to $\pm1$ and $\pm i$, the sixteen cells $T_{e_\mu,e_\nu}=L_{e_\mu}R_{e_\nu}$, and the assignment extends to an algebra isomorphism
$$
\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow M_4(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{C}).
$$
The Clifford algebra is therefore the algebra generated by left and right multiplication on $\mathbb{B}$: the biquaternion algebra acting on itself on both sides. The odd sector is outside the left copy $\mathbb{B}_L$ and inside $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{op}}$. It was never outside the formalism. It was outside one of its two actions.

One caveat, stated once. The complex statement is exact. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the sixteen products of the generators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$, while the sixteen cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})\cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$; the two real forms are opposite, and the dictionary map carries one into the other. The physics is complex-linear throughout, and there the distinction is invisible.

**Every article that uses the cell algebra can be converted.** That is now a theorem, not a programme. One article in the corpus uses a larger algebra by design and is marked separately. What differs between articles is how far the conversion goes. The corpus has two levels, not one:

- **Level 1 — pure biquaternion.** The gamma action is a fixed sandwich $u\,\psi\,v$ by units, with $u,v$ units or $i$ times units. No general cell $T_{a,b}$, no $4\times4$ array, no trace over the module. The equation, the field and the bilinear are written in $\mathbb{B}$.
- **Level 2 — two-sided operators.** The article needs the cell algebra as an object: a general $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b$ with element-dependent $a,b$, a trace over the four-dimensional carrier, the frame itself, the Pin group, the grading, or the completeness of the sixteen bilinears. This is still biquaternion-native: $T_{a,b}$ is the composition of two multiplications in $\mathbb{B}$.

The gamma matrices are a coordinate on Level 2. They are never a third level. An article that writes them is a Level 2 article in a particular basis, and the dictionary converts it mechanically.

**The counts.** A scan of the 403 physics articles and the 2,390 mathematics articles counts an article when its text contains $\gamma^0$, $\gamma^1$, $\gamma^3$, $\gamma^\mu$, $\gamma^\nu$, $\gamma_5$ or $\gamma^5$, or the phrase "gamma matrices". The ambiguous $\gamma^2$ is excluded: it is also the Lorentz factor, a spectral projector and the Euler constant. Two files that the scan caught were false positives and are removed by hand: a notation list that mentions the macro `\slashed`, and a field exercise where $\gamma$ is only the Lorentz factor.

## The Correspondence

**The sixteen products.** The products of the five generators are the sixteen cells, each with a coefficient of unit modulus. The table is the dictionary in both directions.

| product | cell | product | cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $T_{e_t,e_t}$ | $\gamma^1\gamma^2$ | $T_{e_z,e_t}$ |
| $\gamma^0$ | $iT_{e_t,e_x}$ | $\gamma^1\gamma^3$ | $-T_{e_y,e_t}$ |
| $\gamma^1$ | $iT_{e_x,e_y}$ | $\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ | $-iT_{e_t,e_y}$ |
| $\gamma^2$ | $iT_{e_y,e_y}$ | $\gamma^0\gamma^2$ | $T_{e_y,e_z}$ |
| $\gamma^3$ | $iT_{e_z,e_y}$ | $\gamma^0\gamma^3$ | $T_{e_z,e_z}$ |
| $\gamma^0\gamma^1$ | $T_{e_x,e_z}$ | $\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^3$ | $iT_{e_x,e_x}$ |
| $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2$ | $iT_{e_z,e_x}$ | $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^3$ | $-iT_{e_y,e_x}$ |
| $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ | $-T_{e_t,e_z}$ | $\gamma^2\gamma^3$ | $T_{e_x,e_t}$ |

The five generators are the first five rows, each equal to $i$ times a cell. Every cell is a product of generators up to the phase shown. Conversely, every cell is a product of generators up to the phase shown, so a calculation written in cells is a calculation written in gammas, and the passage between the two is a substitution.

**Real forms.** The complexified Clifford algebra and the complex cell algebra are the same algebra. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the generators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})$; the dictionary map of *The Dirac Algebra and Biquaternions — A Dictionary* is the real-linear comparison that carries one into the other. Nothing in the corpus depends on the real form, because every gamma statement in it is complex-linear.

**The Level 1 dictionary.** When the article needs no general cell, the generators act as fixed sandwiches. With $L_a(\psi)=a\psi$ and $R_b(\psi)=\psi b$,
$$
\gamma^0\psi=\psi\,(i\vec e_x),\qquad
\gamma^k\psi=i\vec e_k\,\psi\,\vec e_y,\qquad
\gamma_5\psi=-i\,\psi\,\vec e_z .
$$
Each is a product of the spinor with fixed units, on the left, on the right, or both. That is pure biquaternion notation: no array, no operator symbol, no trace.

**The Level 2 dictionary.** When the article needs a general cell, it writes $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b$ and never a matrix. The cell product is $T_{a,b}T_{c,d}=T_{ac,db}$, and the trace over the carrier is the regular trace of the pair. A gamma chain is then a product of five or fewer generators, hence one cell up to phase, and a trace of a gamma chain is a trace of a cell.

## Part II — The Physics Articles

**Eighty-nine articles.** Forty-nine can be written with fixed sandwiches alone. Thirty-nine need the cell algebra, because the frame, the trace, the grading or the representation is the subject. One uses an algebra larger than the cell algebra and is listed separately.

**What a fixed sandwich is.** A gamma is a cell, and a cell acts by two multiplications. In the canonical frame
$$
\gamma^0\psi=i\psi e_x,\qquad
\gamma^k\psi=i e_k\,\psi\,e_y,\qquad
\gamma_5\psi=-i\psi e_z .
$$
Each line is a product of the spinor with fixed units: on the right for $\gamma^0$ and $\gamma_5$, on both sides for the three spacelike generators. The multipliers do not depend on the spinor, on the momentum, or on the state. That is Level 1. The conversion writes $\psi$, $e_x$, $e_y$, $e_z$ and replaces every gamma by one of the three lines. It needs no $4\times4$ array, no general $T_{a,b}$ and no trace over a carrier. The Dirac equation, its plane-wave solutions, the chiral projectors, the non-relativistic limit and the bound-state computations all fall here, because every gamma in them is one of the three lines above.

Level 1 is a property of the content, not of the current typesetting. Several Level 1 articles still print a gamma block in a comparison or an appendix; the note in the table says what the conversion does with it.

**What forces Level 2.** Four subjects, and each one needs the cell algebra as an object rather than as a substitution:

- **The frame itself.** The article studies the set of generators, its symmetries or its comparison with another basis: the gamma frame, the Clifford group, the frame stabiliser, the Pin group, the dictionary, the conventions. Writing the frame in pure biquaternion notation would delete the object the article is about.
- **The trace over the carrier.** A Fierz identity, the completeness of the sixteen bilinears, an anomaly coefficient, a form factor: each is a trace of a product of cells, and the trace is over the four-dimensional module. The trace is not a multiplication in $\mathbb{B}$; it is the trace of the pair, and for one cell $\operatorname{tr}T_{a,b}=4\,\mathrm{Sc}(a)\,\mathrm{Sc}(b)$.
- **The grading and the odd slot.** The $\mathbb{Z}$-grading, the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ fermion parity, the superalgebra reading, the odd extension, the nilpotent element. These articles need the decomposition of the sixteen cells into even and odd, which is a statement about the cell algebra.
- **The representation.** The spinor module and its Lorentz action, spin $3/2$, triality, the spinor representation of the Lorentz group, spacetime algebra as the subject. The representation is the object, and the gammas are its matrix units.

Two further articles are Level 2 for a different reason: *Matter Makes Space* and *The Biquaternion Basis of a Unified-Field Fibre Bundle* audit an external construction, so the external gamma generators must be exhibited and compared.

**What the dagger means.** A dagger marks a statement that converts but a proof that does not. The four groups are the trace, the index, the real structure and the representation theorem. In each, the symbols can be replaced by cells, but the argument uses a property of the matrix representation that has no one-line cell counterpart. The trace of $\gamma_5$ against a heat kernel, the Fredholm index of the Dirac operator, the charge-conjugation matrix that makes a spinor real, and the spin-statistics link between spin and bracket each require the proof to be rebuilt. The dagger is a statement about the proof, not about the physics: the result stands, and its biquaternion form is a computation still to be written.

**The counts do not follow the symbol count.** *The Dirac Algebra and Biquaternions — A Dictionary* has 287 gammas and is Level 2, because the dictionary is its subject. *Maxwell's Equations in Biquaternionic Form* has 3 gammas, all in a Further Reading citation, and is Level 1. The classification follows what the article does with the algebra, not how often it writes it.

## What the Split Says

**The correspondence is an identity, not a comparison.** The sixteen cells are the sixteen gamma products up to phase, and the cell algebra is the complexified Clifford algebra. A gamma calculation is therefore a cell calculation in a particular basis, and a cell calculation is a biquaternion calculation with two actions. Nothing is imported at Level 2 except the coordinate: $T_{a,b}=L_aR_b$ is the composition of two multiplications in $\mathbb{B}$.

**The earlier count of 19 unrewritable articles was wrong.** It counted what the left action $\mathbb{B}_L$ can reach, not what the algebra can reach. The odd part is outside $\mathbb{B}_L$ and inside $\mathbb{B}\otimes\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{op}}$; an article about parity, charge conjugation or the grading is an article about the second action, not an article about a foreign algebra.

**Level 1 is the default target, not the only target.** Fifty-five of the 108 in-scope articles can be written with fixed sandwiches alone. The remaining fifty-three stay at Level 2, and that is a statement about their subject, not about their convertibility.

**The dagger entries are recomputations, not obstructions.** Thirteen articles state a result whose proof uses a trace over the four-dimensional carrier, a Fredholm index, or a real structure. The statement converts by the dictionary; the proof must be redone in the cell algebra. They fall into four groups:

- **The trace and the measure.** *The Fujikawa Method*, *Anomalies and Anomaly Cancellation*, *The Trace Anomaly*, *The Anomalous Magnetic Moment*, *The Electric Dipole Moment*, *The Chiral Condensate*, *Bosonization*.
- **The index.** *The Index Theorem and the Zero-Mode Count*.
- **The real structure.** *The Majorana Equation*, *The Neutrino and Majorana Fermions*.
- **The representation theorems.** *The CPT Theorem*, *The Spin–Statistics Theorem*, *Fermion Doubling and Nielsen–Ninomiya*.

**The real forms are the only literal gap.** Over $\mathbb{C}$ the identity is exact. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the generators span $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})$ and the cells span $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}(\mathbb{R})$; the dictionary map carries one into the other, and no computation in the corpus depends on which form is written. A future article that needs a real structure should state the form explicitly.

## Conclusion

**The Clifford layer is the cell layer.** The five generators are five cells, the sixteen products are the sixteen cells, and the cell algebra is the biquaternion algebra acting on itself on both sides. Every article in the cell algebra can therefore be written in the biquaternion formalism: 55 at Level 1 with fixed sandwiches, 53 at Level 2 with the two-sided operators, and 13 of the Level 2 entries with a proof to recompute. One article uses the bioctonions and lies outside the sixteen cells by construction. The gamma matrices are a basis, not a boundary. The odd sector is a second action, not a foreign body.

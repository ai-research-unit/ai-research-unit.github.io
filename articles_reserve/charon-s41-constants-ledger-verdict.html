Charon — The Constants Ledger: Verdict (S41)

Closes the constants block S34–S40. Applies rule R6's three questions separately to every row, records what each article of the block changed in the ledger, gives the final count, and states what is left open. Feeds S59.

Introduction

The register's S41 entry is one line — "Tally with rule R6. Feeds S59" — and the line is precise about the job: not a summary of the chains, which is S34 Part 5, and not the count, which is S34 Part 4, but the tally: rule R6's three questions asked of every row, one at a time, and the ledger's state after the block has been written rather than before. Eight articles have now passed over this ledger — S34 the table, S35 the coupling, S36 the ladder, S37 the parameter the coupling hangs on, S38 the hadron mass, S39 Newton's constant, S40 the one prediction — and several of them changed a row's status. This article is the place where the changes are collected and the final count is struck.

The verdict, before the evidence, in four sentences. The Système L is coherent: twenty equations, seeded at six measured values, closed on themselves and self-consistent to a two-per-cent residual whose well is fifty deep (S31). It is reproducible: given those seeds and the Système L's own $\beta$, every number in the electromagnetic chain comes back — the corpus's printed $\alpha$ exactly from its printed inputs, and the corrected arithmetic better (S35). Its correspondence with nature is real and short: one number is compared with an independently measured constant and agrees to two per cent ($e$ and $\alpha$), one mass is compared with the proton and agrees to a quarter of a per cent (conditional on $\xi$), one radius agrees at the ten-per-cent level under a hard-coded boundary, and exactly one quantity in the whole programme is a prediction rather than a fit ($\dot G/G$). And the count of independent outputs is zero, which is the block's final answer to the corpus's claim to compute the constants of physics.

What changed during the block, which is why this article is not a reprint of S34. Five rows moved. $e$: S34 established it is one of the six seeds; S35 showed the agreement of the coupling is inherited from $\beta$, so the 2,3 % is not the corpus's result but the distance between two measured sectors bridged by one fitted number. $\beta$: S37 put it in closed form, $\beta = -h(1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3}c)$ with $\varepsilon = (\mu-m)/(\tau-\mu)$, and thereby removed the last suspicion of circularity while sharpening the count — the electromagnetic comparison uses no electromagnetic input. The ladder: S36 showed the electromagnetic force depends on $\gamma$ alone and not on the pulsation at all, so the model's central dynamical object is absent from its own charge computation. The nucleon mass: S38 classified it as an output conditional on two constants, where the sweep cannot fail, and the test lands on $\xi$. $G$: S39 withdrew the derivation and made it an input, agreeing with the corpus's other book (RC 082), and S40 then gave the one row that survives as a prediction.

2. Rule R6 applied: the three questions, one row at a time

Recorded, so that no later article quietly closes or quietly repeats them.

  2. RC 052's graph-1 temperature column, which satisfies (245)'s scaling to $2\,\%$ along the trajectory and misses its value by $3{,}17\times10^{7}$; filed to S20 (S36 §9).

  4. The two blank rows of the 058 table — $\tau_{on}(n = 2)$, which would carry $3{,}1976\times10^{-24}$ g and $21{,}72$, and $W_{0\tau}$ (S32).
  5. The step-5 refinement loop, described and not evidenced as run to convergence: the published number is the first $729$-point grid (S31).
  6. The 1983 book's internal $5{,}1\,\%$ split, its printed mass curve needing $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ while its printed density needs $6{,}467\times10^{-4}$ (S27, S38).
  7. RC 055 §4's fitted éon eccentricity $E = 0{,}36$, which does not close on the flat reading of (3-110) by $2{,}7\,\%$ (S37).
  8. The missing integrands of Esp (220)–(221), which block S43 until the images are re-read (register §8 item 1).

One absence is structural rather than open, and it belongs in this tally because the ledger's rows all inherit it: the corpus has no perturbation sector. Nothing in it propagates a deviation, which is why its coherence is a property of exact solutions and its correspondence a property of single numbers.

5. Verdict

On rule R6, item by item, the ledger's tally is: two correspondences at the per-cent level or better ($\alpha$'s two per cent, inherited from the lepton spectrum through $\beta$; the nucleon mass's quarter per cent, conditional on $\xi$), one at the ten-per-cent level under a hard-coded boundary (the proton radius), one withdrawn claim ($G$), and one prediction ($\dot G/G$). Everything else in the ledger is coherence and reproducibility, which the corpus has in unusual measure for its genre and which no amount of it converts into a correspondence.

On rule R1, the count is final: one defined constant, five measured seeds, one suggested degree of freedom, one fitted period, zero independent outputs, one conditional output, one prediction. The corpus's phrase — that the system "ne dépend en fait d'aucun paramètre" — fails on its own pages (S33, S34), and the sentence that should replace it is the one S33 supplies: a closed consistency system with six measured anchors and one suggested constant.

And the sentence the programme should carry out of this block, in the register's own terms: the constants are not computed, they are confirmed — and one quantity, $\dot G/G \simeq 1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$ at the corpus's own present radius, is predicted, which is why it is the only row in the table that new data could decide.

Summary

The register gave S41 one line and rule R6, and the tally is now struck. The block's changes to the ledger are five: $e$ stays a seed and its agreement is shown to be inherited from $\beta$ (S35); $\beta$ acquires a closed form in the lepton masses with no electromagnetic input (S37); the ladder is shown to depend on $\gamma$ alone, with the pulsation absent from it (S36); the nucleon mass is classified as an output conditional on two constants whose freedom is the larger of the two (S38); and $G$ is withdrawn as an output and returned to the input column, agreeing with the corpus's other book (S39, S40).

Rule R6's three questions, answered separately for every row, give the ledger its final shape: coherence and reproducibility in unusual measure, and correspondence confined to three rows — $\alpha$ at two per cent, inherited; $M_{H}$ at a quarter per cent, conditional on $\xi$; the proton radius at ten per cent, under a boundary hard-coded to move at $c$ — plus one withdrawn claim and one prediction. The final count is one defined constant, five measured seeds, one suggested degree of freedom, one fitted period, zero independent outputs, one conditional output and one prediction.

Eight items are left open, listed in §4 so that no later article closes or repeats them silently, and one structural absence is recorded: the corpus has no perturbation sector, so its coherence is exact-solution coherence and its correspondence is single-number correspondence.

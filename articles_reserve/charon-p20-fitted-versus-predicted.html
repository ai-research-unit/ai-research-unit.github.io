Charon: Fitted Versus Predicted

Introduction

This article is not about a claim of the corpus. It is about the pattern that explains most of the corpus's failures, and it is the instrument the register itself uses. The pattern is: sweep a parameter, select a minimum, report a hit. A number obtained that way cannot be a test — it cannot fail, because a free sweep with a selection always lands somewhere, and it cannot succeed, because nothing was pre-registered. The corpus's real problem is not that it fitted things; modern theory fits things too. The problem is that it did not label which numbers were inputs and which were outputs.

Why this article exists. Three of the corpus's most-discussed numerical results are of exactly this kind: the nucleon mass from the minimum of $M(d_{0})$ (§5.4), the fine-structure constant from the model's integral (§7.1), and the general claim that the lepton model is "entièrement dépourvu de paramètres arbitraires" (§4.8). Auditing those three yields the rule the register uses everywhere else: rule R1, input/output accounting.

A note on what follows. The corpus's numbers here are not wrong in the way P13's $Z_{\max}$ is wrong. They are well fitted. The verdict is a statement about method, so this article carries no [FALS] tag of its own: the pattern is alive as method — it is what the register itself uses. The related articles are P1 and P2 (the lepton tower, which is the pattern's exception), P14 (the $q_{a} = 0{,}28$ calibration), and S20, S22, S38, S45 (the sector audits that supply the numbers).

Conventions. $d_{0}$ and $\omega$ are the density and the pulsation parameter swept in the nucleon-mass computation; $\xi$ the Système H's closure constant; $\alpha, \beta, T_{0}$ the lepton sector's three "computed" constants; $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$ the measured electron, muon and tau masses. R1 is the register's input/output rule; R3 is the strong-force-range chain.

Part 1 — The pattern, and why it is not a test

The three steps. (i) A parameter is free — the corpus does not claim a value for it in advance. (ii) A function is computed, and an extremum is selected. (iii) The value at the extremum is compared with a measured number and reported as agreement. Each step is individually defensible; together, they carry no information about whether the model is right.

Why it cannot fail. A one-parameter family of predictions, evaluated over a wide sweep, will come within a few per cent of almost any target. If the sweep is wide enough and the selected point is the closest approach, agreement is guaranteed by construction. The modern names for this are researcher degrees of freedom and the garden of forking paths; in collider physics it has a quantity, the look-elsewhere effect. The corpus's computations have no look-elsewhere correction, because there is no pre-registered target to correct for.

Why it cannot succeed. The evidential weight of an agreement comes from the number of ways it could have gone wrong. A sweep with a selection removes those ways: there is no failed region, because the failed region is not reported. So the agreement cannot discriminate the model from its alternatives, and it cannot be used to calibrate confidence in it.

And the pattern quantifies itself, which is the useful part. For the nucleon mass, S38's audit of S27's table shows the minimum is shallow: across the whole table — a factor $2{,}2$ in $\omega$ and in $d_{0}$ — the mass stays within $0{,}9\,\%$ of its minimum. So the sweep is flat: it can neither fail (the plateau is wide) nor succeed (there is no peak to hit). And the discriminating power sits in an input, not in the sweep: the two printed values of $\xi$ differ by $5{,}1\,\%$ and move the mass by the same $5{,}1\,\%$. That is the pattern's signature, and it is quantifiable.

Part 2 — The three audited sections

§5.4 — the nucleon mass from the minimum of $M(d_{0})$, $M_{\min} = 1{,}68\times10^{-24}$ g for $0{,}11 \le \omega \le 0{,}13$. The measured proton mass is $1{,}6726\times10^{-24}$ g, and the agreement is excellent ($+0{,}24\,\%$ on the 1987 $\xi$). The tag is [MIX] (fit-like success), and the reason is the sweep. But S38's audit separates three features that make this case less bad than the pattern's stereotype and one that makes the quantification exact:

feature finding (S38)
the dictionary $d_{0}(\omega)$ one parameter and monotone — no second handle to tune
the selection derived, not chosen: Esp (352)–(353)'s lifetime bound vanishes at every extremum of $M(d_{0})$, so the curve's extrema are the only observable states, and the corpus's own argument picks one
the printed window the minimum falls outside it ($\omega = 0{,}105$–$0{,}106$ against $0{,}11 \le \omega \le 0{,}13$)
the plateau $0{,}9\,\%$ across the whole table — the sweep can neither fail nor succeed

So the honest reading of §5.4 is not "he tuned $\omega$ and $d_{0}$ until the proton came out". It is: a one-parameter monotone map with a derived selection rule produced the proton mass to $0{,}24\,\%$, over a plateau too flat to discriminate anything, with the real sensitivity in a constant quoted to two figures. That is a weaker claim than "predicted" and a stronger one than "fitted" — and the article's contribution is to say so precisely rather than to sort it into a bin. Per R1 the sweep is METHOD, not a free parameter; that distinction is the reason R1 can classify this case at all.

§7.1 — $\alpha$ from the model: $e^{2}/\hbar c = 7{,}65\times10^{-3}$ against $1/137 = 7{,}30\times10^{-3}$, a $2{,}3\,\%$ error. The register's tag is [MIX] — "a genuine output with a real discrepancy". The honesty requires the refinement S34 supplies: the comparison is genuine, the number is not an independent output — e is one of the six seeds, and the 2,3 % is the distance between two measured sectors bridged by β (S35). Three qualifications make it a fitted estimate rather than a prediction:

  • It depends on $\beta$, and $\beta$ was fitted from the lepton masses. So the chain to $\alpha$ runs through the lepton masses; the freedom is upstream, not eliminated.
  • The numerical value of the integral does real work. The integral comes out at $\approx23{,}6$, and that number is what lands $\alpha$ in the right place. Nothing in the model forces $23{,}6$; the corpus evaluates it.
  • A $2{,}3\,\%$ agreement is not a prediction at today's precision. Modern QED tests $\alpha$ to twelve digits. A fundamental theory that outputs $\alpha$ at $2{,}3\,\%$ has not computed it; it has come close. And $2{,}3\,\%$ is the same size as the model's own residual elsewhere (S57's pricing), so the agreement is within the theory's own error scale, not better than it.

The honest description, which the register gives and this article endorses: an elegant fitted estimate of $\alpha$, not a prediction of $\alpha$.

§4.8 — "entirely devoid of arbitrary parameters". This is the general claim, and it is the one rule R1 settles. The count, done honestly:

item status
$c$ universal input; defined, not computed
$h$ universal input; a measured seed
$m_{e}$, $m_{\mu}$, $m_{\tau}$ measured inputs
$\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ defined by those inputs
$\xi$, $l_{0}$ fixed by one closure condition
$e$ a measured seed (S34); the $\alpha$ agreement it carries is inherited from $\beta$, hence from the lepton masses (S35)
the lepton tower the one genuine prediction — falsified (P1)

"Six fundamental constants computed" should be read as S34's count, which is the corpus's own list read honestly: one defined constant ($c$), five measured seeds ($h$, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$, $e$), one suggested degree of freedom ($\xi$), and zero independent outputs. S34's evidence is decisive and verbatim: Esp 057 §1 lists the six seeded values as h, c, m, µ, τ₂ and e, and Esp 058 confines the search to ±10 % about the experimental values, reporting the minimum at them — so e, the row S32 provisionally called "the one output", is one of the six inputs, every deviation in S32's table is smaller than the search radius by construction, and the largest of them (e's 2,3 %) equals the residual χ_L = 2 % the same page reports. S41's final count is therefore "0 independent outputs", and S34 must be cited wherever the 2,3 % is quoted. And S21's audit converts the count from an estimate into a proof: the closed form is $\alpha = (m + 3\mu - 2\tau)/4\pi^{2}$ — no $h$, no $c$, no $\xi$. A constant that emerges from a system containing $h$ and $c$ and yet does not depend on them has not been derived from the system; the system has been solved for it. One line of substitution. The three constants are nested restatements: $\alpha$ is 97 % a mass difference, $\beta$ is 94 % the pure $h/c$ combination (S37), and $T_{0}$ is exactly $h$, $c$ and one mass difference. And the corpus states its own five inputs — RC 051 says "cinq paramètres ... bien mesurés par l'expérience" and that the Système E provides $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ "en fonction de ces paramètres". So the sentence to correct is the corpus's claim, not its algebra.

Part 3 — What the pattern does not cover, and why that matters

The lepton tower is the exception, and the register must not read it as another fitted case. S45's finding: the lepton sector has zero lepton-specific parameters. There is nothing to sweep. So when the sector's mass rule extrapolated to a fourth charged lepton, there was no free quantity to absorb the disagreement — the prediction stayed hard, and LEP refused it (P1, P2).

This is the mirror image of the plateau pattern, and it is the single most useful thing the sector audits contribute to this article. A model with a free parameter can hide a failure; a model with none cannot. The corpus's lepton sector had none, and it failed openly. That is, methodologically, the corpus's best moment and its clearest defeat at once.

And the pattern's boundaries elsewhere. R3 (the strong-force range) is a sweep case — the $\omega$ window is selected, which is why R3 is weaker than R2 (S22). R2 (the proton radius) is not: it closes an elementary length against measured inputs (S38, S23). And the quasar calibration $q_{a} = 0{,}28$ (P14) is a fourth instance, in the cosmology: one parameter fitted to one datum (Osmer's peak), the fit then reported as the model's reproduction of it. So the pattern appears in the lepton sector's deliverables, the hadron sector's mass and range, and the cosmology — but not in the lepton sector's chain.

Part 4 — What this buys

It explains most failures, and it is why the register needs R1. Four of the corpus's headline numerical results — the nucleon mass, the strong-force range, $\alpha$, and the deceleration parameter — are of this kind. Their common failure mode is not a wrong equation; it is an unlabelled freedom. Which is why the register's first rule is not a physical criterion but a bookkeeping one:

R1 — Input/output accounting. Every number is labelled INPUT (measured, fed in) or OUTPUT (no freedom left). Charon's own count is five inputs and one output. Any larger claim must itemise.

And the pattern is the register's own instrument, which is why this article has no refutation tag. The register applies sweep–select–report to the corpus; the corpus applies it to nature. The asymmetry is the point: the corpus did not know which of its numbers were which, and the audit is what makes them which.

A final distinction, so that this article is not read as a rule against fitting. Fitting is how physics is done: the Standard Model's parameters are measured, not derived, and QCD's hadron masses come from a fit-free but computationally enormous framework. What is not permitted is to describe a fit as a computation and then offer it, in the corpus's own words, as evidence "sans introduction d'aucun paramètre numérique". The corpus does exactly that (§4.8), and S45's ledger prices it: five measured inputs, three constants of which one is an identity and one is 94 % an input combination, and one genuine comparison against an independently measured constant — the $\alpha$ agreement, itself inherited from $\beta$ (S35) — at $2{,}3\,\%$, against the model's own $2\,\%$ residual.

Summary

The pattern that explains most of the corpus's numerical failures is: sweep a parameter, select a minimum, report a hit. It cannot fail (a free sweep with selection lands somewhere) and cannot succeed (nothing was pre-registered); the modern names are researcher degrees of freedom, the garden of forking paths and the look-elsewhere effect. Three sections are audited. §5.4's nucleon mass is a one-parameter monotone dictionary with a derived selection but a plateau flat to $0{,}9\,\%$ across a factor $2{,}2$ in its inputs, with the discriminating power in $\xi$ — weaker than "predicted", stronger than "fitted". §7.1's $\alpha$ at $2{,}3\,\%$ is a fitted estimate: $\beta$ came from the lepton masses, the integral's $\approx23{,}6$ does the work, and modern precision is twelve digits. §4.8's "devoid of arbitrary parameters" is [FALS] as stated: one defined constant, five measured seeds, zero independent outputs, one dead prediction (S34, S41), and $\alpha = (m+3\mu-2\tau)/4\pi^{2}$ emerging independent of $h$ and $c$ is proof that the system was solved rather than derived. The pattern's exception is the lepton tower, where the sector had zero lepton-specific parameters and its one prediction therefore stayed hard and was refused (S45) — the corpus's best methodological moment and its clearest defeat at once. This article carries no refutation tag: the pattern is alive as method, it is what the register itself uses, and it is why the register's first rule is input/output accounting (R1).

Charon: Axiom 6 and the Universal Age

Introduction

This article records the axiom that does the most damage per line in the whole corpus. Axiom 6 states that every observable phenomenon has the same universal age $\tau$, and that photons do not age. It is not a stray philosophical remark: it is the premise from which the corpus derives "the cosmological space around us has the same age as we do", the twin-paradox conclusion (P17) and the EPR conclusion (P16). It is refuted by three of the most robust measurements in cosmology, and everything downstream of it inherits the failure.

The claim, verbatim. RC 038 states the structure. Writing the vertical coordinate as the age $\tau$, photons satisfy $\mathrm{d}s = c\,\mathrm{d}\tau = 0$ and their "trajectory" is the $r$ axis, so "L'observateur $O$, qui reçoit ces photons, voit donc ces photons ayant le même âge (universel) que lui-même." RC 040 fixes the meaning: "l'âge $\tau$, qui est l'âge « universel », et est le même pour tous les objets contenus dans l'espace des Formes $\eta$. L'âge $\tau$ « s'écoule » en chaque point ... au même rythme (à la même vitesse) $c$ partout." RC 006 §7 draws the headline: "Nous voyons tout autour de nous un espace cosmologique qui a le même âge que nous."

The verdict is [FALS]. Look-back time is measured, not inferred: the CMB is a photograph of a 380 000-year-old universe; supernova light curves are stretched by $(1+z)$; the CMB temperature scales as $(1+z)$; and the light-element abundances are a photograph of an epoch 200 seconds after the beginning. Each of these is an observation of the past, and the corpus's universe has no past — every point of it is as old as the observer.

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §8.7, sourced to Esp §VII.19 and RC 006/038/039/040. It is the premise of R9 and of P17. The related articles are P9, P13, P16 and P17; S59 carries the chain verdicts.

Conventions. $\tau$ is the corpus's universal age; $t$ is ordinary proper time; $z$ is redshift; $\eta$ is the corpus's "Forme-âge" space; $\mathrm{d}s$ the line element. Standard cosmology's cosmic time is written $t_{\mathrm{cosmic}}$.

Part 1 — What the axiom says, and what it is not

The axiom is a representation choice read as physics. RC 038–040 introduce a coordinate system in which the vertical axis is $c\tau$ and the spatial sections are surfaces of constant $\tau$. In such a chart, a photon's worldline projects onto the $r$ axis by construction, because $\mathrm{d}s^{2} = c^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau^{2} - \mathrm{d}l^{2} = 0$ for light. Every point of a constant-$\tau$ slice carries the same $\tau$ label, trivially, because that is the definition of the slice. The step the corpus takes — from "these points share a coordinate label" to "these objects share an age" — is where the physics enters, and it is the step that fails.

It is not the relativity of simultaneity. Relativity already says that simultaneity is frame-dependent, so a slicing in which distant events share a time coordinate is not in itself objectionable. What is objectionable is the further claim that this label is the ageing variable — that a clock's accumulated time is $\tau$ rather than the proper time along its worldline. That is what makes P17's twin conclusion follow, and that is what observation refutes.

It is also not the comoving/free-fall time of standard cosmology. In FLRW the cosmic time $t$ is a global time label too, but it is not the ageing variable of a moving clock: a galaxy at $z = 3$ has a proper age smaller than ours, and its light reaches us redshifted and time-dilated by the factor $(1+z)$. The corpus has the same freedom to define a coordinate, and it uses it to draw the opposite conclusion about ageing.

Part 2 — Four measurements of the past

Each of these is an observation of an object as it was, which requires that the object be younger than us and that its light take time to arrive.

The CMB is a photograph of a 380 000-year-old universe. Its blackbody temperature is 2.725 K today and its spectrum is thermal to $<10^{-4}$. A thermal spectrum with no distortions requires the emitting plasma to have been in equilibrium and to have last scattered long ago; the spectrum's temperature scales as $T = T_{0}(1+z)$, and this scaling is measured — through the Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect's frequency dependence, and through the excitation temperature of high-redshift molecular and atomic absorbers, which give $T \propto (1+z)^{1-\beta}$ with $\beta = -0{,}007 \pm 0{,}027$, i.e. consistent with $(1+z)^{1}$. If the CMB's last-scattering surface had our age, there would be no last-scattering surface.

Supernova light curves are stretched. A Type Ia supernova at redshift $z$ has its light curve broadened by the factor $(1+z)$. This was predicted from the expansion (Wilson 1939) and measured first in a single high-$z$ supernova by Goldhaber et al. (2001), then in samples of dozens out to $z \approx 0{,}8$ and $z \approx 1{,}0$, and in individual objects to $z \approx 2{,}3$. The widths track $(1+z)$ to a few percent. Time-dilation of a signal is a measurement of the geometry between emitter and observer; a universe in which every object shares the observer's age has no such stretching to offer, because there is no propagation delay to dilate.

The look-back time is the whole point of extragalactic astronomy. Quasar and galaxy evolution, the change of the cosmic star-formation rate by an order of magnitude between $z \approx 2$ and today, the existence of the helium abundance before any stars — all of these are statements about when we see things. The corpus's cosmology cannot express "when": its sole time is one number shared by everything in the sky.

And BBN is a photograph of 200 seconds. The light-element abundances are the best-measured relic of the first minutes, and they are measured now, in material formed then. In the corpus's scheme, material formed at 303 Myr has the observer's age (P9), so the abundances are not relics of anything (P9, Part 2) — the axiom and the antimatter era are the same mistake told twice.

Part 3 — Why the axiom was adopted, and what it costs

The axiom is needed. It is what makes the corpus's "Forme-âge" representation coherent, and it is what makes the "Réel/Imaginaire" split do work: the imaginary sector is the memory/age sector, and a single universal $\tau$ is what lets a micro-universe's internal time be compared with ours. So the axiom is not gratuitous — it is the load-bearing beam of the ontology.

And the beam breaks the roof. Because the axiom is needed, it cannot be dropped to save the twin result (P17) or the EPR result (P16); dropping it removes the meaning of the imaginary sector as an age. This is the structural point of the article: the axiom is simultaneously the corpus's most consequential premise and its most thoroughly refuted one, and it has no replacement inside the model. S50's and S52's bounce and cycle inherit the same difficulty, because their chronology is expressed in $\tau$.

What survives, and it is worth stating. The representational content of RC 038–040 is a legitimate coordinate construction: a $(r, c\tau)$ chart in which light rays are at $45^{\circ}$ is a standard device. The failure is not in the chart but in reading the chart's labels as ages. A defender could keep the formalism and drop the ageing claim — but then the twin and EPR articles lose their conclusions, which is P17's and P16's business.

Summary

Axiom 6 makes one universal age $\tau$ the ageing variable of every observable phenomenon, and makes photons non-ageing. It is [FALS]. The CMB's $T \propto (1+z)$ scaling, the $(1+z)$ stretching of supernova light curves (measured to $z \approx 2{,}3$), the BBN abundances, and the entire practice of look-back time all require that distant objects are seen as they were, younger than the observer, with propagating light carrying the delay. The axiom is not a loose remark but a load-bearing premise: it is what makes the corpus's imaginary sector an "age" sector, and what generates the twin (P17) and EPR (P16) conclusions. Dropping it saves the formalism and destroys those conclusions; keeping it contradicts the data. Chains R9 and R10 die here.

The Cyclic Model: A Period That Is Derived, a Mechanism That Is Absent, and a Number No Observation Can Ever See

Introduction

The corpus's universe oscillates. Its half-period is 2371 billion years, its maximum radius is $1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm — 88 times the present radius — and it returns.

"Pas de singularité à l'origine et une expansion qui doit durer 2371 milliards d'années; et, aussi, un Univers cyclique." (RC 083 §1)

The cycle is not an appendix to the model; it is what the model is. The universe starts at a finite radius with a finite density and temperature (S51), expands for $T/2 = 2371$ Gyr, and comes back. The register tags this [SPEC] with the note that cyclic models are live — Steinhardt–Turok's ekpyrotic cycle (2001–2002), Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology (2010) — but with entirely different mechanisms and no observational support yet. Both halves of that note need refinement, and the refinement is the article's finding.

Three results, all verified by recomputation, and they point in different directions.

  1. The period is not a speculation — it is a consequence of the corpus's own equation of motion, and it reproduces the printed number to $2\,\%$. With the corpus's $R_{\max} = 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm, the closed-universe equation (5-17) gives $T/2 = 2325$ Gyr against the printed 2371 — and the exact analytic form is

$$T/2 = \frac{\pi}{2}\,\frac{R_{\max}}{c},$$

which is the quarter-oscillation of a curvature-plus-$\Lambda$ closed universe. Numerical integration of the corpus's own equation at its own printed $R_{\max}$ confirms it (§2). So the register's [SPEC] is right about the cycle as a claim about the world and wrong about the number: the period is a derived quantity and the corpus computed it correctly. 2. And the same equation of motion derives $\Lambda < 0$, which is the one thing the bounce needed (S51) — and the value it derives is $7200\times$ smaller in magnitude than the observed $\Lambda$ and of the opposite sign. So the sign conflict that S50 traced to a stipulation is also forced by the corpus's closed geometry with a finite maximum radius. It is structural, not a slip (§3). 3. And the cycle is unobservable in principle, not merely in practice, with no mechanism in place at either turnaround. The period is 270 times the present age; the present epoch sits at $0{,}74\,\%$ of the half-period; the bounce's ingredient fails (S51); the mass bookkeeping needs the 303-Myr annihilation that is [FALS] (§8.4); and there is no perturbation sector, so no CMB signature — which is exactly where the live cyclic models are tested (§4–§6).

And one structural finding about the cycle's status, which cuts both ways. The corpus's equation of motion, $R'^{2} = c^{2}f(R)$ with $f$ a function of $R$ alone, is autonomous and time-reversal symmetric: if a solution turns around at $R_{i}$ and again at $R_{\max}$, the return journey exists automatically, without any new postulate. So the cycle is not an extra assumption the corpus made — it is what the equation does. That credits the corpus (nothing was smuggled in) and deflates the claim (nothing new was added, either): the "cyclic model" is the generic behaviour of a closed cosmology with a negative $\Lambda$, and its content is entirely the content of (5-17).

Conventions. $\tau$ is the corpus's Forme-âge cosmic time; $R(\tau)$ the spatial radius; $R' = dR/d\tau$; the cosmological equation of motion is (5-17),

$$\frac{R'^{2}}{c^{2}} = -\frac{1}{6}\frac{\xi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{R^{4}} + K + \frac{1}{3}\Lambda R^{2} + \frac{A}{R^{2}},$$

with $K = +1$, $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$, $\alpha = -1{,}30\times10^{53}$ g, $\beta = 3{,}712\times10^{79}$ g cm, $A = -1{,}522\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$. The cycle's scales: $R_{i} = 6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm at $\tau = 0$; $R_{0} = 3{,}297\times10^{26}$ cm at $\tau_{0}$, where $R' = c$ (345 Myr); $R$ today $= 1{,}6\times10^{28}$ cm at $\tau = 17{,}55$ Gyr; $R_{\max} = 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm at $\tau = T/2$. $R_{m} = 2{,}855\times10^{26}$ cm is the characteristic radius of the $G$ law (S40); $T$ is the full period, $T = 2\times(T/2)$.

1. The Cycle Is What the Equation Does, Not a Postulate

Start with the status of the claim, because it is more interesting than a verdict on the mechanism.

The corpus's cosmological equation (5-17) is first order in $R'^{2}$, autonomous, and even in $R'$. Its right-hand side is a function $c^{2}f(R)$ of the radius alone:

$$f(R) = -\frac{\xi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{6R^{4}} + K + \frac{\Lambda R^{2}}{3} + \frac{A}{R^{2}}.$$

Three consequences follow immediately, and none requires a new physical assumption.

  • Wherever $f(R) > 0$, the solution has two branches, $R' = \pm c\sqrt{f}$, expanding and contracting. The contracting branch is not postulated; it is the other sign of a square root.
  • Wherever $f(R_{t}) = 0$, the velocity vanishes and the branches join. A radius with $f(R_{t}) = 0$ is a turning point, and the solution passes through it smoothly, so a solution with two turning points is periodic — it closes on itself in the $(R, R')$ phase plane.
  • The period is then an integral, and it is computable:

$$T = 2\oint \frac{dR}{c\sqrt{f(R)}} = \frac{2}{c}\int_{R_{i}}^{R_{\max}} \frac{dR}{\sqrt{f(R)}} .$$

So the corpus's cycle is not an assumption at all. It is the phase-plane topology of its own equation of motion. A closed ($K = +1$) cosmology with a negative $\Lambda$ and a finite maximum radius is an oscillation, in the same sense that a pendulum is. This is a real point in the corpus's favour: the register's [SPEC] might suggest a model with a cyclic postulate grafted on, and that is not what it is. Nothing was added.

And it is a real deflation too. Because the cycle is generic, it has no content beyond (5-17) and its parameters. Everything the cycle is — its period, its turnarounds, its maximum radius — is already fixed by the equation, $K$, $\Lambda$ and $\alpha$. The "cyclic model" is a name for the behaviour of the closed cosmology, not an additional theory, and §2 and §3 show that its two numbers both follow from what was already there.

One check that the topology is really as claimed. The turning points are the roots of $f$. The corpus's own numbers give $f(R_{i}) = -9{,}5\times10^{-4}$ — not zero, but $10^{-3}$ against terms of order unity, and $R_{i}$ printed to four figures while $\xi$ carries only two, so the residual is entirely within the printed precision (this is the same near-cancellation §3 of S51's companion articles uses; see also the check below). At $R_{\max}$ the two small terms are $10^{-22}$ and $10^{-91}$ and $f$ vanishes through the balance $K + \Lambda R^{2}/3 \approx 0$. So $f$ has exactly the two roots the cycle needs, one at each printed radius, and the well between them is where the universe lives.

2. The Half-Period Is Derived, and It Reproduces the Corpus's Number to Two Per Cent

This is the article's strongest positive result, and it was not in the register.

In the regime where the two small terms are negligible — which is everywhere except the first instants of the expansion, as §1's residual shows — the equation (5-17) is

$$\frac{R'^{2}}{c^{2}} = 1 - \frac{R^{2}}{R_{\max}^{2}}, \qquad \text{with} \qquad \Lambda = -\frac{3}{R_{\max}^{2}} ,$$

because $\Lambda R_{\max}^{2}/3 = -1$ is exactly the condition $f(R_{\max}) = 0$. This equation has an elementary solution, $R(\tau) = R_{\max}\sin(c\tau/R_{\max})$, running from $R = 0$ at $\tau = 0$ to $R = R_{\max}$ at $c\tau/R_{\max} = \pi/2$. The corpus's own solution starts at $R_{i}$ rather than at $0$, and the difference is $R_{i}/R_{\max} = 4{,}3\times10^{-6}$ — so the elementary form is exact for the corpus's case to five figures, which is what the numerical integration below confirms. So

$$\boxed{\;T/2 = \frac{\pi}{2}\frac{R_{\max}}{c} \;}$$

and this is worth stating as what it is: the half-period of the corpus's cycle is the light-travel time across its maximum radius, times $\pi/2$.

The numerical integration confirms the analytic form to four figures: the matter-like and $A$ terms change the answer by less than $0{,}1\,\%$, because they are confined to the first instants of the expansion. So the corpus's printed pair — a half-period of 2371 Gyr and a maximum radius of $1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm — are mutually consistent to within the precision of the printed radius. That is a genuine, verified positive: the corpus did not fit this period; it computed a period from a closed-universe oscillation and printed a number $2\,\%$ from what its own equation gives.

What this changes in the register. The S52 entry records "half-period ≈ 2371 Gyr" under a [SPEC] tag. The tag should be split: the cycle as a claim about the world is [SPEC] — unobserved, and §4–§6 show why it is unobservable — but the number is a derived quantity of the closed-universe equation, and it is correct. A reader should not be told that a computable period is a speculation. The entry should read: the period is [MATH]-derived from (5-17) with $K = +1$; the cycle's physical realisation is [SPEC].

And the derivation gives the corpus a number it never prints. Inverting the relation,

$$|\Lambda| = \frac{3}{R_{\max}^{2}} = 1{,}53\times10^{-60}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}} ,$$

or equivalently, from the printed period, $|\Lambda| = 3(\pi/2)^{2}/(c\,T/2)^{2} = 1{,}47\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$. The corpus specifies its $\Lambda$ only through an inequality (RC 051, (3-69-2)) and never gives it a value; the cycle's own numbers fix it at about $-1{,}5\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$. §3 does something with that.

3. The Equation Also Forces $\Lambda < 0$ — and the Forced Value Is the Wrong One

S50 shows that the corpus's negative vacuum descends from the zero-total-energy stipulation, and S51 shows the bounce needs $\Lambda \le 0$. This section shows something more robust: the corpus's cosmology alone forces $\Lambda < 0$, and forces a value that observation excludes.

The forcing is immediate from §2. $f(R_{\max}) = 0$ with $K = +1$ and the other terms negligible is $1 + \Lambda R_{\max}^{2}/3 = 0$, so

$$\Lambda = -\frac{3}{R_{\max}^{2}} < 0 \qquad \text{for any finite } R_{\max} \text{ and } K = +1.$$

So the corpus does not need (3-69-2) to get $\Lambda$'s sign: a closed universe with a finite maximum radius has it necessarily. That is a cleaner and more general reason than the corpus gives, and it strengthens the register's §8.5 entry — the requirement $\Lambda \le 0$ is structural, not a bookkeeping choice.

And the value the structure forces is wrong. The observed cosmological constant, from the accelerated expansion, is

$$\Lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 3\Omega_{\Lambda}H_{0}^{2}/c^{2} = 1{,}10\times10^{-56}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}} \qquad (\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0{,}689,\ H_{0} = 67{,}4\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}).$$

The corpus's is $-1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$: opposite in sign, and $7168\times$ smaller in magnitude. So the conflict is a factor $7\times10^{3}$ in the geometric constant, not merely the factor $200$ in the energy density that §8.5 quotes — and the two statements are the same statement, since $\Lambda = 8\pi G\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}/c^{2}$ in the standard relation. The register's §8.5 figure should be accompanied by this one, because $\Lambda$ is the coefficient the corpus's own field equation (2-4) contains (S7), and the comparison is therefore like for like with no unit conversion in the way.

One point of care, and it is a real open question rather than a defect. The corpus uses $\Lambda$ in two places: in the cosmological equation (5-17), and in the Système E's existence condition (3-69-2), where it is a function of the free parameter $R_{0}$,

$$\Lambda = \frac{\xi^{2}}{R_{0}^{6}}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}} - \alpha^{2}\right) - \frac{3}{R_{0}^{2}} \;\le\; 0 ,$$

with the Système E's own $\alpha = -1{,}46\times10^{-25}$ g and $\beta = -1{,}74\times10^{-39}$ g cm (RC 051 (3-68)) — not the cosmological pair of (5-68), which is the notation trap S51 flags. Whether one number serves both uses cannot be settled from the transcribed pages, and it matters, because the two uses point in opposite directions:

  • If $\Lambda$ is one universal constant, then (3-69-2) with the corpus's own $\xi = \Omega_{0}c/\hbar = 6{,}94\times10^{-4}$ cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ (from $l_{0} = R_{H} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm, (3-70)) admits $\Lambda \le 0$ only for $8{,}02\times10^{-15} \le R_{0} \le 11{,}9\times10^{-15}$ cm, over which $\Lambda$ runs from $0$ to $-2{,}1\times10^{28}$ cm$^{-2}$. Attaining the cosmology's $|\Lambda| \sim 1{,}5\times10^{-60}$ then requires $R_{0}$ to sit within $\sim10^{-103}$ cm of the sign-change boundary — a tuning of the free parameter to about one part in $10^{89}$. The corpus never states this constraint, and it is the sharpest internal tension the two uses of $\Lambda$ produce.
  • If the corpus holds two $\Lambda$'s — a micro-universe one and a cosmological one — then (3-69-2) says nothing about the cosmology, and S51's "the corpus requires $\Lambda \le 0$ by (3-69-2)" needs its scope stated as the Système E's. But §2 and this section restore the conclusion independently: the cosmological $\Lambda < 0$ is forced by $K = +1$ with a finite $R_{\max}$, whatever (3-69-2) is about.

So the finding survives either reading, and this is worth stating plainly because it is the article's most transferable point. S51's $\Lambda \le 0$ claim should not rest on (3-69-2)'s scope. The closed geometry forces the sign; the corpus's requirement and the corpus's bounce both follow from that; and observation denies it. Hand-off to the S51 entry: replace the citation of (3-69-2) as the reason for $\Lambda \le 0$ with the geometric argument, and keep (3-69-2) for the Système E's own range.

4. The Scales: 270 Times the Present Age, and the Present at $0{,}74\,\%$

The cycle's numbers are extraordinary, and their consequence is that the cycle cannot be observed.

the cycle, at the corpus's own values
half-period $T/2$ 2371 Gyr
full period $T$ 4742 Gyr
present age $\tau_{a}$ 17,55 Gyr
present epoch as a fraction of the half-period $0{,}74\,\%$
$T/\tau_{a}$ 270
radii: $R_{i} \to R_{a} \to R_{\max}$ $6{,}0\times10^{24} \to 1{,}6\times10^{28} \to 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm
expansion still to come, in radius a factor 87,5

So the corpus's universe is now at the very beginning of its expansion, in the corpus's own words at "un tout petit début", and 99,3 % of the expansion is in the future. The corpus is aware of this and treats it as a feature: a young universe explains why $G$ is still far from its limit and why $\Delta G/G$ is still measurable (S40).

And it is also the reason the cycle is unobservable in principle rather than merely in practice. The observable window — from the CMB at $z \sim 1100$ (about 0,4 Myr) to the present — is roughly $10^{-4}$ of the half-period; even a galaxy-formation survey reaching $z = 20$ samples the first one per cent of the cycle. Nothing in the cycle's repetition can be seen: the previous cycle ended at least 2353 Gyr ago and the contraction begins 2353 Gyr from now. So the model predicts a cyclic universe whose cyclicity no measurement can access, and its "cycle" is therefore observationally indistinguishable from a single bounce followed by eternal expansion — until $R$ reaches $R_{\max}$, at $2{,}4\times10^{12}$ years.

That comparison is what makes the register's §8.3 note incomplete. Ekpyrotic and conformal cyclic models are live because they are testable now: ekpyrotic predicts a specific tensor-to-scalar ratio and non-Gaussianity in the CMB; CCC predicts concentric low-variance circles in the CMB and correlations in the low-multipole alignments. Both make near-term predictions from their cyclic structure. The corpus's cycle makes none from its — the period is too long and, §5, there is no perturbative sector to make one with. So "live cyclic models exist" is true and does not transfer: what makes them live is precisely what this one lacks.

5. The Missing Perturbation Sector — the Shared Gap, and Why It Is Decisive Here

The corpus's cosmology is a background solution and nothing more: there is no perturbation theory anywhere in the corpus. S51 records this as the reason the bounce has no CMB prediction; S52 records it as the reason the cycle has none, and here it is more damaging, because every live cyclic model earns its standing from perturbations.

What a cyclic model must predict to be testable, and what the corpus cannot supply:

what a live cycle predicts why the corpus cannot
the primordial power spectrum $P(k)$ and its tilt no perturbed field equations are set up for $T_{\alpha\beta} = \xi^{2}\rho^{2}u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}$
the tensor-to-scalar ratio $r$ (ekpyrotic's signature) no tensor perturbations are treated at all
non-Gaussianity $f_{\mathrm{NL}}$ (ekpyrotic's second signature) second-order perturbation theory is absent
CMB anomalies or circle signatures (CCC's signature) requires an across-turnaround matching of perturbations; no matching is defined
the turnaround's signature in the CMB or in $G$ the turnaround is $2{,}4\times10^{12}$ years away

So the cycle's one potentially observable consequence is not that it repeats but that it turns around — and the turnarounds are inaccessible, with no perturbation sector in between. The gap is shared with S50, S51 and the no-inflation claim (§8.11), and this section's contribution is to say where it bites hardest: not at the bounce (S51, where it costs a signature) but at the cycle (here, where it costs the entire case).

6. Two Bookkeeping Questions the Cycle Raises, and the Corpus's Answers

A cycle needs to close in more than geometry. Two quantities must return, and the corpus addresses one and not the other.

6.1 The mass closes, and the corpus says how

The ponderable mass of the universe is not constant in this model. RC 085 gives four values (S50's notation list): $M(\tau{=}0) = 1{,}22\times10^{56}$ g, a minimum $M_{\min} = 2\times10^{54}$ g at $\tau_{0} = 345$ Myr, $M_{\text{today}} = 1{,}14\times10^{56}$ g, and $M(T/2) = 9{,}5\times10^{57}$ g. So the mass grows by a factor $78$ from the bounce to the maximum, and for the cycle to close, it must come back down by that factor on the contraction.

The corpus does state the mechanism, and the register should credit it: §5 of RC 085 is titled "La création (et annihilation) continue de matière". Matter is created on the expansion and annihilated on the contraction, and the cycle closes. This is not a gap the corpus left open. And it is the natural pairing: it is what one would expect from the zero-total-energy ledger of S50, where the matter's energy is drawn from the negative-energy vacuum and returned to it.

But the annihilation is the problem, and it is not a new problem. The corpus's cosmology has an explicit antimatter era, ending at the "bang" at $\tau_{b} = 303$ Myr when the last antiparticle disappears (RC 080 §3.3; register §8.4). Matter–antimatter annihilation on that scale has a hard gamma-ray signature, and §8.4 tags the antimatter era [FALS] on exactly those grounds. So: the cycle's bookkeeping closes only if an annihilation epoch that is observationally excluded took place — twice per cycle. The link is not this article's to adjudicate (§8.4 and P9 own it); what S52 records is that the cyclic closure depends on it, so the chain that closes the cycle passes through a dead link. By §12.2's rule, that is a Part P dependency, and it is the cleanest example in the cosmology of a surviving claim resting on a falsified one — which the register's §12.2 should note, since its "clean split" is stated for R1–R11 and the cycle is not among them.

6.2 The entropy does not close, and the corpus does not say how

An exactly periodic universe must return its entropy to its initial value every cycle, which the second law forbids unless some mechanism resets it. This is the standard objection to exact cycles, and every live cyclic model answers it: Penrose's CCC uses the conformal rescaling at the crossover to erase the previous aeon's entropy; the ekpyrotic cycle dilutes it by the enormous expansion of each cycle. The corpus proposes no reset.

And the corpus has a natural place to put one — which makes the omission more interesting than a simple gap. Its ontology includes a negentropic principle: the holon is defined by its "mémoire néguentropique" (RC 021's discussion of individual versus collective behaviour), an explicitly entropy-decreasing structure. That is exactly the kind of object a cycle needs at its turnaround, and the corpus uses it for particle behaviour and never for the cycle. So the omission is not an absence of machinery; it is machinery not deployed. Recorded as a suggestion rather than a defect: a negentropic holon at the turnaround is the one place the corpus's own ontology might close its own cycle, and the corpus does not attempt it.

And this belongs with §6.1 as the cycle's two bookkeeping conditions. Mass closes, by the corpus's own account and through a falsified link; entropy does not close, and no account is offered. So the cycle's books balance on the quantity observation has already ruled on and do not balance on the quantity thermodynamics rules on.

Verdict

The period is derived and correct; the cycle is what the equation does; and the cycle's mechanism is absent at every point where it would have to be present.

The positive case, which is stronger than the register's [SPEC] suggests.

  1. The cycle is not a postulate. (5-17) is autonomous in $R'^{2}$, so both branches exist, and two turning points make the solution periodic. Nothing was added to the formalism to make the universe cyclic — §1.
  2. The half-period is derived and the corpus's number is right. $T/2 = (\pi/2)R_{\max}/c$ gives 2325 Gyr against the printed 2371 Gyr, a $2{,}0\,\%$ agreement within the two-figure precision of the printed $R_{\max}$, confirmed by numerical integration of the corpus's own equation with all terms kept — §2. The register's [SPEC] should be split: the cycle is [SPEC], the period is [MATH]-derived.
  3. The closed geometry independently forces $\Lambda < 0$, which is what the bounce needed (S51) — and the forcing does not depend on (3-69-2)'s scope, which §3 flags as an open question — §3.
  4. The corpus states the mass bookkeeping and pairs creation with annihilation (§5 of RC 085), so the cycle closes on mass by the corpus's own account — §6.1.

And the case against, in three parts that do not overlap.

  1. The period is 270 times the present age and the present epoch is $0{,}74\,\%$ of the half-period. So the cyclicity is unobservable in principle, not merely unpractical, and the model is observationally a single bounce followed by 2353 Gyr of expansion — with no perturbation sector to make any other prediction (§4–§5). This is where the comparison with the live cycles turns: ekpyrotic and CCC are live because they predict in the CMB now, and this one cannot.
  2. The forced $\Lambda$ is the wrong one. $\Lambda = -3/R_{\max}^{2} = -1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$ against the observed $+1{,}10\times10^{-56}$ cm$^{-2}$ — opposite sign, $7168\times$ smaller. The conflict is structural, and the same $-1{,}5\times10^{-60}$ is what makes the period right, so the corpus cannot have the period without the wrong $\Lambda$ (§3).
  3. The cycles' two books do not both balance. Mass closes only through the 303-Myr annihilation that §8.4 marks [FALS] — so the closure of the cycle depends on a dead link, the clearest Part-P dependency in the cosmology, and one §12.2's "clean split" does not currently cover; entropy does not close at all, though the corpus's own negentropic holon is where a reset would live (§6).

And the article's most useful transferable statement. S51's requirement $\Lambda \le 0$ should not be cited to (3-69-2), whose scope is the Système E and whose free parameter makes it a range rather than a value. It should be cited to the closed geometry with a finite maximum radius, which forces the sign with no free parameter and no appeal to the micro-universe. That restatement is more robust than the one the register currently carries, and it costs the corpus nothing it did not already have — which is the point: the sign conflict cannot be repaired by re-scoping (3-69-2).

One last observation about what is not wrong here, because the temptation is to overstate. The corpus's cyclic cosmology is not incoherent: given (5-17), $K = +1$, a negative $\Lambda$ and a matter source that returns its energy to the vacuum, the cycle follows, its period is computable, and the corpus computed it correctly to within its own printed precision. The corpus's failure is not in the cycle's construction. It is that the ingredients — a negative $\Lambda$, an annihilation epoch, an entropy reset, a perturbation sector — are, respectively, excluded by observation, excluded by observation, absent, and absent. A cyclic universe built this way is a well-formed model of a world that is not ours.

  • $R_{i}$, $R_{0}$, $R_{a}$, $R_{\max}$, $R_{m}$ — five radii, and the letters overlap dangerously. $R_{i} = 6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm, the bounce radius at $\tau = 0$; $R_{0} = 3{,}297\times10^{26}$ cm, the radius at $\tau_{0} = 345$ Myr where $R' = c$; $R_{a} = 1{,}6\times10^{28}$ cm today; $R_{\max} = 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm at $T/2$, so $R_{\max}/R_{a} = 87{,}5$ and $R_{\max}/R_{i} = 2{,}33\times10^{5}$; $R_{m} = 2{,}855\times10^{26}$ cm, the $G$ law's characteristic radius (S40), not a turning point. $R_{0}$ here is the cosmological radius; the Système E's $R_{0}$ (S51, RC 051) is bounded by $11{,}9\times10^{-15}$ cm and is a different object.
  • $f(R)$ — the dimensionless right-hand side of (5-17), $f = -\xi^{2}\alpha^{2}/6R^{4} + K + \Lambda R^{2}/3 + A/R^{2}$. The cycle is the statement that $f$ has two positive roots, at $R_{i}$ and $R_{\max}$, and that $f > 0$ between them. This symbol is S52's own, not the corpus's.
  • $K = +1$ — the closed curvature sign, the geometric prerequisite of the cycle. §8.10 of the register notes that Planck mildly disfavours $K = +1$, which is the cycle's other observational cost.
  • $\Lambda$ — here the cosmological constant of (5-17) and (2-4), which §3 derives: $\Lambda = -3/R_{\max}^{2} = -1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$. Distinguish from the Système E's $\Lambda$ of (3-69-2), a function of the free $R_{0}$ with the Système E's $\alpha$, $\beta$ — §3's open question.
  • $M$ — the corpus's mass of the universe, (5-59)/(5-71): $1{,}22\times10^{56}$ g at $\tau = 0$; $M_{\min} = 2\times10^{54}$ g at $\tau_{0}$; $1{,}14\times10^{56}$ g today; $9{,}5\times10^{57}$ g at $T/2$ — a factor 78 of growth per half-cycle, removed by the corpus's annihilation. See S50's notation list for $M$'s definition.
  • $\alpha$, $\beta$, $A$, $\xi$ — the cosmological (5-68) values, not the Système E's pair of the same names: $\alpha = -1{,}30\times10^{53}$ g, $\beta = 3{,}712\times10^{79}$ g cm, $A = -1{,}522\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$, $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$.

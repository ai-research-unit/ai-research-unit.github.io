Charon: The Size of the Electron

Introduction

This article records a claim of the corpus that is rarely noticed because it arrives as a correction to something else. To make his computed charge exact, Charon deforms the éon from a sphere into an oblate spheroid with a free eccentricity $E$. The deformation buys the charge and, in the same move, gives the electron a finite, anisotropic extension of order the éon's radii. The first is a fit; the second is a claim about the electron's structure, and the electron is the one particle whose point-like nature has been tested to many decimal places.

The claim, in the corpus's words. The difference between his computed charge and the measured $e$ is attributed to spherical symmetry being an approximation: "Nous pouvons admettre que la petite différence … provient du fait que, dans le modèle éonique, nous nous sommes limités à la symétrie sphérique, alors que plus généralement (spin éonique) nous aurions dû choisir une symétrie axiale, admettant la possibilité d'une excentricité $E$ de l'espace éonique" (RC 055, §VII.4). With the deformed cross-section (3-110), "le même calcul … fournit alors la valeur expérimentale exacte de la charge $e$" for $r/R = 0{,}933$, that is $E = 0{,}36$.

The verdict is [FALS]. The electron is point-like below about $10^{-18}$ m: its magnetic moment agrees with the quantum-electrodynamic prediction to ten significant figures, and the agreement is better than any composite model with a structure at the éon's scale could give. The corpus's own radii put the extension at $0{,}08$ to $0{,}48$ fm, one and a half to two and a half orders of magnitude above the bound. And the corpus concedes the point elsewhere: Esp 053, explaining why the elementary length $l_{0}$ is not the electron's radius, writes that the point-electron hypothesis "est confirmée par l'expérience".

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §4.7, sourced to RC (3-110), (3-111), (3-112) at scan 055_improved.md, with the éon's radii from RC (3-72) and (3-68). The audit is S19's, whose finding that $E$ is a fit is adopted here. The experimental bound is the electron $g-2$ agreement with QED and the collider limits on an excited electron.

Conventions. Gaussian cgs. $r$ and $R$ are the small and large semi-axes of the éon, $E$ its eccentricity, $\rho_{n}$ the proper density, $\Omega_{0}$ the elementary cell, $l_{0}$ the elementary length. Note the corpus's radii are printed in cm; comparisons with the experimental bound are made after converting.

Part 1 — How the deformation buys the charge, and what it costs

The construction is short and its logic is worth following, because the electron's size is a by-product of a fit.

The un-deformed charge. The charge is computed from blackbody radiation trapped in the éon (inventory §3.3). S19's audit recomputes the chain: $\gamma = -c\beta/h$, the infra-red cut-off $x_{1} = (\pi h/30c|\beta|)^{1/4} = 1{,}9103$, the truncated Planck integral $23{,}455$, and finally the coupling $e^{2}/\hbar c = 7{,}608\times10^{-3}$ against the observed $7{,}2993\times10^{-3}$. The unfitted result misses by 4.2 % in the coupling, which is 2 % in the charge.

The deformation. RC 055 replaces the electrostatic cross-section $\overline{\sigma} = \pi R^{2}/2$ by its oblate form (3-110), and reports that the deformed calculation gives the measured charge exactly when $r/R = 0{,}933$, i.e. $E = 0{,}36$. The relation between the two is the spheroid's, $r/R = \sqrt{1-E^{2}}$, and $E = 0{,}36$ does give $\sqrt{1-0{,}36^{2}} = 0{,}933$.

The cost, verified. Charon's own correction multiplies his unfitted coupling by $(1-E^{2})^{1/2} = 0{,}933$, giving $7{,}098\times10^{-3}$ — which is 2.8 % below $1/137$, not the exact value the text claims. The eccentricity that would land exactly is $E = 0{,}282$. Since $E$ is free, "exact" means "chosen", and the honest headline is the unfitted 2 %. S19's finding is adopted: the eccentricity is a fit, and the fit does not even land where the text says it lands.

The by-product. Once the éon is an oblate spheroid with $r/R = 0{,}933$, the model has a preferred axis and two radii. The electron's structure is no longer a point and no longer isotropic. That is the size claim, and it is now load-bearing whether or not the author wanted it.

Part 2 — The size, and the experimental bound

The corpus's own lengths. From RC (3-72) and (3-68) the éon's radii are

$$R_{0} = 8{,}01\times10^{-15}\ \mathrm{cm}, \qquad R_{\max} = 4{,}82\times10^{-14}\ \mathrm{cm},$$

that is $0{,}080$ fm and $0{,}482$ fm. The elementary length is $l_{0} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm $= 0{,}18$ fm, and the holon's maximum radius is $R_{H\max} = 9{,}2\times10^{-14}$ cm $= 0{,}92$ fm. The eccentricity makes the electron's extension of order the éon's radii, with the anisotropy fixed by r/R.

The experimental bound. The electron's anomalous magnetic moment, $g-2$, agrees with the QED prediction to ten significant figures. Any composite structure at a scale $\Lambda$ would shift the magnetic moment by terms of order $m_{e}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}$; the agreement bounds $\Lambda$ far above the éon's radii. Direct searches for an excited electron at LEP and the LHC bound the scale of structure at or above a few hundred GeV to a TeV, corresponding to lengths below $10^{-18}$ m $= 10^{-16}$ cm.

The comparison, done carefully.

quantity value (cm) value (fm) against a $10^{-16}$ cm bound
éon small semi-axis $R_{0}$ $8{,}01\times10^{-15}$ $0{,}080$ $\sim 80$ times above
éon large semi-axis $R_{\max}$ $4{,}82\times10^{-14}$ $0{,}482$ $\sim 480$ times above
elementary length $l_{0}$ $1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ $0{,}18$ $\sim 180$ times above

So the model's electron is a structure of tenths of a femtometre, and the bound is a hundredth of an attometre. The extension is one and a half to two and a half orders of magnitude above the point-like bound — decisively excluded, and worth stating precisely rather than loosely.

Correction to the inventory's wording. The companion (§4.7) reads the scale as $\sim10^{-14}$ m and calls it "four orders of magnitude above the bound". The corpus's lengths are printed in cm, and $10^{-14}$ cm $=10^{-16}$ m is below, not above, the bound; the correct reading is the table above, with the éon's extension at $10^{-15}$ to $10^{-14}$ cm, one and a half to two and a half orders above. The verdict is unchanged; the number is corrected.

Part 3 — The corpus concedes the point

This is the part of the article that should be quoted in any reissue, because it removes the disagreement.

In Esp 053, discussing why $l_{0}$ is introduced, Charon writes that the elementary length can be considered the "radius" of the electron — "(ce qui est, naturellement, contradictoire avec l'hypothèse d'un électron ponctuel qui a été utilisée pour développer le formalisme quantique et qui, par ailleurs, est confirmée par l'expérience)". The parenthetical states that the point-electron hypothesis is confirmed by experiment. So the corpus is not in the dark about the electron's structure; it treats the point electron as established, and treats $l_{0}$ as a cut-off parameter rather than a literal radius. The size claim of §VII.4 is the tension the rest of the corpus avoids.

What this means for the eccentricity. The eccentricity is load-bearing in three places at once (S19): the charge "exact" (§3.3), the electron's real size (§4.7), and the spin of the éon. The corpus cannot keep the charge result and drop the size, because one fit produces both. The correct disposition is the one S19 reached: record the arithmetic of the charge as a real success, and mark the mechanism and the size as post-mortem.

Summary

To make his computed charge exact, Charon gave the éon an eccentricity $E = 0{,}36$, and the same deformation gives the electron a finite, anisotropic extension of 0.08 to 0.48 fm. The electron is point-like below about $10^{-18}$ m, with $g-2$ agreeing with QED to ten figures, so the extension sits one and a half to two and a half orders of magnitude above the bound — not four orders, as the inventory loosely states, since the corpus prints radii in cm. The corpus itself concedes the point at Esp 053, where the point-electron hypothesis is called confirmed by experiment. The eccentricity is a free fit that does not even land exactly ($E = 0{,}282$ would, against the printed $0{,}36$), and it carries the electron size, the "exact" charge and the éon spin together. Verdict [FALS].

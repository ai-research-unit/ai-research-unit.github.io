$\dot G/G$ — The One Prediction: What the Charon Corpus Claims About a Varying Newton's Constant, and Whether It Is Yet Derived

Introduction

The corpus makes exactly one prediction that established theory does not make, and that is the subject of this article. General relativity takes Newton's constant to be constant. The corpus does not: it makes $G$ a function of the age of the universe, and it gives a number for how fast $G$ is changing now,

$$\boxed{\ \frac{1}{G}\frac{dG}{d\tau} \;\simeq\; 5\times10^{-16}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}\ } \qquad (\text{RC (4-49), RC (3-103) §3, RC 084 §3})$$

about one part in $2\times10^{15}$ per year, or some $0{,}05\,\%$ over the present age of the universe. A measured rate of change of $G$ would falsify general relativity rather than refine it, so this is the one claim in the whole programme that could be right without any of the rest of it being right. The register's §16 records it as the corpus's live prediction; this article is where it is examined.

The examination has two halves, and they point opposite ways.

The claim itself is sound in kind, and it is the corpus's best-posed statement. The corpus's own second book answers the question general relativity cannot: "la Relativité générale ne nous dit rien sur la variation possible de $G$ avec l'âge de l'Univers" — and then supplies an answer. And the number is in the right place. Dirac's large-numbers hypothesis predicted a drift of order $H_{0}$, some $10^{-10}$–$10^{-11}$ yr$^{-1}$, and that prediction is dead, excluded by lunar laser ranging and pulsar timing. The corpus's value is five to six orders smaller, which is exactly why it survived where Dirac's did not. Present bounds do not reach $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$; the claim is allowed, and it is not confirmed.

But the derivation, as transcribed, does not close. This article follows the chain step by step, as the register requires, and the tracing produces four numerical findings, of which two are decisive. (1) The corps's working equation (5-61-a) is inconsistent with its own limit (5-62) by an exact factor $16/9$, and (5-62) is the anchored one — it reproduces the observed $G_{a} = 6{,}67\times10^{-8}$ cgs, which the corpus uses as an input, from the model's own published parameters. (2) Equation (5-61-a)'s dependence on the radius is bounded: its bracket takes values only in $[0{,}7071,\,1{,}0308]$ over the range of interest, so that equation — the one the corpus names as the source of the graph and of the $5\times10^{-16}$ — cannot produce the variation the corpus reports from it, a factor of 3032 between $\tau = 0$ and today. (3) The quoted rate is not reproducible from (5-61-a) together with the corpus's own cosmological parameters. (4) The $16/9$ error does not affect the rate, because a constant factor cancels in $(1/G)\,dG/d\tau$; the drift claim is robust to it.

So the verdict of this article is a split one, and both halves matter. The prediction is live, testable and not excluded — that stands, and it is the reason this article exists. The derivation is not yet secure from the recovered text, and (5-61-a) is now the single highest-priority page to re-read from the printed book. Nothing here shows the prediction is wrong. What is shown is that the corpus's own equation, as transcribed, does not carry it, and that the number $5\times10^{-16}$ therefore cannot yet be quoted as derived.

Conventions. $\tau$ is the corpus's cosmic age parameter, $R(\tau)$ the spatial radius of the Réel, $R' = dR/d\tau$ the expansion rate, and $\rho = \alpha/R^{3}$ the vacuum density, with $\alpha$ negative. $G$ is in cgs, $\approx 6{,}67\times10^{-8}$ today. The register's symbol is $\dot G/G$; the corpus writes $\Delta G/G$ and $(1/G)\,dG/d\tau$, and this article uses the corpus's form. The single most important structural fact is stated once, early, and used throughout: $G$'s value is an input of the corpus's cosmology, and only $G$'s drift is an output.

The Claim, in Its Simplest Form

What the corpus says. $G$ is not a constant but a function of cosmic time, fixed by the model's geometry. Its value today, its value at the beginning, its maximum, and its present rate of change are all given:

epoch $G$ (cgs) where
$\tau = 0$ $2{,}2\times10^{-11}$ — about $1/3000$ of today RC 084 §3, RC 072
maximum, at 607 Myr $7{,}07\times10^{-8}$ — "à peine $6\,\%$ de plus que sa valeur actuelle" RC 084 §3
today $6{,}67\times10^{-8}$ RC 082 (as an input), RC 084 §3
limit at $T/2$ (2371 Gyr) $6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ RC 084 §3, RC (5-62), (5-67)

and the rate of change today,

$$\frac{1}{G}\frac{dG}{d\tau} \simeq 5\times10^{-16}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}, \qquad \frac{dG}{d\tau} < 0 .$$

The sign is a real part of the prediction and is easy to lose. The corpus places us past the maximum of $G$ — the peak is at 607 Myr and the present epoch is 13.8 Gyr — so $G$ is decreasing now, and will continue to decrease slowly toward $6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ at the maximum of expansion. A detection of a rising $G$ at any sensitivity would therefore contradict the model, not merely fail to confirm it. The register's falsification clause — "or one whose sign is opposite to the model's" — rests on this, and the sign is stated explicitly in the corpus.

The corpus's own framing of the claim is admirably direct. RC 072 §3.4 asks the question in plain words and answers it: "cette valeur a-t-elle changé au cours du temps, quelle était-elle au début de l'Univers, au moment où se formaient les corps cosmiques? Quelle sera-t-elle dans des milliards d'années?" — has the value changed over time, what was it at the beginning, what will it be in billions of years? Very few claims in this corpus are put so cleanly.

Why This Claim Is Different From Every Other in the Corpus

Two contrasts make the claim worth the effort, and the second is the sharper.

Against established theory: the claim is beyond it, not below it. In general relativity, $G$ is a coupling constant of the theory; nothing in the theory makes it a function of time. A measured $\dot G \neq 0$ would not be a correction to general relativity — it would be a falsification of it. This is the property that distinguishes the claim from everything else in the corpus. The corpus's $\alpha$ chain, its lepton tower, its cosmology: all of them are about phenomena that established theory already handles, and the corpus is at best an alternative account. Here it says something established theory forbids, and says it with a number.

Against Dirac: the same idea, five orders apart, one dead and one alive. Dirac's large-numbers hypothesis tied the drift of $G$ to the expansion itself, $\dot G/G \sim H_{0}$, giving $10^{-10}$–$10^{-11}$ yr$^{-1}$. That is excluded. Lunar laser ranging constrains $\dot G/G$ at the $10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$ level — Williams, Turyshev and Boggs give $(4 \pm 9)\times10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$, later $(2{,}1 \pm 2{,}5)\times10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$ — and pulsar timing and big-bang nucleosynthesis constrain it near $10^{-12}$ yr$^{-1}$. Dirac's value is two orders above the limits and dies. The corpus's value is two and a half orders below, and survives.

Side by side, the comparison is the clearest single illustration of why this programme's verdicts are not simply "old physics is wrong."

The corpus's number sits in the gap between the measurement frontier and zero. That gap is the whole of its claim to life, and it is a real gap: a two-and-a-half-order improvement in $\dot G/G$ sensitivity would reach it.

The Chain, Step by Step

The register's chain is S7 → S8 → S10 → S50 → S51 → S40, and the requirement is that at each step the article say whether $G$ enters as an input or leaves as an output. That is done here as the steps are written, with the result flagged at each stage, and gathered in the next section.

Step 1 — The field equations fix how $G$ can enter (S7, S8, S9)

The left-hand side is Einstein's, untouched, with $\Lambda$ free (S7). S8 supplies the geometric source tensor, and S10 and S9 carry the dimensional link and the two new equations (25)–(27). $G$ is not derived at this step and is not used at this step: what the step fixes is the place $G$ can occupy — a coupling between the Riemannian curvature and the stress-energy of the real sector. That is all that is needed later, and it is why the chain can pass through S7 and S8 without either of them mentioning a varying $G$. $G$: neither.

Step 2 — The dimensional link (S10, drafted)

S10's quadratic curvature law $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} + 4\Lambda$ carries the dimensional relation between curvature and density, and it is the step at which a gravitational coupling acquires its dimension. $G$: an input. The link is written with $\xi$ and $\Lambda$, not with $G$, and $G$'s value is not produced by it. This is the same finding the register's S39 records from the other direction: because the field equation carries no explicit $8\pi G/c^{4}$, its strength is fixed by $\xi$, and it therefore predicts a gravitational length of its own — one that comes out forty orders from the value S39 computes. Both readings agree that the framework supplies the form of gravity's coupling and not its value. That matters here: it means nothing in the first three steps of the chain can output a number for $G$, and everything numerical about $G$ must come from the cosmology.

Step 3 — The cosmological closure (S50, S51)

S50 supplies the zero-total-energy condition, $D_{\rho} + \beta c^{2}/R^{4} + D_{M} = 0$, and S51 supplies the bounce with $R_{i} = 6\times10^{24}$ cm in place of a singularity. These are the steps that give $G$ its $\tau$-dependence, because they fix $R(\tau)$ — and $G$ will turn out to be a function of $R$ alone. $G$: an output, but only its functional form; the value has still not appeared.

Step 4 — $G$ as a function of the cosmological radius (5-58), (5-59-a), (5-61)

This is where the corpus defines its gravitational constant, and the definition is the key step of the whole article. RC proposes that the spatial radius of the Réel is, at each instant, the Schwarzschild radius of a metric belonging to the universe at that instant:

$$(5\text{-}58)\quad R(\tau) = \frac{2G(\tau)M(\tau)}{c^{2}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad G = \frac{Rc^{2}}{2M} .$$

The mass $M$ is fixed by the zero-total-energy condition (5-60) and the vacuum density, giving

$$(5\text{-}59\text{-}a)\quad M = 2\pi^{2}R^{3}\sqrt{\rho^{2} + \frac{P_{\rho}^{2}}{\Omega_{0}^{2}c^{2}}},$$

and substituting,

$$(5\text{-}61)\quad G(\tau) = \frac{c^{2}}{4\pi^{2}R^{2}\left(\rho^{2} + P_{\rho}^{2}/\Omega_{0}^{2}c^{2}\right)^{1/2}}, \qquad \rho = \frac{\alpha}{R^{3}} .$$

So $G$ is a function of $R$ and of the vacuum impulse $P_{\rho}$. Nothing else enters. $G$: an output of the cosmology, in form. The value is still unspecified, and the next subsection is where the corpus fixes it — from observation.

Step 5 — The working equation (5-61-a) and its limit (5-62)

Eliminating $P_{\rho}$ through (5-53) and (5-54) gives the corpus's working form,

$$(5\text{-}61\text{-}a)\quad G = -\frac{c^{2}R_{m}}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\left(\frac{9}{16} - \frac{1}{2}\frac{R_{m}^{2}}{R^{2}} + \frac{R_{m}^{4}}{R^{4}}\right)^{1/2}, \qquad \alpha < 0,$$

and the corpus states its asymptotic limit explicitly — "pour $R \gg R_{m}$ on remarque que (5-61-a) tend vers la limite $G_{1}$ qui est la valeur rigoureuse de $G$ pour le rayon maximum $R$ de l'Univers":

$$(5\text{-}62)\quad G_{1} = -\frac{c^{2}R_{m}}{3\pi^{2}\alpha} .$$

These two equations are the load-bearing pair, and the sentence that links them is the key to the whole article. The corpus says the first tends to the second. That statement is a constraint on the exponent of the bracket: the bracket tends to $9/16$, and $9/16$ raised to $-1/2$ is $4/3$, so $-c^{2}R_{m}/(4\pi^{2}\alpha) \times 4/3 = -c^{2}R_{m}/(3\pi^{2}\alpha)$, which is (5-62) — whereas the $+1/2$ of the transcription gives $3/4$ of the prefactor and misses (5-62) by $16/9$. So the corpus's own limit relation identifies the exponent, and the next section shows that the printed numbers agree.

Step 6 — The number

The corpus quotes the rate rather than deriving it in the text, and the same page describes the graph. RC 084 §3 reports both together: the graph "montre ... la variation calculée (5-61-a) de $G$", and "on calcule pour la valeur de $G$ actuelle une variation relative $\Delta G/G$ qui est aujourd'hui de $5\times10^{-16}$ par an." RC 072's (4-49) is an earlier statement of the same claim, and it is more generous with numbers: it gives $G(\tau = 0) = 2{,}21\times10^{-11}$ cgs, a maximum of $7\times10^{-8}$ cgs at about 650 million years, and a limit of $6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ cgs at the half-period. The corpus gives, in other words, four numbers and a graph for a curve it says comes from one equation — which is what makes the equation checkable.

Rule R1: $G$ Is an Input, and Only Its Drift Is an Output

This is the structural finding of the article, and it is unambiguous in the corpus.

RC 082 §4 lists the three observed values used to compute the unique cosmological solution, and $G_{a}$ is the third of them. In the corpus's own words: "nous allons choisir les trois valeurs que nos connaissances théoriques et expérimentales actuelles ... nous permettent de connaître avec la plus grande précision" — and they are

$$H_{a} = 1{,}8\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}, \qquad \theta_{a} = 2{,}7\ \mathrm{K}, \qquad \boxed{G_{a} = 6{,}67\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{cgs}} .$$

And the role of the third is stated: it is (5-61-a) that ties it to the model — "la valeur actuelle $G_{a}$ de la constante de la gravitation, qui est reliée par la formulation (5-61-a) établie par la Relativité complexe au modèle cosmologique."

So the corpus's gravitational constant is a measured input, used to close a system of three inputs and one output-per-parameter. Only the drift is an output. The Système C takes $(H_{a}, \theta_{a}, G_{a})$ and returns $\alpha$, $\beta$, $A$, $R_{i}$, $R_{m}$, $R_{0}$ and $R$:

$$(5\text{-}68)\quad \alpha = -1{,}30\times10^{53}\ \mathrm{g}, \quad \beta = +3{,}712\times10^{79}\ \mathrm{g\ cm}, \quad A = -1{,}522\times10^{-60}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-2},$$ $$R_{i} = 6{,}006\times10^{24}\ \mathrm{cm}, \quad R_{m} = 2{,}855\times10^{26}\ \mathrm{cm}, \quad R_{0} = 3{,}297\times10^{26}\ \mathrm{cm}, \quad R = 1{,}4\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{cm}, \quad T/2 = 2371\ \mathrm{Gyr}.$$

The present radius is the one number this list does not give, and it is stated elsewhere: $R_{a} = 1{,}6\times10^{28}$ cm (RC 083), today's age being $\tau_{a} = 17{,}55$ Gyr. The $R = 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm of (5-68), not the present radius, is the maximum of the expansion — RC 083 says it is "234000 fois le rayon initial" and "88 fois le rayon actuel", and both statements check against the other radii: $1{,}4\times10^{30}/6{,}006\times10^{24} = 233\,100$, and $88\times1{,}6\times10^{28} = 1{,}408\times10^{30}$ cm.

Rule R1's answer is therefore clean, and it is the same answer the constants ledger gives for the Système L: the corpus's method is to seed a closed system with measured values and then compute the rest. $G$'s value is one of the seeds. What is not a seed is the time-dependence. $G_{a}$ fixes a single number today; the functional form (5-61-a) and the trajectory $R(\tau)$ then determine $G$ at every other epoch, and the drift is a genuine output.

That is why the claim is a genuine prediction, not a fit, and it is the right way to state it. The corpus does not fit a drifting $G$ to anything: it takes today's $G$ as data, determines the universe, and then reads off how $G$ must have behaved. The prediction is that $G$ varied, by how much, and with which sign — and none of that was fitted. The framework cannot output $G$'s value at all (Step 2 and S39), so the value had to be taken from observation; but the drift is the model's own consequence.

Where the prediction becomes falsifiable is therefore narrow and clear. The model says: $G$ was $1/3000$ of today at $\tau = 0$, peaked $6\,\%$ above today at 607 Myr, is falling today, and will settle at $6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ at the maximum of expansion. Every one of those is a committed number, and the re-scan below shows that (5-61-a) delivers all of them. What the model cannot claim is that it computed $G$ today.

The Re-scan: One Printed Sign, Five Numbers

This section reverses the finding of this article's first draft, and the reversal is itself the result. That draft reported three defects in (5-61-a). Two of them — the $16/9$ gap with (5-62) and the impossibility of reproducing the reported variation — are two faces of one defect, and the third was an arithmetical error committed in the draft. The method used here is the register's §15: read the page, then recompute every printed number from the equation.

What the printed page shows

The transcription is faithful, and this can now be said with confidence. Page 081 carries the coefficient $-c^{2}R_{m}/(4\pi^{2}\alpha)$ in (5-61-a) and $-c^{2}R_{m}/(3\pi^{2}\alpha)$ in (5-62), and the bracket $9/16 - \tfrac{1}{2}(R_{m}/R)^{2} + (R_{m}/R)^{4}$ with a superscript exponent whose glyph, at the 150-dpi resolution of the scan, is a few pixels high. The coefficient is not the problem. The $16/9$ gap between (5-61-a) and its own stated limit is in the book — and it is a sign in the exponent.

The exponent is determined by the corpus's own limit

The corpus says that (5-61-a) tends to (5-62) for $R \gg R_{m}$. The bracket tends to $9/16$. Raising $9/16$ to the power $+1/2$ gives $3/4$; raising it to $-1/2$ gives $4/3$. So

$$+\tfrac{1}{2}:\quad G \to -\frac{c^{2}R_{m}}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\cdot\frac{3}{4} = -\frac{3c^{2}R_{m}}{16\pi^{2}\alpha} \qquad\text{(not (5-62))},$$ $$-\tfrac{1}{2}:\quad G \to -\frac{c^{2}R_{m}}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\cdot\frac{4}{3} = -\frac{c^{2}R_{m}}{3\pi^{2}\alpha} \qquad\text{= (5-62) exactly}.$$

The corpus's own limit relation therefore requires the exponent to be $-1/2$, and with it the $16/9$ inconsistency disappears: the two printed equations agree exactly, with no coefficient touched. With the $+1/2$ of the transcription they agree only to $16/9$, and the anchored equation is (5-62) — which is the one that reproduces the observed input $G_{a} = 6{,}67\times10^{-8}$ cgs from the published (5-68) parameters, since $c^{2}R_{m}/(4\pi^{2}|\alpha|) = K = 5{,}000\times10^{-8}$ and $4K/3 = 6{,}666\times10^{-8}$.

Five printed numbers, and one character

This is the decisive test, because the numbers come from three different pages and none of them was computed by the corpus from (5-61-a) as a check. With $u = (R_{m}/R)^{2}$, $f = 9/16 - u/2 + u^{2}$ and $K = c^{2}R_{m}/(4\pi^{2}|\alpha|) = 5{,}000\times10^{-8}$, the exponent $-1/2$ gives $G = K/\sqrt{f}$:

the corpus prints where $-1/2$ gives agreement
(5-62) limit, $6{,}666\times10^{-8}$ cgs RC 081 $4K/3 = 6{,}66627\times10^{-8}$ exact (ratio $1{,}000000$)
$G(\tau{=}0) = 2{,}21\times10^{-11}$ cgs RC 072 (4-49) $2{,}21284\times10^{-11}$ $+0{,}08\,\%$
$G(\tau{=}0) = 2{,}2\times10^{-11}$ cgs RC 084 same $+0{,}6\,\%$
maximum $7{,}07\times10^{-8}$ cgs RC 084 $7{,}07065\times10^{-8}$ at $R = 2R_{m}$ $+0{,}01\,\%$
"à peine $6\,\%$ de plus que sa valeur actuelle" RC 084 $+6{,}01\,\%$ ✓

The mechanism is that the minus sign produces the shape the corpus describes, and the plus sign produces one it does not. With $-1/2$, the bracket $f$ is minimised at $u = 1/4$ — that is, at $R = 2R_{m}$ — where $f = 1/2$, so $G$ is maximised there at $K\sqrt{2} = 7{,}0707\times10^{-8}$ cgs, the corpus's peak, at a radius fixed by the corpus's own parameters and by nothing else. As $R$ falls toward the bounce, $u$ grows and $f$ grows, so $G$ falls steeply: at $R_{i}$ the bracket is dominated by $u^{2}$ and $G \to K/u = K(R/R_{m})^{2} = 2{,}213\times10^{-11}$ cgs — the corpus's starting value. As $R$ grows without bound, $G$ tends to $4K/3$, the corpus's limit. The curve rises from $2{,}21\times10^{-11}$, peaks at $7{,}07\times10^{-8}$ at $R = 2R_{m}$, and decays to $6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ — which is the graph RC 084 describes, from the equation the corpus wrote, once one sign is set right.

With $+1/2$ there is no maximum at all. $G = K\sqrt{f}$ is decreasing in $R$ throughout the range: it starts at $K u = 1{,}13\times10^{-4}$ cgs at the bounce — six orders above the corpus's value, and on the wrong side of it — falls monotonically to $3{,}75\times10^{-8}$ cgs, and has no peak anywhere. So the printed exponent generates a curve with none of the four features the corpus attributes to it. This is worth stating plainly, because it is a strong result: the reading of (5-61-a) is not a matter of taste, and the corpus's own numbers choose between the two exponents.

And the first draft's two errors, for the record. The draft claimed the bracket was bounded between $0{,}7071$ and $1{,}0308$ so that $G$ could vary by at most $\pm20\,\%$. That bound is real but it applies only for $u \le 1$ — equivalently $R \ge R_{m}$ — and the draft silently restricted the range to $u \le 3$ while the model's own initial radius gives $u = 2259$. Over the physical range the bracket is unbounded above, and the draft's conclusion was wrong. What the draft should have noticed is that the sign of the exponent is what puts the extremum where the corpus puts it.

The one number that does not reproduce: the present rate

Everything above concerns the graph. The headline number is a rate, and it is the one number the corpus gives that its own model does not deliver. With the $-1/2$ law, and the corpus's own present radius $R_{a} = 1{,}6\times10^{28}$ cm together with its own expansion rate $R' = H_{a}R_{a} = 2{,}88\times10^{10}$ cm s$^{-1}$ — that is, $0{,}96c$ —

$$\frac{1}{G}\frac{dG}{d\tau} = -\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(1-4u)\,u}{f}\;H_{a} = -\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1{,}585\times10^{-4}}{0{,}5623}\cdot1{,}8\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}} = -1{,}6\times10^{-14}\ \mathrm{yr^{-1}},$$

verified against a finite difference of $G(R_{a} + R'\Delta\tau)$ over one year, which agrees to $0{,}2\,\%$. The corpus prints $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$: a factor 32 smaller. So the equation is right, its shape is right, its sign is right, every value in its graph is right — and its present rate is 32 times off.

And the printed value is not arbitrary, which is the interesting part. The same formula gives exactly $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$ at $R = 9{,}07\times10^{28}$ cm — $5{,}7$ times the corpus's present radius. So the printed number is the right formula evaluated at the wrong radius, or the right radius with a missing factor; the recovered text cannot say which. It is now the only open numerical question in the article.

What the corpus gets right about the rate is its direction and its behaviour. At $R_{a}$ the rate is negative: $G$ is falling today, as the corpus says, because $R_{a}$ is far beyond the maximum at $2R_{m} = 5{,}71\times10^{26}$ cm. At the bounce the rate is $+1{,}1\times10^{-10}$ yr$^{-1}$ — positive and very much larger, which is the corpus's "beaucoup plus grande pendant les premières années". And at the maximum of expansion it is $-2{,}1\times10^{-18}$ yr$^{-1}$, "pratiquement nulle", as RC 072 says. Every qualitative statement the corpus makes about the rate holds; only its present magnitude is wrong.

The corpus's present radius, and the two checks on it

RC 083 supplies $R_{a}$ and two independent arithmetic checks confirm it. The maximum radius $R = 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm is "88 fois le rayon actuel", and $88\times1{,}6\times10^{28} = 1{,}408\times10^{30}$ cm; it is also "234000 fois le rayon initial", and $1{,}4\times10^{30}/6{,}006\times10^{24} = 233{,}100$, within $0{,}4\,\%$ of the printed figure. With $R_{a}$, everything else ties together: $G$ today comes out $6{,}6672\times10^{-8}$ cgs against the printed $6{,}67\times10^{-8}$; the vacuum density $\rho = \alpha/R_{a}^{3} = -3{,}2\times10^{-32}$ g cm$^{-3}$ is within $10\,\%$ of the $-2{,}9\times10^{-32}$ g cm$^{-3}$ that RC 084 prints for today; and the corpus's age $\tau_{a} = 17{,}55$ Gyr matches its own $1/H_{a} = 17{,}6$ Gyr. So the model is internally coherent at the present epoch, and the first draft's inability to identify today's radius — it listed two candidates and could not choose — is resolved.

What this does to the claim

The rate moves by a factor 32, and the claim's standing moves with it. This article's headline has been $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$, "420 times below the tightest limit". At the model's own present radius the corpus's own model gives $1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$, which is $13$ times below the lunar-laser-ranging limit of $(2{,}1\pm2{,}5)\times10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$. The prediction is therefore one order from being testable, not two and a half — and the same instruments that would test it are the ones already bounding it. That cuts both ways: it makes the claim far more interesting as a live prediction, and far more exposed.

The Observational Comparison

The claim is live because the measurement frontier has not reached it — and after the re-scan it is much closer to the frontier than the printed number suggests.

The tightest published limits. Lunar laser ranging, through the analysis of the Moon's orbit against the LLR data set, gives $\dot G/G = (4 \pm 9)\times10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$ (Williams, Turyshev and Boggs) and later $(2{,}1 \pm 2{,}5)\times10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$. Pulsar timing — in particular the orbital decay of binary pulsars — and big-bang nucleosynthesis constrain $\dot G/G$ near $10^{-12}$ yr$^{-1}$ over cosmological time. The present precision is therefore of order $10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$.

Two candidate values, and the article must give both. The corpus prints $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$; its model at its own present radius yields $1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$. They differ by 32, and the recovered text cannot say which the corpus meant — but both stand against the limits:

candidate value (yr$^{-1}$) below the tightest limit by testable?
the corpus's printed value $5\times10^{-16}$ $\times420$ not with present instruments
the model at $R_{a}$ $1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ $\times13$ a factor-13 gain would reach it
  • Neither is testable now. No current measurement has the sensitivity to see either.
  • Neither is excluded. The limits bound $|\dot G/G|$ from above at $10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$; both values lie inside.
  • Both are consistent in sign with everything measured. The LLR central value is positive — $G$ rising — but with an uncertainty larger than its own magnitude, so it is consistent with zero and with a small negative value. The corpus's predicted negative sign is not in tension with the present data; it would be in tension with a confirmed positive detection.
  • And the model's own value is the more falsifiable of the two. $1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$ is one order below the limit, not two and a half. If one takes the corpus's model rather than its printed number, this claim is among the nearest-term testable predictions in the whole programme — which is a stronger position than the article's first draft attributed to it.

The honest one-line summary: both values are allowed, and the model's own value is close to the frontier. Nothing in this section supports the prediction; nothing in it refutes it either.

The Falsification Path

What would kill the claim, and what would make it testable — and the re-scan sharpens both, because the model's own rate is 32 times larger than the printed one.

  • A detection of $\dot G/G \neq 0$ at or above $10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$ would exclude the model's own value, $1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$, since that is 13 times below. A confirmed rate that large kills the claim regardless of sign. This is now a near-term test, not a remote one.
  • A detection with the opposite sign. The corpus says $G$ is decreasing today. A confirmed rising $G$ at any sensitivity would contradict the model directly, because it places the maximum of $G$ at $R = 2R_{m}$, reached about 607 Myr after the bounce, with the present radius $R_{a}$ far beyond it. The sign is the cheapest part of the prediction to test and the most informative.
  • A two-order improvement in sensitivity — to $\sim10^{-15}$–$10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$ — would make the claim decisively testable. The register's assessment is that next-generation lunar ranging, pulsar timing arrays and LISA-era data could plausibly deliver part of that; the improvement from the pre-LLR era to the present has already been of more than that order, and the model's own rate requires only a factor 13.
  • A pinning of the present rate to the printed $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$ would not kill the claim, but it would detach the number from the model: the equation as the corpus wrote it gives 32 times more at the corpus's own present radius. The two must be reconciled before the prediction is quoted.
  • A confirmation of Dirac's value would not touch the corpus's claim — it would kill the corpus's and Dirac's together, since Dirac's is the larger.
  • An independent computation of $G$ within the same model. The corpus inputs $G$ at $6{,}67\times10^{-8}$ (RC 082) and outputs it at $(5{,}4 \pm 2{,}7)\times10^{-8}$ from the hadron model (S39). A model that does both owes an account of why, and S39 is where the account belongs.

Verdict

The corpus's one prediction stands, and after the re-scan its derivation closes. That is what this article now reports, and it is a different result from the one its first draft reported.

Sound in kind. General relativity forbids a varying $G$; the corpus asserts one and gives numbers; those numbers survive the limits that killed Dirac's version of the same idea. $G$ falling today, about $1/3000$ of its present value at $\tau = 0$, a maximum $6{,}01\,\%$ above today at $R = 2R_{m}$, and a limit of $6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ cgs at the maximum of expansion. That is a real, committed, contemporary-looking claim, and it is the only thing in this programme that could be right while everything else in it is wrong. Section 16 of the register is right to rank it first.

Clean in statement, and clean under rule R1. $G$'s value is an input — the third observed value of the Système C, $G_{a} = 6{,}67\times10^{-8}$ cgs — because the framework supplies the form of gravity's coupling and not its magnitude (Step 2, corroborated by S39's own calibration failure). Its drift is an output, and there is no free parameter in it: the corpus takes today's $G$, determines the universe from three observations, and reads the trajectory off. So the claim is a prediction and not a fit.

And the derivation closes — one exponent sign, five numbers. The corpus states that (5-61-a) tends to (5-62); that relation forces the bracket's exponent to be $-1/2$, the transcription and the printed page agree on everything else, and with $-1/2$ the single equation reproduces:

  • the limit (5-62) exactly, removing the $16/9$ gap with no coefficient touched;
  • $G(\tau{=}0) = 2{,}2128\times10^{-11}$ cgs against the printed $2{,}21\times10^{-11}$;
  • the maximum $7{,}07065\times10^{-8}$ cgs against the printed $7{,}07\times10^{-8}$, at $R = 2R_{m}$, a radius fixed by the corpus's own parameters;
  • the "à peine $6\,\%$ de plus que sa valeur actuelle", at $+6{,}01\,\%$;
  • $G$ today $6{,}6672\times10^{-8}$ cgs at the corpus's own present radius $R_{a} = 1{,}6\times10^{28}$ cm, against the printed $6{,}67\times10^{-8}$; and
  • the limit $6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ at $T/2$.

Five printed numbers and a described shape, from three pages, with one character changed. The $+1/2$ of the transcription produces a curve that is monotone decreasing, has no maximum, starts six orders away from the corpus's starting value, and misses its own stated limit by $16/9$. The corpus's numbers choose between the two readings, and they choose $-1/2$.

One number is left over, and it is the headline. The model at its own present radius gives $\dot G/G = -1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$; the corpus prints $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$ — a factor 32. The same formula gives exactly the printed value at $R = 9{,}07\times10^{28}$ cm, so the printed number is either the right formula at the wrong radius or the right radius with a missing factor, and the recovered text cannot say which. Everything qualitative the corpus says about the rate holds — the sign, the rise from $\tau = 0$, the size of the early variation, the vanishing at maximum expansion. Only its present magnitude is wrong.

What this article therefore claims, and what it does not. It claims that the corpus's derivation of its one live prediction closes, that the equation needs one sign rather than three repairs, and that its numbers are anchored in its own published parameters. It does not claim the corpus's printed rate is right, and it does not resolve the factor 32. It also records the correction of its own first draft, which reported three defects where there is one, and which reached the wrong conclusion on the strength of an unstated range restriction. The prediction is live, and it is now derived — and the number to quote with it is $1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$, the model's own, thirteen times below the tightest measurement rather than four hundred and twenty.

Summary of Notation

  • $G$, $G_{a}$, $G_{1}$, $G_{l}$ — Newton's constant; $G_{a}$ is its present observed value, $6{,}67\times10^{-8}$ cgs, and an input of the Système C; $G_{1}$ is (5-62)'s asymptotic value and $G_{l}$ the value at $T/2$, both $\approx 6{,}66\times10^{-8}$ cgs. The model's own value of $G$ today is $6{,}6672\times10^{-8}$ cgs — not a symbol the corpus uses, but the output of (5-61-a) at $R_{a}$, and the number to compare with $G_{a}$. It is $0{,}03\,\%$ below the input, which is the size of the thing being checked.
  • $\dot G/G$ / $(1/G)\,dG/d\tau$ / $\Delta G/G$ — the fractional rate of change of $G$, the article's subject. The corpus prints $5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$ and attributes it to (5-61-a); (5-61-a), read with the exponent $-1/2$ that its own limit requires, gives $-1{,}6\times10^{-14}$ yr$^{-1}$ at the corpus's own present radius. The factor 32 between them is the article's one open numerical question.
  • $\tau$, $R(\tau)$, $R'$ — the corpus's cosmic age parameter, the spatial radius of the Réel, and its rate of change. The half-period is $T/2 = 2371$ Gyr; the corpus's present Hubble parameter is $H_{a} = 1{,}8\times10^{-18}$ s$^{-1}$, so that $R' = H_{a}R$ at the present epoch.
  • $R_{i}$, $R_{m}$, $R_{0}$, $R$, $R_{a}$ — the cosmological radii. $R_{i} = 6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm is the initial (bounce) radius; $R_{m} = 2{,}855\times10^{26}$ cm is the radius at which the bracket's variable $u = 1$; $R_{0} = 3{,}297\times10^{26}$ cm is a further characteristic radius of (5-68); $R = 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm is the maximum radius, reached at $T/2$ — $234\,000$ times $R_{i}$ and $88$ times the present radius per RC 083; and $R_{a} = 1{,}6\times10^{28}$ cm is today's radius, which (5-68) does not list and RC 083 supplies. The maximum of $G$ sits at $2R_{m} = 5{,}71\times10^{26}$ cm, between $R_{i}$ and $R_{a}$.
  • $\rho$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $P_{\rho}$, $\Omega_{0}$ — the vacuum density $\rho = \alpha/R^{3}$ and its two coefficients, the vacuum impulse, and the elementary volume $\Omega_{0} = \hbar\xi/c$. $\alpha$ is negative here ($-1{,}30\times10^{53}$ g) and is not the fine-structure constant, nor the lepton-sector $\alpha$ of S37's notation note — the corpus reuses the letter three times.
  • $M$ — the mass of the universe, connected to $G$ and $R$ by the Schwarzschild-radius identification (5-58). It has two equivalent printed forms and the equivalence is a condition, not a definition. (5-59)/(5-71) define it as $M = 2\pi^{2}R^{3}(D_{M}/c^{2} + \beta/R^{4})$ — the ponderable matter plus the black-body radiation — while the identification with (5-61-a) gives $M = 2\pi^{2}R^{3}(\rho^{2} + P_{\rho}^{2}/\Omega_{0}^{2}c^{2})^{1/2}$; the two agree once the zero-total-energy identity (5-46) is imposed. So $M$ excludes the vacuum density $\rho$ as a term and inherits it through that identity. Corrected in the S50 pass — an earlier note here said the corpus includes the vacuum explicitly, whereas RC 085 §5 says the opposite about the term and the identity is what brings $\rho$ in.
  • $H_{a}$, $\theta_{a}$, $G_{a}$ — the three observed inputs of the Système C: the Hubble parameter, the present black-body temperature and the gravitational constant. Rule R1's answer for the whole cosmology turns on this triple.
  • S7, S8, S9, S10, S50, S51, S39 — the chain's articles. S8, S9 and S10 are drafted; S7, S50, S51 and S39 are not, and Step 3's closure (S50, S51) is argued from the transcribed equations rather than from a drafted article.

Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The zero divisor of a product is a property of one element alone: it says that some nonzero partner exists that the element kills. The annihilator says which partners those are, and it is the finer object. It is the set of elements killed by a fixed element, $$ A_\star(\tilde p)=\{\tilde x:\tilde p\star\tilde x=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde p)=\{\tilde x:\tilde x\star\tilde p=0\}, $$ the left and the right annihilator of the element in the product $\star$; the zero divisor and the radical are Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, §The Element Vocabulary. When the product is carried by a multiplication operator the annihilator is the kernel of that operator, so it is a linear subspace.

The block of this article is the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p\bigr)=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q, $$ scalar part zero and vector part the mixed term with the cross term subtracted (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions). It is alternating, so every nonzero element is a two-sided zero divisor with itself as partner and the zero-divisor set is the whole punctured space (Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, above this article in this category). The annihilator is where the block is not degenerate: it is the real line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$ for every nonzero element, of real dimension one, and the whole space at $0$. The article owns that reading, the operator with its characteristic polynomial, and the shape of the annihilator on the remarkable subspaces; the zero-divisor set and the comparison with the other five structures are the cited article's.

The word annihilator is also used one level up, for the elements that kill the whole space at a fixed product, which is the radical; that is the structure-level question of Annihilators in the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space (articles_maths/annihilators-in-the-algebraic-structures-over-the-quaternioid-r-space.md), not the annihilator of a fixed element read here.

The Annihilator of a Product

Definition (annihilator). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$ and let $\tilde p\in\mathbb{H}$. The left annihilator and the right annihilator of $\tilde p$ in $\star$ are $$ A_\star(\tilde p)=\{\tilde x:\tilde p\star\tilde x=0\},\qquad B_\star(\tilde p)=\{\tilde x:\tilde x\star\tilde p=0\}. $$ The element $\tilde p$ is a left zero divisor precisely when $A_\star(\tilde p)\neq\{0\}$ and $\tilde p\neq0$, and a right zero divisor precisely when $B_\star(\tilde p)\neq\{0\}$ and $\tilde p\neq0$: the annihilator decides the zero-divisor property element by element.

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $\diamond$, and unqualified, annihilator means the annihilator of $\diamond$. The left multiplication is the operator $$ L_{\tilde p}\tilde x=\tilde p\diamond\tilde x, $$ so that $A_\diamond(\tilde p)=\ker L_{\tilde p}$. The block is the twin of the antisymmetric plain algebra, whose annihilator of a noncentral element is the real plane $\mathbb{R}e_t\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec p$ (Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra); the two are the two antisymmetric blocks of the six, and this one fails the Jacobi identity where the plain one holds it.

The Two Annihilators Coincide

Proposition (the two annihilators coincide). For every $\tilde p$, $$ B_\diamond(\tilde p)=A_\diamond(\tilde p)=\ker L_{\tilde p}. $$

Proof. The block is alternating, $\tilde x\diamond\tilde p=-\tilde p\diamond\tilde x$, so $\tilde x\diamond\tilde p=0$ exactly when $\tilde p\diamond\tilde x=0$; the two annihilators are therefore one set, the kernel of $L_{\tilde p}$.

The Annihilator of an Element

Theorem (the annihilator is the line of the element). For a nonzero element $\tilde p$, $$ A_\diamond(\tilde p)=B_\diamond(\tilde p)=\mathbb{R}\tilde p, $$ the real line of the element, of real dimension one; for $\tilde p=0$ both annihilators are the whole space $\mathbb{H}$, of real dimension four.

Proof. The annihilator is the kernel of $L_{\tilde p}$. The line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$ lies in it by alternation, $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$. Conversely, the criterion of Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, §The Criterion, states that $\tilde p\diamond\tilde x=0$ with $\tilde p\neq0$ forces $\tilde x=\lambda\tilde p$: the perpendicular component of the partner is killed by the quarter-turn comparison of the scalar and cross terms, and only the parallel component survives. The kernel is therefore exactly the line. At $\tilde p=0$ the operator is zero and the kernel is all of $\mathbb{H}$.

Corollary (every element lies in its own annihilator). For every $\tilde p$ the element itself is annihilated, $\tilde p\in A_\diamond(\tilde p)$, by alternation; for a nonzero element the annihilator is exactly the line that $\tilde p$ spans.

Example. At the scalar unit $e_t$ the operator is $L_{e_t}\tilde x=x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z$, which vanishes exactly on the centre $\mathbb{R}e_t$, of real dimension one. At a nonzero pure element $\vec p$ the annihilator is the line $\mathbb{R}\vec p$, inside the vector subspace. The real block has no isotropic vector, so the line is the only value the annihilator takes away from the origin.

The Multiplication Operator and the Rank

Proposition (the operator and its characteristic polynomial). In the basis $e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ the operator $L_{\tilde p}$ of an element $\tilde p=p_0e_t+\vec p$ has matrix $$ \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -p_1 & p_0 & p_3 & -p_2 \\ -p_2 & -p_3 & p_0 & p_1 \\ -p_3 & p_2 & -p_1 & p_0 \end{pmatrix}, $$ its trace is $3p_0$, its determinant is $0$, and its characteristic polynomial is $$ \chi_{L_{\tilde p}}(t)=t\,(t-p_0)\bigl((t-p_0)^{2}+|\vec p|^{2}\bigr),\qquad |\vec p|^{2}=p_1^{2}+p_2^{2}+p_3^{2}. $$

Proof. The columns are the values $\tilde p\diamond e_\nu$: at $\nu=0$ the value $-\vec p$, at $\nu=k$ the value $p_0\vec e_k-\vec p\times \vec e_k$. The lower three-by-three block is $p_0\mathrm{I}_3-[\vec p]_\times$, whose eigenvalues are $p_0$ and $p_0\pm i|\vec p|$, of characteristic polynomial $(t-p_0)\bigl((t-p_0)^{2}+|\vec p|^{2}\bigr)$; the first row is zero, so the eigenvalue $0$ is added and the determinant, the constant term, vanishes. The trace $3p_0$ is the sum of the three diagonal entries $p_0$ of the lower block.

Corollary (the rank and the annihilator). The rank of $L_{\tilde p}$ and the annihilator are

$\tilde p$ $\operatorname{rank}L_{\tilde p}$ $\dim\ker L_{\tilde p}$ $\ker L_{\tilde p}$
$\tilde p\neq0$ $3$ $1$ $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$
$0$ $0$ $4$ $\mathbb{H}$

Proof. The lower block $p_0\mathrm{I}_3-[\vec p]_\times$ is invertible when $p_0\neq0$, its eigenvalues being nonzero, so its three rows already give rank three and the whole matrix, whose first row is zero, has rank exactly three; when $p_0=0$ and $\tilde p\neq0$ the lower block is $-[\vec p]_\times$, of rank two and image the plane orthogonal to $\vec p$, and the first column $-\vec p$ is independent of that image, giving rank three again. The rank is therefore three for every nonzero element and zero at the origin, and the kernel is the line or the whole space by the theorem above. The same invariants are computed in The Adjoint Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category.

The Dichotomy

The zero-divisor set of the block is the whole punctured space, so it separates nothing. The annihilator separates the elements by the degenerate origin alone: off the origin the annihilator is the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$, on it the whole space. There is no intermediate case, and the reason is real: the cone of the parent quaternionic product is the locus $p_0^{2}+|\vec p|^{2}=0$ of its central square, and the quaternion form is definite over $\mathbb{R}$, so this cone is the origin alone; no nonzero element falls on it and the line never widens.

The real-versus-complex difference. In the complex blocks the annihilator is larger. The complex twin, the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra over $\mathbb{C}$, has the line $\mathbb{C}\tilde p$ off the isotropic cone of the central square and the larger plane on it (Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, the biquaternion article of the same name); over $\mathbb{R}$ the cone degenerates to the origin and the annihilator is the line at every nonzero element. The complex antisymmetric quaternionic sesqualgebra, whose product is the single cross term, has for a noncentral element the plane $\mathbb{C}e_t\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\vec Q}$, of dimension two over $\mathbb{C}$ (Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Sesqualgebra); the coupling of the scalar and the vector parts, which the block here carries and the sesquilinear one does not, is what cuts the annihilator down to the real line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$.

The real antisymmetric plain algebra. The real twin, the antisymmetric plain algebra, has for a noncentral element the two-dimensional plane $\mathbb{R}e_t\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec p$, because its cross product ignores the scalar coordinate (Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra); the coupling of the scalar and the vector parts in the block here is again what cuts the annihilator down to the line.

The Annihilator on the Remarkable Subspaces

The quaternion space has two remarkable real subspaces, the scalar line $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_t$ and the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$ (Introduction to the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions).

The restriction is alternating. On each subspace the block restricts to an alternating operation, so every element of a subspace is annihilated by itself, $\tilde x\diamond\tilde x=0$, and the annihilator of an element in the restriction is the ambient annihilator intersected with the subspace.

Proof. The restriction of the block to a subspace is the block read on the elements of the subspace, and $\tilde x\diamond\tilde x=0$ holds for every $\tilde x$ of the ambient space, hence for every element of the subspace; the kernel of the restricted operator is the intersection of the ambient kernel with the subspace.

The centre. A nonzero central element $\tilde p=\lambda e_t$ has $L_{\lambda e_t}\tilde x=\lambda(x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z)$, the scalar multiple of the projection onto the vector subspace, so its annihilator is the centre $\mathbb{R}e_t$: the central elements annihilate the centre alone.

The vector subspace. On the vector subspace, where $p_0=0$ and $\tilde x$ is pure, the operation is the negative cross product $\tilde p\diamond\tilde x=-\vec p\times(x\,\vec e_x+y\,\vec e_y+z\,\vec e_z)$. The annihilator of a nonzero pure element meets the vector subspace in the line $\mathbb{R}\vec p$ and is that line in the ambient space; the plane $\mathbb{R}e_t\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec p$ of the plain twin, which would add the centre, does not occur here.

Summary

The annihilator of an element in a product is the set of partners the element kills, the left and the right annihilator being the kernels of the left and the right multiplication. The antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication is alternating, so the two annihilators of an element coincide and contain the element itself. The operator $L_{\tilde p}$ has trace $3p_0$, determinant $0$ and characteristic polynomial $t(t-p_0)\bigl((t-p_0)^{2}+|\vec p|^{2}\bigr)$: its rank is three for every nonzero element and its kernel the real line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$, of real dimension one, while at the origin the operator is zero and the annihilator is the whole space, of real dimension four. At $e_t$ the annihilator is the centre, and on the vector subspace it is the line of the element inside the subspace. The zero-divisor set of the block is the whole punctured space and gives none of this, and the annihilator reads the block's single exceptional element, the origin. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the annihilator widens on the isotropic cone of the parent; over $\mathbb{R}$ the form is definite, the cone degenerates to the origin, and the annihilator is the line at every nonzero element.

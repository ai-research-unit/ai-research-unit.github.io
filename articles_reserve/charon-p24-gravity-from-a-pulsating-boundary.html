Charon: Gravity from a Pulsating Boundary

Introduction

This is the second post-mortem article on Charon's pulse, and it records the pulse's largest claim. The first, P23, records what the pulse was said to do to the lepton ladder. This one records what the pulse was said to do to gravity.

The claim is that a particle's gravitational potential is generated by the radial pulsation of its own boundary. Charon states it twice. In the introduction of L'Esprit (Esp 011) he writes that the hypothesis of a pulse at every point of Einstein's space

entraînerait une pulsation radiale de la « frontière » des hadrons ... cette pulsation radiale de la particule serait elle-même à l'origine [9] du potentiel gravitationnel créé par chaque particule.

and in the hadron chapter (Esp 031) he writes that the radial pulsation of the boundary $\Gamma$ "pourrait être responsable de la création du potentiel gravitationnel créé par cette particule". The later statement is the cautious one — "pourrait", could — and the division of labour that goes with it is unambiguous: the spin "jouerait alors un rôle essentiel au niveau des interactions électromagnétiques et nucléaires", while "on devrait tenir compte du pulse au niveau des interactions gravitationnelles". The spin is the gauge sector; the pulse is the gravitational sector. This is the only place in the whole programme where the pulse is given a job that meets a measurement.

The verdict is [FALS] on the force law and [OUT] as a mechanism. There are two independent failures and neither is a matter of interpretation.

First, the mechanism is too weak to be a source at all. The field equation's source is $\xi^{2}\rho^{2}$, and the pulsation's own mean-square excess in the density is $1{,}2\times10^{98}$ times smaller than the matter's. A source smaller by that factor gives a field smaller by that factor, because the field equation is linear in its source. The check in the language of forces says the same: integrating the wave's excess for a single nucleon and evaluating at one astronomical unit gives an enclosed excess mass of $1{,}4\times10^{-76}$ g and an acceleration of $5{,}1\times10^{-53}$ cm s$^{-2}$, against Newton's $0{,}59$ cm s$^{-2}$ — a ratio of $8{,}6\times10^{-53}$. The pulsation is not a weak source of gravity. It is not a source of gravity.

Second, the profile the mechanism does predict gives the wrong force law. The wave amplitude falls as $1/r$ — this is forced, exactly as a spherical wave's amplitude is always forced to fall as $1/r$ — so the mean-square excess, which is what the field equation uses, falls as $1/r^{2}$. That is the corpus's $1/r^{2}$, and it is a statement about the source density, not a force law. A source density falling as $1/r^{2}$ gives a force falling as $1/r$, not $1/r^{2}$. So even if the magnitude were right, the shape would be wrong: the mechanism predicts a force that does not fall off like gravity.

This article is the register's record of that refutation. The physics audit — the recomputation of both counts, the wave solution, the boundary conditions, and the question of whether the mechanism can be patched — is S29, charon-s29-gravity-from-a-pulsating-boundary, and this article does not repeat it. What is here is the claim verbatim, the verdict dated, the observation that settles each count, and the list of what breaks downstream.

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §5.8, sourced to RC ch. IV §I.1 and §III.3 and to Esp §10. The primary passages are Esp 011, Esp 031, Esp 067, Esp 085, Esp 086, Esp §10.1–10.3, Esp 091–095, RC (4-43)–(4-55), and RC ch. IV §I.2. The recomputed numbers are S29's, and the two printed pages read directly from the scans to settle textual questions were Esp 094 — for (374) — and RC 070 for (4-48) and (4-50).

Conventions. Gaussian cgs. $\rho$ is the proper density, $\xi$ the leptonic constant, $\Gamma$ the boundary of a particle, $r_T$ the boundary's radius, $d_c$ the cosmic density and $d_0$ the hadron's central density. The period $T_0$ is the fundamental period of the proper density at a point; $\omega$ is the corpus's "pulsation propre" of the hadron. The measured $G$ is $6{,}674\times10^{-8}$ cgs.

Part 1 — The claim, and the chain it sits in

The claim is not an isolated sentence. It is the end of a chain that begins with a physical postulate about the pulse and ends with a statement about the force law, and the chain is worth following because the first two links are sound and the failure is at the second and third.

Link one — every point pulsates. Esp 011 decomposes the quantised action $\pm nh$ into a spin and "un mouvement d'oscillation radiale de chaque point de la « carte » Relativité générale dans l'espace complexe". The radial oscillation is not an extra assumption laid on top of the pulse; it is what the pulse is. Every point of the space moves in and out.

Link two — the boundary pulsates, because every point on it does. Esp 031 draws the consequence for a finite particle: since every point $M$ of the particle pulsates, the boundary $\Gamma$ — which is a locus of such points — does too, "caractérisée par l'action $\pm\tfrac12nh$". The corpus offers the measured form factors of the neutron and the proton as corroboration, a boundary "mal définie" in high-energy electron–nucleon scattering. This link is fine as a description of the model's own content.

Link three — the pulsation creates the gravitational potential. Here the corpus says two different things, and the difference matters for the verdict. In Esp 067 the potential is said to pulsate "autour de zéro" with a time-average of zero:

le potentiel gravitationnel de la particule n'est pas statique, mais qu'il est en pulsation autour de zéro et que sa valeur moyenne dans le temps est nulle.

A potential that averages to zero cannot be the conservative potential of a static gravitational field, and the corpus knows it: this is why the source in $T_{\alpha\beta}$ is the square $D_\rho^{2}$, so that a zero-mean pulsation contributes a positive mean to the energy budget. The programme's mechanism is therefore a density wave whose square is the source. That is the mechanism audited below, and it is also the mechanism whose amplitude S29 finds in (4-47).

Link four — the field equation turns the source into curvature. Esp 085 takes the wave equation (339) to the static limit and reports "la solution bien connue de Schwarzschild pour le potentiel gravitationnel en Relativité générale". So the exterior field of the hadron is Schwarzschild, and the corpus says so. Nothing in this article is an objection to that solution; it is standard general relativity, imported.

The claim to be judged is link three: that the pulsation is what sources the field. The register states it, and the two counts below settle it.

Part 2 — The magnitude failure, and why it is decisive

The field equation of the programme is $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} + 4\Lambda$, with $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ in the corpus's units. The source is quadratic in the proper density, and the mechanism's contribution to the density is the pulsation's mean-square excess, $\tfrac12a^{2}$, where $a(r)$ is the wave amplitude the corpus's own solution (4-47) supplies.

The number is $1{,}2\times10^{98}$. Two factors produce it, and both are the corpus's own. The amplitude the corpus normalises to is the cosmic density $d_c$, not the hadron's central density $d_0$, because (374) imposes $a(x_T) = \delta_c$ as the boundary condition; and $d_c$ is $7{,}9\times10^{48}$ times $d_0$. And the source is quadratic in the density, so the ratio in the source is the square of the ratio in the density. Hence roughly $(7{,}9\times10^{48})^{2} \approx 6\times10^{97}$, and the recomputation gives $1{,}2\times10^{98}$.

Why this is decisive rather than a large number. The field equation is linear in its source. A source smaller by $10^{98}$ gives a field smaller by $10^{98}$, in every frame, under every restatement of the geometry. There is no way to make a quantity $10^{98}$ times too small into the cause of the observed field — the observed field is what it is, and $10^{98}$ is not a factor any normalisation or interpretive choice can absorb. The check by enclosed mass makes the point in the language of forces: at one astronomical unit the mechanism's enclosed excess mass is $1{,}4\times10^{-76}$ g, and the acceleration it would produce is $5{,}1\times10^{-53}$ cm s$^{-2}$, where Newton gives $0{,}59$ cm s$^{-2}$. The ratio is $8{,}6\times10^{-53}$.

What the corpus actually uses the pulsation for. Not as a source. The corpus needs the pulsation of the boundary to fix a boundary value for the static field, and S29 shows that this is exactly what the worked solution does: the number that comes out of the hadron model is a normalised boundary value, "une simple constante d'intégration", chosen by the requirement that the exterior field match at the boundary. A boundary value is a legitimate thing for a model to supply. It is not a source, and it is not the mechanism the introduction advertises.

Settled by. For the magnitude, the corpus's own field equation, and no experiment is required. The verdict rests on a recomputation of Charon's own numbers, from Charon's own equations, with the arithmetic set out in S29 §2.1 and available to any reader.

Part 3 — The shape failure, and three quantities that share one symbol

The second count is subtler and it is the more interesting one, because it corrects a puzzle the inventory itself records. Inventory §5.8 notes that the corpus's $1/r^{2}$ is "not immediately comparable" to the metric perturbation of a static body, which also falls as $1/r$. The correction is that the corpus's $1/r^{2}$ is not the metric amplitude at all. Three different quantities fall as three different powers of $r$, and they must not be conflated:

quantity falloff source
the wave amplitude $a(r)$ $1/r$ (4-47), exact
the mean-square excess $\tfrac12a^{2}$ — the source in $\rho^{2}$ $1/r^{2}$ (4-48) reads $\rho^{2}$
the metric perturbation of a static point mass $1/r$ Schwarzschild, imported in (4-52)

The $1/r$ amplitude is forced, not chosen. The wave equation (4-45) is sourceless, and its solution at large $r$ is a spherical wave. A spherical wave's amplitude must fall as $1/r$ if its energy flux through successive spheres is to be constant — energy density $\propto a^{2} \propto 1/r^{2}$, area $\propto r^{2}$, flux constant. A spherical wave falling as $1/r^{2}$ would have to lose energy on the way out. So no third possibility exists: $1/r$ is forced for the amplitude, $1/r^{2}$ follows for its square, and the square is the source.

And a $1/r^{2}$ source does not give a $1/r^{2}$ force. This is the step that closes the claim. A source density falling as $1/r^{2}$ produces a force falling as $1/r$, not $1/r^{2}$; the extra power of $r$ that would be needed is simply not there. So the corpus's own profile, taken seriously, predicts a force with the wrong exponent — a $1/r$ force rather than an inverse-square one.

Settled by. The inverse-square law, for the shape. Laboratory tests of the gravitational inverse-square law, and lunar laser ranging, bound any $1/r$ component far below the strength this mechanism would require; the strongest modern constraints are those of Adelberger, Heckel and Nelson (2003) and of Williams, Turyshev and Boggs (2004). The exponent of the force law is among the best-measured quantities in physics, and it is 2.

A consequence the corpus reaches itself, and gets right. Esp 091–095 rejects the static route, on the ground that the enclosed gravitational energy $4\pi c^{2}\int_0^{r}(d_c-d)r^{2}\,dr$ diverges if $d$ varies as $1/r$ like the potential. The arithmetic is correct — with $d_c - d \propto 1/r$ the integrand behaves as $r$ and the enclosed energy grows as $r^{2}$ — and Charon uses it to argue that the configuration cannot be static and must pulsate. That is a real argument, and it is one of the few places where the corpus reasons from an infinity to a physical conclusion. It creates the mechanism; it does not save it, because the pulsating alternative is the one that fails the two counts above.

Part 4 — What dies, and what survives

Dies. The claim that the pulsation is the source of the gravitational field. This is the pulse's only empirical content: the equipartition's one definite consequence died with the muon's spin (P23), and this was the last meeting the pulse had with a measurement. With it gone, the pulse has no empirical content at all — which is the statement that justifies this article and its companion.

Dies with it — the derivation of $G$, as a derivation. The register's chain R4 ($G = (5{,}4 \pm 2{,}7)\times10^{-8}$ cgs) runs through the hadron model, and S29 shows that the number is a normalised boundary value rather than an output: it is proportional to $1/d_0$, and $d_0$ is fitted. The register already rates R4 "STANDS, weakly — a ±50% error bar is not a derivation". This article makes the reason sharper: the number comes from matching a boundary condition, not from the pulsation sourcing anything. R4 is a consistency check and should be kept only as one.

Loses one route — R5, the variation of $G$. S29 finds a third route to $\dot G/G \sim 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, distinct from the programme's own $5\times10^{-16}$, arising if the mechanism's divergence is cured by the cosmic cutoff. That route dies here with the mechanism. R5 itself survives, and remains the only chain in the whole register whose endpoint is a prediction rather than a fit; it simply has one fewer argument under it.

Is resolved — inventory §5.8. The entry's puzzle about the "$1/r^{2}$ amplitude" is resolved: the $1/r^{2}$ is the falloff of the mean-square source density, not of the metric. The entry needs that one correction, and this article supplies it.

Survives — the profile, as a computation. The wave solution (4-46)–(4-47), its two boundary conditions, and the function combination the book prints are correct; S29 verifies the solution to double precision and the printed bracket to a relative $2\times10^{-9}$. Nothing in this article alleges a computational error. The mechanism fails on its physics, not its algebra.

Survives — the divergence argument. Esp 091–095's rejection of the static $1/r$ configuration, because the enclosed energy diverges, is correct and is worth keeping as a reason to consider a non-static configuration. It is an argument about what a static solution can do, and it stands whether or not the pulsating alternative works.

Survives — the exterior field. The Schwarzschild solution the corpus imports in (4-52) is standard general relativity. It was never the pulse's, and it is not harmed here.

The lesson. The register's rule is that a disproved claim is quoted, dated and given a post-mortem article rather than quietly dropped. This claim earned its article twice over. It met an observation — the one the pulse was built to meet — and it failed on two independent counts, magnitude and shape. And the magnitude failure is the cleanest kind of refutation the register records: it requires no new experiment, only the corpus's own field equation and its own boundary condition. A quantity $10^{98}$ times too small is not a source, and a source falling as $1/r^{2}$ is not an inverse-square law.

Summary

Charon claimed that the radial pulsation of a particle's boundary generates its gravitational potential (Esp 011, Esp 031), and made the pulse the gravitational sector of the programme while the spin carries the gauge sector. The verdict is [FALS] on the force law and [OUT] as a mechanism, on two independent counts.

Magnitude. The field equation's source is $\xi^{2}\rho^{2}$, and the pulsation's own mean-square excess is $1{,}2\times10^{98}$ times smaller than matter's, because the boundary condition (374) normalises the wave to the cosmic density, $7{,}9\times10^{48}$ times the hadron's, and the source is quadratic in the density. Since the field equation is linear in its source, a source smaller by $10^{98}$ gives a field smaller by $10^{98}$. In the language of forces: an enclosed excess mass of $1{,}4\times10^{-76}$ g and an acceleration of $5{,}1\times10^{-53}$ cm s$^{-2}$ at one astronomical unit, against Newton's $0{,}59$ — a ratio of $8{,}6\times10^{-53}$.

Shape. The wave amplitude falls as $1/r$, forced by the constancy of energy flux; its square, which is the source, falls as $1/r^{2}$; and a $1/r^{2}$ source density produces a $1/r$ force, not a $1/r^{2}$ one. Three quantities with three different falloffs have been read as one, which is why the inventory found its "$1/r^{2}$ amplitude" hard to compare with anything.

What dies is the claim that the pulsation is the source of gravity, and with it the derivation of $G$ as a derivation — R4 becomes a normalised consistency check, which the register already called "weak"; R5 loses the third route S29 found. Inventory §5.8 is resolved. What survives is the wave computation itself (correct algebra, S29), the divergence argument of Esp 091–095, and the exterior Schwarzschild metric, which is standard general relativity and was never the pulse's. The pulse's founding job — reconciling the integer action with Dirac's four-component condition — is untouched by this verdict, and is why the pulse is not moved to a post-mortem wholesale.

The pair of articles, P23 and P24, removes the pulse's whole empirical content: one claim about the muon's spin, false, and one claim about gravity, false by ninety-eight orders of magnitude in magnitude and by one power of $r$ in shape. What is left is an argument, and an argument is allowed to survive.

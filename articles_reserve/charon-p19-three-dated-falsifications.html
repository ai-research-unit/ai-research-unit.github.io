Charon: Three Dated Falsifications

Introduction

This article collects the three claims of the corpus that were falsified by unambiguous measurements, and treats them as a teaching case. The finite redshift $Z_{\max}$ (P13), the charged-lepton mass tower (P1), and Axiom 6 (P12) each made a definite, dated claim, and each was refuted by data. Real, documented falsifications of this kind are far more useful than a general verdict of "speculative", because they say exactly which premises the corpus can no longer use.

Why these three. They are the clearest, not the only, failures — the register's Part P holds twenty-four. They qualify because in each case the corpus states a value or a rule, the statement is testable, and a later measurement settled it. Two of the three are cases where the corpus deserves credit for making a falsifiable claim at all; one is a case where the refuting data were already in print when the corpus was written.

The article's new finding, and it is a refinement of the usual account. The register's compressed phrasing says the three were "each killed by data that arrived later". That is true of the redshift ceiling and the lepton tower, and false of Axiom 6: time dilation had been measured since Rossi–Hall (1941) and Hafele–Keating (1971), the corpus cites the muon result itself (P17), and look-back time is the daily practice of astronomy. So the three failures are of two kinds, and the distinction matters: two are cases of the corpus being overtaken by new instruments, and one is a case of the corpus rejecting data in hand.

A note on sources. This article is methodological and owns no claim of its own: P13, P1 and P12 own the three verdicts, and P15, P20 and S60 carry the surrounding analysis. The dated falsifications are the register's Part P contribution to the "case study" literature.

Part 1 — The three, in one table

# claim the corpus's value refuted by first refuting datum margin data available to Charon?
1 finite maximum redshift (P13) $Z_{\max} = 4{,}736$ (1983) / $5{,}438$ (1987) quasar and galaxy surveys $z = 4{,}897$ (1991); record now $z = 14{,}32$ (2024) 2,6× no — the 1983 record was $z = 3{,}53$
2 infinite charged-lepton tower (P1) rungs at $\approx3{,}47, 5{,}15, 6{,}83, 8{,}51$ GeV … LEP no charged lepton to $\approx100{,}8$ GeV (1989–2000) ≈29× in mass no — LEP ran after the books
3 universal age, non-ageing photons (P12) all phenomena share $\tau$; no look-back time CMB, SN time dilation, muon lifetime Rossi–Hall 1941; Hafele–Keating 1971; CMB 1992 — (a category, not a margin: see below) yes — and cited by the corpus itself

Read the last column first. It is the article's refinement. Two of these are scientific failures of the ordinary kind — a model made a prediction that the instruments of the day could not test, and the next generation of instruments tested it. The third is not: Axiom 6 is contradicted by measurements published forty-two and twelve years before the corpus's own books, and the corpus quotes one of them ("résultat qui a bien été expérimentalement retrouvé, avec les muons du rayonnement cosmique", P17). That is a different kind of error and it should be labelled differently.

And the margins differ in kind. $2{,}6\times$ and $29\times$ are quantitative disagreements. The third is not a margin but a category: if there is no look-back time then the CMB cannot be a photograph of a 380 000-year-old universe, and the disagreement is with a practice rather than with a number.

Part 2 — What each failure costs the corpus

Each of the three is a premise of other work, and this is why the falsifications matter beyond themselves.

The redshift ceiling takes the modified Hubble law with it. RC's (5-36) is the same statement as its (5-39) evaluated at $l = L$ (P13, §7.7). Its failure is not an isolated number: the corpus's account of why recession stays below $c$ goes with it, and so does Esp 099's inference that the universe is spherical rather than elliptical — an inference drawn from the observed $z = 3{,}53$ being a lower bound on $Z_{\max}$.

The lepton tower is the program's only numerological chain. R7 (S20 → S21 → P1 → P2) dies at P1. The corpus's three charged leptons fit only because $T_{0}$ was fitted to $\tau-\mu$ (S21, S31, S43). So the failure is not that the model missed a fourth lepton; it is that the fit to the three observed leptons was the whole empirical content of the lepton sector's mass formula, and the extrapolation beyond it was the prediction — and the prediction is dead (P1, P2, P20).

Axiom 6 is the most damaging, by a wide margin. It is the premise of the twin-paradox conclusion (P17) and the EPR mechanism (P16), and it is what makes the corpus's imaginary sector an "age" sector. Its failure is therefore inherited by two other Part P articles and by everything in the corpus that uses a universal $\tau$ — which S59's chain map records.

The one thing the failures do not take is gravity. R5's $\dot G/G \simeq 5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$ passes through none of the three; it is the corpus's one live, dated prediction and it remains allowed (P20, S40).

Part 3 — The teaching case: what to do with a dated claim

The corpus did something right, and the thing it did right is what makes this article possible. It staked its observability on a number. §10.11 records its own assessment that the finite $Z_{\max}$ is the only genuine claim to a new observability in the whole program. A programme that says "here is a value, go and measure it" is falsifiable, and falsifiable is better than unfalsifiable even when the value is wrong. Most of the corpus's 88 articles are not like this; the three that are can be audited exactly.

And the pattern of what survived is the lesson. Compare the three dead claims with the numbers that stand:

number status why it stands
$Z_{\max}$ (dead) [FALS] a declared prediction, tested by instruments that came later
the tower (dead) [FALS] the extrapolation of a fit to three data points
Axiom 6 (dead) [FALS] a premise contradicted by measurements in print
$\dot G/G \simeq 5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$ [SPEC], allowed a derived number with no data yet to test it (R5)
$\alpha$ to $2{,}3\%$ [MIX] an internal consistency check seeded from measured masses (R1)
the proton radius, $\approx0{,}92$ fm STANDS a closure of the elementary length with measured inputs (R2)

The corpus's declared prediction died, and its undeclared numbers are the ones that stand. The reason is structural: a declared prediction has to be derived from the model's own dynamics, and the corpus's dynamics could not support it, while the surviving numbers are either internal consistency checks or closures of fitted inputs (P20's subject). This is the most useful single observation the falsification record yields, and it is more useful than any individual verdict.

Three claims that are often called dead and are not. It is worth recording them so that this article is not read as a blanket verdict:

  • The closed universe (P15). Planck's $\Omega_{k} = -0{,}0007 \pm 0{,}0019$ makes it disfavoured, not excluded — though the corpus's own $R' = 0{,}884c$ excludes its specific solution by a factor 640 (P15, Part 2).
  • The bounce and the cycle (S51, S52). The period is derived and correct to 2 %; the mechanism's $\Lambda$ sign is wrong (P10); the claim is untestable for lack of a perturbation sector, not refuted by a datum.
  • $\dot G/G$ (R5). Still allowed, and the only one of the corpus's numbers a future measurement could confirm (S40).

So the honest summary of the program's empirical record is narrower than "it was wrong". Three claims died; one is disfavoured; two are live-but-unaddressed; and the numerical survivors are consistency checks rather than predictions. That is what a dated audit can say, and a general verdict cannot.

Part 4 — Dates, once more, because they are the whole point

The record of maximum redshift, as it stood when each book went to press. Esp (March 1982) cites OQ 172, $z = 3{,}53$, discovered 1973. RC (1986–87) cites PKS 2000$-$330, $z = 3{,}78$. Both books therefore predicted a ceiling that the next decade's instruments would cross: $z = 4{,}897$ in 1991, $z = 6{,}28$ in 2001, $z = 8{,}55$ in 2010, $z = 11{,}1$ by 2016, $z = 14{,}32$ in 2024.

The record of excluded charged leptons. When the corpus was written, no accelerator could reach 3.5 GeV in the relevant channel — so the tower's first rung at $\approx3{,}47$ GeV was, in 1983, beyond reach. LEP was approved in 1981 and ran from 1989; by 2000 the sequential-lepton search had reached the Z pole and beyond, and the $Z$ width fixed the number of light neutrino species at $N_{\nu} = 2{,}984 \pm 0{,}008$. So the tower's window closed between the two books and the LEP results.

The record of time dilation. Rossi–Hall (1941) measured the dilated muon lifetime; Hafele–Keating (1971) flew caesium clocks round the world and found the east/west asymmetry; the muon storage-ring measurement (1977) fixed the dilation at $\gamma = 29{,}3$. All three precede the corpus's 1983 book, and the corpus cites the muon result. So on this claim the corpus was not overtaken; it was contradicted.

And that distinction is the article's closing methodological point. A dated audit separates three things that a general verdict runs together: (i) claims refuted by later data, which are the normal cost of doing predictive science; (ii) claims refuted by data in hand, which are errors of method; and (iii) claims merely disfavoured or untested, which are not refutations at all. The corpus has examples of all three, and only the audit can tell them apart.

Summary

Three corpus claims are cleanly falsified, and they are worth auditing by date: the finite redshift $Z_{\max} = 4{,}736$/$5{,}438$ (record now $z = 14{,}32$, a factor 2,6), the infinite charged-lepton tower (no charged lepton below $\approx100$ GeV, a factor $\ge20$ in mass), and Axiom 6's universal age (contradicted by time dilation and look-back time). The article's refinement: two of the three were refuted by instruments that arrived later — the ordinary cost of a predictive claim — and one, Axiom 6, was refuted by measurements in print since 1941 and 1971, which the corpus cites and rejects. Their costs differ too: the redshift ceiling takes the modified Hubble law and Esp's topology inference with it; the tower was the only numerological chain (R7) and it rested on a $T_{0}$ fitted to $\tau-\mu$; Axiom 6 is inherited by the twin-paradox and EPR articles. And the pattern is the lesson: the corpus's one declared prediction died, while its undeclared numbers — the derived $\dot G/G$, the $\alpha$ consistency check, the proton radius closure — are the ones that stand. The closed universe, the bounce and $\dot G/G$ are not refuted, and saying so is part of the audit.

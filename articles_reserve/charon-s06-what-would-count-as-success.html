Charon: What Would Count as Success

Introduction

This article pre-registers the criteria. It states, before any further calculation is done, what would count as the Charon sub-theory succeeding and what would count as it failing, in a form that could have been applied in 1987 and can be applied in 2026.

The register's own diagnosis fixes the need. "The failure mode of the original program was not bad physics. It was arithmetic presented as prediction." The corpus derives a chain, arrives at a number, and compares it with a constant it had already fed in at the start; the comparison then reads in the book as a confirmation. A reassessment that does not fix its criteria in advance will reproduce exactly that error at one remove, and it will do so in good faith, because every individual step can look correct.

This article is therefore a pre-registration, and its deliverable is the gate register of Part 3. It works with the register's rules of engagement — R1 input/output accounting, R2 pre-registration, R3 recomputation, R4 dated verdicts, R5 tag discipline, R6 the three questions kept apart — and it adds one rule the register does not yet state, and one piece of vocabulary.

One page, and no calculations. Every gate in Part 3 is quoted from the register as fixed; Part 4 records the verdicts dated, in two data vintages; Part 5 says what failure looks like in advance; Part 6 says what a pre-registration cannot do.

Part 1 — The four ways a reassessment cheats

Rule R2 remedies three of them. The fourth is new here, and it is the reason this article is not merely a restatement of the register's §7.

  1. Arithmetic presented as prediction. The chain closes, a number appears, and the number is called a prediction although its inputs were measured constants. The corpus's error; it is why the ledger (S34) counts one defined constant, five measured seeds, one suggested and zero independent outputs.
  2. The tolerance chosen after the residual is seen. A model that is 4% away is a confirmation "to within 5%" only if 5% was fixed before the computation. Remedy: R2 — state target, input set and tolerance before computing.
  3. The input list assembled after the number is seen. The remedy is the ledger of rule R1, and its practical content is that an input named in the final comparison must have been named before it was used.
  4. The criterion revised after the verdict — in either direction. A gate that is loosened after a failure is a goalpost moved to hide the failure; a gate that is tightened after a pass is a goalpost moved to manufacture a failure, and both are moves. The rule the register does not state, and this article does:

R2′ — Revision creates a new gate. A criterion may be revised, but a revised criterion is a new gate with a new date. The original gate's verdict stands, is reported, and is not erased. A tightening after a pass and a loosening after a failure are the same move and both are recorded as moves.

Part 3 carries the one amendment this series proposes, and it is filed under R2′: G-B's tightening. The proposed tightened gate is a new gate, and the original G-B's verdict stands as originally stated.

Part 2 — The three questions, made operational

Rule R6 says coherence, reproducibility and correspondence are never merged. To be usable as a criterion, each must have an explicit test. These are the series' tests.

Coherence — does the formalism close? Test: every step of the chain follows from the corpus's own definitions and equations, with no unmarked postulate; and where the corpus's printed equations and its own definitions disagree, the definition wins and the disagreement is recorded. Pass condition: the chain closes under the corpus's own definitions. This is decidable only because S5's hazard list exists — the notation, the units, the two books' constants, the axioms' unstable numbering. A coherence pass is silent about the world.

Reproducibility — does the number follow? Test: recompute the number independently, from the corpus's own printed inputs, with the input list fixed in advance, and check the arithmetic. Pass condition: the corpus's printed value is recovered from the corpus's own printed inputs, and the arithmetic is checked — because the corpus's printed arithmetic and its printed inputs give different answers in at least one place. A reproducibility pass means the number is a consequence of the corpus. It is still silent about the world.

Correspondence — does the number match nature? Test: compare with the measurement at the tolerance fixed in advance, against the data of the claim's own decade and against today's data, separately (rule R4). Pass condition: the difference lies inside the pre-stated tolerance, with the tolerance fixed by the measurement's precision and not by the residual. This is the only one of the three that speaks about the world.

The flagship α case, run through the three tests, is the clearest example of why they must not be merged. Coherence: passes — the chain (245)→(268) reproduces every step. Reproducibility: passes — the corpus's printed $\alpha = 7{,}65\times10^{-3}$ is recovered exactly from its own printed inputs. Correspondence: the honest figures are +3,64% on the coupling and +1,82% on the charge with correct arithmetic, or +4,80% and +2,38% reproducing its printed arithmetic — so the model is 3–5% away and stays 3–5% away at any precision, because the distance is set by $\beta$ (S35, S37). A reader told only "passed" would conclude that the corpus's fine-structure number had been confirmed. It has not: it has been shown to follow from a postulated law, and to sit 3–5% from the measured value. That sentence is what R6 exists to force.

Part 3 — The gate register, as pre-registered

These are the criteria, fixed before the articles were written. They are quoted from the register's §7, which is their pre-registration date; the source column names the article that decides each.

gate condition pre-registered tolerance / input set consequence if failed owner
G-A a dictionary maps both sectors without contradiction the full dictionary of register §2; no tolerance, it is a formal question the two frameworks are different theories — stop and say so S2
G-B the geometric source tensor admits an expanding solution, or its failure is proved the corpus's own $\rho$, $M$, $R$; the pulsation period to the corpus's own stated precision if it cannot expand, the cosmology is dead in the rebuilt theory S9

Two notes on the register's own pre-registration, both in the register's favour.

  • G-B is stated in the strong form — "admits an expanding solution, or its failure is proved" — which is the correct form for a gate: it can be lost as well as won. A criterion that can only be won is not a criterion.
  • G-C's failure condition is stated as a state of affairs, not as a number — "$\alpha$ only appears once $\beta$ is fitted" — which is the right kind of condition for a pre-registration about method, and it is what makes the verdict decidable: S37 shows $\beta$ is determined in closed form from the lepton masses and that no electromagnetic input reaches it, so the failure condition is not met.

The one amendment, filed under R2′. The register proposes tightening G-B to "require the model's own turning points, not merely expansion". That is a tightening after a pass, so under R2′ it creates a new gate — call it G-B′ — and G-B's original verdict stands. G-B: passed, with the bounce at $R_0$ derived, $K = +1$ derived and also stated in the 1987 book, and the pulsation reproducible at $0.3\%$. G-B′: to be decided, and it is a harder criterion than the one the register fixed in advance. Reporting G-B′ as if it had been the gate all along would be the exact move rule R2′ forbids.

Part 4 — The verdicts, dated

Rule R4 requires each judgment to be rendered against two data vintages. A claim that fails on 2020s data and passes on 1980s data is [FALS] with a date; a claim that cannot be decided in either is [OUT] with the reason.

gate verdict against its own decade's data against 2020s data status
G-A PASSED (refined) the question is algebraic and the data are irrelevant same [EST]; S2
G-B PASSED the pulsation and $K = +1$ are read in the corpus's own numbers unchanged — the result is internal to the model [MATH]/[SPEC]; S9
G-C PASSED IN FORM, after correction $\alpha$'s value moved by 0,028 points between 1/137 and CODATA; the verdict does not move unchanged: 3–5% away, set by $\beta$ [MIX]; S35, S37
G-D OPEN the candidate did not exist as a test one candidate, $\dot G/G$ (S40), against the LLR bound [SPEC]
G-E OPEN — — —

The vocabulary, defined here because the series uses it and has not defined it. Passed means the gate's condition is met. Passed in form means the gate's stated failure condition is not met, and the gate's purpose is nevertheless served by the article's own labelling — so G-C's failure condition ("$\alpha$ only appears once $\beta$ is fitted") is not met, while the result is still not a computation of $\alpha$, and S35 says so in its own text. Refined means the gate's condition is unchanged and the finding is about what the dictionary maps — S2 did not weaken G-A, it discovered that the objects being mapped needed a per-quantity rule. Neither "passed in form" nor "refined" is a pass, and no article may report them as one.

One further dated element the register does not require but the three-question rule does. For every numerical claim the series records which vintage of measurement was available to the corpus, so that a failure is not read as a failure of its own decade's physics. The clearest case in the corpus is the lepton tower (P1, R7): the charged-lepton mass tower is dead against LEP-era and 2020s bounds, and its three lowest rungs reproduce with $T$ fitted — so the fit is retained as a fit and the prediction is filed dead, with its date.

Part 5 — What failure looks like, stated in advance

Each gate's consequence column already says what losing it costs. The register's own two programme-level statements are worth separating from the per-gate ones, because they are the statements that would end the programme rather than a block of it.

  • If G-D fails — that is, if $\dot G/G$ (R5, S40) is closed by measurement at a value outside the model's band — then no number in the corpus survives comparison with 2020s data, and the programme is historical. The sub-theory would still be a speculative reconstruction, and the register's instruction is to label it as such rather than to look for a softer test.
  • If G-E fails — no new testable statement — then the sub-theory is a reformulation. This is the more likely of the two outcomes and it is not a scandal: a reformulation that is coherent, that computes, and whose failures are dated is a legitimate result. What is not legitimate is calling a reformulation a theory of everything, and it is why the register's §16 reassessment outranks every constants article.

And the failure conditions of the blocks are already stated in their articles, which is the point of writing them as audits. F1's uniqueness theorem means the source must carry everything (S7); F2's tension with Friedmann–Lemaître is a real local defect that the block survives (S8, S9); F3's quadratic law is the most falsifiable structural claim in the programme and no measurement tests it (S10); the pulse's two claims are dead and its existence argument lives (S11, P23, P24). A block whose failure condition is not stated in advance is not audited, it is described.

Part 6 — What a pre-registration cannot do

It cannot supply a criterion where there is no measurement to compare against. Three parts of the corpus have no data-vintage at all: the mind material beyond the EPR interpretation (S53–S57), the numerologies of the fine-structure constant that are neither derivational nor testable, and the cosmological claims whose subject — the negative vacuum, continuous creation, the universal age $\tau$ — is not the kind of thing an experiment reports. For these, the correct label is [SPEC] or [PHIL] and the correct action is to say so; a criterion that can never be applied is not a gate.

It cannot manufacture a prediction. G-E's honesty is that it begins empty. The series' one live candidate, $\dot G/G$ (R5, S40), is a prediction in the corpus's own sense — a stated value for a quantity nobody had measured — and G-E's answer will be decided by whether the series can name a second one, or whether the first is all.

And it cannot substitute for recomputation. Rule R2 fixes the criteria; rule R3 supplies the checks. Where rule R3 stops — the integrands of (220)–(221) are missing from the scan, so S43 cannot be written — the correct outcome is a stopped article and a register entry, not an estimate.

Summary

The register's diagnosis is that the corpus's failure mode was arithmetic presented as prediction, so this article pre-registers what success and failure would be, in a form that could have been applied in 1987 and can be applied in 2026. Its deliverable is the gate register of Part 3: G-A (a dictionary maps both sectors without contradiction — no tolerance, a formal question, owned by S2); G-B (the source tensor admits an expanding solution or its failure is proved — owned by S9); G-C ($\alpha$ is reproduced with the input list stated in advance, the failure condition being "$\alpha$ only appears once $\beta$ is fitted" — owned by S35 and S37); G-D (at least one number survives comparison with 2020s data — owned by S40); G-E (the rebuilt theory makes at least one new testable statement — owned by the consolidation block). All five were fixed before the articles that decide them were written, and G-B is in the correct strong form, losable as well as winnable.

One rule is added, because the register's R2 covers only three of the four ways a reassessment cheats. R2′ — revision creates a new gate: a revised criterion is a new gate with a new date, the original verdict stands and is reported, and a tightening after a pass and a loosening after a failure are the same move. The one amendment this series proposes is filed under it: the proposed tightening of G-B to require the model's own turning points is G-B′, a new gate, while G-B's original verdict — passed, with the bounce derived, $K = +1$ derived, and the pulsation reproducible at $0.3\%$ — stands as originally stated.

The three questions are made operational. Coherence: the chain closes under the corpus's own definitions, the definition winning where the corpus's printings disagree — decidable only because S5's hazard list exists. Reproducibility: the printed value is recovered independently from the corpus's printed inputs, with the arithmetic checked. Correspondence: the difference from measurement lies inside the tolerance fixed in advance, against both data vintages. The flagship $\alpha$ case passes the first two and fails the third at 3–5%, and the reason this article exists is that a reader told only "passed" would conclude the opposite of the truth.

The verdicts are dated. G-A passed (refined), the question being algebraic and data-independent. G-B passed. G-C passed in form, after correction — its stated failure condition is not met, because S37 shows $\beta$ is a closed form in the lepton masses with no electromagnetic input, while the result is still a postulated law and not a computation. G-D and G-E are open. The vocabulary is fixed here: "passed in form" and "refined" are not passes, and no article may report them as one.

What failure looks like is stated in advance. If G-D fails, no number survives against 2020s data and the programme is historical. If G-E fails, the sub-theory is a reformulation — the more likely outcome and a legitimate one, provided it is named as such. What a pre-registration cannot do is supply a criterion where there is no measurement, manufacture a prediction, or replace recomputation: where rule R3 stops — the missing integrands of (220)–(221) — the correct outcome is a stopped article, not an estimate.

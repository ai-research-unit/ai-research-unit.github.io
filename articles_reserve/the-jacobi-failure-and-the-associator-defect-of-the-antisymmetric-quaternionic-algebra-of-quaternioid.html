The Jacobi Failure and the Associator Defect of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$ is alternating, vector valued and without a unit, and it fails the Jacobi identity (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions). This article does the failure in full: the cyclic sum and its closed form, the basis computation and the witnesses, the criterion that relates the defect to the associator of the parent quaternionic product, the derived bracket that remains a Lie bracket, and the comparison with the cross

The failure is one linear condition on the scalar parts of the triple: with the cross products as coefficients, the cyclic sum vanishes exactly on a hyperplane of the scalar triple, and its witness is the triple $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$, whose value is $-\vec e_z$.

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The defect is read on the parent product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, not on an associative product. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new. The symbol $\diamond$ is reserved for this block.

The operation and its table are Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions; the criterion is The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product, and the associator of the parent is The Associator and the Ternary Product of the Quaternionic Product. Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x^2=\vec e_y^2=\vec e_z^2=-e_t$; a generic element is $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$, the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\vec q\}$ and $\vec p\times\vec q$ is the vector product. The cyclic sum is the left-nested one,

$$ J(\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r) =(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)\diamond\tilde r +(\tilde q\diamond\tilde r)\diamond\tilde p +(\tilde r\diamond\tilde p)\diamond\tilde q , $$

and the block satisfies the Jacobi identity exactly when $J$ vanishes identically. The associator of the parent product is $[\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r]=(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}\tilde r-\tilde p^{\natural}(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde r)$.

The Cyclic Sum and Its Closed Form

Theorem (the closed form). For all $\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r$,

$$ J(\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r) =-p_0\,(\vec q\times\vec r)+q_t\,(\vec p\times\vec r)-r_0\,(\vec p\times\vec q). $$

In particular $J$ is a pure vector for every triple, the scalar part of every cyclic sum is zero, and $J$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-trilinear alternating map of its three arguments.

Proof. Insert the explicit form $\tilde a\diamond\tilde b=a_0\vec b-b_0\vec a-\vec a\times\vec b$ into the three terms. The first factor of each term is a value of the block and has zero scalar part, so each term is $-c_0\tilde u-\tilde u\times\tilde c$ with $\tilde u$ the bracket of the first two arguments; collecting the three terms, the double cross products collapse by the identity $\vec q_a\times(\vec a\times\vec b)=\vec a(\vec q_a,\vec b)-\vec b(\vec q_a,\vec a)$ and the terms with the scalar product of vectors cancel in pairs, leaving the displayed expression. Alternation follows from the antisymmetry of the cross product.

Corollary (the vanishing condition). The cyclic sum vanishes identically in the scalar triple $(p_0,q_t,r_0)$ exactly when the three vector parts are pairwise parallel; otherwise the triples with $J=0$ are those whose scalar triple lies in the hyperplane of the coefficients above.

Proof. The three coefficients of $p_0,q_t,r_0$ are the three cross products; a cross product vanishes exactly when its factors are parallel.

The Basis, the Witnesses and the Measure

Proposition (the basis decides). The cyclic sum is trilinear and alternating, so it vanishes identically exactly when it vanishes on the triples of distinct basis elements, and on the basis

$$ J(e_\mu,e_\nu,e_\rho)=0\ \text{unless } \{\mu,\nu,\rho\}=\{0,j,k\}\ \text{with } j

There are three such unordered triples and eighteen ordered triples out of the sixty-four, and the values are

$$ J(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)=-\vec e_z,\qquad J(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_z)=\vec e_y,\qquad J(e_t,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)=-\vec e_x , $$

the negatives of the three cross products.

Proof. Alternation kills the triples with a repeated argument, and a triple without the scalar unit has all three scalar parts zero, so its cyclic sum vanishes by the closed form; the reduction to $\{\mu,\nu,\rho\}=\{0,j,k\}$ and the values are the table of the block.

Theorem (the witness). At the triple $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$,

$$ J(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)=(e_t\diamond \vec e_x)\diamond \vec e_y+(\vec e_x\diamond \vec e_y)\diamond e_t+(\vec e_y\diamond e_t)\diamond \vec e_x =\vec e_x\diamond \vec e_y+(-\vec e_z)\diamond e_t+(-\vec e_y)\diamond \vec e_x=-\vec e_z\neq0 , $$

so the block is not a Lie algebra; the minimal witnesses are $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$, $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_z)$ and $(e_t,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$.

Measure (the defect). The cyclic sum is nonzero on $18$ of the $64$ basis triples; the parent associator is nonzero on $24$ of the $64$. Both counts are recomputed from the table.

The Associator Criterion

Theorem (the criterion). For an algebra $A$ of characteristic not two with antisymmetrisation $A^{\wedge}$, the block satisfies the Jacobi identity exactly when the associator of $A$ satisfies the cyclic identity, equivalently the Jacobi sum of the commutator is the signed sum of the six associators,

$$ [[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]=\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,[x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},x_{\sigma(3)}] . $$

Proof. This is The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product, proved there by expanding the twelve monomials; it is general and is not redone here.

Corollary (the criterion for the block). For the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication, half the commutator and half the associator stand in the same relation,

$$ J(\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r)=\tfrac14\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,[\tilde p_{\sigma(1)},\tilde p_{\sigma(2)},\tilde p_{\sigma(3)}] , $$

with the associator taken in the parent product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$. The block is Lie-admissible exactly when the parent product is; the parent is not associative, hence the failure.

Proof. The commutator of the parent product is $[\tilde p,\tilde q]_{\natural}=2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)$, so the Jacobi sum of the commutator is four times $J$, and the theorem identifies it with the signed sum of the six associators. The parent product is not associative, so its associator does not vanish identically, and the criterion is not met.

The Associator of the Quaternionic Product

Theorem (the associator). For the parent product the associator has the closed form

$$ [\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r]=\bigl(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural}\bigr)\tilde r , $$

and it is nonzero at $(\vec e_x,e_t,e_t)$, where it equals $2\vec e_x$, and at $24$ of the $64$ basis triples.

Proof. The closed form uses that ${}^{\natural}$ is an anti-automorphism of order two, $(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}=\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p$, whence the associator is the displayed product; it is verified on general elements and on the basis. The witness is the arithmetic of the table, $(\vec e_x^{\natural}e_t)^{\natural}e_t=\vec e_x$ against $\vec e_x^{\natural}(e_t^{\natural}e_t)=-\vec e_x$.

the signed sum of the six is $-4\vec e_z$, and its quarter is the cyclic sum $-\vec e_z$ computed above.

Proof. For $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$ the parent product gives $e_t^{\natural}\vec e_x=\vec e_x$ and $\vec e_x^{\natural}\vec e_y=-\vec e_z$, so $(e_t^{\natural}\vec e_x)^{\natural}\vec e_y=\vec e_x^{\natural}\vec e_y=-\vec e_z$ and $e_t^{\natural}(\vec e_x^{\natural}\vec e_y)=e_t^{\natural}(-\vec e_z)=-\vec e_z$, and the two agree; the other orderings are the arithmetic of the table, the two survivors being those that put $e_t$ in the middle slot, $(\tilde r,\tilde p,\tilde q)=(\vec e_y,e_t,\vec e_x)$ with $-2\vec e_z$ and $(\tilde q,\tilde p,\tilde r)=(\vec e_x,e_t,\vec e_y)$ with $2\vec e_z$.

Remark (why the defect is the whole failure). The two nonzero associators are the two orderings with $e_t$ in the middle slot, exchanged by the transposition of the outer arguments; the identity would hold at this triple if they cancelled, and their two signs are the two signs of the transposition. It is the conjugation in the first slot of the parent product that prevents the cancellation, and the defect of the block is therefore the non-associativity of the parent alone.

The Derived Bracket

Proposition (the derived bracket is a Lie bracket). The image of the block is the vector subspace, $\mathbb{H}\diamond\mathbb{H}=\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, and it is closed under the operation. On it the block is the negative of the cross product,

$$ \vec p\diamond\vec q=-\,\vec p\times\vec q , $$

which satisfies the Jacobi identity; the subalgebra $(\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H}),\diamond)$ is therefore a Lie algebra, isomorphic to the antisymmetric plain block and hence to $\mathfrak{so}(3)$.

Proof. The image is the vector subspace and the closure is read on the table, $e_j\diamond \vec e_k=-e_j\times \vec e_k\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$. On pure vectors the scalar factors of the closed form all vanish, so $J$ vanishes identically there, and the remaining Lie axioms are alternation and bilinearity; the negative of the cross product is the same bracket with every bracket reversed, and it is the antisymmetric plain operation $\mathrm{APA}$ of The Lie Algebra of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, above this article in this category, of structure $\mathfrak{so}(3)$.

Remark (the failure is the scalar–vector interaction). The block is a Lie bracket on $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$ and not on $\mathbb{H}$: the derived bracket is the cross product and is closed and Lie, and what the block adds to it is the two mixed terms $p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p$, which carry the scalar part into the vector subspace and break the identity. The failure is thus the failure of the extension, not of the derived bracket.

Summary

The antisymmetric quaternionic block fails the Jacobi identity. Its cyclic sum is the pure vector $J(\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r)=-p_0(\vec q\times\vec r)+q_t(\vec p\times\vec r)-r_0(\vec p\times\vec q)$, linear in the scalar triple with the three cross products as coefficients; it vanishes identically in the scalar triple exactly on the pairwise parallel vector parts, and it is nonzero on $18$ of the $64$ basis triples. The witness is $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$, of value $-\vec e_z$, and the minimal witnesses are the three triples $\{0,j,k\}$. The defect is exactly the associator defect of the parent product, $J=\tfrac14\sum_\sigma\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,[\tilde p_\sigma,\tilde q_\sigma,\tilde r_\sigma]$, the associator having the closed form $(\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p-\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q^{\natural})\tilde r$ and nonzero on $24$ of the $64$ basis triples, with the witness $(\vec e_x,e_t,e_t)=2\vec e_x$. The derived bracket remains a Lie bracket: on the vector subspace the block is the negative of the cross product, of structure $\mathfrak{so}(3)$, and the failure is exactly the scalar–vector interaction the block adds to it. same way and has $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ as derived algebra.

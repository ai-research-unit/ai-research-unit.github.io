Matrix Representations: Restructuring Table

Introduction

This table is the working plan for the gathered series Matrix Representations. One row is one article. The four content columns say what that article carries in each of the three displays and what it carries independently of them.

The three displays are the $4\times4$ coefficient frame, the $4\times4$ Peirce frame and the $2\times2$ model. The coefficient frame is written $S_3$ because the frame is the unit basis $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$ and the ordered frame is $C_3$-covariant. The Peirce frame is written z because its idempotent pair is built on $\vec e_z$. The $2\times2$ model is written e because its entries are the coefficients, through $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_k)=-i\sigma_k$.

The columns.

  1. The title, with the common prefix Matrix Representations :.
  2. What the article states once, in a form that no frame changes: definitions, invariants, laws.
  3. What exists only, or differently, in the $S_3$ frame.
  4. What exists only, or differently, in the z frame.
  5. What exists only, or differently, in the $2\times2$ entry model.

How to read an empty cell. An empty cell means the subject has no content in that display, not that the content was forgotten. Emptiness is concentrated in three places: the operators, which act on the four-dimensional carrier and have no $2\times2$ form; the $S_3$ column of the spectral articles, whose bases are element-dependent; and the z column of the coefficient articles, where the Peirce display adds nothing.

What the Table Shows

The common column carries more than expected. Items 2, 5, 7 and 19 state their result once and the three displays are corollaries. The frame matters for the entries, not for the theorems.

Three subjects have no $2\times2$ form. Items 14, 15 and 16 are operators on the four-dimensional carrier. The $2\times2$ model represents the element, not the operator, so the cell is empty by construction, not by omission. Item 21 is the borderline case: the spin operators exist in both, but the $4\times4$ version is two copies of the $2\times2$ one.

Two subjects have no $S_3$ form. Items 11 and 12 choose a basis adapted to the element. The $S_3$ frame is fixed for the algebra, so it cannot host a per-element basis. The coefficient display appears only as the determinant of the change, $\tfrac14$.

The z frame is the only place where the $2\times2$ is a compression. In the Peirce frame the left regular matrix is $I_2\otimes B$, so the $2\times2$ block is visible twice. In the coefficient frame no block of $\mathsf M_4^{L}$ equals $B$ or $-B$. Items 14, 19 and 20 record the consequence.

Item 1 is load-bearing. Every other row names a frame, and item 1 is the only place where the change law and $C_\Pi$ are fixed. Removing it would make the other 22 rows unverifiable against each other.

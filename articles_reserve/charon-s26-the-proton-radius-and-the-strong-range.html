Charon — The Proton Radius and the Strong Range (S26)

Audits §5.3 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md (and the half of §10.8 that is the range rather than the arithmetic), against RC ch. IV §4–5 (scan 063) and RC §4.III (scans 066–068), with S23.

Introduction

The number reproduces, and the honest description of it is narrower than the register's. The register calls R2 — the proton radius at 0.92 fm and the strong range at 2 fm — "the best numerical hit in the program", and the arithmetic is exactly right. But the quantity that is compared with the proton's charge radius is not the holon's radius: it is the holon's radius plus half the charged-lepton period, and the lepton term is 81 % of it. Recomputed here, the Système $H$'s own radius is $R_{H} = 1{,}7435\times10^{-14}$ cm $= 0{,}174$ fm — $4{,}8$ times below the proton's charge radius — and the whole hit comes from adding $cT_{0}/2 = 0{,}739$ fm, which is $87{,}8\,\%$ of the proton radius by itself and has nothing to do with the holon. So S23's finding, that R2's two outputs are one coincidence reported twice, sharpens into a statement about what the coincidence is made of: four fifths of it is the charged-lepton period, and one fifth is the closure's elementary length.

The comparison is also not between like objects. The corpus compares $R_{H\max}$, the maximum of a pulsating radius, with the measured charge radius, which is a static quantity. With the corpus's own condition $V_{r} = \pm c$ the radius is a triangle wave and its time average is $R_{H} + cT_{0}/4 = 0{,}544$ fm — $35\,\%$ below the measured radius. And the same is true of the range: the contact criterion two holons must satisfy gives a pulsating range between $0{,}35$ fm and $1{,}83$ fm, and the corpus quotes the top of that interval. The interval straddles the pion range $\hbar/m_{\pi}c = 1{,}414$ fm; its top is $+29\,\%$ and its mean is $-23\,\%$.

Two results, then. The arithmetic stands and the order of magnitude stands. The attribution does not: this is a check on the lepton fit and the closure, not a computation of a hadronic size, and the answer to the register's question — does it predict or merely check — is that it merely checks.

What the Corpus Claims

§5.3 — $R_{H} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm, $R_{H\max} = 9{,}2\times10^{-14}$ cm $\approx 1$ fm, and the strong-force range $\lambda_{f} = 1{,}84\times10^{-13}$ cm $\approx 2$ fm. RC (4-17), (4-17-f), (4-30). Tag: [MIX] — a genuine numerical success, with the register's own qualification: "he gets there by imposing 'boundary moves at c' plus a fitted period, so it is a consistency check rather than a prediction. Still, it is a real order-of-magnitude hit."

The corpus's own statements, verbatim. RC (4-17-e) and (4-17-f):

La vitesse radiale de la frontière $\Gamma$ du holon (où la densité propre $\rho$ est nulle) est égale à la vitesse de la lumière $c$ ... $R_{H}$ calculé par (4-17) ci-dessus doit donc être considéré comme la valeur minimum du rayon en pulsation du nucléon; la valeur maximum du rayon pulsant est alors $R_{H\max} = R_{H} + c\,T_{0}/2 = 9{,}2\cdot10^{-14}$ cm, qui est proche de 1 Fermi, ce qui est bien l'ordre de grandeur du rayon observé du nucléon.

and the range, RC 068, with the criterion — a contact criterion between two moving boundaries:

Tant que la distance $r$ entre deux holons $A$ et $B$ est plus grande que $R_{H\max}$, il n'y a pas d'«intersection» entre les deux Formes holoniques, la seule interaction est gravitationnelle. Aucun point du premier holon $A$ ne peut donc suivre les géodésiques holoniques du second holon $B$ ... Par conséquent, la portée $\lambda_{f}$ de l'interaction forte est $\lambda_{f} = 2 R_{H\max} = 2 \times 9{,}2\cdot10^{-14} = 1{,}84\cdot10^{-13}$ cm, soit environ 2 fermis (ce qui est la portée correcte observée).

Division of labour. S23 has already audited §10.8's arithmetic and the two comparisons: $cT_{0}/2 = 7{,}3899\times10^{-14}$ cm, $R_{H\max} = 9{,}1899\times10^{-14}$ cm, $\lambda_{f} = 1{,}8380\times10^{-13}$ cm, $\lambda_{f} = 2R_{H\max}$ by construction, $+9{,}3\,\%$ against the charge radius, $+30\,\%$ against $\hbar/m_{\pi}c$, and the $cT_{0}$ coincidence filed with P19. This article owns the other half: the radius the Système $H$ itself produces, what the sum $R_{H} + cT_{0}/2$ is made of, and what the comparison compares.

1. The Radius the Système $H$ Itself Produces

Recomputed from the corpus's own (4-17). The radius is $R_{H} = x_{\Gamma}/(\xi\rho_{H})$, and with $\rho_{H} = \omega_{0}/(\xi c\,\omega)$ from (351) it is $R_{H} = x_{\Gamma}c\,\omega/\omega_{0}$ — the same $x_{\Gamma}$ that S24's integration produces, evaluated at the solution the corpus selects:

solution $x_{\Gamma}$ $R_{H}$ (cm) $R_{H}$ (fm) corpus's print
$\omega = 0{,}105$ (the mass minimum, S27) $7{,}08947$ $1{,}74347\times10^{-14}$ $0{,}17435$ $1{,}7$ (Esp) / $1{,}8$ (RC) — $2{,}5\,\%$ and $-3{,}1\,\%$
$\omega = 0{,}10826$ (RC's own $\rho_{H} = 5{,}8\times10^{17}$ with its $\xi$) $6{,}87512$ $1{,}74318\times10^{-14}$ $0{,}17432$ same

The radius is right, and it is the most robust number in the whole hadron block. Two properties make it so.

It does not depend on $\xi$, given (351). $R_{H} = x_{\Gamma}c\,\omega/\omega_{0}$ contains no $\xi$ at all: the density that would carry it cancels between $\rho_{H}$ and the non-dimensionalisation. So the 1983/1987 disagreement over $\xi$ — which costs the mass $5\,\%$ (S27) — costs the radius nothing. That is why both books print nearly the same radius while their central densities differ by $5\,\%$.

And it is nearly independent of $\omega$ in the window the corpus selects. Over the whole window within $1\,\%$ of the mass minimum ($\omega \in [0{,}06, 0{,}16]$), $R_{H}$ varies only between $1{,}74258$ and $1{,}75168\times10^{-14}$ cm — a $0{,}5\,\%$ band. The curve is shallow, with a minimum at $\omega \approx 0{,}125$ and a local maximum at $\omega \approx 0{,}9$ ($1{,}9381\times10^{-14}$ cm), falling to zero only as $\rho_{0} \to 0$. So the holon's size is fixed by the equations nearly independently of both free choices, and it is $0{,}174$ fm. The register's §5.3 reading — $l_{0} = R_{H} = 0{,}18$ fm, which S22 corrected to "$4{,}7\times$ below the proton's charge radius" — is confirmed from the solution rather than from the printed value, and the correction stands.

2. What $R_{H\max}$ Is Made Of

The sum is dominated by its second term, and the second term is the lepton fit. With $cT_{0}/2 = 7{,}38988\times10^{-14}$ cm:

piece value (cm) value (fm) share of $R_{H\max}$
$R_{H}$ — the holon, from the closure $1{,}74347\times10^{-14}$ $0{,}174$ $19{,}1\,\%$
$cT_{0}/2$ — half the charged-lepton period, from the lepton fit $7{,}38988\times10^{-14}$ $0{,}739$ $80{,}9\,\%$
$R_{H\max}$ $9{,}13335\times10^{-14}$ $0{,}913$ $100\,\%$

So $R_{H\max}$ is the lepton period in disguise, plus a $19\,\%$ correction. $cT_{0}/2$ alone is $0{,}739$ fm, i.e. $87{,}8\,\%$ of the measured charge radius $0{,}8414$ fm; the holon's own radius alone is $4{,}8$ times too small. Neither term is a hadronic size, and the sum is not a cancellation between two wrong numbers that happens to work — it is a sum of a large lepton quantity and a small closure quantity, and the large one is doing the work. This is the sharpening of S23's finding that $\lambda_{f} = 2R_{H} + cT_{0}$ "loses only $20\,\%$ to the holon": the proton-radius hit itself loses only $19\,\%$ to the holon.

And the lepton term's proximity to the pion scale is the coincidence S23 filed with P19. $cT_{0} = 1{,}47798$ fm is $4{,}5\,\%$ from $\hbar/m_{\pi}c = 1{,}41382$ fm, and the reason is $(\tau - \mu)/m_{\pi} = 11{,}9739$: the muon–tau mass difference is twelve pion masses to $0{,}2\,\%$. Carried into R2's own article, this says what R2's hit is largely a statement about: the charged-lepton mass difference.

3. What Is Being Compared

A pulsating maximum against a static radius. The corpus's own condition is $V_{r} = \pm c$ at the boundary (4-17-e). If the boundary moves at $c$ in both directions, the radius is a triangle wave between $R_{H}$ and $R_{H} + cT_{0}/2$, and its time average is

⟨R_H⟩ = R_H + cT₀/4 = 5,43841×10⁻¹⁴ cm = 0,544 fm

which is $35\,\%$ below the measured charge radius, while the maximum is $8{,}6\,\%$ above it (with RC's rounded $R_{H} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$, $+9{,}2\,\%$ — the register's $+9{,}3\,\%$ rounds the charge radius to $0{,}841$ fm). The comparison therefore works only at the extremity of the pulsation. A measured charge radius is an average over the charge distribution; the corpus's maximum is attained for an instant. The ratio $R_{H\max}/R_{H} = 5{,}24$ is the whole content of the discrepancy: the object the corpus compares with a proton is five times the size of the object the Système $H$ computes.

And the same structure appears in the range. RC 068's criterion is that the two holonic forms must intersect, so the range is the separation below which the pulsating forms overlap — not a fixed number:

range value (fm) against $\hbar/m_{\pi}c = 1{,}414$ fm
$2R_{H}$ — minimum separation, both at their smallest $0{,}349$ $-75\,\%$
$2(R_{H} + cT_{0}/4)$ — the time average $1{,}088$ $-23\,\%$
$2R_{H\max}$ — the corpus's $\lambda_{f}$ $1{,}827$ $+29\,\%$

The interval $[0{,}35, 1{,}83]$ fm straddles the pion range, and the corpus quotes its top. That is not wrong — the criterion is about the maximum excursion, so $2R_{H\max}$ is the natural upper end — but "environ 2 fermis (ce qui est la portée correcte observée)" presents the top of an interval as the range, and the mean is $23\,\%$ the other way. The honest statement: the contact criterion of two pulsating lumps gives a strong range of order 1 fm, which is the right order, and the precise value depends on when in the pulsation the contact is evaluated.

One more thing the criterion needs, recorded because it is not in the corpus: the strong interaction in RC 068 is a force between two boundaries that intersect, with the corpus's own $F_{f}$ computed from the metric at the boundary. That computation, and its comparison with the electrostatic force $F_{e} = 1{,}77\times10^{8}$ cgs at the same distance, is the pulsation's gravity and force sector, and it belongs to S29 and P24 — the register has already refuted the pulsation as a source there. This article stops at the range.

And the corpus's two books disagree about what the computed radius is. Esp 089's footnote says of $d_{0\mu}$ and $R_{\mu}$: "ces valeurs numériques ... sont calculées dans l'approximation d'une particule sphérique et sans pulsation; alors que la particule physique est ellipsoïdale (spin) et en pulsation; ces valeurs doivent donc être considérées comme des moyennes, dans l'espace et le temps." RC (4-17-f) says the opposite about the same number: "$R_{H}$ calculé par (4-17) ... doit donc être considéré comme la valeur minimum du rayon en pulsation du nucléon." The arithmetic settles it in RC's favour, because the two readings are incompatible. If $R_{H} = 1{,}7435\times10^{-14}$ cm were the time average of a pulsation whose maximum is $R_{H\max} = R_{H} + cT_{0}/2$, the minimum would have to be $R_{H} - cT_{0}/2 = -5{,}65\times10^{-14}$ cm — a negative radius. So the computed radius is the pulsation's minimum, as RC says, and the 1983 footnote's "moyennes" cannot be right. This is worth recording because it is the reverse of the usual pattern in this corpus: here the later book is the careful one, and the earlier one's caveat is the one that fails.

4. One Integration, Two Numbers

R2 and R3 are not independent hits, and the register's §12.1 should say so once more: they are the same solution. The mass and the radius both come from the same $x_{\Gamma}$ and the same $\omega$: S27's minimum fixes $\omega \approx 0{,}105$, S24's integration then gives $x_{\Gamma} = 7{,}089$ and the mass integral $I = 15{,}001$, and that is the same solution from which $R_{H} = x_{\Gamma}c\,\omega/\omega_{0}$ is read. There is one free parameter, one closure, and one integration; the mass and the radius are two readings of it. A reader who counts "the proton radius reproduced" and "the nucleon mass reproduced" as two successes is counting one solution twice.

And the two readings fail differently. The mass needs the right $\xi$ ($6{,}8\times10^{-4}$) to land within $0{,}25\,\%$ and is $5\,\%$ off under the other; the radius is indifferent to $\xi$ and lands within $3\,\%$ under both. A number that is insensitive to the disputed constant is a weaker check than it looks, and a number that needs one of the two disputed values is a stronger check than its error bar suggests. Both belong in the ledger.

5. Predicts or Checks?

It checks, and now the check can be named precisely. Three inputs produce $R_{H\max}$:

  1. $R_{H}$, from the closure $\xi = (4/3)\pi l_{0}^{3}c/\hbar$ with $l_{0} = R_{H}$, i.e. S22's self-consistency condition — itself fed by $T_{0}$;
  2. $cT_{0}/2$, i.e. the charged-lepton period, from the lepton fit of S21 — the corpus's own $T_{0}$ is fitted to $\tau - \mu$;
  3. the condition $V_{r} = \pm c$, which is imposed, and which S23 showed is derived only on the imaginary sheet where the density vanishes.

None of the three is a hadronic measurement, and the largest contributor is a leptonic one. So the number is a consistency check that four independent pieces of the corpus's structure can be combined to land near the proton's size — which is worth having, because a wrong structure would not land there — and it is not a computation of a hadronic size from hadronic data. The register's phrasing, "a consistency check rather than a prediction", is right, and the article adds the reason it cannot be otherwise: the two ingredients are a lepton period and a length fixed by the same period.

One thing the check does establish, and it is not trivial. $R_{H} = 0{,}174$ fm comes out of the Système $H$ at the solution selected by the mass minimum, with no further input, and the corpus's own printed $1{,}7$–$1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm is right to $3\,\%$. So the closure does produce a length of the right order for the object it claims to describe, and the failure is not in the closure's output but in the claim that this output is the proton's size. A $0{,}174$ fm lump whose boundary swings out to $0{,}913$ fm is not a $0{,}913$ fm particle.

Verdict

§5.3 stands as the register states it, with its tag unchanged: [MIX] — a genuine numerical success, and a check rather than a prediction. The article's refinements:

(1) The holon's own radius is $R_{H} = 1{,}7435\times10^{-14}$ cm $= 0{,}174$ fm, reproduced from the corpus's own (4-17) and (351), and right to $3\,\%$ against the corpus's printed values.

(2) $R_{H}$ is insensitive to both disputed choices — no $\xi$-dependence at all, and a $0{,}5\,\%$ band across the window of the mass minimum. The radius is the most robust number in the hadron block and the mass is the least.

(3) $R_{H\max}$ is $80{,}9\,\%$ the charged-lepton period. $cT_{0}/2$ alone is $87{,}8\,\%$ of the measured charge radius; the holon contributes $19{,}1\,\%$.

(4) The comparison is a pulsation maximum against a static radius. The time-average radius is $0{,}544$ fm, $-35\,\%$; the ratio $R_{H\max}/R_{H} = 5{,}24$ is where the difference lives.

(5) The same is true of the range. The contact criterion gives $0{,}35$–$1{,}83$ fm with a mean of $1{,}09$ fm ($-23\,\%$ against $\hbar/m_{\pi}c$); the corpus quotes the top ($+29\,\%$). "La portée correcte observée" is the top of an interval, not a value.

(6) The comparison is honest as an order-of-magnitude hit and overstated as a statement. $+8{,}6\,\%$ ($+9{,}2\,\%$ with RC's rounded $R_{H}$) against the charge radius is a real hit; "proche de 1 Fermi" and "environ 2 fermis" are the same hit described twice, as S23 showed, and described at the favourable end of a pulsation.

(7) And the corpus's own two books disagree about what the computed radius is — a space-time average (Esp 089's footnote) or the pulsation's minimum (RC (4-17-f)). The arithmetic decides for the minimum, because a mean of $1{,}74\times10^{-14}$ cm under a maximum of $9{,}13\times10^{-14}$ cm would require a negative minimum.

What survives, in one line: the corpus's closure produces a compact object of radius $0{,}17$ fm, its boundary swings by a factor $5$ at the lepton period, and the resulting maximum lands within $9\,\%$ of the proton's charge radius — a check that passes, on a mechanism that has not been shown to be the proton's.

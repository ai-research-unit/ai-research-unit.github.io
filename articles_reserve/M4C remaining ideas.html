$M_4(\mathbb{C})$ — Remaining Ideas

What This Note Is

The question. The 23 legacy $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ Representation articles are checked against the six live articles: $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ e-basis Representation, $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ e-basis Representation : Operators, $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ Standard Peirce Representation, $M_4(\mathbb{C})$ Standard Peirce Representation : Operators, $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Standard Peirce Representation, $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ Standard Peirce Representation : Operators. An item is listed only if none of the six states it.

The test. A bullet survives only if the six lack the statement, the display or the worked case. Where the six carry a neighbouring object that a reader might mistake for the item, the bullet says which object that is.

The doubling. At the regular size every object is the inflation of an entry object. The invariants double: the trace by two, the determinant by a square, and each root by two. A legacy result that differs from its $M_2$ form only by a factor of two is not a new idea, and the bullets below state the difference where it exists.

Ten articles are absent from the six, seven are partly there, two are reduced to one or two statements, and four are fully carried.

1. The Product, the Trace and the Determinant (most)

In the six. The model, the product, the traces $4Q_t$ and $2Q_t$, the determinant $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}^2$, the characteristic polynomial $(\lambda-Q_t)^4+2B^2(\lambda-Q_t)^2+B^4$ with each root twice, the rank, the compression, the determinant of a sum, and the statement that the trace is not the norm.

Missing.

  • The image is not everything: the algebra maps onto a complex subspace of dimension four inside the sixteen of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$, and the twelve transverse directions are lost. The six never state the codimension, and a reader can finish them believing that every $4\times4$ matrix is an element.
  • The inverse map: the four coefficient formulas that read $\tilde Q$ back off the $2\times2$ block of $M$, namely $Q_t=\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}M$, $Q_x=\tfrac{i}{2}(m_{12}+m_{21})$, $Q_y=\tfrac12(m_{21}-m_{12})$, $Q_z=\tfrac{i}{2}(m_{11}-m_{22})$. The six give the assignment and never invert it in closed form.
  • The structure map and the presentation: the scalars act by $z\mapsto ze_t$, and the four units with their relations present $\mathbb{B}$ as a quotient of the free algebra. The six use the scalar action without naming the map.

2. The Six Subspaces as Matrix Conditions (all)

In the six. The six conditions on the matrix inside the image, the real dimensions $2,6,4,4,4,4$, the traces $4q_t$ and $4iq'_t$, the determinants of the regular matrix, the rule $\det M=(\det\mathsf M_2)^2$, the warning that the conditions hold only for matrices in the image, the three splits, the intersections and the closure. Each row is a fixed or anti-fixed space of $\natural$, $\overline{\,\cdot\,}$, $*$ or $\flat$.

Missing. Nothing. The only refinement left is the multiplication table of the four real forms read entry by entry.

3. Peirce Coordinates, Matrix Units and the Spinor Planes (most)

In the six. The two orders, the multiplication table, the matrix-unit table, the coordinates by trace duality, the two ideal bases, the star $S_\pm^{*}=T_\pm$, the images of $\mathrm I_2$ and the three $\sigma_k$, the swap of the nilpotents, the corner compression and the $C_\Pi$ switch.

Missing.

  • The reciprocal scaling $E_{12}\mapsto\mu E_{12}$, $E_{21}\mapsto\mu^{-1}E_{21}$, its identification with the diagonal torus $\{a\tilde\Pi_++b\tilde\Pi_-:a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\}$, and the statement that it is the only freedom of a system of matrix units once the idempotents are fixed.
  • The theorem that every system of $2\times2$ matrix units is the image of the standard one under an inner automorphism.
  • The lift of the scaling to the regular display: the sixteen cells split into four blocks, and the torus acts on the two off-diagonal blocks with the reciprocal weights $a/b$ and $b/a$.

4. The Spectral Projectors (none)

In the six. The two standard idempotents, called the two projections belonging to the root, and the eigenvalues with their multiplicities. The projector of a general element is never formed.

Missing.

  • The spectral direction $\vec\Xi=-i\vec Q/B$ and the projectors $\rho_\pm^{(4)}=\tfrac12(\mathrm I_4\pm\mathsf M_4(\vec\Xi))$, with $\mathsf M_4(\vec\Xi)=\mathrm I_2\otimes\mathsf M_2(\vec\Xi)$.
  • The theorem $\rho_\pm^{(4)}$ idempotent of trace two, orthogonal, summing to $\mathrm I_4$, commuting with $M$, and the spectral form $M=\lambda_+\rho_+^{(4)}+\lambda_-\rho_-^{(4)}$.
  • The explicit matrices, $\frac{1}{2B}\begin{pmatrix}B\mp Q_z&\mp Q_x\pm iQ_y\\\mp Q_x\mp iQ_y&B\pm Q_z\end{pmatrix}$ in each $2\times2$ block, and the statement that the two blocks coincide.
  • The Lagrange form $\rho_+^{(4)}=(M-\lambda_-\mathrm I_4)/(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)$, and the residue $(z-M)^{-1}=\rho_+^{(4)}/(z-\lambda_+)+\rho_-^{(4)}/(z-\lambda_-)$ with contour independence.
  • The non-continuity at the scalar line, and the statement that the standard pair is the case $\vec\Xi=\pm i\vec e_z$.
  • The comparison with the entry projectors: the regular projectors are the inflations, so they have rank two and trace two, and they do not distinguish the two blocks.

5. The Centraliser and the Double Centraliser (none)

In the six. Each one-sided copy is the commutant of the other, and the two copies intersect in the scalars. The commutant of a single element is never formed.

Missing.

  • The commutant of $M=\mathrm I_2\otimes A$, equal to $M_2(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}[A]$, of complex dimension eight for a non-scalar $A$; the commutant of a scalar is $M_4(\mathbb{C})$.
  • The commutant of the Jordan form $J_2(Q_t)\otimes\mathrm I_2$, equal to $\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\otimes M_2(\mathbb{C})$, of complex dimension eight, and the reading of its parameters as four scalars plus four nilpotent directions.
  • The three spectral types in the eigenbasis, with the value algebras $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}$ and the dual numbers, repeated twice.
  • The adjugate inside the commutant for every element, and the dagger inside it exactly on the normal locus.
  • The double centraliser $C(C(M))=\mathbb{C}[M]$, of complex dimension two, and the chain $M_2\subseteq M_4$ that shows the compression does not change the double centraliser.
  • The minimal polynomial, of degree two rather than four, and its coincidence with the characteristic polynomial squared.

6. The Eigenbasis and the Diagonalising Matrix (none)

In the six. The eigenvalues with multiplicity, and the two Peirce orders. No eigenvector is named, and no matrix that displays one.

Missing.

  • The eigenbasis $\mathcal{E}=(\rho_+^{(4)},\rho_-^{(4)})$ and the spectral matrix units $P_{ij}^{(4)}=\mathsf C_{Q,\mathrm{coef}}E_{ij}\mathsf C_{Q,\mathrm{coef}}^{-1}$.
  • The regular diagonalising matrix $\mathsf C_{Q,\mathrm{coef}}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathsf C_Q,\mathsf C_Q)$, the identity $\mathsf C_{Q,\mathrm{coef}}^{-1}M\mathsf C_{Q,\mathrm{coef}}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_+,\lambda_-,\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$, and $\det\mathsf C_{Q,\mathrm{coef}}=\tfrac14$.
  • The second chart, and the proof that the two determinants cannot both vanish.
  • The eigenspaces as planes, the flags they define, and the statement that the eigenbasis of the regular matrix is not the Peirce frame: the two coincide only for $\vec Q$ along the $z$-axis.
  • The freedom: left multiplication by a scalar and right multiplication by a diagonal matrix in each block, of complex dimension four after the scalar is removed, the diagonal part being already in the commutant.
  • The normal case and the unitary diagonalisation up to $1/2B$.
  • The transitions $\mathsf C_{Q,\mathrm{coef}}^{-1}\mathsf C_{Q',\mathrm{coef}}$ with their cocycle.

7. The Jordan Basis and the Jordanising Matrix (none)

In the six. The nilpotent $\vec\Upsilon_-$ and the statement that it is square-zero. No Jordan basis, no Jordanising matrix, no defect.

Missing.

  • The Jordan theorem at the regular size: for a non-scalar degenerate $\tilde Q$, the regular matrix is similar to $J_2(Q_t)\otimes\mathrm I_2$, with two blocks of size two.
  • The double Jordanising matrix $\mathsf S_Q^{(4)}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathsf S_Q,\mathsf S_Q)$ acting in the coefficient frame, and the deflation $D$ that returns the entry $\mathsf S_Q$.
  • The defect $\mathsf S_Q^{(4)\dagger}\mathsf S_Q^{(4)}=\operatorname{diag}(1,s^{-2},1,s^{-2})$ and $\lvert\det\mathsf S_Q^{(4)}\rvert=1/s^2$, with $s=\sqrt{2\langle\vec Q\rangle_*}$.
  • The statement that the Jordan form of a regular matrix is never a single block of size four: the minimal polynomial is $(t-Q_t)^2$, so the largest block has size two.
  • The multiplicities of the Jordan structure across the three types: two blocks of size two in the degenerate non-scalar case, four blocks of size one elsewhere.
  • The commutant in the Jordan frame, $\mathbb{C}[E_{12}]\otimes M_2(\mathbb{C})$, whose only idempotents are the two Peirce ones.
  • The statement that the regular display adds no new invariant: the Jordan data of $M$ are the doubled entry data.

8. Worked Examples (partial)

In the six. The three worked cases of the rank formula, of rank one, two and four, and the case $E_{11}$ as a rank-one projection with trace one and determinant zero.

Missing.

  • The eight element types carried through the regular model: scalar, Jordan block, distinct eigenvalues, singular, rank one, $*$-unitary, Hermitian, normal, each with the $4\times4$ matrix, trace, determinant, spectrum, projectors and Jordan data.
  • The pattern table of diagonalisability, normality, Hermiticity and $*$-unitarity for the eight types, with the two converses that fail.
  • The comparison table entry against regular: which quantities double, which square, and which are unchanged — trace, determinant, rank, spectrum, commutant dimension, length.
  • The geometric reading of each type as a rotation, a boost, a null vector or a projector, in the regular display.
  • The four products of an element with its own words, $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\natural}$, $\tilde Q\tilde Q^{*}$, and their spectra.

9. The Four Words and the Four Traces (partial)

In the six. The plain form $\operatorname{Sc}(XY)$ with Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, the identities $L(\tilde Q)^{\mathsf T}=L(\tilde Q^{\natural})$ and $L(\tilde Q)^{\dagger}=L(\tilde Q^{*})$, and the six subspace conditions, which are the fixed spaces of the four words.

Missing.

  • The four words as bilinear pairings, $\operatorname{Sc}(XY)$, $\operatorname{Sc}(X^{\natural}Y)$, $\operatorname{Sc}(XY^{*})$, $\operatorname{Sc}(X^{\natural}Y^{*})$, with the Gram matrix, the determinant and the signature of each, lifted to the regular frame by the inflation.
  • The statement that only the Hermitian form is positive definite, and the failure of the other three.
  • The Klein relations among $\natural$, $\overline{\,\cdot\,}$ and $*$, the definition $\flat=-*$, and the group of order four they generate.
  • The four adjoints of a linear map, at the entry size and at the regular size, and the statement that they differ from the four element-level words.

10. Trace, Determinant and the Biquaternion Norm (partial)

In the six. The trace, the determinant as the squared norm, multiplicativity, the characteristic polynomial, and the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product used for the orthogonality of the sixteen products.

Missing.

  • The four norms as regular matrix invariants: plain $2(Q_t^2-B^2)$, quaternionic $\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}^2$, Hermitian $\operatorname{Tr}(M^{\dagger}M)$, Krein $\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(M^{\mathsf T}M^{\dagger})$, with the factor of two and the square stated exactly.
  • The identity $\operatorname{adj}M=\mathsf M_4(\tilde Q^{\natural})=M^{\mathsf T}$, which makes the Krein norm a pairing between the adjugate and the dagger.
  • The Cayley–Hamilton identity $\chi_M(M)=0$ with $\chi_M(t)=(t-Q_t)^4+2B^2(t-Q_t)^2+B^4$, and the degree-two block identity $M^2-2Q_tM+\langle\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}\mathrm I_4=0$. The six use only the null-cone case $L(L-2Q_t\mathrm I_4)=0$, inside the rank proof.
  • The signature of each of the four forms on the real sixteen-dimensional algebra, and which of them is positive definite.
  • The discriminant $-4B^2$ as the spectral criterion, and the inequality $\lvert\det M\rvert\le\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}(M^{\dagger}M)^2$ with equality on the quaternionic unit sphere.

11. The Polar Decomposition of a Matrix (none)

In the six. The word polar occurs once, for the polar form of the norm. The decomposition is absent.

Missing.

  • The theorem $M=PV$ with $P$ positive semidefinite and $V$ a partial isometry, and the uniqueness on the invertible locus.
  • The closed form $P=(MM^{\dagger}+\lvert N\rvert\mathrm I_4)/\sqrt{\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(MM^{\dagger})+2\lvert N\rvert}$, with $\lvert N\rvert=\lvert\det\mathsf M_2\rvert$ the modulus of the norm, polynomial in the entries.
  • The diffeomorphism $Herm^{>0}_4\times U(4)\to GL_4$, and the reading of the factors as boost and rotor.
  • The word $M_bM_r$ with $M_b$ positive Hermitian of determinant one and $M_r$ unitary, and the check $\det M=r^2\zeta^2$ in the regular display.
  • The singular case with $V$ a partial isometry, the singular values $s_{1,2}=re^{\pm\eta/2}$ each twice, and $s_1s_2=\lvert N\rvert$.
  • The statement that the entry polar decomposition is the deflation of the regular one, and that the halved trace is the only difference in the closed form.

12. Rank-One Matrices and the Determinantal Variety (partial)

In the six. The rank formula for a pair, the rank-one coefficient criterion, the length, the two one-sided boundaries, and $E_{11}$ as a rank-one projection. The six use Segre for a different object: the cone $\mathbb{P}^3\times\mathbb{P}^3$ of pure tensors in the operator space.

Missing.

  • The factorisation $M=uv^{\mathsf T}$ of a nonzero rank-one entry matrix, its uniqueness up to the reciprocal scaling, and the regular form $C_\Pi\operatorname{diag}(uv^{\mathsf T},uv^{\mathsf T})C_\Pi^{-1}$, of rank two.
  • The four coefficients of the element in terms of $u$ and $v$, and the translation of the pair into the regular frame.
  • The two strata at the regular size: the nilpotents, doubled, of real dimension four, and the nonzero scaled idempotents, doubled, of real dimension six, the first as the boundary of the second.
  • The determinantal variety as the zero set of $\det$, a complex cone of complex dimension three, and its projectivisation as the Segre quadric $\mathbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^1$ with the two rulings given by the image line and the kernel line; the doubling keeps the cone and its dimensions.
  • The annihilators $\{X:XM=0\}=\{X:Xu=0\}$ and $\{X:MX=0\}=\{X:v^{\mathsf T}X=0\}$, and the two ideals they generate.
  • The nilpotent corner: a nonzero nilpotent is a single square-zero block of rank one at the entry size and of rank two at the regular size, and its square-zero partner is fixed by the product with the idempotents.

13. Spin, Qubits and the Bloch Vector (none)

In the six. The Pauli matrices in the display of $\mathsf M_2$, the Hermitian subspace with its explicit matrix and indefinite quadratic determinant, the anti-Hermitian subspace. The state space is never formed.

Missing.

  • The table $\mathsf M_2(e_t)=\mathrm I_2$, $\mathsf M_2(\vec e_k)=-i\sigma_k$, $\mathsf M_2(i\vec e_k)=\sigma_k$, and its inflation $\mathrm I_2\otimes\cdot$ to the regular frame.
  • The spin operators $\tilde S_k=\tfrac{\hbar}{2}i\vec e_k$, their commutators, and the regular matrices as the doubled spin operators.
  • The pure states, the spinor, the complementarity $\tilde\Pi(\vec u)+\tilde\Pi(-\vec u)=e_t$, and the statement that a pure state is a null vector and an idempotent.
  • The Bloch ball at the regular size: $\rho_4=\tfrac12(\mathrm I_2\otimes\rho)$ with eigenvalues $\tfrac14(1\pm\lvert\vec r\rvert)$ each twice, $\det\rho_4=\tfrac1{256}(1-\lvert\vec r\rvert^2)^2$, $\operatorname{Tr}\rho_4^2=\tfrac14(1+\lvert\vec r\rvert^2)$, trace one.
  • The Born rule $p_\pm=\tfrac12(1\pm\vec r\cdot\vec u)$, the affine expectation, and the Hopf fibration with the unobservable phase, taking the two-to-one cover of the state space into account.
  • The list of what the correspondence does not give: the real form, the three directions as space, and any dynamics.

14. The Conjugations and the Adjoints (partial)

In the six. The transpose and the dagger identities for the two families, the identity ${}^{*}=\natural\circ\overline{\,\cdot\,}$ in use, the four symbols as the conditions of the six subspaces, and the plain adjoint $\approx$.

Missing.

  • The table of the four words as involutions, with definition, fixed space, anti-fixed space and dimension for each.
  • The Klein relations, the definition $\flat=-*$, and the group of order four generated by the three commuting involutions.
  • The adjugate of the regular matrix, $\operatorname{adj}M=NM^{\mathsf T}=\mathrm I_2\otimes\operatorname{adj}A$, homogeneous of degree three, with $\operatorname{adj}(M_1M_2)=\operatorname{adj}M_2\operatorname{adj}M_1$ and $\operatorname{adj}(\operatorname{adj}M)=N^4M$. It is not an involution here, unlike the entry size, and it lies outside the group of four words by the scalar factor $N$ — a reader who guesses the entry formula is wrong, which is why the case is worth one paragraph.
  • The fixed locus of the adjugate on the invertible matrices, $M^2=(\det M)\mathrm I_4$, which contains every involution up to scale and every nilpotent of the fixed space.
  • The fifth involution, conjugation by $\vec e_y$, as a symmetry of the display and not one of the four words.

15. $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$, $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ and the Determinant (none)

In the six. Invertible pairs give $GL_2\times GL_2$, eight complex parameters, with the scaling as kernel; $PGL_2$ is the automorphism group; $\mathbb{B}^{\times}$ is named and counted of complex dimension four; the basis matrices lie in $SL_2$.

Missing.

  • The sequence $1\to SL_2\to GL_2\xrightarrow{\det}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to1$, its non-central splitting $s(\lambda)=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,1)$ with $GL_2\cong SL_2\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}$, and the absence of a central splitting, since $\mu(-1)^2$ would have to be both $1$ and $-1$.
  • The twofold cover $GL_2\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times SL_2)/\{\pm1\}$ through $(\lambda,U)\mapsto\lambda U$, with $\det(\lambda U)=\lambda^2$.
  • The level sets of the determinant, smooth of complex dimension three off zero, homogeneous under the action, and the descent to $PGL_2$ only up to squares.
  • The topology: $GL_2$ connected with $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}$, $SL_2$ simply connected with the homotopy type of $S^3$.
  • The Bruhat decomposition $GL_2=B\sqcup BwB$, with cells of complex dimensions three and four, read as the projective line cut into the affine line and the point at infinity.
  • The three double covers with kernel $\{\pm\mathrm I_2\}$: $SU(2)\to SO(3)$, $SL_2(\mathbb{C})\to SO^{+}(1,3)$, $SL_2(\mathbb{C})\to PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$, against the Hopf fibration.
  • The Lorentz action $H\mapsto MHM^{\dagger}$ on the Hermitian matrices, and its lift to the regular display.

16. The Projective Line and the Möbius Action (partial)

Missing.

  • The family $I_L=\{M:\operatorname{im}M\subseteq L\}$ over the projective line, the bijection $L\mapsto I_L$, and the proof of minimality by the column argument.
  • The action on the family, its kernel, its quotient $PGL_2$, and the idempotent form $\Pi\mapsto M\Pi M^{-1}$.

  • The compact picture $PU(2)\cong SO(3)$, with the projective line and its real structure as the two-sphere.

17. The Killing Form (none)

In the six. Nothing. The word does not occur, and no invariant bilinear form on the algebra is formed beyond the plain and Hilbert–Schmidt forms.

Missing.

  • The adjoint representation of the four-dimensional algebra and its diagonal form in the Peirce frame, with eigenvalues $0,x_1-x_2,x_2-x_1,0$.
  • The complex form $\kappa_{\mathbb{C}}(X,Y)=2\operatorname{tr}_4(XY)-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}_4X\operatorname{tr}_4Y$, the real form $4\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}_4(XY)-\operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{tr}_4X\operatorname{tr}_4Y)$, and the coordinate form $-8\vec X\cdot\vec Y$ in either normalization.
  • The ambient restriction: the regular image is not an ideal of $M_4(\mathbb{C})$, the ambient form is $8\operatorname{tr}(XY)-2\operatorname{tr}X\operatorname{tr}Y$, and on the image it is four times the intrinsic one, the factor being the twelve transverse directions.
  • The radical equal to the centre, the invariance $\kappa([X,Y],Z)+\kappa(Y,[X,Z])=0$, the signature $(3,3)$ on the trace-free part, and the descent to the projective group.
  • The Casimir $\Omega=-\tfrac18\sum_k\mathsf M_4^{L}(\vec e_k)^2=\tfrac38\mathrm I_4$, the dual basis $-\vec e_k/8$, and the statement that the scalar $\tfrac38$ is the same as at the entry size and acts on each copy of the defining module.
  • The comparison with the trace form: ratio $-2$ on the vector part, and on the centre the trace form is positive while the Killing form vanishes.

18. The Functional Calculus (none)

In the six. Polynomials are evaluated only through the characteristic polynomial. No integral, no exponential, no logarithm, no square root.

Missing.

  • The Cauchy integral and the two spectral forms, $f(\lambda_+)\rho_+^{(4)}+f(\lambda_-)\rho_-^{(4)}$ and $f(\lambda)\mathrm I_4+f'(\lambda)N$ with $N$ the nilpotent part.
  • The independence of the contour, the homomorphism property, and the spectral mapping theorem.
  • The exponential $\exp L=e^{Q_t}\bigl(\cos B\,\mathrm I_4+\tfrac{\sin B}{B}\mathsf M_4(\vec Q)\bigr)$, with the three regimes.
  • The theorem $\det(\exp L)=\exp(\operatorname{Tr}L)$, the corollary $\exp(\mathfrak{sl}_4)=SL_4$, and $\langle\exp\tilde Q\rangle_{\natural}=\exp(2Q_t)$.
  • The kernel of the exponential, not a subgroup, and its intersection with the null cone.
  • The logarithm on the invertible locus, with the $\mathbb{Z}^2$ family in the distinct case and the $\mathbb{Z}$ family in the repeated one.
  • The square-root classification by eigenvalue type, and the closed form $X^{1/2}=(X+\sqrt N\mathrm I_4)/\sqrt{\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}X+2\sqrt N}$.

22. The General Endomorphisms (partial)

In the six. The operator space is displayed in the coefficient frame and in the Peirce frame, the switch is computed, the four families are named, and the count of the defining data is given. The general matrix is not developed.

Missing.

  • The bracket $[F]_{\mathcal{B}}$ as a notation for the matrix of a general operator in a frame, and the statement that no frame-free matrix exists.
  • The general change law $[F]_{\mathcal{B}'}=C^{-1}[F]_{\mathcal{B}}C$ for an arbitrary frame $C$, beyond the two frames of the corpus and the switch between them.
  • The orbit problem: the similarity class is the invariant, and no canonical form exists for a general operator of the algebra.
  • The units of the operator space, the unit table, and the statement that the units of the algebra and the units of the operator space are different objects.
  • The invariants of a general operator read on its matrix: trace, determinant, spectrum, rank, image, and the characteristic polynomial of a general member of $\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{B})$.

23. The Hermitian Sandwich (none)

In the six. The sandwich is named once, as the subcone $\tilde P=\tilde Q^{*}$ of the cone of products, and deferred. Nothing else.

Missing.

  • The multiplicativity $\Theta_{\tilde Q}\Theta_{\tilde P}=\Theta_{\tilde Q\tilde P}$, the defect $\tilde Q^{*}\tilde Q$ in the non-Hermitian case, and the automorphism group the $*$-unitary slice $U(2)$.
  • The invariance of the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian halves and of the centre for every parameter, and of the vector and quaternion subspaces exactly for central multiples of real unit quaternions.
  • The Peirce matrix $\overline B\otimes B$, with trace $4\lvert Q_t\rvert^2$, determinant $\lvert N\rvert^4$, rank $\operatorname{rank}(B)^2\in\{4,1,0\}$ and eigenvalues $\lambda_i\overline{\lambda_j}$.
  • The norm scaling $\lvert N\rvert^2$, hence the preservation of the null cone, and the count of four parameters against the seven of the general two-sided operator.
  • The Lorentz action on the anti-Hermitian half, $\Lambda^{\mathsf T}g\Lambda=\lvert N\rvert^2g$, with $\Lambda\in SO^{+}(1,3)$ on the norm-one slice and $\det\Lambda=r^8$; the sandwich is conformal, not orthogonal.
  • The positivity: the sandwich of a positive Hermitian element is positive, and its trace-one slice is the Bloch ball.

What the Remainder Forms

The normal forms (4 to 7). Spectral projectors, commutant, eigenbasis, Jordan basis. The six treat an element through its coefficients and its polynomial invariants, never through the frames that put it in normal form. At the regular size each object is the inflation of an entry object, and the interest lies in the multiplicity: the eigenspaces are planes, the commutant gains the $M_2$ factor, and the minimal polynomial drops from four to two.

The analysis (11, 18). Polar decomposition and functional calculus, with the halved trace and the modulus of the norm in place of the modulus of the determinant.

The group and the geometry (15, 16, 23). The determinant sequence with its Bruhat cells, the projective line with the Möbius action and the cross-ratio, and the Hermitian sandwich with the Lorentz action.

The forms and the involutions (9, 10, 14, 17). The four traces, the four norms, the four words with the cubic adjugate, and the Killing form with the Casimir.

The readings (12, 13). Rank-two matrices in the regular image, the cone over the Segre quadric with its two rulings, and the Bloch ball with the regular density matrix.

The bookkeeping (1, 3, 8, 22). The adjugate and Cayley–Hamilton, the reciprocal scaling, the eight worked types, and the general endomorphism with its change law.

B. The Length Is Frame-Invariant.

Thesis. The length $\ell(M)=\operatorname{rank}c(M)$ is defined through a coefficient matrix that depends on the frame; it is nevertheless a matrix invariant, because a change of algebra frame acts on the operator basis by a congruence.

Why no host. The length theorem is proved in one frame in each operator article, and the e-basis article lists the frame dependence among its open questions. The question is the length well defined? is therefore open in print, and it has a one-line answer.

Outline. (1) The coefficient matrix and its frame dependence. (2) A change of algebra basis $C$ sends $T_{\mu\nu}=L_\mu R_\nu$ to $\sum C_{\mu\alpha}C_{\nu\beta}T_{\alpha\beta}$, hence $c$ to $C^{-1}c\,C^{-\mathsf T}$, whose rank is unchanged. (3) The two frames of the corpus as the two cases, with $C_\Pi$ and $S$. (4) The consequences: the length is an invariant of the matrix, the stratification by length is frame-free, and the open question in the e-basis article can be closed.

Done, as §The Coefficient Matrix and the Length in both matrix product, trace and determinant articles — §The Change of Frame Is a Congruence and §The Sixteen Lengths inside it. The two operators articles keep a two-line pointer.

Three corrections, made while writing. (i) The congruence is right and was checked numerically on $C_\Pi$: $c'=C_\Pi^{-1}c\,C_\Pi^{-\mathsf T}$, rank preserved across ranks one to four. But $C_\Pi$ is not the change between the two displays: both operator articles build the sixteen from the same four coefficient units $e_\mu$, so their abstract operator basis is identical and $c$ is literally unchanged — $T^{\mathcal P}_{\mu\nu}=C_\Pi^{-1}T^{\mathcal E}_{\mu\nu}C_\Pi$ and $M^{\mathcal P}=C_\Pi^{-1}M^{\mathcal E}C_\Pi$ move together. The congruence applies to a change of the element basis — for instance building the sixteen from the four Peirce elements instead — and that basis is not orthogonal, so its coefficients must be read with the dual basis. (ii) Only $\det C\ne0$ is used, so the non-orthogonality of $C_\Pi$ costs nothing. (iii) The sharper statement is negative: the same array read in two frames names two different operators, and its length may differ, as $E_{11}$ shows. So the length is an invariant of the operator, not of the matrix as an array.

Length and risk. 1200 words, one proposition. Verify the congruence before writing: the claim is that the frame change acts on the algebra basis and hence by congruence on the operator basis, and this must be checked on $C_\Pi$ explicitly, since $C_\Pi$ is not orthogonal.

C. The Sixteen Lengths.

Thesis. Every one of the sixteen matrix units has length four, so the open question in the e-basis operators article has the answer they do not differ.

Why no host. The article states $\ell(E_{11})=4$ and adds that "the other units may differ". A finite computation settles it, and a table of sixteen numbers is not a section of a theorem-driven article.

The computation. With $T_{\mu\nu}^{e}=L^{e}_\mu R^{e}_\nu$ and $c_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((T^{e}_{\mu\nu})^{\mathsf T}M\bigr)$, all in the coefficient frame of the e-basis, the rank of $c$ is four for each of the sixteen units. The check is five lines of exact rational arithmetic. Two control values: $\ell(e_t)=1$, since $e_t=T^{e}_{tt}$ is a single product, and $\ell(E_{11})=4$, which matches the article.

Done. This is now §The Sixteen Lengths, inside §The Coefficient Matrix and the Length of both matrix product, trace and determinant articles. In the e-basis all sixteen units have length four, and the table of sixteen fours is proved without a sixteen-fold computation: $c(E_{ij})_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac14(T_{\mu\nu})_{ij}$ is a quarter of a monomial matrix, because multiplication by a unit permutes the basis up to sign. In the Peirce frame all sixteen have length one, and the sixteen factorisations are tabulated; every factor has $\mathsf M_2$ a scalar multiple of a matrix unit. The two results are consistent because the arrays name different operators.

Length and risk. 800 words, one table, one proposition. Re-prove rather than cite the computation, and state the frame: the result is a computation in the coefficient frame, and article B is what would make it a frame-free statement.

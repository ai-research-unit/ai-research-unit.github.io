Charon — What To Do Next (S61)

Consolidation, fourth article. The open threads worth keeping, each with the cheapest next test.

Introduction

The programme's remaining work is short, and most of it is cheap. After S60's assessment the live questions are few: one prediction to push toward testability, three internal checks that would settle open numbers, a handful of archival recovery tasks, and two structural questions that belong to the framework rather than to Charon. This article lists them in order of cost, because the programme's history is a history of expensive work done in the wrong order.

The selection rule: a thread is worth keeping if a cheap test could change its status. A thread that needs a new theory before it can be tested is not a thread; it is a research programme. A thread whose test is a reading of a page already on disk is the cheapest kind and is listed first.

And the standing warning, from the register's own method notes. Every check below must be run in dimensioned units, never with $c = \hbar = 1$. An earlier reconstruction of (58) read $\xi^{2}c^{2}\rho^{2}$ on both sides where the printed equation has $\Omega_0^{2}c^{2}\rho^{2}$, and the error survived a numerical test because the test ran in units where $\xi = \Omega_0$ and the two forms coincide. The dimensional check separates them at once: $\Omega_0^{2}c^{2}\rho^{2}$ has the dimension of $E^{2}/c^{2}$, while $\xi^{2}c^{2}\rho^{2}$ is $[\mathrm{s}^{-2}]$. The lesson is the first item of method in this article.

1. The one prediction, and how to make it testable

Thread: $\dot G/G \simeq 5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$ (R5, S40). It is the only claim in the programme that could still be right without the rest being right, and it sits two to four orders below the present frontier: lunar-laser-ranging and binary-pulsar timing reach the $10^{-12}$–$10^{-13}$ yr$^{-1}$ level.

The cheapest next test is not observational; it is an internal one: close the derivation's open factor of 32. The re-scan resolved S40's equation and promoted the prediction to a derived one, but left the present rate open by a factor 32. That factor is a derivation gap in a written chain, checkable against the transcribed pages. If the factor closes, the prediction's value is fixed and the observational comparison becomes a clean one-sided statement; if it does not close, the honest report is that the chain gives an order of magnitude only.

The second-cheapest: state the prediction in the currency an experiment reports. LLR measures a secular change in the lunar orbit; pulsar timing measures a change in the binary's orbital period; neither reports $\dot G/G$ directly. The conversion is standard and one page of arithmetic, and until it is done the claim cannot be compared with anything.

What is not worth doing: waiting for the frontier to move by two orders. That is a decade-scale prospect and is not a reason to keep or drop the thread. The thread's value is that it exists and is stated, and the article that owns it is the one article in the list whose subject is a prediction rather than a fit.

2. The internal checks that would settle open numbers

Thread: the two printed values of $\xi$ (S27, S38, R3). The nucleon-mass chain stays within 1 % of its minimum across a factor 2,7 in $\omega$ and $d_0$, while the 1987 and 1983 $\xi$'s differ by 5,1 % — so the discriminating power sits in an input, and the check is to decide which $\xi$ is the corpus's own. This is a reading task: Esp 057's suggestion footnote and the 1983 density both bear on it, and the 1983 book's printed curve needs $6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ while its printed density needs $6{,}467\times10^{-4}$. Two internal inconsistencies, one page each, no new physics.

Thread: the 1977 first edition of Théorie de la relativité complexe is not in the corpus. It may hold the definition of $\gamma$ (S37) in clearer form, and $\gamma$ is the last unfixed quantity in the $\alpha$ chain. The cheapest test is a library or archive copy and one page read against S37's reconstruction. If the definition differs, S35's "+3,64 % on the coupling" is the number that moves.

Thread: the two regularities in the éon's table (S20). $\rho = \alpha/R^{3}$ reproduces both turning points with one constant across a factor 218 in $\rho$, and $\theta R = 3{,}76\times10^{-9}$ K cm agrees at the two ends to 0,14 %. Neither definition is transcribed, so this is a reading task in RC 046–052, not a scan task — the pages are already on disk. If the definitions are found, the éon's spectrum gains a second internal check and S20's tag can be raised.

Thread: $\Omega_0 = \tfrac43\pi l_0^{3}$ is the Euclidean ball, while the micro-universe's volume is the closed $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$ (RC 047, (5-41)). The two differ by a factor $3\pi/2$ at $K = +1$. The cheapest test is to read the definitions at (2-18-a) in RC 019–023, which are already transcribed. This matters because S50, S51 and S52 all use $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$, and a genuine discrepancy would touch the cosmology's bookkeeping.

And one method item, which costs nothing: run every equation check in dimensioned units. The (58) error is the worked example, and the rule is already in the register's method notes. It is listed here because it is the cheapest possible improvement to the programme's reliability.

4. The two structural questions, which are not the corpus's

Thread: the spin-two lead (S64). Charon's source tensor $T_{\alpha\beta} = \xi^{2}\rho^{2}u_\alpha u_\beta$ is symmetric rank-two, its symmetric traceless part must be carried, and the framework carries exactly that object through the metric's own device $h_{\mu\nu} = \langle\varepsilon_\mu,\delta\tilde E_\nu\rangle + \langle\delta\tilde E_\mu,\varepsilon_\nu\rangle$ with $16 = 10+6$. The open question: can a matter source be written as a frame perturbation? This is the rewrite's one research direction, and it is handed to the framework.

Thread: the inter-sector coupling (S62, §16.3). The framework has no dynamics coupling $\mathbb{M}_+$ to $\mathbb{M}_-$; the corpus's whole theory is a proposal for exactly that coupling. The open question: can $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2}+4\Lambda$ be given as a dynamics rather than an ansatz? This is the framework's open item and it is a research direction, not a computation.

Neither has a cheap test, and the honest report is that neither is testable yet. They are listed because they are the programme's two live structural contributions, and because a reader who follows the framework's side will need to know that they came from here. The cheapest thing that can be done with them is a one-page statement of what would count as success — the same move S6 made for the corpus, applied to the framework.

5. What to resurrect, and what not to

The register's layer rule is the criterion: a dead verdict at the level of a claim does not propagate to the level of the argument underneath it. Applied to the post-mortem block, the candidates sort as follows.

candidate layer (i) — the claim layer (iii) — the separable remainder worth doing?
P1 lepton tower dead at LEP the three real charged-lepton masses reproduce with $T_0$ fitted — a fit, retained as a fit yes, as a labelled fit
P4–P6 quark levels dead (identifications) the holon geometry (S24) survives on its own yes, separately audited
P9–P15 cosmology mostly dead the bounce (S51) is already kept as an idea yes, already done
P16 EPR dead as a mechanism the ψ-wave ontology (S54) is kept separately yes, already done
P3 electron size dead ($g-2$, point-like) none found no
P7 $W/Z$ as a neutrino pair dead none found no
P18 boson spins dead none found no
P20 fitted versus predicted not a claim but a rule alive, as method — it is what the register itself uses yes, and it is already in use

The pattern in the last two rows is the general one: the failures that were methods rather than claims are the ones that transfer. P20's sweep-and-report diagnosis is not a dead claim; it is a working rule that this whole assessment applies. The register's rules of engagement are largely composed of such transfers, which is the sense in which the programme's errors were productive.

And the explicit negative list, so that effort is not spent twice. Do not resurrect: the electron's internal structure (P3); the $W/Z$ identity (P7); the boson-spin identifications (P18); the lepton tower's tower (P1) — only its first three masses, and only as a fit; the antimatter era and the negative vacuum (P9, P10); the finite redshift (P13); the EPR mechanism (P16). Each of these has been tested and refused, and re-opening one without a new mechanism would repeat the programme's central error: continuing to work on what nature has already answered.

6. The work order, in one page

Cheapest first, and each item stated as a single action.

  1. Run every existing check in dimensioned units. No new source; finds errors of the (58) class. Cost: one pass over the checked equations.
  2. Read (2-18-a) in RC 019–023 for the volume definition ($\tfrac43\pi l_0^{3}$ versus $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$). Cost: one page.
  3. Read Esp 058's page for the tau row and the $\nu_\tau$ row. Cost: one page; closes the table.
  4. Read RC 060/061 for (4-11), the missing half of the éon/holon coupling. Cost: one page.
  5. Read the éon table's definitions in RC 046–052 for $\rho = \alpha/R^{3}$ and $\theta R = 3{,}76\times10^{-9}$ K cm. Cost: six pages; would raise S20's tag if found.
  6. Close S40's factor 32 in the $\dot G/G$ chain. Cost: one derivation against transcribed pages.
  7. Convert $\dot G/G$ into the currency of LLR and pulsar timing. Cost: one page of arithmetic.
  8. Decide which printed $\xi$ is the corpus's own (1983's $6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ or $6{,}467\times10^{-4}$). Cost: two readings; would fix R3's verdict.
  9. Find the 1977 first edition for $\gamma$'s definition. Cost: a library.
  10. Write the framework-side statement of what would count as success for the spin-two lead and the inter-sector coupling. Cost: one page, but it is a research document and not a reading.

Items 1–5 are reading tasks on pages already on disk. Item 6 is a derivation. Items 7–9 are bounded. Item 10 is the only one that opens new ground. A programme that spends a month on items 1–6 would close every internal question this assessment found open.

Summary

The remaining work is short and mostly cheap: five reading tasks on pages already on disk, one derivation to close the live prediction's open factor, three bounded external tasks, and two structural questions that belong to the framework.

The one prediction, $\dot G/G \simeq 5\times10^{-16}$ yr$^{-1}$, is made testable not by waiting for the experimental frontier but by closing the derivation's open factor of 32 and converting the result into the currency that LLR and pulsar timing actually report. The internal checks that would settle open numbers are: which of the two printed $\xi$'s is the corpus's own (R3's discriminating power sits there, a 5,1 % spread against a plateau of 1 %); the definition of $\gamma$ (which the 1977 first edition may carry and which fixes the $\alpha$ chain's last unfixed quantity); the éon table's two regularities (a reading in RC 046–052); and the volume definition $\tfrac43\pi l_0^{3}$ versus $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$, a factor $3\pi/2$ apart, settled by (2-18-a).

The archival tasks, in order of value: (4-11) in RC 060/061, the missing half of the éon/holon coupling; the thirty figure pages; Esp 058's five defects, of which the blank tau row matters most; and Esp 039's symbol collapse. All are readings of images already on disk. The write-back rule is stated once: mark true provenance, never alter what is legible, never touch the archives.

The two structural questions — can a matter source be written as a frame perturbation (the spin-two lead), and can $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2}+4\Lambda$ be given as a dynamics rather than an ansatz (the inter-sector coupling) — have no cheap test and are handed to the framework. The cheapest thing to do with them is a one-page statement of what would count as success, the same move S6 made for the corpus.

The resurrection list applies the layer rule: keep the lepton masses as a labelled fit, the holon geometry on its own, the bounce and the ψ-wave ontology as already separated, and the sweep-and-report rule as live method. Do not re-open the electron's size, the $W/Z$ identity, the boson spins, the tower, the antimatter era, the negative vacuum, the finite redshift or the EPR mechanism: each was tested and refused, and re-opening one without a new mechanism would repeat the programme's central error.

And the method item that costs nothing: run every check in dimensioned units, never with $c = \hbar = 1$. The (58) error is the worked example, and it is the cheapest possible improvement to the programme's reliability.

Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The antisymmetric quaternionic algebra of $\mathbb{H}$ carries the operation $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions. The parent quaternionic product has no zero divisor, the ambient algebra $\mathbb{H}$ being a division algebra; the antisymmetric block is at the opposite extreme. This article determines its zero divisors: the criterion, the annihilator, the rank of the multiplication,

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The two share the space, the addition and the real scalars, but $\diamond$ is a different multiplication, alternating and without a unit. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new. The symbol $\diamond$ is reserved for this block.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x^2=\vec e_y^2=\vec e_z^2=-e_t$. A generic element is $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$ with $\vec q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}q_k\vec e_k$ and $q_\mu\in\mathbb{R}$; $\vec p\cdot\vec q$ is the Euclidean dot product, $\vec p\times\vec q$ the vector product, the centre is $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_t$ and the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\vec q\}$; the quaternion form is $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q$.

The Notion and the Product

Definition. A nonzero element $\tilde p$ is a zero divisor of the block when there is a nonzero element $\tilde q$ with

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=0 . $$

Because the operation is alternating, $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$ for every $\tilde p$; hence every nonzero element is a zero divisor, with itself as partner on both sides, and the zero-divisor set is the punctured space $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$.

Theorem (the zero-divisor set is total). The set of zero-divisor elements is $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$, of real dimension four. The block is the opposite of the parent quaternionic product, whose only zero divisor is $0$, and agrees with its plain companion, the cross product.

Proof. For every $\tilde p\neq0$ the relation $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$ exhibits the partner $\tilde q=\tilde p$; no nonzero element is excluded.

The Criterion

Theorem (the criterion). For nonzero $\tilde p,\tilde q$,

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=0\iff p_0\vec q=q_t\vec p+\vec p\times\vec q \iff \tilde q\ \text{is a multiple of}\ \tilde p,\ \tilde q=\lambda\tilde p . $$

For a nonzero element the annihilating partners are exactly the multiples of the element.

Proof. The equation $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q=0$ is the displayed criterion. Let $\vec q=\alpha\vec p+\vec q_\perp$ with $\vec q_\perp\perp\vec p$; the criterion splits into the parallel part $(p_0\alpha-q_t)\vec p=0$ and the perpendicular part $p_0\vec q_\perp=\vec p\times\vec q_\perp$. The first gives $\alpha=q_t/p_0$ when $p_0\neq0$; the second is $p_0\vec q_\perp=|\vec p|R(\vec q_\perp)$ with $R$ a quarter turn of the plane orthogonal to $\vec p$, so that $(p_0/|\vec p|)\vec q_\perp=R(\vec q_\perp)$ would make the real number $p_0/|\vec p|$ an eigenvalue of $R$, and a quarter turn has no real eigenvalue; hence $\vec q_\perp=0$, so $\vec q=\alpha\vec p$ and $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$. For a pure $\tilde p$ the parallel part gives $q_t=0$ and the perpendicular part $\vec p\times\vec q=0$, so again $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$, then pure. The direct verification by the rank of the multiplication operator is below.

The Parallel and the Perpendicular Parts

Remark (why no perpendicular partner). The criterion couples the scalar direction and the vector part. The cross product contributes the perpendicular component alone, and the scalar part times the perpendicular part is compared with a quarter turn of it, a rotation of the plane orthogonal to $\vec p$; a quarter turn has no real eigenvector, so the perpendicular component of the partner is forced to vanish. Only the parallel component survives, and it fixes the ratio of the scalar parts. The partners are therefore the parallel pairs, and the perpendicular pairs are exactly the pairs that are not partners.

The Annihilator and the Rank

The annihilator of an element is the kernel of the left multiplication, the two sides coinciding because the block is alternating, and it is the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$ for a nonzero element. The annihilator, the operator with its characteristic polynomial, the rank table and the contrast with the cross product are the companion article Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category.

The Families and the Dimension Count

Theorem (the families). The zero-divisor pairs $(\tilde p,\tilde q)$ with $\tilde p,\tilde q\neq0$ and $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=0$ form two families:

$$ \text{(i) the pure pairs: } \tilde p=\vec q_a,\ \tilde q=\lambda\vec q_a\ \text{with}\ \vec q_a\neq0,\ \lambda\neq0 ; \qquad \text{(ii) the non-pure pairs: } \tilde q=\lambda\tilde p\ \text{with}\ \tilde p\ \text{non-pure},\ \lambda\neq0 . $$

No pair mixes a pure and a non-pure element.

Proof. By the criterion $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$. If $\tilde p$ is pure, $\tilde q$ is pure; if $\tilde p$ is non-pure, $\tilde q$ is non-pure for $\lambda\neq0$. The two families meet at no pair, the pure and the non-pure being exclusive.

Proposition (the dimension count). The zero-divisor set has real dimension four; the set of zero-divisor pairs has real dimension five; and the annihilator of a nonzero element is a line, of real dimension one.

Proof. The zero-divisor set is $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$, of dimension four. A pair is an element $\tilde p\neq0$, four parameters, together with its partner $\lambda\tilde p$, one parameter, giving a five-dimensional family; the second argument is not free, the annihilator being a line.

Summary

The antisymmetrised quaternionic block has no nontrivial divisor theory: the operation is alternating, so every nonzero element is a zero divisor with itself as partner and the zero-divisor set is the punctured four-dimensional space. The criterion for a pair is $p_0\vec q=q_t\vec p+\vec p\times\vec q$, which forces the partner to be a multiple of the element, $\tilde q=\lambda\tilde p$: the perpendicular component is killed by the quarter-turn comparison of the scalar and cross terms, and the annihilator is the line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$ for a nonzero element, cutting the multiplication operator down to rank three, with trace $3p_0$, vanishing determinant and characteristic polynomial $t(t-p_0)\bigl((t-p_0)^2+|\vec p|^2\bigr)$. The zero-divisor pairs split into the pure parallel pairs and the non-pure proportional pairs, of real dimension five. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the same criterion holds and the annihilator widens on the isotropic cone of the central square; over $\mathbb{R}$ the cone is empty and the annihilator is always the line.

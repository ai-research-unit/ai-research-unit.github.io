The $\beta$–$\gamma$ Chain — What Sets the Coupling in the Charon Corpus, and the Closed Form Behind It

Introduction

The companion article S35 recomputed the Charon corpus's derivation of the fine-structure constant and found that it holds: the corpus's printed $\alpha = 7{,}65\times10^{-3}$ comes back exactly from its own printed inputs, with an honest agreement of $+3{,}6\,\%$ on the coupling and $+1{,}8\,\%$ on the charge. It also found that the model has one input worth watching, the constant $\beta$, on which the coupling depends as $\alpha \propto \beta^{5/4}$ exactly — so that one significant figure in $\beta$ fixes the coupling to within 4 %. S35 could say no more about $\beta$, because $\beta$ arrives in the $\alpha$ chain by assertion: "obtenue par la résolution du Système $L$", obtained by solving the Système L.

This article traces $\beta$, and the tracing changes the picture. $\beta$ is not a free parameter and not a fitted one: the corpus gives it in closed form, and the closed form contains nothing but the three charged-lepton masses along with $h$ and $c$. The corpus's lepton equations $L7$, $L8$ and $L16$ — quoted at Esp 056 and combined at Esp 057 — yield $\beta$ exactly, and the same closed form is printed at RC (3-68). The result of the tracing is a one-line expression for the corpus's whole $\alpha$ claim:

$$\alpha \;\approx\; \frac{1}{4\pi^{3}}\cdot\frac{\tau - 2\mu + m}{\tau - \mu},$$

a pure number built from $4\pi^{3}$ and the charged-lepton mass ratios. That is what the corpus's electromagnetic model, its Système L and its lepton quantisation condition amount to when they are put together and simplified. The article derives it, checks it against both books, and asks what it is worth.

Four further findings, each stated where it belongs and gathered here so that the reader knows what is coming.

  1. $\beta$ has an exact closed form in the lepton masses, $\beta = -h(1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3}c)$ with $\varepsilon = (\mu-m)/(\tau-\mu)$ — and $\gamma = -c\beta/h$ is therefore the pure dimensionless number $(1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3})$.
  2. The corpus's second book independently confirms S35's $2\pi$ correction. RC 055 derives the same force with the coefficient $4\pi^{2}/3$, where Esp's printed (264) has $8\pi^{3}/3$. The two are the same stage of the same calculation, and RC's is the correct one.
  3. The corpus's three printed $\gamma$ values are explained. Esp's $7{,}716\times10^{-3}$ is reproduced exactly — using the corpus's own rounded $c = 3\times10^{10}$ and $h = 6{,}61\times10^{-27}$.
  4. A fitted shape parameter. RC 055 §4 introduces an eccentricity $E = 0{,}36$ chosen to make the charge come out exactly, and its own arithmetic does not close. This is the corpus's second instance of a post-hoc fit, and it is a stronger one than $\beta$.

Conventions. The corpus uses $\alpha$ and $\beta$ for two different pairs of quantities, and the collision matters. In the lepton sector — Esp 056–058, RC 051 — $\alpha$ is a mass, $-1{,}47\times10^{-25}$ g, and $\beta$ is a mass-length, $-1{,}7\times10^{-39}$ g cm; they are coefficients of the evolution equation and of $D_E = \beta c^{2}/R^{4}$. In the electromagnetic chain of Esp (245)–(268), $\alpha$ is the fine-structure constant $e^{2}/\hbar c$. This article writes $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$ for the fine-structure constant wherever the two could be confused, and $\beta$ always means the lepton-sector mass-length. Masses: $m$ is the electron, $\mu$ the muon and $\tau$ the tau, in cgs. Values quoted from the corpus keep the corpus's decimal comma.

What the Corpus Says $\beta$ Is

$\beta$ appears in the corpus twice over, and both appearances are needed.

On the electromagnetic side, (245) defines it implicitly through the radiation energy density,

$$D_{E} = \frac{\beta c^{2}}{R^{4}} = \pm a\theta^{4} \qquad (\text{Esp } 245),$$

so that $\beta$ is the coefficient that fixes the black-body energy as a function of the lepton radius. Dimensionally this makes $\beta$ a mass-length, g cm.

On the lepton side, $\beta$ is a coefficient of the evolution equation, and RC (3-68) prints it alongside its two companions:

$$(3\text{-}68)\quad \begin{cases} \alpha = \dfrac{m+\mu}{4\pi^{2}} - \dfrac{h}{\pi^{2}c^{2}T_{0}} = -1{,}46\cdot10^{-25}\ \mathrm{g},\\[2mm] \beta = \dfrac{(\mu-m)c^{2}T_{0} - 2h}{8\pi^{3}c} = -1{,}74\cdot10^{-39}\ \mathrm{g}\cdot\mathrm{cm},\\[2mm] T_{0} = \dfrac{2h}{c^{2}(\tau-\mu)} = 4{,}93\cdot10^{-24}\ \mathrm{s}. \end{cases}$$

The two $\beta$'s are the same quantity. Esp's $D_E = \beta c^{2}/R^{4}$ makes $\beta$ a mass-length, which is exactly RC's unit; Esp adopts $-1{,}7\times10^{-39}$ and RC prints $-1{,}74\times10^{-39}$; and the corpus's own solution table at Esp 058 lists $\beta = -1{,}7\times10^{-39}$ beside $\alpha = -1{,}472\times10^{-25}$, the same pair RC's (3-68) prints. So the corpus has one $\beta$, quoted to two figures, and RC (3-68) is where it comes from.

And RC (3-68) is a closed form, not a fit. Its right-hand sides contain $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$, $h$, $c$ and nothing else. That is the whole of this article's finding in one line, and the remainder of the article is the derivation, the simplification and the consequences.

$\beta$ Is Not a Free Parameter: $L7$, $L8$ and $L16$

The Three Equations

The Système L is stated at Esp 056 as a numbered list, and three of its entries do all the work here. In the corpus's notation,

$$L7:\quad \int_{0}^{T} 2\pi^{2}c^{2}\left(\alpha - \frac{\beta}{R}\right)dt + h = mc^{2}T,$$

$$L8:\quad \int_{0}^{T} 2\pi^{2}c^{2}\left(\alpha + \frac{\beta}{R}\right)dt + 2\left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)h = \tau_{n}c^{2}T,$$

$$L16:\quad \int_{0}^{T}\frac{dt}{R} = \frac{2\pi}{c},$$

with $n = 0$ the electron, $n = 1$ the muon and $n = 2$ the tau. Esp 057 §2 states the method in one sentence: "on commence par calculer $\alpha$ par addition de $L7$ et $L8$, puis $\beta$ par soustraction de $L7$ à $L8$, en se servant de $L16$." The addition gives $\alpha$, the subtraction gives $\beta$, and $L16$ supplies the integral that closes both.

The Difference Gives $\beta$

Subtracting $L7$ from $L8$ at $n = 1$ — the muon — the $\alpha$ terms cancel and the two powers of $R$ add:

$$\int_{0}^{T}4\pi^{2}c^{2}\,\frac{\beta}{R}\,dt + 2\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)h - h = \mu c^{2}T \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4\pi^{2}c^{2}\beta\int_{0}^{T}\frac{dt}{R} + 2h = \mu c^{2}T - mc^{2}T .$$

With $L16$, $\int dt/R = 2\pi/c$, so the first term is $8\pi^{3}c\beta$ and

$$\boxed{\ \beta = \frac{(\mu-m)c^{2}T - 2h}{8\pi^{3}c}\ }\qquad\text{— RC (3-68), exactly.}$$

The corpus's closed form is $L8 - L7$ with $L16$. Nothing is fitted, and no further input enters. So $\beta$ is not fitted and not free: it is a closed form in the three lepton masses.

The Addition Gives $\alpha$, and the Lepton Pair Gives $T$

The same two equations yield the corpus's lepton-sector $\alpha$. Adding $L7$ and $L8$ at $n = 1$, the $\beta$ terms cancel:

$$\int_{0}^{T}4\pi^{2}c^{2}\alpha\,dt + h + 3h = (m+\mu)c^{2}T \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha = \frac{m+\mu}{4\pi^{2}} - \frac{h}{\pi^{2}c^{2}T},$$

again RC (3-68) exactly. And the period follows from $L8$ taken at $n = 2$ and $n = 1$, where the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ terms cancel identically and only the quantisation terms survive:

$$2\left(2+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)h - 2\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)h = (\tau-\mu)c^{2}T \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_{0} = \frac{2h}{c^{2}(\tau-\mu)} .$$

This is the third line of (3-68), and the corpus's own note at Esp 057 says exactly this — "$L8$, considéré avec $n=1$ et $n=2$, donne aussi $T = 2h/c^{2}(\tau-\mu)$."

So all three of RC (3-68)'s outputs are $L7$, $L8$ and $L16$. Evaluated with the corpus's 1983 masses ($m = 9{,}11\times10^{-28}$, $\mu = 1{,}884\times10^{-25}$, $\tau$ inferred at $3{,}179\times10^{-24}$ g from the corpus's own $T_{0}$), the three reproduce the corpus's printed values:

(3-68) output corpus prints recomputed agreement
$T_{0}$ $4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s $4{,}9301\times10^{-24}$ s exact to the printed figures
$\alpha$ (lepton sector) $-1{,}46\times10^{-25}$ g $-1{,}4672\times10^{-25}$ g $0{,}5\,\%$
$\beta$ $-1{,}74\times10^{-39}$ g cm $-1{,}6704\times10^{-39}$ g cm $4{,}0\,\%$

The first two close, and the third does not. $\beta$ is the corpus's own formula evaluated at the corpus's own masses, and it misses the printed $-1{,}74$ by 4 %. This is the discrepancy the scan-risk register already records; this article confirms it and now explains what it costs, in the section on the eccentricity and in the one on the coupling.

The Closed Form, Exactly

The three lines of (3-68) simplify further, and the simplification is worth doing because it turns the corpus's numerical claim into an algebraic one.

Eliminate $T_{0}$ from $\beta$. Substituting $T_{0} = 2h/[c^{2}(\tau-\mu)]$ into $\beta = [(\mu-m)c^{2}T_{0} - 2h]/(8\pi^{3}c)$,

$$(\mu-m)c^{2}T_{0} = \frac{2h(\mu-m)}{\tau-\mu}, \qquad \beta = \frac{2h\left[\dfrac{\mu-m}{\tau-\mu} - 1\right]}{8\pi^{3}c} = -\frac{h}{4\pi^{3}c}\left(1-\varepsilon\right), \qquad \varepsilon \equiv \frac{\mu-m}{\tau-\mu}.$$

So the corpus's $\beta$ is $h/(4\pi^{3}c)$, times a single lepton-mass ratio. Verified identically: the two expressions for $\beta$ agree to all digits of the working precision.

The ratio is small, and that is the point. With the measured masses $\varepsilon = 0{,}0627$, so

$$\beta = -1{,}7821\times10^{-39}\times(1-0{,}0627) = -1{,}6704\times10^{-39}\ \mathrm{g\ cm},$$

against $-h/(4\pi^{3}c) = -1{,}7821\times10^{-39}$. The corpus's $\beta$ is a combination of $h$ and $c$ alone, corrected by 6 % for the lepton spectrum. That is why it is so insensitive to the masses: recomputing $\beta$ with modern CODATA lepton masses instead of the corpus's 1983 values shifts it by $0{,}02\,\%$, from $-1{,}6704$ to $-1{,}6700\times10^{-39}$.

This settles S35's open question about the provenance of $\beta$. S35 could only say that $\beta$ comes from the Système L and that the Système L's seeds include $e$. The tracing shows something sharper and, for the corpus, better: $\beta$ does not depend on $e$ at all. It is a function of $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$, $h$ and $c$. The charge $e$ is a seed of the Système L, but it is not a seed of $\beta$.

$\gamma$, and Why the Corpus's Three Printed Values Differ

$\gamma$ is defined at Esp (263) and RC (3-103) as a rescaling of $\beta$:

$$\gamma = -\frac{c\beta}{h} \qquad (\text{Esp }263,\ \text{RC }3\text{-}103).$$

Substituting the closed form gives $\gamma$ exactly, with no dimensional input left:

$$\gamma = -\frac{c}{h}\cdot\left[-\frac{h}{4\pi^{3}c}(1-\varepsilon)\right] = \frac{1-\varepsilon}{4\pi^{3}} = \frac{\tau-2\mu+m}{4\pi^{3}(\tau-\mu)} .$$

Verified identically. Two consequences, and the second is the more important.

First, $\gamma$ is dimensionless in every unit system, and not by cancellation of cgs. The corpus's own quantity $c\beta/h$ has dimensions $(\mathrm{cm/s})(\mathrm{g\ cm})/(\mathrm{g\ cm^{2}/s})$, which is $1$ — the units cancel identically, in cgs, SI or any other system, because $\beta$ and $h/c$ carry the same dimension. The register's note that $\gamma$ "lands near $\alpha$ by a coincidence of cgs bookkeeping" is therefore wrong and has been corrected. Both $\gamma$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$ are pure numbers, and their nearness is a statement about numbers, not about units. The corrected account of the nearness is in the next section.

Second, $\gamma$ depends on the lepton spectrum through one ratio, and barely at all. With $\varepsilon = 0{,}0627$, $1-\varepsilon = 0{,}9373$ and

$$\gamma = \frac{0{,}9373}{4\pi^{3}} = \frac{0{,}9373}{124{,}025} = 7{,}5574\times10^{-3}.$$

Now the three printed values are all explained.

So S35's unexplained $0{,}3\,\%$ gap between Esp's $7{,}716\times10^{-3}$ and the CODATA recomputation $7{,}6915\times10^{-3}$ is the corpus's rounding of $c$ and $h$, and nothing else. Esp (263) is not arithmetically wrong; it is arithmetically right at the precision of the table it used.

One caution, which the $\alpha$ chain makes necessary. The three values are not interchangeable, because the coupling depends on $\gamma^{5/4}$: the exact $\gamma$ is $2{,}1\,\%$ below Esp's printed $\gamma$, and that difference propagates into the coupling as $1{,}25\times2{,}1\,\% = 2{,}6\,\%$. An article that mixes the printed $\gamma$ with the closed-form $\beta$ will misstate the model's agreement by a couple of points, in the corpus's favour. The four-row grid of S35 is the safe form, and this is the reason.

A Second Book, and the Disappearance of the $2\pi$

The corpus derives the same force twice, in two books, and the two versions settle S35's $2\pi$ question.

S35 found that Esp's printed (264) carries a spurious factor $2\pi$: as transcribed it reads $\overline{F} = (8\pi^{3}ch/3r^{2})(15\gamma/8\pi^{5})^{5/4}\int$, where the re-derivation gives $4\pi^{2}ch/3$ at that stage, the corpus's own (266) confirming $4\pi^{2}/3$ by its prefactor $8\pi^{3}/3 = (4\pi^{2}/3)(2\pi)$. The claim was made on internal consistency, and S35 could not check it against the printed page.

RC 055 §3 derives the same quantity with the coefficient printed correctly. Its equation (3-101) reads

$$(3\text{-}101)\quad \overline{F} = \frac{4\pi^{2}}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{h^{4}c^{4}}\cdot\frac{K^{5}}{r^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}R^{5}\theta^{5}\int_{1{,}91}^{\infty}\frac{x^{4}}{e^{x}-1}\,dx\,d\tau,$$

with the coefficient $4\pi^{2}/3$ — the value S35's re-derivation requires. RC then collapses $K^{5}$ and $R^{5}\theta^{5}$ into the same $(15\gamma/8\pi^{5})^{5/4}$ of the same two constants, and the $h \to \hbar$ step introduces the $2\pi$ that turns $4\pi^{2}/3$ into $8\pi^{3}/3$ — which is what RC's (3-104) and (3-106) then carry. So RC's chain contains the $2\pi$ once, where it belongs, and Esp's (264) as transcribed contains it twice.

This is an independent corroboration, and it is the strongest kind available without the printed page. The question S35 left open was whether Esp's (264) is a misprint in the 1983 book or a slip in the recovered text. RC 055 does not answer that directly, but it establishes beyond arithmetic doubt that the $8\pi^{3}ch/3$ reading of (264) is not what the corpus's own calculation intends, because the same author, in the same programme, wrote $4\pi^{2}/3$ at the same stage. The correction stands, and the remaining question — misprint or transcription — stays open.

The two books also disagree in their numbers, harmlessly. RC's integral is $23{,}46$ from the lower limit $x_l = 1{,}91$, where Esp's is $23{,}6$ from $x_1 = 1{,}92$; RC's $\gamma$ is $7{,}72\times10^{-3}$ where Esp's is $7{,}716\times10^{-3}$; and RC's printed result is $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}} = 7{,}60\times10^{-3}$ against Esp's $7{,}65\times10^{-3}$. Recomputed, RC's combination gives $7{,}609\times10^{-3}$, reproducing its printed value. And RC states its own error the same way Esp does — "à $1{,}9$ pour cent près", with (3-108) comparing the charges $e_{\mathrm{calc}} = 4{,}89\times10^{-10}$ and $e_{\mathrm{obs}} = 4{,}80\times10^{-10}$, whose ratio is $+1{,}88\,\%$, while the coupling ratio printed immediately above is $+4{,}11\,\%$. Both books state the charge error, and neither states the coupling error, which is twice as large. S35's central distinction therefore holds for the corpus as a whole and not for one page of it.

The $\alpha$ Chain in One Line

The closed form of $\gamma$, substituted into S35's final equation, collapses the corpus's $\alpha$ claim to a single expression. From S35,

$$\alpha_{\mathrm{f}} = \frac{8\pi^{3}}{3}\left(\frac{15\gamma}{8\pi^{5}}\right)^{5/4}I, \qquad \gamma = \frac{\tau-2\mu+m}{4\pi^{3}(\tau-\mu)}, \qquad I = 23{,}4323 .$$

Evaluating the prefactor, one finds

$$\alpha_{\mathrm{f}} = 0{,}9863\,\gamma = 0{,}9863\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi^{3}}\cdot\frac{\tau-2\mu+m}{\tau-\mu},$$

verified: the ratio of the model's output to $\gamma$ is $0{,}986254$, so the corpus's entire electromagnetic chain is, to $1{,}4\,\%$, the statement

$$\boxed{\ \alpha_{\mathrm{f}} \;\approx\; \frac{1}{4\pi^{3}}\cdot\frac{\tau-2\mu+m}{\tau-\mu}\ }$$

— a pure number times a ratio of charged-lepton masses, with no measured constant anywhere in it except the masses.

Three things follow, and they are the article's assessment of the claim.

First, the scale of the claim is a pure number. Since $\varepsilon = 0{,}0627$ is small, the model is essentially asserting

$$\alpha_{\mathrm{f}} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi^{3}} = \frac{1}{124{,}03} \quad\text{against the measured}\quad \frac{1}{137{,}04},$$

and then correcting the $+10{,}5\,\%$ miss down to $+2\,\%$ through the lepton ratio and the prefactor. The corpus's electromagnetic model is, at bottom, a claim that $\alpha$ is $1/4\pi^{3}$ — the reciprocal of $4\pi^{3}$, a number built out of the geometry of the interaction — brought closer by the lepton spectrum. Whether that is deep or numerological is a judgement the reader can now make on the correct evidence, which is this formula and not the printed $2{,}3\,\%$.

Second, the fit's depth is now visible and it is one formula against one number. The model maps two measured dimensionless ratios — $m/\tau$ and $\mu/\tau$, or equivalently the single combination $\varepsilon$ — onto one measured dimensionless number, $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$. There is one relation and one target, and the relation's form comes from the corpus's own axioms: the $\pi^{2}$'s from $L7$ and $L8$, the $2\pi$ from $L16$, the $4\pi^{3}$ from the combination, the $\tfrac{5}{4}$ power from the Stefan–Boltzmann chain, and the $23{,}4323$ from a universal integral. The formula could have been otherwise in every one of those places, and it succeeds to $2$–$3\,\%$. That is not a derivation of $\alpha$, and it is not nothing: it is a non-trivial numerical relation between the charged-lepton spectrum and the electromagnetic coupling, produced by a specific and independently motivated construction.

Third, and this is where S35's verdict needs refining. S35 said that the comparison at the end of the chain is a consistency check of the Système L's own seed set, because both sides trace to the same seeds. The tracing corrects that in the corpus's favour. $\beta$ depends on $m$, $\mu$ and $\tau$ and not on $e$; the model's side of the comparison therefore uses no electromagnetic quantity at all. The comparison is between the charged-lepton spectrum and $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$, two independently measured sectors — and $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$ enters the lepton masses only through radiative corrections three orders below the agreement being claimed. So the chain is not circular in the way S35 said. What it is, is a formula of the corpus's own construction that takes one measured sector and reproduces an independent measured number to $2$–$3\,\%$.

And that changes the shape of the verdict without softening it. S35 concluded that the result is a fit. The tracing shows the more precise statement: the result is not a fit to a free parameter, because there is no free parameter — but neither is it a derivation, because the formula is postulated rather than derived. The corpus's own axioms, $L7$, $L8$ and $L16$, are chosen; $\alpha$ follows from them; and the agreement is the agreement of those axioms with nature. That is the honest description, and it is a genuine improvement on "fitted": a postulated law that reproduces an independent constant to 2 % is a candidate, not a curve-fit.

One numerical consequence of the tracing, in the corpus's favour. Esp adopts $\beta = -1{,}7\times10^{-39}$ for the $\alpha$ chain, but its own Système L and RC's own closed form give $-1{,}6704\times10^{-39}$. Carrying the derived value through S35's formula gives $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}} = 7{,}4535\times10^{-3}$, an error of $+2{,}11\,\%$ on the coupling against $+4{,}38\,\%$ for the adopted value. Charon's own formula was worth two percentage points, and he did not collect them, because he rounded $\beta$ to one significant figure before using it.

The Eccentricity: a Fitted Shape Parameter, and the Gap in It

RC 055 §4 is the corpus's clearest instance of tuning, and it is not $\beta$.

Having obtained $e_{\mathrm{calc}} = 4{,}89\times10^{-10}$ against $e_{\mathrm{obs}} = 4{,}80\times10^{-10}$, RC does not leave the $1{,}9\,\%$ standing. It attributes it to the spherical symmetry of the model, and repairs it by making the éon a spheroid:

$$(3\text{-}110)\quad \overline{\sigma} = \frac{\pi R^{2}}{2}(1-E^{2})^{1/2} = \frac{\pi rR}{2}, \qquad k \equiv \frac{r}{R} = (1-E^{2})^{1/2},$$

and then states that "le même calcul que ci-dessus, utilisant cette nouvelle section efficace (3-110), fournit alors la valeur expérimentale exacte de la charge $e$ pour"

$$(3\text{-}111)\quad \frac{r}{R} = 0{,}933, \qquad (3\text{-}112)\quad E = 0{,}36 .$$

The structure is a fit, and RC presents it as one. The eccentricity is not derived; it is chosen so that the charge comes out exactly. This is a free shape parameter introduced to absorb a numerical disagreement, and it is a stronger instance of the phenomenon than $\beta$ — because $\beta$, though it needed tracing, turned out to be determined.

And on the flat reading of (3-110) the arithmetic does not close. Since the force is proportional to the mean cross-section, the eccentricity multiplies $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$ by $k$; to bring $e$ from $4{,}89$ to $4{,}80\times10^{-10}$ exactly requires $k = 0{,}9578$, and to bring $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$ to $1/137$ exactly requires $k = 0{,}9593$. RC prints $0{,}933$, which is $2{,}7\,\%$ away, and would put $e$ at $4{,}74\times10^{-10}$ — below the observed value, turning a $+1{,}9\,\%$ error into a $-1{,}3\,\%$ one.

Open, and worth one sentence of honest statement. Either the eccentricity enters the force somewhere besides (3-110) — a spheroid's volume scales as $k^{2}$ and would give a different dependence — or the printed $0{,}933$ is a slip for $\approx0{,}96$. On the reading of (3-110) the second is the likelier; but the article does not claim it, because the dependence of the corpus's force on the éon's shape is the corpus's own to define and (3-110) may not be the whole of it. What is not in doubt is the structural finding: the corpus's best agreement is obtained by fitting a shape. The eccentricity is recorded here so that the $1{,}9\,\%$ of RC and the $2{,}3\,\%$ of Esp are not quoted as if no parameter had been spent on them.

Summary

The corpus's $\beta$ is not a free parameter, and the tracing settles S35's open question. $\beta$ is the difference $L8 - L7$ of the Système L with $L16$ supplying the closing integral, and the same closed form is printed at RC (3-68): $\beta = [(\mu-m)c^{2}T_{0} - 2h]/(8\pi^{3}c)$ with $T_{0} = 2h/[c^{2}(\tau-\mu)]$. Evaluated at the corpus's own masses it gives $-1{,}6704\times10^{-39}$ g cm against the printed $-1{,}74\times10^{-39}$ — the formula and the printed figure disagree by $4{,}0\,\%$, which is the discrepancy the scan-risk register records and which this article now prices.

The closed form simplifies to two exact expressions. $\beta = -h(1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3}c)$ and $\gamma = -c\beta/h = (1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3}) = (\tau-2\mu+m)/(4\pi^{3}(\tau-\mu))$, with $\varepsilon = (\mu-m)/(\tau-\mu) = 0{,}0627$ — both verified identically. So $\beta$ is $h/(4\pi^{3}c)$ corrected by 6 % for the lepton spectrum, and $\gamma$ is a pure dimensionless number built from $4\pi^{3}$ and the charged-lepton mass ratios. $\gamma$'s units cancel identically in any system, which corrects the register's "cgs bookkeeping" note: the nearness of $\gamma$ to $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$ is a statement about numbers. Esp's printed $7{,}716\times10^{-3}$ is reproduced exactly from the corpus's rounded table values $c = 3\times10^{10}$ and $h = 6{,}61\times10^{-27}$, which is the whole of the $0{,}3\,\%$ gap S35 could not explain.

The corpus's second book confirms S35's $2\pi$ correction. RC 055 §3, deriving the same force, prints the coefficient $4\pi^{2}/3$ at the stage where Esp's (264) carries $8\pi^{3}/3$; RC then introduces the $2\pi$ once, at the $h\to\hbar$ step, and its (3-104) and (3-106) carry $8\pi^{3}/3$. The corpus's own later calculation requires S35's correction, and the only question left is whether Esp's slip is in the book or in the recovered text.

The whole $\alpha$ claim collapses to one line. $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}} = 0{,}9863\,\gamma$, so the corpus's electromagnetic model, its Système L and its lepton quantisation together assert

$$\alpha_{\mathrm{f}} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi^{3}}\cdot\frac{\tau-2\mu+m}{\tau-\mu} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi^{3}} = \frac{1}{124{,}03},$$

corrected down from a $+10{,}5\,\%$ miss to $+2\,\%$ by the lepton ratio and the prefactor. That is the correct form of the corpus's claim, and it is stated here so that it can be judged.

S35's verdict is refined, in the corpus's favour. $\beta$ depends on $m$, $\mu$ and $\tau$ and not on $e$, so the model's side of the comparison uses no electromagnetic quantity: the chain relates the charged-lepton spectrum to $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$, two independently measured sectors, and is not circular as S35 stated. But it is still not a derivation: $L7$, $L8$ and $L16$ are postulated, the $\pi$-powers and the $\tfrac{5}{4}$ power and the integral come from that postulation, and every one of them could have been otherwise. The precise verdict is therefore neither "a fit to a free parameter" nor "a computation", but this: a postulated law, with no free parameter left in it once the lepton masses are measured, that reproduces an independent constant to 2 %.

And the corpus's best result was available to it and not taken. Esp adopts $\beta = -1{,}7\times10^{-39}$ after rounding, where its own Système L gives $-1{,}6704\times10^{-39}$; carrying the derived value gives $+2{,}11\,\%$ on the coupling instead of $+4{,}38\,\%$. RC 055 §4 then spends a free parameter — an éon eccentricity $E = 0{,}36$, chosen, not derived — to convert the residual into exactness, and on the flat reading of (3-110) its own printed $r/R = 0{,}933$ does not close, needing $\approx0{,}96$. The eccentricity, not $\beta$, is where the corpus's fitting is visible.

Summary of Notation

  • $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ — in the lepton sector (Esp 056–058, RC 051, RC (3-68)) these are the evolution-equation coefficients: $\alpha$ a mass ($-1{,}47\times10^{-25}$ g, also the maximum lepton density unit), $\beta$ a mass-length ($-1{,}7\times10^{-39}$ g cm, the coefficient of $D_{E} = \beta c^{2}/R^{4}$), $T_{0}$ the pulsation period ($4{,}93\times10^{-24}$ s). None of them is a coupling.
  • $\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}$ — the fine-structure constant $e^{2}/\hbar c$, the subject of Esp (245)–(268) and RC 055 §3. The collision of the two $\alpha$'s is the corpus's, not this article's; the subscript is added here to remove it.
  • $\gamma$ — the rescaled coefficient $\gamma = -c\beta/h$ of Esp (263) and RC (3-103), dimensionless. Exact closed form $\gamma = (1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3})$.
  • $\varepsilon$ — the single lepton-spectrum combination $\varepsilon = (\mu-m)/(\tau-\mu)$, equal to $0{,}0627$. This is the only way the lepton masses enter the corpus's $\alpha$ claim.
  • $L7$, $L8$, $L16$ — the corpus's lepton quantisation equations: $L7$ and $L8$ are the mass equations for $n$ and the spin term $2(n+\tfrac{1}{2})h$, and $L16$ is the closure relation $\int_{0}^{T}dt/R = 2\pi/c$. Their sum is $\alpha$, their difference is $\beta$, and $L8(n{=}2)-L8(n{=}1)$ is $T_{0}$.
  • $k$, $E$ — the éon's shape parameters at RC (3-110)–(3-112): $k = r/R$ the axial ratio and $E$ the eccentricity, with $k = (1-E^{2})^{1/2}$. Introduced at RC 055 §4 as a fit.
  • "Reproduced exactly" — used here in the strict sense: a printed figure recovered to its printed precision from its printed inputs. Esp's $\gamma = 7{,}716\times10^{-3}$ is reproduced exactly this way by the table's rounded $c$ and $h$.

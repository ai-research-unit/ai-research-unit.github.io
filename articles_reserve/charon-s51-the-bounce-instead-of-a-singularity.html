The Bounce Instead of a Singularity: A Live Idea in an Implementation That Cannot Produce It

Introduction

The corpus's universe has no beginning in the singular sense. At $\tau = 0$ its spatial radius is not zero: it is $R_{i} = 6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm, the expansion rate $R' = dR/d\tau$ vanishes there, and the density and the temperature are finite. RC 083 §1 states it as a contrast with general relativity — "notre modèle cosmologique, à l'inverse des modèles déduits de la Relativité générale, n'était pas « frappé » d'une singularité à rayon nul (et température et densité infinies) à l'origine des âges $\tau=0$" — and the register's §8.2 tags it "live idea, non-standard implementation".

Both halves of that tag are correct, and this article supplies the missing quantification of the second. The idea is live: bouncing cosmologies are respectable and actively pursued, in loop quantum cosmology, in string-gas and ekpyrotic models, and in Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology. The implementation is not — and the reason is not a matter of taste. Charon's bounce happens at a temperature of $7615$ K and a density of order $10^{-20}$ g cm$^{-3}$, where the respected bounces happen at the Planck density, $5\times10^{93}$ g cm$^{-3}$, and the Planck temperature. That is a gap of $113$ orders of magnitude in density and $28$ in temperature, and it says something exact: the corpus's bounce needs no quantum gravity, and therefore nothing protects it.

And the one ingredient it does need is the one observation has since measured against it. The corpus's existence conditions require a negative cosmological constant, $\Lambda \le 0$ (RC (3-69-2)), and its vacuum density is negative by construction ($\rho = \alpha/R^{3}$ with $\alpha < 0$). The vacuum energy has been measured since, and it is positive, by some $200\times$ the corpus's magnitude and of the opposite sign (§8.5). So the bounce is bought with a sign that the data have settled the other way.

The verdict is therefore two-sided and neither side is vague. The corpus anticipated a real alternative to the initial singularity, and committed to a number for it. But the specific bounce is a turning point of a classical pulsation at stellar-surface temperatures, resting on the sign of a term that is now measured, and it makes no prediction that could test it — no perturbation theory, hence no CMB signature. S40's prediction inherits its starting value from this radius; this article is where that radius is examined.

Conventions. $\tau$ is the corpus's cosmic age parameter, $R(\tau)$ the spatial radius of the Réel, and $R' = dR/d\tau$ its rate of change. $R_{i} = 6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm is the minimum (bounce) radius; $R_{a} = 1{,}6\times10^{28}$ cm the present radius; $R = 1{,}4\times10^{30}$ cm the maximum, at $T/2 = 2371$ Gyr. $\alpha$, $\beta$ are the cosmological coefficients of RC (5-68), $\alpha = -1{,}30\times10^{53}$ g and $\beta = +3{,}712\times10^{79}$ g cm — not the Système E's $\alpha$, $\beta$ of the lepton sector, nor the fine-structure constant. Densities are in g cm$^{-3}$; $\rho$ is always the vacuum density $\alpha/R^{3}$.

One correction to the register's own note, because a number in it is wrong and this article is the place to fix it. §8.2 records $R_{i} = 6\times10^{24}$ cm as "$\approx 1{,}9$ Gpc" and as "$\approx 0{,}44$ of today's Hubble radius". $6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm is $1{,}946$ Mpc — a factor $1000$ smaller — and the ratio to the Hubble radius is $3{,}6\times10^{-4}$ using the corpus's own $H_{a} = 1{,}8\times10^{-18}$ s$^{-1}$, or $4{,}4\times10^{-4}$ using the modern $H_{0}$. Both of the register's figures are the same single slip, since the ratio is $0{,}44$ exactly when the radius is read as Gpc. Corrected here; the physical point the note was making is unaffected, and in fact strengthened — the bounce radius is two-million light-years, not two billion.

The Four Findings

1. The idea is live, and the corpus reached it in 1983

A non-singular bounce is not an eccentricity; it is one of the two live programmes in early-universe cosmology. Loop quantum cosmology replaces the singularity with a bounce at the Planck density, driven by the area gap of the quantum geometry (Ashtekar, Pawłowski and Singh, 2006). Ekpyrotic and string-gas models put a brane collision or a gas of wrapped strings at the turnaround (Khoury, Ovrut, Steinhardt and Turok, 2001). Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology takes the remote future of one aeon as the big bang of the next (2010). All of them are answers to the same question Charon was asking in 1983, and all of them are taken seriously. The corpus deserves the credit the tag gives it and this article records it.

And the corpus's bounce has the property that makes a bounce a bounce, which is worth stating because it is easy to assert and rarely to check. $R' = 0$ at $\tau = 0$: the expansion rate vanishes at the minimum, so the singularity is replaced by a turning point. The density and the temperature are finite there because the radius is finite. Nothing in the corpus's early history diverges, and the claim is exact rather than approximate.

1.1 And the bounce radius is derived, not chosen — verified here

Added after S52's recomputation, and it belongs in S51 because $R_{i}$ is this article's number.

$R_{i}$ is not a free parameter of the corpus. It is fixed by the corpus's own equation of motion. The cosmological (5-17) is

$$\frac{R'^{2}}{c^{2}} = -\frac{1}{6}\frac{\xi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{R^{4}} + K + \frac{1}{3}\Lambda R^{2} + \frac{A}{R^{2}} ,$$

and $R_{i}$ is the radius where $R' = 0$. At $R_{i}$ the two terms that dominate are the curvature term $K = +1$ and the $\xi^{2}\alpha^{2}/R^{4}$ term — the $\Lambda$ and $A$ terms are smaller by eleven and a hundred orders respectively — so the turning point is at the near-exact cancellation

$$R_{i}^{4} = \frac{\xi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{6} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad R_{i} = \sqrt{\frac{|\xi\alpha|}{\sqrt{6}}} .$$

With the corpus's own $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ and $\alpha = -1{,}30\times10^{53}$ g this gives $R_{i} = 6{,}0074\times10^{24}$ cm against the printed $6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm — agreement to $0{,}02\,\%$. And inverting it, the printed $R_{i}$ and $\alpha$ imply $\xi = 6{,}797\times10^{-4}$ cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ against the lepton sector's $6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ — agreement to $0{,}05\,\%$, and better than $\xi$'s own two-figure precision, which means the relation is the equation the corpus solved rather than a coincidence.

Three consequences.

  • The bounce radius is a derived number. It is not seeded; it follows from $\xi$ (a lepton-sector input) and $\alpha$ (a cosmological output). So S34's ledger gains nothing here and loses nothing: $R_{i}$ is one of the corpus's genuine outputs, and it is the cleanest derived cosmological length it has.
  • The residual is worth reading exactly. $f(R_{i}) = -9{,}5\times10^{-4}$ against terms of order unity — so the printed $R_{i}$ sits one part in a thousand inside the true turning point. That is not an error: it is the rounding of $\alpha$ to three figures and $\xi$ to two, and it is why S52 can integrate the model's own equation and recover the corpus's half-period to $2\,\%$.
  • And the relation ties two sectors together. $\xi$ is fixed in the lepton sector (RC 051 (3-70), $l_{0} = R_{H} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm) and $\alpha$ in the cosmology (5-68). That the two satisfy $\xi^{2}\alpha^{2} = 6R_{i}^{4}$ to five figures is the strongest cross-sector consistency this programme has found — a genuine point in the corpus's favour, and one no register entry currently records.

2. The bounce happens where nothing can produce one

This is the finding, and it is a comparison of two numbers.

loop quantum cosmology / ekpyrotic the Charon corpus
density at the bounce $\sim5\times10^{93}$ g cm$^{-3}$ (Planck) $\sim10^{-20}$ g cm$^{-3}$
temperature at the bounce $\sim10^{32}$ K (Planck) $7615$ K
what drives it quantum geometry / new degrees of freedom at the Planck scale the sign of the vacuum density

The gap is $113$ orders of magnitude in density and $28$ in temperature, and it is not a detail — it is the whole question. The respected bounces are quantum-gravity effects: they happen precisely where general relativity is expected to fail, and their existence is a prediction of a theory that says what happens there. Charon's bounce happens at $7615$ K and $10^{-20}$ g cm$^{-3}$ — the temperature of a cool star's surface, and a density about $10^{9}$ times the present mean density of the universe. At those values, general relativity is supposed to hold to extraordinary accuracy, and nothing in known physics has any reason to bounce.

So the corpus avoids the singularity without quantum gravity, and that is a liability, not an economy. A bounce at the Planck scale is protected by the fact that the theory breaks down there; a bounce at $7615$ K is a claim that the classical equations themselves turn around, which means the turning must come from the corpus's own modification of them — and it does. Which leads directly to finding 3.

3. The bounce rests on the sign of the vacuum energy, and that sign is measured

The corpus's own existence conditions settle the sign, and it is negative. RC (3-69-1) and (3-69-2) impose, as conditions for a solution of the Système E to exist at all,

$$(3\text{-}69\text{-}2)\quad \Lambda = \frac{\xi^{2}}{R_{0}^{6}}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}} - \alpha^{2}\right) - \frac{3}{R_{0}^{2}} \;\le\; 0,$$

"conditions qui impliquent d'obtenir une valeur négative de la constante « cosmologique » $\Lambda$". And the cosmology carries the same sign by construction: $\rho = \alpha/R^{3}$ with $\alpha = -1{,}30\times10^{53}$ g, so the vacuum density is negative at every epoch — $-6{,}0\times10^{-22}$ g cm$^{-3}$ at the bounce, $-3{,}2\times10^{-32}$ g cm$^{-3}$ today (S40's re-scan).

The measurement has gone the other way. The accelerated expansion discovered in 1998 (Riess; Perlmutter) is described by a positive vacuum energy density, of order $6\times10^{-30}$ g cm$^{-3}$ equivalent — about $200\times$ the corpus's present magnitude and, more decisively, the opposite sign. So the single ingredient the corpus's bounce needs — a negative $\Lambda$ — is the quantity the last three decades of cosmology have measured with increasing precision, and it has the sign that forbids the corpus's mechanism.

This is a stronger statement than "the bounce is not observed". A cold bounce is not excluded by any direct search; what is excluded is the ingredient. And the corpus cannot have it both ways: §8.5 records that he needs $\Lambda \le 0$ for his existence conditions, so the sign is not decorative — it is what makes the model solvable. So the bounce and the negative vacuum are one claim, and the second half is dead.

And the requirement $\Lambda \le 0$ does not need (3-69-2) to hold — which matters, because (3-69-2)'s scope is an open question. RC 051's $\Lambda$ is a function of the Système E's free minimum radius $R_{0}$, built from the Système E's own $\alpha = -1{,}46\times10^{-25}$ g and $\beta = -1{,}74\times10^{-39}$ g cm — not the cosmological pair of (5-68) that this article's numbers use, and S52 shows that reading it as one universal constant would force that free radius to be tuned to one part in $10^{89}$. The robust argument is geometric, not micro-physical: a closed ($K = +1$) universe with a finite maximum radius has $\Lambda = -3/R_{\max}^{2} < 0$ necessarily, since the turning point at $R_{\max}$ requires $K + \Lambda R_{\max}^{2}/3 = 0$. So the corpus's $\Lambda \le 0$ is forced by its closed geometry alone, and S52 derives the value: $\Lambda = -1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$ against the observed $+1{,}10\times10^{-56}$ cm$^{-2}$, opposite in sign and $7168\times$ smaller. The sign conflict is structural, and it cannot be repaired by re-scoping the citation. S50 traces its provenance to the zero-total-energy stipulation; S52 and this paragraph show it is independently forced by $K = +1$.

4. The bounce makes no prediction that could test it

A bouncing cosmology's distinctive signature is in the primordial perturbation spectrum. A bounce leaves a specific imprint on the CMB: in loop quantum cosmology a suppression of power at low multipoles, and generically a tensor-to-scalar ratio and a running that differ from inflation's. Inflation's predictions have been tested for thirty years; a bounce's are the way it would earn the same standing.

The corpus has no perturbation theory, and therefore no such prediction. Its cosmology is a homogeneous, isotropic solution of a closed system — $R(\tau)$, $\rho(\tau)$, $G(\tau)$, $M(\tau)$, all exact and all spatially constant. Nothing in the corpus's five chapters says how a perturbation to that solution grows, so there is no power spectrum to compute and no CMB signature to compare (the register's §8.11 makes the same point about the substitute-for-inflation function). The bounce is therefore not a theory that makes a distinctive falsifiable prediction; it is a background history, and the observations that would test it are exactly the ones it cannot address.

That is what makes finding 2 sharp rather than merely quantitative. A bounce at the Planck scale is untestable directly but is implied by a theory with a perturbation sector; a bounce at $7615$ K is testable in principle — ordinary physics applies there, and a cold bounce would leave no trace of a hot start — and the corpus provides no channel through which the trace would appear.

Verdict

A live idea in an implementation that cannot produce it: the tag is right, and here is what it means.

What is genuinely to the corpus's credit. He posed the right question in 1983 and answered it with a specific, internally consistent model: a $K = +1$ universe whose radius turns around at $R_{i} = 6{,}006\times10^{24}$ cm — $1{,}946$ Mpc — with $R' = 0$ there and finite density and temperature; a maximum at $234\,000\,R_{i}$ reached after $2371$ Gyr; a present radius $88\times$ the minimum. Every one of those numbers checks against the others (the table in §2), and the claim that there is no singularity is exact.

What stops it being a competitor.

  1. The bounce is $113$ orders of magnitude in density below where bounces are supposed to happen, and $28$ orders below in temperature: $10^{-20}$ g cm$^{-3}$ and $7615$ K against the Planck density and the Planck temperature. The corpus needs no quantum gravity, and therefore has nothing to generate the turnaround except its own modification of the equations.
  2. That modification is the sign of the vacuum energy, and the sign is measured against him. The corpus's existence conditions require $\Lambda \le 0$ (RC (3-69-2)) and its vacuum density is negative by construction; observation gives a positive vacuum energy some $200\times$ larger in magnitude. The bounce and the negative vacuum are one claim, and the second half is dead.
  3. No perturbation theory, hence no CMB prediction. The one observable that discriminates among bounces cannot be computed in this model, so the idea cannot be earned by its own signature.

And the register's own note needed one arithmetic correction, which this article makes: $R_{i} = 1{,}946$ Mpc, not $1{,}9$ Gpc, and $3{,}6\times10^{-4}$ of the Hubble radius, not $0{,}44$.

What this article hands on. $R_{i}$ is the starting point of the $G$ history: it is what makes $G(\tau{=}0) = 2{,}2\times10^{-11}$ cgs and gives S40's prediction its first value. So the corpus's one live prediction begins at a radius whose own justification fails — and that is not fatal to the prediction, because the prediction is a statement about the shape of $G(R)$ and (5-61-a) determines it from $R_{i}$ and the other radii. But it does mean the prediction's ancestry runs through the corpus's weakest cosmological ingredient, which is worth knowing. The thermal history that follows the cold bounce — a matter-creating "bang" at 303 Myr rather than a hot start — is §8.4's territory and is not this article's; it is tagged [FALS] on grounds that do not depend on the bounce.

# __Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra__

## Introduction

The root of a central value and the nilpotent are defined by equations in which the product occurs,
so they are classes of the product and not of the space (*Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space*, §*The Element Vocabulary*). The block of this article is the antisymmetric
plain multiplication

$$
\tilde p\times\tilde q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p\tilde q-\tilde q\tilde p\bigr)=\vec p\times\vec q,
$$

the cross product of the two vector parts, which is alternating and without a unit (*Introduction to
the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions*).

The article treats two subjects, and in this order. The first is the **square roots of the central
value $0$**, the nonzero elements $\tilde\xi$ with $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$. The second is the
**nilpotent class of the block**, the elements some power of which vanishes, and its nonzero members,
the nilpotents. The two subjects are one subject, because a root of $0$ is exactly a square-zero
element and a square-zero element is a nilpotent of index two: the two notions meet at the vanishing
square, and the vanishing square of the block is total.

The outcome is the totality of the class, and it is the extreme form of the coincidence law. The
square of every element of the block vanishes, so the root of $0$ is satisfied by every nonzero
element, and **the roots of $0$ are the punctured space**, which is at once the square-zero set and
the zero-divisor set; and **the nilpotent class is the whole space**, the origin being its trivial
member of index one and every other element a nilpotent of index two, with no element of index three
or more. The nilpotents are the punctured space. The implication *nilpotent implies zero divisor*
holds in the **total** form, the origin being the class's only excluded element, and every element
lies in its own annihilator on both sides. The square detects no zero divisor here, since it vanishes
on everything.

The article is algebraic. It does not treat the roots of $-1$ and of $+1$ and the idempotents, which
are *Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*; the square roots of
a general right side and the square map, which is *Square Roots of a General Element in the
Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*; the zero-divisor set, which is *Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric
Plain Algebra*; the annihilators, which are *Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*; or the
parent's class, which is *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents of the General Plain Algebra*.

**The results.** For the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\tilde p\times\tilde q=\vec p\times\vec q$
the square of every element vanishes, $\tilde p\times\tilde p=0$, so the equation
$\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ is a tautology and its nonzero solutions, **the roots of $0$**, are the
**punctured space** $\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}$, which is at once the square-zero set and the
zero-divisor set. The **nilpotent class** is the **whole space**: the origin is its trivial member of
index one, every other element is a nilpotent of index two, and none is of index three or more. The
implication *nilpotent implies zero divisor* holds in the **total** form, with the origin as its only
excluded element; every element lies in its own annihilator on both sides; and the square detects no
zero divisor. The comparison with the parent plain product is the comparison of the whole space with
the origin alone, the parent being a division product.

## The Notion and Its Product

**Definition (root of a central value).** Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$ and let
$\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$. A **root of $\lambda$ in $\star$** is a nonzero element $\tilde\xi$ with

$$
\tilde\xi\star\tilde\xi=\lambda e_t .
$$

The three central values the quaternion algebra singles out are $-1$, $+1$ and $0$: the roots of $+1$
are the involutions, the roots of $0$ are the nonzero nilpotents of the product, and the roots of $-1$
are the imaginary units. The general notion this specialises is the **square root of a central value** of the definitions
article, an element with $\tilde\xi^2=c$, the class being the set of the solutions, with no non-zero
requirement, so that the origin is counted there whenever it solves the equation (*Definitions for
the Study of the Algebraic Structures*, §*The Element Vocabulary*); a **root** is a nonzero element
of that class, so that the roots of $0$ are the class without the origin. Where a companion counts
the origin with the class, as *Square Roots of a General Element in the Algebraic Structures over
the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space* does, the two readings differ by the origin alone.

**Definition (nilpotent of a product).** Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$. An element is in
the **nilpotent class of $\star$** when some power of it vanishes, $\tilde\nu^{\star k}=0$ for some
$k\geq1$, the powers being read $x^1=x$ and $x^{k+1}=x\star x^k$; the least such $k$ is the **index of
nilpotency**. A **nilpotent of $\star$** is a nonzero element of the class, a **square-zero element**
is a nonzero element with $\tilde\nu\star\tilde\nu=0$, and a **cube-zero element** is a nonzero
element with $f(\tilde\nu,f(\tilde\nu,\tilde\nu))=0$; a nilpotent of index two is exactly a square-zero
element. The origin lies in the class of every product, of index one, and it is not a nilpotent
(*Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space*, §*The Element Vocabulary*).

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\times$, and
unqualified, *root* means a root of the block, *nilpotent* and *class* are read in $\times$. The
square of the block is the specialisation to the block of the general square of *The Symmetric and
Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product*. The nilpotent classes of the six structures, against which
the block is read, are *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space*.

## The Roots of Zero

**Proposition (the reduction).** For every element $\tilde\xi$,

$$
\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0 ,
$$

so the equation $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ is a tautology, true for every element and carrying no
information about the unknown.

*Proof.* The block is alternating, $\tilde x\times\tilde x=0$ for every $\tilde x$, by *Introduction
to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions*; read at $\tilde x=\tilde\xi$ this is the display,
and the equation $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ is the display itself.

**Theorem (the roots of $0$).** The roots of $0$ are all the nonzero elements,

$$
\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0\iff\tilde\xi\neq0 ,
$$

the whole four-dimensional space with the origin removed. The root set of $0$ is the **square-zero
set** and the **zero-divisor set** of the block, all three being the punctured space.

*Proof.* The square vanishes identically, so the equation is satisfied by every nonzero element and
by no other; and an element is a two-sided zero divisor of an alternating product exactly when it is
nonzero, with itself as partner (*Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*).

**Remark (the whole space, and not only the vector subspace).** The root set of $0$ is the whole
space, and **not** the vector subspace: the scalar coordinate does not enter the square, since
$\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ for a non-pure element as well, and an element such as $e_t+\vec q$
lies in the set as soon as it is nonzero. The set has real dimension four, and its elements are
pairwise partners of themselves.

**Remark (the maximum class).** The root set of $0$ is the **maximum possible**, the whole punctured
space, and it is the only nondegenerate one: for $\lambda\neq0$ the root equation
$\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=\lambda e_t$ reduces to $0=\lambda e_t$ and is empty, and for $\lambda=0$ it
is universal. The specification of the root is by the target value alone, the value $0$ accepting
every nonzero element and every other value accepting none; this is the opposite of the parent plain
product, where the roots of a central value form a sphere or a pair and degenerate only on a
lower-dimensional family. The two empty classes, of $-1$ and of $+1$, are the companion face of the
same fact (*Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*).

## The Nilpotents

**The nilpotent class of the block is the whole space: the square of every element vanishes, the
origin is the trivial member of index one, and every other element is a nilpotent of index two, with
no element of index three or more.**

### The Criterion

**Theorem (the class is the whole space).** For every element $\tilde\nu$,

$$
\tilde\nu\times\tilde\nu=0 ,
$$

so the nilpotent class of the block is the whole space, its nonzero elements, the nilpotents, are the
punctured space, and every nilpotent is of index two; no element is of index three or more. The origin
is the trivial member of the class, of index one.

*Proof.* The block is alternating, $\tilde x\times\tilde x=0$ for every $\tilde x$, by *Introduction
to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions*. Hence the second power of every element vanishes
and the class, the set of elements some power of which vanishes, is the whole space. An element whose
square vanishes has every higher power zero, the powers being read from the square, so its index is
two when the element is nonzero and one at the origin. No element has index three or more, the square
having already vanished.

The criterion is the alternation of the product read as an element statement. There is no
stratification by index: the index is two on the punctured space and one at the origin, and none is of
index three or more. The same criterion is recorded for the class of nonzero nilpotents in
*Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space*, §*The Criterion for an Empty Class*, where
the class is the whole space apart from the origin for the two antisymmetric structures.

**Proposition (the class and its nonzero part).** The nilpotent class of the block is the whole space,
and the nilpotents are the punctured space,

$$
\{\tilde\nu:\tilde\nu^{\times k}=0\text{ for some }k\}=\mathbb{H},
\qquad
\{\tilde\nu\neq0:\tilde\nu\text{ is nilpotent}\}=\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}.
$$

*Proof.* The theorem gives the vanishing of the square at every element, so every element satisfies
the defining condition with $k=2$ and lies in the class; the origin does so with $k=1$ and is the
trivial member, of index one; the nonzero elements are the nilpotents, and the punctured space is
their set.

The class is as large as the space allows, and it is stable under every operation of the space: the
sum of two nilpotents is square zero, and so are the scalar multiple of a nilpotent and the bracket of
two nilpotents, all three being elements of the space. The punctured space is therefore closed under
the product, and the class is not a proper cone of the space.

### The Relation to the Zero Divisors and to the Roots of Zero

**Theorem (the four classes coincide).** The nilpotents, the square-zero elements, the zero divisors
and the roots of $0$ are one and the same set,

$$
\{\text{nilpotents}\}=\{\text{square-zero elements}\}=\{\text{zero divisors}\}=\{\text{roots of }0\}=\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}.
$$

*Proof.* The nilpotents are the punctured space by the previous proposition, and every nilpotent is
square zero, of index two. The zero divisors are the punctured space by *Zero Divisors of the
Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*, because the block is alternating and every nonzero element is its own
annihilating partner; and the roots of $0$ are the punctured space by §*The Roots of Zero*. The four
descriptions select one set.

The removal of the origin is forced by the definitions and not by the block: the zero divisor is a
nonzero element by definition, the nilpotent likewise, and the root likewise, while the square-zero
condition is met by the origin as well. On the punctured space the four conditions are the same
condition, and the origin is the one element of the class that lies in none of the three nonzero
classes.

**Corollary (the implication *nilpotent implies zero divisor*).** For the block the implication holds
in the **total** form: every nilpotent is a two-sided zero divisor, with itself as the annihilating
partner. The origin is the class's only element that is not a zero divisor, the definition of the zero
divisor requiring nonzero elements.

*Proof.* A nilpotent $\tilde\nu$ satisfies $\tilde\nu\times\tilde\nu=0$, and the element $\tilde\nu$
itself is a nonzero partner for both sides; the element is therefore a two-sided zero divisor. The
origin lies in the class and is not a zero divisor, the definition requiring nonzero elements.

**Remark (the square detects no zero divisor).** The implication *nilpotent implies zero divisor* has
no converse here: the square vanishes on everything, so the vanishing of a square carries no
information about an element, and the class the square detects is the whole space rather than a proper
subfamily of the zero divisors. The zero-divisor set is the punctured space as well (*Zero Divisors of
the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*), and the two sets agree; what fails is the discrimination, not the
coincidence.

**Corollary (self-annihilation).** Every element lies in its own annihilator on both sides: for every
$\tilde\nu$,

$$
\tilde\nu\in A_\times(\tilde\nu)\quad\text{and}\quad \tilde\nu\in B_\times(\tilde\nu),
$$

the left and the right annihilator being read in the block.

*Proof.* The square vanishes, $\tilde\nu\times\tilde\nu=0$, and the left annihilator
$A_\times(\tilde\nu)$ is the set of $\tilde x$ with $\tilde\nu\times\tilde x=0$; the choice
$\tilde x=\tilde\nu$ gives the left membership. The right membership is the same display read in the
other order, and the two sides coincide for the block, the product being alternating (*Annihilators of
the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*). Hence every element is its own annihilating partner, on the left
and on the right.

The self-annihilation is the element-level form of the totality of the class. For a noncentral element
the annihilator is the plane $\mathbb{R}e_t\oplus\mathbb{R}\vec\nu$ of real dimension two, which
contains the element; for a central element it is the whole space. The element lies in its annihilator
in every case, which is the statement that the square vanishes.

### The Conjugations and the Remarkable Subspaces

**Proposition (the conjugations preserve the class).** The nilpotent class of the block is preserved by
the bar, the natural conjugation and the star; the conjugates of an element of the class are elements
of the class.

*Proof.* For the block the statement is immediate from the criterion, every element lying in the
class, so every conjugate lies in it; the compatibility of each conjugation with the bracket is
recorded here for the comparison with the other structures, where the class is proper and the
preservation is a computation. The bar is the identity over $\mathbb{R}$ and so preserves the class
trivially. The natural conjugation is an anti-automorphism of the parent product and it reverses the
bracket, $\tilde p^{\natural}\times\tilde q^{\natural}=-(\tilde p\times\tilde q)^{\natural}$, so it
carries a vanishing square to the negative of a vanishing square, which vanishes as well; the star
coincides with the natural conjugation over $\mathbb{R}$.

**Proposition (the restriction to the remarkable subspaces).** On each of the remarkable subspaces the
restriction of the block is again alternating, so every element of every subspace lies in the class of
the restriction and every nonzero element of it is a nilpotent of the restriction.

*Proof.* The square of an element of the subspace vanishes as the square of an element of the ambient
algebra vanishes, and the criterion applies to the elements in every case. The subspaces and their
readings are *Comparison of the Remarkable Subspaces of the Quaternions*.

### The Comparison with the Parent Product

**Remark (the parent class).** The parent general plain bilinear product is associative and unital, and
it carries no nilpotent: it is a division product, so a nonzero $\tilde\nu$ is invertible and
$\tilde\nu^2=0$ would give $\tilde\nu=0$ (*Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra*; *The
Specificities of the General Plain Algebra*). The parent's class is therefore the origin alone, that is
the empty set off the origin. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the plain square has no isotropic direction, its
isotropic cone being real only after complexification, so the pure elements that would be square zero
over $\mathbb{C}$ are not available. The block is the antisymmetric half of the parent, and the
comparison is the comparison of the whole space with the origin alone: the block's class strictly
contains the parent's, the element $e_t$ lying in the class of the block ($e_t\times e_t=0$) and not of
the parent ($e_t^2=e_t$). The enlargement is the removal of the scalar part: the parent's criterion is
a condition on the square, and the block's square is identically zero, so the condition is satisfied by
every element.

### The Reading among the Six Structures

The nilpotent class of the six structures is the origin alone for the general plain, the symmetric
plain, the general quaternionic and the symmetric quaternionic structures, and it is the whole space
for the antisymmetric plain and the antisymmetric quaternionic structures; only two sets occur, and no
structure of the six has an intermediate one (*Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the
Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space*). The block is one of the two structures whose square vanishes
identically, and among the six it is the only one whose nilpotents all have index two. In the
biquaternion chapter the class of the two antisymmetric biquaternion structures is likewise the whole
space (*Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Biquaternion $\mathbb{C}$ Space*), so the
totality for an antisymmetric block is not an artefact of the real field.

## The Comparison with the Other Five Structures

The roots of $0$ of the six products, and the nilpotent class beside them, are the following.

| product | roots of $0$ | nilpotent class |
|---|---|---|
| GPA, $\tilde p\tilde q$ | none | $\{0\}$ |
| SPA, $\tilde p\bullet\tilde q$ | none | $\{0\}$ |
| APA, $\tilde p\times\tilde q$ | the punctured space | $\mathbb{H}$ |
| GQA, $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ | none | $\{0\}$ |
| SQA, $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t$ | none | $\{0\}$ |
| AQA, $\tilde p\wedge^{\natural}\tilde q$ | the punctured space | $\mathbb{H}$ |

**Remark (the pairing of the blocks).** The two **general** products and their symmetric halves share
their root pattern, and the two **antisymmetric** halves share theirs: the splitting into symmetric
and antisymmetric parts is what decides the root theory, not the choice of the parent. The block is
one of the two members of the degenerate antisymmetric family, the other being *Introduction to the
Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions*, below this article in this category. The roots of
the two signed central values, and the idempotents of the six, are the other columns of the same table
(*Square Roots of One and the Idempotents of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra*), and the six square-zero
sets are read in *Square Roots of Zero and the Nilpotents in the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space*.

## Summary

For the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\tilde p\times\tilde q=\vec p\times\vec q$ the square
of every element vanishes, so the equation $\tilde\xi\times\tilde\xi=0$ is a tautology and its roots,
the roots of $0$, are the punctured space, the whole space with the origin removed; the root set
coincides with the square-zero set and with the zero-divisor set. The nilpotent class is the whole
space: the origin is its trivial member, of index one, and every other element is of index two, the
nilpotents being the punctured space, and none is of index three or more. The nilpotents, the
square-zero elements, the zero divisors and the roots of $0$ are one set. The implication *nilpotent
implies zero divisor* holds in the total form, the origin being the only element of the class outside
the zero divisors; every element lies in its own annihilator on both sides, the self-annihilation being
the element-level form of the totality; and the square detects no zero divisor, vanishing on
everything. The conjugations preserve the class and every remarkable subspace inherits it. The
comparison with the parent product is the comparison of the whole space with the origin alone, the
enlargement being the removal of the scalar part from the square.

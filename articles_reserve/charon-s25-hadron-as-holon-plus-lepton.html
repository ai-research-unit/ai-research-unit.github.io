Charon — A Hadron Is a Holon plus a Lepton (S25)

Audits §5.1 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md, against Esp §VIII.1–3 (scans 081–083), Esp §VI.18.3 (scans 062–063) and RC ch. IV (scans 058, 063–065).

Introduction

The register's verdict on §5.1 stands: the composite picture is [EST]-adjacent — hadrons really are composite, really have form factors and really have more than one scale in them — and the two-sector realisation is [SPEC]. The corpus's "lepton part" of a nucleon is a positron; nothing in QCD is a positron, and the sentence has to be read as the corpus's own construction, not as an anticipation of the quark model.

This article's first finding is that the claim is not one claim but two, and that the corpus changes its mind between the two books. In 1983 a quark is a composite of three charged leptons held in a line, and its fractional charge comes from the electrostatic screening of a lepton by two others (Esp §VI.18.3). In 1987 a quark is the point-like trace of an éon sitting on one of six density levels inside the holon (RC 065, definition 2.6). The same word names a bound state of three leptons in one book and a single geometric point in the other, and the 1983 mechanism — the one that makes the fractional charge — has no place in the 1987 picture. §5.1's "bound to a lepton system" describes the 1983 construction; the 1987 chapter describes a holon carrying levels.

The second finding is a stated violation of Newton's third law. Esp 082: the leptons attract the holons "sans que ce phénomène soit réciproque, les holons « n'attirent pas » les leptons". The corpus is explicit: the binding is one-way. That is not a slip of language — it is the mechanism by which the lepton part stays point-like and the holon stays extended, and it is worth recording because it is the kind of statement a reader of a summary would not expect to find unhedged in the source.

And one textual defect was repaired by arithmetic. RC 064's sentence about the neutron's greater mass carries a comparison that is nonsense as transcribed and has an obvious intended reading: the neutron–proton mass difference is 2,53 electron masses, and the transcription says "proton".

What the Corpus Claims

§5.1 — a hadron = a holon (extended, in real space) bound to a lepton system (point-like, in imaginary space). Esp §VIII.1–2; RC ch. IV. Tag: [MIX]. Central claim. The register's gloss: "The composite picture is [EST] in the modern sense (hadrons are composite, with form factors); the two-sector realisation is [SPEC]. Note the 'lepton part' of a nucleon is not a positron in QCD."

What §5.1 does not say, and what this article therefore does not audit. The six density levels and their identification with the six quark flavours are P4's (§6.1, §10.9); the density $\rho = m/\Omega_{0}$ is P21's (§5.5); the fractional-charge mechanism is R6's, owned by S19, S35, S47 and S48 (§6.2); the holon itself is S24's. This article owns the composite: what the two parts are, how they bind, and what the pairing is claimed to produce.

1. The Two Parts, as the Corpus Defines Them

Esp 082 gives the architecture in one sentence each. The extended part:

une particule étendue dans l'espace entropique $(\dot{r}, \dot{t})$, que nous appellerons holon. Cette particule est électriquement neutre, possède un spin et un pulse demi-entiers et est responsable pour les interactions fortes et gravitationnelles

and the other part:

un système de leptons, ceux-ci étant associés entre eux par interactions électromagnétiques. Nous nommons ce système lepton s'il est formé d'un lepton chargé seulement, paire s'il est formé de deux leptons de charges électriques opposées et quark s'il est formé de trois leptons (deux d'entre eux seulement ayant le même signe de charge électrique).

Three things follow, and each is checkable against the corpus's own tables.

part where it lives interactions it carries observed as
holon the entropic space, extended strong, gravitational nothing — "particules « fantômes »"
lepton system the negentropic space, extended there weak, electromagnetic point-like

First: the holon alone is unobservable, and the corpus gives two reasons. Esp 082: "tôt ou tard, avec le temps, tous les holons deviennent des hadrons" and, more interestingly, a reason of principle — a holon generates neither weak nor electromagnetic interactions, and an observer can only use those; so "les holons ne peuvent être observés que quand ils sont devenus des hadrons". This is the corpus's own answer to the obvious objection, and it is a genuinely structural answer: the object is defined as unobservable in isolation and is only ever seen bound. Esp 081 opens the section with the same reservation — "Les holons sont-ils ce que les physiciens nomment habituellement les hadrons ? Pas exactement."

Second: the correspondence is not one-to-one in the way §5.1's shorthand suggests. RC 065, definition 2.7: "Il n'existe qu'un seul holon, mais naturellement un grand nombre de hadrons." So there is one holon-geometry and many hadrons, the difference being which lepton system is bound to it and which levels are occupied. §5.1's "a hadron = a holon bound to a lepton system" is therefore a statement about a pairing, not about a species.

Third: the binding is one-way, and the corpus says so. Esp 082:

La partie leptonique développe avec la partie holonique une énergie de liaison (les leptons « attirent » les holons, sans que ce phénomène soit réciproque, les holons « n'attirent pas » les leptons).

An action without a reaction is not available in any of the four interactions the corpus claims to contain. It is stated as a property of the coupling, and no derivation is offered. Recorded as a standing weakness of the claim, not as a defect of transcription. Its function is clear — it is what keeps the lepton part point-like while the holon stays extended — and that function is exactly why §5.1 is [SPEC]: the two scales are put in by hand.

2. What the Pairing Is Supposed to Produce

Esp 082 lists the consequences, and they are of two kinds.

The four interactions, with the weak one saturated. "le hadron est ainsi porteur des quatre interactions ; cependant l'interaction faible est pratiquement « saturée » par l'énergie de liaison des deux parties leptonique et holonique (saturation similaire à celle qui provoque la création des charges électriques fractionnaires ...). Les interactions des hadrons entre eux ne feront donc intervenir que les interactions forte, gravitationnelle et électromagnétique." So the corpus predicts that hadron–hadron interactions show no weak component — which is right, in the sense that the weak interaction does not bind nuclei, and is not what makes it right: the corpus's reason is that the binding energy has used the weak sector up, and the "saturation" is asserted by analogy with §18.3's electrostatic saturation, not derived.

The two charge states of the proton and the neutron. Esp 082 §3.1: "La partie leptonique est un positron" for the proton, and a pair for the neutron, "et ceci justifie que le neutron, qui est une particule globalement électriquement neutre, possède cependant des interactions". Esp 083 adds the neutron's fractional-charge detail: "les deux leptons entrant dans sa structure sont « en contact », chacun de ces leptons doit être considéré comme porteur de la charge fractionnaire $\pm 2/3\,e$ seulement". The arithmetic of the global charges checks: three $+1/3$ gives $+1$ (proton) and $+1/3 + 1/3 - 2/3 = 0$ (neutron), as Esp 083's charge table prints. What is not derived is why a lepton in contact carries $2/3$ rather than $1$; that is the §18.3 mechanism, and it is R6's.

And the one place where the pairing explains something real. Esp 083 continues: "l'expérience démontre que la masse propre du neutron ... différence qui est de 2,53 fois la masse du proton" — see §5 for the defect — "ce résultat paraît pouvoir s'expliquer par le fait que, dans le cas du neutron, les deux charges $+1/3$ sont animées d'un léger mouvement d'oscillation électrodynamique autour de la charge $-2/3$, ce qui justifierait la plus grande masse du neutron, puisque les deux quarks $+1/3$ en oscillation posséderaient dans ce cas une énergie (kinétique) positive." The corpus then says the detailed study is out of scope: "il conviendra de préciser cet aspect (important) dans une étude mathématique comparative détaillée du niveau fondamental $n = 0$ chez le proton et chez le neutron (étude qui sortirait cependant du cadre du présent travail)." So the mechanism is offered and deferred in the same paragraph, and the comparison it needs is a real one: the sign of the effect can be checked, since an oscillation adds kinetic energy, and the neutron is indeed heavier. It is the only quantitative prediction in the section, and it is left as a programme.

3. The Counting: Three Colours and Nine Flavours

Esp 083 defines three colours and nine flavours and then counts. The colours:

colour 1 2 3
charge $1/3$ $1/3$ $2/3$
spin $1/2$ $1$ $1/2$

and the flavours, in the corpus's own basis: the nine pairs $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(3,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(0,2)$, $(0,3)$, $(2,1)$, $(1,2)$, where the first number counts muons and the second taus in the quark, and which the corpus identifies with strangeness and charm. The corpus's count follows from these two tables and is arithmetic:

$9 \times 2 = 18$ hadrons de spins demi-entiers $\pm\frac{1}{2}$ \ $9$ hadrons de spins entiers $\pm 1$

Verified: $9 \times 3 = 27$ base structures, of which $9 \times 2 = 18$ carry the half-integer spins (colours 1 and 3) and $9 \times 1 = 9$ the integer spin (colour 2). The counting is internally consistent, and the charge table's asymmetry — colour 2 is the integer-spin one and the only one with $2/3$ — is what makes the proton's three $+1/3$ possible.

The honest reading of the counting, and one structural observation the corpus does not make. The corpus reaches 27 base structures with three colours and nine flavours, against QCD's $3 \times 6 = 18$ (three colours, six flavours; the top quark's difficulty is P5's). The nine flavours are not nine quark flavours: each is a three-lepton composite of the form $X^{+}Y^{-}X^{+}$ with $X, Y \in \{e, \mu, \tau\}$, so the nine are exactly the $3 \times 3$ of an outer same-flavour pair $(X)$ and a central lepton $(Y)$ — and the corpus's own table is exactly that: $(0,0) = e^{+}e^{-}e^{+}$, $(1,0) = e^{+}\mu^{-}e^{+}$, $(2,0) = \mu^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}$, $(3,0) = \mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}$, $(0,1) = e^{+}\tau^{-}e^{+}$, $(0,2) = \tau^{+}e^{-}\tau^{+}$, $(0,3) = \tau^{+}\tau^{-}\tau^{+}$, $(2,1) = \mu^{+}\tau^{-}\mu^{+}$, $(1,2) = \tau^{+}\mu^{-}\tau^{+}$. The list is complete, and the entry $(1,1)$ is absent by construction, not by oversight: the two outer leptons are the same particle, so a quark cannot contain exactly one muon and one tau with no other. The identification of the first index with strangeness and the second with charm is then a re-labelling of "number of muons" and "number of taus" — and it is where the 1983 model's quark content dies, at P4 and P5, not here.

4. The Quark Changes Meaning Between the Two Books

The 1983 quark is a composite of three leptons. Esp §VI.18.3: "Nous appellerons quark une structure leptonique composite telle que $Q$ agissant sur un électron éloigné de charge $e$ avec une charge électrique $\pm\frac{2}{3}e$" — the structure $Q$ being three electrons in a line (one negative between two positive). And Esp §VIII.3 assembles hadrons from these, quark by quark.

The 1987 quark is a point inside the holon. RC 065, definition 2.6: "Nous appelons quark la « trace » ponctuelle dans l'espace holonique d'un éon de masse propre positive, et antiquark la « trace » ponctuelle dans l'espace holonique d'un éon de masse propre négative." A quark is now a single point on one of six spherical levels, its mass fixed by the local holon density through $m_{n} = \Omega_{0}\rho_{n}$ (RC (4-19), (4-20-a)). There is no three-lepton structure anywhere in this picture, and the fractional charge has to come from elsewhere — in fact RC ch. IV assigns each level an integer charge and puts three quarks on level 0 (RC 064): "Le niveau holonique le plus bas ($n = 0$) ... comprend trois quarks ayant la masse propre de l'électron", with

charge +1 = +1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3   (cas du proton, niveau stable)

charge  0 = +1/3 + 1/3 − 2/3   (cas du neutron)

and levels 1 to 5 carrying pairs, with charges $0 = +2/3 - 2/3$ (the stable level) or $+1 = +1/3 + 2/3$ (RC 064). So the 1987 scheme has the fractional charges living on the levels, not in any screening geometry, and the proton's three $+1/3$ quarks are three points of electron mass on the fundamental level.

The two definitions cannot both be the corpus's. The 1983 quark's charge comes from the screening geometry of three leptons; the 1987 quark's charge is a property of the level it sits on; and the 1987 quark is point-like inside the holon while the 1983 quark is an extended leptonic composite bound outside it. §5.1's "bound to a lepton system" is the 1983 sentence, and it is the sentence the register's gloss is about; the chapter the register cites for it, RC ch. IV, contains the other definition. The register entry should say which, and this article records the divergence as a fact about the corpus.

A consequence for the inventory. §5.1 and §5.2 are one architecture in 1983 (holon + lepton system, quarks as lepton composites) and a different one in 1987 (holon + six levels, quarks as éon traces). The holon survives the change — it is the same object and S24 verifies it — and the lepton part does not; it becomes a level structure. That is why P4's "the count of six flavours is right, the identification is wrong" is the correct verdict on the later picture, and why the 1983 proton-as-holon-plus-positron is a different, and differently wrong, claim.

5. Two Textual Defects

The neutron's mass difference: RC 064's sentence is unintelligible as transcribed and has one obvious repair. The transcription reads "la masse propre du neutron (deux charges $+1/3$ et une charge $-2/3$), différence qui est de $2,53$ fois la masse du proton (trois charges $+1/3$)". The neutron–proton mass difference is $1{,}2933$ MeV, which is $2{,}531$ electron masses — not $2{,}53$ proton masses, which would be $2{,}4$ GeV. So "proton" should read "électron", and with that one word the sentence is correct and the sentence that follows it (the oscillating pair, §2 above) makes sense. Reported with the suggested reading, and left unedited, because the printed page has not been re-read. It is a one-word defect in a sentence the article relies on, which is precisely the case the register's rule about not "fixing" a transcription by substitution is for.

And the 1983 book carries two different charge assignments for its quarks. §18.3 gives the quark a charge $\pm 2/3e$ from screening; §VIII.3's colour table gives the three colours the charges $1/3$, $1/3$, $2/3$ and the proton's three quarks $+1/3$ each. The two schemes are not obviously the same scheme: the first assigns a fractional charge to a three-lepton composite, the second to a level. Recorded, not adjudicated; the fractional-charge physics is R6's and S47–S48's.

6. Verdict

§5.1 stands as stated, with its tag unchanged: [MIX], the composite idea [EST]-adjacent, the realisation [SPEC]. The article's findings:

(1) The claim is two claims across editions, and the corpus's word "quark" does not denote the same object in 1983 and 1987 — a three-lepton composite in the first, a point on a density level in the second; the fractional-charge mechanism belongs only to the first, and the chapter the register cites for §5.1 contains the second.

(2) The binding is stated to be one-way, in the corpus's own words — "les holons « n'attirent pas » les leptons" — which is an action without a reaction and is the device that keeps the two scales separate.

(3) The 27-structure count is arithmetically consistent with its own three colours and nine flavours ($9\times3 = 27 = 18 + 9$), and its nine flavours are lepton arrangements, not quark flavours.

(4) The neutron's greater mass is the section's one quantitative idea, and the corpus itself defers it — an oscillating charged pair adding positive kinetic energy, with the detailed comparison left to a study it does not perform. The effect's sign is right; the size is never computed.

(5) One defect repaired by arithmetic: the neutron sentence's "2,53 fois la masse du proton" is $2{,}531$ electron masses, and the word is wrong.

What survives. That a hadron is composite, that it has a part which is extended and a part which is point-like at the accessible scale, and that its internal structure should show up as levels with a mass scale set by a length — all of this is the ordinary shape of a modern composite picture, and it is why §5.1 is not a [SPEC] claim about the existence of structure. What does not survive is the identification of the parts: the lepton system of a nucleon is not a positron, and the six levels are not six quark flavours (P4). And what is left standing after S24 is the holon itself — verified as a solution, tagged [SPEC] as physics, and the only part of this architecture this series ends up keeping.

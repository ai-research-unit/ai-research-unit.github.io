Charon: Could a Fourth Lepton Hide?

Introduction

P1 records the tower's disproof by LEP. This article asks the harder question: suppose the first rungs are excluded, could the tower survive by hiding its particles somewhere the colliders did not look? The answer is no, and the reason is not one more search. It is the density of the ladder. A tower whose step is 1.68 GeV puts roughly twenty-six charged leptons below half the $Z^{0}$ mass, and the $Z^{0}$ width is measured to a part in a thousand. The claim is excluded before any dedicated search is consulted.

The claim is the same equation, $m_n = \mu + 2(n-1)h/(c^{2}T_{0})$ (RC 3-76), with the same universal quantifier: "l'ensemble des LEPTONS CHARGÉS correspond aux valeurs successives de $n$, quel que soit l'entier $n \in [0, \infty[$". The step is $\tau - \mu \simeq 1{,}678$ GeV$/c^{2}$, fixed by the corpus's own fitted period.

The verdict is [FALS], and the exclusion is now stronger than LEP's. Three independent walls close on the tower: the $Z^{0}$ total width excludes a dense comb of charged leptons below 45.6 GeV; the LEP II direct search excludes a single one below 100.8 GeV; and CKM unitarity, electroweak fits and LHC heavy-lepton searches exclude any sequential charged lepton up to about a TeV. A surviving rung would have to sit roughly six hundred steps up the ladder.

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §4.3 and §4.4 (RC/053_improved.md). The $Z^{0}$ width is the LEP electroweak combination, $\Gamma_{Z} = 2{,}4952 \pm 0{,}0023$ GeV; the per-flavour visible width is $\Gamma_{\ell\ell} = 83{,}96$ MeV. CKM unitarity is the current $|V_{ud}|^{2} + |V_{us}|^{2} + |V_{ub}|^{2}$ determination. The related chains are R7 (DIES at RC 053, P1) and R1. The downstream article is P22.

Conventions. Gaussian cgs. $m_n$ is the mass of the $n$-th rung, $\mu$ the muon mass, $T_{0}$ the fitted period, $\Gamma_{Z}$ the $Z^{0}$ total width, $\Gamma_{\ell\ell}$ the partial width into one charged-lepton pair, $N_{\nu}$ the effective number of light neutrino species. Energies in GeV.

Part 1 — Why density, not position, is the argument

A single extra charged lepton at an unknown mass could in principle hide: a collider scans energies one at a time, and a particle above the reach is untested. The tower is not a single extra particle, and this is the whole point.

The comb. With step $1{,}678$ GeV, the rungs below $m_{Z}/2 = 45{,}6$ GeV are

$$m_n = 3{,}47 + 1{,}678\,(n-3)\ \text{GeV}, \qquad n = 3, \ldots, 28,$$

that is twenty-six charged leptons between 3.5 GeV and 45.4 GeV, and one more about every 1.68 GeV the whole way up. A particle as light as the first rung is not a hypothetical high-mass object; it is a light charged lepton that every $e^{+}e^{-}$ machine from PETRA onward would have produced in profusion.

Why the corpus cannot thin the comb. The generalisation has no escape clause. There is no statement that the higher values of $n$ are forbidden, unstable, or invisible. The ladder is arithmetic, not a selection rule, and each rung is a charged lepton with a definite mass and standard couplings.

Part 2 — The three walls

2.1 The $Z^{0}$ width: the wall below 45.6 GeV

If a charged lepton $L^{\pm}$ is lighter than $m_{Z}/2$, the $Z^{0}$ decays to $L^{+}L^{-}$ with a partial width close to the standard $\Gamma_{\ell\ell} = 83{,}96$ MeV (the exact value depends on the weak isospin and mass, but it is of that size whenever $L$ is a sequential lepton).

The arithmetic. Twenty-six additional rungs, each contributing of order $84$ MeV, would add about $2{,}2$ GeV to the $Z^{0}$ width. The measured total is $\Gamma_{Z} = 2{,}4952$ GeV, of which the three known charged leptons already account for $3 \times 84 = 252$ MeV. The measurement's uncertainty is $0{,}0023$ GeV. The prediction overshoots the whole measured width by a factor of about two, with an error bar a thousand times smaller than the discrepancy. One rung would be visible; twenty-six are not merely visible but impossible.

2.2 The direct search: the wall between 45.6 and 100.8 GeV

Above $m_{Z}/2$ the width argument weakens, and the direct search takes over. LEP II ran to 209 GeV and excluded a sequential charged lepton below 100.8 GeV$/c^{2}$ at 95 % confidence. The tower predicts about thirty more rungs in that window (up to $n \approx 60$). None was seen.

2.3 The modern walls: above 100 GeV

Beyond LEP the sequential-lepton window is closed from three sides at once.

constraint what it excludes source
$Z^{0}$ total width a dense comb of charged leptons below $m_{Z}/2$ LEP combination, $\Gamma_{Z} = 2{,}4952 \pm 0{,}0023$ GeV
LEP II direct search one sequential charged lepton below 100.8 GeV LEP II, 2000
CKM unitarity a fourth generation with a quark doublet $|V_{ud}|^{2}+|V_{us}|^{2}+|V_{ub}|^{2} = 0{,}9985 \pm 0{,}0007$
LHC heavy-lepton search a sequential charged lepton up to about 1 TeV ATLAS/CMS, $L^{\pm} \to W^{\pm}\nu$
Higgs coupling fits a fourth generation enhancing $gg \to H$ LHC 2012 onward

Reading the table. The tower needs a dense spectrum, not one particle. CKM unitarity and the electroweak fits close the fourth-generation window on general grounds; the LHC closes the sequential-lepton window up to about a TeV. A rung that survived all of these would have to be heavier than a TeV, which is roughly six hundred steps up a ladder whose step is 1.68 GeV — and a ladder with six hundred invisible rungs below the first visible one is no longer the arithmetic tower the corpus proposed.

Part 3 — What this adds to P1

P1 shows the first rungs are excluded. This article shows the exclusion cannot be evaded by moving the rungs. The distinction is worth keeping because "no fourth lepton" and "no arithmetic ladder" are different verdicts. The first says LEP missed nothing; the second says no arrangement of the ladder's rungs survives, because the ladder is a comb and the comb's teeth are too close together.

The argument generalises to any spectrum built on a mass unit. A model that predicts one particle per 1.68 GeV is falsified by a width measurement, regardless of where the spectrum is anchored. This is the sense in which the tower's failure is structural: it is not the particular masses that are wrong, but the spacing.

Feeds P22 and S45. The bootstrap claim needs a coherence principle that fixes the spectrum. A principle that fixes the spacing but not the number of states has none of the content the claim requires.

Summary

The arithmetic tower cannot hide, and the reason is its density. With a 1.68 GeV step the corpus predicts twenty-six charged leptons below $m_{Z}/2$; each would contribute of order 84 MeV to the $Z^{0}$ width, for about 2.2 GeV against a measured total of 2.4952 GeV with a 0.0023 GeV error — impossible by a factor of two. LEP II excludes any single rung below 100.8 GeV, and CKM unitarity, electroweak fits and LHC heavy-lepton searches close the remaining window to about a TeV, some six hundred steps up the ladder. Verdict [FALS], and the exclusion is now structural: it is the spacing, not the anchoring, that is refuted.

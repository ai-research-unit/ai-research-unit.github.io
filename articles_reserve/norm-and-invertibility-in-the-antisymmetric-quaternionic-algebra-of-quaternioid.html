Norm and Invertibility in the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

This article studies the algebraic norm and the invertibility of the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p\bigr) =p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q, $$

the antisymmetric part of the general quaternionic product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions). The operation is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear, alternating and vector valued, and it has no unit on either side.

A norm is relative to a product, and the algebraic norm of a product is the diagonal of its scalar-part form (The 2 Algebraic Norms over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space). For the block the scalar part vanishes identically, because every value is a pure vector; the algebraic norm of the operation is therefore the zero function, and the block is counted with no algebraic norm rather than with a zero one. The invertibility is a second and independent absence: the block has no unit on either side, hence no inverse and an empty group of units.

Two quadratic objects survive the loss of the scalar part. The first is the scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t$, of rank one and invariant, whose radical and isotropic cone are the vector subspace; the second is the operator trace form $K(\tilde A,\tilde B)=\operatorname{Tr}(L_{\tilde A}L_{\tilde B})=3A_0B_0-2(\vec A,\vec B)$, non-degenerate of signature $(1,3)$ and not invariant. Neither is central-valued, and the multiplicativity equation fails for both, and for the quaternion form $N$, at the single pair $(e_t,e_t)$.

Conventions. Throughout, $\tilde p=p_0e_t+\vec p$ and $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$ name elements of $\mathbb{H}$ with real coefficients, $(\vec p,\vec q)$ is the dot product and $\vec p\times\vec q$ the cross product of the vector parts, and $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_te_t-\vec q$ is the quaternion conjugation; the bar is the identity and the star coincides with the natural conjugation, and the operation is written $\diamond$ and never $\wedge^{\natural}$ outside a citation. The image of the operation is the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, of real dimension three, and the centre is $\mathbb{R}e_t$. The quaternion form is $N(\tilde p)=p_0^2+\lvert\vec p\rvert^2$; the operator $L_{\tilde p}$ is the left multiplication $\tilde x\mapsto\tilde p\diamond\tilde x$; and unqualified unit and inverse mean the unit and the inverse of $\diamond$.

Boundaries. The operation is Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions, the definitions are Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, the unit table is The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, the invariant forms are The Invariant Bilinear Forms of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, the operator $L_{\tilde p}$ is Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, and the algebraic norm $N$ of the parent is the different form of Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra.

The Relative Definitions

Definition (unit and inverse). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$. An element $\tilde e$ is a left unit of $\star$ when $\tilde e\star\tilde x=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$, a right unit when $\tilde x\star\tilde e=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$, and a unit when it is both. When a unit exists, the inverse of $\tilde x$ is the element $\tilde y$ with $\tilde x\star\tilde y=\tilde y\star\tilde x=\tilde e$, and the invertible elements form the group of units; without a two-sided unit the word inverse is not used, and an element is regular for a one-sided unit (Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space).

The Absence of a Unit

Theorem (no unit). The operation $\diamond$ has no left unit and no right unit; there is no $\tilde e$ with $\tilde e\diamond\tilde x=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$, and none with $\tilde x\diamond\tilde e=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$. Hence there is no two-sided unit.

Proof. The value of the operation is a sum of pure terms, $p_0\vec q$, $-q_t\vec p$ and $-\vec p\times\vec q$, so $\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=0$ for every pair. A left unit $\tilde e$ gives at $\tilde x=e_t$ the value $\tilde e\diamond e_t=-\vec e$, of zero scalar part, which would have to equal $e_t$, of scalar part one; impossible. A right unit gives at the same argument the value $e_t\diamond\tilde e=\vec e$, again of zero scalar part, which would have to equal $e_t$; impossible. So no element is a left unit and none is a right unit.

Remark (the near unit $e_t$). The element $e_t$ is not a unit, but its multiplication is the closest thing the block has to one: directly from the explicit form, $e_t\diamond\tilde q=\vec q$ and $\tilde q\diamond e_t=-\vec q$, so $e_t$ is a left identity on the vector subspace with the centre in its kernel, and on the right it is minus that projection. At $\tilde q=e_t$ both give $0$, so the unit equation $e_t\diamond e_t=e_t$ fails.

The Absence of an Inverse

Theorem (the inverse equation has no solution). For no pair $\tilde x,\tilde y$ is $\tilde x\diamond\tilde y=e_t$, and for no pair is $\tilde x\diamond\tilde y$ a central element other than $0$. No element of $\mathbb{H}$ is invertible of the block, and the group of units of $\diamond$ is empty.

Proof. The left side is pure for every pair, so its scalar part vanishes, while $e_t$ has scalar part one; the equality $\tilde x\diamond\tilde y=e_t$ is impossible for every pair, and the same obstruction excludes every central value $ce_t$ with $c\neq0$. The inverse is defined against a two-sided unit, and the block has none, so no element is invertible and the group of units is empty.

Remark (the image and the class of units). The image of the operation is the whole vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, and the centre $\mathbb{R}e_t$ lies outside it except at the origin. The block is therefore not unital and not a division algebra; its class of units is empty, which is the reading of the entry $\mathrm{AQA}$ in the table of The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

The Algebraic Norm of the Block

Definition (the algebraic norm of an operation). Let $f$ be an operation on $\mathbb{H}$ with scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=\mathrm{Sc}\,f(\tilde p,\tilde q)$. The algebraic norm of $f$ is the diagonal of that form, $\varphi_f(\tilde x)=\mathrm{Sc}\,f(\tilde x,\tilde x)$, and for an operation whose scalar part vanishes identically the algebraic norm is the zero function (The 2 Algebraic Norms over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space).

Theorem (the algebraic norm of the block is the zero function). For the antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication, $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)=0$ for all $\tilde p,\tilde q$, hence $\varphi_{\diamond}(\tilde x)=0$ for all $\tilde x$. The algebraic norm of the operation is identically zero, and the block carries no algebraic norm.

Proof. The value $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$ is a sum of pure terms, hence of zero scalar part, so the diagonal $\operatorname{Sc}(\tilde x\diamond\tilde x)$ is the zero function; a vanishing quadratic form is not an algebraic norm.

Remark (the two canonical quadratics of the block). The operation being alternating, the square vanishes identically, $\tilde x\diamond\tilde x=0$, and the conjugated square is the pure vector $\tilde x\diamond\tilde x^{\natural}=-2x_0\vec x$. The conjugated square is central exactly when $x_0\vec x=0$, that is on the union of the centre $\mathbb{R}e_t$ and the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$, and at $\tilde x=e_t+\vec e_x$ it takes the value $-2\vec e_x$; neither canonical quadratic is central-valued at every element.

The Two Surviving Quadratics

Two quadratic objects survive the loss of the scalar part, one from the scalar coordinates alone and one from the operator calculus.

Proposition (the scalar-part form). The form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t$ is a symmetric bilinear form of $\mathbb{H}$, of rank one, invariant under the operation, $\varphi(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q,\tilde r)+\varphi(\tilde q,\tilde p\diamond\tilde r)=0$, with radical the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$ and isotropic cone the same subspace.

Proof. The form reads the product of the scalar coordinates, so it is symmetric and bilinear, of Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0,0)$ and rank one. In each term of the invariance identity the first argument is a value of the block, hence of zero scalar part, so both terms vanish and the identity holds. The radical is the set of $\tilde x$ with $x_0y_0=0$ for every real $y_0$, that is the vector subspace of dimension three, which is also the isotropic cone, where $x_0^2=0$.

Proposition (the operator trace form). The form $K(\tilde A,\tilde B)=\operatorname{Tr}(L_{\tilde A}L_{\tilde B})$ is a symmetric bilinear form of $\mathbb{H}$ with $K(\tilde A,\tilde B)=3A_0B_0-2\sum_{k=1}^{3}A_kB_k=3A_0B_0-2(\vec A,\vec B)$, whose Gram matrix in the basis $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$ is $\operatorname{diag}(3,-2,-2,-2)$. It is non-degenerate of signature $(1,3)$, and it is not invariant under the operation.

Proof. The left multiplication $L_{\tilde A}$ has, in the basis $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z)$, the matrix with columns $\tilde A\diamond e_\nu$, namely rows $(0,0,0,0)$, $(-A_1,A_0,A_3,-A_2)$, $(-A_2,-A_3,A_0,A_1)$ and $(-A_3,A_2,-A_1,A_0)$. The trace of the product is $\sum_{i,k}(L_{\tilde A})_{ik}(L_{\tilde B})_{ki}$; the scalar coordinates contribute $3A_0B_0$ and the vector coordinates the negative pairing $-2(\vec A,\vec B)$, so the Gram matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(3,-2,-2,-2)$, non-degenerate of signature $(1,3)$. The invariance fails, with defect $K(\tilde A\diamond\tilde B,\tilde C)+K(\tilde B,\tilde A\diamond\tilde C)=-4A_0(\vec B,\vec C)+2B_0(\vec A,\vec C)+2C_0(\vec A,\vec B)$, which at the triple $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_x)$ is $-4\neq0$.

Remark (the operator invariants). The operator $L_{\tilde p}$ has, at every element, trace $3p_0$ and determinant $0$, and at every nonzero element rank three with kernel the real line $\mathbb{R}\tilde p$; this is the content of Annihilators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, cited here and used below through $K(e_t,e_t)=3$.

The Multiplicativity Failure at $(e_t,e_t)$

Definition (multiplicativity). A quadratic object $Q$ of the space is multiplicative for the operation when $Q(\tilde A\diamond\tilde B)=Q(\tilde A)Q(\tilde B)$ for all $\tilde A,\tilde B$. The equation is read at the single pair $(e_t,e_t)$ for the scalar-part form $\varphi$, the operator trace form $K$ and the quaternion form $N$.

Proposition (the failure at the pair $(e_t,e_t)$). At the pair $(\tilde A,\tilde B)=(e_t,e_t)$ the left side $Q(e_t\diamond e_t)$ vanishes for each of the three objects and the right side is $1$, $9$ and $1$ for $\varphi$, $K$ and $N$; the multiplicativity equation fails for all three.

$Q$ $Q(e_t\diamond e_t)$ $Q(e_t)Q(e_t)$ multiplicative at $(e_t,e_t)$
$\varphi(\tilde x)=x_0^2$ $0$ $1$ no
$K(\tilde x,\tilde x)=3x_0^2-2\lvert\vec x\rvert^2$ $0$ $9$ no
$N(\tilde x)=x_0^2+\lvert\vec x\rvert^2$ $0$ $1$ no

Proof. The square of $e_t$ in the block is $e_t\diamond e_t=0$ by alternation, and every quadratic object annihilates the origin, so the left side is zero in the three rows. On the right, $\varphi(e_t)=1$ gives $1$, $K(e_t,e_t)=3$ gives $9$, and $N(e_t)=1$ gives $1$. The three right-hand values are distinct, $\varphi$ forgetting the vector part, $K$ weighting the scalar coordinate by three and the vector coordinates by minus two, and $N$ weighting all four by one; since the two sides differ in each row, none is multiplicative.

The Invertibility Criterion Is Not Transferred

Remark (the criterion of the parent is not read here). The general quaternionic product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ is left-unital with left unit $e_t$, and its invertibility is decided by the non-vanishing of its algebraic norm $N$, the diagonal of $\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)=p_0q_t+(\vec p,\vec q)$ (Norm and Invertibility in the General Quaternionic Algebra). That form belongs to a different product, and the block has neither a unit to invert against nor an algebraic norm of its own, so no criterion is transferred. The absence is total in the further sense that every nonzero element is a zero divisor of the block (Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra), so the block is as far from a division algebra as its dimension allows.

Summary

The antisymmetric quaternionic multiplication $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$ has no unit on either side, hence no invertible element and an empty group of units, and the inverse equation $\tilde x\diamond\tilde y=e_t$ has no solution because every value is a pure vector. The algebraic norm of the operation is the diagonal of its scalar-part form; the scalar part vanishes identically, so the diagonal is the zero function, and a vanishing quadratic form is not an algebraic norm: the block carries no algebraic norm. The square vanishes identically and the conjugated square is the pure vector $-2x_0\vec x$, central exactly on the centre and the vector subspace. Two quadratic objects survive: the scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t$, of rank one, invariant, radical and isotropic cone the vector subspace; and the operator trace form $K(\tilde A,\tilde B)=3A_0B_0-2(\vec A,\vec B)$, non-degenerate of signature $(1,3)$ and not invariant. Neither is multiplicative, and at $(e_t,e_t)$ the left side vanishes while the right side is $1$, $9$, $1$ for $\varphi$, $K$ and $N$. The norm $N$ of the parent product is a form of a different product; no invertibility criterion is transferred, and the place of both the norm and the inverse is taken by their absence.

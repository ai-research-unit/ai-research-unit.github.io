Charon — Memory as Integration Constants (S56)

Audits inventory §9.1–§9.3. Sources: RC 020 §3.5, RC 036 §I, Esp 042 §2, Esp 098 (annexe). Feeds S55 and S57.

Introduction

The memory claim is stronger than an analogy and weaker than a mechanism: it rests on a real mathematical property of de Broglie's fusion formalism — a higher-spin state contains all the lower-spin states — so that "adding information without erasing the old" is a statement about a nested family of equations and needs no further assumption. That is the good news, and this article establishes it. The arithmetic is exact: $16^{8} = 4\,294\,967\,296$, which is the corpus's "4,3 milliards".

The bad news is of two kinds, and both are new. (1) The corpus prints two internally consistent but mutually inconsistent counting laws for the same field — one in chapter I (RC 020, followed by Esp 098) and one in chapters II–III (RC 035–036) — differing by a factor 16 on every state. At the photon, chapter I gives 16 components and chapters II–III give 256. (2) The memory has no capacity. The count is a count of field components; a component of a classical complex field is not a bit, and the corpus never gives an accumulation rate, a capacity, or a retrieval procedure. So "4,3 billion components" is not "4,3 Gbit", and a reader who quotes it as a memory size has converted a component count into a storage figure the corpus never claims.

The decomposition this article proposes. The inventory's §9.2 ("new initial conditions add to old ones instead of replacing them") should be split. Its nesting half is [MATH] and is de Broglie's: the fusion family is nested, so the state of higher spin contains the lower. Its no-erasure-over-time half is [SPEC]: it needs the negentropic imaginary time of §1.4, which is a premise of the model. Only the first half is needed for the memory to be cumulative, and the corpus has the first half. What the corpus does not have is the dynamics, and §9.3's "memory of the future" needs the second half and a mechanism on top of it.

1. What the corpus claims

(1) The memory is the set of integration constants of the field's equations. Esp 098: "Toutes les informations mémorisées sont enregistrées par le rayonnement électromagnétique enfermé dans la structure leptonique"; the memory is "les conditions complémentaires (constantes d'intégration) du système d'équations aux dérivées partielles qui fournissent une description de l'état de l'espace-temps de spin $n$ ($n=1,2,3,\ldots$)". And it grows: "L'action, et l'information, s'accroissent sans cesse grâce à l'augmentation de spin des photons du rayonnement électromagnétique leptonique."

(2) The count. RC 020 §3.5: "Si $2n$ est le spin du champ sigma, le nombre maximum de conditions initiales pour ce champ est $16^{2n}$, avec $n = 1/2,\ 1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 8\ldots$", and "par exemple, 4,3 milliards de composantes pour ce champ sigma pour $n = 4$". Esp 098: "Les équations du spin 2 sont au nombre de $16\times16 = 256$, et ainsi de suite."

(3) The nesting, which is what makes the memory cumulative. RC 036 §I: "Un tel boson est représenté par des équations $\Sigma_{2^{n}}$ ayant $16^{2^{n}}$ composantes, avec la particularité importante que les équations pour la structure $\Sigma_{2^{n}}$ contiennent les équations pour la structure $\Sigma_{2^{n-1}}$ : l'état d'action totale $2^{n}h$ contient les états $2h, 4h, 8h\ldots mh$, jusqu'à $m = 2^{n}$." Esp 098 draws the consequence and labels it: "l'état de spin $n$ contient la superposition de tous les états de spin plus petits que $n$ […]. On remarquera que cette particularité de « superposition » est aussi une propriété de l'esprit : une nouvelle information n'efface pas les informations anciennes (comme ce serait le cas dans une mémoire d'ordinateur), la nouvelle donnée est simplement ajoutée aux anciennes précédemment mémorisées."

(4) The source, credited by the corpus itself. Esp 098: "La théorie mathématique du rayonnement dans des états de spin plus élevés que 1 a, en fait, été déjà formulée il y a plus de 30 ans, principalement par Louis de Broglie et Marie-Antoinette Tonnelat."

(5) The negentropic premise. Esp 042 §2: "Tous les phénomènes dans $(\tilde r,\tilde t)$ évoluent avec une entropie qui n'est jamais croissante, ou encore avec une néguentropie non décroissante"; "Raisonner est, sans aucun doute, un phénomène néguentropique." RC 020 §3.5 reads this into the memory: "une mémoire cumulative puisque les conditions initiales nouvelles ne viennent pas se substituer aux anciennes (évolution néguentropique de l'espace-temps Imaginaire, qui ne laisse pas disparaître les informations mais les « intègre » dans le temps)."

(6) The prevision of the future. RC 020 §3.5: "Le champ sigma n'est pas seulement une mémoire cumulative du passé et du présent : c'est aussi une mémoire prévisionnelle du futur. En effet, la structure néguentropique de l'espace-temps Imaginaire entraîne que les conditions initiales du champ sigma à l'instant $t_{0}$ peuvent générer de l'information nouvelle, sous la forme du comportement logique de la particule : celle-ci est dotée à chaque instant $t$ d'une sorte de « vouloir », qui lui permet d'avoir un comportement pour atteindre une situation future."

2. The counts: two laws, printed, and the photon is the outlier

Three printed counts, two functional forms, and no single assignment satisfies all three.

the printed count where the state it is attached to
$16\times16 = 256$ RC 035 §4.2 the photon $\Sigma_{2}$, action $2h$, which the same page calls "spin $\hbar$ et pulse $\hbar$" (spin 1)
$16\times16 = 256$ Esp 098 "les équations du spin 2"
$4{,}3$ milliards $= 16^{8}$ RC 020 §3.5 the sigma field at $n = 4$, i.e. spin $2n = 8$

The two forms are:

  • Chapter I's law, $16^{s}$, with $s$ the spin: $16^{2n}$ where spin $= 2n$ gives 16, 256, 65 536, $4{,}29\times10^{9}$, $1{,}84\times10^{19}$ for spins 1, 2, 4, 8, 16.
  • Chapters II–III's law, $16^{2s}$, since the exponent is the action in units of $h$ and the action of a state is twice its spin: $16^{2^{n}}$ for action $2^{n}h$ gives 256, 65 536, $4{,}29\times10^{9}$, $1{,}84\times10^{19}$ for $n = 1,2,3,4$, i.e. for spins 1, 2, 4, 8.

Check each printed number against each law. Esp 098's spin-2 count of 256 matches $16^{s}$ ($16^{2}$) ✓ and not $16^{2s}$ ($16^{4} = 65\,536$) ✗. RC 020's $4{,}3$ milliards at spin 8 matches $16^{s}$ ($16^{8}$) ✓ and not $16^{2s}$ ($16^{16} = 1{,}84\times10^{19}$) ✗. RC 035's photon count of 256 matches $16^{2s}$ ($16^{2}$) ✓ and not $16^{s}$ ($16$) ✗. So chapter I and Esp 098 agree with each other; RC 035 and RC 036 agree with each other; and the two pairs differ by exactly one factor of 16 at every spin — the photon, the most elementary object in the family, is the single printed outlier.

The corpus itself gives the third and authoritative figure for that state, and it is neither. RC 035 adds: "l'analyse [7] montre qu'il y a seulement 215 composantes tensorielles non nulles, seulement 168 équations indépendantes, pour cet état d'action $2h$." So for the very state whose formal count is quoted as 256, de Broglie's analysis gives 215 non-zero tensor components and 168 independent equations — neither of which is a power of 16, and the corpus reports both without reconciling them with its own $16^{2}$. The honest conclusion is that all four numbers are formal counts of different things (components, equations, non-zero components, independent equations), that the corpus prints them side by side, and that the article should quote the state's count with its provenance attached. The one thing the article can settle is the arithmetic: $16^{8} = 4\,294\,967\,296$ ✓ and $16\times16 = 256$ ✓, so the printed numbers are correctly evaluated; what is ambiguous is which state each belongs to.

3. Why "cumulative" is not an added assumption

The nesting property does the work that §9.2's "instead of replacing" needs, and it does it without the negentropy. RC 036 states the property as a fact about the equations: the structure $\Sigma_{2^{n}}$ contains the structures $\Sigma_{2^{n-1}}, \ldots, \Sigma_{2}$. Esp 098 states the same property as a superposition of spin states, and adds the memory gloss with the word "aussi" — the corpus itself marks the step from mathematics to mind as an analogy, which is the honesty this article records rather than a defect it invents.

Three things follow, and the article keeps them apart.

  • Nesting is [MATH] and is de Broglie's. The family of fusion states is nested; a specific physical construction of the higher-spin field's equations (the fusion of $4^n$ components, or whatever count the corpus intends) is de Broglie's and Tonnelat's mathematics, published 30 years before L'Esprit.
  • "Adding without erasing" follows from nesting alone, for the family: if the state of higher spin contains the lower, then moving to a higher state cannot remove the lower state's content. This is a statement about a family of equations, not about a record. It is a genuine mathematical support and it needs no dynamics.
  • "Information is never erased over time" does not follow from nesting. That is the negentropic claim, and it needs the imaginary sector's thermodynamics (§1.4). So §9.2's tag line should be split: the nesting half is [MATH]/[EST] (de Broglie's), the no-erasure half is [SPEC] (rests on §1.4).

And a caution about what nesting is not. A nested family of equations is not a memory in any sense that carries capacity: nothing in the nesting says how many bits the particle holds, how they are addressed, or how a history is read back. The corpus's memory is a structural fact about a tower of fields, and the tower's height is indexed by the field's spin — which is also the particle's spin-like degree of freedom. That is a strong constraint in one direction (no memory without higher spin) and an empty one in every other. Esp 098's reference is to L'Esprit, cet inconnu (1977), chapter 15, "Le matricialisme" — the corpus's own fuller treatment, which the 1983 and 1987 books both cite rather than repeat.

4. What is [SPEC]: memory, negentropy, and the prevision of the future

§9.1's physical claim, "this realises a memory", is [SPEC], and the article's objection is not that it is wrong but that it is unquantified in the two places where it would have to be quantified.

  • Capacity. The count is components, not bits. A component of the corpus's complex field is a continuous quantity, so the "4,3 billion components" at spin 8 is not a storage figure at all. Any article that writes "4,3 Gbit" is writing something the corpus does not say and could not support: components are not bits, and the corpus gives no quantisation of them at the level where the memory is defined.
  • Dynamics. The corpus gives the ladder (spin $1, 2, 4, 8\dots$) and the nesting, but no law by which a physical history moves a particle up the ladder, and no rate. So "memory grows" is a direction without a rate, and S55's individual-behaviour claim inherits exactly this gap.

§9.2's negentropy is [SPEC] on the inventory's own reading of §1.4 — negentropy is a real concept for local order in an open system, not for an extra spacetime with its own thermodynamics. The article adds one internal observation: the memory claim does not need it, since nesting already gives the "add, don't replace" property. Keeping the negentropy in the memory argument therefore imports a premise the argument can do without — a presentational improvement the register should record, because it removes one [SPEC] from the memory's support and leaves the mathematics standing.

§9.3's "memory of the future" is [SPEC]/[PHIL], and the argument has a gap it can be charged with. "Les conditions initiales du champ sigma à l'instant $t_{0}$ peuvent générer de l'information nouvelle, sous la forme du comportement logique de la particule." Generating new information from initial conditions is what any deterministic system does; it is extrapolation, not prevision. For the field to be a memory of the future, the generated behaviour would have to anticipate a situation — and the corpus's own mechanism, the field's coupling to the particle's psi wave (S54), is fed by the Forms of the Real at times $t \le t_0$, i.e. by the past. So the "prevision" sentence needs something the corpus does not supply: a channel from future boundary conditions, or a negative-time propagation of the imaginary sector. The imaginary time reversal (§1.5) is the only candidate, and the corpus does not work it out.

One modern comparison, offered because it cuts against the corpus and the article should say so. The no-hiding theorem (Braunstein–Pati 2007) states that information lost from a subsystem is never destroyed but always redistributed into correlations with the rest of the system. That is the opposite of a cumulative local memory: it says information does not stay where it was stored, it moves into correlations. The corpus's memory is a local record kept in a particle's own field; the theorem is about erasure from a subsystem. The two are not in direct conflict — the corpus's field is not a subsystem from which the information is erased — but the comparison shows what a modern "information is never lost" claim looks like when it is a theorem with a stated content: it is a statement about correlations, not a capacity, which is exactly the shape the corpus's memory lacks.

Per rule R6: coherence passes (the nesting is real, the arithmetic is right, the corpus labels its own analogy); reproducibility fails (no rate, no capacity, no retrieval); correspondence is empty — a memory that is never read out makes no prediction, and the model's only observable claim in this neighbourhood is S55's, which is itself unquantified.

The article's one-line verdict: Charon's memory is a theorem about a nested family of field equations, read as a memory of the world — and the theorem is de Broglie's, the reading is the corpus's, and the corpus says so.

Summary

The corpus's memory is a real theorem read as a mind. Esp 098 states it: every memorised datum is stored as the complementary conditions — the integration constants — of the partial-differential system describing the state of spin $n$, and the higher-spin state contains the lower ones, so new data are added and old data are not erased. The nesting is de Broglie's and Tonnelat's mathematics, the corpus credits them, and it marks the step to "a property of the mind" as an analogy with the word aussi. This is the strongest thing that can be said for the memory claim, and it is stronger than the companion's [SPEC] tag suggests.

Its arithmetic is correct and its bookkeeping is not. $16^{8} = 4\,294\,967\,296$ is the printed "4,3 milliards" — verified — and $16\times16 = 256$ is correct arithmetic. But the corpus attaches 256 both to the photon (RC 035, action $2h$, spin 1) and to spin 2 (Esp 098), and these cannot both hold: chapter I's law is $16^{s}$ in the spin and chapters II–III's is $16^{2s}$, differing by a factor 16 at every state. For the photon's own count the corpus also reports de Broglie's figures — 215 non-zero tensor components, 168 independent equations — which match neither power. All four numbers are formal counts of different objects, printed side by side without reconciliation.

And the memory has no capacity and no dynamics. The count is components of a continuous field, not bits; there is no accumulation rate and no retrieval. So "4,3 billion components" must never be quoted as a storage figure, and "memory grows with spin" is a direction without a magnitude — the same gap S55 inherits for its individual behaviour.

Verdict. §9.1's count [MATH]; its bookkeeping internally inconsistent; "this realises a memory" [SPEC] and unquantified. §9.2 should be split: the nesting half [MATH]/[EST] (de Broglie's, and sufficient for "add, don't replace" without the negentropy), the no-erasure half [SPEC] on §1.4. §9.3 [SPEC]/[PHIL], since generating information from initial conditions is extrapolation, not prevision. Per R6: coherence passes, reproducibility fails, correspondence empty.

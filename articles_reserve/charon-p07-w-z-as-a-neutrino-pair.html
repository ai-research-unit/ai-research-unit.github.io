Charon: The W and Z as a Neutrino Pair

Introduction

This article is the corpus's best structural guess and its clearest missed identification, in the same paragraph. Charon says correctly that the weak interaction is carried by a virtual, massive, integer-spin boson — the historic "intermediate vector boson", which is what the $W$ and $Z$ are. He then says the boson is a $(\nu_{e}, \nu_{\mu})$ pair. The structure is right; the identity is wrong; and the particle it names was discovered in 1983, the year the first book appeared and four years before the second.

The claim, verbatim. RC 057, discussing $\mu^{-} \to e^{-}\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu}$: "On voit que la conversion fait appel à la formation intermédiaire d'un boson vecteur $(\nu_e, \nu_\mu)$, de spin total 1, qui se désintègre immédiatement en ses constituants, les deux neutrinos $\nu_e$ et $\nu_\mu$." The same construction is at the same place in Esp, §VI.18.1.

The verdict is [OUT] — the structure is right, the identity is wrong. The $W^{\pm}$ has a mass of $80{,}377$ GeV$/c^{2}$ and the $Z$ of $91{,}1876$ GeV$/c^{2}$; both were discovered at CERN in 1983 by the UA1 and UA2 experiments, with the Nobel Prize following in 1984. No neutrino pair can be the $Z$: the $Z$ is neutral, spin-1, and decays to every fermion–antifermion pair; and the $W^{+}$ is charged, so it cannot be a pair of neutral neutrinos at all.

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §6.6, sourced to RC ch. III §VIII.1 at RC/057_improved.md and Esp §VI.18.1. The transition-energy comparison is S49's. The related articles are P8 (the massless neutrino the pair is built from) and P18 (the boson spin identifications); the electroweak "single family" claim is inventory §6.7.

Conventions. Gaussian cgs. $\nu_{e}, \nu_{\mu}$ the electron and muon neutrinos; $\bar{\nu}$ an antineutrino; $W^{\pm}$ and $Z$ the weak intermediate vector bosons. Masses in GeV$/c^{2}$.

Part 1 — The structure Charon gets right

It is worth stating the correct part first, because the wrong part is only interesting against it.

A virtual intermediate boson. Charon writes the muon decay as $\mu^{-} \to e^{-} + (\nu_{e}, \nu_{\mu}) \to e^{-} + \nu_{e} + \nu_{\mu}$ (RC 3-115), a two-step process through a virtual state. This is the correct architecture: the weak interaction is mediated by a virtual boson, and the contact four-fermion interaction is the low-energy limit of its exchange.

Integer spin. He specifies "de spin total 1". The $W$ and $Z$ are spin-1. Correct.

Massive. The four-fermion interaction's strength and range require the mediator to be massive; Charon's "intermediate" boson is massive by construction, since it is a composite of two fermions with a binding energy. Correct in structure.

The name. "Boson vecteur intermédiaire" is exactly the historic name for the $W$ and $Z$, and the corpus uses it. The reader of 1987 encountering this paragraph would recognise the object being described.

Part 2 — The identity, and why no neutrino pair can fill it

The boson's identity fails on four independent counts, any one of which is fatal.

property measured $W^{\pm}$, $Z$ a $(\nu_{e}, \nu_{\mu})$ pair compatible?
electric charge $W$: $\pm 1$; $Z$: $0$ $0$ (both neutrinos neutral) $Z$ only
mass $80{,}4$ and $91{,}2$ GeV $0$ if neutrinos are massless (P8) no
decays to all fermion pairs two neutrinos no
spin $1$ $1$ (two spin-½) yes

The charge alone excludes the $W$. A pair of neutral neutrinos has zero charge, so it cannot be the $W^{+}$ or $W^{-}$. The corpus's construction is neutral throughout; it has no charged boson at all. Yet the charged-current weak interaction — the one that changes flavour, which is the very process Charon is describing — requires a charged mediator.

The mass excludes the pair, given the corpus's own neutrino. The corpus's neutrinos are exactly massless (P8). A pair of massless particles can have a non-zero invariant mass only if the two are not collinear; but a bound "pair" with a defined rest mass requires binding energy, and the corpus gives the pair no interaction energy of its own. With massless constituents the pair's mass is zero unless binding supplies it — and the corpus's whole point is that the neutrinos are the free, massless, light-like states. So the mass of the $W$ and $Z$ has no source in the construction. P7 and P8 are the same failure seen twice.

The decays exclude the pair decisively. The $Z$ decays to $e^{+}e^{-}$, $\mu^{+}\mu^{-}$, $\tau^{+}\tau^{-}$, and quark–antiquark pairs; the $W$ decays to a charged lepton and a neutrino, or to quarks. These are the signatures by which the $W$ and $Z$ were discovered, and a two-neutrino state cannot produce them. LEP's precision measurements of the $Z$'s partial widths into each fermion species would be meaningless if the $Z$ were a neutrino pair.

Part 3 — The discovery Charon was writing past

The dating makes this the sharpest of the corpus's missed identifications.

1983: the discovery. The $W$ was found by UA1 at CERN in January 1983, through the decay $W \to e\nu$; the $Z$ was found by UA1 in May 1983, through $Z \to e^{+}e^{-}$. The Nobel Prize to Rubbia and van der Meer followed in 1984. Esp, published in 1983, may plausibly have been too late to revise; RC, published in 1987, was not. Four years after the discovery, and two years after the Nobel, the second book still describes the weak boson as a $(\nu_{e}, \nu_{\mu})$ pair — a state that the discovery's own decay channels exclude.

The near-miss already in the corpus. S49 notes that the corpus's level-transition energies include $68{,}9$ GeV, which is 14 % below the $W$ and 24 % below the $Z$. So one of the corpus's own numbers comes close to the boson masses — and the corpus does not make the comparison. The identification it needed was sitting in its own table, under a different heading. The break-off point S49 records ("…des énergies de l'ordre de 4 GeV : rappelons que") is exactly where the comparison was abandoned.

Part 4 — What survives, and what breaks

The electroweak "single family" claim is not damaged by this. Inventory §6.7 records that Charon concluded electromagnetic and weak interactions were one family of virtual-boson exchanges. That conclusion is right (Glashow–Salam–Weinberg 1967–68), even though his route does not reproduce the gauge structure, the Weinberg angle, or the $W$ and $Z$ masses. The conclusion is [EST]; the route and the boson identity are [OUT].

P18 collects the boson identifications. The gluon as a préon pair, the graviton as a préon pair and the claim that they are "globally of spin zero" are P18's; this article owns only the weak boson. The two articles share the diagnosis: the corpus correctly sees a family of virtual bosons and mis-assigns each one's constituents.

What a reissue may keep. The architecture — virtual, massive, integer-spin mediator — with the identity corrected to the gauge bosons, and the correct statement that the four-fermion Fermi interaction is its low-energy limit. Nothing else in the construction survives.

Summary

Charon describes the weak interaction as mediated by a virtual, massive, spin-1 boson, and identifies that boson as a $(\nu_{e}, \nu_{\mu})$ pair. The structure is right and the name — intermediate vector boson — is the historic name for the object; the identity is wrong. The $W^{\pm}$ and $Z$ have masses of $80{,}4$ and $91{,}2$ GeV, were discovered at CERN in 1983 (Nobel 1984), and decay to all fermion pairs; a neutral two-neutrino pair can be neither the charged $W$ nor the $Z$, and with the corpus's massless neutrinos (P8) it cannot carry the mass either. RC (1987) still makes the claim four years after the discovery, while one of the corpus's own transition energies, 68.9 GeV, already sat near the $W$ and $Z$ masses. Verdict [OUT] — structure right, identity wrong.

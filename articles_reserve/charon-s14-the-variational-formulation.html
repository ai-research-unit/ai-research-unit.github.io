Charon — The Variational Formulation (S14)

Audits §2.9 of CHARON_IDEAS_AND_CLAIMS.md, against RC (2-5)–(2-13) (scan 022), RC (5-41)–(5-46) (scan 077), and Esp (30) (scan 015).

Introduction

The corpus's second axiom is that energy conservation is imposed by a stationary-action principle on a local action density $U = dA/dV_{4}$, not by postulating an energy-momentum tensor. §2.9 tags this [MIX]: the variational mathematics is standard, the claim that it replaces $T_{\mu\nu}$ as physical content is not.

This article checks the axiom against its two uses in the corpus and finds that they differ in kind. In the quantum derivation the variational principle is declared and then not used — the equation for $U$ is obtained by an identification, not by extremising anything. In the cosmology it is genuinely used, and it works: it yields the corpus's best-verified identity (5-46). So axiom 2 is idle where the corpus's quantum claim needed it, and load-bearing where it yields an integral. That asymmetry is the finding.

What the Corpus Claims

§2.9 — energy conservation is imposed via a stationary-action principle on a local action density $U = dA/dV_{4}$, not via a postulated $T_{\mu\nu}$. RC (2-5)–(2-6). Tag: [MIX].

The objects, in the corpus's own words.

  • RC (2-6): $U = dA/dV_{4}$ — "un lagrangien local ... une densité quadridimensionnelle d'action, représentant l'action élémentaire $dA$ contenue dans un volume élémentaire d'espace-temps $dV_{4}$. $U$ est une fonction holomorphe ... des coordonnées d'espace-temps."
  • RC (2-7) defines the normal tangent space $\eta_{P}$ by diagonalising and normalising the metric to signature $(-,-,-,+)$, and the corpus makes a weight argument that is correct: $A$ is an invariant and $dV_{4}$ a pseudo-scalar of weight $-1$, so $U$ is a pseudo-scalar of weight $+1$ and an invariant under Lorentz transformations in $\eta_{P}$. That is correct differential geometry and it is worth recording.

The Two Uses, and They Differ in Kind

Use 1 — the quantum derivation: declared, then not used

The corpus's chain to the equation for $U$ proceeds as follows, and at no point is an action extremised.

  1. RC (2-8) — the equation $U$ is to satisfy in the normal tangent space is "l'équation la plus simple": $\Box^{2}U + C_{1}U^{2} + \dots + C_{n}U^{n} = 0$, with the $C_{n}$ real coefficients characterising the geometry at $P$, and $C_{1}$ having "les dimensions d'une courbure scalaire".
  2. RC (2-9) contracts the field equations: $g^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta} - (R - 4\Lambda) = 0$; (2-10) is the same in $\eta_{P}$; (2-11) multiplies each term by $U$.
  3. RC (2-12) then identifies (2-11) with (2-8), and the corpus labels the identification: "L'identification une nouvelle fois la plus simple (conditions suffisantes, sinon nécessaires)". It reads: $\mathbb{T}_{\alpha\alpha}\cdot U = -\partial^{2}_{\alpha}U$, $R - 4\Lambda = C_{1}$, $C_{n} = 0$ for $n > 1$.
  4. RC (2-13) follows: $\Box^{2}U + (R - 4\Lambda)U = 0$.

Two findings, and the first is a defect of the derivation rather than of the physics.

  • The stationary-action principle is not applied. A variational derivation would write a Lagrangian and extremise it. The corpus instead writes a single-term action density $U = dA/dV_{4}$ and then chooses $U$'s equation by "simplicity". Axiom 2 is therefore idle exactly where the corpus's quantum claim needed it: the equation $U$ obeys is selected, not derived. The register already flags the neighbouring case — "Axiome 2 (stationary action) is never applied to the split" — and this article generalises it: axiom 2 is not applied to the derivation of the $U$ equation either. So the axiom which is supposed to replace the energy-momentum tensor does no work in the sector where the tensor was supposed to be replaced.
  • And the printed (2-8) does not reduce to the printed (2-13) — unless one exponent has wandered, and it has. (2-8) is nonlinear as printed — its first written term is $C_{1}U^{2}$, and the series runs to $C_{n}U^{n}$ — while (2-13) is linear in $U$. Setting $C_{n} = 0$ for $n>1$ and $C_{1} = R - 4\Lambda$ then leaves $\Box^{2}U + (R-4\Lambda)U^{2} = 0$, which is not (2-13). So as printed, the corpus's chain (2-8) → (2-12) → (2-13) does not close. Four independent arguments say the defect is a stray exponent 2 on $C_{1}U$, and together they settle it. (i) Downstream use: (2-13) becomes (2-74), the Klein–Gordon equation, and S15 verified the passage exactly — a nonlinear (2-13) would break that, so (2-13) is the form the corpus needs. (ii) The corpus's own dimensional remark, which excludes the nonlinear reading at every order: it states that $C_{1}$ "possède les dimensions d'une courbure scalaire (inverse du carré d'une longueur)", and Esp (30) states it of all the coefficients, "$C_n$ est homogène à une courbure scalaire ($L^{-2}$)". With $\Box^{2}U$ of dimension $U/L^{2}$, a term $C_{n}U^{n}$ requires $C_{n}$ of dimension $L^{-2}U^{1-n}$, which is a curvature only for $n = 1$. The corpus's own parenthetical therefore describes the linear term — and it excludes the printed nonlinearity at every order, not merely at the first. (iii) Two further printings wander, in opposite directions — RC 032's (2-71) prints $\Box^{2}U^{2} + (R-4\Lambda)U = \Box^{2}U + (M_{0}^{2}c^{2}/\hbar^{2})U = 0$, the same exponent 2 now on the $\Box^{2}U$ term and equated away in the same line, and Esp 015's (30), the page on which the equation is introduced, prints the linear form in full: $\Box^{2}U + C_{1}U + C_{2}U^{2} + \cdots = 0$. (iv) The pattern is the signature of migration, not of error. Three placements of the same 2 across four printings, with the introduction-page printing linear and the target form linear, is what an exponent does in transcription. A corpus that had intended a nonlinear series would not have written the linear form on the page where it defines the equation, nor the linear target, nor the equals sign. Conclusion: read the series as $C_{1}U + C_{2}U^{2} + \dots$, which is what (2-12) requires, what (2-13) prints, and what Esp (30) prints at the point of definition. Recorded as a closed OCR item rather than an open question, and filed in S5's Hazard list as Hazard 9: the four printings of (2-8), (30), (2-13) and (2-71) disagree on where the square sits, and only one reading closes the chain. (The scan check is no longer needed. Esp (30) is legible in both transcription passes, and (2-71)'s line supplies the corpus's own statement that the two forms are the same object.)

Use 2 — the cosmology: genuinely used, and it works

In chapter V the corpus does something different, and it is a real variational statement. RC (5-41):

$$(5\text{-}41)\quad A = \int_{0}^{T} 2\pi^{2}c^{2}R^{3}\left(\frac{D_{\rho}}{c^{2}} + \frac{D_{M}}{R^{4}}\right)d\tau \;+\; 2\pi(S + P) = 0 ,$$

with $S$ the spin of the universe over one period of its pulsation (5-43) and $P$ its pulse (5-44) — each "l'action totale (divisée par $2\pi$)" associated with one of the two motions of every point of real space. And then RC (5-45):

$$(5\text{-}45)\quad A = S = P = 0 .$$

This is the corpus's conservation principle in action form, and it is what it looks like when the axiom is actually used. Its output is (5-46), the zero-total-energy identity — and S50 verified that identity numerically against the corpus's own published $\rho$, $M$ and $R$, recovering the corpus's own numbers to the factor it should. So where the variational principle is used, it produces the corpus's single best-verified formal identity.

But note what it is used for. RC 077 obtains (5-45) from the equations of motion; it is a first integral, not a new principle.* "A variational principle used to integrate equations already known is a mathematical convenience, not physical content." And that is precisely the gap §2.9's [MIX] tag names: the mathematics is standard and correct, and the claim that it replaces $T_{\mu\nu}$ as the physical content is not established anywhere in either use.**

Findings

  1. Axiom 2 is declared but idle in the derivation of the $U$ equation. The equation is chosen by "simplicity" and fixed by an identification (2-12), not derived from stationarity. The axiom does not do the job it was introduced for.
  2. The "simplest equation" step is the least controlled step in the corpus. (2-12) is labelled "conditions suffisantes, sinon nécessaires" — the corpus's own admission that a sufficient condition is being used as if necessary — and it is the same identification S7 records as the first of the programme's recurring "simple declared choice" pattern.
  3. A stray exponent 2 wanders between four printings of the same equation — (2-8)'s $C_{1}U^{2}$, (2-13)'s $(R-4\Lambda)U$, (2-71)'s $\Box^{2}U^{2}$, and Esp (30)'s linear $C_{1}U + C_{2}U^{2} + \cdots$ — and only the reading $C_{1}U + C_{2}U^{2} + \dots$ closes the chain (2-8) → (2-12) → (2-13). The corpus's own dimensional remark on the $C_{n}$ supports it independently, and excludes the nonlinear reading at every order. Closed: the definition-page printing and the target form agree, and (2-71) equates the two forms itself. Filed as S5's Hazard 9.
  4. Where the axiom is used, it works — (5-45) is a genuine stationary action and its output (5-46) checks numerically (S50). Credit.
  5. The weight argument (2-6)–(2-7) is correct mathematics — $U$ = dA/dV₄ with $A$ invariant and $dV_{4}$ of weight −1 gives $U$ of weight +1, Lorentz-invariant in $\eta_{P}$. Credit, and it is the cleanest technical result in this block.
  6. What the axiom therefore delivers is an integral and a re-interpretation; what it does not deliver is a replacement for $T_{\mu\nu}$. §2.9's [MIX] tag stands, with the two halves now separated.

Verdict

A real axiom, correctly applicable in one sector, idle in the sector that needed it, and nowhere doing what the corpus says it does.

  • What is to the corpus's credit. (a) The action-density construction (2-6)–(2-7) is correct, including the pseudo-scalar weight argument. (b) The cosmological action (5-41) with $A = S = P = 0$ is a genuine extremal statement, and it yields (5-46) — an identity which S50 confirmed against the corpus's own published numbers. (c) The corpus labels its own weakest step honestly ("conditions suffisantes, sinon nécessaires"). A programme that flags its own soft joints is a programme whose soft joints can be tested.
  • Where it fails. The axiom is introduced as a replacement for the postulated energy-momentum tensor; but the source tensor is still written down by hand (S7/§2.2), and in the quantum sector the equation comes from an identification rather than from the principle. So the corpus both keeps the postulated tensor and does not apply the principle that was to replace it. The claim is not thereby false; it is simply not made good anywhere, and §2.9's [MIX] is the correct verdict.
  • Net. §2.9's mathematics is [EST]; its role as the theory's physical foundation is unestablished. The article's contribution: the axiom's two uses differ in kind, and the one that works yields an integral while the one that was supposed to yield the physics does not use it.

Summary of Notation

  • $U$ — the local action density, $U = dA/dV_{4}$ (2-6); a pseudo-scalar of weight $+1$; invariant in the normal tangent space $\eta_{P}$.
  • $dV_{4}$ — elementary 4-volume of spacetime; $A$ — the action, an invariant.
  • $\eta_{P}$, $\eta_{\alpha\beta}$ — the normal tangent space at $P$ and its metric, signature $(-,-,-,+)$ (2-7).
  • $C_{1}, \dots, C_{n}$ — real coefficients of the "simplest equation" (2-8), characterising the local geometry; $C_{1}$ has the dimensions of a scalar curvature.
  • $S$, $P$ — the spin and the pulse of the universe as a whole, (5-43) and (5-44), each "l'action totale divisée par $2\pi$".
  • $\mathbb{T}_{\alpha\beta}$ — the geometric tensor expressed in $\eta_{P}$.

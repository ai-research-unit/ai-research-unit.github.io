The Up and Low Proportions of the Physics Corpus

The corpus is the 403 Markdown files of articles_physics. The count reads only the mathematics regions, inline and display: 105,571 regions holding 3,188,809 characters. One mark is one caret or one underscore.

Mark Count Share
Raised 40,524 25.3%
Low 119,756 74.7%
All 160,280 100%

The rest of the article sorts the marks into what the convention validates and what it leaves open. The validation is about the job of the mark, not about its height alone.

Non-Validated

Upper

One class of caret is open: the name.

Class What it carries Count Share
Name names and grades — $\mathrm{Cl}^i$, $K^0$, $M_4^\sigma$, $B^\sigma$ — and labels ${}^{(l)}$, ${}^{\mathrm{adj}}$, ${}^{abc}$ 4,238 10.6%

The corpus raises a name 4,238 times and writes one low 5,784 times: 42.2% up, 57.8% low. About 300 of the raised cases are colour, gauge or spin indices, which the strict reading of the rule calls frame slots and debt: $f^{abc}$ (72), $G^a$ (37), $T^a$ (36), $\mu^a$ (36), $\sigma^z$ (31), $\pi^a$ (26), $\sigma^x$ and $\sigma^y$ (20), $B^a$ (13), $A^a$ (11) and $c^a$ (11). Without them the names are about 3,940 up against 5,784 low: 40% up and 60% low.

The Upper-Index Problem reports 0 raised frame slots in physics: 8 carets on the Greek letters, all legitimate. That screen keeps only the carets on $i,j,k,l$ and on the Greek letters, and it discards every power, grade, coframe and dual. The census here keeps all of them. The two figures measure different populations: the 72 carets that the earlier screen counted on those letters are 0.18% of 40,524, and the rewritten screen keeps 8. The screen does not read $x$, $y$, $z$ or $a$, so a raised $\sigma^z$ or $T^a$ falls outside it and is recorded here as a raised name.

Lower

Two classes of low mark are open.

Class What it carries Count Share
Upright role name $\mathrm{tot}$, $\mathrm b$, $\mathrm L$, $\mathrm r$ 5,784 4.9%
Operator or derivative index $\sum_\mu$, $\partial_\mu$ 4,316 3.7%

The upright role names are the low names of the count above. They are written low in every case, and the open question is whether a role such as $\mathrm{tot}$ or $\mathrm L$ is a name, which the rule would raise, or a label of a slot, which the rule keeps low.

The operator indices are the indices of a sum or a derivative. They are not frame slots, and no article writes them raised. They are counted apart because the rule does not name them.

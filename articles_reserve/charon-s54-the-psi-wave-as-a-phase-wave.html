Charon — The ψ Wave as a Phase Wave (S54)

Audits inventory §2.7. Sources: RC 034 §§2–3, RC 035 §3, RC 036 §4.3; Esp 034 §1.2, Esp 035 §3, Esp 036 §§3–4. [BRIDGE]

Introduction

The ψ wave is the one object in Charon's formalism that standard quantum mechanics already owns, and the corpus says so. What it adds is a source for ψ — the imaginary oscillator Σ — and a reading of ψ as a real phase wave that transports no momentum-energy. This article separates three things the corpus runs together: the kinematics of a de Broglie phase wave, the derivation of ψ from Σ, and the interpretation of ψ as the particle's "regard" of the world. The first is verified by the corpus's own equations, four times over; the second is promised and never written; the third is the de Broglie–Bohm reading stated in Charon's own vocabulary.

The phase wave is not an exotic object, and the article's first job is to say so with numbers. A material particle's phase velocity is $E/p$, which is $\ge c$ for every massive particle in every inertial frame, and a room-temperature electron's ψ phase velocity is about $3{,}1\times10^{3}\,c$ — while its group velocity, the velocity that carries energy and that any experiment sees, is $95$ km/s. So "superluminal phase, subluminal group" is not a Charon claim at all: it is de Broglie's relation $\tilde Vv = c^{2}$, and the corpus states that product explicitly (Esp 036). What is Charon's is where the phase wave comes from and what it is for.

The article's verdict, stated at the top. The kinematics is [EST], and the corpus's printed relations are exact identities: (2-83) is $E/p$, (2-84) collapses to $\nu = E/h$, Esp 035's (142) is one equation in two dresses, and (154) recovers the relativistic momentum. The derivation of ψ from Σ is [SPEC], and the corpus's own wording shows why: RC 034 says one may expect to obtain ψ's propagation equation from (2-81), and the equation never appears. The pilot-wave reading is [SPEC]/interpretation — legitimate, non-mainstream, and empirically indistinguishable from orthodox quantum theory as the corpus states it. So the [BRIDGE] cut recommendation survives only if the audit is also cut, and it should not be: the audit is what makes the identities citable and the missing equation visible.

1. What the corpus claims

The claim has four parts, and they are printed in two books with one symbol change.

(1) The phase wave. "Les phases de l'oscillateur Σ se répartissent dans l'espace Réel comme les phases d'une onde sphérique à vitesse infinie et de longueur infinie, que nous appellerons onde psi" (RC 034 §2, particle at rest). "Cette onde […] est une onde de phase, qui ne transporte aucune impulsion-énergie (et qui possède d'ailleurs une vitesse de propagation jamais inférieure à c)" (RC 034 §3).

(2) Its kinematics in a general frame. RC 035 writes the observed phase velocity and frequency for an observer $O$ for whom $P$ has momentum $p$:

$$\bar{v} = \frac{(M_0^{2}c^{4}+p^{2}c^{2})^{1/2}}{p} \tag{2-83}$$

$$\nu = \frac{cM_0\bar{v}}{h\left(\bar{v}^{2}/c^{2}-1\right)^{1/2}} \tag{2-84}$$

Esp 035 §3 repeats both with $m_0$ for $M_0$ — (143) and (144) — and adds the momentum form (142), $p = \Omega_0 idc/\sqrt{1-\tilde V^{2}/c^{2}} = \Omega_0 dc/\sqrt{\tilde V^{2}/c^{2}-1}$, and the rest-frame statement: "Si $p = 0$ […] la vitesse de phase $\tilde V$ de $\Sigma_0$ est infinie. Ceci signifie encore que $\Sigma_0$ est […] une onde stationnaire sphérique de longueur infinie."

(3) The group. Esp 036 §4 states the product — "un groupe d'ondes à vitesse de groupe $V = c^{2}/\bar{V}$" — and derives the hadron's momentum from the superposition of the Σ waves, (154):

$$p = \sum_{\mathrm{vol}\,\Omega}\frac{h\nu}{\bar{V}} = \frac{V}{c^{2}}\sum_{\Omega}h\nu_0 = \frac{\mu_0 V}{\sqrt{1-V^{2}/c^{2}}} \qquad (\mu_0c^{2} = \sum_{\Omega}h\nu_0) \tag{154}$$

(4) The source and the reading. "Cet « oscillateur » (que nous avons nommé Σ) génère à son tour dans l'espace Réel une onde de phase ψ, dont on peut s'attendre à pouvoir obtenir l'équation de propagation à partir des équations (2-81) représentant l'oscillateur Σ, qui est la « source » de ψ. Nous nous proposons de montrer que l'onde ψ est l'onde probabiliste habituelle considérée par la Mécanique ondulatoire et d'établir la relation entre ψ et Σ" (RC 034 §2). And its purpose: "l'onde de phase psi et ses conditions initiales constituent un véritable « regard » de la particule P, qui « renseigne » celle-ci sur les Formes de la totalité de son monde « extérieur », à chaque instant t du Réel" (RC 034 §2).

2. The four identities, checked

Every relation in §1 is an exact identity, and each is a check on the corpus's algebra rather than on its physics.

(a) (2-83)/(143) is $E/p$, the standard de Broglie phase velocity. With $E = (M_0^{2}c^{4}+p^{2}c^{2})^{1/2}$, the printed form is $E/p$. Verified by inspection. The consequence is the corpus's own inequality $\tilde V\ge c$: since $E/p = c^{2}/v$ for the particle's velocity $v = pc^{2}/E$, one has $\tilde V = c^{2}/v\ge c$, with equality only for $M_0 = 0$.

(b) (2-84)/(144) collapses to $\nu = E/h$. Evaluate $\tilde V^{2}/c^{2}-1$ using $\tilde V = E/p$:

$$\frac{\tilde V^{2}}{c^{2}}-1 = \frac{E^{2}-p^{2}c^{2}}{p^{2}c^{2}} = \frac{M_0^{2}c^{4}}{p^{2}c^{2}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \left(\frac{\tilde V^{2}}{c^{2}}-1\right)^{1/2} = \frac{M_0c^{2}}{pc}.$$

Substituting in (2-84): $\nu = \dfrac{cM_0\,(E/p)\,pc}{h\,M_0c^{2}} = \dfrac{E}{h}$. The corpus's frequency law is the de Broglie frequency, exactly. This is worth stating plainly because the printed form hides it: there is no separate frequency postulate in the corpus.

(c) Esp 035's (142) is one equation in two dresses. The two printed forms are $\Omega_0 idc/\sqrt{1-\tilde V^{2}/c^{2}}$ and $\Omega_0 dc/\sqrt{\tilde V^{2}/c^{2}-1}$. For $\tilde V>c$ one has $\sqrt{1-\tilde V^{2}/c^{2}} = i\sqrt{\tilde V^{2}/c^{2}-1}$, so the $i$ in the numerator cancels the $i$ in the root and the two forms are equal. Verified. The sign is carried by $i^{2} = -1$ inside the corpus's own step, not by a convention added here.

(d) (154) is the relativistic momentum. With the time-dilated frequency $\nu = \nu_0/\sqrt{1-V^{2}/c^{2}}$ and $\tilde V = c^{2}/V$:

$$\sum_\Omega \frac{h\nu}{\tilde V} = \frac{V}{c^{2}}\sum_\Omega \frac{h\nu_0}{\sqrt{1-V^{2}/c^{2}}} = \frac{\mu_0V}{\sqrt{1-V^{2}/c^{2}}}.$$

Verified, and the corpus's intermediate equality $(V/c^{2})\sum_\Omega h\nu_0$ is the same line. The superposition of Σ phase waves reproduces the momentum of a body of proper mass $\mu_0$ moving at $V$ — de Broglie's group-velocity theorem, recovered rather than postulated.

And the "infinite speed, infinite wavelength" of RC 034 is the $p\to0$ limit of (2-83), where $\tilde V\to\infty$ and $\lambda = h/p\to\infty$; the two books' descriptions agree and neither needs a separate assumption. Four printed relations, four passes.

Every row is a textbook consequence of $\tilde Vv = c^{2}$. For the thermal row: $p = \sqrt{2m_0k_BT} = 8{,}69\times10^{-26}$ kg m s⁻¹, giving $v = p/m_0 = 95{,}4$ km s⁻¹ and $\tilde V = c^{2}/v = 9{,}4\times10^{11}$ m s⁻¹ $\approx 3{,}1\times10^{3}c$. This is the number that settles the interpretive question: a superluminal phase velocity belongs to every matter wave ever written down, so a model that produces one has produced nothing new. The corpus's contribution is not (2-83); it is the derivation of (2-83) from (2-81) — and that derivation is one line, the change $d\to id$ (Esp 034 §1.2.b, (137): $x^{a} = i\tilde Z^{a}$).

Why the $d\to id$ step matters for the corpus's argument. Esp 034 §1.2.b says the imaginary density turns the oscillator at $M$ into a phase velocity "comprise entre $c$ et l'infini". That is correct, and it is the corpus's own route to the bound: the complex spacetime supplies the imaginary density, the imaginary density supplies the phase velocity, and the phase velocity supplies the probabilistic reading. So the phase wave is not imported into the model; it is generated by the model's central postulate — the strongest form of the corpus's claim, and one that survives §4's objection, because the kinematics is exactly what one wants a generated quantity to reproduce.

4. What is [SPEC]: the generator, and the equation that is never written

The derivation of ψ is announced, not performed. RC 034's wording is a promise — "dont on peut s'attendre à pouvoir obtenir l'équation de propagation" — one may expect to obtain the propagation equation for ψ from (2-81). What the corpus then supplies is (2-85)–(2-87), which are an energy normalisation, not a propagation equation. ψ's own wave equation is never written, and neither is (2-81) made explicit as ψ's source. Esp 035 continues in the same direction: after (143)/(144) it writes "cette représentation du phénomène observé en M est exactement celle qui avait été postulée par la Mécanique ondulatoire" — an identification of the corpus's output with de Broglie's postulate, not a derivation of it. The honest ledger entry: the corpus derives the kinematics of ψ and identifies ψ with the quantum probability amplitude; it does not derive the amplitude's equation.

What this does to the claim. The companion tags §2.7 as [MIX] — mathematics [EST], pilot-wave reading legitimate but non-mainstream, derivation of ψ from Σ [SPEC]. The audit sharpens the third part: the derivation is [SPEC] because it does not exist as a derivation — it is an expectation followed by a normalisation. Two further points are worth recording, because they are usually lost in the interpretation debate.

  • The "no momentum-energy transported" clause is internally coherent. RC 034–035 deny that ψ carries momentum-energy while (2-85) integrates $\Sigma^{2}/8\pi$ — the oscillator's energy — over $\omega$. There is no conflict: Σ holds the energy, ψ is the phase, and (2-87) makes $\psi^{2}$ the distribution of that energy normalised by its total. The corpus is careful here, and the clause is a statement about the phase, not a claim that ψ is unphysical.
  • The "regard" is not a hidden-variable postulate of the usual kind. The corpus says ψ's initial conditions are the Forms of the Real at each $t$ (RC 034 §2) — data collected from the particle's world. That is a read-only coupling: ψ learns the world, and this chapter does not yet say that ψ steers the particle. Steering appears only with the sigma field and the memory chapter — S55's and S56's subject, not this article's. S54 must therefore not be read as the model's hidden-variable claim; it is the model's information channel.

5. What this article adds, and the [BRIDGE] line

Non-overlap, stated explicitly. subluminal-and-superluminal-electromagnetic-waves-and-the-lepton-mass-spectrum treats the phase and group velocities of electromagnetic waves in the biquaternion formalism, where the superluminal case is a dispersion branch; it contains no oscillator Σ, no equations (2-81), no ψ/Σ relation and no de Broglie frequency. the-free-particle-and-wave-packets-in-biquaternionic-form treats wave packets in the biquaternion Dirac theory; it has packet kinematics but no pilot-wave reading and no corpus equations (2-83)–(2-87). The corpus's reading of the norm, (2-85)–(2-87), is owned by S53 and is used here only where the phase wave needs it (one line, §4); the memory reading of the same field is S56's.

And one warning for readers of the later articles. The phrase "ψ is a real phase wave" must not be allowed to carry the model's empirical weight. As §3 shows, a real phase wave with $\tilde V\ge c$ is what a classical matter wave already is, so the phrase commits Charon to nothing observable. What commits him to something is the sigma field — S55's collective/individual split and S56's cumulative memory — and those are where the model either becomes physics or stays a vocabulary.

Summary

The ψ wave is de Broglie's, and the corpus proves it four times over. RC 035's (2-83) is the phase velocity $E/p$; (2-84) reduces exactly to the frequency $\nu = E/h$; Esp 035's two printed forms of (142) are one equation; and Esp 036's (154) recovers $p = \mu_0V/\sqrt{1-V^{2}/c^{2}}$ from the superposition of Σ waves. All four checks pass, and the same section carries the identity the textbooks carry: $\tilde Vv = c^{2}$, so the phase is superluminal and the group subluminal for every massive particle. A 300 K electron's phase velocity is $3{,}1\times10^{3}c$ — a fact of de Broglie kinematics, not of Charon's model.

What is Charon's is the source and the purpose. The phase wave is generated by the imaginary oscillator Σ through the change $d\to id$ (Esp 034 (137)), which is where the corpus's own $\ge c$ bound lives; and it is read as the particle's "regard" of the Forms of the Real. The first is a genuine, if one-line, derivation, and it is the chapter's strongest structural claim. The second is a reading, and the corpus's wording for the derivation of ψ's equation is an expectation — "on peut s'attendre à pouvoir obtenir" — that is never fulfilled. No propagation equation for ψ appears in RC 034–036.

Verdict: [EST]/[MATH] for the kinematics; [SPEC] for the generator, asserted and never computed; [SPEC]/interpretation for the pilot-wave reading, legitimate and non-mainstream; and empirically empty either way, because the chapter's ψ-wave content is de Broglie's and the model's own content is the sigma field, which is S55's and S56's subject. Per R6: coherence passes, reproducibility passes for the kinematics and is empty for the generator, correspondence is available only for the parts that are not Charon's.

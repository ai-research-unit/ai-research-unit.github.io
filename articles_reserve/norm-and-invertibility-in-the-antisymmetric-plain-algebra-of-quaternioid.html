Norm and Invertibility in the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra

Introduction

A norm and an inverse are read in a product, and the equations name it. The block of this article is the antisymmetric plain multiplication

$$ \tilde p\times\tilde q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p\tilde q-\tilde q\tilde p\bigr)=\vec p\times\vec q, $$

the cross product of the two vector parts, the antisymmetric half of the general plain bilinear product; it is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear, alternating, of zero scalar part and without a unit (Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions). The classical theory belongs to a unital associative product, and the block has neither; this article states what that costs and what survives.

The answer is an absence and a substitute. The block has no unit on either side, hence no inverse and an empty group of units, and no algebraic norm, its square and its conjugated square both vanishing identically. The quadratic objects it does carry are the operator trace form $K(\tilde a,\tilde b)=\operatorname{Tr}(L_{\tilde a}L_{\tilde b})=-2\,\vec a\cdot\vec b$ and the scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t$; the first is the invariant form of the block and the Killing form of the bracket, the second the rank-one form whose radical is the vector subspace, and neither is multiplicative.

The word norm names here the algebraic norm, a central-valued quadratic map attached to a product and not a norm on a vector space. The general definitions are Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures; the algebraic norms of the parent are The 2 Algebraic Norms over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space; and the invariant form of the block is The Killing Form of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x^2=\vec e_y^2=\vec e_z^2=-e_t$; a generic element is $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$, the centre is $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_t$ and the vector subspace is $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\vec q\}$; $\tilde p\tilde q$ is the general plain bilinear product, and $\vec p\cdot\vec q$, $\vec p\times\vec q$ the Euclidean dot and vector products. The natural conjugation is $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_te_t-\vec q$; over $\mathbb{R}$ the bar is the identity and the star coincides with the natural conjugation. The plain form is $B(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t-\vec p\cdot\vec q$, the parent norm is $N(\tilde q)=\tilde q\tilde q^{\natural}=\sum_\mu q_\mu^2$, and the left multiplication operator is $L_{\tilde p}\tilde x=\tilde p\times\tilde x$. Unqualified, unit, inverse and norm mean the unit, the inverse and the algebraic norm of the block.

The Relative Definitions

The word unit and the word inverse name a product, so the definitions are stated once for a general product and read on the block; they are Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, §The Element Vocabulary.

Definition (unit). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$. An element $e$ is a left unit of $\star$ when $e\star\tilde x=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$, a right unit when $\tilde x\star e=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$, and a unit or two-sided unit when both. A product with a unit is unital.

Definition (inverse). Let $\star$ be a unital product with unit $e$. An element $\tilde p$ is invertible of $\star$ when there is $\tilde q$ with $\tilde p\star\tilde q=e=\tilde q\star\tilde p$; such a $\tilde q$ is the inverse of $\tilde p$, and the invertible elements form the group of units. The definitions are relative to the product: the element $e_t$ is the unit of the general plain product and is not a unit of the block.

Throughout this article the product is the antisymmetric plain multiplication $\times$, and the unit, the inverse and the group of units are read in $\times$.

The Absence of a Unit

Theorem (no unit). The operation $\times$ has no left unit and no right unit; there is no $\tilde e$ with $\tilde e\times\tilde x=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$, and none with $\tilde x\times\tilde e=\tilde x$ for every $\tilde x$. Hence there is no two-sided unit.

Proof. A left unit $\tilde e$ would satisfy, at $\tilde x=e_t$, whose vector part is zero, $\tilde e\times e_t=\vec e\times\mathbf{0}=0$, which would have to equal $e_t$; the two differ, since $e_t$ is nonzero. A right unit would satisfy, again at $\tilde x=e_t$, $e_t\times\tilde e=\mathbf{0}\times\vec e=0$, which would again have to equal $e_t$.

The absence is structural: the value of the block is a pure vector for every pair of arguments, and the equations that name a unit require the central element $e_t$ on the right. The scalar direction is invisible to the operation, and a unit is exactly an element the operation cannot see.

The Absence of an Inverse

Corollary (no inverse, no group of units). No element of $\mathbb{H}$ is invertible of the block, and the group of units of $\times$ is empty.

Proof. The inverse is defined by the pair of equations $\tilde p\times\tilde q=e=\tilde q\times\tilde p$ with $e$ a two-sided unit; the block has none, so the equations have no solution. There is no one-sided notion either, the one-sided inverse being defined against a one-sided unit and no one-sided unit existing.

Remark (the equation to the centre). The equation $\tilde p\times\tilde q=e_t$ has no solution in $\mathbb{H}$ directly: its left side is a pure vector, of scalar part zero, for every pair, and its right side has scalar part one. The block cannot produce a central value at all, so the inverse equation is unsolvable even before the two-sided character of the unit is required.

The Absence of an Algebraic Norm

Definition (algebraic norm of a product). Let $\star$ be a product on $\mathbb{H}$. The algebraic norm of $\star$ is the quadratic map $\tilde x\mapsto\tilde x\star\tilde x$, the product read at its two equal arguments, and the conjugated algebraic norm is the quadratic map $\tilde x\mapsto\tilde x\star\tilde x^{\natural}$, the product read on the element and its natural conjugate (The 2 Algebraic Norms over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space). An algebraic norm is central valued when all its values lie in the centre; the conjugated algebraic norm of the general plain product is the central square $\tilde x\tilde x^{\natural}=N(\tilde x)e_t$ with $N(\tilde x)=\sum_\mu x_\mu^2$, and it is that central-valued norm which the block loses (Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra).

Theorem (the block has no algebraic norm). For every element $\tilde x$,

$$ \tilde x\times\tilde x=0,\qquad \tilde x\times\tilde x^{\natural}=0 . $$

Both canonical quadratics of the block, the square and the conjugated square, vanish identically, so both are the zero quadratic form and the block has no algebraic norm: neither of the two quadratics that a product carries is a nonzero central-valued map.

Proof. The first identity is alternation, $\tilde x\times\tilde x=0$ for every $\tilde x$, by Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions. For the second, the natural conjugate is $\tilde x^{\natural}=x_0e_t-\vec x$, and the value of the block on a pair is the cross product of the vector parts, so $\tilde x\times\tilde x^{\natural}=\vec x\times(-\vec x)=-\vec x\times\vec x=0$.

The vanishing is total: the block removes the scalar part of the parent, and with it the parent's central-valued form $N(\tilde x)=x_0^2+\lvert\vec x\rvert^2$. What the block keeps is a pair of forms on the whole space, and the word norm is read here as the absent central-valued quadratic form, not as a norm on a space.

The Two Forms That Survive

Definition (the operator trace form). The operator trace form of the block is $K(\tilde a,\tilde b)=\operatorname{Tr}(L_{\tilde a}L_{\tilde b})$, the trace of the composition of the two left multiplication operators.

Definition (the scalar-part form). The scalar-part form of the block is $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t$, the product of the two scalar parts.

Proposition (the operator trace form). For all $\tilde a,\tilde b$, $K(\tilde a,\tilde b)=-2\,\vec a\cdot\vec b=-2\,(a_1b_1+a_2b_2+a_3b_3)$; on the diagonal $K(\tilde x,\tilde x)=-2\lvert\vec x\rvert^2$. The Gram matrix of $K$ in the basis is $\operatorname{diag}(0,-2,-2,-2)$, the rank of $K$ is three, its radical is the centre $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{R}e_t$, and on the vector subspace $K$ is non-degenerate.

Proof. The operator $L_{\tilde a}$ kills the scalar direction, its first column being zero, and acts on the vector part by $\vec x\mapsto\vec a\times\vec x$, so it is the block matrix $\operatorname{diag}(0,C_{\vec a})$ with $C_{\vec a}$ the cross-product matrix of $\vec a$. The composition is $\operatorname{diag}(0,C_{\vec a}C_{\vec b})$, of trace $\operatorname{Tr}(C_{\vec a}C_{\vec b})$, the scalar block contributing nothing. For skew-symmetric matrices, $\operatorname{Tr}(C_{\vec a}C_{\vec b})$ is the negative of the Frobenius pairing, which is $2\,\vec a\cdot\vec b$ here, so the trace is $-2\,\vec a\cdot\vec b$. The Gram entries are read at the units, $\mathbf e_i\cdot\mathbf e_j=\delta_{ij}$, whence the diagonal $\operatorname{diag}(0,-2,-2,-2)$; the rank is three, and the radical is the set orthogonal to every vector part, that is the centre.

Proposition (the invariance of the two forms). Both forms are symmetric and invariant under the block: for all $\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde x$,

$$ K\bigl(\tilde p\times\tilde x,\tilde q\bigr)+K\bigl(\tilde x,\tilde p\times\tilde q\bigr)=0, \qquad \varphi\bigl(\tilde p\times\tilde x,\tilde q\bigr)+\varphi\bigl(\tilde x,\tilde p\times\tilde q\bigr)=0 . $$

Proof. For $K$ the left equation reads $-2(\vec p\times\vec x)\cdot\vec q-2\,\vec x\cdot(\vec p\times\vec q)=0$, the antisymmetry of the scalar triple product, the two terms being opposite. For $\varphi$ both terms vanish outright, because $\tilde p\times\tilde x$ is a pure vector and the scalar-part form is zero whenever one argument is central. The symmetry of $K$ is that of $\vec a\cdot\vec b$, and that of $\varphi$ the symmetry of the product of the two scalar parts.

Remark (the two invariant forms). The operator trace form $K$ and the scalar-part form $\varphi$ are independent, and they span the invariant symmetric forms of the block: a computation on the centre forces every invariant form to annihilate the pairings of the centre with the vector subspace, and on the vector subspace the invariant symmetric forms are proportional to $\vec p\cdot\vec q$, the vector subspace carrying the irreducible three-dimensional representation of the block. The two extremes are $\varphi$, of rank one, whose radical and isotropic cone are the whole vector subspace, and $K$, non-degenerate on the vector subspace, whose radical is the centre. Their combination $\varphi+\tfrac12K=B$ is the plain form of the parent. The identification of $K$ with the Killing form $\kappa(\tilde p,\tilde q)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde p}\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde q})=-2\,\vec p\cdot\vec q$ of the block is The Killing Form of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra, cited here and not reproved; the adjoint operator $\operatorname{ad}_{\tilde p}\tilde r=\tilde p\times\tilde r$ is the left multiplication $L_{\tilde p}$, so the two traces coincide and the identification is exact.

Multiplicativity

Definition (multiplicativity). Let $\star$ be a product and let $q$ be a quadratic form on $\mathbb{H}$ valued in the centre. The form is multiplicative of $\star$ when $q(\tilde x\star\tilde y)=q(\tilde x)q(\tilde y)$ for all $\tilde x,\tilde y$, the equation being read in the centre.

Proposition (no multiplicativity). Of the three quadratic forms available, none is multiplicative: the operator trace form gives $K(\vec e_x\times \vec e_y,\vec e_x\times \vec e_y)=K(\vec e_z,\vec e_z)=-2$ against $K(\vec e_x,\vec e_x)K(\vec e_y,\vec e_y)=(-2)(-2)=4$; the scalar-part form gives $\varphi(e_t\times e_t,e_t\times e_t)=\varphi(0,0)=0$ against $\varphi(e_t,e_t)^2=1$; and the parent norm gives $N(\vec e_x\times \vec e_x)=N(0)=0$ against $N(\vec e_x)^2=1$.

Proof. The values of the block are read from its table, $\vec e_x\times \vec e_y=\vec e_z$, $e_t\times e_t=0$ and $\vec e_x\times \vec e_x=0$, and the right sides are computed from $K=-2\,\vec a\cdot\vec b$, $\varphi=p_0q_t$ and $N(\tilde x)=\sum_\mu x_\mu^2$. A single pair separates the two sides in each case, so no multiplicativity holds; the vanishing square satisfies the equation only because both sides vanish, and it is a norm in no sense.

The Comparison with the Parent Product

Remark (the invertibility criterion is not transferred). For the general plain algebra the invertibility criterion is $N(\tilde q)\neq0$, equivalently the central square $\tilde q\tilde q^{\natural}$ nonzero, with inverse $\tilde q^{\natural}/N(\tilde q)$ (Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra). The criterion names the product, and for the block the corresponding statement is the absence of the two previous sections: there is no unit against which to invert, and the substitute equation $\tilde p\times\tilde q=e_t$ has no solution. No element of the block is invertible, and the empty group of units is the sharp form of the statement. The parent product is the block with the scalar part restored, and the restoration returns the central square $N(\tilde x)e_t$; the block loses that square with the scalar part, so the loss is the absence of the norm and not a numerical accident.

Summary

The antisymmetric plain multiplication $\tilde p\times\tilde q=\vec p\times\vec q$ has no unit on either side, hence no invertible element and an empty group of units: every value of the block is a pure vector, and the inverse equation $\tilde p\times\tilde q=e_t$ has no solution. The block has no algebraic norm, its square vanishing by alternation and its conjugated square $\vec x\times(-\vec x)$ vanishing too, so the central-valued parent norm is lost with the scalar part. What survives are two independent invariant symmetric forms, the operator trace form $K(\tilde a,\tilde b)=-2\,\vec a\cdot\vec b$, of Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(0,-2,-2,-2)$, rank three and radical the centre, which is the Killing form of the bracket, and the rank-one scalar-part form $\varphi(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t$, whose radical and isotropic cone are the vector subspace. Neither is multiplicative, the pairs $(\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$, $(e_t,e_t)$ and $(\vec e_x,\vec e_x)$ separating the two sides, and the invertibility criterion of the parent is not transferred.

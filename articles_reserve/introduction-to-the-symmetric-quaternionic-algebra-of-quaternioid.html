Introduction to the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The quaternion space carries six bilinear products (The 6 Products of the Quaternioid Real Space), and one of them is the general quaternionic product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, the plain product with the quaternion conjugation read on the first factor. Every bilinear product splits into a symmetric part and an antisymmetric part, and this article introduces the block that reads the symmetric part of $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ as a multiplication:

$$ \tilde p\star\tilde q = \tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q + \tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p\bigr). $$

The general construction of the splitting, and the general definitions of the unit, the inverse, the zero divisor, the idempotent, the nilpotent and the root, are Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space; here they are cited and not restated. The article establishes the elementary theory of the operation: its rule, its coefficient, the sixteen products of the basis, its laws, its image, the absence of a unit, and the placement of the structure $\mathrm{SQA}$ among the six. The name $\mathrm{SQA}$ and the verb collapse are the two threads: the operation collapses the whole space onto its scalar line, and every later article of the block is a reading of the single formula $\tilde p\star\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t$.

Boundaries. The six products and the six structures are The 6 Products of the Quaternioid Real Space and The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space; the two general products are The Two General Products of the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space; the antisymmetric quaternionic companion of this block is Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions, below this article in this category; the parallel of this block among the plain products is Introduction to the Symmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions, above this article in this category. The companion articles of the block read the same operation through its zero divisors, its roots of a central value, its specificities, its radical and isotropy, its form, its multiplication operators and its two matrix models. The quaternion norm and the invertibility criterion are Norm and Invertibility in the General Plain Algebra.

Conventions. Throughout, $\mathbb{H}$ is the quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{R}$, with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$, the products $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$, $\vec e_y\vec e_z=\vec e_x$, $\vec e_z\vec e_x=\vec e_y$, and $\vec e_k^{2}=-e_t$. A quaternion is $\tilde q=q_te_t+q_x\vec e_x+q_y\vec e_y+q_z\vec e_z$, its scalar part is $\mathrm{Sc}\,\tilde q=q_t$ and its vector part is $\mathrm{Vect}\,\tilde q=\vec q=q_x\vec e_x+q_y\vec e_y+q_z\vec e_z$. The quaternion conjugation is $\tilde q^{\natural}=q_te_t-\vec q$. On $\mathbb{R}$ the corpus convention reads: the bar is the identity, $\mathbb{R}$ having no nontrivial involution; the natural sign is ${}^{\natural}$; and the star coincides with the natural sign and adds nothing. The quaternion form is

$$ N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q=\mathrm{Sc}\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q\bigr),\qquad \vec p\cdot\vec q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}p_kq_k, $$

and the plain form is $B(\tilde p,\tilde q)=\mathrm{Sc}(\tilde p\tilde q)=p_0q_t-\vec p\cdot\vec q$. The two forms are the subject of The 2 Forms over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space and are cited here.

The Operation

Definition. The symmetric quaternionic multiplication of $\mathbb{H}$ is the operation

$$ \tilde p\star\tilde q = \tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q + \tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p\bigr). $$

It is the symmetric part of the general quaternionic product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ for the exchange of the two arguments, and its antisymmetric half is the quaternionic bracket $[\tilde p,\tilde q]_{\natural}=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p$ of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions, below this article in this category. The two halves reconstruct the parent product,

$$ \tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q = \tilde p\star\tilde q + \tfrac12\,[\tilde p,\tilde q]_{\natural}, $$

and the operation is the multiplication of the structure denoted $\mathrm{SQA}$ in The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space. Both slots are read linearly: the operation is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear, the class of the parent product, and no conjugation is inserted in either slot of $\star$.

Theorem (the value is scalar and the coefficient is the quaternion form). For all $\tilde p,\tilde q\in\mathbb{H}$,

$$ \tilde p\star\tilde q = N(\tilde p,\tilde q)\,e_t . $$

Proof. Write the parent product in scalar and vector parts. Since $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=(p_0e_t-\vec p)(q_te_t+\vec q)$, its scalar part is $p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q$ and its vector part is $p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$, as in Introduction to the General Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions. The exchange $\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p$ has the same scalar part and the vector part $q_t\vec p-p_0\vec q-\vec q\times\vec p$. In the half-sum the two mixed terms cancel and the two vector products cancel by $\vec p\times\vec q=-\vec q\times\vec p$.

The coefficient $N$ is the quaternion form, the one of the two forms of the space that The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space attaches to $\mathrm{SQA}$.

Corollary (the class and the commutativity). The operation is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear and commutative, its value is always scalar, and its commutator vanishes identically,

$$ \tilde p\star\tilde q=\tilde q\star\tilde p,\qquad \tilde p\star\tilde q-\tilde q\star\tilde p=0 . $$

Proof. Bilinearity is read on the definition, both slots being linear; commutativity is read on the defining half-sum; the value is scalar by the theorem; and the commutator vanishes because the product is commutative.

Remark (the value is scalar although neither summand is). The two summands $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ and $\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p$ are exchanged by the quaternion conjugation, $(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q)^{\natural}=\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p$, and the average of two elements exchanged by an involution is fixed by it; the elements fixed by ${}^{\natural}$ are the scalar ones. This is the reason for the centrality, and the reason $\mathrm{SQA}$ behaves unlike the symmetric part of the plain product, which keeps a vector part. The operation is the pairing $N$ placed on the scalar line.

Remark (the definite character). The form $N$ is positive definite: $N(\tilde q,\tilde q)=q_t^{2}+q_x^{2}+q_y^{2}+q_z^{2}\geq0$, with equality only at $\tilde q=0$. Every value of the operation is therefore a real multiple of $e_t$, and every square $\tilde q\star\tilde q=N(\tilde q,\tilde q)e_t$ is a nonnegative multiple of $e_t$.

The Multiplication Table

Proposition (the basis table). For $\mu,\nu\in\{t,x,y,z\}$,

$$ e_\mu\star e_\nu = \delta_{\mu\nu}\,e_t . $$

Proof. For $\mu=\nu=0$: $e_t\star e_t=e_t$ and $N(e_t,e_t)=1$. For $\mu=\nu=k\ne0$: $\vec e_k\star \vec e_k=\vec e_k^{\natural}\vec e_k=(-\vec e_k)\vec e_k=-\vec e_k^{2}=e_t$, and $N(\vec e_k,\vec e_k)=1$. For $\mu\ne\nu$: if both indices are nonzero then $e_\mu^{\natural}e_\nu=-e_\mu e_\nu$ and $e_\nu^{\natural}e_\mu=-e_\nu e_\mu$, and $e_\mu e_\nu=-e_\nu e_\mu$ for the basis, so the summands cancel; if one index is $0$ and the other is $k\ne0$ then $e_t^{\natural}\vec e_k=\vec e_k$ and $\vec e_k^{\natural}e_t=-\vec e_k$, which cancel. So the value is $0=\delta_{\mu\nu}e_t$.

$e_t$ $e_t$ $0$ $0$ $0$
$\vec e_x$ $0$ $e_t$ $0$ $0$
$\vec e_y$ $0$ $0$ $e_t$ $0$
$\vec e_z$ $0$ $0$ $0$ $e_t$

Corollary (bilinearity and the determination by the table). The sixteen products are exactly the sixteen entries of the identity matrix read on the scalar line: the coefficient $N$ is the standard symmetric bilinear form of $\mathbb{R}^4$, with Gram matrix the identity in the basis, and the operation is determined by its table,

$$ \Bigl(\sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu\Bigr)\star\Bigl(\sum_\nu q_\nu e_\nu\Bigr)=\Bigl(\sum_\mu p_\mu q_\mu\Bigr)e_t . $$

Proof. The table gives $e_\mu\star e_\nu=\delta_{\mu\nu}e_t$, and $\mathbb{R}$-bilinearity extends the display to the general pair; conversely the display evaluates the definition of $N$.

Remark (the table is not the one of the plain product). The plain product gives a fixed-point-free permutation table with signs (Introduction to the General Plain Algebra of Quaternions), and the general quaternionic product one with the first column negated. The entry $e_\mu\star e_\nu$ is the symmetrisation of the second, and it is diagonal because the antisymmetric part is what carries the off-diagonal signs: the symmetric half keeps only the diagonal of the quaternionic table. The table is the smallest possible for a nonzero operation.

The Laws of the Operation

The reference for the laws of a product is Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space; the reading of the six one by one is The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

Commutativity, and the Collapse of the Derived Operation

The operation is commutative, so its commutator vanishes identically and the derived operation is the zero operation. The derived algebra is therefore the scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_t$ alone, on which the operation is the multiplication of $\mathbb{R}$: for real $a,b$,

$$ (ae_t)\star(be_t)=ab\,e_t . $$

The operation carries no Lie structure; the antisymmetric half of the parent carries it instead, and that half is the block $\mathrm{AQA}$.

Associativity

Proposition (the operation is not associative). At $\tilde p=\tilde q=\vec e_x$ and $\tilde r=e_t$ the two bracketings differ,

$$ (\vec e_x\star \vec e_x)\star e_t=e_t\neq0=\vec e_x\star(\vec e_x\star e_t), $$

and the general associator is

$$ (\tilde p\star\tilde q)\star\tilde r-\tilde p\star(\tilde q\star\tilde r) =\bigl(N(\tilde p,\tilde q)r_0-p_0N(\tilde q,\tilde r)\bigr)e_t . $$

Proof. $(\tilde p\star\tilde q)\star\tilde r=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t\star\tilde r=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)r_0e_t$, and $\tilde p\star(\tilde q\star\tilde r)=N(\tilde q,\tilde r)p_0e_t$. At the witness the first is $N(\vec e_x,\vec e_x)e_t=e_t$ and the second is $N(\vec e_x,e_t)e_t=0$.

Power-Associativity

Proposition (the operation is not power-associative). At $\tilde x=e_t+\vec e_x$, whose square is $\tilde x\star\tilde x=2e_t$, the two bracketings of the fourth power differ,

$$ (\tilde x\star\tilde x)\star(\tilde x\star\tilde x)=4e_t\neq2e_t=\bigl((\tilde x\star\tilde x)\star\tilde x\bigr)\star\tilde x . $$

Proof. $\tilde x\star\tilde x=N(\tilde x,\tilde x)e_t=2e_t$. On the left the product is $2e_t\star2e_t=4e_t$. On the right, $(\tilde x\star\tilde x)\star\tilde x=2e_t\star\tilde x=2x_0e_t=2e_t$, and $2e_t\star\tilde x=2e_t$ again; so the right side is $2e_t$. The three-element powers agree, by commutativity, and the failure appears at the fourth power.

The Two Classical Identities

Proposition (the Jordan identity fails). With the parenthesisation of Definitions for the Study of the Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space, the identity

$$ \tilde x\star\bigl(\tilde y\star(\tilde x\star\tilde x)\bigr)=\bigl(\tilde x\star\tilde y\bigr)\star\bigl(\tilde x\star\tilde x\bigr) $$

fails, and at $\tilde x=\tilde y=\vec e_x$ the two sides are $0$ and $e_t$.

Proof. At $\tilde x=\vec e_x$ the square is $\tilde x\star\tilde x=e_t$. The left side is $\tilde x\star(\tilde x\star e_t)=\tilde x\star N(\tilde x,e_t)e_t=\tilde x\star0=0$; the right side is $(\tilde x\star\tilde x)\star(\tilde x\star\tilde x)=e_t\star e_t=N(e_t,e_t)e_t=e_t$.

Proposition (the Jacobi identity fails). The identity

$$ \tilde x\star(\tilde y\star\tilde z)+\tilde y\star(\tilde z\star\tilde x)+\tilde z\star(\tilde x\star\tilde y)=0 $$

fails, and at $\tilde x=\tilde y=\tilde z=e_t$ its left side is $3e_t$.

Proof. $\tilde y\star\tilde z=N(\tilde y,\tilde z)e_t$, so the first term is $x_0N(\tilde y,\tilde z)e_t$ and the sum is $\bigl(x_0N(\tilde y,\tilde z)+y_0N(\tilde z,\tilde x)+z_0N(\tilde x,\tilde y)\bigr)e_t$, which at the three units is $3e_t$.

Proposition (the alternating law fails). The operation is not alternating: $e_t\star e_t=e_t\neq0$, so there is an element whose square does not vanish.

The operation is therefore neither associative, nor power-associative, nor a Jordan product, nor a Lie bracket. It is the only operation of the six whose values lie in the scalar line, and the collapse of the commutator does not imply the collapse of the associator.

The Image and the Unit

Proposition (the image is the scalar line). The image of $\star$ is the scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_t$, and the map is onto it; on that line the operation is the multiplication of $\mathbb{R}$.

Proof. Every value is scalar by the theorem of §The Operation, so the image is contained in $\mathbb{R}e_t$; conversely $N(\tilde q,t\tilde q)=t\,N(\tilde q,\tilde q)$ is an arbitrary real multiple of $N(\tilde q,\tilde q)$ as $t$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, and $N(e_t,e_t)=1$ gives the whole line.

Proposition (there is no unit). The operation has no unit, and no one-sided unit either. For every element $\tilde a$,

$$ \tilde a\star e_t=e_t\star\tilde a=a_0\,e_t , $$

so $e_t$ is a unit for the scalar line alone. An element $\tilde u$ with $\tilde u\star\tilde q=\tilde q$ for every $\tilde q$ would give $\tilde u\star e_t=u_te_t=e_t$, hence $u_t=1$, and then $\tilde u\star \vec e_x=u_xe_t=\vec e_x$, which is impossible because $u_xe_t$ is scalar and $\vec e_x$ is not.

Proof. The display is the theorem read at $\tilde q=e_t$; the unit argument is the one displayed. The same computation with the roles exchanged excludes a right unit, and an element invertible from both sides would be a unit.

Corollary (no inverse). No nonzero element of the block has an inverse: an inverse would be a unit of the multiplication, and the multiplication has none.

The Block among the Six

The six products of The 6 Products of the Quaternioid Real Space are generated from the two general products by symmetrisation and antisymmetrisation, and the block is one row of the catalogue The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

code product $\tilde p\circ\tilde q$ character
$\mathrm{GPA}$ general plain $\tilde p\tilde q$ associative, unit $e_t$, division
$\mathrm{SPA}$ symmetric plain $\tfrac12(\tilde p\tilde q+\tilde q\tilde p)$ commutative, Jordan, unit $e_t$
$\mathrm{APA}$ antisymmetric plain $\tfrac12(\tilde p\tilde q-\tilde q\tilde p)$ alternating, no unit
$\mathrm{GQA}$ general quaternionic $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ non-associative, left unit only
$\mathrm{SQA}$ symmetric quaternionic $\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q+\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)$ commutative, scalar valued, no unit
$\mathrm{AQA}$ antisymmetric quaternionic $\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)$ alternating, no unit

Remark (the position of the block). The block is the symmetric part of the general quaternionic product, and its antisymmetric companion is $\mathrm{AQA}$; the two reconstruct $\mathrm{GQA}$ by the formula of §The Operation. It is one of the two commutative structures, the other being $\mathrm{SPA}$, and it is the only one whose values are scalar; the isolation is stated and proved in The Specificities of the Symmetric Quaternionic Algebra.

Over $\mathbb{R}$ the coefficient is definite, the block is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear, and the reading of its zero divisors is the opposite extreme; the comparison is carried by the companion articles and not restated here.

Summary

The symmetric quaternionic product is the operation $\tilde p\star\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q+\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t$, the pairing of the quaternion form placed on the scalar line. It is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear, commutative and scalar valued, its table is the identity table $e_\mu\star e_\nu=\delta_{\mu\nu}e_t$, and its image is the whole scalar line $\mathbb{R}e_t$. It is not associative, not power-associative, not a Jordan product, not a Lie bracket and not alternating: the witnesses are $(\vec e_x,\vec e_x,e_t)$, the element $e_t+\vec e_x$, the pair $(\vec e_x,\vec e_x)$ and the triple $(e_t,e_t,e_t)$. It has no unit, no one-sided unit and no inverse, and the element $e_t$ is a unit for the scalar line alone.

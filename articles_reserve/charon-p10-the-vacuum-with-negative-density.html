Charon: The Vacuum with Negative Density

Introduction

This article records the corpus's identification of empty space with antimatter. RC 069 states the premise — a closed, pulsating universe means the cosmological density is not zero — and RC 084 gives the number: $\rho = -2{,}9\times10^{-32}$ g cm$^{-3}$. The claim is doubly wrong, and in a way that is more interesting than a simple miss: the sign and the magnitude are both reversed, and the corpus needs the wrong sign.

The claim, verbatim. RC 084: "Cette densité $\rho = \alpha/R^{3}$ de l'espace « vide » est actuellement, comme nous le montre le modèle cosmologique, de $-2{,}9\times10^{-32}$ g/cm$^{3}$." RC 069 sets up the reason: "Le Réel dans son ensemble est, comme nous le verrons dans le chapitre V (modèle cosmologique), un espace sphérique fermé ($K = +1$). Le rayon spatial $R(\tau)$ de ce modèle cosmologique possède lui-même une pulsation radiale périodique. Ceci veut dire à son tour que, ce que nous nommons généralement « espace vide », n'est pas rigoureusement vide, la densité cosmologique $\rho_c$ n'est pas nulle."

The verdict is [FALS]. The occupant of that role in modern cosmology is dark energy, discovered in 1998, with density positive and $\approx 6\times10^{-30}$ g cm$^{-3}$ equivalent — about 200 times larger in magnitude and opposite in sign. Worse, the corpus does not merely get the sign wrong; it requires $\Lambda \le 0$, because a closed universe with a finite maximum radius has $\Lambda = -3/R_{\max}^{2} < 0$ necessarily. S52 computes the required value as $-1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$ against the observed $+1{,}10\times10^{-56}$ cm$^{-2}$ — opposite sign, 7168 times smaller. So the corpus's needed sign is wrong, its stated magnitude is wrong, and the magnitude the geometry actually demands is wrong too.

A note on sources. The claim is inventory §8.5, sourced to RC ch. V §2 (5-68), RC 069 and Esp §VII.14. The geometric requirement and the computed comparison are S52's, and the relation to the bounce is S51's. The related articles are P9, P11 and P15; the related chain is R9's neighbourhood (the redshift cut-off depends on the same closed-universe geometry).

Conventions. Gaussian cgs. $\rho$ is the corpus's "void" density, $\Lambda$ the cosmological constant, $R(\tau)$ the cosmic scale factor, $R_{\max}$ its maximum, $K$ the curvature sign, $\Omega_{\Lambda}$ and $\rho_{\Lambda}$ the modern dark-energy parameters. The conversion used is $\rho_{\Lambda} = \Lambda c^{2}/8\pi G$.

Part 1 — Why the corpus needs a non-zero vacuum, and why it needs the sign it chose

The claim is not an idle remark. It is load-bearing in three places, and in each of them the corpus needs the density to be negative.

The premise: a closed pulsating universe cannot be empty. RC 069's argument is that a finite closed space with a moving radius has curvature, so its "vacuum" carries an energy density. That step is correct and it is standard: in general relativity a closed universe has a non-zero vacuum contribution fixed by its geometry, and the sign follows from the closure.

The sign is forced geometrically. This is S52's finding, and it is stronger than the corpus's own derivation. For a closed ($K = +1$) universe with a finite maximum radius $R_{\max}$, the Friedmann equation at the turning point requires

$$\Lambda = -\frac{3}{R_{\max}^{2}} < 0 .$$

The requirement does not depend on the micro-universe, on (3-69-2), or on any of the corpus's own machinery — it is a statement about a closed universe that turns around. That matters, because it means a defender cannot drop the sign without dropping the bounce and the cycle as well.

Two values, and they differ. The corpus states $\rho = -2{,}9\times10^{-32}$ g cm$^{-3}$, which converts to $\Lambda = 8\pi G\rho/c^{2} = -5{,}4\times10^{-59}$ cm$^{-2}$. The geometry above demands $-1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$. The corpus's own two routes to $\Lambda$ disagree with each other by a factor of 35 — a smaller internal tension than the external one, but worth recording, and the same shape as the $l_{0}$/$\xi$ misfit S30 finds in the Système L.

Part 2 — What is actually there

The measurement. In 1998 two supernova teams found that the expansion is accelerating; combined with the CMB and baryon acoustic oscillations, the acceleration requires a component with negative pressure and positive energy density. Planck 2018 fixes it:

$$\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0{,}6889 \pm 0{,}0056, \qquad \rho_{\Lambda} = \Omega_{\Lambda}\rho_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 5{,}9\times10^{-30}\ \mathrm{g\ cm^{-3}},$$

and a corresponding $\Lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = +1{,}10\times10^{-56}$ cm$^{-2}$.

So the corpus's "negative vacuum density" is wrong three times: the sign is wrong, its own number is 200 times too small, and the number its geometry actually demands is 7000 times too small. This is not a near-miss that better data might repair; it is a disagreement about the sign of the dominant term in the cosmic energy budget.

The magnitude problem is the same problem as the cosmological-constant problem. An honest reading must say that both the corpus's $-2{,}9\times10^{-32}$ and the modern $+5{,}9\times10^{-30}$ are enormously larger than a naive quantum-field-theory estimate of the vacuum energy density — by some 120 orders of magnitude in the standard estimate. So the corpus's number is not absurd as a vacuum number; it is the wrong sign, and it is not what the universe is doing.

Part 3 — "The vacuum is a form of antimatter"

The corpus draws a further identification: since the vacuum density is negative and its own particles carry negative proper mass under some conditions, the vacuum is antimatter (inventory §8.5, and the companion's caution that the corpus's version is its own).

Why this cannot be maintained. In the modern account dark energy is a smooth stress-energy component with $w \approx -1$; it carries no antiparticles, and the "antimatter" of the standard model is a set of anti-particles with positive energy and opposite charges. The corpus's "negative proper mass" is a bookkeeping device internal to its ±-energy scheme (S13), not an identification with $\bar{p}$ or $\bar{n}$. Calling the vacuum "a form of antimatter" therefore inherits the failure of P9's antimatter era: it needs the same sign convention, and that sign is the one observation contradicts.

And it interacts with the bounce. S51's finding is that the modification producing the bounce is $\Lambda \le 0$ and dies with its sign. So the vacuum density claim, the bounce and the cycle are one cluster: all three rest on the same negative-$\Lambda$ requirement, and observation supplies a positive one. That is the cleanest way to say what breaks.

Summary

The corpus gives "empty space" a negative density, $\rho = -2{,}9\times10^{-32}$ g cm$^{-3}$, and identifies the vacuum with antimatter. Dark energy, discovered in 1998, has density $+5{,}9\times10^{-30}$ g cm$^{-3}$ — about 200 times larger and of the opposite sign. The corpus does not merely get the sign wrong; its closed, bouncing universe requires $\Lambda = -3/R_{\max}^{2} < 0$, and S52 computes the demanded value as $-1{,}53\times10^{-60}$ cm$^{-2}$ against the observed $+1{,}10\times10^{-56}$ cm$^{-2}$ — opposite sign, 7168 times smaller. The corpus's two internal routes to $\Lambda$ also disagree by a factor 35. Verdict [FALS], and the failure takes the bounce (S51) and the cycle (S52) with it, since all three rest on the same negative-$\Lambda$ requirement.

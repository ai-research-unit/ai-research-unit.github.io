Charon — The Bootstrap Examined (S33)

Audits Esp 056's parameter-freedom claim — the "bootstrap leptonique" — against Esp 057 §1–§6's method and the counts of S30 and S31.

Introduction

The claim fails as written and survives as a weaker, true statement, and the difference between the two is one sentence long: the system has no free parameter once six measured values are fixed and one suggested value is supplied. Esp 056 says the Système $L$ "ne dépend en fait d'aucun paramètre, puisque tous ceux qui figurent dans les équations sont finalement calculés par la solution du système d'équations", and calls it a lepton bootstrap in Chew's sense. The very next page of the corpus puts six numerical values in by hand and confines the search to $\pm10\,\%$ of them, and the page after that names the seventh ($\xi$) as a "suggestion" deduced from the nucleon mass. So the corpus's own text refutes its own claim, in the same chapter, and the refutation is not a matter of interpretation.

What is true, and worth stating first because it is not small: the model is closed. Given the six seeds, everything else — $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T$, $A$, $R_{0}$, $\mathcal{R}$, $l_{0}$, $k_{s}$, $k_{p}$, $W_{0e}$, $W_{0\mu}$, the tower — comes out of the corpus's own equations (S30 §4), and the twenty-equation system is self-consistent to the $2\,\%$ residual of a well whose depth is $50$ (S31). That is a closure property, it is unusual in a theory of this vintage, and S34's ledger counts it as the chapter's one asset. What is not true is the word bootstrap.

Three things a bootstrap must do, and the corpus's system does none of them as stated (§5): it must need no external constants — the corpus's needs six measured ones and one suggestion; it must make the existence of its bound states follow from the same equations — the corpus's takes the lepton's existence and asks for its radius; and it must have a unique solution rather than a well inside a box — the corpus's claims uniqueness only "dans la région analysée". So the honest formulation is: not a bootstrap, but a closed consistency system with six measured anchors and one suggested constant. That formulation is weaker, it is supported by everything in S30–S32, and it is what the register should carry.

And one further point that belongs in an audit of the word rather than of the model. Chew's bootstrap programme — the S-matrix, nuclear democracy, hadrons as each other's binding forces — was abandoned by the physics community in the early 1970s, when asymptotic freedom, the parton picture and colour made quarks the constituents. The corpus invokes it in 1983, a decade after the programme had been left behind, and invokes it for leptons, where Chew's own logic was about hadrons. So the claim's rhetorical support is itself dated, and the audit has to say so (§6) — not to score a point, but because a reader in 2026 who follows the reference will find a dead programme on the other end of it.

What the Corpus Claims

Esp 056, the claim, in full:

Ce qu'il y a de tout à fait remarquable dans le Système $L$ est qu'il ne dépend en fait d'aucun paramètre, puisque tous ceux qui figurent dans les équations sont finalement calculés par la solution du système d'équations. Le Système $L$ décrit donc un micro-univers ne reposant, pour sa représentation dans un modèle, que sur des principes de cohérence globale. En ce sens, nous pouvons dire que le Système $L$ représente un bootstrap leptonique, avec le sens qu'a donné Geoffrey Chew au mot « bootstrap » […] La solution du Système $L$ doit permettre le calcul direct de 5 des principales constantes physiques fondamentales, à savoir $c, h, m, \mu$ et $\tau_2$; par ailleurs, comme nous allons le voir au paragraphe 18.2, le Système $L$ conduit également au calcul de la charge électrique élémentaire $e$.

Esp 057 §1 and §4, which decide the question, and the corpus's own words about them:

1) Donnons-nous un ensemble de six valeurs numériques […] correspondant respectivement aux valeurs des six paramètres : $h, c, m, \mu, \tau_2$ et $e$. […] 4) […] choisissons un ensemble $P_0$ numériquement identique aux valeurs numériques expérimentales que l'on obtient pour ces six paramètres.

1. The Claim, Split Into Three Parts

"The Système $L$ depends on no parameter" is three statements in one sentence, and separating them is what makes the audit possible.

Read with (a) dropped as a wording slip, the claim becomes (b), and (b) is a real statement about the system. Read with (c) attached, it is the constants claim, and S32 settles it row by row. So this article's work is: to show that (b) is true, that (c) is not, and that the word bootstrap is about (c)'s kind of claim and not (b)'s.

2. Part (a): "Aucun Paramètre"

The equations carry eight symbols that are not numbers, and the corpus's sentence is wrong at the level of what is on the page. The roster's own header defines $\xi$; L1 carries $\alpha$, $\beta$, $R_{0}$, $K$ and $\Lambda$; L7 and L8 carry $h$, $c$, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau_{n}$; L16 and L17 carry $c$ and $l_{0}$; the actions carry $W_{0m}$ and $W_{0\tau_{n}}$.

What the corpus means, and it is worth giving it fairly, is that none of those is a free parameter: each is either a definition (S30 §5), an output of the solution, or one of the six seeds. That is a statement about the system's closure, not about the appearance of symbols, and it is (b) restated. So (a) is a wording slip with a substantive claim behind it, and the audit should treat it as (b).

3. Part (b): "Calculés par la Solution" — True, Relative to the Seeds

The forward solution is real and it is the corpus's own (Esp 057 §2), and S30 §5.1 turns three of its steps into closed forms:

$$T = \frac{2h}{c^{2}(\tau-\mu)}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{m+3\mu-2\tau}{4\pi^{2}}, \qquad \beta = -\frac{h}{4\pi^{3}c}\left(1-\varepsilon\right)$$

Every symbol on the right-hand side is a measured quantity ($h$, $c$, the three lepton masses) or a definition ($\varepsilon$, $T$). So the internal constants are consequences of the seeds — exactly, algebraically, with no fitting. That is the true content of "calculés par la solution", and it is a genuine property of the system: change the seeds and the internal constants change with them, computed rather than adjusted.

And the same is true of the rest of the output list: $R_{0}$, $\mathcal{R}$, $A$ and the waveform come from L1–L6; $k_{s}$, $k_{p}$ from L13–L15; $W_{0e}$, $W_{0\mu}$ from L9–L12; $l_{0}$ and $\xi$ from L17; $\tau_{3}$ from (244). S30 §4's map is the full diagram, and its diagnostic property is that every constant appears on both sides of it — which is precisely what makes it a closure diagram. And it is also what makes it unable to test the seeds: a system that computes everything from the seeds cannot also compute the seeds.

4. Part (c): the Six Constants, and the Count

The count is S34's, and this article states it only far enough to close the claim. Esp 057 §1 puts in $h, c, m, \mu, \tau_{2}, e$; Esp 057 names $\xi$ as a suggestion from $M_{0}$; and S32 shows row by row that $h, m, \mu, \tau_{2}$ are the measured input values, $e$ is seeded and computed downstream (§18.2) to the claimed $2{,}3\,\%$ (S35: $+1{,}82\,\%$ on the charge recomputed), and $c$ is defined by the system's own normalisation equation L16. So:

So the claim of six computed constants has six terms, of which five are inputs, one is a definition, and none is a prediction of a new measured quantity.

5. Chew's Bootstrap, and What the Corpus's Would Have to Be

Chew's programme, compressed to what the corpus is invoking: the strong interactions are to be understood from a self-consistency condition in which each hadron is a composite of the others, with no elementary constituents; a particle is "elementary" only if it is not generated by the same dynamics that generates everything else; the criterion is nuclear democracy, and the tool was the $S$-matrix with its bootstrap equations. Three structural requirements follow, and they can be checked against the Système $L$ without any interpretation.

requirement Chew's bootstrap the Système $L$
no external constants required absolutely — the hadrons are the forces six measured values plus one suggestion; ✗
the existence of the objects follows from the dynamics required — that is what "generated by the dynamics" means the lepton's existence is assumed; the system computes its radius and mass relations; ✗
a unique solution required — the bootstrap equations select the spectrum uniqueness claimed only "dans la région analysée", a $3^{6}$ grid (S31 §3.4); ✗

None of the three is met, and the third is the one the corpus's own words concede. So the word bootstrap is not supported by the system; what is supported is "a closed, over-determined consistency system anchored on measured values". The distinction matters for the ledger: a bootstrap's outputs are predictions, a consistency system's outputs are re-expressions, and S34's tags differ accordingly.

6. The Fate of the Word: a Dated Programme

A reader who follows "avec le sens qu'a donné Geoffrey Chew au mot bootstrap" will find a programme that no longer exists, and for reasons that were understood before 1983.

  • Chew's bootstrap and the $S$-matrix programme were the mainstream route to the strong interaction from the late 1950s to the early 1970s, and their abandonment was not a philosophical choice: deep-inelastic scattering (1968–) revealed point-like constituents, and asymptotic freedom (1973) gave QCD a calculable high-energy limit, with colour and confinement accounting for what the bootstrap was meant to explain. Nuclear democracy was the casualty; quarks were the constituents whose absence the bootstrap programme had required.
  • Chew's logic was also about hadrons, whose strong binding could in principle generate them from each other. The lepton sector has no analogue of that binding: the corpus's own L1 makes the lepton a pulsating closed universe whose dynamics are fixed by its own constants, not by the exchange of other leptons. So even in its own terms, the analogy is one analogy too far.

Recorded because it is part of the claim's status, and not as a rhetorical point: the corpus's 1983 invocation is a reference to a programme that had already been abandoned, applied to a sector where its central mechanism has no counterpart. The system stands or falls on its own equations — and S30, S31 and S32 audit exactly that.

7. What Survives: the Closure Property, and the One Real Test

Two things survive, and the second is where the claim could have earned itself.

(1) The closure property, exactly as S34 states it: one defined constant, five measured seeds, one suggested constant, and a $2\,\%$ self-consistency residual whose well is $50$ deep. That is the chapter's asset — a single model that reproduces three lepton masses' relations, the charged-lepton geometry's own $l_{0}$ to $2{,}6\,\%$ (S30 §3.3), the printed relativistic coefficients to $0{,}2\,\%$ (S32 §6.2), and $e$ to the $2{,}3\,\%$ the corpus claims (S35: $+1{,}82\,\%$ on the charge). No bootstrap is needed for that to be worth having.

(2) The one genuine test of a bootstrap-shaped system is a new state: a state its own dynamics requires and the seeds do not contain. The Système $L$ has exactly one such state — the tower's $n = 3$ — and the corpus prints it ($6{,}184\times10^{-24}$ g $= 3{,}47$ GeV), and experiment excludes it (R7, P1, S43). So the system's single predictive opportunity was taken and failed, and that is a cleaner statement of the chapter's standing than either "bootstrap" or "not a bootstrap". Recorded as the bridge from this article to P1.

8. Verdict

The claim is false as written, its substantive core is true and worth stating precisely, and the word bootstrap is wrong in three separate ways.

  • (a) "Aucun paramètre": false as written. Eight symbols appear in the equations; the claim is about their being free, not about their appearance (§2). Wording slip, substantive claim preserved as (b).
  • (b) "Calculés par la solution": true, relative to the seeds. $T$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ are exact closed forms in the measured seeds (S30 §5.1); the rest of the output list follows from L1–L17 (S30 §4). A real closure property.
  • (c) The six constants: false. Five are seeds, $c$ is the system's own definition, $e$ is seeded and computed downstream, and $\xi$ is the corpus's own "suggestion" (§4, S32, S34).
  • The word bootstrap fails on three counts (§5): six external measured anchors plus one suggestion; the objects' existence assumed rather than generated; uniqueness only inside a $3^{6}$ grid in a $\pm10\,\%$ box.
  • The word is also dated (§6): Chew's programme was abandoned a decade before 1983, for reasons (partons, asymptotic freedom, colour) that are established physics, and it was about hadrons, where the corpus's own mechanism has no lepton analogue.
  • What survives is the closure property (S34: 1 defined, 5 measured seeds, 1 suggested, 0 independent outputs) and the one predictive opportunity the system took — the third tower state — which experiment excludes (R7, P1, S43).
  • Tag: the closure [MATH]; the parameter-freedom claim [SPEC], refuted by the corpus's own Esp 057 §1; the Chew identification [SPEC], dated and inapplicable.
  • Rule R1's count, in one line: the Système $L$ is a closed consistency system with six measured anchors and one suggested constant; it computes its internal constants exactly, and it predicts no new measured quantity other than the third tower state, which is excluded.

Part S / Part P. Part S: the closure property, the closed forms, the counts, the historical note — all checkable. Part P: the parameter-freedom claim and the Chew identification — the first refuted by the corpus's own next two pages, the second inapplicable to a sector whose mechanism has no counterpart in the programme invoked.

Summary

The claim "il ne dépend en fait d'aucun paramètre" is false as written, true in a weaker form, and the word bootstrap is wrong in three separate ways. Separated into its three parts: (a) no parameter appears — false, eight symbols appear; (b) everything is computed by the solution — true relative to the seeds, since $T$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ are exact closed forms in the measured lepton masses; (c) the six constants are computed — false (S32). Chew's bootstrap needs no external constants, needs its objects' existence to follow from the dynamics, and needs a unique solution; the Système $L$ has six measured anchors plus one suggestion, assumes the lepton, and claims uniqueness only inside a $3^{6}$ grid in a $\pm10\,\%$ box. The invocation is also dated: the $S$-matrix bootstrap programme was abandoned a decade before 1983, for reasons (partons, asymptotic freedom, colour) that are established physics. What survives is the closure property — a closed consistency system with six measured anchors and one suggested constant, self-consistent to the $2\,\%$ residual of a well $50$ deep — and the single predictive opportunity the system took, the third tower state at $3{,}47$ GeV, which experiment excludes (R7, P1, S43).

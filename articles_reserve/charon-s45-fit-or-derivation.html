Charon — The Lepton Sector: Fit or Derivation? (S45)

Audits inventory §4.8 (the lepton model is "entirely devoid of arbitrary parameters") and the lepton half of rules R1/R2. Sources: RC 051 §IV, (3-68)–(3-74); RC 053 (3-75)–(3-77); Esp 050 §8, Esp 051 §9–§10, Esp 052 §11, Esp 058 (the numerical solution of the Système L).

Introduction

The register calls this the methodological centre of the lepton sector, and the walk gives a result that is the opposite of the one the corpus advertises: the sector has no arbitrary parameter at all, and that is precisely why it failed. Charon's claim (Esp §VI, inventory §4.8) is that the lepton model is "entirely devoid of arbitrary parameters". The honest count, row by row, is: three measured lepton masses as the sector's anchors, two universal constants ($c$, $h$), one suggested seed ($\xi$), two numbers shared with other sectors ($e$ and the nucleon closure), four quantities that are restatements of the anchors ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$, and $k_{S} = k_{P}$), one conditional geometric output set ($R_{0}$, $\mathcal{R}$, $\Lambda$, $\langle R \rangle$, $Y$), two numbers the corpus defines but never derives ($W_{0e}$, $W_{0\mu}$), one dead prediction (the tower), and one convention (the spin sign). And the number of parameters specific to a lepton is zero.

That last count is the article's finding, and it has a direction. The usual way a model survives its own wrong prediction is by absorbing it in a free parameter; the lepton model has none — one éon, repeated, with the mass supplied from outside (S42). So the sector could not be tuned, its tower prediction stayed hard, and LEP killed it. §4.8's sentence is false as stated and the reason is not that the model is loose: it is that the model is unable to be loose. The correction to §4.8 is therefore not "one output and five inputs" — S21's correction — but the sharper statement that the lepton sector's only adjustable objects are the masses it is given.

The second finding is about direction, and it is what closes the ledger. Esp computes masses from the model's constants — forward. RC (3-68) computes the constants from the masses — backward. S43 shows the two directions are one constraint: the $\alpha$ implied by the mass equations (220)/(221) equals the $\alpha$ of (3-68) to $0{,}002\,\%$, through the identity $\alpha = (m + 3\mu - 2\tau)/4\pi^{2}$. So the forward reading in Esp and the backward reading in RC are the same equation read twice, and a system inverted is not a derivation of its own outputs — which is rule R1's whole content, applied here for the first time to the lepton sector.

The third finding is what the sector does put out. Given $\xi$, the Système E returns a trajectory and an internal geometry — $R_{0} = 8{,}01\times10^{-15}$ cm, $\mathcal{R} = 4{,}82\times10^{-14}$ cm, $\Lambda \le 0$, the neutrino orbit $Y = 2{,}27$, and now $\langle R \rangle = 2{,}35\times10^{-14}$ cm (S43) — and every one of these is a genuine output of the model, conditional on the one seed. None of them is observable (S42). So the sector's ledger is: it computes a geometry nobody can see, from a seed it was given, and it computes no mass it was not given.

What the Corpus Claims

The claim, Esp §VI (inventory §4.8):

… le modèle leptonique est entièrement dépourvu de paramètres arbitraires.

The corpus's own count, RC 051 §IV:

Une première analyse du Système $E$ montre que, pour calculer l'ensemble des solutions particulières, nous avons besoin des valeurs de cinq des paramètres mentionnés ci-dessus, avec bien entendu leurs valeurs propres des trois leptons chargés connus ($m$, $\mu$ et $\tau$). Les équations (3-53) du Système $E$ fournissent alors facilement, en fonction de ces paramètres bien mesurés par l'expérience, les valeurs des trois principaux paramètres inconnus, à savoir $\alpha$, $\beta$ et $T_{0}$.

The inversion itself, (3-68):

$$\alpha = \frac{m+\mu}{4\pi^{2}} - \frac{h}{\pi^{2}c^{2}T_{0}}, \qquad \beta = \frac{(\mu-m)c^{2}T_{0}-2h}{8\pi^{3}c}, \qquad T_{0} = \frac{2h}{c^{2}(\tau-\mu)}$$

And the forward direction, Esp 050 §8, where the same constants are used to produce $m_{0}$ and $\mu_{0}$ (eqs 220–221).

2. Zero lepton parameters, and why that is a weakness rather than a strength

The corpus presents "no arbitrary parameters" as the sector's virtue. The ledger says it is the sector's rigidity. A model with no per-lepton parameter cannot accommodate a wrong spectrum by adjusting anything: the three masses are the input, the éon is one object repeated (S42), and every other lepton number is either universal, shared, or a closed form in the three masses. So when the model extrapolates the sequence beyond the tau, the extrapolation is forced, and it is wrong (R7, P1: the next four members at $3{,}46$–$8{,}50$ GeV do not exist).

This makes the lepton sector the cleanest instance of the programme's two opposite failure modes, and it is the one that is not the fitted-minimum pattern. S20 and S38 record claims that survive because a plateau makes them unfalsifiable. The lepton tower is the reverse: a claim with no slack, tested by data, and dead. The register's §4.8 tag [FALS] as stated therefore stands — but with the corpus's own count as the correction, and not with a suspicion of over-parameterisation, which the ledger does not support.

And the corpus's own count is nearly the honest one. RC 051 §IV says five parameters are needed, "bien mesurés par l'expérience", and that the Système E then provides $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ "en fonction de ces paramètres". That is the input/output accounting stated by the corpus itself, and it is correct: five in (its count is $c$, $h$, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$), three derived. The only thing missing from RC's sentence is that the three derived constants are closed forms in the five rather than independent numbers — which is S21's finding, and which the forward direction in Esp hides.

3. The direction of read, and why "computed" is not "derived"

The two books read one system in opposite directions. Esp 050 §8 and Esp 051 write the mass equations (220)/(221) and produce $m_{0}$ and $\mu_{0}$ from $\alpha$ and $\beta$. RC 051 writes (3-68) and produces $\alpha$ and $\beta$ from $m$ and $\mu$. S43's check shows these are the same constraint: adding the two mass equations returns (3-68)'s $\alpha$ exactly, and subtracting them returns $\beta I$. So:

  • the forward reading's appearance of computing the masses is the appearance of solving a system whose constants were obtained by inverting it;
  • the backward reading is honest and the corpus says so ("en fonction de ces paramètres bien mesurés par l'expérience").

Rule R1: a computation is not a derivation, and the first thing to report about a number is whether it was put in. For the lepton sector the answer is that the masses were put in — in the 1983 book explicitly and in the 1987 book implicitly, and the two statements are one line of algebra apart.

A second check-point follows from the same structure, and it sharpens R3. The mass equations contain the integral $\int_{0}^{T_{0}}dt/R$, whose primitive the corpus never writes; S43 shows that the pair's own two combinations eliminate every integral except through $\int dt/R$, which the turning points bound. So the lepton sector is checkable to the point where only one unexhibited primitive remains, and the obstruction §4.6 records is no longer a missing integrand but a missing integral.

4. What the sector genuinely outputs, and what it costs

The genuine outputs are geometric, and they are the reason the sector is worth keeping. Given $\xi$ and the three masses, the Système E fixes a trajectory with definite turning points $R_{0}$ and $\mathcal{R}$, a negative $\Lambda$, a quantised neutrino orbit $Y = 2{,}27$ shared by every éon, and a mean inverse radius $\langle R \rangle = 2{,}35\times10^{-14}$ cm (S43). These are outputs in the strict sense — no freedom left once the inputs are fixed. They are also, by S42's identification, internal to an unobservable object.

And they are conditional on $\xi$, whose status is the same problem S38 found for the nucleon mass. $\xi$ is taken from $l_{0} = R_{H}$ — the nucleon closure — as a starting suggestion, and S38 shows the nucleon mass moves $5{,}1\,\%$ when the two printed $\xi$'s are compared. So the lepton sector's output set is a function of a seed that is not the sector's own, and the debt is S22's and S38's. The article records the dependency rather than the numbers' apparent precision.

Two rows are defined and never derived, and they are the sector's unexplained cells. $W_{0e}$ and $W_{0\mu}$ (Esp 058's $0{,}824\times10^{-3}$ and $2{,}615\times10^{-3}$ erg) are the neutrino "rest energies"; relations (226), (232), (233) define them in terms of the spin and pulse integrals (223)/(224)/(229)/(230), and the corpus evaluates none of those integrals anywhere in the transcribed text. The table's $W_{0\tau}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{on}}$ rows are blank as transcribed. So two numbers the sector uses are carried on faith, and S41 already lists the blanks among the block's open items.

5. The corpus's strongest form of the claim: four principles, six constants

Esp 116 states the claim in its sharpest form, and it is the place P22 sends S45 to adjudicate. The passage lists the four coherence principles — the principle of relativity; homogeneity and isotropy; conservation of energy-momentum; quantisation (action $nh$ divided equally between a spin and a pulse) — and asserts both halves:

Nous avons pu effectivement vérifier que le modèle leptonique, construit comme un micro-univers obéissant aux 4 principes de cohérence globale précédents, et à eux seulement, était entièrement dépourvu de paramètres arbitraires, et permettait en fait le calcul direct de 6 des constantes fondamentales de la Physique ($c$, $h$, $e$, $m$, $\mu$ et $\tau$).

The ledger's answer to the six constants is the shortest possible: $c$ and $h$ are measured universal constants, and no theory computes its own universal constants — they are the units in which every other number is expressed; $e$'s value is R1's chain, whose agreement is inherited from $\beta$ through the lepton spectrum (S34, S35, S37); and $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$ are this article's three anchors. So of the six constants the corpus lists as directly computed, the ledger returns five as inputs and one as an inherited agreement — none as an independent output.

The second half of the claim fails on a different clause, the word "only". "Ces 4 principes et à eux seulement" is a sufficiency claim, and the walk finds an input that none of the four principles supplies: $\xi$, or equivalently $l_{0}$, taken from the nucleon closure $l_{0} = R_{H}$ as a starting suggestion (S22, S34). The quantisation principle fixes the action $(n+\frac{1}{2})h$ and the equipartition; it does not fix the elementary length. So the model is built from the four principles plus a closure it borrows from the hadron sector, and the sentence's "et à eux seulement" is the part that does not survive the ledger. That is P22's [FALS] as stated, in the form the lepton sector can supply: not "the bootstrap idea is wrong", but "these four principles are not sufficient, and the sixth input is visible in the table".

What the four principles do produce is the geometry of §4 — and its dependence on the borrowed seed is exactly why the sufficiency claim cannot be repaired by counting outputs instead of inputs.

6. Verdict

§4.8's sentence is false as stated, and the corrected sentence is stronger than the register's. The corpus claims "entirely devoid of arbitrary parameters"; the walk finds seven inputs — of which three are the lepton masses — four restatements, one conditional geometric output set, two defined-but-not-derived numbers, one dead prediction and one convention, and zero parameters specific to a lepton. The correction is therefore not "one output, five inputs" alone but: the lepton sector has no lepton-specific freedom at all, which is why its one hard prediction died.

Per rule R1, marked: the sector's inputs are its three measured masses plus $c$, $h$, $\xi$ (suggested) and $e$ (shared); its restatements are $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$, $k_{S}/k_{P}$; its outputs are the geometry; its prediction is dead; its convention is unobservable. Per rule R2, there is nothing to pre-register and nothing to back-fit: the sector never had a number to adjust. Per rule R6, the three questions separate cleanly: coherence passes (one system, two directions); reproducibility passes for the geometry and fails for the masses, which are inputs; correspondence fails, and not by a near miss — the geometry is invisible and the prediction is excluded.

The register's methodological centre produces a verdict that is neither a rescue nor a dismissal: the lepton sector is the programme's most constrained construction, and the constraint is what killed it.

Summary

The inventory's §4.8 says the lepton model is "entirely devoid of arbitrary parameters", and the register asks for each lepton number to be marked INPUT or OUTPUT. The walk gives seven inputs, four restatements, one conditional output set, two undefined-in-practice numbers, one dead prediction, one convention — and zero parameters specific to a lepton.

First, the inputs. $c$ and $h$ universal; $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$ at their 1983 values, which are the sector's anchors; $\xi = 6{,}8\times10^{-4}$ suggested from the nucleon closure $l_{0} = R_{H}$ (S22, S34); and $e$ shared with the hadron sector. RC 051 §IV states this count itself — five parameters, "bien mesurés par l'expérience", from which the Système E provides $\alpha$, $\beta$, $T_{0}$ — and it is correct.

Second, the restatements, and this is where the claim fails. $\alpha = (m+3\mu-2\tau)/4\pi^{2}$ (S21, and to $0{,}002\,\%$ from the mass equations themselves, S43); $\beta = -h(1-\varepsilon)/(4\pi^{3}c)$, i.e. $94\,\%$ a pure $h/c$ combination (S37); $T_{0} = 2h/(c^{2}(\tau-\mu))$, which is also the ladder's step (S21, S43); and $k_{S} = k_{P} = 1{,}36$ as the harmonic $1{,}3632$ (S32). Nothing here is an independent number, and the two books read the same system in opposite directions: Esp computes masses from the constants, RC computes the constants from the masses, and S43 shows the two are one line of algebra apart.

Third, the outputs. Conditional on $\xi$, the Système E returns $R_{0} = 8{,}01\times10^{-15}$ cm, $\mathcal{R} = 4{,}82\times10^{-14}$ cm, $\Lambda \le 0$, $Y = 2{,}27$ for every éon, and $\langle R \rangle = 2{,}35\times10^{-14}$ cm (S43) — genuine outputs, and invisible (S42), and dependent on a seed that is not the sector's (S38). $W_{0e}$ and $W_{0\mu}$ are defined but their integrals are never evaluated.

Fourth, the dead prediction and the absent freedom. The tower at $3{,}46$, $5{,}14$, $6{,}82$, $8{,}50$ GeV is forced and excluded by LEP (R7, P1) — and it could not have been tuned, because the sector has no lepton-specific parameter: the lepton masses are the input, and the éon is one object repeated. This is the mirror image of S20's and S38's plateau pattern: a claim with no slack, tested, and dead.

Fifth, the claim's strongest form fails on two clauses, and this is the item P22 asks S45 for. Esp 116 lists the four coherence principles (relativity; homogeneity and isotropy; conservation of energy-momentum; quantisation with equipartition) and asserts that a model obeying those and only those is "entirely devoid of arbitrary parameters" and permits the direct computation of six constants — $c$, $h$, $e$, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$. The ledger returns five of the six as inputs and one as an inherited agreement: $c$ and $h$ are the units, $m$, $\mu$, $\tau$ are the anchors, and $e$'s agreement comes from $\beta$ through the lepton spectrum (S34, S35). No theory computes its own universal constants. And the word "only" fails separately: $\xi$ (i.e. $l_{0} = R_{H}$) is supplied by the nucleon closure, not by any of the four principles, so the model is built from the four plus a borrowed seed. That is P22's [FALS] as stated, in the lepton sector's own terms.

Per rules R1, R2 and R6: the masses were put in; there was nothing to pre-register and nothing to back-fit; coherence passes, reproducibility passes for the geometry and fails for the masses, correspondence fails on an invisible output and an excluded prediction.

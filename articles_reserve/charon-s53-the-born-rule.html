Charon — The Born Rule (S53)

Audits inventory §11 item 4 (the Cartan conditions; "curvature = Born probability") and §2.5–§2.6 (the fusion machinery it rests on). Sources: RC 021 §II.1 (the Cartan conditions), RC 022 ((2-3), (2-4)), RC 028 §II.6 (the status of (2-46)), Esp 030 §4.6 (eq. (135)), RC 035 §II.3 ((2-83)–(2-87)). [BRIDGE] — see §5.

Introduction

The register's line is that "Cartan is [EST], 'curvature = Born probability' is [SPEC], and what is actually derived is that $|\psi|^{2}$ gives the observation probability". After doing the three parts, the split holds and the third part is sharper than that: what the corpus derives is exact, and it is not the Born rule — it is the identification of the probability density with the fraction of the oscillator's field energy stored in one elementary cell. And the phrase "curvature = Born probability", read through the corpus's own curvature law, is wrong by two powers.

Three findings, in order of what they are worth.

(1) Cartan's conditions are stated correctly. RC 021–022 gives three: a four-dimensional Riemannian metric (2-2); a rank-2 tensor $T_{\alpha\beta}$ expressible in the $g_{\alpha\beta}$ and their derivatives to second order; and $\nabla_\alpha T^{\alpha}_{\ \beta} \equiv 0$ (2-3). These are the hypotheses of the uniqueness theorem — Cartan's (1922) in four dimensions, of which Lovelock's theorem (1971) is the general form — and their conclusion in 4D is $T_{\alpha\beta} = \alpha G_{\alpha\beta} + \Lambda g_{\alpha\beta}$ up to normalisation, which is (2-4). So the corpus's attribution is right, its conditions are complete, and [EST]/[MATH] stands. One qualification is owed and the corpus makes it itself (RC 028): the theorem fixes the form of the geometric tensor; the further identification of that tensor with the physical energy-momentum tensor is Einstein's principle, not Cartan's theorem.

(2) The Born rule is not derived and the normalisation is. The corpus says so in its own words: "L'idée fondamentale de la Mécanique ondulatoire a été de dire (et vérifiée dans l'expérience) que le carré $\psi^{2}$ de l'amplitude de l'onde psi est proportionnel à la probabilité de présence" — the rule is assumed, as verified. What follows is the constant: (2-87), $\psi^{2} = \Sigma^{2}/(8\pi W)$ with $W = (M_0^{2}c^{4}+p^{2}c^{2})^{1/2}$. The dimensional check passes — an energy density divided by an energy is an inverse volume, which is what $\int\psi^{2}d\omega = 1$ requires.

(3) And (2-87) is not new: it is Esp 030's (135) rewritten, and that identifies its integration volume. Esp 030 gives $W = \pm\frac{\Omega_0}{8\pi}\Sigma^{2}$ — the energy at a point is proportional to the field intensity there, with the elementary volume $\Omega_0$ as the factor. RC 035's (2-85) instead integrates: $\int_\omega\frac{\Sigma^{2}}{8\pi}d\omega = W$. The two are the same statement exactly when $\omega = \Omega_0$ and $\Sigma^{2}$ is uniform over it. So the corpus's Born-normalised wave function is cell-local: $\psi^{2} = \Sigma^{2}/(8\pi W) = \Sigma^{2}/(\Omega_0\bar{\Sigma}^{2})$, the local field intensity divided by its cell average, and the probability of finding the particle in a region is the fraction of the oscillator's field energy stored in that region. For the monochromatic fundamental state, $\Sigma^{2} = 8\pi W/\Omega_0$ and the density is flat: $\psi^{2} = 1/\Omega_0 = 4{,}10\times10^{40}$ cm⁻³ over a cell of side $1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm — the elementary length $l_0$, which is the nucleon's radius in this corpus (S22).

And the curvature version of the claim is off by two powers. The register, following the inventory, summarises the corpus as "the lepton's curvature is the Born probability". Read through the corpus's own curvature law, $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} + 4\Lambda$ (4-18), and $\rho = W/(\Omega_0c^{2})$ with $W\propto\Sigma^{2}$: $R - 4\Lambda \propto \rho^{2} \propto \Sigma^{4} \propto (\psi^{2})^{2}$. So the curvature is the square of the probability density, not the probability density. The correction is algebraic and needs no modern input — it is the corpus's own two equations (135) and (4-18) combined — and it matters because the phrase appears in the register and in the inventory's item 4 as though the identity were the corpus's.

The verdict. Cartan's conditions [EST]/[MATH], confirmed, with the corpus's own caveat about (2-46). The derivation of the normalisation constant [MATH] — exact and dimensionally sound. The identification that the probability density is the field-energy share [SPEC]: it is a real physical reading (a detector in a region responds to the energy in it), and it depends entirely on (2-85), the statement that the particle's whole energy is trapped field energy in a finite volume. "Curvature = Born probability" [SPEC] and, as stated, wrong by two powers. Rule R6: coherence passes; reproducibility passes for (2-87) and for the cell identity; correspondence fails, and has to — the Born rule's empirical content is not derivable from a wave equation, and the existing corpus article on the subject (the-born-rule-as-a-trace-formula-derivation-and-comparison) shows that the biquaternion framework relocates the rule rather than eliminating it.

What the Corpus Claims

The Cartan conditions, RC 021 §II.1 and RC 022:

Ces conditions complémentaires, appelées conditions de Cartan, sont les suivantes : a) L'espace-temps est représenté par une métrique riemannienne quadridimensionnelle : $ds^{2} = g_{\alpha\beta}dy^{\alpha}dy^{\beta}$ (2-2) […] b) $T_{\alpha\beta}$ est un tenseur de rang 2 qui peut être exprimé en termes des $g_{\alpha\beta}$ et leurs dérivées partielles jusqu'au second ordre. c) $T_{\alpha\beta}$ satisfait identiquement les relations de conservation : $\nabla_\alpha T^{\alpha}_{\ \beta} \equiv 0$ (2-3).

Les conditions de Cartan conduisent univoquement à la formulation usuelle de la Relativité générale […] : $R_{\alpha\beta} - \frac{1}{2}Rg_{\alpha\beta} + \Lambda g_{\alpha\beta} = -T_{\alpha\beta}$ (2-4).

And the corpus's own caveat, RC 028 §II.6:

Le premier principe accepté par Albert Einstein au moment de la création de sa Relativité générale a été l'équivalence possible entre la représentation physique et la représentation géométrique de l'Univers : $S_{\alpha\beta} = T_{\alpha\beta}$ (2-46) où $S_{\alpha\beta}$ est un tenseur géométrique supposé être défini par les conditions de Cartan et $T_{\alpha\beta}$ un tenseur impulsion-énergie.

The field energy at a point, Esp 030 §4.6 (135):

On démontre que [23] : $W = \pm\frac{\Omega_0}{8\pi}(E^{2}+H^{2}+A^{2}+V^{2}) = \pm\frac{\Omega_0}{8\pi}\Sigma^{2}$ (135), où les carrés désignent ici les produits des quantités conjuguées : $\Sigma^{2} = \Sigma\Sigma^{*}$ (136).

The confinement statement and the Born rule, RC 035 §II.3:

Si les conditions initiales du monde « extérieur » de $P$ sont telles que $P$ est « confiné » à l'intérieur d'un volume spatial $\omega$ (éventuellement infini), alors […] $$\int_\omega\frac{\Sigma^{2}}{8\pi}d\omega = \int_\omega\frac{E^{2}+H^{2}+A^{2}+V^{2}}{8\pi}d\omega = (M_0^{2}c^{4}+p^{2}c^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{2-85}$$

L'idée fondamentale de la Mécanique ondulatoire a été de dire (et vérifiée dans l'expérience) que le carré $\psi^{2}$ de l'amplitude de l'onde psi est proportionnel à la probabilité de présence dans le volume $\omega$ de l'oscillateur $\Sigma$ […] Et d'écrire, plus précisément encore, la proportionnalité entre $\psi^{2}$ et $\Sigma^{2}/8\pi$. Si l'on tient alors compte de la normalisation usuelle de toute probabilité : $\int_\omega\psi^{2}d\omega = 1$ (2-86), on est très naturellement conduit à la relation suivante entre $\psi^{2}$ et $\Sigma^{2}$ : $$\psi^{2} = \frac{\Sigma^{2}}{8\pi(M_0^{2}c^{4}+p^{2}c^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}} \tag{2-87}$$

1. Cartan's conditions, and the one step that is not Cartan's

The three conditions are quoted above and they are the right three. Read as hypotheses on a rank-2 symmetric tensor built from a 4-dimensional metric and its first two derivatives, and divergence-free as an identity, they admit in four dimensions exactly the Einstein tensor and the metric, in any linear combination: $S_{\alpha\beta} = \alpha G_{\alpha\beta} + \Lambda g_{\alpha\beta}$, which is (2-4) up to the constant and the sign convention. The result is due to Cartan (1922) in this dimension; Weyl and Vermeil's earlier variational arguments reach it, and Lovelock (1971) generalised it, adding the Gauss–Bonnet combination in $d \ge 5$ — so the four-dimensional statement the corpus uses is the strongest form of the theorem and is not affected by the higher-dimensional terms. Charon names the right person for the right statement, which is worth recording because misattributions in this area (Cartan for Lovelock, Weyl for Cartan) are common.

The qualification the corpus makes itself. A uniqueness theorem about a geometric tensor is a theorem about geometry. The next line of the corpus — "Einstein a de cette manière admis la complète équivalence entre la courbure riemannienne scalaire $R$ […] et la densité d'impulsion-énergie" (RC 028) — identifies that tensor with momentum-energy, and the corpus then departs from Einstein under a different principle (its own; S8's subject). So the [EST]/[MATH] tag covers exactly (2-2)–(2-4), and (2-46) is [SPEC] on the same footing as the rest of the corpus's ontology. The register's item 4 must be read with that boundary.

2. What the corpus derives, and what it assumes

Assumed, in the corpus's own words: the Born rule — that $\psi^{2}$ is proportional to the probability of presence, established by experiment. No chapter of the corpus claims to derive it, and no reading of (2-85)–(2-87) derives it.

Derived: the constant. (2-87) follows from (2-86) plus (2-85) in one line: normalise $\psi^{2}\propto\Sigma^{2}/8\pi$ against $\int\psi^{2}d\omega = 1$ and use $\int_\omega\Sigma^{2}/8\pi\,d\omega = W$. The interesting content is therefore not in (2-87) but in (2-85).

And (2-85) is an assumption with a physical name. It says: the particle's total energy is exactly the field energy stored in the confinement volume. That is the corpus's substitute for the classical self-energy problem — the same move as S19's trapped radiation and S47's photon gas — and it is where the model is [SPEC]. Two consequences follow, and both are checked.

Check 1 — the cell. Esp 030's (135) gives the local statement $W = \Omega_0\Sigma^{2}/8\pi$, and RC 035's (2-85) gives the integrated one. Verified: they agree exactly when $\omega = \Omega_0$ and $\Sigma^{2}$ is uniform over it, since then $\int_\omega\Sigma^{2}/8\pi\,d\omega = \Omega_0\Sigma^{2}/8\pi = W$. So (2-86)'s normalisation fixes the integration volume at $\Omega_0 = \frac{4}{3}\pi l_0^{3} = 2{,}44\times10^{-41}$ cm³, i.e. $l_0 = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm — the elementary length, and the nucleon's radius in this corpus (S22). The probability density is normalised per elementary cell.

Check 2 — the density. With $W = \Omega_0\bar{\Sigma}^{2}/8\pi$, (2-87) becomes $\psi^{2} = \Sigma^{2}/(\Omega_0\bar{\Sigma}^{2})$ — the local field intensity over its cell average — and for the fundamental monochromatic state, where the corpus's (133)–(135) apply point by point, $\psi^{2} = 1/\Omega_0 = 4{,}10\times10^{40}$ cm⁻³, a constant. So the corpus's Born density in the fundamental state is flat over the cell, which is what consistent normalisation over a single cell means, and the position-dependence of $\psi^{2}$ is the position-dependence of the field intensity at scales larger than $l_0$.

What this is worth. The statement "the probability of finding the particle in a region is the fraction of its field energy stored in that region" is a real physical reading: it is what a detector does — it responds to the energy in its own volume, so the frequency of detections tracks the energy share. And it is the corpus's own running identification, not a one-off: the same "the information is the trapped radiation" move carries Esp 098's memory chapter (S56). So S53 hands the identification to S56 and does not re-argue it there.

3. The curvature version is off by two powers

The register and the inventory both summarise item 4 as "the lepton's curvature is the Born probability". The corpus's own equations do not say that.

  • The curvature law: $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} + 4\Lambda$ (4-18, S10's subject), with $\rho$ the proper density.
  • The density and the energy: $\rho = W/(\Omega_0c^{2})$ (from $m = \Omega_0\rho$, RC 047's $m_n = \Omega_0\rho_n$).
  • The energy and the field: $W = \Omega_0\Sigma^{2}/8\pi$ (Esp 030, (135)), so $\rho = \Sigma^{2}/(8\pi c^{2})$.
  • The probability density: $\psi^{2} = \Sigma^{2}/(8\pi W)$ (2-87).

Combining: $R - 4\Lambda = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} = \xi^{2}\Sigma^{4}/(64\pi^{2}c^{4}) \propto \Sigma^{4} \propto (\psi^{2})^{2}$.

So the curvature goes as the square of the probability density, not as the probability density. The correction is internal — no modern physics enters — and it is worth stating because the phrase circulates as a summary of the corpus's position: what the corpus's own chain gives is $R - 4\Lambda \propto \rho^{2}$ with $\rho \propto \psi^{2}$, i.e. $R - 4\Lambda \propto \psi^{4}$. Anyone citing "curvature = Born probability" is citing a summary, not a derivation, and the summary is two powers wrong.

Why the summary was made is understandable, and the article says so: $\rho$ is the corpus's carrier of "how much of the oscillator is here", and $\psi^{2}$ is the probability of presence, so the two are verbally interchangeable in a sentence. They are not interchangeable in $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2} + 4\Lambda$, and that is the whole of the correction.

Rule R6, asked separately. Coherence: passes — the three equations (2-85), (135) and (4-18) are mutually consistent and the normalisation is exact. Reproducibility: passes — (2-87) and the cell identity are recoverable in one line each, and the flat density $1/\Omega_0$ is checked. Correspondence: fails, and necessarily: a wave equation cannot supply the identification of $|\psi|^{2}$ with observed frequencies, which is why the corpus says it takes the rule as experimentally verified. The honest summary is the one the existing biquaternion article uses for its own framework: the model relocates the Born rule rather than eliminating it — and what Charon relocates it into is the field-energy share of one elementary cell.

Against the-born-rule-as-a-trace-formula-derivation-and-comparison (the duplicate the register names): no overlap in the mathematics. That article works in the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$, reads the Born rule off the trace pairing, and shows what the algebra supplies (the pairing, the rank-one idempotents, the $\cos^{2}(\theta/2)$ transition probability) and what it does not (the identification with frequencies). It contains no field energy, no elementary cell, no curvature law, and no Charon. This article contains no matrices, no trace and no biquaternions. What is shared is the conclusion about the rule's status — relocated, not derived — and the two routes to that conclusion are independent, which is itself a fact about the programme worth one sentence: both the algebra and the corpus arrive at an argument that must locate the Born rule outside themselves.

Summary

The register asks for item 4 in three parts: Cartan, the curvature claim, and what is actually derived. All three are answered, and the second needs a correction.

Cartan is [EST]/[MATH], and the corpus states it correctly. RC 021–022 gives the three conditions — a 4-D Riemannian metric, a rank-2 tensor built from the metric and its derivatives to second order, and $\nabla_\alpha T^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\equiv0$ — whose unique 4-D consequence is the Einstein tensor plus the metric, i.e. (2-4). The attribution is right: this is Cartan's (1922) result, of which Lovelock's theorem (1971) is the general form, and the 4-D case is unaffected by the higher-dimensional Lovelock terms. The corpus itself marks the further step (2-46), the identification of the geometric tensor with momentum-energy, as Einstein's principle — so [EST]/[MATH] covers (2-2)–(2-4) and stops there.

The Born rule is assumed, and the norm is derived. The corpus's words: "L'idée fondamentale de la Mécanique ondulatoire a été de dire (et vérifiée dans l'expérience) que le carré $\psi^{2}$ […] est proportionnel à la probabilité de présence". What follows — (2-87), $\psi^{2} = \Sigma^{2}/(8\pi W)$ — is exact, dimensionally sound, and a one-line consequence of (2-85).

And the identification behind it fixes a volume. Esp 030's (135), $W = \Omega_0\Sigma^{2}/8\pi$, and RC 035's (2-85), $\int_\omega\Sigma^{2}/8\pi\,d\omega = W$, agree exactly when $\omega = \Omega_0$ and $\Sigma^{2}$ is uniform over it. So the normalised $\psi^{2} = \Sigma^{2}/(\Omega_0\bar{\Sigma}^{2})$ is the field intensity divided by its cell average — the probability of finding the particle in a region is the fraction of the oscillator's field energy stored there — and the fundamental monochromatic density is flat, $\psi^{2} = 1/\Omega_0 = 4{,}10\times10^{40}$ cm⁻³, over a cell of size $l_0 = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm, the nucleon's radius.

"Curvature = Born probability" is wrong by two powers, and the correction is internal. With $R = \xi^{2}\rho^{2}+4\Lambda$ (4-18), $\rho = W/(\Omega_0c^{2})$ and $W\propto\Sigma^{2}$: $R - 4\Lambda \propto \rho^{2} \propto \Sigma^{4} \propto (\psi^{2})^{2}$. The curvature is the square of the probability density, not the probability density. Anyone citing the shorthand is citing a summary; the corpus's equations give $R - 4\Lambda \propto \psi^{4}$.

Verdict: Cartan [EST]/[MATH] confirmed; (2-46) [SPEC]; the Born rule [EST] and assumed; the normalisation [MATH]; the energy-share identification [SPEC] with the cell $\Omega_0$; "curvature = Born probability" [SPEC] and two powers wrong. Per rule R6: coherence and reproducibility pass, correspondence fails necessarily — the rule is relocated, not derived, and this article's addition is to say what Charon relocates it into.

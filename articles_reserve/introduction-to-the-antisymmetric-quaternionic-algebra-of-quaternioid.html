Introduction to the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra

Introduction

The underlying real space of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$ carries the general quaternionic bilinear product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, the product of Introduction to the General Quaternionic Algebra of Quaternions, with the quaternion conjugation read on the first factor. The exchange of the two arguments splits that product into a symmetric and an antisymmetric part, and this article reads the antisymmetric part as a multiplication in its own right:

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p\bigr) =p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q, $$

the quaternionic product with its scalar part removed. The space $\mathbb{H}$ with this operation is the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra of the block, the operation named $\mathrm{AQA}$ in The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space.

This is not the algebra $\mathbb{H}$. The two share the space, the addition and the real scalars, but $\diamond$ is a different multiplication: it is alternating, it is not associative, it has no unit, and it fails the Jacobi identity, unlike the antisymmetric plain half of the same space. Throughout, the natural sign ${}^{\natural}$ is the quaternion conjugation, the bar is the identity because $\mathbb{R}$ has no nontrivial involution, and the star coincides with the natural sign and gives nothing new. The symbol $\diamond$ is reserved for this block, the star $\star$ for the parent product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$ of the general quaternionic block.

The article owns the operation as a multiplication: the rule, the class, the laws, the sixteen products of the basis, the image, the absence of a unit and the reconstruction of the parent product from its two halves. The element theory is the companion articles of the same section, all below this article in this category — Zero Divisors of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra and The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra; the defect theory is The Jacobi Failure and the Associator Defect of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, the form is The Invariant Bilinear Forms of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, the operators are The Adjoint Operators of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, and the matrix readings are its two representation articles, also below this article in this category. The split that produces the block is The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product; the comparator, whose Jacobi identity holds, is the block of Introduction to the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra of Quaternions, above this article in this category.

Conventions. The algebra is $\mathbb{H}$ with basis $e_t=1,\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_z$ and $\vec e_x\vec e_y=\vec e_z$, so that $\vec e_x^2=\vec e_y^2=\vec e_z^2=-e_t$ and $e_j\vec e_k=-\vec e_ke_j$ for $j\neq k$. A generic element is $\tilde q=q_te_t+\vec q$ with $\vec q=\sum_{k=1}^{3}q_k\vec e_k$ and $q_\mu\in\mathbb{R}$; $\vec p\cdot\vec q$ is the Euclidean dot product and $\vec p\times\vec q$ the vector product of the vector parts. The parent product is $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, of scalar–vector rule $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=(p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q)+p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$; the quaternion form is $N(\tilde p,\tilde q)=p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q$. The circle $\circ$ is reserved for the composition of maps.

The Antisymmetrised Quaternionic Product

The Rule

Definition. The antisymmetrised quaternionic product of two quaternions is

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12\bigl(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p\bigr). $$

It is the antisymmetrisation of the parent product $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q$, the operation called the antisymmetric part of the product in The Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts of an Algebra Product, and the operation named $\mathrm{AQA}$ in The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space. The space $\mathbb{H}$ with $\diamond$ is the antisymmetric quaternionic algebra of the block.

The Coordinate and Scalar–Vector Forms

The parent product reads $\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=(p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q)+p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$, and its exchange $\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p$ differs in the sign of the scalar part, in the order of the mixed terms and in the sign of the cross term. Subtracting and halving, the scalar part cancels and the operation is

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q . $$

Read on the coordinates,

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q =(p_0q_x-q_tp_1-p_2q_z+p_3q_y)\vec e_x +(p_0q_y-q_tp_2-p_3q_x+p_1q_z)\vec e_y +(p_0q_z-q_tp_3-p_1q_y+p_2q_x)\vec e_z . $$

Remark (the scalar part of the parent is removed). The operation is the parent product with its scalar coordinate deleted: the scalar value $p_0q_t+\vec p\cdot\vec q$ of the parent is the trace of its $2\times2$ model, and the antisymmetrisation kills the trace. Every value is a pure element.

Bilinearity and the Class

Proposition. The operation is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear and alternating,

$$ \tilde p\diamond\tilde q=-\tilde q\diamond\tilde p,\qquad \tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0 , $$

it is neither commutative nor associative, and it is not the antisymmetric plain operation: at the units $\tilde p=e_t,\tilde q=\vec e_x$ it gives $e_t\diamond \vec e_x=\vec e_x$, while $e_t\times \vec e_x=0$.

Proof. The difference of two biadditive maps is biadditive; the exchange of the arguments reverses the difference, which is the alternation. The computation on the units is the table below.

The Identity with the Quaternionic Bracket

Definition. The quaternionic bracket of the parent product is

$$ [\tilde p,\tilde q]_{\natural}=\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p . $$

Proposition. The operation is half the quaternionic bracket,

$$ [\tilde p,\tilde q]_{\natural}=2\,(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q), $$

and it is the commutator of the parent product, which is its structural reason: the commutator of a product is alternating, and it satisfies the Jacobi identity exactly when the parent is associative; the parent quaternionic product is not associative, hence the failure read below.

Proof. The identity is the definition against the definition of the halved difference.

The Image, the Absence of a Unit and the Reconstruction

Proposition (the image). The image of the block is the vector subspace,

$$ \mathbb{H}\diamond\mathbb{H}=\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})=\{\vec q\}=\{q_t=0\}, $$

of real dimension three, and the block is onto it, since $\vec e_x\diamond \vec e_y=-\vec e_z$ and its images generate the vector subspace. The left action of $e_t$ is the projection onto the vector subspace, $e_t\diamond\tilde q=\vec q$, and the right action is its negative, $\tilde q\diamond e_t=-\vec q$.

Proposition (no unit). The block has no left unit, no right unit and no two-sided unit. The candidate $e_t$ has $e_t\diamond\tilde q=\vec q$, which is not $\tilde q$ at a non-pure element, and no other candidate reaches the scalar direction, every value being pure.

Proposition (the reconstruction). The parent product is the sum of its two halves,

$$ \mathrm{GQA}=\mathrm{SQA}+\mathrm{AQA},\qquad \tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t+\tilde p\diamond\tilde q , $$

the symmetric half being $\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q+\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=N(\tilde p,\tilde q)e_t$, which collapses onto the scalar line. The square map sees only the symmetric half, $\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde q=N(\tilde q,\tilde q)e_t$, so the antisymmetric half carries no square and no norm.

Remark (the splitting realises one half by an element). The left multiplication by $e_t$ is the projection onto the vector subspace, and the right one is its negative: this is the property the specificities article reads as the block's criterion.

The Sixteen Brackets of the Basis

The scalar unit acts by the projection and the vector units multiply by minus the cross product:

$\diamond$ $e_t$ $\vec e_x$ $\vec e_y$ $\vec e_z$
$e_t$ $0$ $\vec e_x$ $\vec e_y$ $\vec e_z$
$\vec e_x$ $-\vec e_x$ $0$ $-\vec e_z$ $\vec e_y$
$\vec e_y$ $-\vec e_y$ $\vec e_z$ $0$ $-\vec e_x$
$\vec e_z$ $-\vec e_z$ $-\vec e_y$ $\vec e_x$ $0$

The table has three kinds of entries: the zero diagonal $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$; the eight mixed entries $e_t\diamond \vec e_k=\vec e_k$ and $\vec e_k\diamond e_t=-\vec e_k$, which are the projection and its negative; and the six pure entries $e_j\diamond \vec e_k=-e_j\times \vec e_k$.

The Failure of the Jacobi Identity

Definition. The cyclic sum of the block at a triple is

$$ J(\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r) =(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)\diamond\tilde r +(\tilde q\diamond\tilde r)\diamond\tilde p +(\tilde r\diamond\tilde p)\diamond\tilde q . $$

Theorem (the failure). The cyclic sum does not vanish; its closed form is

$$ J(\tilde p,\tilde q,\tilde r) =-p_0\,(\vec q\times\vec r)+q_t\,(\vec p\times\vec r)-r_0\,(\vec p\times\vec q), $$

and the catalogue witness is the triple $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$, where

$$ J(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)=(e_t\diamond \vec e_x)\diamond \vec e_y+(\vec e_x\diamond \vec e_y)\diamond e_t+(\vec e_y\diamond e_t)\diamond \vec e_x =\vec e_x\diamond \vec e_y+(-\vec e_z)\diamond e_t+(-\vec e_y)\diamond \vec e_x=-\vec e_z . $$

The block therefore satisfies the Jacobi identity nowhere but on the triples whose scalar parts satisfy the linear condition $J=0$, and it is not a Lie algebra; the full reading is The Jacobi Failure and the Associator Defect of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra, below this article in this category.

Proof. Inserting the explicit form into the three terms and collecting gives the closed form, which is linear in the scalar triple $(p_0,q_t,r_0)$ with the three cross products as coefficients; at $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$ only the first term survives and equals $-(\vec e_x\times \vec e_y)=-\vec e_z$. The count of nonzero basis triples is $18$ of the $64$.

Remark (the comparator). The antisymmetric plain block of the same space differs in this alone: it satisfies the cyclic sum identically, its closed form being zero, and is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ of The Lie Algebra of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra.

The Placement of the Block among the Six

The block is the antisymmetrisation of the general quaternionic product, the second of the two antisymmetric operations of the six, set out in The 6 Products of the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space and structured in The Algebraic Structures over the Quaternioid $\mathbb{R}$ Space: the two antisymmetric structures are this one and the antisymmetric plain one, and the law table separates them on the Jacobi identity, which the plain one satisfies and this one fails (The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Plain Algebra and The Specificities of the Antisymmetric Quaternionic Algebra). The block agrees with its plain companion on alternation, on the properties that alternation implies, and on the element theory, and differs on the identity and the structure it defines.

Summary

The antisymmetrised quaternionic multiplication is $\tilde p\diamond\tilde q=\tfrac12(\tilde p^{\natural}\tilde q-\tilde q^{\natural}\tilde p)=p_0\vec q-q_t\vec p-\vec p\times\vec q$, the anticommutative half of the general quaternionic product, of quaternion bracket $[\tilde p,\tilde q]_{\natural}=2(\tilde p\diamond\tilde q)$. It is $\mathbb{R}$-bilinear and alternating, with $\tilde p\diamond\tilde p=0$; its image is the vector subspace $\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{H})$; the left multiplication by $e_t$ is the projection onto the vector subspace and the right one its negative; and it has no unit. The parent product is reconstructed as $\mathrm{GQA}=\mathrm{SQA}+\mathrm{AQA}$, and the square map sees only the symmetric half. Unlike its plain companion it fails the Jacobi identity: the cyclic sum has the closed form $-p_0(\vec q\times\vec r)+q_t(\vec p\times\vec r)-r_0(\vec p\times\vec q)$ and the witness $(e_t,\vec e_x,\vec e_y)$ gives $-\vec e_z$. This failure is the whole difference between the two antisymmetric blocks.

Charon — Fractional Charge from Saturation: the Geometry of 2/3 (S47)

Audits inventory §6.2 (fractional charge 1/3, 2/3 from electrostatic saturation) and its number. Sources: Esp 062 §VI.18.3, eqs (265)–(268) and the tangency construction; RC 056 §V.5 and RC 064 §III (the level bookkeeping). The dead part of the same item — the six levels as flavours and the top quark — is P4 and P5, not this article.

Introduction

The register says of §6.2: "the mechanism is geometric and gives exactly 2/3 from $\alpha = \pi/6$, which is elegant but arbitrary", and asks for the geometry to be done. Done, it splits into one exact result and one unmotivated step, and the split is the article. The exact result: in the corpus's tangency configuration — two equal spheres in contact, centres $2R$ apart — the angle is $\alpha = \arcsin(R/2R) = \arcsin(1/2) = \pi/6$ exactly, and the corpus's ratio $N'/N = 1 - (\pi/6)/(\pi/2)$ is $2/3$ exactly. The unmotivated step is the denominator $\pi/2$. Nothing in the corpus fixes the reference angle; with $\pi$ as the reference the same tangency gives $5/6$, and with $2\pi$ it gives $11/12$. So the corpus's 2/3 is exact, and it is produced by a ratio whose second term is a free choice.

And the choice is not innocent, because the quantity being divided is not an angle. The corpus wants the fraction of the electrons' photons still available for an interaction with a distant electron; that fraction should be a ratio of solid angles or of a projected area, and the same tangency read in three dimensions gives a blocked fraction $\frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos \pi/6) = 0{,}0670$ — not $1/3$. The corpus's construction is planar, and it converts a plane angle into a photon count. That is the sense in which §6.2's mechanism is elegant and arbitrary: the elegance is in the tangency, which is forced, and the arbitrariness is in the map from angle to photon fraction, which is not.

The third finding is a count, and it narrows the mechanism's achievement. The geometry produces one number, $2/3$. The second fractional charge, $1/3$, is not produced by any geometry in the corpus: it is the remainder forced by the requirement that each holon level carry an integral total charge, distributed over three (level 0) or two (levels 1–5) quarks (RC 056 §V.5, RC 064 §III.4). So the mechanism derives $\pm 2/3$ geometrically and obtains $1/3$ arithmetically, and the two observed quark charges are one geometric output plus one integrality condition.

The verdict, per rule R6. Coherence: passes; the construction closes and the arithmetic is exact. Reproducibility: passes on the algebra — $\alpha = \pi/6$, $N'/N = 2/3$, both to the last digit, and the corpus's figure description is consistent with the equations. Correspondence: the number matches nature (quark charges $\pm 1/3$, $\pm 2/3$ are established since deep-inelastic scattering), and the match is worth less than it looks, because 2/3 is what the construction was built to give and because the reference angle that produces it is unmotivated. §6.2's [MIX] tag stands, with the value confirmed [EST] and the derivation confirmed [SPEC] — and the [SPEC] located, for the first time, in one symbol.

What the Corpus Claims

The construction, Esp 062 §VI.18.3:

Une partie des photons des électrons composant la structure $Q$ interagissent entre eux de façon permanente, c'est cette interaction qui justifie la cohésion de la structure $Q$ elle-même; mais cela signifie aussi que le nombre de photons dans chaque électron composant $Q$ qui sont disponibles à chaque instant pour une interaction électrostatique avec $E$ n'est plus (246) : $N = \int_{v_1}^{\infty}\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}n_v\,dv$ mais seulement une fraction $N'$ de ceux-ci […] avec $n'_v < n_v$. Nous pouvons calculer $N'/N$ en considérant deux électrons interagissant à distance « de contact », c'est-à-dire à $r = 2R$ :

$$\sin\alpha = \frac{R}{2R} \quad\text{soit}\quad \alpha = \frac{\pi}{6}, \qquad \frac{N'}{N} = 1 - \frac{\pi/6}{\pi/2} = \frac{2}{3}.$$

Cela signifie encore que la structure $Q$ agira sur un électron éloigné tel que $E$ comme si $Q$ avait une charge électrique de seulement $\frac{2}{3}e$. Nous appellerons quark une structure leptonique composite telle que $Q$ […] Une structure $Q$ peut seulement s'annihiler avec une autre structure $Q$, afin d'assurer la conservation de la charge électrique : $Q^{+} + Q^{-} \to 2\gamma$.

The three-lepton structure and the geometry's figure, Esp 062:

[…] une structure « en ligne » $Q$, dans laquelle un électron négatif est à distance $2R$ de deux électrons positifs, l'ensemble formant de manière « naturelle » un alignement des trois particules.

The bookkeeping that supplies $1/3$, RC 056 §V.5:

[…] si cette charge totale est produite par trois leptons chargés enfermés dans les limites nucléoniques, la saturation électrostatique ne pourra conduire qu'à trois charges électrostatiques de $+1/3$, ou encore deux de $+2/3$ et une de $-1/3$ (proton); ou deux charges de $+1/3$ et une de $-2/3$ (neutron). On voit ainsi apparaître les charges « fractionnaires » de $1/3$ et $2/3$.

And the level assignment, RC 064 §III.4:

Le niveau holonique le plus bas ($n=0$) […] comprend trois quarks ayant la masse propre de l'électron : charge $+1 = +1/3+1/3+1/3$ (cas du proton, niveau stable), charge $0 = +1/3+1/3-2/3$ (cas du neutron). […] Chaque niveau supérieur porte deux quarks (ou antiquarks) en rotation géodésique. Les charges électriques sur chaque niveau supérieur ont deux possibilités : charge $0 = +2/3-2/3$ (niveau stable), charge $+1 = +1/3+2/3$.

1. The tangency, recomputed

The configuration is two equal spheres of radius $R$ whose centres are separated by $2R$: they are tangent. The corpus measures, from the centre of one, the angle subtended by the other's point of contact, and writes $\sin\alpha = R/2R$. Verified: $\alpha = \arcsin\frac{1}{2} = \pi/6 = 30°$ exactly, and the distance $2R$ is the corpus's own condition of "contact" — the same condition that RC 056 §V.5 states as the saturation threshold $r \le 2l_{0}$.

quantity value
$\alpha = \arcsin(1/2)$ $\pi/6$ exactly, $30°$
$(\pi/6)/(\pi/2)$ $1/3$ exactly
$N'/N = 1 - (\pi/6)/(\pi/2)$ $2/3$ exactly
the corpus's stated charge $\pm\frac{2}{3}e$

So the arithmetic is exact and depends on nothing but the tangency. That is the register's "elegant", and it is earned: $2/3$ is not a fit and is not rounded, and it comes from the single most natural configuration of two identical extended charges, the one at distance $2R$.

Two further consistencies are worth recording. (i) The saturation condition is stated twice in the corpus, identically. Esp 062 needs two electrons at $r = 2R$; RC 056 §V.5 says saturation begins when two éons are separated by $r \le 2l_{0}$, which is the same number if $R = l_{0}$ — the elementary length of RC (2-18-a), $l_{0} = 1{,}8\times10^{-14}$ cm, so the threshold is $3{,}6\times10^{-14}$ cm. (ii) The "in-line" structure $Q$ of three leptons is what makes the contact geometry a pairwise argument: each outer lepton is at $2R$ from the central one, so one tangency suffices, and the corpus's figure (three leptons in a row plus a distant test electron) is consistent with the equation it is used for.

2. The denominator is the free choice, and the corpus never defends it

Everything hinges on dividing by $\pi/2$. The corpus's step is $N'/N = 1 - \alpha/(\pi/2)$ — the blocked angle measured against a right angle. No sentence in the corpus explains why the reference is a right angle, and the same geometry against the other natural references gives different charges:

reference angle $N'/N$ the charge it would predict
$\pi/2$ (the corpus's choice) $2/3$ $\pm\frac{2}{3}e$ — the observed value
$\pi$ (a straight angle) $5/6$ $\pm\frac{5}{6}e$ — not observed
$2\pi$ (a full turn) $11/12$ $\pm\frac{11}{12}e$ — not observed

So the mechanism does not produce $2/3$ from a principle; it produces $2/3$ from a principle plus an unstated normalisation. The article states this as the sharpest available form of the register's "arbitrary", and it is a statement the register could not make, because the arbitrariness is not in $\alpha = \pi/6$ — that is forced — but one line further on.

A second, independent sign that the map is unmotivated: the same tangency in three dimensions does not give $2/3$. If the quantity to be counted is the fraction of the structure's directions permanently engaged with its partner, the correct measure is the solid angle cut off by a tangent equal sphere, $\Omega/4\pi = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos\alpha) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos 30°) = 0{,}0670$. The blocked fraction would then be $6{,}7\,\%$ and the charge $0{,}933e$, not $\frac{2}{3}e$. The corpus's construction is therefore planar throughout: it divides one plane angle by another, and the ratio of plane angles is neither a fraction of directions nor a fraction of photons. This is a shape remark about the mechanism, not a refutation of it — a planar construction may be what the corpus means, since its figure is a cross-section — but it shows that the step from "angle" to "photon count" carries all of the model's content, and it is the step the corpus leaves blank.

3. The mechanism produces one number; $1/3$ is arithmetic

The corpus's geometry is about a two-charge structure at contact. It never produces $1/3$ geometrically. What supplies $1/3$ is the integrality of the level charge, stated in RC 056 §V.5 and tabulated in RC 064 §III.4:

  • level 0 carries three quarks, whose charges must sum to $0$ or $+1$: the admissible triples from $\{\pm 1/3, \pm 2/3\}$ are $1/3+1/3+1/3$ (proton), $1/3+1/3-2/3$ (neutron), $2/3-1/3-1/3$, and the negatives;
  • each level 1–5 carries two, whose charges must sum to $0$ or $+1$: $2/3-2/3$, $2/3+1/3$, and the negatives.

So the set $\{1/3, 2/3\}$ is the set of charges that $3$- and $2$-tuples of the geometric unit $\pm 2/3$ can be reduced to while keeping the level charge integral — and $1/3$ is not a geometric output but the arithmetic consequence of the reduction. Verified by enumeration: for three objects each of charge $\pm 2/3$ or $\pm 1/3$, the integral sums are $0$ and $\pm 1$, and the observed proton and neutron charge assignments are the corpus's own table. The count that matters is therefore: one geometric number, $2/3$; one arithmetic number, $1/3$; and the observed quark charges are exactly these two.

And the corpus's confinement claim is where the mechanism stops being a mechanism. Esp 062 writes that a structure $Q$ can annihilate only with another $Q$, $Q^{+}+Q^{-}\to 2\gamma$, and RC 056 §V.5 speaks of quarks as "des leptons chargés enfermés dans le volume hadronique" while Esp 062 also allows "particules composites, en principe indépendantes, telles que les quarks $Q$". The two statements cannot both hold, and neither is the observed situation: quarks are confined, and the corpus's geometry does not confine them. That defect belongs to §6.3 and P6, and the article records the boundary rather than the argument.

4. What the value is worth

The observed charges are $\pm 1/3$ and $\pm 2/3$, and the corpus gets them. Two things must be said about that, in opposite directions.

(a) The value is not a prediction in the ordinary sense. The number $2/3$ was measured long before the model — its value was established by deep-inelastic scattering and by meson and baryon spectroscopy — so the corpus is reproducing a known quantity. What the corpus claims is the reason, and the reason's only free element is the reference angle of §2.

(b) But the construction is not a fit either, and this is the distinction the register's [MIX] needs. A fit chooses its parameters to match; the corpus chooses a configuration (two equal spheres in contact) and a reference (a right angle), and both are natural rather than tuned. The honest description is: a natural geometric construction plus an unmotivated normalisation, giving the observed charges exactly. That is worth more than an arithmetic coincidence (which S43 and S37 record elsewhere in the corpus) and less than a prediction, and it is exactly what [MIX] means here.

One comparison is owed, and it cuts the other way. In the standard account the fractional charges are eigenvalues of the electric-charge generator acting on the quarks' SU(3) and weak multiplets: their values are fixed by the gauge group and the representations, and their measurement confirms the group structure. The corpus's version fixes the same numbers from geometry with no group at all. The two routes agree on the numbers and share nothing else — which is why §6.2's [SPEC] stands and why the agreement is a coincidence of value, not of structure (S48).

5. Verdict

§6.2's [MIX] stands, and the article relocates its two halves. The value of the fractional charges is [EST] — established physics, correctly quoted. The mechanism is [SPEC] and remains [SPEC] after the geometry is done, and the article's contribution is to say where: not in $\alpha = \pi/6$, which the tangency forces exactly, but in the division by $\pi/2$, which nothing forces; and not in $1/3$, which is not geometric at all.

Rule R6, asked separately. Coherence: passes — the configuration, the angle and the ratio close exactly, and the corpus's saturation threshold $2l_{0}$ is the same $2R$ the geometry needs. Reproducibility: passes — every number in the section is recovered to the last digit, and the alternatives in §2 are computable in one line. Correspondence: the value matches nature and the derivation does not earn that match, because the same construction gives $5/6$ or $11/12$ or $0{,}933e$ under substitutions the corpus does not exclude. The distinction the register must keep: a model that reproduces a known number exactly by a construction with one free choice is [MIX] — not [FALS] (nothing is contradicted) and not [EST] (nothing is established).

One line for the register's own use: the corpus's geometry is exactly right, and it derives one of the two quark charges.

Summary

The register asks for the geometry behind §6.2's $N'/N = 2/3$. Done, and its status is narrower than the inventory's phrase "elegant but arbitrary" suggests, in both halves of the sentence.

First, the tangency is exact and forced. Two equal spheres in contact, centres $2R$ apart, give $\sin\alpha = R/2R$, so $\alpha = \pi/6 = 30°$ exactly — the same $2R$ that RC 056 §V.5 states as the saturation threshold $r \le 2l_{0}$, i.e. $3{,}6\times10^{-14}$ cm. The corpus's ratio $1 - (\pi/6)/(\pi/2) = 2/3$ is then exact to the last digit, and it matches the corpus's stated charge $\pm\frac{2}{3}e$ and the observed quark charges.

Second, the arbitrariness is not in $\alpha$ but in the denominator, and the corpus never defends it. No sentence explains why the blocked angle is measured against a right angle; against $\pi$ the same geometry gives $5/6$, and against $2\pi$ it gives $11/12$. And the ratio is one of plane angles, whereas the quantity wanted is a fraction of photons: the same tangency read in three dimensions blocks $\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos 30°) = 6{,}7\,\%$ of directions, giving $0{,}933e$ rather than $\frac{2}{3}e$. The step from angle to photon count carries all the model's content and is the step left blank.

Third, the mechanism produces exactly one number. $2/3$ is geometric; $1/3$ is not. It is the remainder forced by the integrality of the level charge over three quarks (level 0: $1/3+1/3+1/3$, $1/3+1/3-2/3$) or two (levels 1–5: $2/3-2/3$, $2/3+1/3$), as RC 056 §V.5 and RC 064 §III.4 tabulate. So the observed set $\{1/3, 2/3\}$ is one geometric output plus one integrality condition, and the article records the count.

Fourth, the confinement claim is not the mechanism's. Esp 062 allows quarks "$Q$" to be independent composite particles and to annihilate as $Q^{+}+Q^{-}\to2\gamma$, while RC 056 §V.5 describes them as charged leptons locked inside the hadron volume. Confinement is observed; the corpus's geometry does not supply it. That defect is §6.3's and P6's, and is noted here only as a boundary.

Verdict: [MIX] stands — the value [EST], the mechanism [SPEC], and the [SPEC] now located in a single unmotivated choice; the value matches nature, and the derivation does not earn the match. Per rule R6: coherence and reproducibility pass, correspondence passes on the number and fails on the route.

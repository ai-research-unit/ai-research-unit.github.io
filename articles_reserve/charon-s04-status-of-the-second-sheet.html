Charon: The Status of the Second Sheet

Introduction

The register asks: "The load-bearing move: is a second populated sheet forced, licensed, or posited? Re-read the horizon argument against Kruskal and 't Hooft's complex GR. Audits §1.2."

The answer is that it is licensed and posited, and not forced. The corpus's argument gives the second sheet a natural language — the exchange of space and time across a Schwarzschild horizon — and then the corpus posits it, in its own words, as "un de ses axiomes de base" and as a "représentation non locale". Nothing in general relativity or in the data requires a second populated spacetime, and nothing forbids one. The article's finding is sharper than that verdict, and it is new: the argument's force depends on rejecting the coordinate-independent reading of the horizon, and Charon rejects it explicitly and by name — Novikov, Eddington–Finkelstein, Kruskal–Szekeres, all of them, at Esp 115. His stated reason is that the Schwarzschild metric is only valid where $T_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ and therefore cannot describe his region I. That reason is category-mismatched: his region I is a collapsing star's interior, where $T \ne 0$, while the extensions he rejects are statements about the vacuum manifold. So the second sheet is purchased by a rejection that does not do the work it is asked to do.

A second finding, and it connects this article to the cosmology. The interior region Charon reads as a separate universe has spatial 3-volume $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$ at each instant of its own time — the volume of a closed $K = +1$ three-sphere, Esp 039 §1.2. That is the same $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$ that appears in the cosmological action (RC (5-41); S14). So Charon's second sheet and his closed-universe cosmology are the same structure, and the verdict of this article propagates to S50, S51 and S52.

What this article adds, in the one line the register requires of a [BRIDGE]. The register marks S4 as a bridge, duplicated by the framework's introductory article. What is not in the framework's articles is the contents of the corpus's own Annexe 7 — the rejection of the extension, quoted in Part 3 — and the observation that the second sheet and the closed universe are one structure. The framework's article owns the complex geometry; this article owns the corpus's argument for populating it, and its verdict.

Part 1 — What the corpus says

The argument, in the corpus's words

Esp 039 §1.2 sets up a Schwarzschild frame about an arbitrary point of the space of quantised general relativity (RGQ), takes the vacuum solution

$$ds^{2} = \left(1 - \frac{2I}{r}\right)c^{2}dt^{2} - r^{2}(d\theta^{2} + \sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}) - \left(1 - \frac{2I}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^{2},$$

with $r_s = 2I$ the Schwarzschild radius, and argues as follows. The components $g_{tt}$ and $g_{rr}$ change sign when $r = r_s$ is crossed, so $r$ and $t$ are successively of the space type and the time type, and "ceci peut être interprété en disant que les vecteurs de base sur les 4 axes du référentiel sont tous multipliés par $i = \sqrt{-1}$ quand on traverse $r = r_s$". The corpus then draws the conclusion:

"Cette remarque constitue l'un des meilleurs arguments pour démontrer qu'un espace-temps complexe est nécessaire pour effectuer une représentation complète des phénomènes physiques", because a spacetime that is not rigorously empty requires the coexistence at each point of two complementary spacetimes: an entropic space $(\tilde r, \tilde t)$, the ordinary hyperbolic metric, and a negentropic space, obtained by multiplying the entropic basis vectors by $i$.

Two further statements complete the picture. The projection argument (Esp 039 §1.3): since an observer working in the entropic coordinates ignores the negentropic space, that space's phenomena "vont être « cachés » et confondus au point mathématique $M_0$", so the corpus is "contraint de proposer ce qu'on nomme généralement une représentation non locale de $M_0$". And the corpus says what kind of move this is: "La Relativité complexe, qui suppose la complexité de l'espace physique comme un de ses axiomes de base, justifie un tel type de représentation non locale."

Two counts on the argument, stated before it is criticised. The Schwarzschild radius it computes for the Sun is correct to its one printed figure: "$M = 2.10^{33}$ g ... $r_S = 3.10^{5}$ cm" (Esp 114), and $2GM/c^{2}$ with $M = 2\times10^{33}$ g gives $2{,}97\times10^{5}$ cm. And the corpus's $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$ for the interior observer's spatial volume is the standard 3-volume of a three-sphere of radius $R$. Neither number is at issue.

The horizon "surface" that the exterior observer sees

Esp 039 §1.2 closes with a computation; the corpus gives it the Wheeler attribution:

$$\tilde S = \int_{r = 2I} |g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}|\,dt\,d\phi = 0 .$$

The computation is correct and its content is standard. At $r = 2I$ the component $g_{tt} = 1 - 2I/r$ vanishes identically, so the integrand vanishes and the integral is zero for any metric of this form. What this establishes is that the horizon is a null hypersurface — which is established physics. It is not an independent argument for a second universe; it is the null character of the horizon, re-expressed as a vanishing of a "surface" measured with the metric component that vanishes there.

Annexe 7: the corpus's position on the extension

Esp 114–115 are the corpus's own appendix on the Schwarzschild metric and the formation of "hole-universes", and they are the decisive text for this article. Esp 114 sets out the standard argument — the metric is valid only where the physical tensor vanishes, so the sole region of validity is $r_S \le R \le r$; when a contracting star reaches $R = r_S$ the space separates into region I ($r \le R = r_S$, where $T_{\alpha\beta} \ne 0$ and the metric $S$ is inapplicable) and region II ($r > R$). Charon reads region I as closed on itself, a univers-trou.

Then Esp 115 closes the discussion with the corpus's rejection:

"nous contestons la validité des résultats des recherches concernant la prétendue « pathologie » de la métrique $S$ autour de $r = r_s$. La métrique $S$, pas plus que les métriques équivalentes obtenues par de simples changements de coordonnées (métriques de Novikov, Eddington-Finkelstein, Kruskal-Szekeres...) ne nous paraissent exploitables dans leurs résultats dans la mesure où elles prétendent représenter ce qui se passe dans la région I pour laquelle $T_{\alpha\beta}$ est très différent de zéro."

So the corpus knows the standard literature, names it, and declines it — and the reason it gives is that those metrics "pretend to represent what happens in region I, for which $T_{\alpha\beta}$ is very different from zero".

Part 2 — What the general theory says about the same surface

Three statements, each established, and each of which the corpus's argument has to get past.

The horizon is a null surface, not a curvature singularity. The geometric invariants are finite at $r = r_s$: the Kretschmann scalar $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = 48I^{2}/r^{6}$ diverges only as $r \to 0$, not at $r = 2I$. Nothing local happens to an infalling body at the horizon. The change of sign of $g_{tt}$ and $g_{rr}$ is a property of the static chart, in which the timelike Killing vector is normalised to unit length; the invariant content is that $\partial_t$ becomes spacelike inside, so no static observers exist there.

The maximal analytic extension continues the vacuum solution through the surface. Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates cover the whole vacuum manifold: region I (the exterior), region II (the future interior), region III (a second exterior), region IV (the past interior). Region III is a second exterior region — so the corpus's "two types of space" is not a fantasy — but it is a region of one analytic manifold. It is not reached from region I through the horizon (the horizon is a one-way, null surface), and the extension is a statement about the vacuum solution, canonical and standard, not a licence to populate a second spacetime.

The corpus's stated reason for rejecting the extension does not reach it. Charon objects that the metric $S$ is not valid in "region I, where $T_{\alpha\beta} \ne 0$". That is true, and it is about the interior of a star. His region I is $r \le R = r_S$, a region of high density — matter and radiation — which the vacuum metric cannot describe. The extensions he names are statements about the vacuum manifold, where $T_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ and where the metric is valid by his own criterion. Region I as he defines it — the star's interior — is not Kruskal's region II, which is the vacuum interior of the maximally extended solution. Two different objects share the name "interior", and the objection to the first has been used, by a slip of the same name, against the second.

One further correction, small but load-bearing for the "multiplied by $i$" reading. Crossing $r = r_s$ changes the type of two of the four coordinates — $r$ becomes timelike and $t$ spacelike — while the angular directions remain spacelike. So "the base vectors on the 4 axes are all multiplied by $i$" is stronger than the mathematics. The clean statement is that the metric's overall sign is not flipped while two of its four diagonal entries change sign; a complex tetrad can absorb that change, and absorbing a signature flip into a complex frame is a rewrite, not a second spacetime.

A note on complex general relativity, since the register asks for the comparison. Complex, complexified and biquaternion metrics have a long history as a representation — Einstein, Schrödinger and Lanczos in the classical period, and later work on complex metrics in quantum gravity — and the register records that precedent as the reason inventory §1.1 is tagged [SPEC] with real precedent rather than dismissed. The series draws no technical result from that literature, and the article says so rather than trading on it: what the argument has to get past is the standard reading of the Schwarzschild horizon, which is Part 2's first three statements, and no established reading of a complex metric takes it to license a second populated spacetime. Registering a deliberate citation of that literature is a prerequisite (§8), not a result of this article.

Part 3 — The finding: the second sheet is purchased by a rejection

Put the corpus's own two passages side by side. The justification for the second sheet is the horizon argument: the basis vectors are multiplied by $i$ on crossing $r = r_s$, therefore a complex spacetime is necessary. The annexe is a rejection of the coordinate-change readings — Novikov, Eddington–Finkelstein, Kruskal–Szekeres — on the ground that they claim to describe region I, where the metric is invalid.

The annexe is what keeps the horizon argument viable, and the annexe's reason does not hold. If the maximal extension is accepted, the horizon is a null surface of one analytic manifold, the "sign flip" is a chart statement, and the argument yields at most a natural language for complexification — not a second populated sheet. If the extension is rejected, the two-region picture survives and the second sheet has a home. So:

The corpus's second sheet is not a consequence of its horizon argument. It is a consequence of its horizon argument plus its rejection of the extension — and the rejection is stated on grounds that apply to the stellar interior and not to the vacuum extension it is aimed at.

And the rejection is not a whim. It is a deliberate position, printed in an appendix, with the literature named. This is why the register's [MIX] tag is right and can now be made precise: the premise of the argument is [EST], the rejection of the extension is [SPEC] and arguably [OUT], and the conclusion — a populated second sheet — inherits the [SPEC]. The register's own word for the conclusion is "licensed": this article's finding is that what licenses it is not the horizon alone but the horizon read through a deliberate refusal of the standard reading.

Part 4 — One structure, not two: the second sheet is the closed universe

The interior observer's spatial volume, in the corpus's own words, is "$2\pi^{2}R^{3}$" at each instant of his own time, with $R$ the sphere defined by the Schwarzschild radius $r_s = 2I$ (Esp 039 §1.2). $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$ is the 3-volume of a three-sphere of radius $R$ — a closed, $K = +1$ spatial section. And the same $2\pi^{2}c^{2}R^{3}$ is the coefficient that carries the corpus's cosmological action,

$$A = \int_{0}^{T} 2\pi^{2}c^{2}R^{3}\left(\frac{D_\rho}{c^{2}} + \frac{D_M}{R^{4}}\right)d\tau + 2\pi(S+P) = 0,$$

which the corpus uses to obtain $A = S = P = 0$ and its zero-total-energy identity (RC (5-41), (5-45), (5-46); S14, S50).

The consequence is a structural statement about the whole programme. Charon's region I — the "hole-universe" behind the horizon — is the same object as his closed cosmological model: a closed $K = +1$ three-space of volume $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$, with a quantised radial pulsation, whose interior is the material world. The micro-universe, the holon and the cosmos are the same construction at three scales. (This is exactly the repetition S11 records for the pulse, and S18 for the closure.)

So this article's verdict propagates. If the second sheet is posited rather than forced, then the closed-universe reading of the region behind the horizon is posited with it, and the cosmology that uses the same $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$ — S50 the zero-total-energy identity, S51 $R_i \approx 6\times10^{24}$ cm, S52 the cycle — rests on a spatial section whose closedness is, at that scale, a hypothesis of the same family. S50's identity still verifies numerically (it is an identity of the corpus's own numbers) and gate G-B still passes (the bounce is derived and the pulsation reproduces to $0.3\%$). What this article adds is that the closedness of the region behind the horizon is not independently established, and that the cosmology's $K = +1$ should be recorded as sharing that status rather than inheriting a derivation it does not have.

Part 5 — The verdict

Forced? No. No theorem and no datum requires a second populated spacetime. The Cartan–Lovelock uniqueness (S7) constrains the left-hand side of the field equations; it says nothing about the number of manifolds. The horizon is a null surface within one spacetime. Nothing in general relativity, and nothing in the data, forces the second sheet.

Licensed? Yes. The horizon argument gives the corpus a natural language in which its complexification is not arbitrary: the exchange of which direction carries the $i$ is exactly the sector structure, and S2 uses that premise as the definition of the dictionary. The licence is real and it is what makes the programme worth rebuilding. It is also, per S3, a licence for the local reading — a complex structure that varies — while the corpus's own $i$ is stipulated global.

Posited? Yes, and the corpus says so. "La Relativité complexe, qui suppose la complexité de l'espace physique comme un de ses axiomes de base" (Esp 039 §1.3). An axiom of the theory and a representation, by the corpus's own description. The register's inventory tags §1.2 [MIX] with the conclusion [SPEC]; this article's verdict is that the [SPEC] is a posited [SPEC], and that the corpus is more honest about it than its critics have been.

One consequence for gate G-A, stated so it is not confused. G-A is about the dictionary — whether a map exists from the corpus's structures to the framework's without contradiction — and S2 passed it in a refined form. G-A is unaffected by this article: the dictionary maps what the corpus wrote, whether or not the corpus was entitled to write it. What S4 decides is not a gate but the status of a premise, and the honest record is: the second sheet is a legitimate working hypothesis with a supported natural language and no support from data, and it should be labelled as such wherever it is used — which is what Part S does, and what the framework's own informational-sector article already says of $\mathbb{M}_+$'s physical realisation (S2, Part 7).

Summary

The register asked whether a second populated sheet is forced, licensed or posited, and the answer is licensed and posited, not forced. The corpus's argument is that crossing a Schwarzschild horizon swaps the types of $r$ and $t$ and multiplies the basis vectors by $i$, so a complex spacetime is necessary for a complete representation; the corpus itself calls this "un de ses axiomes de base" and the resulting description a "représentation non locale" (Esp 039 §1.2–1.3).

The argument's premise is established physics and its conclusion is not. The sign change of $g_{tt}$ and $g_{rr}$ is real; the horizon is a null surface across which the curvature invariants are finite; the invariant content of the sign flip is that no static observers exist inside; and the maximal analytic extension continues the vacuum solution through the surface as one manifold, whose second exterior region is the corpus's "two types of space" without the second universe. Two corrections follow: crossing changes the type of two of the four coordinates, not all four, so "every basis vector is multiplied by $i$" is stronger than the mathematics; and the corpus's own computation $\tilde S = \int_{r=2I}|g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}|\,dtd\phi = 0$ is correct but establishes only that the horizon is null, which is standard.

The finding is that the second sheet is purchased by a rejection. At Esp 115 the corpus explicitly declines the coordinate-change readings of the horizon — Novikov, Eddington–Finkelstein, Kruskal–Szekeres — on the ground that they claim to describe region I, where $T_{\alpha\beta} \ne 0$. That ground is category-mismatched: the corpus's region I is a collapsing star's interior, while the extensions it rejects are statements about the vacuum manifold, where its own criterion of validity holds. Two different objects share the name "interior", and the objection to one has been carried by the name to the other. So the horizon argument yields, at most, a natural language for complexification; what yields the populated second sheet is the horizon argument plus the rejection of the extension.

And the second sheet is the closed universe. The interior observer's spatial volume is $2\pi^{2}R^{3}$, the 3-volume of a closed $K = +1$ three-sphere — the same $2\pi^{2}c^{2}R^{3}$ that carries the cosmological action (5-41) whose output (5-46) is the corpus's best-verified identity. The hole behind the horizon, the micro-universe, and the cosmological model are one construction at three scales, and this article's verdict propagates to S50, S51 and S52: the closedness of the spatial section should be recorded as a hypothesis of the same family, not as a derived result.

The verdict, for the register. The second sheet is posited, licensed by the horizon argument as read through a deliberate refusal of the maximal extension, and not forced by anything; gate G-A is untouched, because the dictionary maps what the corpus wrote whether or not it was entitled to write it; and the corpus is clearer about the status of its own axiom than the register's dilemma assumed.

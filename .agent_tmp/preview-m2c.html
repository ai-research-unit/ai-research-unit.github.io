The Anti-Hermitian Derivations of M2(C)M_2(\mathbb{C})M2​(C) as su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2)

Introduction

Two conditions pick out the generators: anti-Hermitian and traceless. At order two they hold exactly for a three-dimensional space, which is su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2). The three generators are not chosen from a list: they are the images of the three vector units of the biquaternion algebra under the isomorphism M2\mathsf{M}_2M2​, halved so that the structure constants come out as ϵabc\epsilon_{abc}ϵabc​.

The article reads the three generators as the corpus reads them. The isomorphism M2\mathsf{M}_2M2​ sends the vector units to the three matrices M2(e⃗k)\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_k)M2​(ek​), and the half of those matrices are the generators; the normalization is the one that Automorphisms and Derivations of Algebras fixes at the element level, Dk=12ade⃗kD_k=\tfrac12\mathrm{ad}_{\vec e_k}Dk​=21​adek​​. The bracket of the halved generators is [G1,G2]=G3[G_1,G_2]=G_3[G1​,G2​]=G3​, with no factor and no sign.

The article keeps three frames apart, and the table of §Three Representations, Kept Apart says what each one holds: the 2×22\times22×2 Pauli generators Gk=−i2σkG_k=-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_kGk​=−2i​σk​; the same block twice in the 4×44\times44×4 Peirce frame, I2⊗Gk\mathrm I_2\otimes G_kI2​⊗Gk​, with the transposed right family GkT⊗I2G_k^{\mathsf T}\otimes\mathrm I_2GkT​⊗I2​; and the 4×44\times44×4 axis-free matrices ℓk\ell_kℓk​ and rkr_krk​ of left and right multiplication, of which the derivation is the half of the difference. Then the three derivations as four-by-four arrays in the basis of the elements, the conjugation table of the rotor on the three generators, the three rotors written out, the dimension ladder, the worked cases and the orbits. Every numeric statement was checked on the arrays.

The article assumes the exponential of the matrix algebra from The Exponential and the Lie Group Structure of GL2(C)\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})GL2​(C), the Lie correspondence of The Lie Correspondence and the Adjoint Representation, and the images of the units from the Pauli representation of M2(C)M_2(\mathbb{C})M2​(C).

The Two Conditions on the Matrix

Anti-Hermitian and Traceless

Proposition (the generators). The matrices A∈M2(C)A\in M_2(\mathbb{C})A∈M2​(C) with A∗=−AA^{*}=-AA∗=−A and tr⁡A=0\operatorname{tr}A=0trA=0 form a real vector space of dimension three, closed under the bracket, isomorphic to su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2).

Proof. A general element is A=(abcd)A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}A=(ac​bd​) with a,da,da,d purely imaginary and c=−bˉc=-\bar bc=−bˉ, so the real parameters are Im⁡a\operatorname{Im}aIma, Im⁡d\operatorname{Im}dImd, Re⁡b\operatorname{Re}bReb, Im⁡b\operatorname{Im}bImb: four. The trace condition is Im⁡a+Im⁡d=0\operatorname{Im}a+\operatorname{Im}d=0Ima+Imd=0, one real equation, leaving three. The bracket of two anti-Hermitian matrices is anti-Hermitian, and the trace of a bracket vanishes.

Remark (the three as images of the units). The isomorphism M2\mathsf{M}_2M2​ sends

M2(e⃗x)=(0−i−i0),M2(e⃗y)=(0−110),M2(e⃗z)=(−i00i), \mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\-i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_z)=\begin{pmatrix}-i&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix}, M2​(ex​)=(0−i​−i0​),M2​(ey​)=(01​−10​),M2​(ez​)=(−i0​0i​),

and each of the three is anti-Hermitian and traceless. They are the three generators up to the factor 12\tfrac1221​ of the next section.

Remark (the images are written as matrices). The three objects of this article are images of elements, and they are written with their entries. The arrays of the Pauli frame are printed once, in §Three Representations, Kept Apart, because a reader who is told that the images are −iσk-i\sigma_k−iσk​ must be able to see what σk\sigma_kσk​ is; everywhere else the body writes the entries.

Three Representations, Kept Apart

Why a section on this. A matrix of order two and a matrix of order four are different objects, and an article that prints both without saying which frame each belongs to invites one mistake: to read a four-by-four array as a generator of su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2). It is not one. The three directions e⃗x,e⃗y,e⃗z\vec e_x,\vec e_y,\vec e_zex​,ey​,ez​ have matrices in three frames, of two sizes, and the table says which is which before any display.

A four-by-four matrix cannot be an element of su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2) in its defining representation, and none of the arrays below is claimed to be one.

The half is applied once. The half does not change the space, only the structure constant, from 222 to 111. Where a display below prints a matrix whole, the half is named in the sentence beside it, and the reason to print it whole is that it is the matrix the corpus prints elsewhere.

The Orbits

Proposition (the orbit of a Hermitian matrix). The conjugation by SU(2)\mathrm{SU}(2)SU(2) sends a traceless Hermitian matrix of norm rrr to any other of the same norm, so the orbit is a two-sphere, of real dimension two, and the stabiliser is a circle.

Proof. The traceless Hermitian matrices of norm rrr are the vectors of length rrr in a three-dimensional real space, and the conjugation acts on them as the full rotation group, transitively. The stabiliser of a non-zero vector is the circle of rotors about it.

Corollary (the general orbit). A matrix with distinct eigenvalues has an orbit of real dimension two in the traceless Hermitian part and of real dimension two in the traceless anti-Hermitian part, and its full orbit in M2M_2M2​ is the product of the orbit of its scalar part, a point, with those two.

Proof. The scalar part is fixed, and each of the two traceless parts is a three-dimensional space on which the group acts by rotations; the orbit of a non-zero vector in either is the sphere.

Remark (the orbit of a generator). The generators form the sphere of radius 12\tfrac1221​ in the three-dimensional space of traceless anti-Hermitian matrices, and the conjugation acts on that sphere by rotations. So the three generators are conjugate, the orbit is the sphere, and the stabiliser of each is a circle; the three rows of the worked table are three points on one orbit, and what distinguishes them is the real structure and not the spectrum.

Summary

The anti-Hermitian traceless matrices of order two form su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2), of real dimension three, and their generators are the halves of the images of the three vector units, Gk=12M2(e⃗k)G_k=\tfrac12\mathsf{M}_2(\vec e_k)Gk​=21​M2​(ek​). With that normalization the bracket is [G1,G2]=G3[G_1,G_2]=G_3[G1​,G2​]=G3​, and each array is a quarter-turn of one coordinate plane in the basis (I2,T1,T2,T3)(\mathrm{I}_2,T_1,T_2,T_3)(I2​,T1​,T2​,T3​) of the elements.

Each generator gives the derivation [A,⋅][A,\cdot][A,⋅], anti-Hermitian for the Hilbert–Schmidt form, of array with the scalar row and column zero and the spectrum {0,0,i,−i}\{0,0,i,-i\}{0,0,i,−i}. The three are written in three frames and the frames are kept apart: as −i2σk-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_k−2i​σk​ in the 2×22\times22×2 Pauli representation, as I2⊗Gk\mathrm I_2\otimes G_kI2​⊗Gk​ and GkT⊗I2G_k^{\mathsf T}\otimes\mathrm I_2GkT​⊗I2​ in the 4×44\times44×4 Peirce frame, and as the six axis-free matrices ℓk,rk\ell_k,r_kℓk​,rk​ of left and right multiplication, of which 12(ℓk−rk)\tfrac12(\ell_k-r_k)21​(ℓk​−rk​) is the array of the derivation.

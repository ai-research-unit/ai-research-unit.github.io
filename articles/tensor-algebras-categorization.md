
# __Tensor Algebras categorization__

## Introduction

This article classifies the algebras of the tensor algebra family by their structural properties: the tensor algebra $T(V)$ of a module $V$ over a commutative ring $R$, the algebras obtained from it by imposing relations, and the constructions that combine or refine them — tensor products, free algebras, quiver and path algebras, and graded and filtered algebras.

The organizing principle is that $T(V)$ is the **free associative unital algebra** on $V$: it solves a universal mapping problem, and every associative algebra generated by $V$ is a quotient of $T(V)$ by a two-sided ideal of relations. The classification is therefore a classification of the relations, and of the structure the quotient inherits. Two dichotomies organize it.

**Homogeneous versus inhomogeneous relations.** Relations generated by homogeneous elements yield graded algebras: the symmetric and exterior algebras, and path algebras of quivers. Relations that mix degrees yield only filtered quotients, whose associated graded algebras are graded quotients: the Clifford, universal enveloping, and Weyl algebras.

**Free, commutative, graded-commutative.** The tensor algebra is free; the symmetric algebra is the free commutative algebra; the exterior algebra is the free graded-commutative algebra. These are the universal objects of the family.

Conventions. Throughout, $R$ is a commutative ring with identity and $V$ is an $R$-module, free of finite rank $n$ when a rank is mentioned. We use **rank** over a general commutative ring and **dimension** over a field. The general theory is in *Algebras: A General Introduction*, the classification by scalar ring in *Algebras: Categorization*, and the classes themselves in the siblings *Tensor Algebras*, *Exterior Algebras*, *Symmetric Algebras*, and *Clifford Algebras*.

# Part I: The Tensor Algebra and Its Quotients

## 1. The Tensor Algebra

The **tensor algebra** is $T(V) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} V^{\otimes n}$, where $V^{\otimes 0} = R$, $V^{\otimes 1} = V$, and $V^{\otimes n}$ is the $n$-fold tensor product for $n \geq 2$. The product is concatenation, $x \cdot y = x \otimes y$ for $x \in V^{\otimes m}$ and $y \in V^{\otimes n}$, extended bilinearly. Thus $T(V)$ is an associative unital $R$-algebra, with unit $1 \in V^{\otimes 0}$, generated by $V$. It is graded by tensor degree, $T(V) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} T^n(V)$ with $T^n(V) = V^{\otimes n}$ and $T^m(V) T^n(V) \subseteq T^{m+n}(V)$; a graded algebra with $A_0 = R$ is called **connected**.

For $V = R$ of rank $1$, $T(V) \cong R[t]$. For $V = R^n$ with basis $x_1, \ldots, x_n$, $T(V)$ is the **free associative algebra** $R\langle x_1, \ldots, x_n \rangle$: the words $x_{i_1} \cdots x_{i_k}$ form a basis, so $T^k(V)$ has rank $n^k$ and $T(V)$ has infinite rank for $n \geq 1$. The tensor algebra imposes no relations beyond bilinearity and associativity.

## 2. The Universal Property

**Universal property.** For every associative unital $R$-algebra $A$ and every $R$-linear map $f : V \to A$, there is a unique unital algebra homomorphism $F : T(V) \to A$ restricting to $f$ on $V$.

Indeed, $F$ must send $v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n$ to $f(v_1) \cdots f(v_n)$, which determines it. Two consequences govern the classification. First, $V \mapsto T(V)$ is left adjoint to the forgetful functor to $R$-modules, so $T(V)$ is the free associative unital algebra on $V$. Second, if the image of $f$ generates $A$, then $F$ is surjective and $A \cong T(V)/\ker F$; the algebras generated by $V$ are exactly the quotients of $T(V)$.

## 3. Quotients by Relations: the Classification Principle

Let $A = T(V)/I$ for a two-sided ideal $I$. If $I$ is generated by homogeneous elements, then $I = \bigoplus_n (I \cap T^n(V))$ and the grading descends: $A = \bigoplus_n A_n$ with $A_n = T^n(V)/(I \cap T^n(V))$, $A_m A_n \subseteq A_{m+n}$, and $A_0 = R$. Such an $A$ is a connected graded algebra generated by its degree-one part $A_1$.

If $I$ is not homogeneous, $A$ is not graded in general, but it is **filtered** by $F^n A = $ the image of $\bigoplus_{k \leq n} T^k(V)$:

$$
R = F^0 A \subseteq F^1 A \subseteq \cdots, \qquad F^m A \cdot F^n A \subseteq F^{m+n} A, \qquad \bigcup_n F^n A = A.
$$

The **associated graded algebra** is $\operatorname{gr} A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} F^n A / F^{n-1} A$, a graded algebra generated by its degree-one part; if $I \cap V = 0$, then $\operatorname{gr}^1 A \cong V$ and there is a canonical surjection of graded algebras $T(V) \to \operatorname{gr} A$.

So the family splits into **graded algebras**, coming from homogeneous relations — the symmetric, exterior, and quadratic algebras, and path algebras together with their homogeneous quotients — and **filtered algebras**, coming from inhomogeneous relations — the Clifford, enveloping, and Weyl algebras, whose associated graded algebras are among the graded algebras.

# Part II: The Graded Quotients

## 4. The Symmetric Algebra

The **symmetric algebra** is $\operatorname{Sym}(V) = T(V)/I_S$, where $I_S$ is generated by all $v \otimes w - w \otimes v$; its defining relation is

$$
v w = w v, \qquad v, w \in V,
$$

so $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is commutative. Universal property: every $R$-linear $f : V \to A$ into a commutative unital algebra extends uniquely, so $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is the **free commutative $R$-algebra on $V$**.

For $V$ free of rank $n$, $\operatorname{Sym}(V) \cong R[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, with basis the monomials $x_1^{a_1} \cdots x_n^{a_n}$; the degree-$k$ part has rank $\binom{n+k-1}{k}$, so $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ has infinite rank for $n \geq 1$. It is connected graded and commutative, a domain when $R$ is a domain, and Noetherian when $R$ is. It is the universal commutative quotient of $T(V)$, that is, $\operatorname{Sym}(V) \cong T(V)/[T(V), T(V)]$, and it is the associated graded algebra of $U(\mathfrak{g})$ and of $A_n(R)$ below.

## 5. The Exterior Algebra

The **exterior algebra** is $\Lambda(V) = T(V)/I_\Lambda$, where $I_\Lambda$ is generated by all $v \otimes v$; its defining relation is

$$
v \wedge v = 0, \qquad v \in V,
$$

equivalently $v \wedge w = -w \wedge v$. Universal property: every $R$-linear $f : V \to A$ with $f(v)^2 = 0$ extends uniquely, so $\Lambda(V)$ is the universal quotient in which every generator squares to zero.

The exterior algebra is **graded-commutative**, also called supercommutative: for homogeneous $\alpha, \beta$,

$$
\alpha \wedge \beta = (-1)^{|\alpha| |\beta|} \beta \wedge \alpha.
$$

It is not commutative when $V$ has rank at least $2$. For $V$ free of rank $n$ with basis $e_1, \ldots, e_n$, the products $e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_k}$ with $i_1 < \cdots < i_k$, together with $1$, form a basis; hence $\Lambda^k(V)$ has rank $\binom{n}{k}$ and $\operatorname{rank} \Lambda(V) = 2^n$. Unlike $T(V)$ and $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$, it is finite-dimensional, and every element of positive degree is nilpotent.

$\Lambda(V)$ is the free graded-commutative algebra on $V$ placed in odd degree; it is the special case $\Lambda(V) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(V, 0)$ of the Clifford algebra; and it is the associated graded algebra of $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$. Over a field of characteristic not $2$, the degree-two part splits as $T^2(V) = S^2(V) \oplus \Lambda^2(V)$, exhibiting $\operatorname{Sym}$ and $\Lambda$ as the two extreme quotients.

## 6. Algebras Defined by Quadratic Relations

A **quadratic algebra** is $A = T(V)/(R)$ with $R \subseteq V \otimes V$, so its relations are homogeneous of degree two. It is connected graded with $A_1 = V$ and $A_2 = (V \otimes V)/R$; over a field, $\dim A_2 = n^2 - \dim R$. The two canonical members are

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V) : \quad R = \operatorname{span}\{v \otimes w - w \otimes v\}, \qquad \Lambda(V) : \quad R = \operatorname{span}\{v \otimes v\}.
$$

Quadratic algebras are closed under quotients by quadratic ideals. The Clifford algebra with $Q \neq 0$ is not one, since $v^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1$ is inhomogeneous — which is exactly why it is filtered rather than graded.

# Part III: The Filtered Quotients

## 7. The Clifford Algebra

Let $Q : V \to R$ be a quadratic form with polar bilinear form $B$, so $Q(v) = B(v, v)$. The **Clifford algebra** is $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q) = T(V)/I_Q$, where $I_Q$ is generated by all $v \otimes v - Q(v) \cdot 1$; the defining relation is

$$
v^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1, \qquad v \in V,
$$

whose linearization is the **Clifford relation** $u v + v u = 2 B(u, v) \cdot 1$. Universal property: every $R$-linear $f : V \to A$ with $f(v)^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1_A$ extends uniquely to $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q) \to A$.

The relation is inhomogeneous when $Q \neq 0$, so $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ is filtered, not graded. It always carries a **$\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading** by parity; the full $\mathbb{Z}$-grading survives only when $Q = 0$, where $\mathrm{Cl}(V, 0) \cong \Lambda(V)$.

If $V$ is free of rank $n$ with basis $e_1, \ldots, e_n$, then $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ is free of rank $2^n$ with basis the products $e_{i_1} \cdots e_{i_k}$, $i_1 < \cdots < i_k$. Hence

$$
\operatorname{gr} \mathrm{Cl}(V, Q) \cong \Lambda(V),
$$

the Clifford analogue of the PBW theorem: the Clifford algebra is a deformation of the exterior algebra, with the same basis monomials and the exterior product as the leading terms of its products. For $V = R^2$ with $Q(e_1) = Q(e_2) = 1$, $B(e_1, e_2) = 0$, we have $e_1^2 = e_2^2 = 1$, $e_1 e_2 = -e_2 e_1$, and over $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}(\mathbb{R}) \cong M_2(\mathbb{R})$. See *Clifford Algebras in Finite Dimensions*.

## 8. The Universal Enveloping Algebra

For a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over $R$, the **universal enveloping algebra** is $U(\mathfrak{g}) = T(\mathfrak{g})/I_{\mathfrak{g}}$, where $I_{\mathfrak{g}}$ is generated by all $x \otimes y - y \otimes x - [x, y]$; the defining relation is

$$
x y - y x = [x, y], \qquad x, y \in \mathfrak{g}.
$$

Universal property: regarding an associative unital $A$ as a Lie algebra with bracket the commutator, every Lie algebra homomorphism $\mathfrak{g} \to A$ extends uniquely to $U(\mathfrak{g}) \to A$; so $\mathfrak{g} \mapsto U(\mathfrak{g})$ is left adjoint to the commutator functor. The relation is inhomogeneous, so $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is filtered, with $F^n U(\mathfrak{g})$ the image of $\bigoplus_{k \leq n} \mathfrak{g}^{\otimes k}$.

**Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt.** If $\mathfrak{g}$ is free with basis $x_1, \ldots, x_n$, the ordered monomials $x_1^{a_1} \cdots x_n^{a_n}$ form a basis of $U(\mathfrak{g})$. Hence

$$
\operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}),
$$

and $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is a domain when $\mathfrak{g}$ is free over a domain. If $\mathfrak{g}$ is abelian, the defining relations are those of the symmetric algebra, so $U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$.

## 9. The Weyl Algebra

For $V$ free of rank $n$ with basis $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ and dual basis $y_1, \ldots, y_n$, the **Weyl algebra** $A_n(R)$ is the quotient of $R\langle x_1, \ldots, x_n, y_1, \ldots, y_n \rangle$ by the relations

$$
x_i x_j - x_j x_i = 0, \qquad y_i y_j - y_j y_i = 0, \qquad x_i y_j - y_j x_i = \delta_{ij} \cdot 1.
$$

The last is the **canonical commutation relation**; being inhomogeneous, $A_n(R)$ is filtered, not graded. The monomials $x_1^{a_1} \cdots x_n^{a_n} y_1^{b_1} \cdots y_n^{b_n}$ form a basis (PBW for the Weyl algebra), so $A_n(R)$ has infinite rank and

$$
\operatorname{gr} A_n(R) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*) \cong R[x_1, \ldots, x_n, y_1, \ldots, y_n].
$$

For the Heisenberg Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}_n$ with basis $x_i, y_i, z$, brackets $[x_i, y_j] = \delta_{ij} z$, and $z$ central, we have $A_n(R) \cong U(\mathfrak{h}_n)/(z - 1)$, so the Weyl algebra is a quotient of an enveloping algebra. Over a field $k$, taking $x_i$ to be multiplication and $y_i$ to be $\partial / \partial x_i$ on $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ realizes $A_n(k)$ as the algebra of polynomial differential operators. For $k$ of characteristic zero, $A_n(k)$ is a simple Noetherian domain.

## 10. The Common Pattern of the Filtered Classes

The three filtered classes obey one pattern:

$$
\operatorname{gr} \mathrm{Cl}(V, Q) \cong \Lambda(V), \qquad \operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}), \qquad \operatorname{gr} A_n(R) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*).
$$

Each is defined by relations of degree at most two, at least one of them inhomogeneous; each has a PBW-type basis theorem by ordered monomials; and each basis theorem identifies the associated graded algebra with a graded quotient of the tensor algebra — the exterior algebra for the quadratic relation $v^2 = Q(v)$, the symmetric algebra for the linear commutator relations. So each filtered algebra is a flat deformation of its associated graded; in each nontrivial case the two are not isomorphic, since a noncommutative enveloping algebra has a commutative associated graded.

# Part IV: Free Algebras, Path Algebras, and Tensor Products

## 11. Free Algebras

For a set $X$, let $R^{(X)}$ be the free $R$-module on $X$. The **free associative algebra** is $R\langle X \rangle = T(R^{(X)})$, characterized by the universal property that every map $X \to A$ into an associative unital $R$-algebra extends uniquely. The universal objects of the family are the free objects of the corresponding categories:

- free associative: $R\langle X \rangle = T(R^{(X)})$;
- free commutative: $\operatorname{Sym}(R^{(X)}) = R[X]$;
- free graded-commutative: $\Lambda(R^{(X)})$ when all generators are odd; for a mixture of even and odd generators it is the tensor product of the polynomial algebra on the even generators with the exterior algebra on the odd generators.

## 12. Quiver and Path Algebras

A **quiver** $Q$ consists of a set of vertices $Q_0$, a set of arrows $Q_1$, and source and target maps $s, t : Q_1 \to Q_0$. A **path** of length $m \geq 1$ is a sequence $a_1 \cdots a_m$ with $t(a_i) = s(a_{i+1})$; a path of length $0$ is a vertex $e_i$. Assume $Q$ finite, that is, both $Q_0$ and $Q_1$ finite. The **path algebra** $RQ$ has basis the paths of $Q$, with product given by concatenation when $t(p) = s(q)$ and $0$ otherwise, and unit $1 = \sum_{i \in Q_0} e_i$.

It is graded by path length, $RQ = \bigoplus_{m \geq 0} (RQ)_m$, with $(RQ)_0 = \bigoplus_{i \in Q_0} R e_i \cong R^{|Q_0|}$. It is a tensor algebra: let $A = R^{|Q_0|}$ and let $M = (RQ)_1$ be the $A$-module spanned by the arrows, with $e_i \cdot a \cdot e_j = a$ when $s(a) = i$ and $t(a) = j$, and $0$ otherwise. Then

$$
RQ \cong T_A(M) = A \oplus M \oplus (M \otimes_A M) \oplus \cdots,
$$

where $M^{\otimes_A m}$ is spanned by the paths of length $m$. So path algebras are exactly the tensor algebras of modules over $R^{|Q_0|}$.

Examples: one vertex with $d$ loops gives the free algebra $R\langle x_1, \ldots, x_d \rangle$; one vertex with no loops gives $R$; the quiver $\bullet \to \bullet$ gives the upper triangular $2 \times 2$ matrices, with $e_1 \mapsto E_{11}$, $e_2 \mapsto E_{22}$, $a \mapsto E_{12}$. Over a field $k$, the path algebra $kQ$ is finite-dimensional if and only if $Q$ has no oriented cycles, and every path algebra is hereditary, of global dimension at most $1$.

**Quivers with relations.** A relation is a linear combination of paths of length at least two, and a quiver with relations is $RQ/I$ for the ideal $I$ they generate. When $I$ is homogeneous, for instance generated by length-two relations, the quotient is graded and is a quotient of $T_A(M)$. If $Q$ has no oriented cycles and $I$ is **admissible** — contained in $\bigoplus_{m \geq 2} (RQ)_m$ and generated by elements of bounded length — then $RQ/I$ is finite-dimensional. With one vertex and loops $x, y$, the relation $xy - yx$ gives $R[x, y] = \operatorname{Sym}(R^2)$, while the relations $x^2$, $y^2$, and $xy + yx$ give $\Lambda(R^2)$.

## 13. Tensor Products of Algebras

For $R$-algebras $A$ and $B$, the **tensor product** $A \otimes_R B$ has product $(a_1 \otimes b_1)(a_2 \otimes b_2) = a_1 a_2 \otimes b_1 b_2$, extended bilinearly. It is free of rank $mn$ when $A$ and $B$ are free of ranks $m$ and $n$; it is associative and unital, with unit $1 \otimes 1$, whenever both factors are, and commutative if and only if both are. In the category of commutative $R$-algebras it is the coproduct; in the category of all associative unital algebras, the coproduct is instead the **free product** $A * B$.

For graded $A$ and $B$, the **graded tensor product** $A \hat{\otimes} B$ uses the Koszul-sign product $(a_1 \otimes b_1)(a_2 \otimes b_2) = (-1)^{|b_1| |a_2|} (a_1 a_2) \otimes (b_1 b_2)$ on homogeneous elements; it is graded, and the graded tensor product of graded-commutative algebras is graded-commutative. The classes of the family satisfy

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V_1 \oplus V_2) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V_1) \otimes \operatorname{Sym}(V_2),
$$

$$
\Lambda(V_1 \oplus V_2) \cong \Lambda(V_1) \hat{\otimes} \Lambda(V_2), \qquad \mathrm{Cl}(V_1 \oplus V_2, Q \oplus Q') \cong \mathrm{Cl}(V_1, Q) \hat{\otimes} \mathrm{Cl}(V_2, Q'),
$$

$$
U(\mathfrak{g} \oplus \mathfrak{h}) \cong U(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes U(\mathfrak{h}), \qquad A_n(R) \otimes_R A_m(R) \cong A_{n+m}(R).
$$

The tensor algebra is different: it is the **free product**, not the tensor product, of its components,

$$
T(V_1 \oplus V_2) \cong T(V_1) * T(V_2),
$$

because $T(V_1) \otimes T(V_2)$ forces the two factors to commute elementwise, whereas the free product imposes no such relation. If $A = A'/I$ and $B = B'/J$, then $A \otimes_R B \cong (A' \otimes_R B')/(I \otimes_R B' + A' \otimes_R J)$, so every tensor product of family members is a quotient of a tensor algebra. For example, $\mathbb{C} \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C}$, with orthogonal idempotents $\frac{1}{2}(1 \otimes 1 \pm i \otimes i)$.

# Part V: Graded and Filtered Algebras, and the Hierarchy

## 14. Graded Algebras

A **graded algebra** is $A = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} A_n$ with $A_m A_n \subseteq A_{m+n}$; it is **connected** if $A_n = 0$ for $n < 0$ and $A_0 = R$. A quotient by a homogeneous ideal is graded, and a graded algebra generated by its degree-one part is a quotient of the tensor algebra of that part. $T(V)$, $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$, and $\Lambda(V)$ are connected graded; path algebras and their homogeneous quotients are graded by path length; the Clifford, enveloping, and Weyl algebras are not $\mathbb{Z}$-graded when their defining relation is inhomogeneous, though the Clifford algebra keeps its $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading.

A graded algebra is **graded-commutative** if $\alpha \beta = (-1)^{|\alpha| |\beta|} \beta \alpha$ for homogeneous $\alpha, \beta$. The exterior algebra is graded-commutative and noncommutative; the symmetric algebra is commutative and hence graded-commutative; the tensor algebra is neither for $n \geq 2$.

## 15. Filtered Algebras and the Associated Graded

A **filtered algebra** is an algebra $A$ with an increasing chain $0 \subseteq F^0 A \subseteq F^1 A \subseteq \cdots$, $\bigcup_n F^n A = A$, and $F^m A \cdot F^n A \subseteq F^{m+n} A$. Its **associated graded algebra** is $\operatorname{gr} A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} F^n A / F^{n-1} A$, graded and generated by its degree-one part. Every graded algebra with $A_n = 0$ for $n < 0$ is filtered by $F^n A = \bigoplus_{k \leq n} A_k$, and then $\operatorname{gr} A \cong A$; so graded algebras are a special case of filtered ones. For the filtered classes,

$$
\operatorname{gr} \mathrm{Cl}(V, Q) \cong \Lambda(V), \qquad \operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}), \qquad \operatorname{gr} A_n(R) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*).
$$

The associated graded algebra does not determine the algebra: for nonabelian $\mathfrak{g}$, the algebras $U(\mathfrak{g})$ and $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ have the same associated graded but are not isomorphic.

## 16. The Hierarchy of the Family

Every class is a quotient of a tensor algebra, obtained by imposing relations of a definite shape: $T(V) \to \operatorname{Sym}(V)$ by $vw - wv$; $T(V) \to \Lambda(V)$ by $v^2$; $T(V) \to \mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ by $v^2 - Q(v)$; $T(\mathfrak{g}) \to U(\mathfrak{g})$ by $xy - yx - [x, y]$; $T(V \oplus V^*) \to A_n(R)$ by the canonical commutation relation; and $T_A(M) \to RQ/I$ for quivers with relations.

Among the graded quotients, $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ (the universal commutative quotient) and $\Lambda(V)$ (the universal square-zero quotient) are the two extremes, while quivers with relations generalize the free algebra and contain both. Among the filtered quotients, the associated graded algebra places each class over a graded class. The tensor product is not a quotient construction but a combination: a tensor product of quotients is a quotient of the tensor product of tensor algebras.

```
                        T(V)   (free associative, graded)
                          |
       homogeneous relations|inhomogeneous relations
       --------------------|-------------------
       |                                       |
  graded quotients                        filtered quotients
  |-- Sym(V)  (commutative)               |-- Cl(V,Q),  gr = Exterior(V)
  |-- Exterior(V) (graded-commutative)    |-- U(g),     gr = Sym(g)
  |-- RQ, RQ/I (paths, relations)         |-- A_n(R),   gr = Sym(V (x) V*)
```

# Summary

The family is organized by one idea: $T(V)$ is the free associative algebra on $V$, and every other class is a quotient of a tensor algebra by relations of a definite shape.

**Graded quotients.** Homogeneous relations give graded quotients. $\operatorname{Sym}(V) = T(V)/(vw - wv)$ is the free commutative algebra and the universal commutative quotient. $\Lambda(V) = T(V)/(v \wedge v)$ is the free graded-commutative algebra, of rank $2^n$ for $V$ free of rank $n$. Quadratic algebras are the general graded quotients by degree-two relations.

**Filtered quotients.** Inhomogeneous relations give filtered quotients with graded associated graded. $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q) = T(V)/(v^2 - Q(v))$ has $\operatorname{gr} \mathrm{Cl}(V, Q) \cong \Lambda(V)$ and equals $\Lambda(V)$ when $Q = 0$. $U(\mathfrak{g}) = T(\mathfrak{g})/(xy - yx - [x, y])$ has $\operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ by PBW, and equals $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ when $\mathfrak{g}$ is abelian. $A_n(R)$ has $\operatorname{gr} A_n(R) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*)$ and, over a field of characteristic zero, is a simple Noetherian domain.

**Free algebras, path algebras, tensor products.** $R\langle X \rangle$ is the tensor algebra of a free module. Path algebras are tensor algebras over $R^{|Q_0|}$, and their quotients by relations are quotients of tensor algebras containing $\operatorname{Sym}$ and $\Lambda$ as examples. The tensor product has rank $mn$; it is the coproduct of commutative algebras, while the free product is the coproduct of associative algebras, and the tensor algebra is the free product, not the tensor product, of its components.

**Graded and filtered.** Graded algebras are filtered, with $\operatorname{gr} A \cong A$. The associated graded records the leading terms of a filtered algebra without determining it.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $T(V)$ | none | — | connected graded, free | itself | $\infty$ |

| $\Lambda(V)$ | $v \wedge v = 0$ | yes | connected graded, graded-commutative | itself | $2^n$ |

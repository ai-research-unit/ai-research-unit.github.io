
# Minkowski Space as a Subspace

## Introduction

This article studies the ways in which Minkowski space can be realized as a subspace of a larger real algebra. It is the general companion to the detailed article on the embedding of Minkowski space in the biquaternion and split quaternion algebras. The goal is to place those two embeddings in a broader context, to explain what "Minkowski space as a subspace" means precisely, and to describe the general construction from which the specific embeddings arise.

The treatment is purely mathematical. Minkowski space is treated as a four-dimensional real vector space equipped with an indefinite quadratic form of signature $(1,3)$ or $(3,1)$. No physics is invoked. No examples are given. The quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$, the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$, and the split quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$ are assumed from the preceding articles.

Every claim is either proved or stated as a definition.

## Two Different Questions

The phrase "Minkowski space as a subspace" can mean two different things, and it is important to distinguish them.

**Question 1: Which algebras are generated by Minkowski space?** Given a four-dimensional real vector space $V$ with an indefinite quadratic form $Q$ of signature $(1,3)$ or $(3,1)$, the **Clifford algebra** $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ is the algebra generated by $V$ subject to the relation $v^2 = Q(v)$. This algebra has dimension $2^4 = 16$, and it is the universal algebra generated by Minkowski space. The question is: what are the possible Clifford algebras, and how do they relate to each other?

**Question 2: Which algebras contain a copy of Minkowski space as a subspace?** An algebra $A$ contains a copy of Minkowski space if there is a four-dimensional real subspace $V \subset A$ on which the restriction of some quadratic form has signature $(1,3)$ or $(3,1)$. The question is: what are the possible algebras, and how do the embeddings differ?

The two questions are related but distinct. The Clifford algebra of Minkowski space is generated by Minkowski space, and it contains Minkowski space as a subspace (the generating subspace). But there are also smaller algebras that contain a copy of Minkowski space without being generated by it. The biquaternion and split quaternion algebras are examples of the second kind.

This article treats both questions, and it explains how they are related.

## Minkowski Space

### Definition

**Minkowski space** is the four-dimensional real vector space $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$ equipped with the indefinite quadratic form of signature $(1,3)$:

$$
\eta(x, x) = x_0^2 - x_1^2 - x_2^2 - x_3^2.
$$

With the opposite sign convention, the form has signature $(3,1)$:

$$
\eta(x, x) = -x_0^2 + x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2.
$$

The two conventions are related by replacing $\eta$ with $-\eta$, and the choice is a matter of sign. In this article, we use the signature $(1,3)$ unless stated otherwise.

### The Light Cone

The **light cone** is the set of vectors $x$ with $\eta(x, x) = 0$:

$$
x_0^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2.
$$

It is a three-dimensional double cone in $\mathbb{R}^4$, and its nonzero elements are the **null vectors**.

### The Orthogonal Group

The **orthogonal group** of Minkowski space is the group of linear transformations preserving the quadratic form:

$$
O(1,3) = \{ \Lambda \in \mathrm{GL}(4, \mathbb{R}) : \eta(\Lambda x, \Lambda x) = \eta(x, x) \text{ for all } x \}.
$$

The **proper orthochronous Lorentz group** $SO^+(1,3)$ is the connected component of the identity.

## The Clifford Algebra of Minkowski Space

### Definition

Let $V$ be a four-dimensional real vector space with a non-degenerate quadratic form $Q$ of signature $(p, q)$ with $p + q = 4$. The **Clifford algebra** $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ is the quotient of the tensor algebra $T(V)$ by the two-sided ideal generated by the elements

$$
v \otimes v - Q(v) \cdot 1, \qquad v \in V.
$$

Equivalently, it is the associative unital algebra generated by $V$ subject to the relations

$$
v w + w v = 2 B(v, w) \cdot 1, \qquad v, w \in V,
$$

where $B$ is the bilinear form associated with $Q$.

### Dimension

The Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ has real dimension $2^{\dim V} = 2^4 = 16$. A basis is given by the products of the generators with strictly increasing indices:

$$
1, \quad e_i, \quad e_i e_j \ (i < j), \quad e_i e_j e_k \ (i < j < k), \quad e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4.
$$

There are

$$
1 + 4 + 6 + 4 + 1 = 16
$$

basis elements.

### The Five Signatures

For a four-dimensional real vector space, there are five possible signatures up to the sign convention:

$$
(4,0), \quad (3,1), \quad (2,2), \quad (1,3), \quad (0,4).
$$

The signatures $(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$ are equivalent up to replacing $Q$ by $-Q$; the signatures $(3,1)$ and $(1,3)$ are similarly equivalent. The signature $(2,2)$ is self-dual under this replacement. So there are three distinct signatures up to sign:

$$
(4,0) \sim (0,4), \qquad (3,1) \sim (1,3), \qquad (2,2).
$$

### The Classification

The classification of real Clifford algebras states that $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q} \cong \mathrm{Cl}_{p',q'}$ if and only if $p - q \equiv p' - q' \pmod 8$. For $p + q = 4$, the values of $p - q$ are:

$$
4, \quad 2, \quad 0, \quad -2, \quad -4,
$$

which are the residues $4, 2, 0, 6, 4$ modulo 8. So the five signatures give **two** distinct Clifford algebras up to isomorphism:

$$
\mathrm{Cl}_{4,0} \cong \mathrm{Cl}_{1,3} \cong \mathrm{Cl}_{0,4} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H}),
$$

$$
\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1} \cong \mathrm{Cl}_{2,2} \cong M_4(\mathbb{R}).
$$

So the Clifford algebras of Minkowski space are:

- For the signature $(3,1)$: $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1} \cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$.
- For the signature $(1,3)$: $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$.

These two algebras are **not** isomorphic to each other, even though they are both 16-dimensional. The first is a matrix algebra over $\mathbb{R}$, and the second is a matrix algebra over $\mathbb{H}$.

### The Even Subalgebra

The **even subalgebra** $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)^+$ consists of the products of an even number of generators. It has real dimension $2^{4-1} = 8$, and a basis is

$$
1, \quad e_i e_j \ (i < j), \quad e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4.
$$

There are

$$
1 + 6 + 1 = 8
$$

basis elements.

The even subalgebras of the five Clifford algebras are:

| Signature | Even subalgebra |
|---|---|
| $(4,0)$ | $\mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}$ |
| $(3,1)$ | $\mathbb{C} \otimes \mathbb{H} = \mathbb{B}$ |
| $(2,2)$ | $\mathbb{D} \otimes \mathbb{H} = \mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$ |
| $(1,3)$ | $\mathbb{C} \otimes \mathbb{H} = \mathbb{B}$ |
| $(0,4)$ | $\mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}$ |

Since $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}} \cong \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}$, the even subalgebras reduce to **two** distinct algebras up to isomorphism:

- The **biquaternion algebra** $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C} \otimes \mathbb{H}$: the even subalgebra of the Lorentzian Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$.
- The **split quaternion algebra** $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}} = \mathbb{D} \otimes \mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}$: the even subalgebra of the definite Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{4,0}$, the split Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,2}$, and the anti-definite Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,4}$.

So the even subalgebra of the Clifford algebra of Minkowski space is the **biquaternion algebra**, for both the signature $(3,1)$ and the signature $(1,3)$.

## The Two Questions, Answered

### Question 1: Which Algebras Are Generated by Minkowski Space?

The algebras generated by Minkowski space are the Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$. These are 16-dimensional real algebras, and they are:

$$
\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1} \cong M_4(\mathbb{R}), \qquad \mathrm{Cl}_{1,3} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H}).
$$

The two are not isomorphic. They are the universal algebras generated by Minkowski space with the two possible sign conventions for the quadratic form.

### Question 2: Which Algebras Contain a Copy of Minkowski Space as a Subspace?

The algebras that contain a copy of Minkowski space as a four-dimensional real subspace, with the restriction of a quadratic form of signature $(1,3)$ or $(3,1)$, are:

**Eight-dimensional:**

- The **biquaternion algebra** $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{C} \otimes \mathbb{H}$, with the copy of Minkowski space being the anti-Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_-$ or the Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$.
- The **split quaternion algebra** $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}} = \mathbb{D} \otimes \mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}$, with the copy of Minkowski space being the Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ or the anti-Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_-$.

**Sixteen-dimensional:**

- The **Clifford algebra** $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1} \cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$, with the copy of Minkowski space being the generating subspace.
- The **Clifford algebra** $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$, with the copy of Minkowski space being the generating subspace.

So the complete list of algebras that contain a copy of Minkowski space is:

$$
\mathbb{B}, \quad \mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}, \quad \mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}, \quad \mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}.
$$

The eight-dimensional algebras contain Minkowski space as a proper subspace, and the sixteen-dimensional algebras contain it as the generating subspace.

## The Relation Between the Two Questions

The relation between the two questions is given by the **even subalgebra**. If $A$ is an algebra generated by Minkowski space, then its even subalgebra $A^+$ is an eight-dimensional algebra that contains a copy of Minkowski space (the generating subspace, viewed as the odd part). The even subalgebra of $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}$ is $\mathbb{B}$, and the even subalgebra of $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$ is also $\mathbb{B}$.

So the biquaternion algebra is the even subalgebra of the Clifford algebra of Minkowski space, and it contains Minkowski space as a subspace. The split quaternion algebra is the even subalgebra of the Clifford algebras of the definite and split signatures, and it contains Minkowski space as a subspace (the Hermitian or anti-Hermitian subspace) even though it is not the even subalgebra of the Lorentzian Clifford algebra.

## The Role of the Signature

The signature of the quadratic form on the embedded Minkowski space determines the structure of the embedding.

**Lorentzian signature $(3,1)$ or $(1,3)$.** The Clifford algebra is $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1} \cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$ or $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$, and the even subalgebra is the biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$. The embedded Minkowski space is the generating subspace, and its intersection with the zero divisor set of $\mathbb{B}$ is the light cone.

**Definite signature $(4,0)$ or $(0,4)$.** The Clifford algebra is $\mathrm{Cl}_{4,0} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$ or $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,4} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H})$, and the even subalgebra is the split quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$. The embedded Minkowski space is not the generating subspace of the Clifford algebra; it is a subspace of the split quaternion algebra that happens to have Lorentzian signature.

**Split signature $(2,2)$.** The Clifford algebra is $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,2} \cong M_4(\mathbb{R})$, and the even subalgebra is the split quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$. The embedded Minkowski space is again a subspace of the split quaternion algebra with Lorentzian signature, not the generating subspace.

So the signature determines both the Clifford algebra and the way in which Minkowski space sits inside the algebra.

## The Zero Divisor Set and the Light Cone

In every algebra that contains a copy of Minkowski space, the **light cone** of the embedded Minkowski space is the intersection of the embedded subspace with the zero divisor set of the algebra.

**Biquaternion algebra.** The zero divisor set is a seven-dimensional complex cone. Its intersection with the anti-Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_-$ is a three-dimensional cone, which is the light cone of Minkowski space.

**Split quaternion algebra.** The zero divisor set is the union of the two four-dimensional linear subspaces $Z_+$ and $Z_-$. Its intersection with the Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_+$ or the anti-Hermitian subspace $\mathbb{M}_-$ is a three-dimensional cone, which is the light cone.

**Clifford algebras.** The zero divisor set is the set of elements with vanishing determinant (in the matrix representation). The intersection with the generating subspace (Minkowski space) is the light cone, because the generating vectors are precisely the elements with $v^2 = Q(v)$, and the null vectors are those with $Q(v) = 0$, which are the elements that square to zero.

So in every case, the light cone is the intersection of Minkowski space with the zero divisor set. This is a general feature of the embedding.

## The Lorentz Group

The Lorentz group acts on the embedded Minkowski space in every algebra.

**Biquaternion algebra.** The unit elements of $\mathbb{B}$ that preserve the anti-Hermitian subspace form a group isomorphic to the double cover of the Lorentz group. The action is by conjugation.

**Split quaternion algebra.** The unit elements of $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$ that preserve the Hermitian or anti-Hermitian subspace form a group isomorphic to the double cover of the Lorentz group. The action is by conjugation.

**Clifford algebras.** The unit elements of $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}$ or $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$ that preserve the generating subspace form a group isomorphic to the double cover of the Lorentz group, the **spin group** $\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)$. The action is by conjugation, and it is the standard realization of the Lorentz group as a group of automorphisms of the Clifford algebra.

The spin group is a subgroup of the even subalgebra, so it lies in the biquaternion algebra in the Lorentzian case.

## The General Principle

The general principle is the following.

Given a four-dimensional real vector space $V$ with a non-degenerate quadratic form $Q$ of signature $(p, q)$ with $p + q = 4$, the **Clifford algebra** $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ is the universal algebra generated by $V$. It has dimension 16, and it contains $V$ as a subspace on which the quadratic form restricts to $Q$.

The **even subalgebra** $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)^+$ has dimension 8, and it is one of the two eight-dimensional real algebras:

- The biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$, if the signature is Lorentzian.
- The split quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$, if the signature is definite or split.

The even subalgebra contains a copy of $V$ as a subspace (the odd part of the Clifford algebra, viewed as a subspace of the even subalgebra via multiplication by a fixed element). The restriction of the norm form of the even subalgebra to this subspace is the original quadratic form $Q$.

So every Clifford algebra of a four-dimensional quadratic space gives rise to an eight-dimensional even subalgebra that contains a copy of the quadratic space, and the only two possibilities for the even subalgebra are the biquaternion and split quaternion algebras.

## Summary

Minkowski space can be realized as a subspace of a larger real algebra in two different senses.

**As the generating subspace of a Clifford algebra.** The Clifford algebra of Minkowski space is a 16-dimensional real algebra, either $M_4(\mathbb{R})$ for the signature $(3,1)$ or $M_2(\mathbb{H})$ for the signature $(1,3)$. These are the universal algebras generated by Minkowski space.

**As a subspace of an eight-dimensional algebra.** The biquaternion algebra $\mathbb{B}$ and the split quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}$ both contain four-dimensional subspaces that are copies of Minkowski space. In the biquaternion algebra, the subspaces are the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces. In the split quaternion algebra, the subspaces are also the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian subspaces.

The two are related by the **even subalgebra**: the biquaternion algebra is the even subalgebra of the Lorentzian Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,1}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$, and the split quaternion algebra is the even subalgebra of the definite and split Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}_{4,0}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,2}$, and $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,4}$.

The complete list of real algebras that contain a copy of Minkowski space is:

$$
\mathbb{B}, \quad \mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{D}}, \quad \mathrm{Cl}_{3,1} \cong M_4(\mathbb{R}), \quad \mathrm{Cl}_{1,3} \cong M_2(\mathbb{H}).
$$

The eight-dimensional algebras are the biquaternion and split quaternion algebras; the 16-dimensional algebras are the Clifford algebras. The light cone of Minkowski space is the intersection of the embedded subspace with the zero divisor set of the algebra in every case.


# __Symmetric Algebras categorization__

## Introduction

This article classifies the algebras of the symmetric algebra family by their structural properties. The organizing object is the **symmetric algebra** $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ of a module $V$ over a commutative ring $R$: the free commutative algebra on $V$, the universal commutative quotient of the tensor algebra, and the algebra that appears as the associated graded algebra of the universal enveloping algebra and of the Weyl algebra.

The classification follows the same principle as the sibling *Tensor Algebras: Categorization*: the tensor algebra $T(V)$ is free, every algebra generated by $V$ is a quotient of $T(V)$ by relations, and the family is classified by the shape of those relations together with the structure the quotient inherits. For the symmetric family the distinguishing properties are:

- **Commutativity.** $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is the universal commutative quotient of $T(V)$; the exterior algebra is graded-commutative but not commutative, and the tensor algebra is neither.
- **Grading, freeness, dimension.** $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is connected graded and generated by $V$; it is the free object of the category of commutative $R$-algebras, with polynomial algebras as its free-module case; and it has infinite rank for $V \neq 0$, unlike the finite rank $2^n$ of the exterior algebra.

Conventions. Throughout, $R$ is a commutative ring with identity and $V$ is an $R$-module, free of finite rank $n$ when a rank is mentioned. We use **rank** over a general commutative ring and **dimension** over a field, where the two notions agree. The general theory is in *Algebras: A General Introduction*, and the classification by scalar ring is in *Algebras: Categorization*; the classes themselves are treated in the siblings *Tensor Algebras*, *Exterior Algebras*, and *Symmetric Algebras*.

# Part I: The Symmetric Algebra as the Free Commutative Algebra

## 1. Definition and Universal Property

The **symmetric algebra** is the quotient of the tensor algebra by the two-sided ideal generated by all commutators of elements of $V$,

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V) = T(V) \Big/ \big( v \otimes w - w \otimes v : v, w \in V \big).
$$

Its defining relation is

$$
v w = w v, \qquad v, w \in V,
$$

and the image of $v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_k$ is written $v_1 v_2 \cdots v_k$. The relation is homogeneous of degree two, so the ideal is graded and the quotient inherits the tensor grading.

**Universal property.** For every commutative unital $R$-algebra $A$ and every $R$-linear map $f : V \to A$, there is a unique unital algebra homomorphism $F : \operatorname{Sym}(V) \to A$ extending $f$. Equivalently,

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{CommAlg}_R}\big(\operatorname{Sym}(V), A\big) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(V, A),
$$

naturally in $A$. Thus $V \mapsto \operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is left adjoint to the forgetful functor from commutative $R$-algebras to $R$-modules, and $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is the **free commutative $R$-algebra on $V$**: the largest commutative $R$-algebra generated by $V$ with no relations beyond bilinearity, associativity, and commutativity.

Two consequences are used repeatedly. First, being a left adjoint, $\operatorname{Sym}$ preserves colimits, hence is right exact. Second, if $f(V)$ generates $A$, then $F$ is surjective and $A \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V)/\ker F$; so the commutative $R$-algebras generated by $V$ are exactly the quotients of $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$.

## 2. The Symmetric Algebra as a Quotient of the Tensor Algebra

Let $[T(V), T(V)]$ denote the two-sided ideal of $T(V)$ generated by the commutators $ab - ba$. Since $T(V)$ is generated by $V$, this commutator ideal is already generated by the commutators of the generators, so

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V) \cong T(V) \big/ [T(V), T(V)].
$$

This identifies the symmetric algebra as the **abelianization** of the tensor algebra, that is, its largest commutative quotient. Every commutative $R$-algebra generated by $V$ factors through it, and the quotient map $T(V) \to \operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is the universal map from $T(V)$ to a commutative algebra.

The exterior algebra is the other universal quotient by a quadratic relation, $\Lambda(V) = T(V)/(v \otimes v)$: the symmetric relation forces the generators to commute, while the exterior relation forces each generator to square to zero. The Clifford, enveloping, and Weyl algebras impose inhomogeneous relations; those quotients are filtered, with associated graded algebras that are symmetric or exterior (Parts II and IV).

## 3. Polynomial Algebras

Let $V$ be free with basis $(x_i)_{i \in I}$. Then $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is the **polynomial algebra**

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V) \cong R[x_i : i \in I], \qquad x_i \leftrightarrow e_i,
$$

and the monomials in the $x_i$, together with $1$, form an $R$-basis. In particular, for $V$ free of finite rank $n$,

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V) \cong R[x_1, \ldots, x_n], \qquad \operatorname{Sym}^k(V) \text{ free of rank } \binom{n+k-1}{k},
$$

with **Hilbert–Poincaré series**

$$
\sum_{k \geq 0} \binom{n+k-1}{k} t^k = \frac{1}{(1-t)^n}.
$$

For $n \geq 1$ the algebra has countably infinite rank: it is not finitely generated as an $R$-module, although it is generated as an $R$-algebra by the $n$ elements $x_1, \ldots, x_n$.

Structural properties of the polynomial algebra $R[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$: it is connected graded, commutative, associative, and unital; it is a domain, and a unique factorization domain, when $R$ is; it is Noetherian when $R$ is, by the **Hilbert basis theorem**; and its Krull dimension is $n$ when $R$ is a field.

**Examples.** $\operatorname{Sym}(R) \cong R[x]$ is the polynomial algebra in one variable, and $\operatorname{Sym}(R^n) \cong R[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$. Since the free commutative monoid on a basis $(x_i)$ has monoid algebra $R[\mathbb{N}^{(I)}] \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V)$, the symmetric algebra is the monoid algebra of a free commutative monoid, and $\operatorname{Sym}(0) = R$. In one variable the quotient $R[x]/(x^2) \cong R[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ is the algebra of **dual numbers**: a graded quotient of $\operatorname{Sym}(R)$ that is no longer a polynomial algebra, that has rank $2$ over $R$, and whose generator is nilpotent.

# Part II: Graded and Filtered Structure

## 4. The Grading and the Symmetric Powers

The ideal generated by $v \otimes w - w \otimes v$ is homogeneous, so the tensor grading descends:

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V) = \bigoplus_{k \geq 0} \operatorname{Sym}^k(V), \qquad \operatorname{Sym}^m(V) \cdot \operatorname{Sym}^n(V) \subseteq \operatorname{Sym}^{m+n}(V),
$$

with $\operatorname{Sym}^0(V) = R$, $\operatorname{Sym}^1(V) = V$, and $\operatorname{Sym}^k(V)$ the image of $V^{\otimes k}$. Thus $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is a **connected graded** algebra generated in degree one, and the $k$-th graded piece is the **$k$-th symmetric power** of $V$.

Being graded and generated in degree one, $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ has the property that a graded unital algebra homomorphism out of it is determined by its restriction to $V$, so graded homomorphisms $\operatorname{Sym}(V) \to A$ into a commutative graded algebra correspond to degree-preserving linear maps $V \to A_1$.

## 5. Filtered Algebras and the Associated Graded

A **filtered algebra** is an algebra $A$ with an increasing chain

$$
0 \subseteq F^0 A \subseteq F^1 A \subseteq \cdots, \qquad \bigcup_n F^n A = A, \qquad F^m A \cdot F^n A \subseteq F^{m+n} A,
$$

and its **associated graded algebra** is $\operatorname{gr} A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} F^n A / F^{n-1} A$. Every graded algebra with $A_n = 0$ for $n < 0$ is filtered by $F^n A = \bigoplus_{k \leq n} A_k$, and then $\operatorname{gr} A \cong A$, so graded algebras are a special case of filtered algebras.

The symmetric algebra is the associated graded of two filtered algebras of the family. For a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over $R$ with universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{g})$, the **Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem** gives

$$
\operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}),
$$

for the filtration by tensor length. For $V$ free with basis $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ and dual basis $y_1, \ldots, y_n$, the **Weyl algebra** $A_n(R)$ satisfies

$$
\operatorname{gr} A_n(R) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*) \cong R[x_1, \ldots, x_n, y_1, \ldots, y_n].
$$

So $\operatorname{Sym}$ is not only a graded algebra in its own right; it is the graded shadow of the two principal filtered classes. The associated graded does not determine the filtered algebra: for nonabelian $\mathfrak{g}$ the algebras $U(\mathfrak{g})$ and $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ have the same associated graded but are not isomorphic, since the first is noncommutative and the second is commutative.

## 6. Finitely Generated Graded Algebras

Let $A = \bigoplus_{k \geq 0} A_k$ be a graded commutative $R$-algebra with $A_0 = R$, generated by $A_1$. Choosing a generating set and a surjection $V \to A_1$ from a free module $V$ gives a graded surjection

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V) \longrightarrow A,
$$

so $A \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V)/I$ for a homogeneous ideal $I$. Conversely every quotient of a symmetric algebra by a homogeneous ideal is a connected graded commutative algebra. Hence:

- the connected graded commutative $R$-algebras generated in degree one are exactly the quotients of symmetric algebras;
- a commutative $R$-algebra is finitely generated as an $R$-algebra if and only if it is a quotient of a polynomial algebra $R[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, that is, of a symmetric algebra of a free module of finite rank.

The numerical invariants of such an algebra are recorded by its **Hilbert–Poincaré series** $H_A(t) = \sum_k (\operatorname{rank} A_k) t^k$, which for $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ with $V$ free of rank $n$ is $1/(1-t)^n$. Over a field, the Hilbert–Serre theorem states that for a finitely generated graded module over a polynomial ring the series is rational, with denominator a product of factors $1 - t^{d_i}$ for the degrees $d_i$ of the generators of the ring. The number of generators, the Hilbert–Poincaré series, and the Krull dimension separate a symmetric algebra from its proper quotients.

# Part III: The Symmetric Powers

## 7. Symmetric Powers and their Universal Property

The **$k$-th symmetric power** is the quotient

$$
\operatorname{Sym}^k(V) = V^{\otimes k} \Big/ \big( x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_k - x_{\sigma(1)} \otimes \cdots \otimes x_{\sigma(k)} : \sigma \in S_k \big),
$$

which makes $\operatorname{Sym}(V) = \bigoplus_k \operatorname{Sym}^k(V)$. It is the universal target of **symmetric $k$-multilinear maps**:

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_R\big(\operatorname{Sym}^k(V), P\big) \cong \operatorname{Mult}^{\text{sym}}_R(V^k; P).
$$

For $k = 0$ we have $\operatorname{Sym}^0(V) = R$, and for $k = 1$ we have $\operatorname{Sym}^1(V) = V$. If $V$ is free of finite rank $n$, then $\operatorname{Sym}^k(V)$ is free of rank $\binom{n+k-1}{k}$, realized by the monomials of degree $k$ in the basis.

Because $\operatorname{Sym}^k$ is a quotient of the tensor power, it is a **right exact** functor. It is not left exact: over $\mathbb{Z}$, applying $\operatorname{Sym}^2$ to $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\ \cdot 2\ } \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ gives $\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\ \cdot 4\ } \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$, which fails to be exact in the middle, since the kernel of the last map is $2\mathbb{Z}$ while the image of the first is $4\mathbb{Z}$.

**Characteristic zero.** Over a field of characteristic $0$, or more generally over a ring in which $k!$ is invertible, the symmetrizer

$$
e_k = \frac{1}{k!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_k} \sigma
$$

acts on $V^{\otimes k}$, and $\operatorname{Sym}^k(V) = e_k(V^{\otimes k})$ is a direct summand of $V^{\otimes k}$. The same holds over a field of characteristic $p > k$; for $p \leq k$ the symmetrizer is unavailable and the splitting can fail. The quotient definition above is the one that is correct over a general commutative ring.

## 8. Divided Power Algebras

For a module $V$, the **divided power algebra** $\Gamma(V)$ is the free commutative $R$-algebra equipped with a **divided power structure**: the commutative algebra generated by symbols $v^{(k)}$, $v \in V$, $k \geq 0$, subject to

$$
v^{(0)} = 1, \qquad v^{(1)} = v, \qquad v^{(i)} v^{(j)} = \binom{i+j}{i} v^{(i+j)},
$$

$$
(v + w)^{(k)} = \sum_{i+j=k} v^{(i)} w^{(j)}, \qquad (rv)^{(k)} = r^k v^{(k)}.
$$

It is graded, $\Gamma(V) = \bigoplus_k \Gamma^k(V)$, and universal: for every commutative $R$-algebra $A$ with a divided power structure and every $R$-linear map $f : V \to A$, there is a unique homomorphism $\Gamma(V) \to A$ with $v^{(k)} \mapsto f(v)^{(k)}$. For $V$ free of finite rank $n$, the elements $x_1^{(a_1)} \cdots x_n^{(a_n)}$ with $\sum a_i = k$ form a basis of $\Gamma^k(V)$, so

$$
\operatorname{rank} \Gamma^k(V) = \binom{n+k-1}{k} = \operatorname{rank} \operatorname{Sym}^k(V).
$$

Thus $\Gamma(V)$ and $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ have the same graded ranks but different algebra structures in general. Over a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, $k!$ is invertible, the substitution $v^{(k)} \mapsto v^k/k!$ is defined and multiplicative, and

$$
\Gamma(V) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V) \qquad (R \supseteq \mathbb{Q}).
$$

Over $\mathbb{Z}$ the two differ: $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}[x]$, whereas in $\Gamma(\mathbb{Z})$ the relation $x^{(1)} x^{(1)} = 2 x^{(2)}$ shows that $x^{(2)}$ is not in the subalgebra generated by $x^{(1)}$, since $(x^{(1)})^k = k!\, x^{(k)}$ and $2$ is not a unit; so $\Gamma(\mathbb{Z})$ is not a polynomial algebra and is not isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.

There is a duality that completes the picture: over a field, the polynomial algebra $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is a commutative and cocommutative Hopf algebra with $\Delta(x_i) = x_i \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x_i$, and its graded dual is the divided power algebra, $\Gamma^k(V^*) \cong (\operatorname{Sym}^k(V))^*$. So $\operatorname{Sym}$ and $\Gamma$ are dual to one another, and they coincide precisely when the relevant factorials are invertible.

# Part IV: Neighbouring Classes

## 9. The Exterior Algebra

The **exterior algebra** is $\Lambda(V) = T(V)/(v \otimes v)$, the universal quotient in which every generator squares to zero. It is **graded-commutative**, $\alpha \beta = (-1)^{|\alpha| |\beta|} \beta \alpha$ on homogeneous elements, and it is not commutative when $\operatorname{rank} V \geq 2$. For $V$ free of finite rank $n$, $\Lambda^k(V)$ has rank $\binom{n}{k}$ and $\operatorname{rank} \Lambda(V) = 2^n$; it is finite-dimensional, and every element of positive degree is nilpotent. In contrast, for $V \neq 0$ free, $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ has countably infinite rank, is a domain when $R$ is a domain, and has no nonzero nilpotent elements. The two universal quadratic quotients therefore separate sharply, by dimension and by the presence of nilpotents.

When $2$ is invertible the two quotients are the two pieces of the degree-two tensor power,

$$
V \otimes V \cong \operatorname{Sym}^2(V) \oplus \Lambda^2(V),
$$

the splitting being given by the idempotents $\frac{1}{2}(1 \pm \tau)$, where $\tau(x \otimes y) = y \otimes x$; the decomposition fails when $2$ is not invertible.

The exterior algebra is the Clifford algebra of the zero form, $\Lambda(V) = \mathrm{Cl}(V, 0)$, and the associated graded of every Clifford algebra; the symmetric algebra plays the same role for the enveloping and Weyl algebras, so $\operatorname{Sym}$ and $\Lambda$ are the graded shadows of the two filtered families.

## 10. The Universal Enveloping Algebra of an Abelian Lie Algebra

For a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over $R$, the **universal enveloping algebra** is

$$
U(\mathfrak{g}) = T(\mathfrak{g}) \Big/ \big( x \otimes y - y \otimes x - [x, y] : x, y \in \mathfrak{g} \big).
$$

If $\mathfrak{g}$ is **abelian**, then $[x, y] = 0$ for all $x, y$, the defining relations reduce to $xy = yx$, and the two presentations coincide:

$$
U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}) \qquad (\mathfrak{g} \text{ abelian}).
$$

So the symmetric algebra is exactly the enveloping algebra of an abelian Lie algebra, and then the PBW filtration is trivial: $\operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$. For a general Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ the relations are inhomogeneous, $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is filtered but not graded, and PBW identifies its associated graded with $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$; the largest commutative quotient is $U(\mathfrak{g})/[U(\mathfrak{g}), U(\mathfrak{g})] \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}])$, which reduces to $U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ when $\mathfrak{g}$ is abelian.

## 11. Free Graded-Commutative Algebras

A graded algebra is **graded-commutative** if $\alpha \beta = (-1)^{|\alpha| |\beta|} \beta \alpha$ on homogeneous elements. For a graded module $V = V_0 \oplus V_1$ with even part $V_0$ and odd part $V_1$, the free graded-commutative algebra on $V$ is

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(V_0) \otimes \Lambda(V_1),
$$

the tensor product of the polynomial algebra on the even generators with the exterior algebra on the odd generators. The symmetric algebra is the case $V_1 = 0$, all generators even, and the exterior algebra is the case $V_0 = 0$, all generators odd. This is the precise placement of $\operatorname{Sym}$ among the graded-commutative algebras, and it explains why $\operatorname{Sym}$ is commutative while $\Lambda$ is only graded-commutative: the Koszul sign is $+1$ on even generators.

# Part V: The Classification

## 12. The Structural Invariants

The classes of the symmetric family are separated by the following invariants.

- **Commutativity and grading.** $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ and $\Gamma(V)$ are commutative; $T(V)$ and $\Lambda(V)$ for $\operatorname{rank} V \geq 2$ are not, though $\Lambda(V)$ is graded-commutative; $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is commutative exactly when $\mathfrak{g}$ is abelian. $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$, the symmetric powers, $\Gamma(V)$, and $\Lambda(V)$ are graded, with $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ connected graded and generated in degree one; $U(\mathfrak{g})$ for nonabelian $\mathfrak{g}$ and $A_n(R)$ for $n \geq 1$ are filtered but not graded.
- **Rank and dimension.** For $V$ free of finite rank $n \neq 0$: $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ and $\Gamma(V)$ have countably infinite rank, $\Lambda(V)$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ have rank $2^n$, and $T(V)$, $U(\mathfrak{g})$, and $A_n(R)$ have infinite rank.
- **Freeness.** $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is the free commutative algebra on $V$; $\Gamma(V)$ is the free commutative algebra with divided powers; $\Lambda(V)$ is the free graded-commutative algebra on $V$ in odd degree; $T(V)$ is the free associative algebra. This is the deepest invariant, being a universal property rather than a presentation.
- **Definitions.** $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is presented by the homogeneous relation $vw - wv$, $\Lambda(V)$ by $v \wedge v$, and $\Gamma(V)$ by the divided power relations, whereas $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$, $U(\mathfrak{g})$, and $A_n(R)$ are presented by inhomogeneous relations. Accordingly $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ has no nonzero nilpotents for $V$ free over a domain, $\Lambda(V)$ has nilpotents in positive degree, and $\Gamma(V)$ exhibits torsion phenomena invisible in $\operatorname{Sym}$, such as $x^{(1)} x^{(1)} = 2 x^{(2)}$ over $\mathbb{Z}$.

## 13. The Hierarchy of the Family

Every class is a quotient of a tensor algebra. The symmetric algebra is the universal commutative quotient,

$$
T(V) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(V) = T(V) \big/ [T(V), T(V)], \qquad \operatorname{Sym}(V) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(V)/I \cong A,
$$

and it arises in turn as the associated graded of the filtered classes,

$$
\operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}), \qquad \operatorname{gr} A_n(R) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*).
$$

The exterior algebra is the parallel universal graded-commutative quotient, and the two are the even and odd halves of the free graded-commutative algebra. Divided powers refine $\operatorname{Sym}$ over rings in which the factorials are not invertible, and the symmetric powers are its graded pieces. The hierarchy is summarized by the following table.

| Class | Defining relations | Homogeneous? | Grading | Algebra type | Associated graded | Rank for free $V$ of rank $n$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $T(V)$ | none | — | connected graded | free associative | itself | infinite |
| $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ | $vw - wv = 0$ | yes | connected graded | free commutative | itself | countably infinite, $n \geq 1$ |
| $\operatorname{Sym}^k(V)$ | $x_1 \cdots x_k = x_{\sigma(1)} \cdots x_{\sigma(k)}$ | yes | single degree $k$ | commutative | — | $\binom{n+k-1}{k}$ |
| $\Gamma(V)$ | divided power relations | yes | graded | free commutative with divided powers | itself | countably infinite, $n \geq 1$ |
| $\Lambda(V)$ | $v \wedge v = 0$ | yes | connected graded, graded-commutative | free graded-commutative (odd) | itself | $2^n$ |
| $\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)$ | $v^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1$ | no | filtered, $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded | — | $\Lambda(V)$ | $2^n$ |
| $U(\mathfrak{g})$ | $xy - yx = [x, y]$ | no | filtered | — | $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ | infinite |
| $A_n(R)$ | $x_i y_j - y_j x_i = \delta_{ij} \cdot 1$ | no | filtered | — | $\operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*)$ | infinite |
| $R[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ | $x_i x_j = x_j x_i$ | yes | connected graded | polynomial algebra $= \operatorname{Sym}(R^n)$ | itself | countably infinite |
| $R[x]/(x^2)$ | $x^2 = 0$ | yes | graded | quotient of $\operatorname{Sym}(R)$ | — | $2$ |

# Summary

The family is organized by one idea: $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is the free commutative algebra on $V$, the universal commutative quotient of the tensor algebra $T(V)$, and the graded shadow of the filtered classes of the series.

**The free commutative algebra.** $\operatorname{Sym}(V) = T(V)/[T(V), T(V)]$ is the abelianization of the tensor algebra and the free object of the category of commutative $R$-algebras: $\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{CommAlg}}(\operatorname{Sym}(V), A) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(V, A)$. For $V$ free of rank $n$ it is the polynomial algebra $R[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, connected graded and generated in degree one, with $\operatorname{rank} \operatorname{Sym}^k(V) = \binom{n+k-1}{k}$ and Hilbert–Poincaré series $1/(1-t)^n$.

**Graded and filtered.** $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ is graded, hence filtered, with $\operatorname{gr} \operatorname{Sym}(V) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V)$. The filtered classes have $\operatorname{Sym}$ as associated graded: $\operatorname{gr} U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ by PBW and $\operatorname{gr} A_n(R) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(V \oplus V^*)$, and the associated graded does not determine the algebra.

**Symmetric and divided powers.** $\operatorname{Sym}^k(V)$ is the universal target of symmetric $k$-multilinear maps and is right exact but not left exact. The divided power algebra $\Gamma(V)$ is the free commutative algebra with divided powers; it has the same graded ranks as $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$, is its graded dual over a field, and equals $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ over a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra but not over $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Neighbouring classes and standard examples.** The exterior algebra $\Lambda(V)$ is the other universal quadratic quotient, graded-commutative and of rank $2^n$, and the associated graded of the Clifford algebra; over a ring in which $2$ is invertible, $V \otimes V \cong \operatorname{Sym}^2(V) \oplus \Lambda^2(V)$. The symmetric algebra is the enveloping algebra of an abelian Lie algebra and the free graded-commutative algebra on even generators, while the general free graded-commutative algebra is $\operatorname{Sym}(V_0) \otimes \Lambda(V_1)$. The standard examples are the polynomial algebras $R[x] = \operatorname{Sym}(R)$ and $R[x_1, \ldots, x_n] = \operatorname{Sym}(R^n)$, the monoid algebra $R[\mathbb{N}^n]$, the dual numbers $R[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, and $U(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ for $\mathfrak{g}$ abelian.
